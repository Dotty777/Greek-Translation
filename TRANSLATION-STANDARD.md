# Bespoke Saints English — Translation Standard

**Version 1.0 · 12 November 2026**

Companion to the [Greek Translation Roadmap](ROADMAP.md). This standard implements the owner's requested priority order and retains the existing Bespoke conventions recorded in the repository. It proposes no bulk Scripture replacements.

## 1. Fidelity governs every choice

Translate the selected Greek passage in its context. Seek the closest accurate English rendering that can be understood as English. Record why a less formally similar rendering is required, particularly for idioms and constructions with no direct equivalent.

Account for every source word, including conjunctions, particles and repeated words. A Greek word may be represented by English grammar, punctuation, a phrase or a word shared with another token. Record that relationship instead of inserting an unnatural English word merely to achieve a one-to-one count.

Preserve:

- Who acts, speaks, receives, commands or is addressed.
- Negation, questions, conditions, modality, comparisons and causal relationships.
- Number, person and meaningful referent distinctions.
- Tense and aspect in context; avoid mechanical claims that every aorist means a once-for-all action or every present denotes continuous action.
- Meaningful repetition, image, metaphor, emphasis and narrative voice.
- Source differences, difficult readings and uncertainty.

Do not add “again,” “continually,” “alone,” “only,” “all,” theological titles or explanatory clauses unless the passage warrants them. Necessary English articles, auxiliaries and other grammatical support are legitimate; material supplied meaning must be distinguishable in the evidence record and, where useful, a reader note.

A lemma's etymology does not decide its meaning in a passage. Avoid reading all of a word's possible senses into one occurrence. Use contextual Greek usage, syntax and appropriate lexicons; cite the exact evidence consulted.

## 2. Three English stages, one accountable reading

| Stage | Aim | Retained evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Close draft | Expose the Greek sense and clause relationships in intelligible English. | Source, analysis, alignment, alternatives and uncertainties. |
| Spoken draft | Make the wording natural to read and hear. | Every substantive change and its Greek justification. |
| Literary draft | Refine dignity, cadence and memorable form. | Comparison with the close/spoken drafts and the final fidelity check. |

These drafts are working stages, not three new public editions. A unit can keep identical wording through all three stages when no improvement is needed.

## 3. Traditional English and pronouns

Use clear contemporary English structure with a restrained traditional biblical register. “Poetic old-English” here means the owner's desired elevated style, including selected Early Modern forms; it does not mean translating into historical Old English.

**Retain the established Bespoke rule:** Thou/Thee/Thy/Thine in direct singular address to God; ordinary address uses you/your. Do not expand archaic pronouns to every character or add archaic third-person endings throughout the Bible.

| Function | Singular divine address |
| --- | --- |
| Subject | Thou |
| Object | Thee |
| Possessive before a noun | Thy; Thine where appropriate to the established cadence |
| Independent possessive | Thine |
| Reflexive | Thyself |
| Common verb agreement | Thou art, hast, dost, wilt, shalt; Thou didst |

Greek number always takes priority: never convert a plural addressee into singular Thou. Where ordinary “you” obscures a significant Greek singular/plural distinction, clarify naturally if the sense requires it or use a note. Do not insert “all” routinely. If the identity of an addressee is disputed, the pronoun and capitalization choice must not silently decide the question.

Preserve the benchmark's devotional capitalization when its referent is clear. Capitalization is editorial interpretation, not evidence that the underlying Greek manuscripts explicitly resolve a referent.

## 4. Cadence and vocabulary

Use parallel clauses, balanced stresses, measured repetition and apt line breaks where the source supports them. Let narrative remain narrative, argument remain argument, command remain command and poetry remain poetry. Do not force rhyme, metre or ornamental inversions.

| Prefer when accurate and clear | Avoid or explain |
| --- | --- |
| Familiar traditional words such as behold, hallow, mercy, covenant and steadfast | Automatically adding behold, lo or verily to make a passage sound sacred |
| Strong concrete verbs and the source's own images | Invented imagery and expanded devotional paraphrase |
| Measured repetition when present in Greek | Deleting repeated phrases merely for variety |
| Source-grounded coin, measure and office names, with helpful notes | Modern currency substitutions and misleading modern offices |
| Natural word order and audible clause boundaries | Inversions or long chains that make a reader guess the subject |
| Clear words with enduring meaning | Obsolete senses likely to mislead, such as prevent meaning precede or let meaning hinder |

This is a contextual guide, not a banned-word list. A beautiful phrase must be revised if it changes the source's meaning.

## 5. Names, divine names and recurring terms

Maintain a name authority list with Greek form, source edition, approved English form, aliases and meaningful name changes. Preserve deliberate distinctions and existing stable IDs; investigate apparent spelling inconsistencies before normalizing them.

Render Greek divine titles according to their actual usage and the adopted name policy. Do not silently insert a Hebrew name such as Yahweh where the selected Greek reads a title. Source comparisons may explain such relationships in notes.

Build the glossary by sense, corpus and context. Words for spirit/breath/wind, soul/life, righteousness/justice, faith/faithfulness, justify and servant/slave require explicit choices. A consistent sense should normally have a consistent rendering; a changed sense may need different English. Do not replace Greek distinctions with a preferred theological slogan.

NASB95 and NKJV may serve as reference points for fidelity and cadence; they are not the source text or an English template to copy. Earlier Saints editions and historical Bibles can suggest solutions after the Greek draft. Church Fathers and other historical interpreters inform reception notes without overriding the Greek by authority alone.

## 6. Quotations, variants and ambiguity

Translate the NT quotation from its own selected NT Greek reading. Compare it with identified OT witnesses. Match the English where wording and sense support the match; preserve omission, addition, substitution and changed emphasis where they differ.

Classify relationships as exact quotation, adapted quotation, allusion, verbal echo or thematic connection with evidence and uncertainty. Do not infer which manuscript Jesus or an apostle used merely from a similar phrase.

Separate textual uncertainty (which Greek reading?) from translation uncertainty (how to render this reading?). Record the edition's brackets and relevant apparatus treatment when actually consulted. Never splice preferred readings from multiple editions without an explicit, reviewable decision.

Preserve genuine ambiguity where English permits it. When a decision is unavoidable, state the alternatives and rationale in the review record; include an appropriate reader note. Commentary, harmonization and doctrinal synthesis belong outside Scripture.

## 7. Reading and review

For a chapter review, record the human reader/listener, date, exact revision and passage scope. Read for natural pauses, referents, long clauses, names, ambiguity, breath and unintended emphasis. A listener should be able to identify the clause relationships without silently seeing punctuation.

TTS and automated checks can reveal likely problems, but cannot be credited as human read-aloud review. An assistant's Greek analysis or second AI pass cannot be credited as independent human expertise.

After literary edits, revisit Greek for every material change. A sound improvement does not justify a different proposition.

## 8. Acceptance checklist

A unit is ready for editorial acceptance only when its exact source and stable identity are recorded, all tokens/constructions are accounted for, alternatives and material issues have dispositions, and meaningful changes have evidence. Review status must identify who actually checked what.

A **fully reviewed book release** additionally requires complete scoped language/apparatus evidence, independent Greek review, human read-aloud review, editorial acceptance and output verification. Where those stages are incomplete, use an accurate provisional label.

Keep accepted Genesis 1–11 as the historical benchmark. Evidence-supported corrections may be proposed, but benchmark status neither proves exhaustive Greek accuracy nor permits silent replacement. The current task establishes this programme; substantive text decisions will be made against concrete passage records.

## 9. Word studies attached to highlights

Maintain a clear boundary between the translation, its lexical explanation and attributed theology. Each popup is specific to its passage, source edition and English revision. Show alternate renderings with their evidence and interpretive cost; never turn a dictionary gloss into a doctrinal conclusion. Colours are thematic annotations and cannot determine a Greek word's meaning. See the [word-study programme](docs/WORD-STUDIES.md).
