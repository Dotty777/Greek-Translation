50:14|Joseph returns to Egypt with his brothers and those who accompanied the burial. The inherited clause keeps the participants joined to his return; it does not leave the brothers in Canaan or extend the return to everyone mentioned elsewhere without qualification.
50:15|The brothers fear that Joseph may hold a grudge and repay all their evil, with an intensified repayment expression. 'Fully repay' conveys that force provisionally; preserve the hypothetical fear rather than report that Joseph has already threatened vengeance.
50:16|The Greek says that they came to Joseph and report a charge given by oath before the father's death. Retain personal coming rather than insert an unnamed messenger, and distinguish their report from an independently narrated occurrence of the alleged instruction.
50:17|The quoted instruction asks forgiveness of iniquity and sin, followed by another plea from the servants of the father's God. The second verb literally concerns receiving the wrongdoing and is rendered 'pardon' contextually; retain both petitions and Joseph's simultaneous tears, with the second idiom explicitly unresolved.
50:18|The brothers come to Joseph and offer themselves as his household slaves. Retain the concrete status and their speech; the verse does not require turning this offer into an accepted contract or adding a physical bow that is not in the displayed sentence.
50:19|Joseph answers that they should not fear because he belongs to God. Preserve the positive first-person belonging statement: a rhetorical question about occupying God's place would import a different reading and alter the reason given in this Greek.
50:20|The brothers planned evil against Joseph, but God planned good concerning him so that many might be fed as today. Retain the repeated planning verb, the differing relational phrases and the feeding purpose; saving lives may explain the result but would replace the displayed wording.
50:21|Joseph repeats the reassurance and promises nourishment for the brothers and their households, then comforts them and speaks to their hearts. Keep the promise joined to consoling speech and preserve the heart idiom without recasting it as an unrecorded act of reconciliation.
50:22|Joseph lives in Egypt with his brothers and father's household, and his lifespan is given as a hundred and ten years. Retain the two living statements and family scope; the verse does not explicitly distinguish years after reunion from total lifespan by supplying a new chronology.
50:23|Joseph sees Ephraim's descendants to the third generation, while Machir's sons are born upon Joseph's thighs. Retain thighs rather than silently replacing the body term with knees. The fostering, acknowledgment or kinship significance of this birth idiom requires further review.
50:24|Joseph announces death, then promises God's emphatic visitation and ascent from Egypt to the land sworn to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. 'Surely visit' captures the doubled visitation force; preserve the three ancestors and the collective destination without narrowing the promise to Joseph's lifetime.
50:25|Joseph binds Israel's sons by oath to carry his bones when God visits them, repeating the visitation wording from the preceding promise. Retain the bones and the accompaniment phrase with you; this is a future transport instruction, not a present burial command.
50:26|Joseph dies at a hundred and ten, receives burial treatment and is placed in a coffin in Egypt. 'Performed his burial rites' renders the burial verb contextually without claiming immediate interment in a grave; the relationship between preparation, burial and coffin placement remains a lexical question.
