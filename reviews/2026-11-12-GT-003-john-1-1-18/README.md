# John 1:1–18 — Bespoke Saints English

**GT-003 · draft 1 · 2026-11-12 · provisional AI translation and review record**

Greek fidelity first, natural speech second, restrained poetic cadence third. New Testament work begins with the roadmap’s John prologue pilot. All eighteen verses have fresh Greek alignment records; twelve candidate readings retain inherited wording and six propose changes. This is a draft, not an approved replacement of the archived edition.

## Reading text

**John 1:1** In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

**John 1:2** He was in the beginning with God.

**John 1:3** All things came into being through Him, and without Him not one thing came into being. What has come into being

**John 1:4** in Him was life, and the life was the light of humankind.

**John 1:5** And the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overcome it.

**John 1:6** There came a man sent from God; his name was John.

**John 1:7** He came as a witness, to bear witness concerning the Light, so that all might believe through him.

**John 1:8** He was not the Light, but came to bear witness concerning the Light.

**John 1:9** The true Light, which gives light to every person, was coming into the world.

**John 1:10** He was in the world, and the world came into being through Him, and the world did not know Him.

**John 1:11** He came to what was His own, and His own people did not receive Him.

**John 1:12** But to all who received Him, He gave authority to become children of God—to those who believe in His name,

**John 1:13** who were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of a man, but of God.

**John 1:14** And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, glory as of the Only-begotten from the Father, full of grace and truth.

**John 1:15** John bears witness concerning Him and has cried out, saying, “This was He of whom I said, ‘The One who comes after me has taken precedence over me, because He was before me.’”

**John 1:16** For from His fullness we all received, even grace in place of grace.

**John 1:17** For the Law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.

**John 1:18** No one has ever seen God; the Only-begotten God, who is in the bosom of the Father, has made Him known.

Read verses 3–4 continuously: the end of verse 3 begins the sentence completed in verse 4. Verse numbers and anchors are preserved.

## Proposed changes

| John 1:11 | He came to what was His own, and His own did not receive Him. | He came to what was His own, and His own people did not receive Him. | Add people to preserve the distinction between what belongs to Him and those who are His own. |
| John 1:12 | But as many as received Him, He gave them authority to become children of God—to those who believe in His name, | But to all who received Him, He gave authority to become children of God—to those who believe in His name, | Recast the opening recipient construction for clearer spoken syntax while preserving all and the believing apposition. |

| John 1:15 | John bears witness concerning Him and has cried out, saying, “This was He of whom I said, ‘The One coming after me has come before me, because He was before me.’” | John bears witness concerning Him and has cried out, saying, “This was He of whom I said, ‘The One who comes after me has taken precedence over me, because He was before me.’” | Make the contrast between later appearance, precedence and prior existence audible; retain the closer wording for review. |

## Verse decisions

### John 1:1

The three imperfect forms preserve was; the creation verbs in 1:3 are different. In the last clause, the article marks the Word as the subject and God is the predicate. The absence of an article before the predicate does not mechanically require an indefinite English article. With represents the relational use of pros; do not add face to face.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: English the before beginning supports natural idiom; the Greek phrase has no article. The Greek article before God in the second clause is represented by the definite referent, without the unidiomatic the God.

Alternatives:

- **divine** (translation alternative): May foreground quality but can weaken the force of the noun God; not selected.
- **a god** (translation alternative): An anarthrous predicate is not automatically indefinite; not selected on this construction and context.


### John 1:2

The masculine demonstrative resumes the Word. He is a natural anaphoric rendering; This One is retained in the close draft to expose the demonstrative. No new person is introduced.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: The before beginning is English idiom.


### John 1:3

The two aorists describe coming into being; the final perfect is has come into being. The last relative clause continues into verse 4. Preserve the publisher’s full stop after one thing; this is a punctuation decision, not a missing-word variant.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: Things/thing realizes substantival neuter adjectives. Not one conveys the emphatic negative without a separate even.

Alternatives:

- **All things came into being through Him, and without Him not one thing came into being that has come into being. In Him was life…** (punctuation alternative): Attaches the relative clause to the preceding sentence. Not selected: retain the displayed NA28 punctuation. No manuscript-level punctuation history was reviewed.

Still open: Independent review of the 1:3–4 sentence division and the attachment of in Him remains open.

### John 1:4

Read with the end of verse 3, not as a detached sentence. Anthrōpōn is plural and generic here. Humankind renders the collective human reference without narrowing it to males. Life/light repetition is preserved.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: Of realizes the genitive. The Greek generic article with human beings is implicit in the collective humankind.


### John 1:5

The present shines and aorist did not overcome are retained. The verb can concern seizing/overtaking or mental grasp. Overcome suits the opposition of darkness and light in this draft; comprehend remains a genuine alternative, not an error ruled out by morphology.

Alternatives:

- **did not comprehend it** (translation alternative): Lexically possible cognitive sense; not selected for the current light/darkness image.
- **did not grasp it** (translation alternative): May suggest both senses, but natural English can overemphasize understanding; not selected.

Still open: Contextual choice between overcome and comprehend requires independent review.

### John 1:6

The perfect passive participle describes the man as one sent by God. Sent preserves that status naturally; it does not need a claim about a once-for-all commission. The verbless naming clause receives an English copula.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: There and a support the English introduction. His name was John represents the Greek name to him John; was is supplied grammar.


### John 1:7

Both purpose constructions are represented: the first by an infinitive, the second by so that. The noun testimony is rendered by as a witness, an idiomatic change of grammatical form, while the cognate witness repetition remains. The final him refers to John in this draft.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: He realizes the demonstrative. Witness as a person is the idiomatic realization of the purpose phrase for testimony; this is recorded rather than treated as a one-to-one lexical equivalent.


### John 1:8

The contrast excludes John from being the Light. Came is supplied from verse 7 to complete the ellipsis; the Greek does not repeat that verb. Square brackets appear only in the working close draft, not as a claim of a textual variant.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: Came is an explicit ellipsis completion from 1:7.


### John 1:9

The candidate takes erchomenon as neuter nominative singular agreeing with Light, with was forming the verbal expression was coming. The same surface form can be masculine accusative singular agreeing with person. The inherited MorphGNT tag selects the latter; the selected parse is therefore explicitly recorded as an alternative, not silently inherited. True concerns the authentic Light; no claim that every person believes is supplied.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: The repeated attributive Greek article is realized by English adjective placement. To supplies English valency for gives light; Greek person is a direct object.

Alternatives:

- **The true Light was there, which gives light to every person coming into the world.** (syntax alternative): Attaches coming to person; there completes English existential syntax. Morphology permits this; current draft follows the Light attachment and the displayed NA28 commas.

Still open: The participle attachment remains an open interpretive decision.

### John 1:10

Retain the three occurrences of world and the contrast between existence, creation and failure to know. He is the subject supplied by the preceding context; masculine Him is explicit later in the verse.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: He supplies the contextual subject of was; it is not a separate Greek token.


### John 1:11

The first own is neuter plural, the second masculine plural. What was His own leaves the domain broad; people makes the second form’s personal reference audible. No possession or group is narrowed to a specific place or population in the translation.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: He supplies the verbal subject. What was supplies English nominal syntax; it adds no separate past event. People makes the substantival masculine adjective explicit.


### John 1:12

To all who is a natural dative recipient construction absorbing the resumptive to them. Authority retains exousia and avoids reducing it to physical power. The apposition identifies the recipients as those believing in His name. The participle does not, by itself, prove a theory of uninterrupted belief.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: He supplies the subject of gave. To all who realizes both the relative description and the resumptive dative them.


### John 1:13

The relative and passive verb are plural, referring back to the recipients in verse 12. The Greek blood noun is plural; blood is the natural English mass-noun rendering here, with the literal plurality retained in the close draft and note. The verse contrasts human generation and God as source. Do not insert a biological mechanism or read the plural as proof of two parental bloodstreams.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: The before will/flesh follows English nominal idiom. Of renders each repeated source preposition.

Alternatives:

- **bloods** (translation alternative): Preserves Greek plurality visibly but is obscure in current English; retained in close draft and note.
- **bloodlines** (explanatory rendering): Makes descent explicit but narrows the image to ancestry; not selected.
- **a husband** (translation alternative): Possible contextual sense of anēr; a man retains the less specific wording.


### John 1:14

Keep flesh and the repeated glory. The adjective full is nominative masculine singular, referring to the Word rather than the accusative feminine glory. Dwelt is the reading text; the tent association of skēnoō needs specialist lexical/reception development before a tabernacle explanation is presented as settled. Only-begotten is retained provisionally as inherited traditional wording, with only/unique a live lexical alternative. The adjective itself should not be expanded into a temporal origin story.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: The definite and capitalized Only-begotten/Father identify the referents from context; the Greek phrase itself is anarthrous.

Alternatives:

- **the Only One** (translation alternative): Conveys uniqueness without specifying begetting, but can be less explicit about the filial relation. Requires editorial and specialist decision.
- **an only child from a father** (explanatory rendering): Makes the familial comparison concrete but adds child as a noun not present here. Not adopted.
- **pitched His tent among us** (explanatory rendering): Makes the tent image explicit but can suggest a literal camping act; not adopted.

Still open: Specialist judgment on monogenēs and the best audible placement of full remains open.

### John 1:15

The present bears witness and perfect has cried out remain. The candidate distinguishes coming later from precedence and prior existence. Taken precedence interprets emprosthen with gegonen as status; the following because-clause retains was before me. This is a proposed contextual clarification, not a claim that Greek has a separate word rank.

Alternatives:

- **has come before me** (translation alternative): Closer to the surface wording, but repeated before can obscure the status/time distinction for listeners; retained in close draft.

Still open: Independent review of rank/time nuance remains open.

### John 1:16

For renders hoti and all preserves the explicit universal within we. Even interprets kai as explanatory, specifying what was received. In place of retains the substitution/succession force of anti. The referents of the two graces are not named in this verse; do not insert a law-versus-gospel formula.

Alternatives:

- **and grace in place of grace** (translation alternative): Preserves additive kai and the inherited wording; retained in close draft.
- **grace upon grace** (interpretive reading): Expresses repeated or increasing grace; LSJ explicitly interprets this occurrence in an increasing sense. It is a defensible contextual alternative, not a simple proof that anti means upon here.

Still open: Additive versus explanatory kai and the relation of the two graces remain review decisions.

### John 1:17

Retain both through phrases and the different verbs given/came. There is no adversative but in the Greek; the semicolon does not introduce one. The mass nouns grace/truth naturally take no English article. Do not imply that the Law was graceless from this clause alone.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: The two Greek articles with grace and truth are represented by the definite contextual concepts without adding English the.


### John 1:18

The selected NA28 running text reads God, not Son, at this point. The open SBL comparison apparatus also prints God and reports the Son reading in its RP comparison edition; this is edition comparison, not a fresh manuscript collation. Bosom retains the image of intimacy. The final Him is an inferred object referring to God; no explicit object is written. The resumptive that One is represented by the English subject without a second pronoun.

Supplied or grammatically realized English: The before Only-begotten God is English definite reference from context; the selected Greek phrase has no article. Him is the understood object of made known. The English perfect is an idiomatic rendering of the aorist here.

Alternatives:

- **the only Son** (textual variant): Requires a different Greek reading; recorded from SBL’s RP comparison, not adopted.
- **the unique God / the Only One, who is God** (translation alternative): Possible ways of representing monogenēs with the selected noun, but their English scope and Christological implications require specialist review.
- **at the Father’s side** (explanatory rendering): Clarifies closeness but removes the bosom image; not selected.

Still open: Full NA28 apparatus, UBS6 comparison and specialist monogenēs judgment remain pending.

## Word-study drafts

### John 1:5: Overcome or comprehend?

The verb can describe overcoming or understanding. This draft retains overcome for the conflict between darkness and light, while noting comprehend as a real alternative.

The image favors the conflict sense in this provisional reading; morphology alone cannot choose the sense.

Greek: κατέλαβεν; katelaben. Review: running text and general lexicon checked. No live popup has been updated.

### John 1:13: Why blood, when the Greek is plural?

The Greek literally has bloods. The reading draft uses the natural English mass noun blood; the close draft preserves the plural. The surrounding clauses concern the source of birth.

Bloods is obscure for listeners. Bloodlines is clearer but more specifically interprets the image as ancestry.

Greek: αἱμάτων; haimatōn. Review: running text checked lexical review pending. No live popup has been updated.

### John 1:14: Only-begotten, only, or unique?

Only-begotten is retained provisionally from the inherited Saints edition. Only One and only child are alternatives to examine; this draft does not treat the traditional wording as the sole possible lexical rendering.

A partial lexical lookup supports only/single-child language, but the specialist study is unfinished. Preserve the inherited term until the alternatives receive an explicit editorial decision.

Greek: μονογενοῦς; monogenous. Review: running text checked specialist lexicon pending. No live popup has been updated.

### John 1:16: Grace in place of grace

The draft keeps the succession or exchange image. Grace upon grace is a defensible interpretive alternative; the preposition alone does not settle which graces are meant.

In place of keeps a well-attested prepositional relation visible. LSJ also interprets this Johannine phrase as increasing grace, so that alternative remains open.

Greek: χάριν ἀντὶ χάριτος; charin anti charitos. Review: running text and general lexicon checked. No live popup has been updated.

### John 1:18: Why God here, rather than Son?

The selected NA28 text reads God here. Son belongs to a different Greek reading; it is not simply another translation of the same noun. The meaning of Only-begotten remains under review.

Keep the governing edition’s noun, and record the alternative reading separately from the lexical question.

Greek: μονογενὴς θεὸς; monogenēs theos. Review: running text and limited edition comparison checked. No live popup has been updated.
