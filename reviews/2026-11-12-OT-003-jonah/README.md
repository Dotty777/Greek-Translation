# Jonah — initial Old Testament Greek comparison

**OT-003 · 12 November 2026 · Provisional AI draft; no acceptance or publication**

All 48 inherited Jonah units were compared with the German Bible Society's displayed Rahlfs–Hanhart 2006 Greek running text: [chapter 1](https://www.die-bibel.de/en/bible/LXX/JON.1), [chapter 2](https://www.die-bibel.de/en/bible/LXX/JON.2), [chapter 3](https://www.die-bibel.de/en/bible/LXX/JON.3), and [chapter 4](https://www.die-bibel.de/en/bible/LXX/JON.4). These pages were actually retrieved and read for this batch. The source-numbered distribution remains 16, 11, 10 and 11 units.

The baseline is `jonah/verses.json` at repository commit `39a2bb990f252f9c867879f14942b200c4a86aa5`. Original book files remain intact. [The comparison records](focused-reviews.json) preserve exact inherited wording, verse identity, proposed wording, its SHA-256 hash, source locator, passage-specific rationale, and open questions. [The builder](build-review.py) reproduces the JSON from that inherited file and the authored notes; it does not retrieve or translate Greek automatically.

Forty-five units are provisionally retained. Three revisions are proposed:

| Passage | Proposal | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jonah 3:9 | “so that we shall not perish” | An intelligible future result within the question about possible relenting. The negative must not become a separate present guarantee. |
| Jonah 4:10 | “You had pity on the gourd” | Avoid implying that Jonah successfully saved the plant; express his concern after its loss. |
| Jonah 4:11 | “shall I not have pity on Nineveh” | Preserve the repeated Greek verb and the analogy with the preceding verse. Sparing remains the contextual implication. |

The paired pity proposal is a contextual judgment. The general [Middle Liddell entry for *pheidomai*](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn:cite2:scaife-viewer:dictionary-entries.atlas_v1:middle-liddell.perseus-eng2-n34599/) was read for its range of sparing, mercy and care. It does not establish this choice as a specialist Septuagint finding. The inherited “spared/spare” remains an explicit alternative for review.

Greek-specific features remain visible: Jonah is a servant of the Lord in 1:9; the sea creature begins at 2:1; 3:4 announces three days; 3:8 narrates past actions; 4:4 and 4:9 ask about grief. These were checked against the selected displayed text, not established by fresh comparison with Hebrew or an apparatus.

The retained wording of **2:7 and 2:10 is unresolved**, particularly the ruin/life imperative and final deliverance construction. Retention is not endorsement as finished English. The water/life expression in 2:6, mercy in 2:9, nautical title, distributive pronouns, plant identification and other noted questions also remain open. No familiar alternate wording has been silently substituted to solve them.

This batch records focused verse-level comparison, **not exhaustive token alignment**. Apparatus and Göttingen comparison, specialist lexical/syntactic review, independent human Greek review, human reading aloud, editorial acceptance and website publication remain pending. The three working draft stages have not been separately documented here. No new unit is counted as a fully reviewed or accepted translation.
