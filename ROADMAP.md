**Version 1.3 · 12 November 2026 · Old Testament work begun across the 43-book scope**

**Governing order: Greek fidelity → natural reading aloud → reverent poetic English.** A later stage may improve an earlier rendering only while preserving its meaning. This roadmap begins a documented, source-led translation and review programme for the existing Saints seventy-book edition.

**This programme will establish a traceable Greek-to-English record for every unit.** Draft directly from the identified Greek passage, then compare with the inherited Saints rendering. ## 2. The translation covenant

| Priority | Required result | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Literal accuracy** | Represent the contextual meaning of every Greek word, construction, relationship, qualification and meaningful repetition as closely as intelligible English allows. | Every source token is accounted for in an English word/span, a grammatical function, or an explained non-explicit rendering. Every meaningful English addition has a recorded basis. |
| **2. Natural aloud** | Clear English syntax, audible clause relationships and a sentence that a reader can speak without guessing its structure. | A human reading and listening review records stumbling points and misunderstandings; changes are checked again against Greek. |
| **3. Poetic elevation** | Dignified, memorable, restrained traditional English that carries the source's imagery, parallelism and cadence. | A comparison with the accurate draft shows no added doctrine, altered agent, lost qualification, softened difficulty or invented imagery. |

“Literal” does not require the same English word for every occurrence of a Greek lemma, or one English word per Greek word. Greek articles, particles, idioms and inflections need context-sensitive treatment. Accountability is exhaustive; English word order need not imitate Greek mechanically.

The intended register is **elevated traditional English with selected Early Modern forms**, rather than historical Anglo-Saxon Old English. Literary beauty serves the sacred text and its public reading; ornament is never evidence of accuracy or authority. See the [full standard](TRANSLATION-STANDARD.md).

## 5. Delivery phases and exit criteria

| Phase | Work and deliverables | Exit criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0 — Establish the evidence baseline** | Import existing source/review evidence; record actual sources per book and special section; freeze the text snapshot and count units reproducibly; record export ownership, source access and unresolved blockers. | Every book has a source record and review scope; every planned batch has accessible source material or a named access blocker. Existing histories remain intact. |
| **1 — Calibrate Bespoke Saints English** | Review Genesis 1:1–5, then all Genesis 1. Preserve the accepted benchmark while documenting evidence-supported proposals separately. Trial John 1:1–18, Psalm 22 LXX / 23 MT, Isaiah 53 with Acts 8:32–35, and Romans 3:21–31 with James 2:14–26. | Vince has concrete wording/style decisions to assess. The workflow handles prose, poetry, quotation and doctrinally sensitive terminology without forcing agreement. Pilot issues have recorded dispositions. |
| **2 — Translate and collate each passage** | Produce Greek analysis, a close English draft and a decision for every retained or changed unit. Resolve source identity and textual variants before polishing. | Every unit in the batch has source locators and lexical/syntactic accountability. Material unresolved readings block final acceptance or receive an explicit provisional disposition. |
| **3 — Read aloud and refine** | Produce natural and literary revisions, listening notes, glossary decisions and a reverse check against Greek after each meaningful edit. | A human read-aloud record exists; no unresolved meaning change was introduced for rhythm. A language reviewer checks substantive refinements. |
| **4 — Review and accept the book** | Independent language review, applicable apparatus review, terminology/quotation audit and Vince's editorial decisions; preserve minority readings and uncertainty in notes. | Every required unit has evidence for each claimed stage. No open meaning-changing defect remains for a reviewed release. Independent review is credited only to actual qualified reviewers. |
| **5 — Publish a staging edition** | Apply accepted records through the verified publishing route; reconcile JSON, HTML/TXT, chapter pages, Parallel, search, highlights, study quotations and downloads. | Exact text and IDs agree for all affected outputs; special units and personal-note anchors survive; served staging and offline/cache behaviour have scoped verification and rollback evidence. |
| **6 — Release and maintain** | Produce a named release manifest, changelog, source/review notices and corrections queue. Production promotion is a separately scoped decision. | The released text is reproducible from its recorded snapshot; readers can identify the edition and review limits. |

Phases 2–4 repeat by passage and book. A book may have a provisional reading release while independent review remains pending, but its label must say so. A polished draft, a successful upload and a fully reviewed translation are different achievements.

## 7. Passage workflow

1. **Identify:** book, reading section, stable anchor, displayed reference, edition and baseline commit.
2. **Establish the Greek:** exact source locator, relevant wording and apparatus observations actually consulted; distinguish evidence from an unresolved proposal.
3. **Analyse:** tokens, lemmas, morphology, clause structure, referents, idiom, semantic range and textual uncertainty. Validate machine analyses against the source.
4. **Draft closely:** translate from Greek before consulting familiar English phrasing. Account for every token and every meaningful English addition.
5. **Compare:** inspect inherited Saints wording, parallel passages, quotations and relevant reference translations. Record retained readings as well as changes.
6. **Make it speakable:** improve English without losing agent, number, tense/aspect, modality, emphasis, imagery or logical relationships.
7. **Refine the cadence:** use the standard's restrained traditional register. Recheck each substantive change against Greek.
8. **Review:** record language, read-aloud and editorial judgments separately, including review scope, date and identity.
9. **Publish and verify:** release accepted text with a manifest; verify exact served readings and dependent study material.

The [template](templates/verse-review.json) is a record shape with empty values, not a completed analysis. It supports many-to-many alignment, grammatical realizations and uncertain readings. Never fill its evidence fields with inferred or invented source text.

## 8. Terminology and difficult readings

Build a contextual glossary during the pilot, starting with terms for God/Lord, spirit/breath/wind, soul/life, flesh, word/saying, faith/faithfulness, righteousness/justice, justify, grace, covenant, law, love, repentance, eternal/age, servant/slave, angel/messenger, Hades/Gehenna, worship/obeisance and salvation.

A glossary records a preferred rendering **for a defined sense**, evidence and allowed exceptions. It is not a global find-and-replace list. Preserve source ambiguity where English can do so; when it cannot, record the viable alternatives and the reason for the selected rendering.

High-priority specialist cases include the existing Genesis queue (especially 4:7 and chapter 49), Old Greek Daniel's difficult clauses, Greek Esther's additions, Psalms/Jeremiah mapping, NT source variants and OT quotations. Import book-specific queues instead of pretending this short list is exhaustive.

## 9. Highlighted word and phrase studies

Expand the existing colour-linked popups as an integrated translation workstream. Each study will show the Greek form and lemma, meaning in context, why Saints uses its wording, defensible alternatives, relevant passages and sources. Keep theological interpretation in a clearly labelled deeper section.

**First popup pilot: James 2:24.** The existing text uses “justified.” The study will assess “declared righteous” as an explanatory rendering and discuss “shown to be righteous / vindicated” as an interpretive alternative requiring argument. Correct “declared righteousness” to “declared righteous.” Do not imply that choosing an English gloss alone settles the theological debate.

The [full word-study plan](docs/WORD-STUDIES.md) contains the draft example, alternative-rendering table, source requirements, colour policy, accessibility checks, versioning and WS-001–WS-005 rollout. It reuses existing popup infrastructure after inventory. Studies follow the exact passage and edition; colours indicate themes and cannot decide lexical meaning. Update and recheck attached studies whenever translation wording changes.

Track popup coverage and review separately from translation completion. No live popup or Scripture change is made by this planning release.

## 10. Honest progress and responsibility

The new [review tracker](review-progress.json) preserves links to all seventy legacy book records. Its new-programme counters begin at zero; historical detailed review coverage remains **not yet reconciled**, rather than being erased or treated as zero. Prior specialist-quality evidence may be credited only after its actual scope is checked.

Track independently:

- Units with a declared and accessed source.
- Units with documented lexical/syntactic collation.
- Units with completed relevant apparatus review.
- Units with independent Greek review.
- Units with human read-aloud review.
- Units with editorial acceptance.
- Units verified in staging and units released to production.
- Open material issues, next batch, blockers and evidence links.

Count distinct stable units against an explicit edition denominator. Never calculate “translation complete” from changed verses, files present, inherited completion flags or automated check totals.

**Vince:** editorial and style decisions, substantive wording decisions and release scope. **Translation editor/assistant:** source-led drafts, citations, evidence records and proposed changes. **Qualified independent reviewer:** Greek and apparatus judgments within a declared scope. **Human reader/listener:** audible clarity. **Publishing maintainer:** output parity and recoverable releases. These are roles to assign; this plan does not claim that reviewers have already been engaged. A second AI pass can find defects, but does not become independent human scholarly review.

The existing book files remain the inherited edition. New review records should be stored under a dated, batch-specific review directory and linked by the tracker. For each accepted text batch, record the old and new text, source decision, affected anchors, reviewer decisions, commit/snapshot, outputs regenerated and rollback route. Keep highlighting and commentary outside the Scripture text. Re-anchor highlights after wording changes and review their meaning; never alter a translation to obtain a colour balance. Cross-reference categories and comparison labels must follow the revised evidence.

The website roadmap's source cards, release manifests, revision explorer and downloads depend on these records. A documentation commit completes planning work only. It does not deploy a website change, migrate the Greek base or authorize an unreviewed text replacement.
