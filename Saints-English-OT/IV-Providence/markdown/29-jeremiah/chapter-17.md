## Text

**17:5** Cursed is the man who has his hope in man, and who rests the flesh of his arm upon him, and whose heart departs from the Lord.

**17:6** And he shall be like the wild tamarisk in the wilderness; he shall not see when good things come, and he shall dwell in salty wastes and in the wilderness, in a salt land which is not inhabited.

**17:7** And blessed is the man who trusts in the Lord, and the Lord shall be his hope.

**17:8** And he shall be like a flourishing tree beside waters, and toward moisture it shall send out its root; it shall not fear when heat comes, and upon it shall be lush branches; in a year of drought it shall not fear, and it shall not cease yielding fruit.

**17:9** Deep is the heart above all things, and he is a man; and who shall know him?

**17:10** I am the Lord, examining hearts and testing the inward parts, to give to each one according to his ways and according to the fruits of his doings.

**17:11** The partridge has cried out and gathered what she did not bring forth; so is he who makes his wealth without justice: in the midst of his days they shall forsake him, and at his end he shall be a fool.

**17:12** An exalted throne of glory is our sanctuary.

**17:13** O Expectation of Israel, Lord, let all who forsake You be put to shame; let those who have departed be written upon the earth, for they have forsaken the fountain of life, the Lord.

**17:14** Heal me, O Lord, and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved, for You are my boast.

**17:15** Behold, they say to me, "Where is the word of the Lord? Let it come!"

**17:16** But I have not grown weary in following after You, nor have I desired the day of man; You know. What goes forth through my lips is before Your face.

**17:17** Do not become an estrangement to me, You who spare me in an evil day.

**17:18** Let those who persecute me be put to shame, and may I not be put to shame; let them be terrified, and may I not be terrified. Bring upon them an evil day, and crush them with a double crushing.

**17:19** Thus says the Lord: "Go and stand in the gates of the sons of your people, by which the kings of Judah enter and by which they go out, and in all the gates of Jerusalem,

**17:20** and you shall speak to them the word of the Lord: 'Kings of Judah, and all Judea, and all Jerusalem, who enter by these gates,

**17:21** thus says the Lord: Guard your souls, and do not carry burdens on the Sabbath day, and do not go out by the gates of Jerusalem,

**17:22** and do not bring burdens out of your houses on the Sabbath day, and you shall do no work; sanctify the Sabbath day, just as I commanded your fathers. Yet they did not listen, nor did they incline their ear,

**17:23** and they stiffened their neck more than their fathers, so as not to listen to Me and not to receive instruction.

**17:24** And it shall come to pass, if you listen to Me, says the Lord, so as not to bring burdens in through the gates of this city on the Sabbath day, and to sanctify the Sabbath day so as not to do any work,

**17:25** then there shall enter through the gates of this city kings and rulers sitting upon the throne of David, riding upon their chariots and horses—they and their rulers, the men of Judah and those who dwell in Jerusalem—and this city shall be inhabited forever;

**17:26** and they shall come from the cities of Judah, and from round about Jerusalem, and from the land of Benjamin, and from the lowland, and from the mountain, and from the south, bringing burnt offerings and sacrifice and incense and manna and frankincense, bringing praise into the house of the Lord.

**17:27** And it shall come to pass, if you do not listen to Me to sanctify the Sabbath day, not to carry burdens and not to enter by the gates of Jerusalem on the Sabbath day, then I will kindle a fire in its gates, and it shall devour the streets of Jerusalem, and it shall not be quenched.'"

## Translator Notes

- **17:5** — The Septuagint omits verses 17:1-4 (present in the Masoretic Text) and begins chapter 17 directly with this verse.
- **17:9** — The Septuagint reads "Deep is the heart above all things, and he is a man," rendering the underlying Hebrew consonants as "deep" (batheia) and "man" (anthropos) rather than "deceitful" and "desperately sick."
- **17:16** — The Greek reads "I have not grown weary in following after You, nor have I desired the day of man," again understanding the Hebrew consonants as "man" rather than "woeful."
- **17:26** — Greek manna ("manna") here transliterates the Hebrew minchah ("grain offering"), appearing in the Septuagint alongside incense and frankincense.
