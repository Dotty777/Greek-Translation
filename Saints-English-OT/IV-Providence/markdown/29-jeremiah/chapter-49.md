## Text

**49:1** Now all the commanders of the forces, and Johanan, and Azariah the son of Maaseiah, and all the people from the least even to the greatest, came near

**49:2** to Jeremiah the prophet and said to him, "Let our plea for mercy now fall before you, and pray to the Lord your God concerning this remnant; for we are left but a few out of many, as your eyes see,

**49:3** and may the Lord your God declare to us the way in which we should walk, and the word which we should do."

**49:4** Then Jeremiah said to them, "I have heard. Behold, I will pray to the Lord our God according to your words; and it shall come to pass that whatever word the Lord answers, I will declare to you. I will not conceal a word from you."

**49:5** And they said to Jeremiah, "Let the Lord be a righteous and faithful witness among us, if we do not act according to every word that the Lord sends to us.

**49:6** Whether it is good or whether it is evil, we will heed the voice of the Lord our God, to whom we send you, that it may be better for us, because we will heed the voice of the Lord our God."

**49:7** And it came to pass after ten days that the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah.

**49:8** Then he called Johanan and the commanders of the forces, and all the people from the least even to the greatest,

**49:9** and said to them, "Thus says the Lord:

**49:10** 'If you indeed remain in this land, I will build you up and will not tear you down, and I will plant you and will not pluck you up; for I have relented concerning the evils that I have done to you.

**49:11** Do not be afraid of the king of Babylon, of whom you are afraid; do not be afraid,' says the Lord, 'for I am with you to rescue you and to save you from their hand.

**49:12** And I will grant you mercy, and I will have mercy on you, and I will bring you back to your own land.'

**49:13** But if you say, 'We will not remain in this land,' so as not to obey the voice of the Lord,

**49:14** 'because we will enter the land of Egypt, and we shall not see war, nor hear the sound of a trumpet, nor hunger for bread, and there we will dwell'—

**49:15** therefore hear the word of the Lord. Thus says the Lord: 'If you set your face toward Egypt and enter there to dwell,

**49:16** then it shall come to pass that the sword which you fear shall find you in the land of Egypt, and the famine about which you are anxious shall overtake you right behind you in Egypt, and there you shall die.

**49:17** And so it shall be with all the men and all the foreigners who set their face toward the land of Egypt to dwell there: they shall perish by the sword and by the famine, and not one of them shall escape from the evils that I am bringing upon them.'

**49:18** For thus says the Lord: 'Just as My wrath was poured out upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so My wrath shall be poured out upon you when you enter Egypt; and you shall become an untrodden waste, and in subjection, and a curse, and a reproach, and you shall no longer see this place.'

**49:19** These are the things which the Lord has spoken concerning you, O remnant of Judah: 'Do not enter Egypt.' And now you shall surely know

**49:20** that you acted wickedly in your souls when you sent me, saying, 'Pray for us to the Lord,' and, 'According to all that the Lord speaks to you, we will do.'

**49:21** Yet you have not obeyed the voice of the Lord in that for which He sent me to you.

**49:22** Now therefore by the sword and by the famine you shall perish in the place where you desire to go and dwell."

## Translator Notes

- **49:1** — Greek reads "Azariah the son of Maaseiah," whereas the Masoretic Text (Jeremiah 42:1) reads "Jezaniah the son of Hoshaiah."
- **49:12** — Greek keeps all three verbs in the first person ("I will grant... I will have mercy... I will bring you back"), making the Lord the direct subject rather than the king of Babylon as in the Masoretic Text.
- **49:17** — Greek includes "and all the foreigners" among those setting their faces toward Egypt, a phrase not present in the Masoretic Text.
- **49:18** — Greek renders the judgment terms as "an untrodden waste, and in subjection," diverging from "an execration and an astonishment" in the Masoretic Text.
