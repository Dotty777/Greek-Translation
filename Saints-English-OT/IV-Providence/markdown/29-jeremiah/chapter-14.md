## Text

**14:1** And the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah concerning the drought.

**14:2** Judea has mourned, and her gates have been emptied; they have grown dark upon the ground, and the outcry of Jerusalem has gone up.

**14:3** And her nobles sent their young men for water; they came to the wells and found no water, and they brought back their vessels empty.

**14:4** And the works of the land failed, because there was no rain; the farmers were ashamed, and they covered their heads.

**14:5** Even the does in the field gave birth and abandoned their young, because there was no herbage.

**14:6** Wild donkeys stood upon the ravines; they gasped for the wind; their eyes failed, because there was no grass.

**14:7** Our sins have stood against us; O Lord, act for us for Your own sake, for our sins are many before You, because against You we have sinned.

**14:8** O Hope of Israel, O Lord, You who save in a time of troubles, why have You become like a sojourner in the land, and like a native turning aside to a lodging place?

**14:9** Shall You be like a man asleep, or like a man unable to save? Yet You are in our midst, O Lord, and Your name has been called upon us; do not forget us!

**14:10** Thus says the Lord to this people: "They have loved to move their feet and have not held back, and God has not prospered them; now He will remember their iniquity."

**14:11** Then the Lord said to me, "Do not pray for this people for their good;

**14:12** for when they fast, I will not hear their supplication, and when they offer burnt offerings and sacrifices, I will not be pleased with them; for by the sword, and by famine, and by death I will make an end of them."

**14:13** And I said, "O Existing One, Lord, behold, their prophets prophesy and say, 'You shall not see the sword, nor shall famine be among you, for I will give truth and peace upon the land and in this place.'"

**14:14** And the Lord said to me, "The prophets prophesy lies in My name; I did not send them, nor did I command them, nor did I speak to them. For false visions, and divinations, and omens, and the purposes of their own heart they prophesy to you.

**14:15** Therefore thus says the Lord concerning the prophets who prophesy lies in My name—though I did not send them—who say, 'Sword and famine shall not be upon this land': By a pestilent death they shall die, and by famine the prophets shall be consumed,

**14:16** and the people to whom they prophesy; and they shall be cast out in the streets of Jerusalem because of the sword and the famine, and there shall be no one to bury them, or their wives, or their sons, or their daughters; and I will pour out their evils upon them.

**14:17** And you shall say to them this word: 'Bring down tears upon your eyes day and night, and let them not cease; for the daughter of my people has been shattered with a great shattering, and with an exceedingly grievous blow.

**14:18** If I go out into the plain, behold, those slain by the sword! And if I enter into the city, behold, the torment of famine! For priest and prophet have gone into a land which they did not know.'"

**14:19** Have You utterly rejected Judah, and has Your soul departed from Zion? Why have You struck us, so that there is no healing for us? We waited for peace, and there was no good; for a time of healing, and behold, turmoil!

**14:20** We acknowledge, O Lord, our sins, the iniquities of our fathers, for we have sinned before You.

**14:21** Relent for Your name's sake; do not destroy the throne of Your glory. Remember, do not break Your covenant with us.

**14:22** Is there among the idols of the nations any that sends rain? Or can heaven give its abundance? Are You not He? And we will wait upon You, O Lord, for You have made all these things.

## Translator Notes

- **14:8** — The Septuagint reads "like a native" (hos autochthon), contrasting with the Masoretic Text's "like a traveler."
- **14:13** — The Greek address Ho on, Kyrie ("O Existing One, Lord") echoes the Septuagint's rendering of the divine name in Exodus 3:14.
- **14:17** — The Septuagint uses a second-person plural command ("Bring down tears upon your eyes") rather than the first-person singular ("Let my eyes run down with tears") found in the Masoretic Text.
