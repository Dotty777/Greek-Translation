## Text

**52:1** Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem; and his mother's name was Hamutal, the daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.

**52:4** Now it came to pass in the ninth year of his reign, in the ninth month, on the tenth day of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and all his army came against Jerusalem; and they laid siege to it and built four-footed siege works all around it.

**52:5** And the city was under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah,

**52:6** on the ninth day of the month; and the famine grew severe in the city, and there was no bread for the people of the land.

**52:7** Then the city was breached, and all the men of war went out by night by the way of the gate between the wall and the outer wall, which was by the king's garden, while the Chaldeans were all around the city. And they went by the way toward the Arabah,

**52:8** but the army of the Chaldeans pursued after the king, and they overtook him beyond Jericho; and all his servants were scattered from him.

**52:9** So they seized the king and brought him to the king of Babylon at Deblatha; and he spoke to him with judgment.

**52:10** And the king of Babylon slew the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes; and he also slew all the rulers of Judah in Deblatha.

**52:11** And he blinded the eyes of Zedekiah and bound him in fetters; and the king of Babylon brought him to Babylon and put him in the millhouse until the day he died.

**52:12** Now in the fifth month, on the tenth day of the month, Nebuzaradan the chief cook, who stood before the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem,

**52:13** and he burned the house of the Lord and the king's house; and all the houses of the city, and every great house, he burned with fire.

**52:14** And the army of the Chaldeans that was with the chief cook tore down every wall of Jerusalem all around.

**52:16** And the chief cook left the remnant of the people behind as vinedressers and as farmers.

**52:17** And the Chaldeans broke in pieces the bronze pillars that were in the house of the Lord, and the bases, and the bronze sea that was in the house of the Lord, and took their bronze and carried it away to Babylon.

**52:18** Also the crown, and the bowls, and the fleshhooks, and all the bronze vessels with which they ministered,

**52:19** and the sapphoth, and the masmaroth, and the pouring vessels, and the lampstands, and the censers, and the ladles—whatever was of gold, in gold, and whatever was of silver, in silver—the chief cook took away.

**52:20** And the two pillars, and the one sea, and the twelve bronze calves beneath the sea, which King Solomon had made for the house of the Lord—the bronze of them was beyond weight.

**52:21** Now as for the pillars, the height of one pillar was thirty-five cubits, and a cord of twelve cubits encircled it, and its thickness was four fingers all around;

**52:22** and a bronze capital was upon them, and five cubits was the length, the height of one capital, with latticework and pomegranates upon the capital all around, all of bronze; and likewise for the second pillar, eight pomegranates to a cubit for the twelve cubits.

**52:23** And the pomegranates were ninety-six on one side, and all the pomegranates upon the latticework all around were one hundred.

**52:24** And the chief cook took the first priest, and the second priest, and the three who guarded the way;

**52:25** and one eunuch who was overseer of the men of war, and seven men of renown who were in the presence of the king, who were found in the city, and the scribe of the armies who acted as scribe for the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land who were found in the midst of the city.

**52:26** And Nebuzaradan the chief cook took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Deblatha.

**52:27** And the king of Babylon struck them down in Deblatha, in the land of Hamath.

**52:31** Now it came to pass in the thirty-seventh year after Jehoiakim king of Judah was carried into exile, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-fourth day of the month, that Ulaimadachar king of Babylon, in the year in which he began to reign, lifted up the head of Jehoiakim king of Judah and shaved him, and brought him out of the house where he was kept under guard;

**52:32** and he spoke kindly to him, and set his throne above the kings who were with him in Babylon;

**52:33** and they changed his prison garment, and he ate bread continually before him all the days that he lived.

**52:34** And his allowance was given to him continually from the king of Babylon, from day to day, until the day he died.

## Translator Notes

- **52:4** — The Greek dates the start of the siege to 'the ninth month' (Masoretic Text: 'the tenth month'), describes the siege works as 'four-footed,' and omits verses 2-3, 15, and 28-30 found in the Masoretic Text.
- **52:9** — The Greek reads 'Deblatha' here and in verses 10, 26, and 27 (Masoretic Text: 'Riblah'), and in verse 11 places Zedekiah in 'the millhouse' (Masoretic Text: 'the prison').
- **52:12** — 'Chief cook' literally renders the Greek title archimageiros, the Septuagint's standard equivalent for the Babylonian captain of the royal guard.
- **52:19** — The Greek retains two transliterated Hebrew terms for temple utensils, 'sapphoth' (likely basins or bowls) and 'masmaroth' (likely snuffers or forks).
- **52:21** — The Greek gives the height of each pillar as 'thirty-five cubits' (matching 2 Chronicles 3:15; Masoretic Text: 'eighteen cubits') and adds 'eight pomegranates to a cubit for the twelve cubits' in verse 22.
- **52:31** — The Greek names the exiled king 'Jehoiakim' (Masoretic Text: 'Jehoiachin') and the Babylonian king 'Ulaimadachar' (Masoretic Text: 'Evil-Merodach'), dates the release to 'the twenty-fourth day' (Masoretic Text: 'twenty-fifth'), and adds that he 'shaved him.'
