## Text

**15:1** And the Lord said to me, "Even if Moses and Samuel stand before My face, My soul is not toward them; send this people away, and let them go out.

**15:2** And it shall be, if they say to you, 'Where shall we go out?' then you shall say to them, 'Thus says the Lord: As many as are for death, to death; and as many as are for the sword, to the sword; and as many as are for famine, to famine; and as many as are for captivity, to captivity.'

**15:3** And I will bring vengeance upon them with four kinds, says the Lord: the sword for slaughter, and the dogs for tearing apart, and the beasts of the earth and the birds of heaven for devouring and destruction.

**15:4** And I will deliver them into distresses to all the kingdoms of the earth because of Manasseh the son of Hezekiah, king of Judah, for all that he did in Jerusalem.

**15:5** Who will have pity on you, O Jerusalem? And who will be dismayed over you? Or who will turn back for your peace?

**15:6** You have turned away from Me, says the Lord; you shall go backward; and I will stretch out My hand and destroy you, and I will no longer spare them.

**15:7** And I will scatter them in dispersion; in the gates of My people they were made childless; they have destroyed My people because of their wickedness,

**15:8** their widows were multiplied more than the sand of the sea. I brought young men against a mother, distress at midday; they suddenly cast upon her trembling and alarm.

**15:9** She who bears seven was emptied, her soul grew faint; her sun went down while it was still midday; she was put to shame and reproached. The remnant of them I will give to the sword before their enemies."

**15:10** Woe is me, mother, as whom did you bear me? A man disputing and contending in all the earth! Neither have I profited anyone, nor has anyone profited me; my strength has failed among those who curse me.

**15:11** May it be so, O Master, while they prosper, if I did not stand before You in the time of their calamities and in the time of their affliction, for good against the enemy.

**15:12** Will iron be known? And a covering of bronze is your strength.

**15:13** And your treasures I will give for plunder, as an exchange for all your sins and in all your borders.

**15:14** And I will enslave you on every side to your enemies in the land which you did not know; for a fire has been kindled out of My wrath; upon you it shall burn.

**15:15** O Lord, remember me and visit me, and vindicate me from those who pursue me, not with longsuffering; know that for Your sake I have borne reproach

**15:16** from those who reject Your words; make an end of them! And Your word shall be to me for gladness and joy of my heart, for Your name has been called upon me, O Lord Almighty.

**15:17** I did not sit in their assembly while they made merry, but I stood in awe before Your hand; I sat alone, for I was filled with bitterness.

**15:18** Why do those who grieve me prevail against me? My wound is severe; from where shall I be healed? Indeed, it has become to me like deceitful water, having no faithfulness.

**15:19** Therefore thus says the Lord: "If you return, then I will restore you, and you shall stand before My face; and if you bring forth the precious from the cheap, you shall be as My mouth. And they shall turn back to you, and you shall not turn back to them.

**15:20** And I will make you to this people as a fortified bronze wall; and they shall fight against you, yet they shall by no means prevail against you, for I am with you to save you

**15:21** and to deliver you from the hand of wicked men, and I will redeem you from the hand of pestilent men."

## Translator Notes

- **15:7** — The Septuagint reads "in dispersion" and third-person plural verbs ("they were made childless; they have destroyed My people because of their wickedness"), differing from the Masoretic Text ("with a winnowing fork... I have bereaved... I have destroyed").
- **15:11** — The Greek frames this verse as Jeremiah's protestation to the Master ("May it be so, O Master, while they prosper..."), whereas the Masoretic Text presents a divine assurance to the prophet.
- **15:16** — Continuing the sentence from verse 15, the Septuagint reads "from those who reject Your words; make an end of them," differing notably from the Masoretic Text ("Your words were found, and I ate them").
- **15:19** — The Greek pairs "precious" (timion) with "cheap" (axiou), using axios in its attested Koine sense of "inexpensive" or "of little worth" in contrast to what is costly.
