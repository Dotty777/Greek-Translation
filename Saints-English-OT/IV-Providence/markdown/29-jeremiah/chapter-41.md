## Text

**41:1** The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord—and Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, and all his army, and all the land of his dominion were fighting against him, against Jerusalem and against all the cities of Judah—saying:

**41:2** "Thus says the Lord: Go to Zedekiah king of Judah, and you shall say to him, 'Thus says the Lord: This city shall surely be delivered into the hands of the king of Babylon, and he shall capture it and burn it with fire,

**41:3** and you shall by no means escape from his hand, and you shall surely be captured and given into his hands, and your eyes shall see his eyes, and you shall enter into Babylon.

**41:4** Yet hear the word of the Lord, O Zedekiah king of Judah! Thus says the Lord:

**41:5** In peace you shall die; and as they wept for your fathers who reigned before you, they shall weep for you also, and even to Hades they shall lament for you; for I have spoken a word, says the Lord.'"

**41:6** And Jeremiah spoke all these words to King Zedekiah in Jerusalem.

**41:7** And the army of the king of Babylon was fighting against Jerusalem and against the cities of Judah, and against Lachish and against Azekah, for these remained among the cities of Judah as fortified cities.

**41:8** The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord after King Zedekiah had concluded a covenant with the people to proclaim release,

**41:9** that every man should send away his manservant and every man his maidservant, the Hebrew man and the Hebrew woman, free, so that no man of Judah should serve as a slave.

**41:10** And all the nobles and all the people who had entered into the covenant to send away each one his manservant and his maidservant turned back, and they forced

**41:11** them to be manservants and maidservants.

**41:12** And the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah, saying:

**41:13** "Thus says the Lord: I made a covenant with your fathers in the day that I delivered them out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage, saying,

**41:14** 'When six years are fulfilled, you shall send away your Hebrew brother who shall be sold to you and shall serve you six years, and you shall send him away free.' Yet they did not listen to Me, nor did they incline their ear.

**41:15** And they turned today to do what is right before My eyes, to proclaim release, every man for his neighbor, and they concluded a covenant before Me in the house upon which My name has been called.

**41:16** And you turned back and profaned My name in bringing back every man his manservant and every man his maidservant, whom you had sent away free according to their own will, to be manservants and maidservants to you.

**41:17** Therefore thus says the Lord: You have not listened to Me to proclaim release, every man to his neighbor. Behold, I proclaim a release for you—to the sword, and to death, and to famine—and I will give you over to dispersion among all the kingdoms of the earth.

**41:18** And I will give the men who have transgressed My covenant, who have not upheld My covenant which they made before Me, the calf which they made to serve it,

**41:19** the princes of Judah and the rulers and the priests and the people,

**41:20** and I will give them to their enemies, and their carcasses shall be food for the birds of the heaven and for the beasts of the earth.

**41:21** And Zedekiah king of Judea and their princes I will give into the hands of their enemies, and the army of the king of Babylon is for those who flee from them.

**41:22** Behold, I command," says the Lord, "and I will bring them back to this land, and they shall fight against it, and take it, and burn it up with fire, and the cities of Judah, and I will make them desolate, without inhabitants."

## Translator Notes

- **41:5** — The Septuagint reads "wept / shall weep" and "even to Hades they shall lament for you," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's reference to burning spices and crying "Alas, lord!"
- **41:10** — The Septuagint condenses verses 10–11 into a single sentence split across the verse boundary; the verb rendered "forced" (ἐῶσαν) reflects an aorist form of ὠθέω ("compel/thrust"), though it may also be read as a Koine form of ἐάω ("left/kept").
- **41:14** — The Septuagint specifies "six years" rather than the seven years found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **41:18** — The Septuagint reads "the calf which they made to serve it," differing markedly from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's reference to cutting the calf in two and passing between its parts.
- **41:21** — The final clause is elliptical in Greek ("and the army of the king of Babylon for those who flee from them"), reflecting a difficult rendering of the Hebrew reference to the retreating Babylonian army.
