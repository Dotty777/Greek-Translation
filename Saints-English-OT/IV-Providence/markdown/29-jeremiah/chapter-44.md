## Text

**44:1** Now Zedekiah the son of Josiah reigned in place of Jehoiakim, whom Nebuchadnezzar made king to reign over Judah.

**44:2** But neither he, nor his servants, nor the people of the land listened to the words of the Lord, which He spoke by the hand of Jeremiah.

**44:3** And King Zedekiah sent Jehucal the son of Shelemiah and Zephaniah the son of Maaseiah the priest to Jeremiah, saying, "Pray now for us to the Lord."

**44:4** Now Jeremiah came and went through the midst of the city, and they had not put him into the prison house.

**44:5** And the army of Pharaoh went out from Egypt; and the Chaldeans heard the report of them, and went up against Jerusalem.

**44:6** Then the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah, saying,

**44:7** "Thus says the Lord: Thus you shall say to the king of Judah, who sent to you to inquire of Me, 'Behold, the army of Pharaoh, which has come out to help you, shall return to the land of Egypt,

**44:8** and the Chaldeans themselves shall come back and fight against this city, and they shall capture it and burn it with fire.

**44:9** For thus says the Lord: Do not suppose in your souls, saying, "The Chaldeans will surely depart from us," for they shall by no means depart.

**44:10** And even if you strike down the whole army of the Chaldeans who fight against you, and there remain some who are pierced through, each one in his place, these shall rise up and burn this city with fire.'"

**44:11** And it came to pass, when the army of the Chaldeans went up from Jerusalem from before the army of Pharaoh,

**44:12** that Jeremiah went out from Jerusalem to go into the land of Benjamin, to buy from there in the midst of the people.

**44:13** And he was at the Gate of Benjamin, and there was a man with whom he lodged, Saruia the son of Shelemiah, the son of Hananiah; and he seized Jeremiah, saying, "Are you fleeing to the Chaldeans?"

**44:14** And he said, "Falsehood! I am not fleeing to the Chaldeans." But he did not listen to him; and Saruia seized Jeremiah and brought him to the princes.

**44:15** And the princes were embittered against Jeremiah and struck him, and they sent him into the house of Jonathan the scribe, for they had made that into a prison house.

**44:16** So Jeremiah went into the house of the pit and into the Chereth, and he remained there many days.

**44:17** Then Zedekiah sent and called him, and the king asked him secretly to tell him, "Is there a word from the Lord?" And he said, "There is; into the hands of the king of Babylon you shall be delivered."

**44:18** Moreover Jeremiah said to the king, "What wrong have I done to you, and to your servants, and to this people, that you put me into a prison house?

**44:19** And where are your prophets who prophesied to you, saying, 'The king of Babylon shall by no means come against this land'?

**44:20** And now, O lord the king, let my plea for mercy fall before your face; and why do you send me back to the house of Jonathan the scribe, that I may not die there?"

**44:21** Then the king gave command, and they cast him into the prison house, and they gave him one loaf of bread each day from outside where they bake, until the loaves failed from the city; and Jeremiah remained in the court of the prison.

## Translator Notes

- **44:5** — The Greek text literally reads "went up against Jerusalem," differing from verse 11 and the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Jer 37:5), which state that the Chaldeans departed from Jerusalem.
- **44:12** — The Greek text reads "to buy from there," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Jer 37:12), which refers to receiving a portion among the people.
- **44:13** — The Greek text identifies the man as "a man with whom he lodged" and names him "Saruia," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Jer 37:13), which refers to "a captain of the guard" named "Irijah."
- **44:16** — "Chereth" transliterates Greek chereth, which renders a Hebrew term for vaulted dungeon cells.
- **44:20** — Greek literally reads "let my mercy fall before your face" (an idiom for a plea for mercy, as in 43:7) and phrases the following clause as a question ("why do you send me back...").
