## Text

**18:1** The word that came from the Lord to Jeremiah, saying,

**18:2** "Arise and go down to the house of the potter, and there you shall hear My words."

**18:3** So I went down to the house of the potter, and behold, he was making a work upon the stones,

**18:4** and the vessel that he was making fell in his hands; and again he made it another vessel, just as it seemed pleasing before him to make.

**18:5** Then the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**18:6** "Shall I not be able to deal with you just as this potter does, O house of Israel? Behold, as the clay of the potter, you are in My hands.

**18:7** In the end I will speak concerning a nation or concerning a kingdom, to pluck them up and to destroy,

**18:8** and if that nation turns back from all their evils, then I will relent concerning the evils that I purposed to do to them.

**18:9** And in the end I will speak concerning a nation and a kingdom, to be built up and to be planted,

**18:10** and if they do wicked things before Me, so as not to listen to My voice, then I will relent concerning the good things that I spoke of doing for them.

**18:11** And now speak to the men of Judah and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, 'Behold, I am fashioning evils against you, and I am devising a plan against you; let each one turn away now from his wicked way, and you shall make your practices better.'"

**18:12** And they said, "We will act courageously, for we will walk after our turnings away, and we will each do the things pleasing to his wicked heart."

**18:13** Therefore thus says the Lord: "Inquire now among the nations, 'Who has heard such horrifying things that the virgin Israel has exceedingly done?'

**18:14** Will breasts fail from a rock, or snow from Lebanon? Will water violently carried by the wind turn aside?

**18:15** For My people have forgotten Me; they have burned incense in vain, and they shall falter in their ways, the ancient tracks, so as to tread paths that have no way for traveling,

**18:16** to appoint their land for desolation and a perpetual hissing; all who pass through it shall be astonished and shall shake their head.

**18:17** Like a scorching wind I will scatter them before the face of their enemies; I will show them the day of their destruction."

**18:18** And they said, "Come, let us devise a plan against Jeremiah, for the law shall not perish from the priest, nor counsel from the discerning, nor the word from the prophet. Come, and let us strike him with the tongue, and we will listen to all his words."

**18:19** Listen to me, O Lord, and hear the voice of my plea.

**18:20** Shall evil things be repaid in return for good things? For they have spoken words together against my soul, and their punishment they have hidden for me. Remember that I stood before Your face to speak good things on their behalf, to turn away Your wrath from them.

**18:21** Therefore give their sons over to famine, and gather them into the hands of the sword; let their wives become childless and widows, and let their husbands be slain by death, and their young men fallen by the sword in battle.

**18:22** Let an outcry arise in their houses; You shall bring robbers upon them suddenly, because they undertook a word for my capture and hid snares against me.

**18:23** And You, O Lord, have known all their counsel against me for death; do not acquit their iniquities, and do not blot out their sins from before Your face. Let their weakness be before You; in the time of Your wrath, deal with them.

## Translator Notes

- **18:4** — The Greek reads "fell" (epesen) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "was spoiled/marred," and in verse 3 renders the potter's wheel literally as "the stones" (ton lithon).
- **18:7** — Greek peras ("in the end" or "finally") renders Hebrew rega' ("at one instant" or "suddenly"); the same term recurs in verse 9.
- **18:12** — Greek andrioumetha ("we will act courageously" or "we will be resolute") stands where the Hebrew reads no'ash ("it is hopeless").
- **18:14** — Greek mastoi ("breasts," possibly referring to breast-shaped crags or peaks) reflects reading Hebrew saday ("field") as shaday ("breasts").
- **18:18** — Unlike the Hebrew ("let us not pay heed to any of his words"), the Greek omits the negative, depicting Jeremiah's opponents as listening intently to trap him.
