## Text

**20:1** Now Pashhur the son of Immer, the priest—and he had been appointed overseer of the house of the Lord—heard Jeremiah prophesying these words.

**20:2** And he struck him and cast him into the dungeon that was in the gate of the upper house set apart, which was in the house of the Lord.

**20:3** And Pashhur brought Jeremiah out of the dungeon, and Jeremiah said to him, "The Lord has not called your name Pashhur, but rather Exile.

**20:4** For thus says the Lord, 'Behold, I am giving you over to exile with all your friends; and they shall fall by the sword of their enemies, and your eyes shall see it; and I will give you and all Judah into the hands of the king of Babylon, and they shall carry them into exile and cut them down with swords.

**20:5** And I will give all the strength of this city, and all its labors, and all the treasures of the king of Judah into the hands of his enemies, and they shall lead them away to Babylon.

**20:6** And you, and all who dwell in your house, shall go into captivity; and in Babylon you shall die, and there you shall be buried—you and all your friends to whom you have prophesied falsehoods.'"

**20:7** You have deceived me, O Lord, and I was deceived; You have been strong and have prevailed. I have become a laughingstock; all the day long I have continued to be mocked.

**20:8** For with my bitter word I shall laugh; treachery and misery I shall call upon, because the word of the Lord has become a reproach to me and a derision all my day.

**20:9** And I said, "I will by no means mention the name of the Lord, nor will I speak anymore in His name." Yet it became like a burning fire blazing in my bones, and I am exhausted on every side and cannot bear it;

**20:10** for I have heard the censure of many gathering all around, all around: "Conspire, and let us conspire against him, all men who are his friends! Watch his design, whether he may be deceived, and we shall prevail against him and take our vengeance from him."

**20:11** But the Lord is with me like a mighty warrior; therefore they pursued and could not understand. They were greatly shamed, because they did not understand their disgraces, which shall not be forgotten forever.

**20:12** O Lord, who tests righteous things, who understands inward parts and hearts, may I see the vengeance from You upon them, for to You I have revealed my pleas.

**20:13** Sing to the Lord, praise Him, for He has delivered the soul of the poor from the hand of evildoers.

**20:14** Cursed be the day on which I was born! The day on which my mother bore me—let it not be blessed!

**20:15** Cursed be the man who brought good tidings to my father, saying, "A male child has been born to you," rejoicing.

**20:16** Let that man be like the cities that the Lord overthrew in wrath and did not relent; let him hear an outcry in the morning and a battle shout at midday,

**20:17** because he did not slay me in the womb, so that my mother might have become my grave, and her womb one of everlasting conception.

**20:18** Why is this that I came forth from the womb to see toils and sorrows, and my days have ended in shame?

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — Greek katarakten ("dungeon" or "trapdoor pit") stands where the Hebrew reads "stocks," and "the upper house set apart" appears instead of the Hebrew "Upper Gate of Benjamin."
- **20:3** — Greek Metoikos ("Exile" or "Migrant") renders the symbolic name given to Pashhur (Hebrew Magor-Missabib, "Terror on Every Side"), linking directly to the exile announced in verse 4.
- **20:8** — Greek "with my bitter word I shall laugh" diverges from the Hebrew ("whenever I speak, I cry out"), continuing the motif of mockery from verse 7.
- **20:11** — Greek noesai / enoesan ("understand") appears twice where the Hebrew reads "stumble" and "prosper," framing the enemies' defeat as a failure of comprehension.
