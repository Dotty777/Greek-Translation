## Text

**46:1** And it came to pass in the ninth month of Zedekiah king of Judah, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and all his army came against Jerusalem, and they besieged it;

**46:2** and in the eleventh year of Zedekiah, in the fourth month, on the ninth day of the month, the city was breached;

**46:3** and all the leaders of the king of Babylon entered and sat in the Middle Gate: Marganasar, and Samagoth, and Nabusachar, and Nabusareis, Nagargasnaser, Rabamath, and the rest of the commanders of the king of Babylon.

**46:14** And they sent and took Jeremiah from the court of the guard, and delivered him to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, the son of Shaphan; and they brought him out, and he dwelt in the midst of the people.

**46:15** And the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah in the court of the guard, saying,

**46:16** "Go and speak to Ebed-Melech the Ethiopian, 'Thus says the Lord God of Israel: Behold, I am bringing My words upon this city for evil and not for good,

**46:17** and I will save you in that day, and I will by no means give you into the hands of the men before whose face you are afraid.

**46:18** For I will surely save you, and you shall by no means fall by the sword; and your life shall be for a prize, because you have trusted in Me, says the Lord.'"

## Translator Notes

- **46:1** — "in the ninth month of Zedekiah" follows the Greek text directly, where the Hebrew text specifies "in the ninth year of Zedekiah, in the tenth month."
- **46:3** — The Greek preserves distinct forms for the Babylonian officials' names and titles (Marganasar, Samagoth, Nabusachar, Nabusareis, Nagargasnaser, Rabamath). In the Septuagint, verse 14 follows immediately after verse 3, without verses 4-13 of the Hebrew text (Jeremiah 39:4-13).
- **46:18** — "for a prize" translates Greek heurema ("a find" or "windfall"), rendering the Hebrew idiom "as spoil," signifying that his life would be spared.
