## Text

**24:1** The Lord showed me two baskets of figs set before the temple of the Lord, after Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried into exile Jeconiah the son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, and the rulers, and the craftsmen, and the prisoners, and the neighbors from Jerusalem, and had brought them to Babylon.

**24:2** One basket had very good figs, like the early figs; and the other basket had very bad figs, which cannot be eaten because of their badness.

**24:3** Then the Lord said to me, "What do you see, Jeremiah?" And I said, "Figs: the good ones, exceedingly good, and the bad ones, exceedingly bad, which cannot be eaten because of their badness."

**24:4** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**24:5** Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel: "Like these good figs, so will I acknowledge the exiled Judeans whom I have sent out from this place into the land of the Chaldeans for good.

**24:6** And I will fix My eyes upon them for good, and I will restore them to this land for good; and I will build them up and will by no means tear them down, and I will plant them and will by no means pluck them up.

**24:7** And I will give them a heart to know Me, that I am the Lord; and they shall be My people, and I will be their God, for they shall return to Me with all their heart.

**24:8** And like the bad figs which cannot be eaten because of their badness"—thus says the Lord—"so will I hand over Zedekiah king of Judah, and his nobles, and the remnant of Jerusalem, those who remain in this land and those who dwell in Egypt.

**24:9** And I will deliver them over to dispersion into all the kingdoms of the earth, and to reproach, and to a byword, and to hatred, and to a curse in every place where I have driven them;

**24:10** and I will send among them famine, and death, and the sword, until they perish from the land which I gave to them."

## Translator Notes

- **24:1** — The Greek text reads τοὺς πλησίους ("the neighbors"), a variant alongside τοὺς πλουσίους ("the rich") in some Septuagint manuscripts, and renders the group known in the Hebrew text as "smiths" as δεσμώτας ("prisoners").
- **24:9** — The Greek reads διασκορπισμόν ("dispersion") and μῖσος ("hatred"), differing from the Masoretic readings "horror" and "taunt."
