## Text

**30:1** Concerning Idumea. Thus says the Lord: "There is no longer wisdom in Teman; counsel has perished from the prudent; their wisdom has vanished,

**30:2** their place has been deceived. Go deep to dwell, O inhabitants of Dedan, for he has done grievous things; I have brought them upon him in the time when I visited him.

**30:3** For grape gatherers have come who shall not leave you a remnant; like thieves in the night they shall put forth their hand.

**30:4** For I have stripped Esau bare; I have uncovered their hidden places, and they shall by no means be able to hide themselves. They have perished by the hand of his brother, My neighbor, and there is no one

**30:5** to leave your fatherless child behind that he may live; yet I shall live, and widows have trusted in Me."

**30:6** For thus says the Lord: "Those for whom there was no law to drink the cup have drunk it; and you shall by no means be held guiltless,

**30:7** for I have sworn by Myself," says the Lord, "that you shall become an untrodden waste, and a reproach, and a curse in the midst of her, and all her cities shall be desolate forever."

**30:8** I have heard a report from the Lord, and He has sent messengers to the nations: "Gather together and come against her; rise up to battle!"

**30:9** "I have made you small among the nations, despised among men.

**30:10** Your mockery has set upon you; the audacity of your heart has taken up lodging in the clefts of the rocks and laid hold of the strength of a lofty hill. Because he has exalted his nest like an eagle, from there I will bring you down."

**30:11** And Idumea shall become an untrodden waste; everyone who passes by her shall hiss.

**30:12** "As Sodom and Gomorrah and her neighboring cities were overthrown," says the Lord Almighty, "no man shall settle there, nor shall a son of man dwell there.

**30:13** Behold, like a lion he shall come up from the midst of the Jordan to the place of Etham, for I will swiftly drive them out from her; and appoint the young men over her! For who is like Me? And who shall withstand Me? And who is this shepherd who shall stand before My face?"

**30:14** Therefore hear the counsel of the Lord which He has counseled against Idumea, and His design which He has devised against those who dwell in Teman: "Surely the least of the sheep shall be swept away; surely their habitation shall be made an untrodden waste against her."

**30:15** For at the sound of their fall the earth was afraid, and the cry of the sea was not heard.

**30:16** Behold, like an eagle he shall look down and spread out his wings over her strongholds; and the heart of the mighty men of Idumea in that day shall be like the heart of a woman in labor.

**30:17** Concerning the sons of Ammon. Thus says the Lord: "Are there no sons in Israel, or is there no heir for them? Why has Melchol taken possession of Gilead, and why shall their people dwell in their cities?

**30:18** Therefore, behold, the days are coming," says the Lord, "when I will cause the tumult of wars to be heard against Rabbah; and they shall become an untrodden waste and a ruin, and her altars shall be burned up with fire, and Israel shall take possession of his dominion.

**30:19** Wail, O Heshbon, for she has perished! Cry out, O daughters of Rabbah; gird yourselves with sackcloth and lament, for Melchol shall go into captivity, his priests and his rulers together.

**30:20** Why do you exult in the plains of the Anakim, O daughter of audacity, who trusts in her treasures, who says, 'Who shall come in against me?'

**30:21** Behold, I am bringing terror upon you," says the Lord, "from all the region round about you; and you shall be scattered, each man before his face, and there is no one who gathers."

**30:23** Concerning Kedar, queen of the court, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon struck. Thus says the Lord: "Arise and go up against Kedar, and fill the sons of Kedem.

**30:24** Their tents and their sheep they shall take; their garments and all their vessels and their camels they shall take for themselves; and call out destruction upon them from every side.

**30:25** Flee! You have gone very deep to dwell, you who sit in the court, for the king of Babylon has counseled a counsel against you and has devised a plan.

**30:26** Arise and go up against a tranquil nation, dwelling at ease, that has no doors, no bolts, no bars; they dwell alone.

**30:27** And their camels shall become plunder, and the multitude of their livestock destruction; and I will winnow them to every wind, those who are shorn before their face; from every region beyond them I will bring their rout," says the Lord.

**30:28** "And the court shall become a haunt of ostriches and an untrodden waste forever; no man shall dwell there, and no son of man shall inhabit it."

**30:29** Concerning Damascus. Themar and Arpad are put to shame, for they have heard an evil report; they are dismayed, they are enraged; they shall by no means be able to rest.

**30:30** Damascus has grown faint; she has turned to flee; trembling has seized her.

**30:31** "How has she not forsaken My city? They have loved a village.

**30:32** Therefore young men shall fall in your broad streets, and all your men of war shall fall," says the Lord,

**30:33** "and I will kindle a fire in the wall of Damascus, and it shall devour the quarters of the son of Hader."

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — Greek Idoumaia ("Idumea") corresponds to Hebrew Edom; throughout 30:1-16 the Septuagint preserves distinctive readings such as "their place has been deceived" (30:2), "by the hand of his brother, My neighbor" (30:4), "yet I shall live" (30:5), "in the midst of her" instead of Bozrah (30:7), "Etham" as a place name (30:13), and "the cry of the sea was not heard" (30:15).
- **30:17** — The Septuagint names the Ammonite deity "Melchol" (Hebrew Milcom/Malcam) and reads "Gilead" where the Masoretic Text has "Gad"; in 30:18 it has "her altars" instead of "her daughters/villages," and in 30:20 "the plains of the Anakim."
- **30:23** — The Septuagint renders Hebrew "kingdoms of Hazor" as "queen of the court" (continuing "the court" in 30:25, 28) and has plesate ("fill") where the Hebrew has "plunder" (some view plesate as an early Greek scribal variant for plexate, "strike"). "Kedem" transliterates the Hebrew name meaning "the East."
- **30:27** — The Greek manuscript reads kekramenous (literally "mingled"), an early scribal metathesis for kekarmenous ("shorn/clipped"), referring to desert tribes who clipped the hair on their temples.
- **30:29** — The Septuagint reads "Themar" where the Masoretic Text has "Hamath," and in 30:31-33 reads "They have loved a village" and "the quarters of the son of Hader" (corresponding to Hebrew "the palaces of Ben-Hadad").
