## Text

**1:1** In the eighth month of the second year under Darius, the word of the Lord came to Zechariah the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo the prophet, saying,

**1:2** "The Lord was angry with your fathers with great wrath.

**1:3** And you shall say to them, 'Thus says the Lord Almighty: Return to Me, says the Lord of hosts, and I will return to you, says the Lord of hosts.

**1:4** And do not become like your fathers, to whom the former prophets called out, saying, "Thus says the Lord Almighty: Turn away from your evil ways and from your evil practices." Yet they did not listen, and they did not give heed to listen to Me, says the Lord.

**1:5** Your fathers, where are they? And the prophets, shall they live forever?

**1:6** Nevertheless, receive My words and My statutes, all that I command by My Spirit to My servants the prophets, who overtook your fathers. And they answered and said, "Just as the Lord Almighty has purposed to do to us according to our ways and according to our practices, so He has done to us."'"

**1:7** On the twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month, which is the month of Shebat, in the second year under Darius, the word of the Lord came to Zechariah the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo the prophet, saying:

**1:8** I saw by night, and behold, a man mounted upon a red horse, and he stood between the shady mountains, and behind him were red, speckled gray, spotted, and white horses.

**1:9** And I said, "What are these, my lord?" And the angel who spoke within me said to me, "I will show you what these are."

**1:10** And the man who stood between the mountains answered and said to me, "These are those whom the Lord has sent out to patrol the earth."

**1:11** And they answered the angel of the Lord who stood between the mountains and said, "We have patrolled all the earth, and behold, all the earth is inhabited and is at rest."

**1:12** And the angel of the Lord answered and said, "O Lord Almighty, how long will You not have mercy on Jerusalem and the cities of Judah, which You have overlooked this seventieth year?"

**1:13** And the Lord Almighty answered the angel who spoke within me with good words and words of comfort.

**1:14** And the angel who spoke within me said to me, "Cry out, saying, 'Thus says the Lord Almighty: I am jealous for Jerusalem and for Zion with great jealousy,

**1:15** and with great wrath I am angry against the nations that join in attacking; because while I was angry a little, they joined in for evil.

**1:16** Therefore thus says the Lord: I will look upon Jerusalem with compassion, and My house shall be rebuilt in her, says the Lord Almighty, and a measure shall yet be stretched out over Jerusalem.'"

**1:17** And the angel who spoke within me said to me, "Cry out, saying, 'Thus says the Lord Almighty: Cities shall yet overflow with good things, and the Lord shall yet have mercy on Zion, and shall choose Jerusalem.'"

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — The Greek alternates between two divine titles here: Kyrios Pantokrator ("the Lord Almighty") and Kyrios ton dynameon ("the Lord of hosts"), both rendering Hebrew Yahweh Sebaoth.
- **1:6** — Unlike the Masoretic Text's rhetorical question, the Septuagint reads an imperative ("receive My words and My statutes") and specifies "by My Spirit" before "to My servants the prophets."
- **1:8** — The Greek reads "the shady mountains" (rather than the Masoretic "myrtle trees in the hollow") and lists four horse colors ("red, speckled gray, spotted, and white") instead of three.
- **1:15** — The Greek participle synepitithemena describes "the nations that join in attacking," in contrast to the Masoretic Text's "nations at ease."
