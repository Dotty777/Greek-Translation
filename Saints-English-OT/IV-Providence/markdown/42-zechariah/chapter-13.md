## Text

**13:1** In that day every place shall be opened to the house of David and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem for removal and for separation.

**13:2** "And it shall be in that day," says the Lord of hosts, "that I will utterly destroy the names of the idols from the land, and there shall no longer be any remembrance of them; and I will remove the false prophets and the unclean spirit from the land."

**13:3** And it shall come to pass, if a man still prophesies, then his father and his mother, who gave birth to him, shall say to him, "You shall not live, because you have spoken falsehoods in the name of the Lord." And his father and his mother, who gave birth to him, shall bind him hand and foot when he prophesies.

**13:4** And it shall be in that day that the prophets shall be put to shame, each one because of his vision when he prophesies, and they shall put on a garment of hairy skin because they have lied.

**13:5** And he shall say, "I am no prophet, for I am a man who tills the ground, because a man fathered me from my youth."

**13:6** And one shall say to him, "What are these wounds between your hands?" And he shall say, "Those with which I was wounded in my beloved house."

**13:7** "O sword, awake against My shepherds and against a man who is My fellow citizen," says the Lord Almighty. "Strike the shepherds, and draw out the sheep, and I will bring My hand against the little ones."

**13:8** "And it shall be in all the land," says the Lord, "that two parts of it shall be utterly destroyed and perish, but the third shall be left in it;

**13:9** and I will bring the third through fire, and refine them by fire as silver is refined, and test them as gold is tested. They shall call upon My name, and I will hear them; and I will say, 'This is My people,' and they shall say, 'The Lord is my God.'"

## Translator Notes

- **13:1** — The Septuagint reads "every place" (reflecting a reading of Hebrew maqom rather than maqor, "fountain") and "for removal and for separation," where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "a fountain... for sin and for uncleanness."
- **13:3** — The Greek verb sympodizo means "to bind hand and foot" (or tie the feet together), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "thrust through" or "pierce."
- **13:4** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("they shall not wear a hairy mantle to deceive"), the Greek omits the negative and states that the shamed prophets "shall put on a garment of hairy skin because they have lied."
- **13:7** — The Septuagint reads plural "My shepherds" and active plural imperatives ("Strike the shepherds, and draw out the sheep"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has a singular shepherd and a passive verb ("the sheep shall be scattered").
