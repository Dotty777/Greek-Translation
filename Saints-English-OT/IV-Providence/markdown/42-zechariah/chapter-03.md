## Text

**3:1** And the Lord showed me Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the Lord, and the devil stood at his right hand to oppose him.

**3:2** And the Lord said to the devil, "The Lord rebuke you, O devil, and the Lord who has chosen Jerusalem rebuke you! Behold, is this not like a brand plucked from the fire?"

**3:3** And Joshua was clothed with filthy garments, and stood before the angel.

**3:4** And he answered and spoke to those who stood before him, saying, "Take away the filthy garments from him." And he said to him, "Behold, I have taken away your iniquities; and clothe him with a full-length robe,

**3:5** and set a clean turban upon his head." And they set a clean turban upon his head and clothed him with garments; and the angel of the Lord stood by.

**3:6** And the angel of the Lord solemnly testified to Joshua, saying,

**3:7** "Thus says the Lord Almighty: You walk in My ways and shall keep watch in My ordinances, and you shall judge My house; and if you diligently guard My court, then I will give you those who walk about in the midst of these who stand here.

**3:8** Hear now, O Joshua the high priest, you and your companions who sit before you, for they are men who observe signs; for behold, I am bringing My servant, the Dayspring.

**3:9** For the stone that I have set before Joshua—upon the one stone are seven eyes. Behold, I am digging a pit, says the Lord Almighty, and I will feel out all the unrighteousness of that land in one day.

**3:10** In that day, says the Lord Almighty, you shall each invite his neighbor under a vine and under a fig tree."

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Greek uses Iesous (rendered with the standard English Old Testament form "Joshua") and translates the adversary's title as ho diabolos ("the devil").
- **3:4** — The Septuagint continues the command to the attendants ("clothe him with a full-length robe [poderes], and set a clean turban upon his head") directly from verse 4 into verse 5 without the Masoretic "And I said."
- **3:7** — This Greek text omits a conditional "if" before the first clause (while retaining "if" before "you diligently guard My court") and renders the final promise as masculine plural anastrephomenous ("those who walk about").
- **3:8** — The Greek describes Joshua's companions as andres teratoskopoi ("men who observe signs") and renders the messianic title as Anatole ("the Dayspring" or "Rising," corresponding to Hebrew "Branch").
- **3:9** — The Septuagint reads "I am digging a pit" and "I will feel out all the unrighteousness," diverging sharply from the Masoretic "I will engrave its inscription" and "I will remove the iniquity."
