## Text

**4:1** And the angel who spoke in me returned and roused me, just as when a man is awakened out of his sleep.

**4:2** And he said to me, "What do you see?" And I said, "I have seen, and behold, a lampstand all of gold, and the lamp bowl upon it, and seven lamps upon it, and seven funnels for the lamps that are upon it,

**4:3** and two olive trees above it, one on the right of its lamp bowl and one on the left."

**4:4** And I inquired and spoke to the angel who spoke in me, saying, "What are these things, lord?"

**4:5** And the angel who spoke in me answered and spoke to me, saying, "Do you not know what these things are?" And I said, "No, lord."

**4:6** And he answered and spoke to me, saying, "This is the word of the Lord to Zerubbabel, saying, 'Not by great might, nor by strength, but by My Spirit,' says the Lord Almighty.

**4:7** 'Who are you, O great mountain before the face of Zerubbabel, to prosper? And I will bring forth the stone of the inheritance, an equality of grace, even grace for it.'"

**4:8** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**4:9** "The hands of Zerubbabel laid the foundation of this house, and his hands shall complete it; and you shall know that the Lord Almighty has sent me to you.

**4:10** For who has despised small days? And they shall rejoice and see the stone of tin in the hand of Zerubbabel; these are seven eyes that look upon all the earth."

**4:11** And I answered and said to him, "What are these two olive trees on the right of the lampstand and on the left?"

**4:12** And I inquired a second time and said to him, "What are the two branches of the olive trees that are in the hands of the two golden nozzles, which pour into and supply the golden funnels?"

**4:13** And he said to me, "Do you not know what these things are?" And I said, "No, lord."

**4:14** And he said, "These two sons of fatness stand before the Lord of all the earth."

## Translator Notes

- **4:7** — The Septuagint differs notably from the Hebrew Masoretic Text here, rendering the Hebrew 'into a plain' as the infinitive 'to prosper,' shifting to the first person 'I will bring forth,' and reading 'the stone of the inheritance, an equality of grace' for 'the capstone with shouts of grace.'
- **4:10** — The Greek literally reads 'stone of tin' (for the plumb weight) and speaks simply of 'seven eyes,' without the phrase 'of the Lord' found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **4:14** — 'Sons of fatness' (Greek huioi tes piotetos) is a literal rendering of the Hebrew idiom for those supplied with rich olive oil, signifying the two anointed ones.
