## Text

**5:1** And I turned and lifted up my eyes and looked, and behold, a flying sickle.

**5:2** And he said to me, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a flying sickle, twenty cubits in length and ten cubits in width."

**5:3** And he said to me, "This is the curse that goes forth over the face of all the earth; for every thief shall be punished by this even to death, and every perjurer shall be punished by this.

**5:4** 'And I will bring it forth,' says the Lord Almighty, 'and it shall enter into the house of the thief and into the house of the one who swears by My name falsely; and it shall lodge in the midst of his house, and it shall consume it, along with its timber and its stones.'"

**5:5** And the angel who spoke in me came forth and said to me, "Look up with your eyes and see this thing that goes forth."

**5:6** And I said, "What is it?" And he said, "This is the measure that goes forth." And he said, "This is their iniquity in all the earth."

**5:7** And behold, a talent of lead was lifted up; and behold, one woman was sitting in the midst of the measure.

**5:8** And he said, "This is lawlessness." And he cast her into the midst of the measure, and he cast the stone of lead into her mouth.

**5:9** And I lifted up my eyes and looked, and behold, two women were coming forth, and wind was in their wings; and they had wings of a hoopoe, and they took up the measure between the earth and the heaven.

**5:10** And I said to the angel who spoke in me, "Where are they carrying away the measure?"

**5:11** And he said to me, "To build a house for it in the land of Babylon and to make preparation; and they shall set it there upon its prepared base."

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — The Septuagint reads 'a flying sickle' (drepanon), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonants that the Masoretic Text reads as 'a flying scroll.'
- **5:6** — The Greek renders the Hebrew dry measure ephah as 'measure' and reads 'their iniquity' (he adikia auton) where the Masoretic Text has 'their eye' or 'their appearance.'
- **5:8** — Because Greek metron ('measure') is neuter while the Hebrew ephah is feminine, the Greek feminine pronoun in 'into her mouth' (eis to stoma autes) grammatically aligns with the woman representing lawlessness.
- **5:9** — The Septuagint identifies the bird as a hoopoe (epopos), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'stork.'
