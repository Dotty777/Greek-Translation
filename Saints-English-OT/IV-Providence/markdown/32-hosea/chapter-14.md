## Text

**14:1** Samaria shall be laid waste, because she has stood against her God; they themselves shall fall by the sword, and their nursing infants shall be dashed to the ground, and their women with child shall be ripped open.

**14:2** Return, O Israel, to the Lord your God, for you have become weak in your iniquities.

**14:3** Take words with you and return to the Lord your God; speak to Him, so that you may not receive iniquity, and that you may receive good things, and we will render the fruit of our lips.

**14:4** Assyria shall by no means save us; we will not mount upon a horse; never again will we say, "Our gods," to the works of our hands; he who is in You shall have mercy on the orphan.

**14:5** I will heal their dwellings; I will love them openly, for he has turned away My anger from him.

**14:6** I will be like dew to Israel; he shall blossom like a lily, and cast forth his roots like Lebanon;

**14:7** his branches shall go forth, and he shall be like a fruitful olive tree, and his fragrance like that of Lebanon.

**14:8** They shall return and dwell under his shelter; they shall live and be intoxicated with grain; and a vine shall blossom as his memorial, like the wine of Lebanon.

**14:9** As for Ephraim, what has he any longer to do with idols? I have humbled him, and I will strengthen him; I am like a dense juniper tree; from Me your fruit is found.

**14:10** Who is wise, and shall understand these things? Or discerning, and shall recognize them? For the ways of the Lord are straight, and the righteous shall walk in them, but the ungodly shall become weak in them.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — Following Septuagint versification, chapter 14 opens with the oracle against Samaria (numbered 13:16 in the Hebrew Masoretic Text), advancing the verse numbers of the chapter by one.
- **14:3** — The Greek renders the clause as a purpose statement ("so that you may not receive iniquity") and concludes with "the fruit of our lips," a Septuagint expression echoed in Hebrews 13:15.
- **14:4** — The final clause ("he who is in You shall have mercy on the orphan") reflects the Septuagint's active verb and relative construction, portraying the one who abides in God as showing compassion.
- **14:5** — The Septuagint reads "dwellings" (katoikias) rather than the Hebrew "apostasy," and the Greek critical text uses a third-person singular verb ("for he has turned away My anger from him").
