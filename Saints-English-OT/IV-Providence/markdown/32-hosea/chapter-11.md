## Text

**11:1** At dawn they were cast off; the king of Israel has been cast off. For Israel was a child, and I loved him, and out of Egypt I called his children.

**11:2** Just as I called them, so they departed from My presence; they sacrificed to the Baals and burned incense to carved images.

**11:3** And I bound the feet of Ephraim; I took him up upon My arm; they did not know that I healed them.

**11:4** In the corruption of men I stretched them out with the bonds of My love, and I will be to them like a man striking upon his cheeks; and I will look toward him; I will prevail over him.

**11:5** Ephraim dwelt in Egypt, and Assyria itself was his king, because he was unwilling to turn back.

**11:6** And he grew weak by the sword in his cities, and ceased in his hands; and they shall eat from their own counsels.

**11:7** And his people hang suspended from his dwelling; and God shall be wrathful against his precious things, and He shall by no means exalt him.

**11:8** How shall I treat you, O Ephraim? Shall I shield you, O Israel? What shall I make of you? I will set you like Admah and like Zeboiim. My heart is turned within at once; My remorse is deeply troubled;

**11:9** I will by no means act according to the fierceness of My wrath; I will by no means abandon Ephraim to be blotted out; for I am God and not man, the Holy One in your midst, and I will not enter into a city.

**11:10** I will walk after the Lord; like a lion He shall roar, for He Himself shall bellow, and the children of the waters shall be astonished.

**11:11** They shall fly forth like a bird out of Egypt, and like a dove from the land of the Assyrians; and I will restore them to their houses, says the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — In the Septuagint, the opening clause ("At dawn they were cast off...") begins 11:1 rather than ending 10:15, and the final phrase reads "his children" rather than "My son" (as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and Matt 2:15).
- **11:3** — The Greek verb synepodisa means "I bound the feet of," reflecting a distinct interpretation of the Hebrew root for "foot" (where the Masoretic Text reads "I taught Ephraim to walk").
- **11:4** — The Greek diverges from the Hebrew Masoretic Text throughout this verse, reading "in the corruption of men," "striking upon his cheeks," and "I will prevail over him."
- **11:10** — The Greek has first-person singular "I will walk" (vs. third-person plural in Hebrew) and "children of the waters" (for Hebrew "children from the west/sea").
