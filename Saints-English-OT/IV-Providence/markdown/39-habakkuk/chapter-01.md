## Text

**1:1** The burden that Habakkuk the prophet saw.

**1:2** How long, O Lord, shall I cry out, and You will not hear? Shall I cry aloud to You while suffering wrong, and will You not save?

**1:3** Why have You shown me troubles and toils to look upon, wretchedness and ungodliness? Judgment has come against me, and the judge receives;

**1:4** therefore the law is annulled, and justice is not carried through to the end, for the ungodly oppresses the righteous; on account of this, justice shall go forth perverted.

**1:5** Look, you despisers, and gaze, and marvel at wondrous things, and vanish away; for I am performing a work in your days which you will by no means believe, even if one recounts it in full.

**1:6** For behold, I am raising up the Chaldeans, that bitter and swift nation, which marches across the breadths of the earth to inherit dwellings not its own;

**1:7** terrible and illustrious is he; from himself shall his judgment be, and his burden shall go forth from himself;

**1:8** and his horses shall leap swifter than leopards, and are fiercer than the wolves of Arabia; and his horsemen shall ride forth and rush from afar, and they shall fly as an eagle eager to devour.

**1:9** Consummation shall come upon the ungodly, who resist with their faces set opposite, and he shall gather captivity as sand.

**1:10** And he shall make sport of kings, and tyrants shall be his playthings; and he shall mock at every stronghold, and cast up a mound and seize it.

**1:11** Then he shall change his spirit, and pass through, and make atonement; this strength belongs to my God.

**1:12** Are You not from the beginning, O Lord God, my Holy One? Surely we shall by no means die. O Lord, You have appointed it for judgment; and He formed me to reprove His chastening.

**1:13** Pure is Your eye, so as not to behold evil things, nor to look upon toils of anguish; why do You look upon those who despise? Will You keep silent while the ungodly swallows up the righteous?

**1:14** And will You make men like the fish of the sea, and like the creeping things that have no ruler?

**1:15** He has drawn up consummation with a hook, and dragged him in a casting net, and gathered him in his dragnets. On account of this his heart shall be glad and rejoice;

**1:16** on account of this he shall sacrifice to his dragnet and burn incense to his casting net, because by them he has made his portion fat and his foods choice;

**1:17** therefore he shall cast his casting net, and he shall not spare to slay nations continually.

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — Greek 'and the judge receives' (καὶ ὁ κριτὴς λαμβάνει) is elliptical, implying the taking of bribes or partiality, and diverges from the Masoretic Text's reference to strife and contention.
- **1:8** — The Greek reads 'the wolves of Arabia' (τοὺς λύκους τῆς Ἀραβίας), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonants rendered 'evening wolves' in the Masoretic Text.
- **1:11** — Unlike the Masoretic Text ('and be guilty, whose own might is his god'), the Greek reads 'and make atonement; this strength belongs to my God' (καὶ ἐξιλάσεται· αὕτη ἡ ἰσχὺς τῷ θεῷ μου).
- **1:12** — The Greek shifts to the third person ('He formed me to reprove His chastening'), reading the Hebrew consonantal root for 'Rock' as the verb 'to form.'
