## Text

**2:1** Upon my watch I will stand, and I will mount upon a rock, and I will watch intently to see what He will speak in me, and what I shall answer concerning my reproof.

**2:2** And the Lord answered me and said, "Write a vision, and plainly upon a tablet, so that he who reads these things may run.

**2:3** For the vision is yet for an appointed time, and it shall spring up at the end and not in vain; if he delays, wait patiently for him, for he shall surely come, and shall by no means tarry.

**2:4** If he draws back, My soul has no pleasure in him; but the righteous shall live by My faithfulness.

**2:5** Now the arrogant man is a despiser; he shall accomplish nothing, who has enlarged his soul like Hades, and he, like death, is never satisfied; and he shall gather to himself all the nations, and shall receive to himself all the peoples.

**2:6** Shall not all these take up a proverb against him, and a riddle for a tale concerning him? And they shall say, 'Woe to him who multiplies for himself what is not his—how long?—and who heavily weighs down his collar!

**2:7** For suddenly those who bite him shall rise up, and those who plot against you shall awake, and you shall become plunder for them.

**2:8** Because you have plundered many nations, all the remaining peoples shall take plunder, because of the blood of men and the ungodliness of the land, and of the city, and of all who dwell in it.'

**2:9** Woe to him who covets an evil gain for his house, to set his nest on high, to be snatched away from the hand of evils;

**2:10** you have devised shame for your house, you have made an end of many peoples, and your soul has sinned;

**2:11** for a stone shall cry out from the wall, and the beetle from the timber shall speak of these things.

**2:12** Woe to him who builds a city with bloodshed, and establishes a city with iniquities.

**2:13** Are not these things from the Lord Almighty? And many peoples have failed in the fire, and many nations have grown fainthearted.

**2:14** For the earth shall be filled to know the glory of the Lord, as water shall cover them.

**2:15** Woe to him who gives his neighbor to drink with a turbid overturning, and makes him drunk, so that he may gaze upon their caverns.

**2:16** Drink also yourself a fullness of dishonor instead of glory; be shaken in heart and tremble; the cup of the right hand of the Lord has come around to you, and dishonor has been gathered upon your glory.

**2:17** For the ungodliness of Lebanon shall cover you, and the wretchedness of wild beasts shall terrify you, because of the blood of men and the ungodliness of the land, and of the city, and of all who dwell in it.

**2:18** What does a carved image profit, that they have carved it? He has fashioned it as a molten image, a false apparition, because he who fashioned it has trusted in his own handiwork, to make mute idols.

**2:19** Woe to him who says to the wood, 'Awake, arise!' and to the stone, 'Be exalted!' Yet it is an apparition; and this is a plating of gold and silver, and there is no breath at all in it.

**2:20** But the Lord is in His holy temple; let all the earth stand in awe before His presence."

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — The Greek shifts from the feminine noun 'vision' (ὅρασις) to the masculine pronoun 'him' (αὐτόν) and participle 'coming' (ἐρχόμενος), forming the basis for the messianic quotation in Hebrews 10:37.
- **2:4** — The Greek reads 'by My faithfulness' (ἐκ πίστεώς μου, also translatable 'by My faith') rather than the Masoretic Text's 'by his faithfulness,' and opens with 'if he draws back, My soul has no pleasure in him' (cf. Hebrews 10:38).
- **2:11** — Greek κάνθαρος literally means 'beetle' ('the beetle from the timber'), differing from the Masoretic Text's architectural 'beam' or 'rafter.'
- **2:14** — Unlike the Masoretic Text ('as the waters cover the sea'), the Greek concludes with 'as water shall cover them' (ὡς ὕδωρ κατακαλύψει αὐτούς).
- **2:15** — Greek τὰ σπήλαια αὐτῶν literally means 'their caverns' (reading the Hebrew consonants for 'nakedness' as 'caves'), and ἀνατροπῇ θολερᾷ denotes a 'turbid overturning.'
