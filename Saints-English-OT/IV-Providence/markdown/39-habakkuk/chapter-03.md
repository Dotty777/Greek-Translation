## Text

**3:1** A prayer of Habakkuk the prophet, with a song.

**3:2** O Lord, I have heard Your report, and I was afraid; I considered Your works, and I was amazed. In the midst of two living creatures You shall be known; as the years draw near, You shall be recognized; when the appointed time arrives, You shall be revealed; when my soul is troubled, in wrath You shall remember mercy.

**3:3** God shall come from Teman, and the Holy One from Mount Paran, shady and densely wooded. Interlude. His excellence covered the heavens, and the earth is full of His praise.

**3:4** And His radiance shall be as the light; horns are in His hands, and He has established a mighty love of His strength.

**3:5** Before His face a word shall go, and it shall go forth into the plains at His feet.

**3:6** He stood, and the earth was shaken; He looked, and the nations melted away; the mountains were shattered with violence, the everlasting hills melted along His everlasting ways.

**3:7** Because of troubles I saw the dwellings of the Ethiopians; the tents of the land of Midian shall also be terrified.

**3:8** Were You angry with the rivers, O Lord? Or was Your wrath against the rivers? Or Your onslaught against the sea? For You shall mount upon Your horses, and Your riding is salvation.

**3:9** Bending, You bent Your bow against scepters, says the Lord. Interlude. The earth shall be split with rivers;

**3:10** peoples shall see You and be in travail. Scattering the waters of the course, the abyss uttered its voice, the height of its apparition.

**3:11** The sun was lifted up, and the moon stood in its order; into the light Your darts shall go forth, at the radiance of the lightning of Your weapons.

**3:12** With threatening You shall diminish the earth, and in wrath You shall bring down the nations;

**3:13** You went forth for the salvation of Your people, to save Your anointed one; You shall cast death upon the heads of the lawless; You have raised up bonds even to the neck. Interlude.

**3:14** You cut asunder in amazement the heads of the mighty; they shall be shaken in it; they shall open their bridles as a poor man eating in secret.

**3:15** And You mount Your horses into the sea, troubling much water.

**3:16** I kept watch, and my belly was terrified at the voice of the prayer of my lips, and trembling entered into my bones, and beneath me my frame was troubled. I shall rest in the day of affliction, to go up to the people of my sojourning.

**3:17** For the fig tree shall not bear fruit, and there shall be no produce on the vines; the labor of the olive tree shall fail, and the plains shall yield no food; the sheep have failed from food, and there are no oxen at the mangers.

**3:18** Yet I will exult in the Lord; I will rejoice in God my Savior.

**3:19** The Lord God is my strength, and He shall set my feet to perfection; He causes me to mount upon the high places, to conquer in His song.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — Renowned in early Christian exegesis, the Greek reads 'in the midst of two living creatures You shall be known' (ἐν μέσῳ δύο ζῴων γνωσθήσῃ), differing from the Masoretic Text's 'in the midst of the years revive it.'
- **3:3** — Greek διάψαλμα ('Interlude,' also in 3:9 and 3:13) is the Septuagint musical direction corresponding to Hebrew Selah.
- **3:5** — The Greek reads 'a word' (λόγος), vocalizing the Hebrew consonantal text for 'pestilence' as 'word.'
- **3:7** — Greek Αἰθιόπων ('Ethiopians') is the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew proper name Cushan.
- **3:19** — The Greek reads 'to perfection' (εἰς συντέλειαν) rather than the Masoretic Text's 'like deer's feet,' and incorporates the concluding Hebrew musical notation into the prayer as 'to conquer in His song' (τοῦ νικῆσαι ἐν τῇ ᾠδῇ αὐτοῦ).
