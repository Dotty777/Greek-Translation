## Text

**4:4** I, Nebuchadnezzar, was at ease in my house and flourishing.

**4:5** I saw a dream and it filled me with dread, and I was troubled upon my bed, and the visions of my head greatly disturbed me.

**4:10** Upon my bed I was looking, and behold, a tree in the midst of the earth, and its height was great.

**4:11** The tree grew large and became strong, and its height reached to heaven, and its expanse to the end of all the earth.

**4:12** Its leaves were fair and its fruit was abundant, and in it was food for all; and beneath it the wild beasts took shelter, and in its branches the birds of heaven dwelt, and from it all flesh was nourished.

**4:13** I was looking in a vision of the night upon my bed, and behold, a watcher and a holy one came down from heaven.

**4:14** And he cried out with might and spoke thus: "Cut down the tree, and tear off its branches, and shake off its leaves, and scatter its fruit; let the beasts be driven from beneath it, and the birds from its branches.

**4:15** Nevertheless, leave the stump of its roots in the earth, and in a band of iron and bronze, and in the green grass outside; and in the dew of heaven he shall make his bed, and with the beasts shall be his portion in the grass of the earth.

**4:16** His heart shall be changed from that of men, and the heart of a beast shall be given to him, and seven times shall pass over him.

**4:17** By the decree of the watchers is this word, and the demand is the utterance of the holy ones, so that the living may know that the Most High is Lord of the kingdom of men, and to whomever He pleases He will give it, and the most despised of men He will set up over it."

**4:18** This is the dream which I, King Nebuchadnezzar, saw; now you, Belteshazzar, declare the interpretation, because all the wise men of my kingdom are not able to make known its interpretation to me; but you, Daniel, are able, for the Holy Spirit of God is in you.

**4:19** Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was struck speechless for about one hour, and his thoughts greatly troubled him. And Belteshazzar answered and said, "My lord, may the dream be for those who hate you, and its interpretation for your enemies.

**4:20** The tree which you saw, which grew large and became strong, whose height reached to heaven and its expanse to all the earth,

**4:21** and its leaves were flourishing and its fruit was abundant, and in it was food for all; beneath it the wild beasts dwelt, and in its branches the birds of heaven took shelter—

**4:22** it is you, O king; for you have grown great and become strong, and your greatness has grown and reached to heaven, and your dominion to the ends of the earth.

**4:23** And because the king saw a watcher and a holy one coming down from heaven, and he said, 'Tear down the tree and destroy it, nevertheless leave the stump of its roots in the earth, and in a band of iron and bronze, and in the green grass outside; and in the dew of heaven he shall lodge, and with wild beasts shall be his portion until seven times pass over him'—

**4:24** this is its interpretation, O king, and it is the decree of the Most High which has come upon my lord the king:

**4:25** they shall drive you out from among men, and your dwelling shall be with wild beasts, and they shall feed you grass like an ox, and in the dew of heaven you shall lodge, and seven times shall pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of men, and to whomever He pleases He will give it.

**4:26** And because they said, 'Leave the stump of the roots of the tree,' your kingdom shall remain yours from the time that you come to know the heavenly sovereignty.

**4:27** Therefore, O king, let my counsel be pleasing to you, and redeem your sins by deeds of mercy, and your iniquities by compassion toward the poor; perhaps God will be longsuffering toward your transgressions."

**4:28** All these things came upon King Nebuchadnezzar.

**4:29** After twelve months, as he was walking upon the palace of his kingdom in Babylon,

**4:30** the king answered and said, "Is this not Babylon the great, which I myself have built for a house of the kingdom, by the might of my strength, for the honor of my glory?"

**4:31** While the word was still in the mouth of the king, a voice came from heaven: "To you they speak, O King Nebuchadnezzar: the kingdom has passed away from you,

**4:32** and from among men they are driving you out, and your dwelling shall be with wild beasts, and they shall feed you grass like an ox, and seven times shall pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of men, and to whomever He pleases He will give it."

**4:33** In that very hour the word was fulfilled upon Nebuchadnezzar, and he was driven out from among men, and he ate grass like an ox, and his body was drenched with the dew of heaven, until his hair grew like that of lions and his nails like those of birds.

**4:34** And after the end of the days, I, Nebuchadnezzar, lifted up my eyes to heaven, and my understanding returned to me, and I blessed the Most High, and I praised and glorified Him who lives forever, for His dominion is an everlasting dominion, and His kingdom is from generation to generation.

**4:36** At that very time my understanding returned to me, and I came into the honor of my kingdom; and my countenance returned to me, and my rulers and my nobles sought me out, and I was firmly established over my kingdom, and even greater majesty was added to me.

**4:37** King Nebuchadnezzar, to all peoples, tribes, and languages who dwell in all the earth: Peace be multiplied to you. It has seemed good before me to declare to you the signs and wonders which the Most High God has done with me. How great and mighty they are! His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and His dominion is from generation to generation. Now therefore I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise and highly exalt and glorify the King of heaven, for all His works are true and His paths are justice, and all those who walk in pride He is able to humble.

## Translator Notes

- **4:13** — The Greek transliterates the Aramaic term for "watcher" (a wakeful heavenly being) as eir here and in 4:17, 23.
- **4:18** — Literally "a holy spirit of God"; the Greek Theodotion text also explicitly includes the name Daniel alongside Belteshazzar.
- **4:27** — The Greek text reads "perhaps God will be longsuffering toward your transgressions," differing from the Aramaic Masoretic Text ("perhaps there may be a lengthening of your prosperity").
- **4:33** — The Greek text compares Nebuchadnezzar's hair to that of lions rather than eagles' feathers as in the Aramaic Masoretic Text.
- **4:37** — In this Greek text, the royal epistolary greeting (found at 4:1-3 in the Aramaic Masoretic Text) stands at the beginning of 4:37 before Nebuchadnezzar's final doxology.
