## Text

**5:1** King Belshazzar made a great feast for his thousand nobles, and before the thousand was the wine.

**5:2** And while drinking, Belshazzar commanded at the taste of his wine to bring the gold and silver vessels which his father Nebuchadnezzar had brought out from the temple in Jerusalem, that the king and his nobles and his concubines and his wives might drink from them.

**5:3** So the gold and silver vessels were brought which he had brought out from the temple of God in Jerusalem, and the king and his nobles and his concubines and his wives drank from them.

**5:4** They drank wine, and praised the gods of gold and silver and bronze and iron and wood and stone.

**5:5** In that very hour fingers of a man's hand came forth and wrote opposite the lamp upon the plaster of the wall and house of the king, and the king watched the joints of the hand that was writing.

**5:6** Then the king's countenance changed, and his thoughts greatly troubled him, and the joints of his loins were loosened, and his knees struck against one another.

**5:7** And the king cried aloud to bring in the magicians, the Chaldeans, and the astrologers; and he spoke to the wise men of Babylon: "Whoever reads this writing and makes known the interpretation to me shall be clothed in purple, and the golden chain shall be upon his neck, and he shall rule as third in my kingdom."

**5:8** Then all the wise men of the king came in, and they were not able to read the writing, nor to make known the interpretation to the king.

**5:9** And King Belshazzar was troubled, and his countenance changed within him, and his nobles were thrown into confusion.

**5:10** And the queen entered into the banqueting house and said, "O king, live forever! Do not let your thoughts trouble you, and do not let your countenance be changed.

**5:11** There is a man in your kingdom in whom is the Spirit of God, and in the days of your father watchfulness and understanding were found in him, and King Nebuchadnezzar your father appointed him chief of the enchanters, magicians, Chaldeans, and astrologers,

**5:12** because an extraordinary spirit is in him, and wisdom and understanding—Daniel, who interprets dreams and declares riddles and loosens knots, and the king gave him the name Belteshazzar. Now therefore let him be called, and he will declare its interpretation to you."

**5:13** Then Daniel was brought in before the king, and the king said to Daniel, "Are you Daniel, who is of the sons of the captivity of Judea, whom the king my father brought?

**5:16** And I have heard concerning you that you are able to interpret judgments; now therefore, if you are able to read the writing and make known its interpretation to me, you shall be clothed in purple, and the golden chain shall be upon your neck, and you shall rule as third in my kingdom."

**5:17** And Daniel said before the king, "Let your gifts be for yourself, and give the gift of your house to another; yet I will read the writing and make known its interpretation to you. And this is the writing that has been inscribed: Mane, Thekel, Phares.

**5:23** And against the Lord God of heaven you have lifted yourself up, and you have brought the vessels of His house before you, and you and your nobles and your concubines and your wives drank wine from them; and you praised the gods of gold and silver and bronze and iron and wood and stone, which do not see and which do not hear and do not know; and the God in whose hand is your breath and all your ways, Him you have not glorified.

**5:26** This is the interpretation of the word: Mane—God has measured your kingdom and brought it to completion;

**5:27** Thekel—it has been weighed in the balance and found lacking;

**5:28** Phares—your kingdom has been divided and given to the Medes and Persians."

**5:29** Then Belshazzar gave the command, and they clothed Daniel with purple, and placed the golden chain around his neck, and he proclaimed concerning him that he should be the third ruler in the kingdom.

**5:30** In that very night Belshazzar the king of the Chaldeans was slain.

**5:19** *(after `5:17`)* and because of the greatness which He gave him, all the peoples, tribes, and tongues trembled and feared before him; whom he wished, he slew, and whom he wished, he struck, and whom he wished, he exalted, and whom he wished, he humbled.

**5:20** *(after `5:17`)* Yet when his heart was lifted up and his spirit grew hardened to deal arrogantly, he was brought down from the throne of the kingdom, and his honor was taken away from him,

**5:21** *(after `5:17`)* and he was driven away from among men, and his heart was given to be with the wild beasts, and his dwelling was with wild donkeys, and they fed him grass like an ox, and his body was drenched with the dew of heaven, until he knew that the Most High God rules over the kingdom of men, and to whomever He chooses, He will give it.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — The Greek text spells both King Belshazzar and Daniel's Babylonian name Belteshazzar identically as Baltasar.
- **5:7** — "Astrologers" renders the Greek loanword gazarenoi (also in 5:11), a transliteration of the Aramaic term for diviners or determiners of fate.
- **5:11** — "Watchfulness" translates Greek gregoresis, whereas the Aramaic Masoretic Text reads "light" or "illumination."
- **5:17** — The Greek inscription preserves three words (Mane, Thekel, Phares), omitting the repeated Mene and reading Phares rather than Upharsin as in the Aramaic Masoretic Text.
- **5:27** — In the Greek text, the third-person verbs and feminine participle refer to Belshazzar's kingdom ("it has been weighed... and found lacking"), rather than second-person "you" as in the Aramaic Masoretic Text.
- **5:22 *(additional)*** — The Greek reads the rhetorical question 'did you not know all these things?' (ou panta tauta egnos) where the Aramaic reads 'even though you knew all this.'
- **5:24 *(additional)*** — Greek astragalos cheiros literally denotes the knuckle or joint of a hand, matching 'the joints of the hand' in 5:5.
