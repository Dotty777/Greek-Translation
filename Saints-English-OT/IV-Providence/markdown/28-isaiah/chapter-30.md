## Text

**30:1** "Woe, rebellious children," says the Lord, "you have formed counsel not through Me, and covenants not through My Spirit, to add sins upon sins;

**30:2** those who proceed to go down into Egypt, yet have not inquired of Me, to be helped by Pharaoh and to be sheltered by the Egyptians.

**30:3** For the shelter of Pharaoh shall be shame to you, and a reproach to those who trust in Egypt.

**30:4** For in Tanis there are princes, evil messengers;

**30:5** in vain they shall toil for a people who will not profit them for help, but rather for shame and reproach."

**30:6** The vision of the four-footed beasts in the wilderness. In tribulation and distress are the lion and the lion's cub; from there also are asps and the offspring of asps; they carried their wealth upon donkeys and camels to a nation that will not profit them.

**30:7** The Egyptians shall profit you in vain and to no avail; declare to them, "This consolation of yours is vain."

**30:8** Now therefore, sit down and write these things upon a tablet and in a book, for these things shall be for days, in due time, and even forever.

**30:9** For this is a disobedient people, lying sons, who were not willing to hear the law of God,

**30:10** who say to the prophets, "Do not declare to us," and to those who see visions, "Do not speak to us, but speak to us and declare to us another deception,

**30:11** and turn us aside from this way; remove this path from us, and remove from us the oracle of Israel."

**30:12** Therefore thus says the Holy One of Israel: "Because you have disobeyed these words and have hoped in falsehood, and because you have murmured and placed your trust in this word,

**30:13** therefore this sin shall be to you like a suddenly falling wall of a captured fortified city, whose collapse comes in an instant;

**30:14** and its fall shall be like the shattering of an earthen vessel, fine fragments from a clay jar, so that among them no potsherd is found with which you may lift fire, or with which you may draw off a little water."

**30:15** Thus says the Lord, the Lord, the Holy One of Israel: "When you turn back and groan, then you shall be saved, and you shall know where you were; when you trusted in vain things, your strength became vain. Yet you were not willing to listen,

**30:16** but you said, 'We will flee upon horses'—therefore you shall flee!—and, 'We will be upon swift mounts'—therefore those who pursue you shall be swift.

**30:17** A thousand shall flee at the voice of one, and at the voice of five, many shall flee, until you are left like a mast upon a mountain, and like one bearing a banner upon a hill."

**30:18** And again God will wait to have compassion on you, and therefore He will be exalted to show mercy to you, because the Lord your God is a Judge; blessed are those who wait upon Him.

**30:19** For a holy people shall dwell in Zion; and Jerusalem wept with weeping, "Have mercy on me." He will have mercy on you; when He saw the voice of your cry, He heard you.

**30:20** And the Lord shall give you bread of affliction and scant water, and those who lead you astray shall no longer draw near to you; for your eyes shall see those who lead you astray,

**30:21** and your ears shall hear the words of those who led you astray from behind, who say, "This is the way; let us walk in it, whether to the right or to the left."

**30:22** And you shall defile the idols overlaid with silver and overlaid with gold; you shall crush them fine and scatter them like the water of a woman in her uncleanness, and you shall thrust them away like dung.

**30:23** Then there shall be rain for the seed of your land, and the bread of the produce of your land shall be abundant and rich; and your livestock shall graze in that day in a fertile and spacious place;

**30:24** your bulls and the oxen that till the ground shall eat chaff mixed with winnowed barley.

**30:25** And upon every high mountain and upon every lofty hill there shall be flowing water in that day, when many perish, when towers fall.

**30:26** And the light of the moon shall be as the light of the sun, and the light of the sun shall be sevenfold, in the day when the Lord heals the fracture of His people, and He shall heal the pain of your wound.

**30:27** Behold, the name of the Lord comes after a long time, burning wrath; with glory is the oracle of His lips, an oracle full of indignation, and the anger of His wrath shall devour like fire;

**30:28** and His breath, like rushing water in a ravine, shall reach even to the neck, and shall be divided to trouble the nations in vain deception; and deception shall pursue them and overtake them before their face.

**30:29** Must you always rejoice, and continually enter into My holy places as though keeping a feast, and as though rejoicing to enter with a flute into the mountain of the Lord, to the God of Israel?

**30:30** And the Lord shall make the glory of His voice heard, and display the wrath of His arm with wrath and anger and devouring flame, with a violent thunderbolt, and like water and hail swept down with force.

**30:31** For through the voice of the Lord the Assyrians shall be defeated, by the blow with which He strikes them.

**30:32** And it shall come upon him from every side, from where their hope of help was, in which he trusted; they themselves, with timbrels and harp, shall wage war against him in turn.

**30:33** For before your days you shall be called to account; has it also been prepared for you to reign—a deep ravine, logs laid out, fire and much wood? The wrath of the Lord is like a ravine kindled by brimstone.

## Translator Notes

- **30:4** — The Greek names Tanis (corresponding to Hebrew Zoan) and reads "evil messengers" (angeloi poneroi) where the Masoretic Text refers to the Egyptian city of Hanes.
- **30:15** — The Greek uses the emphatic double title "the Lord, the Lord" (kyrios Kyrios) and reads "when you turn back and groan" (apostrapheis stenaxes), differing from the Masoretic Text's "in returning and rest."
- **30:20** — In verses 20 and 21 the Septuagint reads "those who lead you astray" (hoi planontes se), whereas the Masoretic Text refers to "your Teacher."
- **30:33** — Rather than naming Topheth as in the Masoretic Text, the Greek addresses the ruler directly with a rhetorical question about a deep ravine prepared with fire and wood.
