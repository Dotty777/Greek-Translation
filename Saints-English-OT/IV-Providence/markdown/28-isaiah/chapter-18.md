## Text

**18:1** Woe to the wings of ships of the land beyond the rivers of Ethiopia,

**18:2** he who sends hostages by sea, and papyrus letters upon the water. For swift messengers shall go to a lofty nation, and to a strange and fierce people; who is beyond it? A nation without hope and trampled down. Now the rivers of the land

**18:3** shall all be inhabited like an inhabited country; when their land is lifted up for a signal from a mountain, it shall be heard like the sound of a trumpet.

**18:4** For thus the Lord spoke to me: "There shall be security in My city like the light of midday heat, and it shall be like a cloud of dew in the day of harvest."

**18:5** Before the harvest, when the blossom is complete, the unripe grape also shall bloom with a sour-grape blossom; and He shall take away the small clusters with pruning hooks, and He shall take away and cut off the vine shoots,

**18:6** and He shall leave them together to the birds of heaven and to the wild beasts of the earth; and the birds of heaven shall be gathered together upon them, and all the wild beasts of the earth shall come upon it.

**18:7** In that time gifts shall be brought to the Lord of hosts from a people afflicted and plucked, and from a great people from now and forevermore—a nation hoping and trampled down, which is in the region of the river of its land—to the place where the name of the Lord of hosts is, Mount Zion.

## Translator Notes

- **18:2** — The Greek reads "hostages" (homera) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "envoys," and uses a future indicative ("swift messengers shall go") rather than an imperative.
- **18:4** — The Greek reads "There shall be security in My city" where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "I will be quiet and look from My dwelling place."
- **18:7** — The Greek transliterates the divine title as Kyrios Sabaoth ("Lord of hosts") throughout Isaiah; note also the reversal from "a nation without hope" in 18:2 to "a nation hoping" here.
