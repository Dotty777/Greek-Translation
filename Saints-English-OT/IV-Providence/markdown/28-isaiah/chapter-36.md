## Text

**36:1** And it came to pass in the fourteenth year of the reign of Hezekiah that Sennacherib king of the Assyrians went up against the fortified cities of Judea and took them.

**36:2** And the king of the Assyrians sent Rabshakeh from Lachish to Jerusalem to King Hezekiah with a great army; and he stood by the aqueduct of the upper pool, on the road of the fuller's field.

**36:3** And there went out to him Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, the steward, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah the son of Asaph, the recorder.

**36:4** And Rabshakeh said to them, "Say to Hezekiah, 'Thus says the great king, the king of the Assyrians: In what are you trusting?

**36:5** Is battle waged by counsel and words of the lips? And now, in whom do you trust, that you disobey me?

**36:6** Behold, you are trusting in this crushed staff of reed, in Egypt; whoever leans upon it, it shall enter into his hand. So is Pharaoh king of Egypt, and all who trust in him.'

**36:7** But if you say, 'We trust in the Lord our God,'

**36:8** now join yourselves to my master the king of the Assyrians, and I will give you two thousand horses, if you are able to put riders upon them.

**36:9** And how can you turn back before the face of the governors? Household servants are those who trust in Egypt for horse and rider.

**36:10** And now, have we come up without the Lord against this country to make war against it? The Lord said to me, 'Go up against this land and destroy it.'"

**36:11** Then Eliakim and Shebna and Joah said to him, "Speak to your servants in Syriac, for we understand it; and do not speak to us in Judean. And why do you speak in the ears of the people on the wall?"

**36:12** And Rabshakeh said to them, "Has my master sent me to your master or to you to speak these words? Is it not to the people sitting on the wall, that they may eat dung and drink urine together with you?"

**36:13** Then Rabshakeh stood and cried out with a loud voice in Judean and said, "Hear the words of the great king, the king of the Assyrians:

**36:14** Thus says the king: Do not let Hezekiah deceive you with words; he shall by no means be able to deliver you.

**36:15** And do not let Hezekiah say to you, 'God will deliver you, and this city shall by no means be given into the hand of the king of the Assyrians.'

**36:16** Do not listen to Hezekiah. Thus says the king of the Assyrians: If you wish to be blessed, come out to me, and each one of you shall eat from his own vine and the fig trees, and you shall drink water from your bronze vessel,

**36:17** until I come and take you to a land like your own land, a land of grain and wine and bread and vineyards.

**36:18** Do not let Hezekiah deceive you, saying, 'God will deliver you.' Have the gods of the nations delivered, each one his own country, out of the hand of the king of the Assyrians?

**36:19** Where is the god of Hamath and Arpad? And where is the god of the city of Eppharuaim? Were they able to deliver Samaria out of my hand?

**36:20** Who among all the gods of these nations is there who has delivered his land out of my hand, that God should deliver Jerusalem out of my hand?"

**36:21** And they kept silent, and no one answered him a word, because the king had commanded that no one should answer.

**36:22** Then Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, the steward, and Shebna the scribe of the army, and Joah the son of Asaph, the recorder, came in to Hezekiah with their tunics torn, and reported to him the words of Rabshakeh.

## Translator Notes

- **36:7** — The Greek text omits the clause found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text concerning Hezekiah's removal of high places and altars, connecting the conditional clause directly into verse 8.
- **36:9** — The Greek text diverges from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, presenting the second clause as a declaration that those who trust in Egypt for horse and rider are household servants.
- **36:16** — The Greek text reads "your bronze vessel" (chalkou), a distinct textual reading where the Hebrew and other Greek witnesses read "your cistern" (lakkou).
- **36:19** — The Greek text uses the distinct place name Eppharuaim where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has Sepharvaim.
