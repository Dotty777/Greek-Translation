## Text

**46:1** Bel has fallen, Nebo is shattered; their carved images are consigned to wild beasts and cattle; lift them up bound together as a burden for one who is weary,

**46:2** faint and hungry, alike without strength, who shall not be able to be saved from war, while they themselves have been led away captive.

**46:3** Listen to Me, O house of Jacob, and all the remnant of Israel, who are carried from the womb and instructed from childhood:

**46:4** Even to old age I am He, and until you grow old I am He; I bear with you; I have made, and I will forgive; I will carry and save you.

**46:5** To whom have you likened Me? Look, contrive, you who go astray,

**46:6** who contribute gold from a pouch and silver in a balance; they shall weigh it on a scale, and having hired a goldsmith, they made things crafted by hands, and bowing down, they worship them.

**46:7** They lift it upon the shoulder and walk on; and if they set it down, it remains in its place and shall by no means move; and whoever cries out to it, it shall by no means hear, nor shall it save him from evils.

**46:8** Remember these things and groan; repent, you who have gone astray; return in heart,

**46:9** and remember the former things from of old, for I am God, and there is no other besides Me,

**46:10** declaring beforehand the last things before they come to pass, and at once they were accomplished; and I said, "All My counsel shall stand, and all things that I have purposed I will do";

**46:11** calling a bird from the east, and from a land far away concerning the things I have purposed; I spoke and I brought it about, I created and I made; I led him and made his way prosperous.

**46:12** Listen to Me, you who have lost heart, you who are far from righteousness.

**46:13** I have brought My righteousness near, and I will not delay the salvation that is from Me; I have given salvation in Zion to Israel for glory.

## Translator Notes

- **46:1** — Together with verse 2, the Greek portrays the idols as bound up like a burden for a weary, hungry bearer who cannot be saved from war, differing markedly from the Hebrew text.
- **46:4** — The Greek future verb meaning "I will forgive" or "I will remit" reflects a different sense of the underlying Hebrew verb than the Masoretic sense "I will bear."
- **46:11** — The Greek reads "concerning the things I have purposed" rather than "the man of My counsel," and includes the additional closing clause "I led him and made his way prosperous."
