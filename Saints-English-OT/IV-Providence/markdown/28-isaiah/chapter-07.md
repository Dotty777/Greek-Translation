## Text

**7:1** And it came to pass in the days of Ahaz the son of Jotham, the son of Uzziah, king of Judah, that Rezin king of Aram and Pekah the son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up against Jerusalem to make war against it, and they were not able to besiege it.

**7:2** And it was reported to the house of David, saying, "Aram has come to an agreement with Ephraim." And his soul and the soul of his people were dismayed, just as a tree in a forest is shaken by the wind.

**7:3** And the Lord said to Isaiah, "Go out to meet Ahaz, you and your son Jashub who is left, to the pool of the upper road of the fuller's field.

**7:4** And you shall say to him, 'Take heed to be quiet and do not fear, nor let your soul grow faint because of these two pieces of wood, these smoking firebrands; for when the wrath of My anger comes, I shall heal again.

**7:5** And as for the son of Aram and the son of Remaliah, because they have devised an evil counsel:

**7:6** "We will go up into Judea, and having conferred with them we will turn them to ourselves, and we will make the son of Tabeel king over it"—

**7:7** thus says the Lord Sabaoth: This counsel shall by no means abide, nor shall it come to pass.

**7:8** Rather, the head of Aram is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin; yet within sixty-five years the kingdom of Ephraim shall cease from being a people.

**7:9** And the head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is the son of Remaliah. And if you do not believe, neither shall you understand.'"

**7:10** And the Lord spoke again to Ahaz, saying,

**7:11** "Ask for yourself a sign from the Lord your God, in the depth or in the height."

**7:12** And Ahaz said, "I will by no means ask, nor will I test the Lord."

**7:13** And he said, "Hear now, O house of David: Is it a small thing for you to cause a struggle for men, and how is it that you cause a struggle for the Lord?

**7:14** Therefore the Lord Himself shall give you a sign: behold, the virgin shall conceive in the womb and bring forth a son, and you shall call His name Immanuel.

**7:15** Butter and honey He shall eat before He knows or prefers evil, to choose the good;

**7:16** for before the child knows good or evil, He refuses wickedness to choose the good, and the land which you dread before the two kings shall be forsaken.

**7:17** Yet God shall bring upon you, and upon your people, and upon your father's house, days that have not yet come since the day that He removed Ephraim from Judah—even the king of the Assyrians."

**7:18** And it shall come to pass in that day that the Lord shall whistle for flies, which shall rule over part of the river of Egypt, and for the bee that is in the land of the Assyrians;

**7:19** and they shall all come into the ravines of the land, and into the clefts of the rocks, and into the caves, and into every crevice.

**7:20** In that day the Lord shall shave the head and the hair of the feet with the hired razor from beyond the river of the king of the Assyrians, and He shall take away the beard.

**7:21** And it shall come to pass in that day that a man shall rear a heifer of the herd and two sheep;

**7:22** and it shall come to pass, from producing abundant milk, everyone who is left upon the land shall eat butter and honey.

**7:23** And it shall come to pass in that day that every place where there are a thousand vines worth a thousand shekels shall become barren ground and thorns.

**7:24** With arrow and bow they shall enter there, for all the land shall be barren ground and thorns.

**7:25** And every mountain that is plowed shall surely be plowed; fear shall by no means come there, for from the barren ground and thorns it shall become a pasture for sheep and a trampling place for oxen.

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — The Greek translates the first element of the Hebrew name Shear-Jashub ("a remnant," rendered "who is left") while transliterating the second element ("Jashub").
- **7:4** — The closing promise ("for when the wrath of My anger comes, I shall heal again") is a distinctive Greek reading contrasting with the Hebrew Masoretic Text's mention of the fierce anger of Rezin and Aram.
- **7:9** — The Greek "neither shall you understand" differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's wordplay "you shall not be established."
- **7:14** — The Greek specifies "the virgin" (Greek parthenos, quoted in Matthew 1:23) and uses the second-person singular verb "you shall call."
- **7:18** — The Greek reads "which shall rule over part of the river of Egypt," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to the fly at the farthest streams of Egypt.
