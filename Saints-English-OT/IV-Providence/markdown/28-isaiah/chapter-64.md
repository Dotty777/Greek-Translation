## Text

**64:1** As wax melts before fire, fire shall also burn up the adversaries, and Your name shall be manifest among the adversaries; at Your presence nations shall be troubled,

**64:2** when You perform glorious deeds; trembling shall seize the mountains before You.

**64:3** From of old we have not heard, nor have our eyes seen a God besides You, and Your works which You will do for those who wait for mercy.

**64:4** It shall meet those who do what is right, and they shall remember Your ways. Behold, You were angry, and we sinned; therefore we went astray,

**64:5** and we have all become as those who are unclean, and all our righteousness is like the rag of a woman in her impurity; we have fallen away like leaves because of our iniquities, and thus the wind shall carry us away.

**64:6** And there is no one who calls upon Your name, nor who remembers to take hold of You; for You have turned Your face away from us, and have delivered us up because of our sins.

**64:7** And now, O Lord, You are our Father; we are clay, and all of us are the works of Your hands.

**64:8** Do not be exceedingly angry with us, and do not remember our sins in due time; and now look upon us, for all of us are Your people.

**64:9** The city of Your sanctuary, Zion, has become a wilderness; Jerusalem has become like a wilderness—for a curse.

**64:10** The house, our sanctuary, and the glory which our fathers blessed have been burned with fire, and all our glorious things have fallen into ruin.

**64:11** And for all these things You have restrained Yourself, O Lord, and have kept silent, and have humbled us exceedingly.

## Translator Notes

- **64:3** — Greek reads "for those who wait for mercy," and the third-person singular verb "It shall meet" in 64:4 naturally continues with "mercy" as its implied subject.
- **64:8** — Greek reads "in due time" (en kairo), reflecting a reading of "at the time" rather than "forever."
- **64:9** — Following the punctuation of the Greek text, "Zion" stands in apposition to "the city of Your sanctuary," and "for a curse" closes the lament over Jerusalem before the temple is described in 64:10.
