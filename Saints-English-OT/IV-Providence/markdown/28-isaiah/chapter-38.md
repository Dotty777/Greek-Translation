## Text

**38:1** Now it came to pass in that time that Hezekiah fell sick to the point of death; and Isaiah the son of Amoz, the prophet, came to him and said to him, "Thus says the Lord: 'Set your house in order, for you are dying and shall not live.'"

**38:2** Then Hezekiah turned his face toward the wall and prayed to the Lord,

**38:3** saying, "Remember, O Lord, how I have walked before You with truth in a true heart, and have done what is pleasing before You." And Hezekiah wept with a great weeping.

**38:4** And the word of the Lord came to Isaiah, saying,

**38:5** "Go and say to Hezekiah, 'Thus says the Lord, the God of David your father: I have heard your prayer and I have seen your tears; behold, I add fifteen years to your time.

**38:6** And out of the hand of the king of the Assyrians I will deliver you and this city, and I will shield this city.

**38:7** And this shall be the sign to you from the Lord, that God will perform this word:

**38:8** behold, I turn back the shadow of the steps—the ten steps of the house of your father down which the sun has gone—I will turn back the sun the ten steps.'" And the sun went up the ten steps which the shadow had gone down.

**38:9** The prayer of Hezekiah king of Judea, when he had fallen sick and rose up from his sickness:

**38:10** I said, "In the height of my days, at the gates of Hades, I shall leave behind the remaining years."

**38:11** I said, "No longer shall I see the salvation of God in the land of the living; no longer shall I see the salvation of Israel upon the earth; no longer shall I see man."

**38:12** It failed from my kindred; I left behind the remainder of my life; it went out and departed from me like one who takes down a tent he has pitched; my spirit within me became like the web of a weaver drawing near to cut it off.

**38:13** In that day I was given over until morning as to a lion; it crushed all my bones, for from day until night I was given over.

**38:14** Like a swallow, so shall I cry out, and like a dove, so I murmur; for my eyes failed from looking into the height of heaven toward the Lord, who delivered me

**38:15** and took away the pain of my soul.

**38:16** O Lord, for it was told to You concerning it, and You revived my breath, and being comforted, I lived.

**38:17** For You chose my soul, that it should not perish, and You cast behind me all my sins.

**38:18** For those in Hades shall not praise You, nor shall those who have died bless You, nor shall those in Hades hope for Your mercy.

**38:19** The living shall bless You, in the same manner as I also do; for from this day I shall father children, who shall declare Your righteousness,

**38:20** O God of my salvation! And I shall not cease blessing You with a psaltery all the days of my life before the house of God.

**38:21** And Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Take a cake of figs and crush it and apply it as a poultice, and you shall be well."

**38:22** And Hezekiah said, "This is a sign to Hezekiah that I shall go up to the house of God."

## Translator Notes

- **38:8** — The Greek reads "the house of your father" (rather than naming Ahaz) and states explicitly that the sun itself went back up the ten steps down which the shadow had gone.
- **38:17** — The Greek reads "You chose my soul" and uniquely says "behind me" rather than "behind Your back."
- **38:19** — Unlike the Hebrew ("the father makes known to the children"), the Greek has Hezekiah declare that from this day forward he will father children who will proclaim God's righteousness.
- **38:22** — The Greek frames Hezekiah's words as a declarative statement rather than a question.
