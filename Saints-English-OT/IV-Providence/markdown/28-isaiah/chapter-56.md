# Isaiah 56 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**56:1** Thus says the Lord: "Keep justice and do righteousness, for My salvation has drawn near to come, and My mercy to be revealed."

**56:2** Blessed is the man who does these things, and the mortal who holds fast to them, who keeps the Sabbaths so as not to profane them, and who guards his hands from doing unjust deeds.

**56:3** Do not let the foreigner who has joined himself to the Lord say, "Surely the Lord will separate me from His people"; and do not let the eunuch say, "I am a dry tree."

**56:4** Thus says the Lord to the eunuchs: "As many as keep My Sabbaths, and choose the things that I desire, and hold fast to My covenant,

**56:5** I will give to them in My house and within My wall a place of renown, better than sons and daughters; I will give them an everlasting name, and it shall not fail.

**56:6** And as for the foreigners who have joined themselves to the Lord to serve Him, and to love the name of the Lord, so as to be His male servants and female servants, and all who keep My Sabbaths so as not to profane them and who hold fast to My covenant—

**56:7** I will bring them into My holy mountain and make them glad in My house of prayer; their burnt offerings and their sacrifices shall be acceptable upon My altar. For My house shall be called a house of prayer for all the nations,"

**56:8** said the Lord who gathers the dispersed of Israel, "for I will gather a congregation to him."

**56:9** All you wild beasts, come and eat, all you beasts of the forest.

**56:10** See that they have all been blinded; they have not understood; mute dogs, they shall not be able to bark, dreaming of a bed, loving to slumber.

**56:11** And the dogs are shameless in soul, not knowing satisfaction; and they are wicked, not knowing understanding; they have all followed their own ways, each one according to his own.

## Translator Notes

- **56:1** — Greek reads "My mercy" (τὸ ἔλεός μου) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "My righteousness."
- **56:6** — Greek explicitly specifies both "male servants and female servants" (δούλους καὶ δούλας) where the Masoretic Text has only "servants."
- **56:10** — Greek opens with the imperative ἴδετε ("See") rather than "his watchmen," reflecting an alternative reading of the underlying Hebrew consonants.
- **56:11** — Greek reads πονηροί ("wicked") rather than "shepherds," and the Septuagint chapter ends at verse eleven without the Masoretic verse twelve.
