## Text

**31:1** Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help, who trust in horses and in chariots—for they are many, and in horses an exceedingly great multitude—yet they did not trust in the Holy One of Israel, and they did not seek the Lord.

**31:2** Yet He Himself, being wise, brought evils upon them, and His word shall by no means be set aside; and He shall rise up against the houses of wicked men and against their vain hope,

**31:3** the Egyptian, a man and not God, the flesh of horses, and there is no help; but the Lord shall bring His hand upon them, and those who help shall grow weary, and all of them shall perish together.

**31:4** For thus the Lord spoke to me: "Just as the lion or the lion's cub roars over the prey that it has seized, and cries out over it until the mountains are filled with its voice, and they are defeated and terrified by the fullness of its fury, so shall the Lord of hosts descend to campaign upon Mount Zion, upon her mountains.

**31:5** Like flying birds, so the Lord of hosts shall shield; He shall defend Jerusalem and deliver it, and He shall preserve and save it."

**31:6** Turn back, O sons of Israel, who devise deep and lawless counsel.

**31:7** For in that day men shall renounce their silver images made by hand and their gold images made by hand, which their own hands made.

**31:8** And Assyria shall fall; neither the sword of a man nor the sword of a mortal shall devour him, and he shall flee, not from the face of a sword; and the young men shall come to defeat,

**31:9** for they shall be encompassed by a rock as by a palisade, and they shall be defeated; and he who flees shall be captured. Thus says the Lord: "Blessed is he who has seed in Zion and kinsmen in Jerusalem."

## Translator Notes

- **31:3** — The Septuagint reads "and there is no help" (kai ouk estin boetheia) where the Masoretic Text has "and not spirit."
- **31:4** — Here and in verse 5, Kyrios sabaoth (transliterating the Hebrew title for armies or hosts) is rendered "the Lord of hosts."
- **31:9** — The Septuagint concludes the chapter with a blessing on those having descendants in Zion and kinsmen in Jerusalem, contrasting with the Masoretic Text's reference to the Lord's fire in Zion and furnace in Jerusalem.
