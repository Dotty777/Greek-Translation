## Text

**2:1** The word that came to Isaiah the son of Amoz concerning Judea and concerning Jerusalem.

**2:2** For it shall come to pass in the last days that the mountain of the Lord shall be manifest, and the house of God upon the summit of the mountains, and it shall be exalted above the hills, and all the nations shall come to it.

**2:3** And many nations shall go and say, "Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, and to the house of the God of Jacob, and He will declare His way to us, and we shall walk in it." For out of Zion law shall go forth, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem.

**2:4** And He shall judge between the nations and reprove a great people; and they shall beat their swords into plows and their spears into sickles, and nation shall not take up a sword against nation, nor shall they learn to wage war anymore.

**2:5** And now, O house of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord.

**2:6** For He has forsaken His people, the house of Israel, because their country has been filled as from the beginning with divinations, like that of the foreigners, and many foreign children have been born to them.

**2:7** For their country was filled with silver and gold, and there was no number to their treasures; and the land was filled with horses, and there was no number to their chariots;

**2:8** and the land was filled with abominations, the works of their hands, and they worshiped what their fingers made.

**2:9** And mankind bowed down, and man was humbled, and I will by no means forgive them.

**2:10** And now enter into the rocks and hide yourselves in the earth from the presence of the dread of the Lord and from the glory of His might, when He arises to shatter the earth.

**2:11** For the eyes of the Lord are lofty, while man is lowly; and the loftiness of men shall be humbled, and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day.

**2:12** For the day of the Lord of hosts is upon every insolent and proud one, and upon every lofty and towering one, and they shall be humbled,

**2:13** and upon every cedar of Lebanon among the lofty and towering, and upon every oak tree of Bashan,

**2:14** and upon every mountain, and upon every high hill,

**2:15** and upon every high tower, and upon every high wall,

**2:16** and upon every ship of the sea, and upon every sight of ships of beauty.

**2:17** And every man shall be humbled, and the arrogance of men shall fall, and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day.

**2:18** And all the things made by hands they shall hide away,

**2:19** carrying them into the caves, and into the clefts of the rocks, and into the holes of the earth, from the presence of the dread of the Lord and from the glory of His might, when He arises to shatter the earth.

**2:20** For in that day a man shall cast out his abominations of silver and gold, which they made to worship, to the vain things and to the bats,

**2:21** in order to enter into the holes of the solid rock and into the clefts of the rocks, from the presence of the dread of the Lord and from the glory of His might, when He arises to shatter the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **2:6** — Greek allophylon ("foreigners") is the standard Septuagint designation for the Philistines, here matching the same root in "foreign children" (tekna allophyla).
- **2:9** — The Greek shifts into the first-person singular (ou me aneso autous, "I will by no means forgive them"), expressing the Lord's own verdict directly.
- **2:11** — The Greek reads "the eyes of the Lord are lofty, while man is lowly," contrasting divine majesty with human lowliness rather than referring to human haughtiness.
- **2:20** — Greek tois mataiois literally means "to the vain things" (corresponding to the Hebrew phrase often translated "to the moles"); in the Septuagint, chapter 2 concludes at verse 21.
