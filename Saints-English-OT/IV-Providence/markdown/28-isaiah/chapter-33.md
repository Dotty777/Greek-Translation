## Text

**33:1** Woe to those who afflict you, yet no one makes you miserable; and he who rejects you does not reject. Those who reject shall be captured and delivered up, and like a moth upon a garment, so shall they be defeated.

**33:2** O Lord, have mercy on us, for in You we have trusted; the seed of the disobedient has come to destruction, yet our salvation is in a time of tribulation.

**33:3** Because of the voice of terror, peoples were dismayed at the fear of You, and the nations were scattered.

**33:4** And now your spoil shall be gathered, both of small and great; just as when one gathers a locust, so shall they mock you.

**33:5** Holy is God who dwells on high; Zion has been filled with judgment, and in righteousness

**33:6** in the law they shall be delivered up. Our salvation is in treasures; wisdom and understanding and godliness toward the Lord have come; these are treasures of righteousness.

**33:7** Behold now, in your fear these shall be afraid; those whom you feared shall cry out because of you; messengers shall be sent forth weeping bitterly, pleading for peace.

**33:8** For their ways shall be made desolate; the fear of the nations has ceased, and the covenant with them is taken away, and you shall by no means regard them as men.

**33:9** The earth mourned, Lebanon was put to shame, Sharon became marshes; Galilee and Carmel shall be made manifest.

**33:10** "Now I will arise," says the Lord; "now I will be glorified, now I will be exalted;

**33:11** now you shall see, now you shall perceive; the strength of your spirit shall be in vain; fire shall devour you,

**33:12** and nations shall be burned completely, like a thorn cast out in a field and burned completely.

**33:13** Those who are afar off shall hear what I have done; those who draw near shall know My strength."

**33:14** The lawless in Zion have turned away; trembling shall seize the ungodly. Who will declare to you that fire is burning? Who will declare to you the eternal place?

**33:15** He who walks in righteousness, speaking of a straight way, hating lawlessness and unrighteousness, and shaking his hands from bribes, stopping his ears lest he hear a judgment of blood, shutting his eyes lest he see unrighteousness,

**33:16** this one shall dwell in a lofty cave of a strong rock; bread shall be given to him, and his water is sure.

**33:17** You shall see a king with glory; your eyes shall behold a land from afar.

**33:18** Our soul shall meditate on fear: Where are the scribes? Where are the counselors? Where is he who numbers those who are nourished,

**33:19** a small and great people? With whom he did not take counsel, nor did he know one of deep speech, so that a despised people might not hear, and there is no understanding for the one who hears.

**33:20** Behold Zion the city, our salvation; your eyes shall see Jerusalem, a wealthy city, tents that shall never be shaken, nor shall the stakes of her tent be moved forever, nor shall her cords ever be broken;

**33:21** for the name of the Lord is great to you. There shall be a place for you, rivers and canals broad and spacious; you shall not travel this way, nor shall a rowing vessel pass.

**33:22** For my God is great; He will not pass me by. The Lord is our Judge, the Lord is our Ruler, the Lord is our King; the Lord Himself will save us.

**33:23** Your cords were broken, for they were not strong; your mast leaned over; it shall not let down the sails, it shall not raise a banner, until it is delivered up to plunder; therefore many who are lame shall take plunder.

**33:24** And the people dwelling among them shall by no means say, "I am weary," for their sin has been forgiven them.

## Translator Notes

- **33:1** — The Greek concludes with the vivid simile 'like a moth upon a garment, so shall they be defeated,' a distinctive Septuagint rendering compared to the Hebrew text.
- **33:5** — The Greek places 'in righteousness' in the dative case at the end of verse 5, linking it syntactically with 'in the law they shall be delivered up' at the opening of verse 6.
- **33:9** — The Greek reads 'Galilee' where the Hebrew text has 'the Arabah,' and transliterates Carmel as 'Chermel.'
- **33:18** — The Greek reads 'those who are nourished' (apparently reflecting an alternate reading of the Hebrew word for 'towers'), and the clause flows directly into the compressed syntax of verse 19.
- **33:24** — The Greek has the inhabitants refrain from saying 'I am weary' (kopio) rather than 'I am sick.'
