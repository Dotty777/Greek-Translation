## Text

**61:1** The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me, because He has anointed Me; He has sent Me to declare glad tidings to the poor, to heal those who are broken in heart, to herald release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind,

**61:2** to declare an acceptable year of the Lord and a day of recompense, to console all those who mourn,

**61:3** that to them, those who mourn in Zion, glory may be given in place of ashes, the ointment of gladness to the mourners, a mantle of glory instead of a spirit of despondency; and they shall be called generations of righteousness, a planting of the Lord for glory.

**61:4** And they shall build up ancient wastelands; those formerly laid desolate they shall raise up; and they shall renew deserted cities, laid waste for generations.

**61:5** And foreigners shall come tending your sheep, and men of other nations as plowmen and vinedressers;

**61:6** but you shall be called priests of the Lord, ministers of God; the strength of the nations you shall consume, and in their wealth you shall be marveled at.

**61:7** Thus they shall inherit the land a second time, and everlasting gladness shall be upon their head.

**61:8** For I am the Lord, who loves righteousness and hates plunder from unrighteousness; and I will give their labor to the righteous, and I will establish an everlasting covenant with them.

**61:9** And their seed shall be known among the nations, and their offspring in the midst of the peoples; everyone who sees them shall recognize them, that these are a seed blessed by God,

**61:10** and with gladness they shall rejoice in the Lord. Let my soul exult in the Lord, for He has clothed me with a garment of salvation and a tunic of gladness; He has placed a turban upon me like a bridegroom, and like a bride He has adorned me with ornament.

**61:11** And as the earth causes its blossom to grow, and as a garden its seeds, so the Lord, the Lord will cause righteousness and exultation to spring forth before all the nations.

## Translator Notes

- **61:1** — The Greek Septuagint reads "recovery of sight to the blind" (quoted in Luke 4:18), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the opening of prison to those who are bound."
- **61:3** — The Greek reads "generations of righteousness" where the Hebrew has "oaks" or "terebinths of righteousness."
- **61:7** — The Greek condenses the opening of the verse and renders the Hebrew "double" as "a second time."
- **61:11** — The doubled Greek title "the Lord, the Lord" (kyrios Kyrios) reflects the Hebrew compound divine name.
