## Text

**20:1** In the year when Tanathan entered into Azotus, when he was sent by Arna, king of the Assyrians, and he fought against Azotus and captured it,

**20:2** then the Lord spoke to Isaiah the son of Amoz, saying, "Go and remove the sackcloth from your loins, and unfasten your sandals from your feet, and do so, going naked and barefoot."

**20:3** And the Lord said, "Just as My servant Isaiah has gone naked and barefoot three years, three years it shall be for signs and wonders to the Egyptians and Ethiopians;

**20:4** for thus the king of the Assyrians shall lead away the captivity of Egypt and of the Ethiopians, young men and old men, naked and barefoot together, covered with the shame of Egypt.

**20:5** And they shall be put to shame, having been defeated because of the Ethiopians, in whom the Egyptians had trusted, who were their glory.

**20:6** And those who dwell on this island shall say in that day, 'Behold, we had trusted to flee to them for help, who were not able to be saved from the king of the Assyrians; and how shall we be saved?'"

## Translator Notes

- **20:1** — The Greek preserves the Septuagint proper names Tanathan (Hebrew Tartan), Azotus (the Hellenistic name for Ashdod), and Arna (Hebrew Sargon).
- **20:2** — The Greek includes "and do so, going naked and barefoot" as part of the Lord's command rather than as a narrative statement of Isaiah's obedience.
- **20:4** — The Greek text here reads kekalymmenous ("covered [with the shame of Egypt]"), whereas some Septuagint manuscripts read anakekalymmenous ("uncovered" or "exposed").
