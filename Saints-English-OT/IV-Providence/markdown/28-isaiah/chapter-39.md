## Text

**39:1** In that time Merodach-Baladan the son of Baladan, the king of Babylonia, sent letters and envoys and gifts; for he had heard that he had fallen sick to the point of death and had risen up.

**39:2** And Hezekiah rejoiced over them, and showed them the house of Nechotha, and of the silver, and of the gold, and of the oil of myrrh, and of the incense, and of the ointment, and all the houses of the vessels of the treasure, and all that was in his treasuries; and there was nothing in his house that Hezekiah did not show.

**39:3** And Isaiah the prophet came to King Hezekiah and said to him, "What do these men say, and from where have they come to you?" And Hezekiah said, "From a land far away they have come to me, from Babylon."

**39:4** And Isaiah said, "What did they see in your house?" And Hezekiah said, "All the things in my house they saw, and there is nothing in my house that they did not see, yes, even the things in my treasuries."

**39:5** And Isaiah said to him, "Hear the word of the Lord of Sabaoth:

**39:6** 'Behold, days are coming when they shall take all that is in your house, and whatever your fathers have gathered until this day shall come to Babylon, and they shall by no means leave anything behind,' and God has said,

**39:7** 'Even some of your children whom you shall father they shall take, and they shall make them eunuchs in the house of the king of the Babylonians.'"

**39:8** And Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "Good is the word of the Lord which He has spoken; let there indeed be peace and righteousness in my days."

## Translator Notes

- **39:2** — "Nechotha" is a Greek transliteration of a Hebrew term for a spice-house or treasure-house; the Greek also speaks of "vessels of the treasure" rather than an armory.
- **39:5** — "Sabaoth" is the Greek transliteration of the Hebrew word for "hosts" or "armies."
- **39:8** — The Greek reads "peace and righteousness" rather than "peace and truth."
