## Text

**3:1** Behold now, the Sovereign Lord of hosts will remove from Jerusalem and from Judea the strong man and the strong woman, the strength of bread and the strength of water,

**3:2** the giant and the strong man and the man of war, and the judge and the prophet and the diviner and the elder,

**3:3** and the captain of fifty and the admirable counselor, and the wise master builder and the discerning hearer.

**3:4** And I will appoint youths as their rulers, and mockers shall have dominion over them.

**3:5** And the people will fall upon one another, man against man, and man against his neighbor; the child will strike against the aged man, the dishonorable against the honorable.

**3:6** For a man will take hold of his brother or of a kinsman of his father, saying, "You have a garment; become our leader, and let my food be under you."

**3:7** And answering, he will say in that day, "I will not be your leader, for in my house there is neither bread nor garment; I will not be a leader of this people."

**3:8** For Jerusalem has been forsaken and Judea has fallen, and their tongues are joined with lawlessness, disobeying what pertains to the Lord; therefore now their glory has been brought low,

**3:9** and the shame of their countenance has stood against them; and they have declared their sin like that of Sodom and made it manifest. Woe to their soul, for they have devised an evil counsel against themselves,

**3:10** saying, "Let us bind the righteous one, because he is burdensome to us." Therefore they shall eat the produce of their works.

**3:11** Woe to the lawless one! Evil things shall befall him according to the works of his hands.

**3:12** O My people, your exactors glean you, and those who demand payment have dominion over you. O My people, those who call you blessed lead you astray and trouble the path of your feet.

**3:13** But now the Lord will rise up for judgment, and He will bring His people to stand for judgment;

**3:14** the Lord Himself will come into judgment with the elders of the people and with its rulers: "And why have you burned up My vineyard, and why is the plunder of the poor in your houses?

**3:15** Why do you wrong My people, and put the countenance of the poor to shame?"

**3:16** Thus says the Lord: "Because the daughters of Zion have been lifted up, and have walked with a lofty neck and with winking of the eyes, and in the gait of their feet trailing their tunics as they go, and sporting with their feet at the same time,

**3:17** God will therefore humble the ruling daughters of Zion, and the Lord will uncover their form

**3:18** in that day; and the Lord will take away the glory of their apparel: the braided ornaments and the tassels and the crescents,

**3:19** and the pendant and the ornament of their face,

**3:20** and the array of glorious adornment, and the bangles and the bracelets and the hair clasp and the finger rings and the armbands and the earrings,

**3:21** and the purple-bordered robes and the purple-woven garments,

**3:22** and the wraps worn about the house and the transparent Laconian garments,

**3:23** and the fine linens and the blue and scarlet garments, and the fine linen interwoven with gold and blue, and the flowing summer veils.

**3:24** And instead of sweet fragrance there shall be dust; and instead of a sash you shall gird yourself with a rope; and instead of the golden ornament of the head you shall have baldness because of your works; and instead of the purple-woven tunic you shall gird yourself with sackcloth.

**3:25** And your fairest son, whom you love, shall fall by the sword, and your strong men shall fall by the sword and be brought low;

**3:26** and the cases of your adornment shall mourn, and you shall be left alone and be leveled to the ground."

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Greek retains the Hebrew title Sabaoth ("hosts") untranslated as Kyrios Sabaoth, and renders the paired support terms as masculine and feminine participles ("the strong man and the strong woman") echoing "strength of bread and strength of water."
- **3:6** — The Greek reads "my food" (to broma to emon), a distinctive Septuagint reading where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "this heap of ruins."
- **3:10** — The Septuagint reads "Let us bind the righteous one, because he is burdensome to us" (echoed in Wisdom 2:12), differing markedly from the Masoretic Hebrew ("Say to the righteous that it shall be well").
- **3:22** — "Laconian garments" (Lakonika) is a Septuagint-distinct Hellenistic term referring to sheer, finely woven Spartan textiles.
- **3:26** — The Greek reads "the cases of your adornment" (hai thekai tou kosmou hymon), referring to jewel chests rather than the Masoretic Hebrew "her gates."
