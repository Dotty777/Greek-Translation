## Text

**48:1** Hear these things, O house of Jacob, who are called by the name of Israel and have come forth from Judah, who swear by the name of the Lord God of Israel, making mention of Him not in truth nor in righteousness,

**48:2** and who hold fast to the name of the holy city, and lean upon the God of Israel—the Lord of hosts is His name.

**48:3** The former things I declared even of old; they went forth from My mouth, and they were heard; suddenly I acted, and they came to pass.

**48:4** I know that you are stubborn, and your neck is an iron sinew, and your forehead is bronze.

**48:5** And I declared to you things of old before they came upon you; I caused you to hear them, lest you should say, "The idols did them for me," and say, "The carved images and the molten images commanded me."

**48:6** You have heard all things, and you did not know; yet I have caused you to hear new things from this time forth, which are about to come to pass, and you did not say,

**48:7** "Now they come to pass, and not of old." And in former days you did not hear them; do not say, "Yes, I know them."

**48:8** You neither knew nor understood, nor from the beginning did I open your ears; for I knew that you would surely act treacherously, and you shall be called a transgressor even from the womb.

**48:9** For the sake of My name I will show you My wrath, and My glorious deeds I will bring upon you, that I may not utterly destroy you.

**48:10** Behold, I have sold you, not for the sake of silver; yet I have delivered you out of the furnace of poverty.

**48:11** For My own sake I will do this for you, for My name is profaned; and My glory I will not give to another.

**48:12** Listen to Me, O Jacob, and Israel whom I call: I am the first, and I am forever,

**48:13** and My hand laid the foundation of the earth, and My right hand established the heaven; I will call them, and they shall stand together,

**48:14** and all of them shall be gathered together and shall hear. Who has declared these things to them? Out of love for you I have performed your will upon Babylon, to take away the seed of the Chaldeans.

**48:15** I have spoken, I have called; I have brought him, and I have made his way prosperous.

**48:16** Draw near to Me and hear these things: from the beginning I have not spoken in secret; when it came to pass, I was there; and now the Sovereign Lord has sent Me, and His Spirit.

**48:17** Thus says the Lord, the Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel: "I am your God; I have shown you how to find the way in which you shall walk.

**48:18** And if you had heeded My commandments, your peace would have been like a river, and your righteousness like a wave of the sea;

**48:19** and your seed would have been like the sand, and the offspring of your womb like the dust of the earth; nor even now shall you by any means be utterly destroyed, nor shall your name perish before Me."

**48:20** Go forth from Babylon, fleeing from the Chaldeans; proclaim a voice of gladness, and let this be heard; declare it to the end of the earth; say, "The Lord has redeemed His servant Jacob."

**48:21** And if they thirst, He will bring water for them through the wilderness; out of a rock He will bring it forth for them; a rock shall be split open and water shall flow forth, and My people shall drink.

**48:22** "There is no rejoicing," says the Lord, "for the ungodly."

## Translator Notes

- **48:9** — The Septuagint reads "I will show you My wrath, and My glorious deeds I will bring upon you," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "I will defer My anger, and for My praise I will restrain it for you."
- **48:10** — The Septuagint reads "I have sold you, not for the sake of silver; yet I have delivered you out of the furnace of poverty," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("I have refined you, but not as silver; I have tested you in the furnace of affliction").
- **48:16** — Greek kyrios Kyrios (literally "Lord, the Lord") renders the Hebrew divine title Adonai YHWH and is translated here as "the Sovereign Lord" (likewise in 50:5 and 50:7).
- **48:21** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which recalls past provision in the wilderness, the Septuagint casts the promise in the future tense and concludes with "and My people shall drink."
