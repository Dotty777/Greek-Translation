## Text

**1:1** The burden of the word of the Lord concerning Israel, by the hand of His messenger; lay it now upon your hearts.

**1:2** "I have loved you," says the Lord. Yet you said, "In what have You loved us?" "Was not Esau the brother of Jacob?" says the Lord. "Yet I loved Jacob,

**1:3** but Esau I hated; and I appointed his borders to desolation, and his inheritance to gifts of the wilderness."

**1:4** Because one will say, "Idumea has been overthrown, so let us return and rebuild the desolate places," thus says the Lord Almighty: "They shall build, and I will tear down; and they shall be called Borders of Lawlessness, and the people against whom the Lord stands arrayed forever.

**1:5** And your eyes shall see, and you shall say, 'The Lord has been magnified above the borders of Israel.'"

**1:6** "A son glorifies his father, and a servant his master. And if I am a Father, where is My glory? And if I am a Master, where is My fear?" says the Lord Almighty. "You are the priests who despise My name!" Yet you said, "In what have we despised Your name?"

**1:7** "By bringing defiled loaves to My altar." Yet you said, "In what have we defiled them?" "By your saying, 'The table of the Lord is defiled,' and you have held in contempt the things set upon it.

**1:8** For if you bring the blind for a sacrifice, is it not evil? And if you bring the lame or the sick, is it not evil? Present it now to your governor: will he accept you, or will he regard your person?" says the Lord Almighty.

**1:9** And now, appease the face of your God and plead with Him. "By your hands these things have been done; will I accept your persons from among you?" says the Lord Almighty.

**1:10** "Because even among you the doors shall be shut, and My altar shall not be kindled in vain. I have no delight in you," says the Lord Almighty, "nor will I accept a sacrifice from your hands.

**1:11** For from the rising of the sun even to its setting, My name has been glorified among the nations; and in every place incense is offered to My name, and a pure sacrifice; for My name is great among the nations," says the Lord Almighty.

**1:12** "But you profane it by your saying, 'The table of the Lord is defiled, and what is laid upon it, even His food, is held in contempt.'"

**1:13** And you said, "These things are from weariness!" "And I blew them away," says the Lord Almighty. "Yet you brought in plundered things, and the lame, and the afflicted; and if you bring the sacrifice, will I accept them from your hands?" says the Lord Almighty.

**1:14** "And cursed is the one who had the means, and there was a male in his flock, and his vow was upon it, yet he sacrifices what is blemished to the Lord. For I am a great King," says the Lord Almighty, "and My name is illustrious among the nations."

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The Septuagint translates the Hebrew title as an appellative, "His messenger" (angelou autou), rather than the proper name Malachi, and adds the closing exhortation "lay it now upon your hearts."
- **1:3** — The Greek reads "gifts of the wilderness" (domata eremou), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonants rendered "jackals of the wilderness" in the Masoretic Text.
- **1:4** — The Septuagint uses the Greek name "Idumea" for Edom and renders the final verb as a military term ("stands arrayed," paratetaktai) rather than "has indignation."
- **1:13** — The Greek uses a first-person singular verb ("I blew them away," exephysesa), expressing the Lord's own rejection of the offerings, rather than the second-person "you sneer at it" of the Masoretic Text.
- **1:14** — The Septuagint reads "the one who had the means" (dynatos) rather than "the deceiver."
