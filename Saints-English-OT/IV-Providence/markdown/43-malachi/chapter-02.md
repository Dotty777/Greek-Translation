## Text

**2:1** "And now, this commandment is for you, O priests.

**2:2** If you will not listen, and if you will not take it to heart to give glory to My name," says the Lord Almighty, "then I will send out the curse upon you, and I will curse your blessing—indeed, I will curse it; and I will scatter your blessing, and it shall not be among you, because you do not take it to heart.

**2:3** Behold, I separate the shoulder from you, and I will scatter the maw upon your faces, the maw of the feasts, and I will take you away to the same place;

**2:4** and you shall know that I have sent forth this commandment to you, that My covenant may be with the Levites," says the Lord Almighty.

**2:5** "My covenant with him was one of life and peace, and I gave it to him that he might fear with fear, and stand in awe before My name.

**2:6** The law of truth was in his mouth, and unrighteousness was not found upon his lips; keeping a straight path in peace, he walked with Me, and turned many away from unrighteousness.

**2:7** For the lips of a priest shall guard knowledge, and they shall seek the law from his mouth; because he is a messenger of the Lord Almighty.

**2:8** But you have turned aside from the way and have weakened many in the law; you have corrupted the covenant of Levi?" says the Lord Almighty.

**2:9** "And I also have made you despised and cast out among all the nations, because you do not keep My ways, but showed partiality in the law."

**2:10** Has not one God created you? Is there not one Father of you all? Why is it that each of you has forsaken his brother, to profane the covenant of your fathers?

**2:11** Judah has been forsaken, and an abomination has been committed in Israel and in Jerusalem; for Judah has profaned the holy things of the Lord, in that he loved and devoted himself to foreign gods.

**2:12** The Lord will utterly destroy the man who does these things, even until he is humbled out of the tents of Jacob, and from among those who bring a sacrifice to the Lord Almighty.

**2:13** "And these things which I hated, you did: you covered the altar of the Lord with tears, with weeping and groaning from troubles. Is it still fitting to look upon a sacrifice, or to receive what is acceptable from your hands?"

**2:14** And you said, "For what reason?" Because the Lord has borne witness between you and the wife of your youth, whom you have forsaken; yet she is your companion and the wife of your covenant.

**2:15** And did He not make what is good, and a remnant of His Spirit? And you said, "What else does God seek except offspring?" Therefore guard yourselves in your spirit, and do not forsake the wife of your youth.

**2:16** "But if in hatred you send her away," says the Lord, the God of Israel, "then ungodliness shall cover your thoughts," says the Lord Almighty. Therefore guard yourselves in your spirit, and by no means forsake her,

**2:17** you who provoke God with your words! Yet you said, "In what have we provoked Him?" By your saying, "Everyone who does evil is good in the sight of the Lord, and in them He Himself is well pleased," and, "Where is the God of justice?"

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — The Greek reads "the shoulder" (omon) rather than "the seed" and "the maw" (enystron, the ruminant's stomach) rather than "dung," recalling the priestly portions prescribed in Deuteronomy 18:3.
- **2:11** — The Septuagint reads passively "Judah has been forsaken" and describes Judah as having "devoted himself to foreign gods" rather than marrying the daughter of a foreign god.
- **2:15** — In this notoriously difficult verse, the Greek reads "did He not make what is good" (ou kalon epoiesen) rather than "did He not make them one" and attributes the following question directly to the people ("And you said").
- **2:16** — Rather than "I hate divorce," the Septuagint reads a second-person condition ("if in hatred you send her away") and warns that ungodliness shall cover "your thoughts" (enthymemata) rather than "his garment."
