## Text

**4:1** And Jonah was grieved with a great grief, and he was confounded.

**4:2** And he prayed to the Lord and said, "O Lord, were not these my words while I was still in my land? Therefore I hastened beforehand to flee to Tarshish, for I knew that You are merciful and compassionate, longsuffering and abundant in mercy, and relenting over calamities.

**4:3** And now, O Sovereign Lord, take my life from me, for to die is better for me than to live."

**4:4** And the Lord said to Jonah, "Are you exceedingly grieved?"

**4:5** And Jonah went out from the city and sat opposite the city; and he made a booth for himself there and sat beneath it in the shade, until he should see what would become of the city.

**4:6** And the Lord God commanded a gourd, and it grew up over the head of Jonah to be a shade above his head, to shade him from his afflictions; and Jonah rejoiced over the gourd with a great joy.

**4:7** And God commanded a worm at dawn on the next day, and it struck the gourd, and it withered away.

**4:8** And it came to pass, as soon as the sun rose, that God commanded a scorching wind of burning heat, and the sun struck upon the head of Jonah; and he grew faint and despaired of his life, and said, "To die is better for me than to live."

**4:9** And God said to Jonah, "Are you exceedingly grieved over the gourd?" And he said, "I am exceedingly grieved, even to death."

**4:10** And the Lord said, "You had pity on the gourd, over which you did not endure hardship, nor did you nurture it, which came into being in a night and in a night perished.

**4:11** And shall I not have pity on Nineveh, the great city, in which dwell more than one hundred twenty thousand people who do not know their right hand or their left hand, and much livestock?"

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — The Septuagint renders Jonah's reaction as intense grief and confusion ("grieved with a great grief, and he was confounded"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text describes him as displeased and angry.
- **4:4** — The Septuagint asks "Are you exceedingly grieved?" (echoed in 4:9), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text asks whether Jonah does well to be angry.
- **4:6** — The Greek kolokynthe specifically denotes a gourd plant, famous in patristic translation debates; the Septuagint also reads "to shade him from his afflictions" rather than "to deliver him."
- **4:8** — The Greek verb apelegeto ("despaired of" or "renounced") conveys giving up on one's life, paralleling the Hebrew idiom for asking to die.
