## Text

**2:1** And the Lord commanded a great sea creature to swallow Jonah; and Jonah was in the belly of the sea creature three days and three nights.

**2:2** Then Jonah prayed to the Lord his God from the belly of the sea creature,

**2:3** and said, "I cried out in my affliction to the Lord my God, and He heard me. Out of the belly of Hades You heard the voice of my cry; You cast me into the depths of the heart of the sea,

**2:4** and rivers surrounded me; all Your towering billows and Your waves passed over me.

**2:5** And I said, 'I have been cast out from Your eyes; shall I indeed look again toward Your holy temple?'

**2:6** Water was poured around me even to the soul; the lowest deep encircled me; my head sank down into the clefts of the mountains.

**2:7** I went down into a land whose bars are everlasting restraints; yet let the corruption of my life ascend, O Lord my God.

**2:8** When my soul was failing from me, I remembered the Lord; and may my prayer come to You, into Your holy temple.

**2:9** Those who observe vain and false things have forsaken their mercy.

**2:10** But I will sacrifice to You with a voice of praise and thanksgiving; all that I have vowed I will pay to You for deliverance, to the Lord."

**2:11** And command was given to the sea creature, and it cast Jonah out upon the dry land.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — Verse numbering in chapter 2 follows the Septuagint (where 2:1 corresponds to 1:17 in most English Bibles); Greek ketos denotes a great sea creature or sea monster rather than specifically a fish.
- **2:6** — Greek reads "my head sank down into the clefts of the mountains," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("seaweed was wrapped around my head").
- **2:7** — Greek reads literally "let the corruption of my life ascend" (a third-person imperative), whereas the Hebrew reads "You brought up my life from the pit."
- **2:11** — Greek uses a passive verb ("command was given to the sea creature") rather than the Hebrew active ("the LORD spoke to the fish").
