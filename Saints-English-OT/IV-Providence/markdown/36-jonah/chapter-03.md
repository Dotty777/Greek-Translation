## Text

**3:1** And the word of the Lord came to Jonah a second time, saying,

**3:2** "Arise, go to Nineveh, the great city, and preach in it according to the former proclamation which I spoke to you."

**3:3** So Jonah arose and went to Nineveh, just as the Lord had spoken. Now Nineveh was a great city to God, about a journey of three days.

**3:4** And Jonah began to enter into the city about one day's journey, and he preached and said, "Yet three days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!"

**3:5** And the men of Nineveh believed in God, and proclaimed a fast, and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them.

**3:6** And the word reached the king of Nineveh; and he rose up from his throne, and removed his robe from himself, and wrapped himself in sackcloth, and sat upon ashes.

**3:7** And it was proclaimed and spoken in Nineveh from the king and from his nobles, saying, "Let men and beasts, and oxen and sheep, taste nothing; neither let them graze, nor let them drink water."

**3:8** And the men and the beasts wrapped themselves in sackcloth, and cried out earnestly to God; and each one turned back from his evil way and from the unrighteousness that was in their hands, saying,

**3:9** "Who knows whether God will relent and turn back from the fury of His wrath, so that we shall by no means perish?"

**3:10** And God saw their works, that they had turned back from their evil ways; and God relented concerning the calamity which He had spoken to do to them, and He did not do it.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — Greek specifies "according to the former proclamation which I spoke to you," referring back to the initial commission in 1:2, whereas the Hebrew reads "the proclamation that I tell you."
- **3:4** — The Septuagint reads "three days," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "forty days."
- **3:8** — In the Greek text, the verbs in verse 8 are past-tense indicatives narrating the Ninevites' actual response rather than imperatives continuing the king's decree as in the Hebrew; accordingly, "saying" introduces the people's words in verse 9.
