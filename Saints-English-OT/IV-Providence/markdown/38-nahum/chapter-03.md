## Text

**3:1** O city of blood, wholly false, full of unrighteousness—prey shall not be handled.

**3:2** The sound of whips and the sound of the rumbling of wheels, and of a pursuing horse and of a bounding chariot,

**3:3** and of a mounting horseman and of a gleaming sword and flashing weapons, and of a multitude of wounded and a heavy downfall; and there was no end to her nations, and they shall grow weak in their bodies because of the multitude of harlotry.

**3:4** A fair and charming harlot, a mistress of sorceries, who sells nations by her harlotry and peoples by her sorceries.

**3:5** "Behold, I am against you," says the Lord God Almighty, "and I will uncover your hinder parts over your face, and I will show nations your shame and kingdoms your dishonor;

**3:6** and I will cast abomination upon you according to your uncleannesses, and I will make you an example;

**3:7** and it shall come to pass that everyone who sees you shall go down from you and say, 'Wretched Nineveh! Who shall groan for her? From where shall I seek comfort for her?'"

**3:8** Prepare a portion, tune the string, prepare a portion, O Ammon, who dwells among the rivers; water is all around her, whose dominion is the sea, and water is her walls.

**3:9** And Ethiopia is her strength, and Egypt, and there is no limit to the flight, and the Libyans became her helpers.

**3:10** Yet she herself shall go into exile as a captive, and they shall dash her infants to the ground at the head of all her streets; and for all her glorious things they shall cast lots, and all her nobles shall be bound with manacles.

**3:11** And you shall become drunk and shall be overlooked, and you shall seek for yourself a standing place from enemies.

**3:12** All your strongholds are fig trees having watchmen; if they are shaken, they shall fall into the mouth of the eater.

**3:13** Behold, your people are like women in your midst; the gates of your land shall surely be opened wide to your enemies, and fire shall devour your bars.

**3:14** Draw for yourself water for the siege, and strengthen your strongholds; step into the clay and be trampled in chaff; grow stronger than brick.

**3:15** There fire shall devour you, the sword shall destroy you, it shall devour you like a locust, and you shall be weighed down like a crawling locust.

**3:16** You have multiplied your merchandise more than the stars of heaven; the crawling locust rushed forth and flew away.

**3:17** Your mixed multitude leaped out like a wingless locust, like a locust mounted upon a hedge in a day of frost; the sun rose, and it leaped away, and did not know its place. Woe to them!

**3:18** Your shepherds have slumbered, O Assyrian king; he has put your rulers to sleep; your people have departed to the mountains, and there was none to receive them.

**3:19** There is no healing for your shattering; your wound is inflamed; all who hear the report of you shall clap their hands over you, for upon whom has your wickedness not come continually?

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Greek "ou pselaphethesetai thera" ("prey shall not be handled") reflects a Septuagint reading of the Hebrew verb ("does not depart") as "to touch or feel."
- **3:3** — Greek "ethnesin" ("nations") appears in the Septuagint where the Masoretic Text reads "corpses," and the Septuagint attaches "because of the multitude of harlotry" to the close of 3:3 rather than 3:4.
- **3:8** — Greek "Prepare a portion, tune the string, prepare a portion, O Ammon" is a distinctive Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew text ("Are you better than No-Amon [Thebes]?"), retaining the Greek form "Ammon."
- **3:12** — Greek "skopous" ("watchmen") is the Septuagint reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to "first-ripe figs."
- **3:17** — Greek "ouai autois" ("Woe to them!") reflects the Septuagint reading of the Hebrew consonants ("Where are they?") as an exclamation of woe.
