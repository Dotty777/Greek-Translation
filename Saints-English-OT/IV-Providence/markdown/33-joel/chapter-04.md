## Text

**4:1** For behold, in those days and at that time, when I Myself restore the captivity of Judah and Jerusalem,

**4:2** I will also gather all the nations and lead them down into the Valley of Jehoshaphat; and I will enter into judgment with them there on behalf of My people and My inheritance Israel, who were scattered among the nations; and they divided up My land,

**4:3** and they cast lots for My people; they gave the young boys to harlots, and sold the young girls for wine and drank.

**4:4** And what are you to Me, Tyre and Sidon, and all Galilee of the foreigners? Are you repaying a recompense to Me, or do you bear malice against Me hastily? And swiftly I will return your recompense upon your heads,

**4:5** because you have taken My silver and My gold, and have brought My choice and fine things into your temples;

**4:6** and the sons of Judah and the sons of Jerusalem you have sold to the sons of the Greeks, that you might drive them out from their borders.

**4:7** Behold, I will raise them up out of the place where you sold them, and I will return your recompense upon your heads,

**4:8** and I will sell your sons and your daughters into the hands of the sons of Judah, and they shall sell them into captivity to a nation far away; for the Lord has spoken.

**4:9** Proclaim these things among the nations: Consecrate war, rouse the warriors; draw near and come up, all you men of war.

**4:10** Beat your plows into swords and your sickles into spears; let the weak one say, "I have strength."

**4:11** Assemble yourselves and come in, all you nations on every side, and be gathered together there; let the meek become a warrior.

**4:12** Let them be roused, let all the nations come up to the Valley of Jehoshaphat; for there I will sit to judge all the nations on every side.

**4:13** Send forth the sickles, for the vintage is at hand; go in, tread, for the winepress is full; cause the vats to overflow, for their evils have been multiplied.

**4:14** Sounds have resounded in the valley of judgment, for the day of the Lord is near in the valley of judgment.

**4:15** The sun and the moon shall grow dark, and the stars shall withdraw their radiance.

**4:16** And the Lord shall cry aloud from Zion, and utter His voice from Jerusalem, and heaven and earth shall be shaken; yet the Lord shall spare His people and strengthen the sons of Israel.

**4:17** And you shall know that I am the Lord your God, who dwells in Zion, on My holy mountain; and Jerusalem shall be holy, and foreigners shall pass through her no more.

**4:18** And it shall come to pass in that day that the mountains shall drip sweetness, and the hills shall flow with milk, and all the channels of Judah shall flow with waters; and a fountain shall go forth from the house of the Lord and shall water the brook of rushes.

**4:19** Egypt shall become a desolation, and Idumea shall become a plain of desolation, on account of the injustices against the sons of Judah, because they shed righteous blood in their land.

**4:20** But Judea shall be inhabited forever, and Jerusalem to generations of generations.

**4:21** And I will require their blood, and I will by no means leave it unpunished; and the Lord shall dwell in Zion.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — "Galilee of the foreigners" translates the Greek Γαλειλαία ἀλλοφύλων, where "foreigners" is the Septuagint's standard rendering for the Philistines and "Galilee" reflects the Hebrew term for "districts" or "regions."
- **4:8** — The Greek text reads "into captivity" (εἰς αἰχμαλωσίαν), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "to the Sabeans."
- **4:11** — The Septuagint reads "let the meek become a warrior" (ὁ πρᾳὺς ἔστω μαχητής), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's petition to bring down the Lord's mighty ones.
- **4:18** — "The brook of rushes" renders the Greek τὸν χειμάρρουν τῶν σχοίνων (literally, "the winter-torrent of rushes"), corresponding to "the Valley of Shittim" (or "Acacias") in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **4:19** — "Idumea" (Ἰδουμαία) is the Septuagint's Greek form of the proper name Edom, and the Greek also reads "a plain of desolation" (πεδίον ἀφανισμοῦ) where the Hebrew reads "a desolate wilderness."
