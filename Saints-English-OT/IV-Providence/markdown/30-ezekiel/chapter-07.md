## Text

**7:1** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**7:2** "And you, son of man, say, 'Thus says the Lord to the land of Israel: An end has come, the end has come upon the four wings of the land.

**7:3** The end has come

**7:4** upon you who dwell in the land; the time has come, the day has drawn near—not with tumults, nor with pangs.

**7:5** Now shortly I will pour out My wrath upon you, and I will accomplish My fury in you, and I will judge you in your ways, and I will lay upon you all your abominations;

**7:6** My eye shall not spare, nor will I have mercy; for I will lay your ways upon you, and your abominations shall be in the midst of you, and you shall know that I am the Lord who strikes.

**7:7** Now the end is upon you, and I Myself will send against you and execute vengeance in your ways, and I will lay upon you all your abominations;

**7:8** My eye shall not spare, nor will I have mercy; for I will lay your way upon you, and your abominations shall be in the midst of you, and you shall know that I am the Lord.

**7:9** For thus says the Lord: Behold, the end has come,

**7:10** behold, the day of the Lord! Even though the rod has blossomed, arrogance has risen up.

**7:11** And it shall crush the support of the lawless, and not with tumult, nor with haste.

**7:12** The time has come; behold, the day! Do not let the one who buys rejoice, and do not let the one who sells mourn;

**7:13** for the one who buys shall no longer return to the one who sells, and a man shall not hold fast in the eye of his life.

**7:14** Sound the trumpet and judge all things!

**7:15** War with the sword is outside, and famine and death are within; he who is in the plain shall die by the sword, and famine and death shall consume those in the city.

**7:16** And the survivors among them shall escape and shall be upon the mountains; I will slay them all, each one in his iniquities.

**7:17** All hands shall grow faint, and all thighs shall be defiled with moisture,

**7:18** and they shall gird themselves with sackcloth, and terror shall cover them; and shame shall be upon every face among them, and baldness upon every head.

**7:19** Their silver shall be cast into the streets, and their gold shall be despised; their souls shall not be satisfied, and their bellies shall not be filled, for it has become the torment of their iniquities.

**7:20** Choice things of adornment they turned into pride, and they made images of their abominations out of them; because of this I have given them to them for uncleanness.

**7:21** And I will deliver them into the hands of strangers to plunder them, and to the pestilent ones of the earth for spoil, and they shall profane them.

**7:22** And I will turn My face away from them, and they shall defile My oversight, and they shall enter into them unguardedly and profane them,

**7:23** and they shall make confusion, for the land is full of peoples, and the city is full of lawlessness.

**7:24** And I will turn back the arrogance of their strength, and their holy places shall be defiled.

**7:25** Atonement shall come and seek peace, and there shall be none.

**7:26** Woe upon woe shall be, and report upon report shall be; and a vision shall be sought from a prophet, and law shall perish from a priest, and counsel from elders.

**7:27** A prince shall clothe himself with desolation, and the hands of the people of the land shall be paralyzed; according to their ways I will do to them, and in their judgments I will execute vengeance upon them, and they shall know that I am the Lord.'"

## Translator Notes

- **7:4** — The Greek reads "not with tumults, nor with pangs" and follows a distinct order and wording across verses 3–9 compared to the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **7:13** — Literally "in the eye of his life" (en ophthalmō zōēs autou), a distinctive Septuagint rendering reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew consonant text ("in his iniquity").
- **7:22** — "My oversight" (tēn episkopēn mou) translates the Greek term for visitation or charge, where the Hebrew tradition refers to God's treasured or secret place; likewise verse 23 reads "confusion" (phyrmon) and "full of peoples" (plērēs laōn).
- **7:25** — "Atonement" (exilasmos) is a notable Septuagint reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "anguish" or "destruction."
