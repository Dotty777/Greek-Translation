## Text

**7:1** Woe is me, for I have become like one who gathers stubble in the harvest, and like gleanings in the vintage, when there is no cluster to eat the first-ripe fruits. Woe is me, O soul,

**7:2** for the godly man has perished from the earth, and there is no upright one among men; they all contend in judgment for blood, each one oppresses his neighbor with oppression;

**7:3** they prepare their hands for evil. The ruler asks, and the judge has spoken words of peace; it is the desire of his soul. And I will take away their good things

**7:4** like a moth devouring and walking upon a measuring line in the day of watching. Woe, woe, your acts of vengeance have come; now shall be their weepings.

**7:5** Do not trust in friends, and do not put your hope in leaders; guard yourself from her who lies with you, lest you entrust anything to her;

**7:6** for a son dishonors his father, a daughter will rise up against her mother, a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law; all those in a man's house are his enemies.

**7:7** But I will look to the Lord; I will wait patiently for God my Savior; my God will hear me.

**7:8** Do not gloat over me, O my enemy, because I have fallen, yet I shall rise again; for even if I sit in darkness, the Lord will give light to me.

**7:9** I will bear the wrath of the Lord, because I have sinned against Him, until He vindicates my cause; and He will execute my judgment, and You will bring me forth into the light; I shall behold His righteousness.

**7:10** And my enemy shall see it, and she shall be clothed with shame—she who says to me, "Where is the Lord your God?" My eyes shall look upon her; now she shall be for trampling down like mire in the streets.

**7:11** In the day of plastering brick, that day shall be your blotting out, and that day shall wear away your ordinances;

**7:12** and your cities shall yield to leveling and to division among the Assyrians, and your fortified cities to division from Tyre to the river, and from sea to sea, and from mountain to the mountain;

**7:13** and the land shall become a desolation together with those who dwell in it, from the fruits of their practices.

**7:14** Shepherd Your people with Your rod, the sheep of Your inheritance, who dwell by themselves in a forest in the midst of Carmel; they shall graze in Bashan and Gilead as in the days of old.

**7:15** And according to the days of your departure from Egypt, you shall behold wondrous things;

**7:16** the nations shall see and be put to shame even because of all their might; they shall lay their hands upon their mouth, their ears shall become deaf,

**7:17** they shall lick dust like a serpent, crawling upon the earth; they shall be confounded in their confinement; they shall be astounded at the Lord our God, and they shall be afraid of You.

**7:18** Who is a God like You, taking away iniquities and passing over ungodly deeds for the remnant of His inheritance? And He has not held fast His wrath for a testimony, because He delights in mercy.

**7:19** He will turn again and have compassion on us; He will submerge our unrighteous deeds, and they shall be cast into the depths of the sea—all our sins.

**7:20** He will give for truth to Jacob, mercy to Abraham, just as You swore to our fathers according to the former days.

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — The Greek reads "the judge has spoken words of peace" and "I will take away their good things" (continuing into 7:4 with "like a moth devouring"), diverging notably from the Masoretic Text's reference to bribery and a brier hedge.
- **7:9** — The Greek shifts abruptly to the second person in "You will bring me forth into the light" (ἐξάξεις) between third-person references to the Lord, a distinctive Septuagint reading preserved here.
- **7:11** — The Greek renders this verse as a judgment oracle concerning "plastering brick" and the "blotting out" of the city and its ordinances, contrasting with the Masoretic Text's promise of rebuilding walls.
- **7:18** — The Greek reads "for a testimony" (εἰς μαρτύριον), reflecting a reading of the Hebrew consonants as "witness" rather than "forever."
- **7:20** — The Greek reads third-person "He will give for truth" (δώσει εἰς ἀλήθειαν) before shifting to second-person "just as You swore," preserving a notable Septuagint textual feature.
