## Text

**16:1** Now Samson went to Gaza, and he saw a harlot there and went in to her.

**16:2** And it was reported to the Gazites, saying, "Samson has come here." So they surrounded him and lay in ambush for him all night at the gate of the city, and they kept silent all night, saying, "Wait until the morning dawns, and let us slay him."

**16:3** And Samson lay until midnight; and he arose in the middle of the night and took hold of the doors of the city gate together with the two posts, and lifted them up along with the bar and placed them upon his shoulders; and he went up to the summit of the mountain that faces Hebron, and laid them down there.

**16:4** And it came to pass after this that he loved a woman in Halsorech, whose name was Delilah.

**16:5** And the rulers of the foreigners went up to her and said to her, "Deceive him, and see in what his great strength lies, and by what means we may prevail against him and bind him to humble him; and we will each give you one thousand one hundred pieces of silver."

**16:6** So Delilah said to Samson, "Please tell me in what your great strength lies, and with what you may be bound so as to be humbled."

**16:7** And Samson said to her, "If they bind me with seven moist bowstrings that have not been spoiled, then I shall become weak and be like any other man."

**16:8** So the rulers of the foreigners brought up to her seven moist bowstrings that had not been spoiled, and she bound him with them.

**16:9** Now her ambush was sitting in the inner chamber. And she said to him, "The foreigners are upon you, Samson!" But he snapped the bowstrings as one would pull apart a twisted strand of flax when it catches the scent of fire. Thus his strength was not discovered.

**16:10** Then Delilah said to Samson, "Behold, you have deceived me and spoken falsehoods to me. Now therefore, tell me with what you may be bound."

**16:11** And he said to her, "If they securely bind me with new ropes with which no work has been done, then I shall become weak and be like any other man."

**16:12** So Delilah took new ropes and bound him with them, and the men in ambush came out of the inner chamber, and she said, "The foreigners are upon you, Samson!" And he snapped them from his arms like a thread.

**16:13** Then Delilah said to Samson, "Behold, you have deceived me and spoken falsehoods to me. Tell me now with what you may be bound." And he said to her, "If you weave the seven locks of my head with the warp and fasten them with the pin into the wall, then I shall become weak like any other man."

**16:14** And it came to pass, while he was sleeping, that Delilah took the seven locks of his head, wove them into the warp, and fastened them with the pin into the wall; and she said, "The foreigners are upon you, Samson!" And he awoke from his sleep and pulled out the pin of the web from the wall.

**16:15** Then Delilah said to Samson, "How can you say, 'I have loved you,' when your heart is not with me? This third time you have deceived me, and you have not told me in what your great strength lies."

**16:16** And it came to pass, when she pressed him with her words all those days and distressed him, that his soul grew faint even to death.

**16:17** So he told her all his heart, and said to her, "Iron has not come upon my head, for I am a holy one of God from my mother's womb. Therefore, if I am shaved, my strength will depart from me, and I shall become weak and be like all other men."

**16:18** And when Delilah saw that he had told her all his heart, she sent and called the rulers of the foreigners, saying, "Come up yet this once, for he has told me all his heart." So the rulers of the foreigners came up to her and brought the silver in their hands.

**16:19** And Delilah put Samson to sleep upon her knees, and she called a man and shaved off the seven locks of his head; and she began to humble him, and his strength departed from him.

**16:20** And Delilah said, "The foreigners are upon you, Samson!" And he awoke from his sleep and said, "I will go out as once and again before, and shake myself free." Yet he did not know that the Lord had departed from him.

**16:21** Then the foreigners seized him and gouged out his eyes, and brought him down to Gaza and bound him in bronze fetters; and he was grinding in the prison house.

**16:22** And the hair of his head began to grow again after he had been shaved.

**16:23** Now the rulers of the foreigners gathered together to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god and to rejoice; and they said, "The god has delivered into our hand Samson our enemy."

**16:24** And the people saw him and praised their god, saying, "Our god has delivered our enemy into our hand, the one who laid waste our land and who multiplied our slain."

**16:25** And when their heart was merry, they said, "Call Samson out of the guardhouse, and let him make sport before us." So they called Samson from the prison house, and he made sport before them; and they struck him on the face, and stationed him between the pillars.

**16:26** And Samson said to the young man who held his hand, "Let me go, that I may feel the pillars upon which the house rests, and I will lean against them."

**16:27** Now the house was full of men and women, and all the rulers of the foreigners were there; and upon the roof were about seven hundred men and women watching the sport of Samson.

**16:28** Then Samson wept to the Lord and said, "Adonai, Lord, remember me now, I pray, and strengthen me yet this once, O God, that I may repay one vengeance upon the foreigners for my two eyes."

**16:29** And Samson embraced the two pillars of the house upon which the house stood, and braced himself against them, grasping one with his right hand and one with his left hand.

**16:30** And Samson said, "Let my soul die with the foreigners!" And he heaved with might, and the house fell upon the rulers and upon all the people who were in it. So the dead whom Samson slew at his death were more than those whom he had slain in his life.

**16:31** Then his brethren and the house of his father came down and took him and brought him up, and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of Manoah his father. And he judged Israel twenty years.

## Translator Notes

- **16:4** — Codex B merges Hebrew nahal Soreq ("valley of Sorek") into the single place name Halsorech and spells Delilah's name Daleida.
- **16:13** — Codex B preserves the fuller clause "and fasten them with the pin into the wall, then I shall become weak like any other man," which is absent from the Masoretic Text.
- **16:17** — Codex B reads sideros ("iron") rather than "razor" and translates Hebrew "Nazirite" as hagios theou ("a holy one of God").
- **16:27** — Codex B reads "about seven hundred" (hos heptakosioi), whereas the Masoretic Text and Codex Alexandrinus read "about three thousand."
- **16:28** — Codex B transliterates Hebrew Adonai directly as vocative Adonaie ("Adonai, Lord") and uses eklausen ("wept") rather than "called out" (as also in 15:18).
