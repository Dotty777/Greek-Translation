## Text

**14:1** And Samson went down to Timnah, and saw a woman in Timnah of the daughters of the foreigners.

**14:2** And he went up and told his father and his mother, and said, "I have seen a woman in Timnah of the daughters of the Philistines; now therefore, get her for me as a wife."

**14:3** Then his father and his mother said to him, "Are there no daughters among your brethren, or a woman from all my people, that you go to take a wife from the uncircumcised foreigners?" And Samson said to his father, "Get her for me, for she is right in my eyes."

**14:4** Now his father and his mother did not know that it was from the Lord, that He was seeking vengeance from the foreigners. And at that time the foreigners were ruling over Israel.

**14:5** So Samson and his father and his mother went down to Timnah; and he came to the vineyard of Timnah, and behold, a young lion came roaring to meet him.

**14:6** And the Spirit of the Lord leaped upon him, and he crushed it as one would crush a young goat, though there was nothing in his hands. But he did not tell his father or his mother what he had done.

**14:7** Then they went down and spoke to the woman, and she was right in the eyes of Samson.

**14:8** And after some days he returned to take her; and he turned aside to see the carcass of the lion, and behold, there was a swarm of bees in the mouth of the lion, and honey.

**14:9** And he scooped it out into his hands, and went on his way, walking and eating. And he went to his father and his mother, and gave some to them, and they ate; yet he did not tell them that he had taken the honey out of the mouth of the lion.

**14:10** Then his father went down to the woman; and Samson made a banquet there for seven days, for thus the young men do.

**14:11** And it came to pass, when they saw him, that they brought thirty guests, and they were with him.

**14:12** Then Samson said to them, "I put forth a riddle to you: if you surely declare it during the seven days of the banquet and find it out, I will give you thirty linen garments and thirty robes of clothing;

**14:13** but if you are not able to declare it to me, then you shall give me thirty linen cloths and thirty changes of garments." And they said to him, "Put forth the riddle, and we will hear it."

**14:14** So he said to them, "What food came forth from the eater, and out of the strong, sweetness?" And they could not declare the riddle for three days.

**14:15** And it came to pass on the fourth day that they said to Samson's wife, "Deceive your husband now, and let him declare the riddle to you, lest we burn you and your father's house with fire. Or have you invited us to despoil us?"

**14:16** Then Samson's wife wept before him and said, "You only hate me and do not love me, for you have not declared to me the riddle which you put forth to the sons of my people." And Samson said to her, "If I have not declared it to my father and my mother, shall I declare it to you?"

**14:17** Now she wept before him for the seven days while their banquet lasted. And it came to pass on the seventh day that he declared it to her, because she troubled him; and she declared it to the sons of her people.

**14:18** So the men of the city said to him on the seventh day before the sun rose, "What is sweeter than honey, and what is stronger than a lion?" And Samson said to them, "If you had not plowed with my heifer, you would not have known my riddle."

**14:19** Then the Spirit of the Lord leaped upon him, and he went down to Ashkelon and struck down thirty men of them, and took their garments, and gave the robes to those who had declared the riddle. And Samson burned with wrath, and he went up to his father's house.

**14:20** And Samson's wife became the wife of one of his friends with whom he had been a companion.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — Here and in verses 3-4, the Greek uses allophyloi ("foreigners"), the Septuagint's regular designation for the Philistines, while verse 2 uses the transliteration Phylistieim ("Philistines").
- **14:8** — Codex Vaticanus reads "in the mouth" here and in verse 9, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "in the carcass/body."
- **14:15** — Codex Vaticanus reads "on the fourth day" (fitting the three days of verse 14), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "on the seventh day."
- **14:18** — Codex Vaticanus reads pro tou anateilai ton helion ("before the sun rose"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is commonly rendered "before the sun went down."
