## Text

**15:1** And it came to pass after some days, in the days of the wheat harvest, that Samson visited his wife with a young goat; and he said, "I will go in to my wife, into the inner chamber." But her father did not permit him to go in.

**15:2** And her father said, "I truly thought that you utterly hated her, so I gave her to one of your companions. Is not her younger sister better than she? Let her now be yours in her place."

**15:3** And Samson said to them, "I am blameless even this once regarding the foreigners, for I am doing harm to them."

**15:4** So Samson went and caught three hundred foxes; and he took torches, turned tail to tail, placed one torch between the two tails, and bound them.

**15:5** And he kindled fire in the torches and sent them forth into the grainfields of the foreigners; and they were burned from the threshing floor even to the standing grain, and even to the vineyard and the olive grove.

**15:6** And the foreigners said, "Who has done these things?" And they answered, "Samson, the son-in-law of the Timnite, because he took his wife and gave her to one of his companions." Then the foreigners went up and burned her and her father with fire.

**15:7** And Samson said to them, "Since you have done thus to her, surely I will take vengeance upon you, and afterward I will cease."

**15:8** And he struck them shin upon thigh with a great slaughter; and he went down and dwelt in the cleft of the rock of Etam.

**15:9** Then the foreigners went up and encamped in Judah, and they spread out in Leuei.

**15:10** And the men of Judah said, "Why have you come up against us?" And the foreigners answered, "We have come up to bind Samson, and to do to him in the manner he has done to us."

**15:11** Then three thousand men from Judah went down to the cleft of the rock of Etam, and they said to Samson, "Do you not know that the foreigners rule over us? And what is this that you have done to us?" And Samson said to them, "In the manner they did to me, so have I done to them."

**15:12** And they said to him, "We have come down to bind you, to deliver you into the hand of the foreigners." And Samson said to them, "Swear to me that you yourselves will not fall upon me."

**15:13** And they spoke to him, saying, "No, we will only bind you securely and deliver you into their hand, and we will by no means put you to death." So they bound him with two new ropes and brought him up from that rock.

**15:14** And they came as far as Jawbone, and the foreigners shouted aloud and ran to meet him. Then the Spirit of the Lord leaped upon him, and the ropes that were on his arms became like flax that has been burned with fire, and his bonds melted away from his hands.

**15:15** And he found the jawbone of a donkey cast aside, and he stretched out his hand and took it, and struck down with it one thousand men.

**15:16** And Samson said, "With the jawbone of a donkey I have utterly blotted them out, for with the jawbone of the donkey I have struck down one thousand men."

**15:17** And it came to pass, when he ceased speaking, that he cast the jawbone out of his hand, and he called that place Slaying of a Jawbone.

**15:18** Then he became exceedingly thirsty, and he wept to the Lord and said, "You have been pleased to grant by the hand of Your servant this great deliverance, and now I shall die of thirst and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised."

**15:19** And God split open the hollow place that is in Jawbone, and water came forth from it; and he drank, and his spirit returned and he revived. Therefore its name was called Spring of the One Who Calls, which is in Jawbone, to this day.

**15:20** And he judged Israel in the days of the foreigners twenty years.

## Translator Notes

- **15:3** — Greek allophyloi ("foreigners") is the Septuagint's consistent designation for the Philistines throughout Judges.
- **15:9** — Codex B transliterates Hebrew Lehi as Leuei here, whereas in verses 14, 17, and 19 it translates the place name directly as Siagon ("Jawbone").
- **15:15** — Greek ekrerimmenen ("cast aside") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "fresh."
- **15:16** — Codex B renders the Hebrew wordplay ("heaps upon heaps") with the emphatic Greek idiom exaleiphon exeleipsa autous ("I have utterly blotted them out").
