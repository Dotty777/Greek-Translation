## Text

**19:1** And there was a Levite man sojourning on the flanks of Mount Ephraim, and he took for himself a concubine from Bethlehem of Judah.

**19:2** And his concubine departed from him, and went away from him to the house of her father, to Bethlehem of Judah; and she was there for the days of four months.

**19:3** And her husband arose and went after her to speak to her heart, to bring her back to himself; and his young servant was with him, and a pair of donkeys. And she brought him into the house of her father; and the father of the young woman saw him, and rejoiced to meet him.

**19:4** And his father-in-law, the father of the young woman, detained him, and he stayed with him for three days; and they ate and drank, and lodged there.

**19:5** And it came to pass on the fourth day that they rose early in the morning, and he stood up to depart; and the father of the young woman said to his son-in-law, "Strengthen your heart with a morsel of bread, and after this you shall go."

**19:6** And he sat down, and the two of them ate together and drank; and the father of the young woman said to the man, "Come now, stay the night, and your heart shall be glad."

**19:7** And the man arose to depart; yet his father-in-law urged him strongly, so he sat down and lodged there.

**19:8** And he rose early in the morning on the fifth day to depart, and the father of the young woman said, "Strengthen your heart, please, and encamp until the day declines." And the two of them ate.

**19:9** And the man arose to depart, he and his concubine and his young servant; and his father-in-law, the father of the young woman, said to him, "Behold now, the day has grown weak toward evening; lodge here, and your heart shall be glad, and tomorrow you shall rise early for your journey, and you shall go to your tent."

**19:10** And the man was not willing to lodge there, and he arose and departed, and came opposite Jebus, which is Jerusalem; and with him was a pair of saddled donkeys, and his concubine was with him.

**19:11** And they came as far as Jebus; and the day was far spent, and the young servant said to his master, "Come now, and let us turn aside into this city of the Jebusites, and lodge in it."

**19:12** And his master said to him, "We will not turn aside into a foreign city where there is none of the sons of Israel here, but we will pass on as far as Gibeah."

**19:13** And he said to his young servant, "Come, and let us draw near to one of the places, and we shall lodge in Gibeah or in Ramah."

**19:14** And they passed on and went their way, and the sun went down on them close by Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin.

**19:15** And they turned aside there to go in to lodge in Gibeah; and they went in and sat down in the open square of the city, for there was no man who took them into a house to lodge.

**19:16** And behold, an old man was coming from his work out of the field at evening; and the man was from Mount Ephraim, and he was sojourning in Gibeah, whereas the men of the place were sons of Benjamin.

**19:17** And he lifted up his eyes and saw the wayfaring man in the open square of the city; and the old man said, "Where are you going, and from where do you come?"

**19:18** And he said to him, "We are passing from Bethlehem of Judah to the flanks of Mount Ephraim; I am from there, and I went as far as Bethlehem of Judah, and I am going to my own house; and there is no man who takes me into a house.

**19:19** Yet indeed there is straw and fodder for our donkeys, and there is bread and wine for me and for the maidservant and for the young man with your servants; there is no lack of anything."

**19:20** And the old man said, "Peace be to you; only let every need of yours be upon me, only in the open square you shall by no means lodge."

**19:21** And he brought him into his house, and made a place for the donkeys; and they washed their feet, and ate and drank.

**19:22** Now while they were making their hearts glad, behold, men of the city, sons of lawless men, surrounded the house, pounding on the door; and they spoke to the old man, the master of the house, saying, "Bring out the man who came into your house, that we may know him."

**19:23** And the man, the master of the house, went out to them and said, "No, brethren, please do not do evil, since this man has entered into my house; do not commit this folly.

**19:24** Behold, here are my virgin daughter and his concubine. I will bring them out, and humble them, and do to them what is good in your eyes; but to this man you shall not do this act of folly."

**19:25** And the men were not willing to listen to him; so the man seized his concubine and brought her out to them outside; and they knew her, and abused her all night until morning, and they let her go as the morning broke.

**19:26** And the woman came toward daybreak, and fell down at the door of the house where her husband was, until it grew light.

**19:27** And her husband arose in the morning and opened the doors of the house, and went out to go on his way; and behold, the woman, his concubine, was lying fallen at the doors of the house, with her hands upon the threshold.

**19:28** And he said to her, "Arise, and let us depart." Yet she did not answer, for she was dead. And he took her upon the donkey, and went to his own place.

**19:29** And he took the sword and laid hold of his concubine, and cut her into twelve pieces, and sent them throughout all the territory of Israel.

**19:30** And it came to pass that everyone who saw it said, "Such a thing has never happened nor been seen from the day the sons of Israel came up from the land of Egypt even to this day; take counsel for yourselves concerning it, and speak."

## Translator Notes

- **19:2** — Codex Vaticanus reads "departed from him," whereas other Greek manuscripts read "became angry with him" and the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "was unfaithful against him."
- **19:8** — Codex Vaticanus uses the unusual military verb meaning "encamp" or "go on campaign," standing where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "linger" or "tarry."
- **19:18** — The Greek text reads "to my own house," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "to the house of the Lord."
- **19:21** — Codex Vaticanus reads "made a place for the donkeys," where the Hebrew Masoretic Text states that he gave fodder to the donkeys.
- **19:28** — The Greek text explicitly includes "for she was dead," a clause absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
