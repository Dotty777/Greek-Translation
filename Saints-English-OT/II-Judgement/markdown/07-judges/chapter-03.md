## Text

**3:1** Now these are the nations which the Lord left behind so as to test Israel by them, all those who had not known the wars of Canaan,

**3:2** only for the sake of the generations of the children of Israel, to teach them war—except that those before them had not known these things:

**3:3** the five satrapies of the foreigners, and all the Canaanites, and the Sidonians, and the Hivites who dwelt in Lebanon, from Mount Hermon as far as Lebo-hamath.

**3:4** And it came to pass so as to test Israel by them, to know whether they would heed the commandments of the Lord, which He commanded their fathers by the hand of Moses.

**3:5** Thus the children of Israel dwelt in the midst of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites.

**3:6** And they took their daughters for themselves as wives, and gave their own daughters to their sons; and they served their gods.

**3:7** So the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the Lord, and forgot the Lord their God, and served the Baals and the groves.

**3:8** Therefore the Lord was incensed with wrath against Israel, and He sold them into the hand of Cushan-rishathaim, king of the rivers of Syria; and the children of Israel served Cushan-rishathaim eight years.

**3:9** And the children of Israel cried out to the Lord; and the Lord raised up a savior for Israel, and he saved them—Othniel the son of Kenaz, the brother of Caleb, who was younger than he.

**3:10** And the Spirit of the Lord came upon him, and he judged Israel. And he went out to war against Cushan-rishathaim; and the Lord delivered Cushan-rishathaim, king of Syria of the rivers, into his hand, and his hand prevailed over Cushan-rishathaim.

**3:11** And the land had rest forty years; and Othniel the son of Kenaz died.

**3:12** And the children of Israel again did evil before the Lord; and the Lord strengthened Eglon, king of Moab, against Israel, because they had done evil in the sight of the Lord.

**3:13** And he gathered to himself all the children of Ammon and Amalek, and went and struck Israel, and took possession of the City of Palms.

**3:14** And the children of Israel served Eglon, king of Moab, eighteen years.

**3:15** And the children of Israel cried out to the Lord; and He raised up for them a savior, Ehud the son of Gera, the son of Jemini, an ambidextrous man. And the children of Israel sent gifts by his hand to Eglon, king of Moab.

**3:16** And Ehud made for himself a two-edged dagger, a span in length, and girded it under his cloak upon his right thigh.

**3:17** And he went and presented the gifts to Eglon, king of Moab; now Eglon was a very handsome man.

**3:18** And it came to pass, when Ehud had finished presenting the gifts, that he sent away those who carried the gifts.

**3:19** But he himself turned back from the carved images that were near Gilgal, and Ehud said, "I have a secret word for you, O king." And Eglon said to him, "Keep silence!" And he sent away from himself all those who stood in attendance upon him.

**3:20** And Ehud came in to him; and he was sitting in his own summer upper chamber, entirely alone. And Ehud said, "I have a word from God for you, O king." And Eglon rose up from the throne near him.

**3:21** And it came to pass, at the very moment he rose up, that Ehud stretched out his left hand and took the dagger from upon his right thigh and thrust it into his belly;

**3:22** and he drove in the hilt also after the blade; and the fat closed over the blade, for he did not draw the dagger out of his belly.

**3:23** And Ehud went out through the porch, and passed out past those stationed on guard, and shut the doors of the upper chamber upon him, and bolted them.

**3:24** And when he had gone out, his servants came and looked, and behold, the doors of the upper chamber were bolted; and they said, "Could he perhaps be relieving himself in the summer chamber?"

**3:25** So they waited until they were ashamed; and behold, there was no one opening the doors of the upper chamber. Therefore they took the key and opened them, and behold, their lord had fallen upon the ground, dead.

**3:26** Now Ehud escaped while they were in confusion, and there was no one paying attention to him; and he passed beyond the carved images and escaped to Seteirotha.

**3:27** And it came to pass, when Ehud came into the land of Israel, that he blew the horn in Mount Ephraim; and the children of Israel went down with him from the mountain, and he was before them.

**3:28** And he said to them, "Go down after me, for the Lord God has delivered our enemies, Moab, into our hand." So they went down after him, and seized the fords of the Jordan belonging to Moab, and he did not allow a man to cross over.

**3:29** And they struck down of Moab on that day about ten thousand men, every stout man and every man of valor; and not a man escaped.

**3:30** So Moab was humbled on that day under the hand of Israel, and the land had rest eighty years; and Ehud judged them until he died.

**3:31** And after him arose Shamgar the son of Deinach, and he struck down the foreigners, to the number of six hundred men, with the plowshare of the oxen; and he also saved Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — The Septuagint refers to the Philistines throughout Judges as 'the foreigners' (allophyloi) and to their five territories as 'satrapies' (satrapiai). It also reads 'Mount Hermon' rather than 'Mount Baal-hermon.'
- **3:15** — The Greek renders the Hebrew idiom for 'left-handed' ('restricted in his right hand') as amphoterodexion, 'ambidextrous.'
- **3:17** — Codex Vaticanus describes Eglon as 'a very handsome man' (asteios sphodra), contrasting with the Masoretic Text's 'a very fat man.'
- **3:24** — The Greek literally reads 'emptying his feet,' a Septuagint euphemism for relieving oneself (compare the Masoretic idiom 'covering his feet').
- **3:31** — Codex Vaticanus reads 'Deinach' instead of Hebrew 'Anath,' and 'the plowshare of the oxen' (arotropodi) instead of 'oxgoad.' Earlier in 3:26, Vaticanus likewise has 'Seteirotha' for Hebrew 'Seirah.'
