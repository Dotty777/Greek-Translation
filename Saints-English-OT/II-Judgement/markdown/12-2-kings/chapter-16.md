## Text

**16:1** In the seventeenth year of Pekah the son of Remaliah, Ahaz the son of Jotham, king of Judah, began to reign.

**16:2** Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem; and he did not do what was upright in the eyes of the Lord God faithfully, like David his father.

**16:3** And he walked in the way of the king of Israel; indeed, he even made his son pass through fire, and practiced the abominations of the nations whom the Lord had removed from before the sons of Israel.

**16:4** And he sacrificed and burned incense on the high places, and on the hills, and under every leafy tree.

**16:5** Then Rezin king of Syria and Pekah the son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up to Jerusalem to war; and they besieged Ahaz, yet they could not fight.

**16:6** At that time Rezin king of Syria restored Elath to Syria, and drove the Jews out of Elath; and the Edomites came to Elath and dwelt there to this day.

**16:7** And Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-Pileser king of the Assyrians, saying, "I am your servant and your son. Come up, save me from the hand of the king of Syria and from the hand of the king of Israel, who rise up against me."

**16:8** And Ahaz took the silver and gold found in the treasuries of the house of the Lord and of the king's house, and sent gifts to the king.

**16:9** And the king of the Assyrians heeded him; and the king of the Assyrians went up to Damascus, and captured it and carried it away captive, and put Rezin to death.

**16:10** And King Ahaz went to meet Tiglath-Pileser king of the Assyrians at Damascus; and he saw the altar in Damascus. And King Ahaz sent to Uriah the priest the likeness of the altar and its pattern, for all its workmanship.

**16:11** And Uriah the priest built the altar according to all that King Ahaz had sent from Damascus.

**16:12** And the king saw the altar, and went up upon it,

**16:13** and burned his burnt offering and his sacrifice and his drink offering, and poured out the blood of his peace offerings upon the altar,

**16:14** the bronze one that was before the Lord; and he brought it forward to the front of the house of the Lord, from between the altar and from between the house of the Lord, and showed it on the side of the altar toward the north.

**16:15** And King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest, saying, "Upon the great altar offer the morning burnt offering and the evening sacrifice, and the king's burnt offering and his sacrifice, and the burnt offering of all the people and their sacrifice and their drink offering; and all the blood of the burnt offering and all the blood of the sacrifice you shall pour out upon it; and as for the bronze altar, it shall be mine for the morning."

**16:16** And Uriah the priest did according to all that King Ahaz had commanded him.

**16:17** And the king cut off the side panels of the stands and removed the laver from them; and he took down the sea from the bronze oxen that were beneath it, and placed it upon a stone base.

**16:18** And the foundation of the seat he built in the house of the Lord, and the outer entrance of the king he turned toward the house of the Lord because of the king of the Assyrians.

**16:19** Now the rest of the acts of Ahaz which he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

**16:20** And Ahaz slept with his fathers, and was buried in the City of David; and Hezekiah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **16:14** — The Greek links 'the bronze one that was before the Lord' directly to 'the altar' in verse 13 and reads 'showed it' (Greek edeixen) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has 'put it.'
- **16:15** — The Greek renders the final phrase 'for the morning' (Greek eis to proi), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonants than the Masoretic Text's 'to inquire by.'
- **16:17** — The Greek transliterates the Hebrew term for the wheeled temple stands as mechonoth.
- **16:18** — The Greek reads 'the foundation of the seat he built' and 'turned toward the house of the Lord,' whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to the Sabbath canopy that they had built and to removing the outer entrance.
