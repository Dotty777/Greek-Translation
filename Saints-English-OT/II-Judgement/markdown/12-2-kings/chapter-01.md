## Text

**1:1** And Moab rebelled against Israel after the death of Ahab.

**1:2** And Ahaziah fell through the lattice in his upper chamber in Samaria, and became ill. So he sent messengers and said to them, "Go and inquire of Baal-Fly, the god of Ekron, whether I shall recover from this illness of mine." And they went to inquire through him.

**1:3** And the angel of the Lord called Elijah the Tishbite, saying, "Arise, go to meet the messengers of Ahaziah king of Samaria, and you shall speak to them, 'Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to inquire of Baal-Fly, the god of Ekron? And it is not so,

**1:4** for thus says the Lord: The bed upon which you have gone up there—you shall not come down from it, for you shall surely die.'" And Elijah went and spoke to them.

**1:5** And the messengers returned to him, and he said to them, "Why is it that you have returned?"

**1:6** And they said to him, "A man came up to meet us and said to us, 'Go, return to the king who sent you, and speak to him: Thus says the Lord, Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to inquire of Baal-Fly, the god of Ekron? Not so! The bed upon which you have gone up—surely you shall not come down from it, for you shall surely die.'"

**1:7** And he spoke to them, "What was the manner of the man who came up to meet you and spoke these words to you?"

**1:8** And they said to him, "A hairy man, girded with a leather belt around his waist." And he said, "This is Elijah the Tishbite."

**1:9** Then he sent to him a captain of fifty with his fifty men, and he went up to him; and behold, Elijah was sitting on the top of the mountain. And the captain of fifty spoke to him and said, "Man of God, the king has called you; come down!"

**1:10** And Elijah answered and said to the captain of fifty, "And if I am a man of God, fire shall come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty men." And fire came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty men.

**1:11** And the king again sent to him another captain of fifty with his fifty men. And the captain of fifty spoke to him and said, "Man of God, thus says the king, 'Come down quickly!'"

**1:12** And Elijah answered and spoke to him and said, "If I am a man of God, fire shall come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty men." And fire came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty men.

**1:13** And the king proceeded yet again to send a commander and his fifty men. And the third captain of fifty came and bowed on his knees before Elijah, and pleaded with him and spoke to him and said, "Man of God, let my life and the life of these servants of yours be precious in your sight.

**1:14** Behold, fire has come down from heaven and consumed the first two captains of fifty; and now, I pray, let my life be precious in your sight."

**1:15** And the angel of the Lord spoke to Elijah and said, "Go down with him; do not be afraid of them." So Elijah arose and went down with him to the king.

**1:16** And Elijah spoke to him and said, "Thus says the Lord: 'Why is it that you have sent to inquire of Baal-Fly, the god of Ekron? Not so! The bed upon which you have gone up there—you shall not come down from it, for you shall surely die.'"

**1:17** So he died according to the word of the Lord which Elijah had spoken.

**1:18** Now the rest of the acts of Ahaziah which he did, behold, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel? And Joram the son of Ahab reigned over Israel in Samaria twelve years, in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah. And he did evil before the Lord, yet not like his brothers nor like his mother; and he removed the pillars of Baal which his father had made and shattered them. Nevertheless he clung to the sins of the house of Jeroboam, who made Israel sin; he did not depart from them. And the Lord was kindled in wrath against the house of Ahab.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — Greek reads Baal myian ("Baal-Fly"), translating the Hebrew name Baal-zebub, and adds the closing clause "And they went to inquire through him."
- **1:3** — Greek reads kai ouch houtos ("And it is not so"), a characteristic Septuagint rendering of Hebrew laken ("therefore") as lo ken ("not so"), which recurs in 1:6 and 1:16.
- **1:18** — The Septuagint includes an expanded accession summary of Joram here that differs in several details from 3:1-3, including "his brothers" rather than "his father" and the closing notice of the Lord's wrath against the house of Ahab.
