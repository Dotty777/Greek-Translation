## Text

**20:1** In those days Hezekiah became sick to the point of death. And Isaiah the son of Amoz, the prophet, came to him and said to him, "Thus says the Lord: 'Give charge to your house; you are dying, and you shall not live.'"

**20:2** Then Hezekiah turned toward the wall and prayed to the Lord, saying,

**20:3** "O Lord, I pray, remember now how I have walked before You in truth and with a full heart, and have done Your good in Your sight." And Hezekiah wept with a great weeping.

**20:4** And Isaiah was in the middle court, and the word of the Lord came to him, saying,

**20:5** "Return and you shall say to Hezekiah, the leader of My people, 'Thus says the Lord, the God of David your father: I have heard your prayer, I have seen your tears; behold, I will heal you. On the third day you shall go up to the house of the Lord.

**20:6** And I will add fifteen years to your days, and from the hand of the kings of the Assyrians I will deliver you and this city; and I will defend this city for My own sake and for the sake of David My servant.'"

**20:7** And he said, "Let them take a cake of figs and lay it upon the boil, and he shall recover."

**20:8** And Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "What is the sign that the Lord will heal me, and that I shall go up to the house of the Lord on the third day?"

**20:9** And Isaiah said, "This is the sign from the Lord, that the Lord will perform the word that He has spoken: shall the shadow go forward ten steps, or shall it turn back ten steps?"

**20:10** And Hezekiah said, "It is a light thing for the shadow to decline ten steps; no, rather let the shadow turn back on the stairs ten steps backward."

**20:11** And Isaiah the prophet cried out to the Lord, and the shadow turned back on the stairs ten steps backward.

**20:12** At that time Merodach-Baladan the son of Balaan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah, for he had heard that Hezekiah had been sick.

**20:13** And Hezekiah rejoiced over them, and showed them the whole treasure house—the silver and the gold, the spices and the fine oil, and the house of the vessels, and all that was found in his treasuries. There was nothing that Hezekiah did not show them in his house and in all his dominion.

**20:14** Then Isaiah the prophet came to King Hezekiah and said to him, "What did these men say, and from where have they come to you?" And Hezekiah said, "They have come to me from a far country, from Babylon."

**20:15** And he said, "What have they seen in your house?" And he said, "All that is in my house they have seen; there was nothing in my house that I did not show them, and even the things in my treasuries."

**20:16** And Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Hear the word of the Lord:

**20:17** Behold, the days are coming when all that is in your house, and all that your fathers have laid up in store until this day, shall be taken to Babylon; and not a thing shall be left, which the Lord has spoken.

**20:18** And your sons who shall go forth from you, whom you shall father, he shall take, and they shall be eunuchs in the house of the king of Babylon."

**20:19** And Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "Good is the word of the Lord which He has spoken."

**20:20** Now the rest of the acts of Hezekiah, and all his might, and all that he made—the fountain and the aqueduct, and how he brought water into the city—are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the days of the kings of Judah?

**20:21** And Hezekiah slept with his fathers, and Manasseh his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **20:4** — The Greek states simply that "Isaiah was in the middle court," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("before Isaiah had gone out of the middle court").
- **20:12** — The Greek gives the father's name as Balaan (Heb. Baladan) and transliterates Hebrew terms here ("a gift," Gk. manaan) and in v. 13 ("the treasure house," Gk. oikon tou nechotha).
- **20:17** — The Greek renders "and not a thing shall be left, which the Lord has spoken," connecting the closing attribution to "thing" (rhema) with a relative pronoun rather than as an independent clause ("says the Lord").
- **20:19** — The Septuagint ends the verse here, omitting Hezekiah's additional reflection found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("Will there not be peace and truth in my days?").
