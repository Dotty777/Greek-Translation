## Text

**4:1** Now those who afflicted Judah and Benjamin heard that the sons of the captivity were building a house for the Lord God of Israel.

**4:2** And they drew near to Zerubbabel and to the heads of the fathers' houses, and said to them, "We will build with you, for like you we seek our God, and to Him we have been sacrificing since the days of Asareathon king of Assyria, who brought us here."

**4:3** But Zerubbabel and Jeshua and the rest of the heads of the fathers' houses of Israel said to them, "It is not for us and for you to build a house for our God; for we ourselves together will build for the Lord our God, as Cyrus king of the Persians commanded us."

**4:4** Then the people of the land kept weakening the hands of the people of Judah, and they hindered them from building,

**4:5** and hiring others against them, seeking to frustrate their counsel, all the days of Cyrus king of the Persians and until the reign of Darius king of the Persians.

**4:6** And in the reign of Astherou, at the beginning of his reign, he wrote against those who dwelt in Judah and Jerusalem.

**4:7** And in the days of Asarthatha, Tabeel wrote in peace to Mithredath and to the rest of the fellow servants; to the king of the Persians the tax collector wrote a document in the Syrian tongue, and translated.

**4:8** Raoul Badatamen and Samasa the scribe wrote one letter against Jerusalem to King Arsartha.

**4:9** Thus decreed Raoum Baal and Samee the scribe and the rest of our fellow servants, the Dinaites, the Pharesthachites, the Taraphallites, the Aphrasites, the Archuites, the Babylonians, the Susunachites who are Elamites,

**4:10** and the rest of the nations whom Asennaphar the great and honorable deported and settled in the cities of Somoron and the remainder beyond the River.

**4:11** This is the copy of the letter which they sent to him: "To King Arsartha, your servants, the men beyond the River.

**4:12** Let it be known to the king that the Jews who came up from Cyrus have come to us to Jerusalem; they are building that rebellious and wicked city, and have restored its walls, and have raised up its foundations.

**4:13** Now therefore let it be known to the king that if that city is built and its walls are restored, there will be no tribute for you, nor will they pay it; and this harms kings.

**4:14** And it is not fitting for us to behold the dishonor of the king; therefore we have sent and informed the king,

**4:15** that search may be made in the book of the records of your fathers; and you will find and know that that city is a rebellious city, bringing harm to kings and provinces, and that places of refuge for slaves have been in its midst from days of old; for these reasons this city was laid waste.

**4:16** We inform the king that if that city is built and its walls are restored, there is no peace for you."

**4:17** Then the king sent peace to Raoum Balgam and Sameais the scribe and the rest of their fellow servants who dwelt in Samaria, and to the rest beyond the River, saying,

**4:18** "The tax collector whom you sent to us was summoned before me.

**4:19** And a decree was issued by me, and we searched and found that from days of old that city has lifted itself up against kings, and rebellions and places of refuge occur in it.

**4:20** And mighty kings arise over Jerusalem, ruling over all the west of the River, and full tribute and a portion are given to them.

**4:21** Now issue a decree to stop those men, and that city shall no longer be built from this decree.

**4:22** Be on your guard against showing negligence concerning this, lest destruction multiply to the harm of kings."

**4:23** Then the tax collector of King Arsartha read before Raoum and Samesa the scribe and their fellow servants; and they went in haste to Jerusalem and into Judah, and stopped them with horses and force.

**4:24** Then the work of the house of God which is in Jerusalem ceased, and it remained at a standstill until the second year of Darius king of the Persians.

## Translator Notes

- **4:2** — "Asareathon" is the Greek form corresponding to Esarhaddon; the Greek also reads "our God" rather than "your God."
- **4:6** — "Astherou" is the Greek form corresponding to Ahasuerus; the singular verb "wrote" lacks the object "an accusation" found in the Hebrew.
- **4:7** — "Asarthatha" (also spelled "Arsartha" in 4:8, 4:11, and 4:23) corresponds to Artaxerxes. The Greek renders the name Bishlam as "in peace" and translates the Aramaic term for "letter" as "the tax collector" (so also in 4:18, 4:23, and 5:5).
- **4:8** — "Raoul Badatamen" (rendered "Raoum Baal" in 4:9, "Raoum Balgam" in 4:17, and "Raoum" in 4:23) transliterates the name Rehum with his Aramaic official title "commander"; "Samasa" (4:9 "Samee," 4:17 "Sameais," 4:23 "Samesa") corresponds to Shimshai, and "Somoron" (4:10) to Samaria.
- **4:12** — The Greek reads "from Cyrus" rather than "from you," and in 4:15 (and 4:19) renders the Aramaic term for "sedition" as "places of refuge for slaves."
