## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Greek text lists ten leaders (omitting Rehum) with the distinct name forms Araiah, Marathchai, Baspham, Malsar, Batusi, and Balleia (corresponding to Seraiah, Mordecai, Bilshan, Mispar, Bigvai, and Baanah in the Hebrew Masoretic Text), and concludes with "the number of the men of Israel" rather than "the men of the people of Israel."
- **2:4** — The Greek text reads "Asaph" and "four hundred and seventy-two" (where the Masoretic Text has Shephatiah and 372), and preserves other Greek-distinct clan names and totals in this section, including Phares (Parosh), Era (Arah), Phalab-Moab (Pahath-Moab), Jobab (Joab), Malam (Elam), Zathua (Zattu), Zacchu (Zaccai), Banu (Bani), Baogei (Bigvai), Basu (Bezai), Ura (Jorah), 3,222 in v. 12 (vs. 1,222), and 92 in v. 16 (vs. 98).
- **2:21** — The Greek text reads "Barthaleem" (corresponding to Bethlehem in the Masoretic Text), alongside other distinctive Greek place-name readings in vv. 20–34: Taber (Gibbar), Asmoth (Azmaveth), Haram (Ramah), Gaithel (Bethel), Magebos (Magbish), Haroth (Hadid), and Jereia (Jericho).
- **2:31** — The Greek renders the Hebrew phrase for "the other Elam" as the single name "Elamar" and gives the number as 2,254 (vs. 1,254 in the Masoretic Text), as it also reads 625 in v. 33 (vs. 725).
- **2:37** — The Greek text uses the alphabetic numeral alpha (1,000) in vv. 37–38 and "tob" (372, vs. 392 in the Masoretic Text) in v. 58, and reads 1,007 in v. 39 (vs. 1,017) and 148 in v. 41 (vs. 128).
- **2:55** — The Greek transliterates Hebrew "abdei Shelomoh" ("servants of Solomon") as the proper names "Abdesel" (v. 55) and "Asedeselma" (v. 58), omits Sisera in v. 53, reads "the sons of the gates" in v. 42 and "Thanieim" (for Nethinim) in v. 70, and preserves distinct Greek name forms throughout vv. 36–61 (such as Jeuda, Soduia, Akum, Abau, Suthia, Kades, Soel, Keded, Gael, Reel, Phison, Bakkuk, Basadoe, Phadura, Phasrath, Haseboein, Thermeleth, Thaaresa, Charus, Lachea, Bua, Labeia, and Zarbelthei).
- **2:62** — "The Methoeseim" is a Greek transliteration of the Hebrew participle meaning "those enrolled by genealogy," and "removed" translates Greek "engchistheuthesan" (literally "treated as next of kin" from the priesthood, reflecting the translator reading the Hebrew root for "disqualified" as its homonym "redeem as kinsman").
- **2:63** — "Athersaa" transliterates the Hebrew title "hattirshata" ("the governor"), and "the Lights and the Perfections" is the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew Urim and Thummim.
- **2:65** — The Greek text reads 7,334 servants (vs. 7,337 in the Masoretic Text), "singers and songs" (Greek "oidai," where the Masoretic Text has male and female singers), 730 horses (vs. 736), 720 donkeys (vs. 6,720), and in v. 69 "pure gold, sixty-one thousand minas" (vs. gold darics).
