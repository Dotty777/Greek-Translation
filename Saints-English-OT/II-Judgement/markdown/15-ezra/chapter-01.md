## Text

**1:1** And in the first year of Cyrus the king of the Persians, that it might be fulfilled from the mouth of Jeremiah, He stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of the Persians, and he sent forth a proclamation throughout all his kingdom, and also in writing, saying,

**1:2** "Thus says Cyrus king of the Persians: The God of heaven has given to me all the kingdoms, and He Himself has charged me to build Him a house in Jerusalem, which is in Judah.

**1:3** Who is among you of all His people? And his God shall be with him, and he shall go up to Jerusalem.

**1:4** And everyone who remains from all the places where he sojourns, the men of his place shall support him with silver and gold, and with goods and livestock, together with the freewill offering for the house of the God who is in Jerusalem."

**1:5** Then the heads of the fathers' houses of Judah and Benjamin arose, and the priests and the Levites, with all whose spirit God had stirred up, to go up to build the house which is in Jerusalem.

**1:6** And all those around them strengthened their hands with vessels of silver, with gold, with goods, and with livestock, and with precious gifts, besides the freewill offerings.

**1:7** And King Cyrus brought forth the vessels of the house, which Nebuchadnezzar had taken from Jerusalem and had placed in the house of his god.

**1:8** And Cyrus king of the Persians brought them forth by the hand of Mithredath the Tasbarene, and counted them out to Sabanasar, prince of Judah.

**1:9** And this is the number of them: for the Lord, thirty of gold and one thousand of silver, twenty-nine replacements,

**1:10** thirty bowls of gold and six of silver, and one thousand other vessels.

**1:11** All the vessels of gold and of silver were five thousand four hundred, all of them going up from the captivity out of Babylon to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The Greek text omits "the word of the Lord" before "from the mouth of Jeremiah" and leaves the subject of "stirred up" implicit (referring to the Lord).
- **1:2** — The Greek text omits "of the earth" after "all the kingdoms" and "the Lord" before "God of heaven," and uses an emphatic double construction (literally "visited me upon me") for the divine commission; verse 3 likewise ends concisely at "go up to Jerusalem."
- **1:8** — "The Tasbarene" represents a Septuagint transliteration (with a letter shift from Gasbarene) of the Hebrew and Persian title for "treasurer" (gizbar), and "Sabanasar" is the Greek form corresponding to Sheshbazzar in the Hebrew text.
- **1:9** — Where the Hebrew text lists "basins" and "knives," this Greek text reads "for the Lord" before the gold and silver items and translates the following item as "replacements" (literally "changed things," deriving from an alternate sense of the Hebrew root).
- **1:10** — "Bowls" renders a Greek transliteration (kepphoures) of the Hebrew term for covered bowls, and this Greek text counts "six" silver bowls rather than the four hundred and ten of the Hebrew text.
