## Text

**1:1** There was a man from Armathaim Sipha, from Mount Ephraim, and his name was Elkanah, the son of Jeremeel, the son of Elihu, the son of Thoke, in Nasib of Ephraim.

**1:2** And he had two wives: the name of the one was Hannah, and the name of the second was Peninnah. And Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no child.

**1:3** And the man went up from year to year from his city, from Armathaim, to worship and to sacrifice to the Lord God Sabaoth at Shiloh. And there were Eli and his two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, priests of the Lord.

**1:4** And the day came when Elkanah sacrificed, and he gave portions to Peninnah his wife and to her sons;

**1:5** and to Hannah he gave one portion, because she had no child, yet Elkanah loved Hannah more than her. And the Lord had closed up her womb,

**1:6** because the Lord had not given her a child, according to her affliction and according to the despondency of her affliction. And she was despondent because of this, that the Lord had shut up her womb so as not to give her a child.

**1:7** Thus she did year by year when she went up to the house of the Lord; and she was despondent and wept, and did not eat.

**1:8** And Elkanah her husband said to her, "Hannah." And she said to him, "Behold, here I am, my lord." And he said to her, "What is the matter with you, that you weep? And why do you not eat? And why does your heart strike you? Am I not better to you than ten children?"

**1:9** And she rose up after they had eaten in Shiloh, and stood before the Lord; and Eli the priest was upon the seat by the doorposts of the temple of the Lord.

**1:10** And she was deeply grieved in soul, and prayed to the Lord, speaking, and wept.

**1:11** And she vowed a vow to the Lord, saying, "Adonai, Lord Eloi Sabaoth, if You will indeed look upon the lowliness of Your maidservant, and remember me, and give to Your maidservant male offspring, then I will give him before You as a gift until the day of his death; and wine and strong drink he shall not drink, and no iron shall come upon his head."

**1:12** And it came to pass, as she continued praying before the Lord, that Eli the priest watched her mouth.

**1:13** Now she was speaking in her heart, and her lips were moving, yet her voice was not heard. Therefore Eli considered her to be a drunken woman;

**1:14** and the young servant of Eli said to her, "How long will you be drunk? Put away your wine from yourself, and go out from the presence of the Lord."

**1:15** And Hannah answered and said, "No, my lord; I am a woman whose day is hard, and I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but I am pouring out my soul before the Lord.

**1:16** Do not count your maidservant as a worthless daughter, for out of the abundance of my brooding I have continued until now."

**1:17** And Eli answered and said to her, "Go in peace; may the God of Israel grant you every petition of yours which you have asked of Him."

**1:18** And she said, "Your maidservant has found favor in your sight." So the woman went her way, and entered into her lodging, and ate with her husband and drank, and her countenance was no longer downcast.

**1:19** And they rose early in the morning and worshiped the Lord, and went their way. And Elkanah entered into his house at Armathaim, and he knew Hannah his wife, and the Lord remembered her.

**1:20** And she conceived, and it came to pass in the course of time that she bore a son; and she called his name Samuel, and said, "Because I have asked for him from the Lord God Sabaoth."

**1:21** And the man Elkanah and all his house went up to offer in Shiloh the yearly sacrifice, and his vows, and all the tithes of his land.

**1:22** But Hannah did not go up with him, for she said to her husband, "Not until the child goes up, when I have weaned him; then he shall appear before the face of the Lord, and abide there forever."

**1:23** And Elkanah her husband said to her, "Do what is good in your sight; remain until you have weaned him; only may the Lord establish what has gone forth from your mouth." So the woman remained and nursed her son until she weaned him.

**1:24** And she went up with him to Shiloh with a three-year-old calf, and loaves of bread, and an ephah of fine flour, and a skin of wine. And she entered into the house of the Lord in Shiloh, and the child was with them.

**1:25** And they brought him before the Lord, and his father slaughtered the sacrifice which he offered from year to year to the Lord. And he brought the child forward and slaughtered the calf; and Hannah, the mother of the child, brought him to Eli,

**1:26** and said, "Oh, my lord! As your soul lives, I am the woman who stood before you, with you, when praying to the Lord.

**1:27** For this child I prayed, and the Lord has granted my petition which I asked of Him.

**1:28** Therefore I also lend him to the Lord all the days that he lives, as a loan to the Lord." And she said:

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The Greek preserves distinct forms for the place and ancestral names: Armathaim Sipha (MT: Ramathaim-zophim), Jeremeel (MT: Jeroham), Thoke (MT: Tohu), and "in Nasib of Ephraim" (MT: "son of Zuph, an Ephraimite").
- **1:5** — The Greek text states that Elkanah gave Hannah "one portion" (MT: "a double portion"), and in verse 6 attributes her despondency directly to her barrenness rather than provocation by Peninnah.
- **1:11** — Greek "Adonai, Lord Eloi Sabaoth" transliterates Hebrew divine titles ("Sabaoth" meaning "of hosts"); the LXX also explicitly includes the Nazirite vow to abstain from wine and strong drink.
- **1:14** — In the Greek text, Eli's young servant rebukes Hannah rather than Eli himself, and in verse 15 Hannah describes herself as "a woman whose day is hard" (MT: "a woman of sorrowful spirit").
- **1:24** — The Greek reads "a three-year-old calf" (MT: "three bulls"), and verse 28 ends chapter 1 with "And she said:" to introduce Hannah's prayer in 2:1.
