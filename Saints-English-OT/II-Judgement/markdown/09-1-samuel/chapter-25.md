## Text

**25:1** And Samuel died; and all Israel gathered together and lamented for him, and buried him at his house in Ramathaim. And David arose and went down to the wilderness of Maon.

**25:2** Now there was a man in Maon whose flocks were in Carmel; and the man was exceedingly great, and he had three thousand sheep and one thousand goats. And it came to pass that he was shearing his flock in Carmel.

**25:3** And the name of the man was Nabal, and the name of his wife was Abigail. And his wife was good in understanding and exceedingly fair in appearance, but the man was harsh and evil in his practices, and the man was dog-like.

**25:4** And David heard in the wilderness that Nabal the Carmelite was shearing his flock.

**25:5** So David sent ten young men, and said to the young men, "Go up to Carmel, and go to Nabal, and greet him in my name with peace,

**25:6** and you shall say thus: 'For seasons to come, may you be in health, and may your house and all that is yours be in health.

**25:7** And now, behold, I have heard that your shepherds who were with us in the wilderness are now shearing for you; and we did not hinder them, nor did we command anything of them all the days that they were in Carmel.

**25:8** Ask your young men, and they will report to you. Therefore let your young men find favor in your eyes, for we have come on a good day; give now whatever your hand may find to your son David.'"

**25:9** So the young men came and spoke these words to Nabal, even all these sayings, in the name of David. And he sprang up.

**25:10** And Nabal answered David's servants and said, "Who is David, and who is the son of Jesse? Today servants have multiplied who depart each one from the presence of his master.

**25:11** Shall I then take my bread and my wine and my meat which I have slaughtered for those who shear my sheep, and give them to men of whom I do not know where they are from?"

**25:12** So David's young men turned back on their way, and returned and came and reported to David according to these words.

**25:13** And David said to his men, "Let each man gird on his sword." And about four hundred men went up after David, while the two hundred stayed with the baggage.

**25:14** Now one of the young men reported to Abigail, Nabal's wife, saying, "Behold, David sent messengers from the wilderness to bless our master, and he turned away from them.

**25:15** Yet the men were exceedingly good to us; they did not hinder us, nor did they command us all the days that we were with them.

**25:16** And while we were in the field, they were like a wall around us both by night and by day, all the days that we were with them tending the flock.

**25:17** Now therefore, know and consider what you will do, for evil is determined against our master and against his house; and he is a pestilent son, so that one cannot speak to him."

**25:18** Then Abigail made haste and took two hundred loaves of bread, and two vessels of wine, and five prepared sheep, and five ephahs of barley meal, and one omer of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs, and loaded them on the donkeys.

**25:19** And she said to her young men, "Go on before me, and behold, I am coming after you." But she did not report it to her husband.

**25:20** And it came to pass, as she was riding on the donkey and going down under the shelter of the mountain, that behold, David and his men were coming down to meet her; and she met them.

**25:21** Now David had said, "Surely to an unjust end I have guarded all that belongs to him in the wilderness, and we gave no command to take anything of all that is his, yet he has repaid me evil for good.

**25:22** May God do so to David, and more also, if I leave of all that belongs to Nabal until morning even one who urinates against a wall."

**25:23** And when Abigail saw David, she hastened and leaped down from the donkey, and fell before David upon her face, and bowed down before him to the earth,

**25:24** at his feet, and said, "Upon me, my lord, be my iniquity! Let your maidservant speak now in your ears, and hear the word of your maidservant.

**25:25** Do not let my lord set his heart upon this pestilent man, for as his name is, so is he: Nabal is his name, and folly is with him. But I, your maidservant, did not see your young men whom you sent.

**25:26** Now therefore, my lord, as the Lord lives and as your soul lives, since the Lord has restrained you from coming to innocent blood and from saving yourself by your own hand, now let your enemies and those who seek evil against my lord become like Nabal.

**25:27** And now, receive the blessing which your maidservant has brought to my lord, and you shall give it to the young men who attend upon my lord.

**25:28** Take away, I pray, the transgression of your maidservant; for the Lord will surely make for my lord a faithful house, because the Lord fights the war of my lord, and evil shall never be found in you.

**25:29** And a man shall rise up pursuing you and seeking your life, yet the soul of my lord shall be bound in the bundle of life with the Lord God; and the soul of your enemies you shall sling in the midst of the sling.

**25:30** And it shall come to pass, when the Lord has done for my lord all the good things that He has spoken concerning you, and has appointed you as ruler over Israel,

**25:31** that this shall not be an abomination and a stumbling block to you, to my lord—to shed innocent blood without cause and for my lord to save himself by his own hand. And may the Lord do good to my lord, and you shall remember your maidservant to do good to her."

**25:32** Then David said to Abigail, "Blessed be the Lord, the God of Israel, who sent you to meet me!

**25:33** And blessed be your conduct, and blessed be you, who have restrained me today, on this day, from coming to bloodshed and from saving myself by my own hand.

**25:34** Nevertheless, as the Lord, the God of Israel lives, who has restrained me today from doing you harm, unless you had hastened and come to meet me, then I said, 'Surely there shall not be left to Nabal until the light of morning one who urinates against a wall.'"

**25:35** So David received from her hand all that she had brought him, and said to her, "Go up in peace to your house. See, I have listened to your voice and accepted your person."

**25:36** And Abigail came to Nabal, and behold, he had a king's drinking feast, and Nabal's heart was merry within him, and he was exceedingly drunk; so she did not report to him a word, small or great, until the light of morning.

**25:37** And it came to pass in the morning, when Nabal had sobered up from the wine, that his wife reported these words, and his heart died within him, and he became like a stone.

**25:38** And it came to pass after about ten days that the Lord struck Nabal, and he died.

**25:39** And David heard and said, "Blessed be the Lord, who has judged the cause of my reproach from the hand of Nabal, and has preserved His servant from the hand of evil; and the wickedness of Nabal the Lord has returned upon his own head." And David sent and spoke concerning Abigail, to take her to himself as a wife.

**25:40** And the servants of David came to Abigail at Carmel and spoke to her, saying, "David has sent us to you, to take you to himself as a wife."

**25:41** Then she arose and bowed down to the earth upon her face, and said, "Behold, let your maidservant be a handmaid to wash the feet of your servants."

**25:42** So Abigail arose and mounted the donkey, and five maidens followed her; and she went after the servants of David, and became his wife.

**25:43** David also took Ahinoam from Israel, and both of them were his wives.

**25:44** But Saul had given Michal his daughter, David's wife, to Palti the son of Amis, who was from Romma.

## Translator Notes

- **25:1** — The Septuagint reads Ramathaim (Greek Harmathaim, the dual form) rather than "Ramah," and places David in the wilderness of Maon (Greek Maan) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Paran."
- **25:3** — Greek kynikos ("dog-like") reflects reading the Hebrew consonants as kelev ("dog") rather than the gentilic "Calebite" found in the Masoretic Text.
- **25:22** — The Septuagint preserves the direct self-imprecation "to David," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads the scribal euphemism "to the enemies of David."
- **25:43** — The Septuagint reads "from Israel" (ex Israel), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "from Jezreel."
- **25:44** — The Septuagint gives the proper names Amis (Greek Ameis) and Romma, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Laish" and "Gallim."
