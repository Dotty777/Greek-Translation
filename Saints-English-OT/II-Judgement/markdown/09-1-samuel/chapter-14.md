## Text

**14:1** And a day came when Jonathan the son of Saul said to the young man who bore his armor, "Come, and let us cross over to Messab of the Philistines, which is on that other side." But he did not tell his father.

**14:2** Now Saul was sitting at the edge of the hill beneath the pomegranate tree that was in Magon, and there were with him about six hundred men.

**14:3** And Ahijah the son of Ahitub, brother of Jochabel, the son of Phinehas, the son of Levi, was priest of God in Shiloh, bearing an ephod. And the people did not know that Jonathan had departed.

**14:4** Now in the midst of the pass by which Jonathan sought to cross over to the outpost of the Philistines, there was a crag of rock on this side and a crag of rock on that side, and a tooth of rock on this side; the name of the one was Bazes, and the name of the other was Sennaar.

**14:5** The one way was from the north as one comes to Michmash, and the other way was from the south as one comes to Geba.

**14:6** Then Jonathan said to the young man who bore his armor, "Come, let us cross over to Messaph of these uncircumcised men; perhaps the Lord will act for us, for there is nothing to restrain the Lord from saving by many or by few."

**14:7** And the one who bore his armor said to him, "Do all toward which your heart inclines; behold, I am with you—as your heart is, so is my heart."

**14:8** Then Jonathan said, "Behold, we are crossing over to the men, and we will roll down toward them.

**14:9** If they say thus to us, 'Stand back there until we send word to you,' then we will stand in our own place and will by no means go up against them.

**14:10** And if they say thus to us, 'Come up to us,' then we will go up, for the Lord has delivered them into our hands; this is the sign to us."

**14:11** So both of them entered into Messaph of the Philistines. And the Philistines said, "Behold, the Hebrews are coming out of their holes where they hid themselves."

**14:12** And the men of Messaph answered Jonathan and the one who bore his armor and said, "Come up to us, and we will make a matter known to you." Then Jonathan said to the one who bore his armor, "Come up after me, for the Lord has delivered them into the hands of Israel."

**14:13** So Jonathan climbed up on his hands and on his feet, and the one who bore his armor with him. And they looked upon the face of Jonathan, and he struck them down, and the one who bore his armor was dealing further blows behind him.

**14:14** And that first slaughter which Jonathan and the one who bore his armor struck down was about twenty men, with darts and pebbles of the field.

**14:15** And there came a terror in the camp and in the field, and all the people—those in Messaph and the destroyers—were seized with terror, and they would not act; and the earth was dismayed, and there came a terror from the Lord.

**14:16** Now the watchmen of Saul in Geba of Benjamin looked, and behold, the camp was thrown into confusion on this side and on that.

**14:17** Then Saul said to the people who were with him, "Make a count now, and see who has departed from among you." And they made a count, and behold, Jonathan and the one who bore his armor were not found.

**14:18** And Saul said to Ahijah, "Bring near the ephod." For he bore the ephod on that day before Israel.

**14:19** And it came to pass, while Saul was speaking to the priest, that the uproar in the camp of the Philistines kept increasing more and more. So Saul said to the priest, "Withdraw your hands."

**14:20** Then Saul and all the people who were with him went up, and they came to the battle; and behold, every man's sword was against his neighbor, a very great confusion.

**14:21** Moreover, the servants who were with the Philistines yesterday and the day before, who had gone up into the camp, they also turned back to be with Israel, with those who were with Saul and Jonathan.

**14:22** And all Israel who were hiding in Mount Ephraim also heard that the Philistines had fled, and they too joined in pursuit after them into the battle.

**14:23** So the Lord saved Israel on that day. And the battle passed beyond Bamoth, and all the people who were with Saul were about ten thousand men; and the battle was spread to every city in Mount Ephraim.

**14:24** And Saul committed a great error of ignorance on that day, and he laid a curse upon the people, saying, "Cursed is the man who eats bread until evening, and I shall take vengeance on my enemy." So none of the people tasted bread.

**14:25** Yet all the land was taking its meal. Now Jaal was a forest of a beehive upon the face of the field.

**14:26** And the people entered the beehive, and behold, they went on speaking; and behold, there was no one returning his hand to his mouth, for the people feared the oath of the Lord.

**14:27** But Jonathan had not heard when his father put the people under an oath; therefore he stretched out the tip of his staff that was in his hand and dipped it into the honeycomb, and returned his hand to his mouth, and his eyes saw clearly.

**14:28** Then one of the people answered and said, "Your father solemnly bound the people with an oath, saying, 'Cursed is the man who eats bread today.'" And the people grew faint.

**14:29** And Jonathan understood and said, "My father has undone the land! See how my eyes saw clearly because I tasted a little of this honey.

**14:30** How much more if the people had indeed eaten today of the spoil of their enemies which they found! For now the slaughter among the Philistines would have been greater."

**14:31** And on that day he struck down some of the Philistines in Michmash; and the people became exceedingly weary.

**14:32** Then the people turned aside to the spoil, and the people took flocks and herds and calves, and slaughtered them on the ground; and the people ate them with the blood.

**14:33** And it was told to Saul, saying, "The people have sinned against the Lord by eating with the blood." Then Saul said, "In Getthaim, roll a great stone to me here."

**14:34** And Saul said, "Disperse yourselves among the people, and tell them to bring here each man his calf and each man his own sheep, and let him slaughter it upon this, and you shall by no means sin against the Lord by eating with the blood." So all the people brought, each man what was in his hand, and they slaughtered them there.

**14:35** And Saul built an altar there to the Lord; in this Saul began to build an altar to the Lord.

**14:36** Then Saul said, "Let us go down after the Philistines by night, and let us plunder among them until day dawns, and let us not leave a man among them." And they said, "Do all that is good in your sight." But the priest said, "Let us draw near here to God."

**14:37** So Saul inquired of God, "Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will You deliver them into the hands of Israel?" But He did not answer him on that day.

**14:38** Then Saul said, "Bring near here all the cornerstones of Israel, and know and see in whom this sin has occurred today;

**14:39** for as the Lord lives, who saved Israel, even if the answer is against Jonathan my son, he shall surely die." And there was no one who answered from among all the people.

**14:40** Then he said to all Israel, "You shall be for servitude, and I and Jonathan my son shall be for servitude." And the people said to Saul, "Do what is good in your sight."

**14:41** And Saul said, "O Lord God of Israel, why have You not answered Your servant today? Is the iniquity in me or in Jonathan my son? O Lord God of Israel, give clear signs; and if it says thus, give now to Your people Israel, give now holiness." And Jonathan and Saul were taken by lot, and the people went out.

**14:42** Then Saul said, "Cast lots between me and Jonathan my son; whomever the Lord chooses by lot, let him die." And the people said to Saul, "This thing is not to be!" Yet Saul prevailed over the people, and they cast lots between him and Jonathan his son, and Jonathan was taken by lot.

**14:43** Then Saul said to Jonathan, "Tell me what you have done." And Jonathan told him and said, "I certainly tasted a little honey with the tip of the staff that was in my hand; behold, I must die."

**14:44** And Saul said to him, "May God do so to me, and more also, for you shall surely die today."

**14:45** Then the people said to Saul, "Shall he be put to death today who has accomplished this great salvation in Israel? As the Lord lives, not a hair of his head shall fall to the ground, for the people of God have brought about this day." So the people prayed for Jonathan on that day, and he did not die.

**14:46** Then Saul went up from pursuing after the Philistines, and the Philistines departed to their own place.

**14:47** And Saul obtained by lot to reign—he was allotted the work over Israel—and he fought on every side against all his enemies: against Moab, and against the sons of Ammon, and against the sons of Edom, and against Baitheor, and against the king of Zobah, and against the Philistines; wherever he turned, he was saved.

**14:48** And he gathered a force of Aunanein, and struck down Amalek, and delivered Israel from the hand of those who trampled him.

**14:49** Now the sons of Saul were Jonathan, and Jessiul, and Malchishua. And the names of his two daughters: the name of the firstborn was Merab, and the name of the second was Michal.

**14:50** And the name of his wife was Ahinoam, the daughter of Ahinaas. And the name of the commander of the army was Abner, the son of Neri, the son of a kinsman of Saul.

**14:51** And Kish was the father of Saul, and Ner the father of Abner was the son of Jamin, the son of Abier.

**14:52** And the war against the Philistines was fierce all the days of Saul; and when Saul saw every mighty man and every man who was a son of strength, he gathered them to himself.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — The Greek text transliterates the Hebrew word for "garrison/outpost" as a place name, "Messab" here and "Messaph" in 14:6, 11, 12, and 15. Other distinctive Septuagint proper names in the opening verses include Magon (v. 2), Jochabel and Levi (v. 3), and Bazes and Sennaar (v. 4).
- **14:14** — The Septuagint reads "with darts and pebbles of the field," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text describes an area of land ("within about half a furrow of an acre of land").
- **14:18** — The Greek text reads "the ephod" twice here (consistent with Ahijah bearing the ephod in 14:3), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the ark of God."
- **14:41** — The Septuagint preserves a longer prayer of Saul invoking the Urim ("clear signs," Greek delous) and Thummim ("holiness," Greek hosioteta), following the distinctive Greek reading "for servitude" in 14:40.
- **14:48** — "Aunanein" renders an obscure untranslated word unique to the Codex Vaticanus Greek text. Other Septuagint-distinct names in the closing summary include Baitheor (v. 47), Jessiul (v. 49), Ahinaas and Neri (v. 50), and Jamin and Abier (v. 51).
