## Text

**19:1** And Saul spoke to Jonathan his son and to all his servants, to put David to death.

**19:2** Now Jonathan, the son of Saul, delighted exceedingly in David; and Jonathan told David, saying, "Saul seeks to put you to death. Therefore be on your guard tomorrow morning, and hide yourself and sit in secret.

**19:3** And I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where you are, and I will speak concerning you to my father; and I will see whatever may be, and I will tell you."

**19:4** And Jonathan spoke good things concerning David to Saul his father, and said to him, "Do not let the king sin against your servant David, because he has not sinned against you, and his deeds are exceedingly good.

**19:5** And he put his life in his hand and struck down the foreigner, and the Lord wrought a great deliverance, and all Israel saw and rejoiced. Why then do you sin against innocent blood, to put David to death without cause?"

**19:6** And Saul heeded the voice of Jonathan, and Saul swore, saying, "As the Lord lives, he shall not die."

**19:7** So Jonathan called David and told him all these words. And Jonathan brought David in to Saul, and he was before him as yesterday and the day before.

**19:8** And war came again against David; and David prevailed and fought against the foreigners, and struck them with an exceedingly great blow, and they fled from before his face.

**19:9** And an evil spirit of God came upon Saul while he was sleeping in the house, and a spear was in his hand, and David was playing music with his hands.

**19:10** And Saul sought to strike the spear into David, but David turned aside from the presence of Saul; and he drove the spear into the wall, and David withdrew and escaped safely.

**19:11** And it came to pass in that night that Saul sent messengers to David's house to watch him, in order to put him to death in the morning. And Michal his wife told David, saying, "If you do not save your own life tonight, tomorrow you shall be put to death."

**19:12** So Michal let David down through the window, and he went away and fled and escaped safely.

**19:13** And Michal took the cenotaphs and laid them upon the bed, and she placed a liver of goats at its head, and covered them with a garment.

**19:14** And Saul sent messengers to take David, and they said that he was ill.

**19:15** And he sent for David, saying, "Bring him on the bed to me, to put him to death."

**19:16** And the messengers came, and behold, the cenotaphs were upon the bed, and a liver of goats was at its head.

**19:17** And Saul said to Michal, "Why have you deceived me in this way, and sent away my enemy so that he escaped safely?" And Michal said to Saul, "He himself said, 'Send me away; otherwise, I will put you to death.'"

**19:18** So David fled and escaped safely, and he came to Samuel in Ramathaim and told him all that Saul had done to him. And David and Samuel went and dwelt in Auath in Ramah.

**19:19** And it was reported to Saul, saying, "Behold, David is in Auath in Ramah."

**19:20** And Saul sent messengers to take David; and they saw the assembly of the prophets, and Samuel was standing, appointed over them. And the Spirit of God came upon the messengers of Saul, and they prophesied.

**19:21** And it was reported to Saul, and he sent other messengers, and they also prophesied. And Saul again sent a third company of messengers, and they also prophesied.

**19:22** Then Saul was furious with wrath, and he himself also went to Ramathaim; and he came as far as the well of the threshing floor that is in Sephei, and he inquired and said, "Where are Samuel and David?" And one said, "Behold, they are in Auath in Ramah."

**19:23** So he went from there to Auath in Ramah, and the Spirit of God came upon him also; and he went along prophesying until he came to Auath in Ramah.

**19:24** And he stripped off his garments and prophesied before them, and he fell down naked all that day and all that night. Therefore they said, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

## Translator Notes

- **19:5** — Greek allophylos ("foreigner") is the Septuagint's regular designation for the Philistines; the Greek also reads "and all Israel saw and rejoiced" rather than addressing Saul directly as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **19:9** — The Greek reads katheudon ("sleeping"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "sitting."
- **19:13** — The Greek renders the household images (teraphim) as kenotaphia ("cenotaphs," i.e., funerary effigies) and reads "a liver of goats" (hepar ton aigon) rather than "a pillow of goat's hair."
- **19:18** — The Septuagint distinguishes "Ramathaim" (Greek Harmathaim) from "Auath in Ramah," the latter corresponding to "Naioth in Ramah" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **19:22** — The Greek reads "the well of the threshing floor that is in Sephei," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "the great well that is in Secu."
