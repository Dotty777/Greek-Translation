## Text

**24:1** And David arose from there and dwelt in the narrow passes of En Gedi.

**24:2** Now it came to pass, when Saul returned from following after the foreigners, that it was reported to him, saying, "David is in the wilderness of En Gedi."

**24:3** And he took with him three thousand chosen men from all Israel, and went to seek David and his men before Eddaiem.

**24:4** And he came to the herds of the flocks that were by the way, and there was a cave there; and Saul went in to prepare himself, while David and his men were sitting in the inner part of the cave.

**24:5** Then the men of David said to him, "Behold, this is the day of which the Lord spoke to you, to deliver your enemy into your hands, and you shall do to him as seems good in your eyes." And David arose and secretly cut off the corner of Saul's mantle.

**24:6** And it came to pass afterward that David's heart struck him because he had cut off the corner of his mantle.

**24:7** And David said to his men, "Far be it from me before the Lord, that I should do this thing to the Lord's anointed, to lay my hand against him, for he is the Lord's anointed."

**24:8** So David persuaded his men with words, and did not permit them to rise up and slay Saul. And Saul arose and went down the way.

**24:9** And David arose after him out of the cave, and David cried out after Saul, saying, "O lord, O king!" And Saul looked behind him, and David stooped with his face to the earth and bowed down before him.

**24:10** And David said to Saul, "Why do you listen to the words of the people who say, 'Behold, David seeks your life'?

**24:11** Behold, on this day your eyes have seen how the Lord delivered you today into my hand in the cave, and I was not willing to kill you, and I spared you and said, 'I will not lay my hand against my lord, for he is the Lord's anointed.'

**24:12** And behold, the corner of your mantle is in my hand; I have cut off the corner and have not killed you. Know and see today that there is no evil in my hand, nor ungodliness and rebellion, and I have not sinned against you; yet you bind my life to take it.

**24:13** May the Lord judge between me and you, and may the Lord avenge me of you; yet my hand shall not be against you.

**24:14** As the ancient proverb says, 'From the lawless shall come forth transgression'; yet my hand shall not be against you.

**24:15** And now, after whom do you go out, O king of Israel? After whom do you pursue? After a dead dog, and after a single flea!

**24:16** May the Lord be judge and arbiter between me and you; may the Lord see and judge my cause, and render judgment for me out of your hand."

**24:17** And it came to pass, when David had finished speaking these words to Saul, that Saul said, "Is this your voice, child David?" And Saul lifted up his voice and wept.

**24:18** And Saul said to David, "You are more righteous than I, for you have repaid me with good, whereas I have repaid you with evil.

**24:19** And you have declared to me today the good that you have done for me, how the Lord shut me up today into your hands and you did not kill me.

**24:20** For if anyone should find his enemy in distress and send him away on a good road, the Lord also will repay him with good, even as you have done today.

**24:21** And now, behold, I know that you shall surely reign, and the kingdom of Israel shall be established in your hands.

**24:22** Now therefore, swear to me by the Lord that you will not destroy my seed after me, and that you will not blot out my name from the house of my father."

**24:23** So David swore to Saul. And Saul went away to his own place, while David and his men went up to the narrow pass of Messara.

## Translator Notes

- **24:3** — The Septuagint renders the place name as Eddaiem, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Rocks of the Wild Goats."
- **24:4** — Greek paraskeuasasthai ("to prepare himself") is the Septuagint's modest euphemism for relieving oneself, corresponding to the Hebrew idiom "to cover his feet."
- **24:20** — The Septuagint reads a conditional statement including "in distress" (en thlipsei), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text frames the clause as a rhetorical question.
- **24:23** — The Septuagint preserves the proper name Messara ("the narrow pass of Messara"), corresponding to the common noun "the stronghold" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
