## Text

**4:1** And it came to pass in those days that foreigners gathered together for war against Israel; and Israel went out to meet them for battle, and they encamped at Ebenezer, while the foreigners encamped in Aphek.

**4:2** And the foreigners drew up in battle array against Israel. And the battle turned, and the men of Israel were defeated before the foreigners, and four thousand men were struck down in the battle line in the field.

**4:3** And the people came into the camp, and the elders of Israel said, "Why has the Lord defeated us today before the foreigners? Let us take the ark of our God from Shiloh, and let it go forth from our midst, and it shall save you from the hand of our enemies."

**4:4** So the people sent to Shiloh, and they brought from there the ark of the Lord who is enthroned upon the cherubim. And both of the sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were with the ark.

**4:5** And it came to pass, when the ark of the Lord came into the camp, that all Israel shouted with a loud voice, and the earth resounded.

**4:6** And the foreigners heard the cry, and the foreigners said, "What is this great cry in the camp of the Hebrews?" And they learned that the ark of the Lord had come into the camp.

**4:7** And the foreigners were afraid and said, "These gods have come to them in the camp! Woe to us! Deliver us, O Lord, today, for such a thing has not happened yesterday or the day before.

**4:8** Woe to us! Who shall deliver us from the hand of these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck Egypt with every plague and in the wilderness.

**4:9** Be strong and be men, and fight against them!"

**4:10** So they fought against them, and the men of Israel were defeated, and every man fled to his tent. And there was an exceedingly great slaughter, and thirty thousand of the ranks of Israel fell.

**4:11** And the ark of God was captured, and both of the sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, died.

**4:12** And a Jeminite man ran from the battle line and came to Shiloh on that day, with his garments torn and earth upon his head.

**4:13** And he came, and behold, Eli was on his seat beside the gate, watching the road, for his heart was in great anxiety concerning the ark of God. And the man entered the city to tell the news, and the city cried out aloud.

**4:14** And Eli heard the sound of the outcry and said, "What is the outcry of this sound?" And the man made haste, came in, and reported to Eli.

**4:15** Now Eli was ninety years old, and his eyes were fixed so that he could not see. And Eli said to the men who were standing around him, "What is the sound of this uproar?"

**4:16** And the man made haste, came up to Eli, and said to him, "I am the one who has come from the camp, and I have fled from the battle line today." And he said, "What is the thing that has happened, child?"

**4:17** And the young man answered and said, "The men of Israel have fled before the foreigners, and there has been a great slaughter among the people, and both of your sons have died, and the ark of God has been captured."

**4:18** And it came to pass, when he made mention of the ark of God, that he fell backward from the seat beside the gate, and his back was broken and he died, for the man was old and heavy. And he had judged Israel twenty years.

**4:19** Now his daughter-in-law, the wife of Phinehas, was with child, near the time to give birth; and she heard the report that the ark of God was captured and that her father-in-law and her husband were dead, and she wept and gave birth, for her labor pains came upon her.

**4:20** And in her hour she was dying. And the women who stood beside her said to her, "Do not fear, for you have borne a son." But she did not answer, nor did her heart take heed.

**4:21** And she named the boy Woe Barchaboth, on account of the ark of God, and on account of her father-in-law, and on account of her husband.

**4:22** And they said, "The glory of Israel has been carried into exile, because the ark of the Lord has been captured."

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Greek allophyloi ("foreigners") is the Septuagint's standard designation for the Philistines throughout 1–4 Kingdoms.
- **4:12** — Greek Iemeinaios ("Jeminite") denotes a man of the tribe of Benjamin.
- **4:15** — Greek gives Eli's age as ninety years and adds his question to the men standing around him, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text gives ninety-eight years.
- **4:18** — Greek states that Eli's back was broken (Hebrew: his neck) and that he had judged Israel twenty years (Hebrew Masoretic Text: forty years).
- **4:21** — Greek renders the child's name as Ouai Barchaboth ("Woe Barchaboth") rather than the Hebrew Ichabod, and in verse 22 attributes the lament to a plural subject ("they said").
