## Text

**11:1** And it came to pass, about a month later, that Nahash the Ammonite went up and encamped against Jabesh Gilead; and all the men of Jabesh said to Nahash the Ammonite, "Make a covenant with us, and we will serve you."

**11:2** And Nahash the Ammonite said to them, "On this condition I will make a covenant with you: by gouging out every right eye among you, and I will lay a reproach upon Israel."

**11:3** And the men of Jabesh said to him, "Grant us seven days, and we will send messengers into all the territory of Israel; and if there is no one to save us, we will come out to you."

**11:4** Then the messengers came to Gibeah to Saul and spoke the words in the ears of the people; and all the people lifted up their voice and wept.

**11:5** And behold, Saul was coming after the morning from the field; and Saul said, "Why is it that the people are weeping?" And they related to him the words of the sons of Jabesh.

**11:6** Then the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon Saul when he heard these words, and his wrath was exceedingly kindled against them.

**11:7** And he took two oxen and cut them in pieces, and sent them into all the territory of Israel by the hand of messengers, saying, "Whoever does not go out after Saul and after Samuel, thus shall they do to his oxen." And the dread of the Lord came upon the people of Israel, and they cried out as one man.

**11:8** And he inspected them at Abiezek in Bama: every man of Israel, six hundred thousand, and the men of Judah, seventy thousand.

**11:9** And he said to the messengers who had come, "Thus you shall say to the men of Jabesh: 'Tomorrow deliverance shall be yours when the sun grows hot.'" So the messengers came into the city and reported it to the men of Jabesh, and they rejoiced.

**11:10** Therefore the men of Jabesh said to Nahash the Ammonite, "Tomorrow we will come out to you, and you shall do to us whatever is good in your sight."

**11:11** And it came to pass on the next day that Saul divided the people into three companies; and they entered the midst of the camp in the morning watch, and struck down the sons of Ammon until the day grew hot. And it happened that those who remained were scattered, so that not two of them were left together.

**11:12** Then the people said to Samuel, "Who is he who said that Saul shall not reign over us? Deliver up the men, and we will put them to death."

**11:13** But Samuel said, "No one shall be put to death on this day, for today the Lord has accomplished deliverance in Israel."

**11:14** Then Samuel said to the people, "Let us go to Gilgal and renew the kingdom there."

**11:15** So all the people went to Gilgal, and Samuel anointed Saul there as king before the Lord in Gilgal, and he offered there sacrifices and peace offerings before the Lord; and Samuel and all Israel rejoiced exceedingly.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — Greek begins with "about a month later" (hos meta mena), matching Dead Sea Scrolls evidence, whereas the Masoretic Text lacks this phrase and ends 10:27 with Saul holding his peace.
- **11:5** — Greek reads "after the morning" (meta to proi), reading the Hebrew consonants as "morning" rather than "oxen" as in the Masoretic Text.
- **11:8** — Greek names the muster site "Abiezek in Bama" and numbers Israel at six hundred thousand and Judah at seventy thousand (MT: Bezek; three hundred thousand and thirty thousand).
- **11:13** — Greek attributes the refusal to execute anyone to Samuel rather than Saul (MT), and in 11:15 likewise states that Samuel anointed Saul as king, offered the sacrifices, and rejoiced with all Israel.
