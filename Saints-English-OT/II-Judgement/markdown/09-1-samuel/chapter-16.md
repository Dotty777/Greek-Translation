## Text

**16:1** And the Lord said to Samuel, "How long will you mourn over Saul, seeing I have rejected him from reigning over Israel? Fill your horn with oil, and come, I will send you to Jesse even to Bethlehem; for I have seen among his sons one to reign for Me."

**16:2** And Samuel said, "How can I go? For Saul will hear and slay me." And the Lord said, "Take a heifer from the herd in your hand, and you shall say, 'I have come to sacrifice to the Lord.'

**16:3** And you shall invite Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will make known to you what you shall do; and you shall anoint him whom I tell you."

**16:4** And Samuel did all that the Lord had spoken to him, and he came to Bethlehem. And the elders of the city were alarmed at meeting him, and they said, "Is your coming in peace, O seer?"

**16:5** And he said, "In peace; I have come to sacrifice to the Lord. Sanctify yourselves and rejoice with me today." And he sanctified Jesse and his sons, and invited them to the sacrifice.

**16:6** And it came to pass as they entered that he saw Eliab, and he said, "Surely His anointed is before the Lord."

**16:7** And the Lord said to Samuel, "Do not look upon his appearance nor upon the stature of his height, for I have rejected him; for God does not see as a man beholds, because man looks upon the face, but God looks upon the heart."

**16:8** And Jesse called Amminadab, and he passed before Samuel; and he said, "Neither has God chosen this one."

**16:9** And Jesse brought Shammah forward; and he said, "Neither has the Lord chosen this one."

**16:10** Thus Jesse brought his seven sons before Samuel; and Samuel said, "The Lord has not chosen among these."

**16:11** And Samuel said to Jesse, "Are there no more of the young men?" And he said, "There is still the youngest; behold, he is tending the flock." And Samuel said to Jesse, "Send and bring him, for we will by no means recline at table until he comes."

**16:12** So he sent and brought him in. Now he was ruddy, with beauty of eyes, and good in appearance to the Lord. And the Lord said to Samuel, "Arise and anoint David, for this one is good."

**16:13** And Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brothers; and the Spirit of the Lord leaped upon David from that day onward. Then Samuel arose and departed to Armathaim.

**16:14** Now the Spirit of the Lord departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the Lord choked him.

**16:15** And the servants of Saul said to him, "Behold now, an evil spirit of the Lord is choking you.

**16:16** Let your servants now speak before you, and let them seek out for our lord a man who knows how to play upon the lyre; and it shall be, whenever an evil spirit is upon you, that he will play upon his lyre, and it shall be well with you, and it will give you rest."

**16:17** And Saul said to his servants, "Provide for me now a man who plays skillfully, and bring him in to me."

**16:18** Then one of his young men answered and said, "Behold, I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite who knows how to play music; and the man is prudent, and a warrior, and wise in speech, and a man of handsome appearance, and the Lord is with him."

**16:19** Therefore Saul sent messengers to Jesse, saying, "Send to me your son David, who is with your flock."

**16:20** And Jesse took a homer of bread, and a skin of wine, and one kid of the goats, and sent them by the hand of his son David to Saul.

**16:21** And David came in to Saul and stood before him; and he loved him exceedingly, and he became his armor-bearer.

**16:22** And Saul sent to Jesse, saying, "Please let David stand before me, for he has found favor in my sight."

**16:23** And it came to pass, whenever an evil spirit was upon Saul, that David would take the lyre and play with his hand; and Saul would be refreshed and it was well with him, and the evil spirit would depart from him.

## Translator Notes

- **16:8** — The Greek reads "Amminadab" where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Abinadab."
- **16:12** — The Greek specifies that David was pleasing in appearance "to the Lord," names David directly in the Lord's command, and states "for this one is good."
- **16:14** — The Greek verb literally means "to choke" or "suffocate" (also in 16:15), vividly expressing the torment caused by the evil spirit.
- **16:20** — The Greek reads "a homer of bread" (a dry measure), reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew consonants rendered "a donkey laden with bread" in the Masoretic Text.
