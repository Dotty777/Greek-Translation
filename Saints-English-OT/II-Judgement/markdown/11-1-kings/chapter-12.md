## Text

**12:1** And King Rehoboam went to Shechem, for all Israel were coming to Shechem to make him king.

**12:3** And the people spoke to King Rehoboam, saying,

**12:4** "Your father made our yoke heavy; now therefore, lighten the harsh servitude of your father, and his heavy yoke which he laid upon us, and we will serve you."

**12:5** And he said to them, "Depart for three days, and return to me." So they departed.

**12:6** And the king summoned the elders who had stood before Solomon his father while he was still alive, saying, "How do you advise that I answer this people a word?"

**12:7** And they spoke to him, saying, "If on this day you will be a servant to this people, and serve them, and speak good words to them, then they will be your servants all the days."

**12:8** But he forsook the counsel of the elders which they had counseled him, and took counsel with the young men who had grown up with him, who stood before his presence,

**12:9** and he said to them, "What do you advise? And what shall I answer this people who have spoken to me, saying, 'Lighten the yoke which your father laid upon us'?"

**12:10** And the young men who had grown up with him, who stood before his presence, spoke to him, saying, "Thus you shall speak to this people who have spoken to you, saying, 'Your father made our yoke heavy, now therefore lighten it from us'—thus you shall speak to them: 'My littleness is thicker than my father's loins.

**12:11** And now, whereas my father loaded you with a heavy yoke, I also will add to your yoke; my father chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scorpions.'"

**12:12** So all Israel came to King Rehoboam on the third day, just as the king had spoken to them, saying, "Return to me on the third day."

**12:13** And the king answered the people harshly, and Rehoboam forsook the counsel of the elders which they had counseled him,

**12:14** and spoke to them according to the counsel of the young men, saying, "My father made your yoke heavy, and I will add to your yoke; my father chastised you with whips, and I will chastise you with scorpions."

**12:15** So the king did not listen to the people, for the turn of events was from the Lord, that He might establish His word which He had spoken by the hand of Ahijah the Shilonite concerning Jeroboam the son of Nebat.

**12:16** And all Israel saw that the king did not listen to them; and the people answered the king, saying, "What portion do we have in David? We have no inheritance in the son of Jesse! Depart, O Israel, to your tents! Now shepherd your own house, David!" So Israel departed to their tents.

**12:18** Then the king sent Aram, who was over the tribute, and they stoned him with stones, and he died; and King Rehoboam hastened to go up in order to flee to Jerusalem.

**12:19** So Israel rebelled against the house of David to this day.

**12:20** And it came to pass, when all Israel heard that Rehoboam had returned, that they sent and called him to the assembly, and made him king over Israel; and there was none following David except the tribe of Judah and Benjamin alone.

**12:21** And Rehoboam entered into Jerusalem, and convened the assembly of Judah and the tribe of Benjamin, one hundred and twenty thousand young men who made war, to fight against the house of Israel, to restore the kingdom to Rehoboam the son of Solomon.

**12:22** But the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah the man of God, saying,

**12:23** "Speak to Rehoboam the son of Solomon, king of Judah, and to all the house of Judah and Benjamin, and to the remnant of the people, saying,

**12:24** 'Thus says the Lord: You shall not go up nor fight against your brethren, the children of Israel; let every man return to his own house, for this matter has come from Me.'" And they listened to the word of the Lord, and ceased from going, according to the word of the Lord. And King Solomon slept with his fathers, and was buried with his fathers in the city of David; and Rehoboam his son reigned in his place in Jerusalem, being sixteen years old when he began to reign, and he reigned twelve years in Jerusalem. And his mother's name was Naanan, the daughter of Ana the son of Nahash, king of Ammon; and he did evil in the sight of the Lord and did not walk in the way of David his father. And there was a man from Mount Ephraim, a servant to Solomon, and his name was Jeroboam, and his mother's name was Sarisa, a harlot; and Solomon appointed him as commander of the staff over the levies of the house of Joseph. And Solomon built Sarira which is in Mount Ephraim, and he had three hundred chariots of horses; this man built the citadel by the levies of the house of Ephraim; this man enclosed the city of David, and he was lifting himself up against the kingdom. And Solomon sought to put him to death; and he was afraid and fled to Susakim king of Egypt, and was with him until Solomon died. And Jeroboam heard in Egypt that Solomon was dead, and he spoke in the ears of Susakim king of Egypt, saying, "Send me away, and I will depart to my own land." And Susakim said to him, "Ask any request, and I will give it to you." And Susakim gave to Jeroboam Ano, the elder sister of Thekemina his wife, to be his wife; she was great in the midst of the daughters of the king, and she bore to Jeroboam Abijah his son. And Jeroboam said to Susakim, "Indeed, send me away, and I will depart." So Jeroboam went out of Egypt, and came to the land of Sarira which is in Mount Ephraim; and all the tribe of Ephraim gathered there, and Jeroboam built a palisade there. And his child fell sick with an exceedingly severe sickness; and Jeroboam went to inquire concerning the child; and he said to Ano his wife, "Arise, go; inquire of God concerning the child, whether he will live from his sickness." Now there was a man in Shiloh whose name was Ahijah, and he was sixty years old, and the word of the Lord was with him. And Jeroboam said to his wife, "Arise and take in your hand for the man of God bread, and cakes for his children, and grapes, and a jar of honey." And the woman arose, and took in her hand bread and two cakes and grapes and a jar of honey for Ahijah; and the man was aged, and his eyes had grown dim so that he could not see. And she arose from Sarira and went; and it came to pass, when she came to the city to Ahijah the Shilonite, that Ahijah said to his servant lad, "Go out now to meet Ano the wife of Jeroboam, and you shall say to her, 'Come in and do not stand still, for thus says the Lord: Hard things I am sending upon you.'" And Ano went in to the man of God, and Ahijah said to her, "Why have you brought me bread and grapes and cakes and a jar of honey? Thus says the Lord: Behold, you shall depart from me, and it shall come to pass, when you enter the gate into Sarira, that your maidens will come out to meet you and will say to you, 'The child is dead.' For thus says the Lord: Behold, I will utterly destroy from Jeroboam him who urinates against a wall; and those of Jeroboam who die in the city the dogs shall devour, and him who dies in the field the birds of the air shall devour. And the child shall be mourned with, 'Alas, lord!' because a good word concerning the Lord has been found in him." And the woman departed when she heard it; and it came to pass, as she entered into Sarira, that the child died, and the outcry went forth to meet her. And Jeroboam went to Shechem, which is in Mount Ephraim, and gathered there the tribes of Israel, and Rehoboam the son of Solomon went up there. And the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah the Enlamite, saying, "Take for yourself a new garment which has not gone into water, and tear it into twelve pieces; and you shall give them to Jeroboam, and you shall say to him, 'Thus says the Lord: Take for yourself twelve pieces to clothe yourself.'" And Jeroboam took them; and Shemaiah said, "Thus says the Lord concerning the ten tribes of Israel." And the people said to Rehoboam the son of Solomon, "Your father made his yoke heavy upon us, and made heavy the provisions of his table; now therefore lighten it upon us, and we will serve you." And Rehoboam said to the people, "Yet three days, and I will answer you a word." And Rehoboam said, "Bring in the elders to me, and I will take counsel with them what word I shall answer the people on the third day." And Rehoboam spoke in their ears just as the people had sent word to him; and the elders of the people said, "Thus the people have spoken to you." And Rehoboam set aside their counsel, and it did not please him; and he sent and brought in his companions, and spoke the same things to them: "These things also the people sent word to me." And his companions said, "Thus you shall speak to the people, saying, 'My littleness is thicker than my father's loins; my father scourged you with whips, but I will rule over you with scorpions.'" And the word pleased Rehoboam; and he answered the people just as his companions, the young men, had counseled him. And all the people spoke as one man, each to his neighbor, and they all cried out, saying, "We have no portion in David, nor inheritance in the son of Jesse! To your tents, O Israel! For this man is neither for a ruler nor for a leader!" And all the people were scattered from Shechem, and every man departed to his tent. And Rehoboam exerted himself, and departed and mounted his chariot and entered into Jerusalem; and all the tribe of Judah and all the tribe of Benjamin went after him. And it came to pass at the turn of the year that Rehoboam gathered every man of Judah and Benjamin, and went up to fight against Jeroboam at Shechem. And the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah the man of God: "Speak to Rehoboam king of Judah, and to all the house of Judah and Benjamin, and to the remnant of the people, saying, 'Thus says the Lord: You shall not go up nor fight against your brethren, the children of Israel; return every man to his own house, for this matter has come from Me.'" And they listened to the word of the Lord, and refrained from going, according to the word of the Lord.

**12:25** Then Jeroboam built Shechem in Mount Ephraim, and dwelt in it; and he went out from there and built Penuel.

**12:26** And Jeroboam said in his heart, "Behold, now the kingdom will return to the house of David.

**12:27** If this people goes up to offer sacrifice in the house of the Lord at Jerusalem, then the heart of the people will turn back to the Lord and to their lord, to Rehoboam king of Judah, and they will slay me."

**12:28** And the king took counsel, and went and made two golden heifers, and said to the people, "Let it be enough for you to go up to Jerusalem! Behold your gods, O Israel, which brought you up out of the land of Egypt!"

**12:29** And he set the one in Bethel, and the other he placed in Dan.

**12:30** Now this thing became a sin; and the people went before the one, even as far as Dan.

**12:31** And he made houses upon high places, and made priests from a portion of the people who were not of the sons of Levi.

**12:32** And Jeroboam ordained a feast in the eighth month, on the fifteenth day of the month, like the feast that was in the land of Judah, and he went up to the altar which he had made in Bethel to sacrifice to the heifers which he had made; and he stationed in Bethel the priests of the high places which he had made.

**12:33** And he went up to the altar which he had made on the fifteenth day in the eighth month, in the feast which he had devised from his own heart; and he ordained a feast for the children of Israel, and he went up to the altar to offer sacrifice.

## Translator Notes

- **12:18** — The Greek names the officer over the tribute Aram (Hebrew Adoram) and does not mention a chariot in Rehoboam's escape.
- **12:20** — The Greek text explicitly reads "Rehoboam" here (where the Hebrew and narrative context have "Jeroboam"), and specifies "the tribe of Judah and Benjamin alone" rather than Judah alone.
- **12:24** — Following the standard verse 24, the Greek includes an extensive alternative narrative (often designated 12:24a–z) detailing Rehoboam's reign (age sixteen, reigning twelve years; mother Naanan), Jeroboam's mother Sarisa, his marriage to Ano (sister of Thekemina), the death of his child Abijah, and a garment-tearing sign performed by Shemaiah the Enlamite ("twelve pieces" concerning "the ten tribes").
- **12:28** — The Greek reads "two golden heifers" (damaleis) rather than "calves," and in verse 27 explicitly names both "the Lord" (God) and "their lord" (Rehoboam).
