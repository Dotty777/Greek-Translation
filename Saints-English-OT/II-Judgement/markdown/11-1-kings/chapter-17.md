## Text

**17:1** And Elijah the prophet, the Tishbite from Tishbe of Gilead, said to Ahab, "As the Lord, the God of hosts, the God of Israel, lives, before whom I have stood, there shall not be dew or rain these years, except by the mouth of my word."

**17:2** And the word of the Lord came to Elijah:

**17:3** "Depart from here toward the east, and hide yourself by the brook Cherith, which is before the Jordan.

**17:4** And it shall be that you shall drink water from the brook, and I will command the ravens to sustain you there."

**17:5** And Elijah did according to the word of the Lord, and dwelt by the brook Cherith before the Jordan.

**17:6** And the ravens brought him loaves of bread in the morning and meat in the evening, and he drank water from the brook.

**17:7** And it came to pass after some days that the brook dried up, because there had been no rain upon the earth.

**17:8** And the word of the Lord came to Elijah:

**17:9** "Arise and go to Zarephath of Sidon; behold, I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain you."

**17:10** So he arose and went to Zarephath, to the gate of the city; and behold, a widow woman was there gathering sticks. And Elijah called out after her and said to her, "Please fetch a little water in a vessel, and I will drink."

**17:11** And she went to fetch it, and Elijah called out after her and said, "Please bring me a morsel of the bread that is in your hand."

**17:12** And the woman said, "As the Lord your God lives, I have no baked cake, only as much as a handful of flour in the jar and a little oil in the flask. And behold, I am gathering two sticks, and I will go in and prepare it for myself and my children, and we shall eat, and we shall die."

**17:13** And Elijah said to her, "Take courage; go in and do according to your word. Only make for me from it a small baked cake first and bring it out to me, and afterward you shall make some for yourself and your children,

**17:14** for thus says the Lord: 'The jar of flour shall not fail, nor shall the flask of oil diminish, until the day the Lord gives rain upon the earth.'"

**17:15** So the woman went and did so; and she ate, and he, and her children.

**17:16** And the jar of flour did not fail, nor did the flask of oil diminish, according to the word of the Lord which He spoke by the hand of Elijah.

**17:17** And it came to pass after these things that the son of the woman, the mistress of the house, fell sick; and his sickness was exceedingly severe, until there was no breath left in him.

**17:18** And she said to Elijah, "What have I to do with you, O man of God? Have you come in to me to bring my iniquity to remembrance and to put my son to death?"

**17:19** And Elijah said to the woman, "Give me your son." And he took him from her bosom and carried him up to the upper room where he himself was staying, and laid him upon the bed.

**17:20** And Elijah cried aloud and said, "Alas, O Lord, the witness of the widow with whom I dwell, You have brought evil upon her by putting her son to death!"

**17:21** And he breathed upon the child three times, and called upon the Lord and said, "O Lord my God, I pray, let the soul of this child return into him."

**17:22** And so it came to pass, and the child cried out.

**17:23** And he brought him down from the upper room into the house and gave him to his mother. And Elijah said, "Look, your son lives."

**17:24** And the woman said to Elijah, "Behold, I know that you are a man of God, and the word of the Lord in your mouth is true."

## Translator Notes

- **17:1** — The Greek text adds "the prophet" and "the God of hosts," identifies Elijah as coming "from Tishbe of Gilead" (Greek Thesbon), and renders the closing phrase literally as "by the mouth of my word."
- **17:12** — Here and in verses 13 and 15, the Greek text reads plural "children" rather than singular "son" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **17:20** — The Greek text addresses the Lord as "the witness of the widow" and frames Elijah's cry as a direct exclamation rather than a question.
- **17:21** — The Greek reads "breathed upon the child three times" (rather than "stretched himself upon the child" in the Hebrew), and verse 22 concisely reports that "the child cried out."
