## Text

**13:1** And behold, a man of God from Judah came by the word of the Lord to Bethel, and Jeroboam was standing by the altar to offer sacrifice.

**13:2** And he cried out to the altar by the word of the Lord, "Altar, altar, thus says the Lord: 'Behold, a son is born to the house of David, Josiah is his name; and he shall sacrifice upon you the priests of the high places who offer sacrifice upon you, and he shall burn the bones of men upon you.'"

**13:3** And he will give a portent on that day, saying, "This is the word which the Lord has spoken, saying, 'Behold, the altar is split apart, and the fatness that is upon it shall be poured out.'"

**13:4** And it came to pass, when King Jeroboam heard the words of the man of God who had cried out against the altar that was in Bethel, that the king stretched out his hand from the altar, saying, "Seize him!" And behold, his hand, which he had stretched out against him, withered, and he was not able to draw it back to himself.

**13:5** And the altar was split apart, and the fatness was poured out from the altar according to the portent which the man of God had given by the word of the Lord.

**13:6** And King Jeroboam said to the man of God, "Implore your God, and let my hand be restored to me." And the man of God implored the face of the Lord, and He restored the king's hand to him, and it became as it was before.

**13:7** And the king spoke to the man of God, "Come into the house with me and dine, and I will give you a gift."

**13:8** And the man of God said to the king, "If you give me half of your house, I will not go in with you, nor will I eat bread or drink water in this place;

**13:9** for thus the Lord commanded me by a word, saying, 'Do not eat bread, and do not drink water, and do not return by the way on which you went.'"

**13:10** So he departed by another way, and did not turn back by the way on which he had come to Bethel.

**13:11** Now a certain old prophet was dwelling in Bethel; and his sons came and recounted to him all the deeds which the man of God had done that day in Bethel—the words which he had spoken to the king; and they turned the face of their father.

**13:12** And their father spoke to them, saying, "By what way has he gone?" And his sons showed him the way by which the man of God who had come from Judah went up.

**13:13** And he said to his sons, "Saddle the donkey for me." So they saddled the donkey for him, and he mounted it.

**13:14** And he went after the man of God, and found him sitting under an oak. And he said to him, "Are you the man of God who has come from Judah?" And he said to him, "I am."

**13:15** Then he said to him, "Come with me and eat bread."

**13:16** And he said, "I cannot return with you, nor will I eat bread or drink water in this place;

**13:17** for thus the Lord has commanded me by a word, saying, 'Do not eat bread there, and do not drink water, and do not return there by the way on which you went.'"

**13:18** And he said to him, "I also am a prophet just as you are, and an angel has spoken to me by the word of the Lord, saying, 'Bring him back to yourself into your house, and let him eat bread and drink water.'" But he lied to him.

**13:19** So he brought him back, and he ate bread in his house and drank water.

**13:20** And it came to pass, as they were sitting, that the word of the Lord came to the prophet who had brought him back;

**13:21** and he said to the man of God who had come from Judah, saying, "Thus says the Lord: Because you have rebelled against the word of the Lord, and have not kept the commandment which the Lord your God commanded you,

**13:22** but have turned back and have eaten bread and drunk water in this place of which He spoke to you, saying, 'You shall not eat bread and shall not drink water,' your body shall not enter into the tomb of your fathers."

**13:23** And it came to pass, after eating bread and drinking water, that he saddled the donkey for him, and he turned back and departed.

**13:24** And a lion met him on the road and put him to death; and his body was cast upon the road, and the donkey was standing beside it, and the lion was standing beside the body.

**13:25** And behold, men were passing by, and they saw the corpse cast upon the road, and the lion was standing close beside the corpse; and they entered and spoke of it in the city in which the old prophet dwelt.

**13:26** And the one who had brought him back from the road heard it, and said, "This is the man of God who rebelled against the word of the Lord."

**13:28** And he went and found his body cast upon the road, and the donkey and the lion were standing beside the body; and the lion had not eaten the body of the man of God, nor had it crushed the donkey.

**13:29** And the prophet lifted up the body of the man of God and laid it upon the donkey, and brought him back into the city,

**13:30** the prophet himself, to bury him in his own tomb; and they lamented over him, "Alas, brother!"

**13:31** And it came to pass, after lamenting him, that he spoke to his sons, saying, "When I die, bury me in this tomb in which the man of God is buried; lay me beside his bones, so that my bones may be saved with his bones.

**13:32** For the word which he spoke by the word of the Lord against the altar in Bethel, and against the high houses that are in Samaria, shall surely come to pass."

**13:33** And after this matter Jeroboam did not turn back from his wickedness, but turned again and made from a portion of the people priests of high places; whoever desired it, he filled his hand, and he became a priest for the high places.

**13:34** And this matter resulted in sin for the house of Jeroboam, and in destruction, and in extermination from the face of the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **13:3** — The Septuagint renders the Hebrew consecutive verb as a future tense ("will give") and reads "the word" (Hebrew "the sign") and "the fatness" (Hebrew "the ashes").
- **13:11** — The Septuagint reads "and they turned the face of their father," diverging from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("and they recounted them to their father").
- **13:26** — The Septuagint preserves a shorter text here, omitting the remainder of verse 26 and all of verse 27 found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **13:31** — The Septuagint reads "after lamenting him" (Hebrew "after burying him") and adds the purpose clause "so that my bones may be saved with his bones."
