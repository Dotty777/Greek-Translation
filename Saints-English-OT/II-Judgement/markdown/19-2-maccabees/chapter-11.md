## Text

**11:1** Now after a very short space of time, Lysias, the guardian of the king, his kinsman, and the one set over the affairs of state, being exceedingly displeased at the things that had happened,

**11:2** gathered about eighty thousand men and all the cavalry, and came against the Jews, intending to make the city a dwelling place for Greeks,

**11:3** and to levy tribute upon the temple, as upon the rest of the sanctuaries of the nations, and to put the high priesthood up for sale every year;

**11:4** in no way taking into account the might of God, but elated in mind by his tens of thousands of foot soldiers, his thousands of horsemen, and his eighty elephants.

**11:5** So entering into Judea and drawing near to Beth-zur, which was a fortified place distant from Jerusalem by about five leagues, he pressed it hard.

**11:6** Now when Maccabeus and those with him learned that he was besieging the strongholds, they, together with the multitudes, implored the Lord with lamentations and tears to send a good angel for the salvation of Israel.

**11:7** And Maccabeus himself was the first to take up arms, and he urged the others to hazard their lives together with him to come to the aid of their brethren; and with one accord they rushed forth eagerly.

**11:8** And while they were right there near Jerusalem, a horseman in white apparel appeared leading them on, brandishing weapons of gold.

**11:9** Then all of them together blessed the merciful God, and were so strengthened in their souls that they were ready to pierce through not only men, but even the fiercest beasts and walls of iron.

**11:10** They advanced in battle array, having their ally from heaven, for the Lord had shown mercy to them.

**11:11** And hurling themselves like lions upon the enemies, they struck down eleven thousand of them and one thousand six hundred horsemen, and forced all the rest to flee.

**11:12** Most of them escaped wounded and unarmed; and Lysias himself escaped by shameful flight.

**11:13** Yet not being without understanding, he reflected within himself upon the defeat that had befallen him, and perceiving that the Hebrews were unconquerable because the mighty God fought on their side, he sent messengers to them

**11:14** and urged them to come to a settlement upon all just terms, promising that he would also persuade the king, constraining him to become their friend.

**11:15** And Maccabeus consented to everything that Lysias proposed, having regard for the common good; for whatever Maccabeus delivered to Lysias in writing concerning the Jews, the secretary granted.

**11:16** For the letter written to the Jews from Lysias was to this effect: "Lysias to the multitude of the Jews, greeting.

**11:17** John and Absalom, who were sent from you, having delivered the petition written below, made request concerning the matters indicated in it.

**11:18** Therefore, whatever needed to be brought before the king as well, I have clearly set forth to him, and what was admissible he has granted.

**11:19** If, then, you maintain your goodwill toward the government, for the future also I will endeavor to promote your good.

**11:20** Concerning the particulars of these matters, I have given instructions both to these men and to my own representatives to confer with you.

**11:21** Farewell. In the one hundred and forty-eighth year, on the twenty-fourth day of Dioscorinthius."

**11:22** Now the king's letter ran as follows: "King Antiochus to his brother Lysias, greeting.

**11:23** Since our father has passed over to the gods, who desired that those of the kingdom should be undisturbed and attend to the care of the Jews,

**11:24** and we have heard that the Jews do not consent to our father's shift to Greek customs, but prefer their own way of life and request that their lawful observances be granted to them;

**11:25** desiring, therefore, that this nation also should be free from disturbance, we determine that the temple be restored to them, and that they conduct their lives according to the customs of their forefathers.

**11:26** You will do well, therefore, to send messengers to them and give them the right hand of friendship, so that, knowing our purpose, they may be of good cheer and gladly attend to the management of their own affairs."

**11:27** And to the nation the king's letter was as follows: "King Antiochus to the senate of the Jews and to the rest of the Jews, greeting.

**11:28** May you be well; it would be as we desire, and we ourselves also are in good health.

**11:29** Menelaus has made known to us his desire that you should return home and attend to your own affairs.

**11:30** Therefore, for those who return home up to the thirtieth day of Xanthicus, there shall be a pledge of friendship with full amnesty,

**11:31** so that the Jews may make use of their own provisions and laws, just as before; and none of them shall in any way be troubled concerning things done in ignorance.

**11:32** I have also sent Menelaus to encourage you.

**11:33** Farewell. In the one hundred and forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth day of Xanthicus."

**11:34** The Romans also sent a letter to them, reading as follows: "Quintus Memmius and Titus Manius, envoys of the Romans, to the people of the Jews, greeting.

**11:35** Concerning the things which Lysias, the kinsman of the king, has granted to you, we also give our consent.

**11:36** As for the matters which he decided should be referred to the king, consider them and send someone immediately, so that we may proceed as befits us; for we are on our way to Antioch.

**11:37** Therefore make haste and send some men, so that we also may know what your mind is.

**11:38** Be in good health. In the one hundred and forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth day of Xanthicus."

## Translator Notes

- **11:5** — Greek 'schoeni' (rendered 'leagues'), a measure of distance of roughly three to four miles each, matching Beth-zur's actual distance from Jerusalem; some manuscripts read 'stadia'.
- **11:15** — The Greek text here reads 'the secretary' (ho grammateus), whereas other manuscripts read 'the king' (ho basileus).
- **11:21** — Dioscorinthius (Greek Dios Korinthiou) is an otherwise unattested month name, possibly a textual corruption of the Macedonian month Dios or a local calendar month.
- **11:23** — The Greek text here reads 'who desired' (boulomenou) and 'the care of the Jews' (ton Ioudaion), whereas other manuscripts read 'we desiring' (boulomenoi) and 'their own affairs' (ton idion).
- **11:31** — Greek dapanemasin ('provisions' or 'expenditures'); other manuscripts read diaitemasin ('dietary customs' or 'way of life').
