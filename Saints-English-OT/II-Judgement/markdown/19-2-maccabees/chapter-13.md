## Text

**13:1** In the one hundred and forty-ninth year, word reached those with Judas that Antiochus Eupator was coming with multitudes against Judea,

**13:2** and with him Lysias his guardian, who was in charge of the affairs of state, each having a Greek force of one hundred and ten thousand foot soldiers, and five thousand three hundred horsemen, and twenty-two elephants, and three hundred chariots armed with scythes.

**13:3** Menelaus also joined them, and with great hypocrisy urged Antiochus on, not for the safety of his country, but supposing that he would be established in his office.

**13:4** But the King of kings stirred up the wrath of Antiochus against the wicked wretch; and when Lysias pointed out that this man was the cause of all the evils, the king commanded that they take him to Berea and put him to death there, as is the custom in that place.

**13:5** Now there is in that place a tower fifty cubits high, full of ashes, and it had a circular contrivance sloping steeply on every side down into the ashes.

**13:6** There all thrust down to destruction anyone guilty of sacrilege, or anyone who has achieved preeminence in any other evils.

**13:7** By such a fate it came to pass that the transgressor Menelaus died, without even obtaining burial in the earth;

**13:8** and most justly so; for since he had committed many sins against the altar, whose fire and ashes were holy, in ashes he received his death.

**13:9** Now the king came on, hardened in his thoughts, intending to inflict upon the Jews things worse than those that had been done in the time of his father.

**13:10** When Judas learned of this, he commanded the multitude to call upon the Lord day and night, that if ever at any other time, so now He would come to the aid of those who were about to be deprived of the law, their country, and the holy temple,

**13:11** and that He would not permit the people, who had only just begun to revive a little, to fall into the hands of the blasphemous nations.

**13:12** When all had done this together, and had implored the merciful Lord with weeping and fasting and lying prostrate for three days without ceasing, Judas encouraged them and commanded them to stand ready.

**13:13** And taking counsel privately with the elders, he resolved that before the king's army should invade Judea and gain control of the city, they would march out and decide the matter with the help of God.

**13:14** Committing the outcome to the Lord of the world, he exhorted those who were with him to contend nobly even to death for the laws, the temple, the city, their country, and their way of life; and he pitched his camp near Modein.

**13:15** Having given to those with him the watchword, "Victory of God," he attacked the royal pavilion by night with the finest chosen young men, slew about two thousand men in the camp, and brought down the foremost of the elephants together with the one in its tower;

**13:16** and in the end they filled the camp with terror and confusion, and withdrew in triumph.

**13:17** This had been accomplished just as the day was beginning to dawn, through the protecting care of the Lord that aided him.

**13:18** Now the king, having had a taste of the boldness of the Jews, tried to take those places by stratagem.

**13:19** He marched against Beth-zur, a small fortress of the Jews; he advanced, he was driven back, he met with reverses, he was defeated.

**13:20** And Judas sent in the necessary supplies to those who were within.

**13:21** But Rhodocus, from the Jewish ranks, disclosed the secrets to the enemy; he was sought out, arrested, and imprisoned.

**13:22** The king negotiated a second time with those in Beth-zur, gave his right hand in pledge, received theirs, departed, attacked those with Judas, was worsted,

**13:23** heard that Philip, who had been left in charge of the affairs of state, had lost his senses in Antioch, was dismayed, appealed to the Jews, yielded, and swore to all just terms, came to a settlement and offered sacrifice, honored the sanctuary and the place, showed kindness,

**13:24** and welcomed Maccabeus, left Hegemonides as governor from Ptolemais to the Gennerites,

**13:25** and came to Ptolemais. The people of Ptolemais were displeased with the treaty, for they were exceedingly fearful; they wished to annul the stipulations.

**13:26** Lysias stepped up to the tribunal, made the best defense possible, persuaded and pacified them, made them well disposed, and departed to Antioch. Thus went the expedition of the king and his return.

## Translator Notes

- **13:2** — The Greek reads hekaston ('each'), indicating each leader having the Greek force; some commentators treat the pronoun as a textual crux.
- **13:9** — This Greek text reads bebaremenos ('hardened' or 'weighed down'), whereas other manuscripts read bebarbaromenos ('barbarous in spirit').
- **13:15** — The Greek synetheken ('brought down') is a variant of synekentesen ('pierced through'), and 'the one in its tower' literally translates 'the one in the house,' referring to the wooden howdah atop the elephant.
- **13:19** — This Greek text describes Beth-zur as 'a small fortress' (oligon), whereas other manuscripts read 'a strong fortress' (ochyron).
- **13:24** — This Greek text reads 'the Gennerites' (ton Genneron), where other Septuagint manuscripts read 'the Gerrenians' (ton Gerrenon).
