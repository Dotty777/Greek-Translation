## Text

**3:1** While the holy city was inhabited in all peace, and the laws were most excellently kept because of the godliness and hatred of wickedness of Onias the high priest,

**3:2** it came to pass that even the kings themselves honored the place and glorified the temple with the finest gifts;

**3:3** so that even Seleucus, the king of Asia, supplied out of his own revenues all the expenses belonging to the services of the sacrifices.

**3:4** Now a certain Simon of the tribe of Benjamin, who had been appointed overseer of the temple, fell into a dispute with the high priest concerning the administration of the city market.

**3:5** And being unable to prevail against Onias, he went to Apollonius the son of Thrasaeus, who at that time was governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia.

**3:6** And he reported that the treasury in Jerusalem was filled with untold riches, so that the abundance of the funds was beyond counting, and that they did not apply them to the account of the sacrifices, and that it was possible for these to come under the authority of the king.

**3:7** Now when Apollonius met with the king, he informed him concerning the riches that had been disclosed to him; and the king appointed Heliodorus, who was in charge of the affairs of state, and sent him with commands to carry out the removal of the aforementioned riches.

**3:8** Immediately Heliodorus set out on his journey, under the pretense of inspecting the cities throughout Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, but in reality to accomplish the purpose of the king.

**3:9** When he arrived in Jerusalem and had been courteously received by the high priest of the city, he recounted the report that had been made and declared plainly the reason for his presence; and he inquired whether these things were truly so.

**3:10** Then the high priest explained that these were deposits belonging to widows and orphans,

**3:11** and some also to Hyrcanus the son of Tobias, a man of very eminent standing—not as the impious Simon had falsely accused—and that the entire sum was four hundred talents of silver and two hundred of gold;

**3:12** and that to wrong those who had trusted in the holiness of the place, and in the majesty and inviolability of the temple honored throughout the entire world, was altogether impossible.

**3:13** But the other, because of the royal commands which he held, insisted that by all means these funds must be taken into the royal treasury.

**3:14** Having appointed a day, he went in to direct the inspection of these funds; and there was no small distress throughout the whole city.

**3:15** The priests cast themselves down before the altar in their priestly garments and called toward heaven upon Him who gave the law concerning deposits, to keep these things safe for those who had deposited them.

**3:16** Whoever beheld the appearance of the high priest was pierced in mind; for his countenance and the altered hue of his face revealed the agony within his soul.

**3:17** For a dread and shuddering of body had come over the man, through which the sorrow weighing upon his heart was made plain to those who watched.

**3:18** People rushed out from their houses in throngs to make public supplication, that the place might not be about to come into contempt.

**3:19** The women, girded with sackcloth beneath their breasts, thronged the streets; and of the maidens who were kept secluded, some ran together to the gates, others to the walls, and some looked out through the windows.

**3:20** And all of them, stretching out their hands toward heaven, offered up their prayer.

**3:21** It was pitiable to behold the mingled prostration of the multitude, and the anxious expectation of the high priest in his great agony.

**3:22** While they were calling upon the Almighty Lord to preserve the things entrusted wholly safe and secure for those who had entrusted them,

**3:23** Heliodorus proceeded to carry out what had been decreed.

**3:24** But when he had already arrived at the treasury with his guards, the Sovereign of spirits and of all authority brought about a great manifestation, so that all who had dared to accompany him were astounded at the power of God and fell into faintness and terror.

**3:25** For there appeared to them a horse with a fearsome rider, adorned with the finest trappings; and rushing fiercely forward, it struck at Heliodorus with its front hooves, while he who sat upon it appeared to be clad in full armor of gold.

**3:26** Moreover, two other young men appeared to him, remarkable in strength, most fair in speech, and splendid in apparel; and standing on either side of him, they scourged him unceasingly, inflicting many blows upon him.

**3:27** When he suddenly fell to the ground and was enveloped in thick darkness, they caught him up and placed him upon a litter;

**3:28** and this man, who just before had entered the aforementioned treasury with a great retinue and his entire bodyguard, they carried away rendered utterly helpless, having openly acknowledged the sovereign power.

**3:29** So he, by the divine working, lay speechless and bereft of all hope and deliverance;

**3:30** while they blessed the Lord who had wondrously glorified His own place; and the temple, which a little while before was full of fear and turmoil, now that the Almighty Lord had appeared, was filled with joy and gladness.

**3:31** Quickly some of the companions of Heliodorus begged Onias to call upon the Most High to grant life to him who lay altogether at his last breath.

**3:32** And the high priest, apprehensive lest the king should suppose that some foul deed had been committed against Heliodorus by the Jews, offered a sacrifice for the man's recovery.

**3:33** While the high priest was making the atonement, the same young men appeared again to Heliodorus, arrayed in the same garments; and standing by him, they said, "Give great thanks to Onias the high priest, for on his account the Lord has granted you life.

**3:34** And you, having been scourged from heaven, proclaim to all the majestic power of God." And having spoken these words, they vanished from sight.

**3:35** Then Heliodorus offered a sacrifice to God and made very great vows to Him who had preserved his life; and having taken warm leave of Onias, he marched back with his forces to the king.

**3:36** And he bore open witness to all concerning the works of the supreme God which he had seen with his own eyes.

**3:37** When the king asked Heliodorus what sort of man would be fit to be sent once more to Jerusalem, he answered,

**3:38** "If you have any enemy or conspirator against the affairs of state, send him there, and you shall receive him back scourged, if indeed he even survives, because there is truly a certain power of God about that place.

**3:39** For He Himself who has His dwelling in heaven is the watcher and helper of that place, and He strikes and destroys those who come to do harm."

**3:40** And so the matters concerning Heliodorus and the preservation of the treasury came to pass.

## Translator Notes

- **3:6** — The Greek text reads prosenegkein ("apply" or "contribute") rather than prosekein ("pertain") found in some other witnesses.
- **3:11** — The clause "not as the impious Simon had falsely accused" renders a compressed Greek construction (ouch houtos diaballon) preserved in this text.
- **3:18** — Following the Greek text here (which includes the negative particle me, as in Codex Alexandrinus), the clause expresses purpose ("that the place might not be about to come into contempt") rather than cause.
- **3:26** — "Most fair in speech" translates the Greek glossan ("tongue/speech," as in Codex Alexandrinus), where other manuscripts read doxan ("glory" or "splendor").
- **3:28** — The Greek singular participle epegnokota refers to Heliodorus himself acknowledging the sovereign power, whereas some manuscripts use a plural participle referring to his attendants and add "of God."
