## Text

**12:1** Now when these agreements had been made, Lysias departed to the king, and the Jews occupied themselves with farming.

**12:2** But of the local commanders, Timothy and Apollonius the son of Gennaeus, and also Hieronymus and Demophon, and in addition to these Nicanor the commander of the Cypriots, would not allow them to remain settled and live in quietness.

**12:3** And the people of Joppa perpetrated so great an impiety: they invited the Jews who dwelt among them to embark with their wives and children into the boats they had provided, as though there were no ill will toward them;

**12:4** and in accordance with the common decree of the city, when these also accepted, as those who desired to live in peace and harbored no suspicion, they led them out and drowned them, not fewer than two hundred.

**12:5** Now when Judas heard of the cruelty that had been done to his countrymen, he gave orders to the men who were with him,

**12:6** and having called upon God, the righteous Judge, he came against the murderers of his brethren, set fire to the harbor by night, burned up the boats, and pierced through those who had fled there for refuge.

**12:7** And because the town was closed against him, he withdrew, intending to come back and root out the entire community of the people of Joppa.

**12:8** But when he learned that those in Jamnia also intended to act in the same manner toward the Jews who dwelt there,

**12:9** he fell upon the people of Jamnia also by night, and set fire to the harbor along with the fleet, so that the glare of the light was seen as far as Jerusalem, a distance of two hundred and forty stadia.

**12:10** When they had marched away nine stadia from there, making their advance against Timothy, Arabs fell upon him, not fewer than five thousand, with five hundred horsemen.

**12:11** And when a fierce battle took place, and those with Judas prospered through the help of God, the nomads, having been defeated, appealed to Judas to give them his right hand in pledge, promising both to give them cattle and to assist them in other ways.

**12:12** And Judas, supposing that they would truly be useful in many things, consented to make peace with them; and having received the pledge of his right hand, they departed to their tents.

**12:13** He also fell upon a certain city, laying a causeway against it—a strong city, enclosed with walls and inhabited by mixed nations, whose name was Caspin.

**12:14** And those within, trusting in the strength of their walls and in their store of provisions, behaved very insolently toward those with Judas, reviling them, and moreover blaspheming and speaking things that are not lawful.

**12:15** Then those with Judas, having called upon the great Sovereign of the world, who without battering rams or engines of war cast down Jericho in the days of Joshua, rushed fiercely against the wall.

**12:16** And having captured the city by the will of God, they carried out an unspeakable slaughter, so that the adjoining lake, which was two stadia wide, appeared to be filled with the blood that flowed into it.

**12:17** And having marched seven hundred and fifty stadia from there, they arrived at Charax, and to the Jews who are called Tubieni.

**12:18** Now they did not overtake Timothy in those regions, for he had departed from those parts without accomplishing anything, though he had left behind a very strong garrison in a certain place.

**12:19** But Dositheus and Sosipater, who were commanders under Maccabeus, marched out and destroyed those whom Timothy had left in the stronghold, more than ten thousand men.

**12:20** Then Maccabeus, having arranged the army with him in divisions, appointed them over the divisions; and they marched against Timothy, who had with him one hundred and twenty thousand foot soldiers, and two thousand horsemen and seven thousand besides.

**12:21** When Timothy heard of the approach of Judas, he sent the women and the rest of the baggage ahead to the place called Carnion; for the place was hard to besiege and difficult to approach because of the narrowness of all the passes.

**12:22** But when the division of Judas appeared first, terror fell upon the enemy, and fear came upon them from the manifestation of Him who beholds all things; they rushed into flight, each driven in a different direction, so that they were often wounded by their own men and pierced through by the points of their swords.

**12:23** And Judas pressed the pursuit all the more vigorously, piercing through those wicked men, and he destroyed about thirty thousand men.

**12:24** And Timothy himself, having fallen into the hands of those with Dositheus and Sosipater, appealed with much craftiness that they let him go safely, because he held the parents of many and the brothers of others, and it would come to pass that these would be shown no regard.

**12:25** So after he had confirmed with many words his agreement to restore them unharmed, they released him for the sake of saving their brethren.

**12:26** Then he marched out against Carnion and the temple of Atargatis, and slaughtered twenty-five thousand persons.

**12:27** And after the rout and destruction of these, he also marched against Ephron, a fortified city, in which there were multitudes of every tribe; and vigorous young men stationed before the walls fought stoutly, and there were great stores of engines of war and missiles.

**12:28** But having called upon the Sovereign who with might shatters the strength of the enemy, they took the city into their hands, and struck down about twenty-five thousand of those who were within.

**12:29** And departing from there, they hastened toward Scythopolis, which is six hundred stadia distant from Jerusalem.

**12:30** But when the Jews who were settled there bore witness to the goodwill that the people of Scythopolis had shown toward them, and their gentle treatment in the times of misfortune,

**12:31** they gave thanks, and further urged them to be favorably disposed toward their nation in the time to come; and they came to Jerusalem, as the Feast of Weeks was close at hand.

**12:32** And after the feast called Pentecost, they marched against Gorgias, the governor of Idumea.

**12:33** And he came out with three thousand foot soldiers and four hundred horsemen.

**12:34** And when they drew up in battle array, it came to pass that a few of the Jews fell.

**12:35** Now a certain Dositheus, one of Bacenor's men, a horseman and a mighty man, laid hold of Gorgias, and seizing him by his cloak, dragged him along vigorously; and while he desired to take the accursed man alive, one of the Thracian horsemen bore down upon him and struck off his shoulder, so Gorgias escaped to Marisa.

**12:36** And when those with Esdris had fought for a long time and were exhausted, Judas called upon the Lord to appear as their ally and to appear as the leader of the battle;

**12:37** and raising the battle cry with hymns in his ancestral language, he rushed unexpectedly upon those with Gorgias and put them to rout.

**12:38** Then Judas gathered his army and came to the city of Adullam; and as the seventh day was approaching, they purified themselves according to the custom, and kept the Sabbath there.

**12:39** On the next day, as by that time it had become necessary, those with Judas came to take up the bodies of those who had fallen and to lay them to rest with their kinsmen in the tombs of their fathers.

**12:40** And under the tunics of each of the dead they found consecrated tokens of the idols of Jamnia, which the law forbids the Jews to have; and it became clear to all that for this cause these men had fallen.

**12:41** Therefore all blessed the judgments of the Lord, the righteous Judge, who makes the hidden things manifest,

**12:42** and turned to supplication, praying that the sin that had been committed might be completely blotted out; and the noble Judas exhorted the multitude to keep themselves free from sin, since they had seen with their own eyes what had happened because of the sin of those who had fallen.

**12:43** And having made a collection man by man to the amount of two thousand drachmas of silver, he sent it to Jerusalem to offer a sin offering, acting very rightly and honorably, taking thought for the resurrection.

**12:44** For if he were not expecting that those who had fallen would rise again, it would have been superfluous and foolish to pray for the dead.

**12:45** Moreover, looking to the most excellent reward laid up for those who fall asleep in godliness, it was a holy and pious thought. Therefore he made atonement for the dead, that they might be delivered from their sin.

## Translator Notes

- **12:13** — The Greek word gephyroun literally means 'to bridge' or 'to lay a causeway'; some interpreters regard it as a textual variant for 'fortified with bridges' or an alternate designation alongside Caspin.
- **12:20** — This Greek text literally reads 'two thousand, and in addition seven thousand' horsemen, whereas other Septuagint manuscripts read 'two thousand five hundred.'
- **12:45** — The Greek reads eucharisterion (literally 'thank-offering' or 'gracious reward'), where other manuscripts read charisterion ('reward' or 'recompense').
