## Text

**7:1** Now it also came to pass that seven brothers with their mother were seized and compelled by the king to partake of the unlawful swine's flesh, being tormented with whips and thongs.

**7:2** And one of them, becoming their spokesman, said, "Are you about to question and learn from us? For we are ready to die rather than to transgress the laws of our fathers."

**7:3** Then the king, becoming enraged, commanded pans and cauldrons to be heated red-hot.

**7:4** And when these were immediately heated, he commanded that the one who had become their spokesman have his tongue cut out, and that they scalp him and cut off his extremities, while the rest of his brothers and his mother looked on.

**7:5** And when he had become helpless in his remaining parts, he ordered him to be brought to the fire while still breathing and to be fried in the pan. And as the vapor from the pan spread far and wide, they exhorted one another, together with their mother, to die nobly, saying:

**7:6** "The Lord God looks on, and truly has compassion on us, just as Moses declared through the song that testifies against them to their face, saying, 'And He will have compassion on His servants.'"

**7:7** Now after the first had departed this life in this manner, they brought the second to the mockery; and tearing off the skin of his head with the hair, they asked him, "Will you eat before your body is punished limb by limb?"

**7:8** But answering in the language of his fathers, he said, "No." Therefore also

**7:9** this one received the next torture in turn, just as the first. And when he was at his last breath, he said, "You indeed, accursed wretch, release us from this present life, but the King of the world will raise us up, who have died for His laws, to an everlasting renewal of life."

**7:10** And after him the third was mocked; and when his tongue was demanded, he quickly put it out and courageously stretched out his hands.

**7:11** And he nobly said, "From Heaven these have been called, and for the sake of His laws I disregard them, and from Him I hope to receive them back again."

**7:12** So the king himself and those with him were astonished at the spirit of the young man, because he counted the pains as nothing.

**7:13** And when this one also had departed this life, they tortured and tormented the fourth in the same manner.

**7:14** And when he was near death, he spoke thus: "It is preferable, when departing from men, to look for the hopes given by God to be raised up again by Him; for as for you, there shall be no resurrection to life."

**7:15** Next, bringing forward the fifth, they tormented him.

**7:16** But looking at him, he said, "Having authority among men, though you are perishable, you do what you wish; yet do not suppose that our race has been forsaken by God.

**7:17** But wait patiently, and behold His mighty power, how He will torment you and your seed."

**7:18** And after him they brought the sixth, and when he was about to die, he said, "Do not deceive yourself any further, for we suffer these things because of ourselves, having sinned against our own God; things worthy of wonder have come to pass;

**7:19** but do not think that you will go unpunished, having attempted to fight against God."

**7:20** Now the mother was exceedingly good and worthy of good remembrance, who, watching seven sons perish within the space of a single day, bore it with good courage because of her hopes in the Lord.

**7:21** And she exhorted each of the men in the language of their fathers, filled with a noble mind and arousing her womanly reasoning with manly courage, saying to them:

**7:22** "I do not know how you appeared in my womb, nor was it I who bestowed breath and life upon you, nor did I set in order the elements of each one of you.

**7:23** Therefore the Creator of the world, who formed the origin of man and devised the origin of all things, with mercy gives back to you both breath and life again, just as you now disregard yourselves for the sake of His laws."

**7:24** Now Antiochus, supposing that he was being despised and suspecting her reproachful voice, while the youngest was still alive, not only made his appeal with words, but also confirmed with oaths that he would at once make him rich and enviable if he turned away from the customs of his fathers, and would take him as a friend and entrust him with public affairs.

**7:25** But when the young man paid no attention at all, the king summoned the mother and urged her to become a counselor to the lad for his preservation.

**7:26** And after he had urged her at length, she agreed to persuade her son.

**7:27** Then leaning down toward him, deriding the cruel tyrant, she spoke thus in the language of their fathers: "Son, have mercy on me, who carried you in the womb nine months, and nursed you three years, and reared you and brought you to this age and sustained you.

**7:28** I implore you, child, to look up to the heaven and the earth, and seeing all that is in them, to know that God did not make them out of existing things, and in this way the race of mankind comes into being.

**7:29** Do not fear this executioner, but proving yourself worthy of your brothers, accept death, so that in mercy I may receive you back together with your brothers."

**7:30** While she was still finishing, the young man said, "What are you waiting for? I do not obey the command of the king, but I obey the command of the law that was given to our fathers through Moses.

**7:31** But you, who have become the deviser of every evil against the Hebrews, shall by no means escape the hands of God.

**7:32** For we are suffering because of our own sins;

**7:33** and if for the sake of rebuke and chastening our living Lord has been angry for a little while, He will also be reconciled again to His own servants.

**7:34** But you, O unholy man and most defiled of all men, do not be lifted up in vain, raging proudly with uncertain hopes, raising your hand against the heavenly children;

**7:35** for you have not yet escaped the judgment of the Almighty, all-seeing God.

**7:36** For our brothers, having now endured a brief pain, have fallen under God's covenant of everlasting life; but you, by the judgment of God, shall receive the just penalties of your arrogance.

**7:37** And I, like my brothers, give up both body and fortune for the laws of our fathers, as we call upon God speedily to become merciful to our nation, and that you, through torments and scourges, may confess that He alone is God;

**7:38** and in me and in my brothers to bring to an end the wrath of the Almighty, which has been justly kindled against our entire race."

**7:39** Then the king, becoming enraged, treated this one more cruelly than the others, feeling bitter over his derision.

**7:40** So this one also departed this life undefiled, trusting wholly in the Lord.

**7:41** And last of all, after her sons, the mother died.

**7:42** Let this much, then, suffice to have been declared concerning the sacrificial meats and the exceeding torments.

## Translator Notes

- **7:11** — The Greek text reads kekletai ("have been called", as in Codex Alexandrinus) rather than kektēmai ("I possess" or "I received").
- **7:18** — The Greek text reads mallon ("any further" or "rather", as in Codex Alexandrinus) rather than matēn ("in vain").
- **7:21** — The Greek text reads tōn anthrōpōn ("of the men", as in Codex Alexandrinus) rather than autōn ("of them").
- **7:36** — The Greek text reads peptōkasin ("have fallen [under]"), referring to coming under God's covenant of everlasting life; some manuscripts read pepōkasin ("have drunk of").
- **7:37** — The Greek text reads tychēn ("fortune" or "lot", as in Codex Alexandrinus) rather than psychēn ("soul" or "life"), and uses the plural participle epikaloumenoi ("as we call upon"), uniting the youngest brother with his brothers.
