## Text

**15:1** Now Nicanor, having learned that Judas and those with him were in the regions of Samaria, resolved to fall upon them with all safety on the day of rest.

**15:2** And the Jews who followed him under compulsion said, "By no means destroy them so savagely and barbarously, but give honor to the day that has been preeminently honored with holiness by Him who beholds all things."

**15:3** Then that thrice-accursed man asked whether there is a Sovereign in heaven who has commanded to observe the Sabbath day.

**15:4** And they declared, "There is the living Lord Himself, the Sovereign in heaven, who has commanded the seventh day to be observed."

**15:5** And the other said, "I also am a sovereign on the earth, who commands that arms be taken up and the king's business be fulfilled." Yet he did not succeed in accomplishing his ruthless design.

**15:6** Now Nicanor, bearing himself loftily with all assurance, had determined to establish a common course for Judas and those with him.

**15:7** But Maccabeus trusted continually, with all hope, that he would obtain help from the Lord.

**15:8** And he exhorted those with him not to fear the onslaught of the Gentiles, but keeping in mind the former acts of help that had come to them from heaven, to expect even now the victory that would come to them from the Almighty.

**15:9** And encouraging them from the Law and the Prophets, and reminding them also of the struggles they had already accomplished, he made them more eager.

**15:10** And having aroused their spirits, he gave his orders, at the same time pointing out the treachery of the Gentiles and their breaking of oaths.

**15:11** Thus arming each of them, not so much with the security of shields and spears as with the encouragement of good words, and relating besides a trustworthy dream, he gladdened them all exceedingly.

**15:12** Now the vision of it was this: Onias, who had been high priest, a noble and good man, modest in his bearing, gentle in his disposition, speaking fittingly, and trained from childhood in all things belonging to virtue—this man was stretching out his hands and praying for the whole community of the Jews.

**15:13** Then in the same manner there appeared a man distinguished by gray hair and glory, and a wondrous and most majestic preeminence was about him.

**15:14** And Onias answered and said, "This is a lover of the brethren, who prays much for the people and the holy city—Jeremiah, the prophet of God."

**15:15** Then Jeremiah stretched out his right hand and delivered to Judas a golden sword, and as he gave it, he addressed him thus:

**15:16** "Take the holy sword, a gift from God, through which you shall crush the adversaries."

**15:17** Now being encouraged by the words of Judas, which were exceedingly noble and able to incite to valor and make manly the souls of young men, they resolved not to carry on a campaign, but to charge nobly, and engaging hand to hand with all courage, to decide the issue, because the city, the holy things, and the temple were in danger.

**15:18** For their fear for wives and children, and also for brethren and kinsmen, was of lesser account to them, while their greatest and foremost fear was for the consecrated sanctuary.

**15:19** And those who were shut up in the city also had no slight anguish, being troubled over the battle in the open field.

**15:20** Now when all were already awaiting the coming decision, and the enemies had already drawn near, and the army was drawn up in battle array, and the beasts were stationed in a favorable position, and the cavalry was arrayed on the flanks,

**15:21** Maccabeus, beholding the presence of the multitudes, the varied array of weapons, and the fierceness of the beasts in their favorable position, stretched out his hands toward heaven and called upon the Lord who works wonders, knowing that it is not through weapons, but according as He decides, that He secures victory for those who are worthy.

**15:22** And calling upon Him, he spoke after this manner: "You, O Master, sent Your angel in the days of Hezekiah king of Judea, and he slew from the camp of Sennacherib as many as one hundred and eighty-five thousand.

**15:23** And now, O Sovereign of the heavens, send a good angel before us to bring dread and trembling;

**15:24** by the greatness of Your arm let those who come with blasphemy against Your holy people be struck with terror." And with these words he ended.

**15:25** Now Nicanor and those with him advanced with trumpets and battle songs,

**15:26** while Judas and those with him met the enemies in battle with invocation and prayers.

**15:27** And fighting with their hands while praying to God in their hearts, they struck down no fewer than thirty-five thousand, rejoicing greatly in the care of God.

**15:28** And when the engagement was over and they were returning with joy, they recognized Nicanor lying fallen in his full armor.

**15:29** Then a shout and commotion arose, and they blessed the Sovereign in the language of their fathers.

**15:30** And he who in every way, in body and soul, was the foremost champion for his fellow citizens, and who had preserved the goodwill of his youth toward his countrymen, commanded that they cut off the head of Nicanor, and his hand with the shoulder, and bear them to Jerusalem.

**15:31** And when he arrived there, having called together his countrymen and stationed the priests before the altar, he sent for those from the citadel.

**15:32** And having displayed the head of the vile Nicanor and the hand of the blasphemer, which he had stretched out against the holy house of the Almighty with arrogant boasting,

**15:33** and having cut out the tongue of the ungodly Nicanor, he declared that it should be piecemeal for the birds, and commanded the wages of his folly to be hung up opposite the temple.

**15:34** And they all offered blessing toward heaven to the manifest Lord, saying, "Blessed be He who has kept His own place undefiled!"

**15:35** And he hung the head of Nicanor from the citadel, visible to all and a clear sign of the help of the Lord.

**15:36** And they all decreed by a common resolution by no means to leave this day unmarked, but to keep as a notable day the thirteenth day of the twelfth month—called Adar in the Lord's language—one day before the day of Mordecai.

**15:37** Therefore, since the affairs concerning Nicanor turned out in this way, and since from those times the city has been held by the Hebrews, I myself also will here bring this narrative to an end.

**15:38** And if it has been well and aptly composed, that is what I myself desired; but if plainly and mediocrely, that was all I was able to attain.

**15:39** Just as drinking wine by itself, and likewise water by itself again, is harmful, whereas wine mixed with water at once produces a delightful charm, so also the skillful arrangement of the discourse delights the ears of those who read the composition. And here shall be the end.

## Translator Notes

- **15:6** — The Greek text reads asphaleias ("assurance") and tropon ("way/course"), whereas other manuscripts read alazoneias ("arrogance") and tropaion ("public trophy/monument").
- **15:17** — The Greek text reads me strateuesthai ("not to carry on a campaign"), whereas other manuscripts read me stratopedeuesthai ("not to remain in camp").
- **15:27** — The Greek text reads epimeleia ("care"), whereas other manuscripts read epiphaneia ("manifestation" or "appearing").
- **15:33** — The Greek text compresses the first clause (without an explicit infinitive "to give") and uses epicheira ("wages," literally "hand-payment") as a play on Nicanor's severed hand.
- **15:36** — The Greek text reads te kyriake phone ("in the Lord's language"), a scribal variant for te Syriake phone ("in the Syrian/Aramaic language") found in other manuscripts.
