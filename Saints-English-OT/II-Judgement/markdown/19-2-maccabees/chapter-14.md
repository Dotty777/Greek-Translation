## Text

**14:1** Now after a period of three years, word reached Judas and his men that Demetrius the son of Seleucus, having sailed in through the harbor of Tripolis with a mighty host and a fleet,

**14:2** had seized control of the land, having put to death Antiochus and his guardian Lysias.

**14:3** Now a certain Alcimus, who had formerly been high priest, but had willfully defiled himself in the times of separation, realizing that in no way whatever was there any safety for him, nor any further access to the holy altar,

**14:4** came to King Demetrius about the one hundred and fifty-first year, presenting to him a crown of gold and a palm branch, and besides these, some of the customary boughs of the temple. And on that day he kept silent.

**14:5** But when he found an opportunity favorable to his own folly, being summoned into council by Demetrius and asked in what disposition and counsel the Jews stood, he answered to these things:

**14:6** "Those among the Jews who are called Hasideans, whose leader is Judas Maccabeus, keep up war and stir up sedition, not allowing the kingdom to attain stability.

**14:7** Therefore, being deprived of my ancestral glory—meaning the high priesthood—I have now come a second time,

**14:8** first, genuinely caring for the interests pertaining to the king, and second, having regard also for my own fellow citizens; for by the recklessness of the men just mentioned, our entire nation suffers no small misfortune.

**14:9** Now having learned each of these matters, O king, take thought both for the country and for our hard-pressed nation, according to the gracious kindness which you hold toward all.

**14:10** For as long as Judas remains alive, it is impossible for affairs of state to attain peace."

**14:11** When such words had been spoken by him, the rest of the Friends, who were hostile toward Judas, swiftly inflamed Demetrius still further.

**14:12** Immediately he chose Nicanor, who had been master of the elephants, and having appointed him governor of Judea, he sent him forth,

**14:13** giving him written instructions to put Judas himself to death, to scatter those who were with him, and to establish Alcimus as high priest of the greatest temple.

**14:14** And the Gentiles throughout Judea who had fled from Judas flocked in droves to join Nicanor, supposing that the misfortunes and calamities of the Jews would turn to their own prosperity.

**14:15** Now when they heard of the advance of Nicanor and the assault of the Gentiles, they sprinkled earth upon themselves and implored Him who established His people forever, and who always with a manifestation upholds His own portion.

**14:16** And when the leader gave the command, he immediately marched out from there and engaged them at the village of Lessau.

**14:17** Now Simon, the brother of Judas, had encountered Nicanor, yet had slowly suffered a setback because of the sudden consternation caused by the adversaries.

**14:18** Nevertheless, Nicanor, hearing of the valor which Judas and his men possessed, and their courage in the struggles for their homeland, shrank from bringing the decision to the issue of bloodshed.

**14:19** Therefore he sent Posidonius, Theodotus, and Mattathias to give and to receive pledges of peace.

**14:20** After thorough consideration had been given to these matters, and the leader had laid them before the multitudes, and a unanimous consent appeared, they agreed to the treaty.

**14:21** They appointed a day on which they would meet together in private; a litter came forward from each side, and they set down seats.

**14:22** Judas stationed armed men in readiness at the strategic places, lest any treachery should suddenly arise from the enemy; and they held a fitting conference.

**14:23** Nicanor remained in Jerusalem and did nothing amiss, and he dismissed the thronging crowds that had gathered together.

**14:24** He kept Judas continually in his presence, for he had become deeply attached to the man in his heart.

**14:25** He urged him to marry and raise children; so he married, settled down in quietness, and shared in the common life.

**14:26** Now Alcimus, perceiving their goodwill toward one another, took the treaty that had been made and came to Demetrius, and told him that Nicanor was disloyal to the affairs of state, for he had appointed Judas, the conspirator against his kingdom, to be his successor.

**14:27** Then the king became furious, and provoked by the slanders of that utterly wicked man, he wrote to Nicanor, declaring that he was deeply displeased with the treaty, and commanding him to send Maccabeus as a prisoner to Antioch without delay.

**14:28** When these words reached Nicanor, he was greatly troubled and took it hard that he should set aside what had been agreed upon, seeing that the man had done no wrong.

**14:29** Yet since it was not possible to oppose the king, he watched for a favorable opportunity to carry out the order by stratagem.

**14:30** But Maccabeus, seeing that Nicanor was conducting himself more harshly toward him, and had grown more abrupt in his accustomed greeting, realized that this harshness did not come from good intent; so he gathered together not a few of his men and concealed himself from Nicanor.

**14:31** When the other realized that he had been shrewdly outmaneuvered by the man, he came to the greatest and holy temple while the priests were offering the appointed sacrifices, and commanded them to hand over the man.

**14:32** And when they declared with oaths that they did not know where the man he sought might be,

**14:33** stretching out his right hand toward the sanctuary, he swore these words: "Unless you deliver Judas to me as a prisoner, I will level this sacred precinct of God to a plain, and I will tear down the altar, and I will raise up here a splendid temple to Dionysus."

**14:34** Having spoken these words, he departed. Then the priests, stretching out their hands toward heaven, called upon Him who is ever the Defender of our nation, saying:

**14:35** "You, O Lord, who are in need of nothing at all, were well pleased that a temple of Your dwelling should be among us.

**14:36** And now, O holy Lord of all sanctification, preserve forever undefiled this house that has been so recently cleansed."

**14:37** Now a certain Razis, one of the elders of Jerusalem, was denounced to Nicanor—a man who loved his fellow citizens, was very well spoken of, and because of his goodwill was called Father of the Jews.

**14:38** For in the former times of separation he had been brought under charges for Judaism, and with all earnestness had risked both body and soul for Judaism.

**14:39** Now Nicanor, wishing to make manifest the hostility which he held toward the Jews, sent more than five hundred soldiers to seize him.

**14:40** For he thought that by seizing him he would bring calamity upon them.

**14:41** When the troops were about to capture the tower, and were forcing the courtyard door and giving orders to bring fire and set the doors ablaze, finding himself surrounded on every side, he fell upon his sword,

**14:42** choosing to die nobly rather than fall into the hands of wicked men and suffer outrages unworthy of his own noble birth.

**14:43** But because he missed his mark with the stroke in the haste of the struggle, and the crowds were rushing inside through the doorways, he ran bravely up onto the wall and cast himself down courageously among the crowds.

**14:44** As they swiftly drew back and an interval opened up, he fell right in the middle of the empty space.

**14:45** Yet still breathing and inflamed with fierce passion, he rose up while his blood gushed forth like a fountain; and though his wounds were grievous, he ran through the midst of the crowds and stood upon a steep rock,

**14:46** and now completely drained of blood, he tore out his entrails, and taking them in both hands, hurled them upon the crowds; and calling upon Him who is Sovereign over life and spirit to restore these very things to him again, in this manner he died.

## Translator Notes

- **14:3** — Greek amixias (also in 14:38) literally means 'non-intercourse' or 'separation,' referring to the period of estrangement and conflict when faithful Jews refused mingling with Gentiles.
- **14:7** — The Greek text here reads deuteron ('a second time'), whereas other Greek manuscripts read deuro ('here').
- **14:16** — The Greek text reads Lessaou ('Lessau'), whereas other manuscripts read Dessaou ('Dessau').
- **14:17** — The Greek text reads bradeos ('slowly'), whereas some manuscripts read bracheos ('slightly'); aphasian denotes sudden speechlessness or consternation.
- **14:44** — Greek keneona can mean either the 'empty space' opened by the retreating crowd or anatomically the 'flank' or 'abdomen' upon which Razis fell.
