## Text

**2:1** Now it is found in the records that Jeremiah the prophet commanded those who were being carried away to take some of the fire, as has been noted;

**2:2** and how the prophet charged those who were being carried away, having given them the law, that they should not forget the statutes of the Lord, nor be led astray in their minds upon seeing images of gold and silver and the adornment around them.

**2:3** And speaking other such words, he exhorted them that the law should not depart from their heart.

**2:4** It was also in the writing that the prophet, having received a divine revelation, commanded that the tabernacle and the ark should go with him, and how he went forth to the mountain where Moses went up and beheld the inheritance of God.

**2:5** And when Jeremiah came there, he found a cavernous chamber, and he brought the tabernacle and the ark and the altar of incense into it, and blocked up the entrance.

**2:6** And some of those who followed with him came to mark the way, but they could not find it.

**2:7** When Jeremiah learned of this, he rebuked them and said, "Indeed, the place shall be unknown until God gathers the congregation of the people together and mercy comes.

**2:8** And then the Lord will disclose these things, and the glory of the Lord and the cloud shall appear, as it was shown in the time of Moses, and as Solomon also prayed that the place might be greatly consecrated."

**2:9** It was also declared how he, possessing wisdom, offered the sacrifice of dedication and of the completion of the temple.

**2:10** Just as Moses prayed to the Lord, and fire came down from heaven and consumed the things of the sacrifice, so Solomon prayed to the Lord, and the fire came down and consumed the burnt offerings.

**2:11** And Moses said, "Because the sin offering was not eaten, it was consumed."

**2:12** In like manner Solomon also kept the eight days.

**2:13** The same things were also recounted in the records and in the memoirs concerning Nehemiah, and how, founding a library, he gathered together the books about the kings and prophets, and the writings of David, and letters of kings concerning votive offerings.

**2:14** In like manner Judas also gathered together these things that had been scattered on account of the war that happened to us, and they are with us.

**2:15** Therefore, if you have need of any of them, send those who will carry them to you.

**2:16** Since, therefore, we are about to celebrate the purification, we have written to you; you will therefore do well to keep the days.

**2:17** Now it is God who has saved all His people, and has restored His inheritance to all, and the kingdom, and the priesthood, and the sanctification,

**2:18** just as He promised through the law. For we have hope in God that He will swiftly have mercy upon us, and will gather us together from under heaven into the holy place; for He has delivered us out of great evils, and has purified the place.

**2:19** Now the things concerning Judas Maccabeus and his brothers, and the purification of the greatest temple, and the dedication of the altar,

**2:20** and further the wars against Antiochus Epiphanes and his son Eupator,

**2:21** and the manifestations that came from heaven to those who strove bravely with honor for Judaism, so that, though few in number, they plundered the whole country and pursued the barbarian multitudes,

**2:22** and recovered the temple renowned throughout the whole inhabited world, and liberated the city, and restored the laws that were about to be abolished, while the Lord with all forbearance became gracious to them—

**2:23** all these things, which have been set forth by Jason of Cyrene in five books, we shall attempt to abridge into a single volume.

**2:24** For considering the flood of figures and the difficulty that exists for those who wish to immerse themselves in the narratives of the history because of the abundance of the material,

**2:25** we have taken care to provide delight for those who wish to read, ease for those who are eager to commit it to memory, and profit for all who come to it.

**2:26** And for us who have undertaken the toil of this abridgment, the task is no easy thing, but a matter of sweat and sleeplessness,

**2:27** just as it is no light thing for one who prepares a banquet and seeks the benefit of others; nevertheless, for the sake of the gratitude of the many, we shall gladly endure the toil,

**2:28** leaving to the author the exact investigation of every detail, while ourselves lacking strength to follow the outlines of the abridgment.

**2:29** For just as the master builder of a new house must be concerned with the entire structure, while the one who undertakes to renovate and paint must consider what is suitable for its adornment, so, I think, it is with us also.

**2:30** To explore the ground, and to range widely in discourse, and to inquire closely into particulars belongs to the original author of the history;

**2:31** but to pursue brevity of expression and to forego an exhaustive treatment of the subject must be granted to the one who makes the summary.

**2:32** From here, then, let us begin the narrative, having added only this much to what has already been said; for it is foolish to enlarge what precedes the history while abridging the history itself.

## Translator Notes

- **2:14** — The Greek text reads "these things" (tauta) rather than "all things" (panta) found in some manuscripts.
- **2:28** — The Greek text reads "lacking strength" (atonountes), whereas other manuscripts read "taking pains" (diaponountes).
- **2:29** — The Greek text reads "to renovate" (enkainizein), whereas other witnesses read "to do encaustic painting" (enkaiein).
