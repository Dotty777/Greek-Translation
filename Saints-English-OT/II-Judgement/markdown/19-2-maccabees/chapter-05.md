## Text

**5:1** Now about this time Antiochus undertook his second expedition into Egypt.

**5:2** And it came to pass throughout the whole city, for nearly forty days, that horsemen were seen charging through the air, wearing robes interwoven with gold and armed with lances in squadrons,

**5:3** and drawings of swords, and troops of cavalry drawn up in battle array, and assaults and charges taking place from both sides, and brandishings of shields, and multitudes of toils, and volleys of darts, and flashings of golden ornaments, and breastplates of every kind.

**5:4** Therefore all prayed that this apparition had occurred for good.

**5:5** Now when a false rumor arose that Antiochus had departed this life, Jason took no fewer than a thousand men and suddenly made an assault upon the city; and when those upon the wall were driven back and at last the city was already being captured, Menelaus fled into the citadel.

**5:6** And Jason carried out slaughters of his own citizens without mercy, not considering that good fortune against one's kinsmen is the greatest misfortune, but supposing that he was setting up trophies over enemies and not over his own countrymen.

**5:7** Yet he did not obtain the rule, but receiving shame as the outcome of his conspiracy, he departed once more as a fugitive into Ammanitis.

**5:8** At last, therefore, he met with an evil downfall: confined by Aretas the tyrant of the Arabians, fleeing from city to city, pursued by all, hated as a deserter of the laws, and abhorred as the executioner of his country and fellow citizens, he was cast out into Egypt.

**5:9** He who had banished many from their native land perished in a foreign land, having set sail to the Lacedaemonians as though to obtain shelter through their kinship;

**5:10** and he who had cast out a multitude unburied was himself unmourned, and had no share in any funeral rite or ancestral law.

**5:11** Now when word of what had happened reached the king, he concluded that Judea was in revolt; therefore, marching out from Egypt furious as a wild beast in his soul, he took the city by force of arms.

**5:12** And he commanded his soldiers to cut down without mercy those whom they encountered, and to slay those who went up into the houses.

**5:13** So there was a destruction of young and old, a wiping out of boys and women and children, and slaughters of virgins and infants.

**5:14** And eighty thousand perished in three days in all, forty thousand in violent hand-to-hand conflict, and no fewer than those who were slain were sold.

**5:15** Yet not content with these things, he dared to enter into the most holy temple of all the earth, having Menelaus as his guide, who had become a traitor both to the laws and to his country,

**5:16** and taking the sacred vessels with his polluted hands, and sweeping away with his profane hands the offerings dedicated by many kings for the advancement, glory, and honor of the place.

**5:17** And Antiochus was lifted up in his mind, not perceiving that because of the sins of those who dwelt in the city the Sovereign Lord was provoked to anger for a little while, and therefore disregard had come upon the place.

**5:18** Now if it had not come to pass that they were entangled in many sins, this man too, just like Heliodorus who was sent by King Seleucus to inspect the treasury, would immediately have been scourged as soon as he pressed forward and turned back from his presumption.

**5:19** Yet the Lord did not choose the nation for the sake of the place, but the place for the sake of the nation.

**5:20** Therefore the place itself also, having shared in the calamities that befell the nation, afterward partook of its blessings; and that which was forsaken in the wrath of the Almighty was restored again with all glory in the reconciliation of the great Sovereign Lord.

**5:21** So Antiochus carried off one thousand eight hundred talents from the temple and departed swiftly to Antioch, supposing in his arrogance that he could make the land navigable and the sea passable on foot, because of the loftiness of his heart.

**5:22** And he also left behind governors to afflict the race: in Jerusalem, Philip, a Phrygian by birth, and more barbarous in his character than the one who appointed him;

**5:23** and at Gerizim, Andronicus; and besides these, Menelaus, who exalted himself over his fellow citizens worse than all the others, having a hostile disposition toward the Jewish citizens.

**5:24** He also sent Apollonius, the commander of the Mysians, with an army of twenty-two thousand, commanding him to slay all who were of full age, and to sell the women and the younger ones.

**5:25** Now when he arrived in Jerusalem and feigned to be a man of peace, he held back until the holy day of the Sabbath; and finding the Jews resting from labor, he ordered those under him to turn out under arms.

**5:26** And he put to the sword all who had come out to watch the spectacle, and rushing into the city with his armed men, he subdued great multitudes.

**5:27** But Judas, who was also called Maccabeus, withdrew with about nine others, and lived in the mountains after the manner of wild beasts with those who were with him; and they continued feeding on wild herbs, so as not to partake of the defilement.

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — The Greek text reads kamaton ("toils" or "exertions"), whereas some manuscripts and editions read kamakon ("spear-shafts" or "pikes").
- **5:10** — The Greek text reads patroou nomou ("ancestral law"), whereas other manuscripts read patroou taphou ("ancestral tomb").
- **5:24** — Greek mysarches ("commander of the Mysians") refers to an officer leading Mysian mercenary troops in the Seleucid army.
- **5:26** — The Greek text reads katestesen ("put down" or "subdued"), whereas other witnesses read katestrosen ("struck down") or katesphaxen ("slaughtered").
