## Text

**10:1** Now Maccabeus and those with him, as the Lord led them forward, recovered the temple and the city.

**10:2** And they tore down the altars constructed in the marketplace by the foreigners, and also the sacred precincts.

**10:3** Having cleansed the sanctuary, they made another altar; and striking stones and taking fire from them, they offered up sacrifices after a period of two years, together with incense and lamps, and made the setting forth of the loaves.

**10:4** When they had done these things, they implored the Lord, falling prostrate upon their bellies, that they might no longer fall into such evils, but that if they should ever sin, they might be dealt with by Him with gentleness and not be delivered over to blasphemous and barbarous nations.

**10:5** Now on the very day on which the sanctuary had been profaned by foreigners, it came to pass that on that same day the cleansing of the temple took place, on the twenty-fifth of the same month, which is Chislev.

**10:6** And with gladness they observed eight days in the manner of the Feast of Tabernacles, remembering how a little time before, during the Feast of Tabernacles, they had been roaming in the mountains and in the caves after the manner of wild beasts.

**10:7** Therefore, bearing leafy wands and beautiful branches, and also palm branches, they gave thanks to Him who had given them success in cleansing His own place.

**10:8** And they ordained by a common edict and decree for the whole nation of the Jews to observe the tenth days year by year.

**10:9** Now such were the events concerning the end of Antiochus, who was called Epiphanes.

**10:10** Now we will declare the events concerning the supreme Antiochus, who was the son of that ungodly man, summarizing the evils that afflicted the cities.

**10:11** For when he succeeded to the kingdom, he appointed a certain Lysias over the affairs of state, and as chief governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia.

**10:12** For Ptolemy, who was called Macron, taking the lead in maintaining justice toward the Jews because of the wrong that had been done against them, endeavored to conduct affairs with them peacefully.

**10:13** Therefore, being accused by the Friends before Eupator and hearing himself called a traitor at every turn—because he had abandoned Cyprus, which had been entrusted to him by Philometor, and had gone over to Antiochus Epiphanes, and had not upheld his noble office in a noble manner—he poisoned himself and departed this life.

**10:14** Now Gorgias, upon becoming governor of those regions, maintained mercenary troops and at every turn kept up war against his own people.

**10:15** Together with these events, the Idumeans also, being in control of strategic strongholds, harassed the Jews, and taking in those who had fled from Jerusalem, they attempted to keep up the war.

**10:16** But those with Maccabeus, having offered solemn supplication and implored God to become their ally, rushed against the strongholds of the Idumeans.

**10:17** Assaulting these vigorously, they gained control of the positions, drove back all who were fighting upon the wall, and slaughtered those who fell into their hands, slaying no fewer than twenty thousand.

**10:18** Now when no fewer than nine thousand fled together into two exceedingly strong towers that had everything needed for a siege,

**10:19** Maccabeus himself departed for places of urgent need, leaving behind Simon and Joseph, and also Zacchaeus and those with him, in sufficient number to besiege them.

**10:20** But those with Simon, overcome by the love of money, were bribed with silver by some of those in the towers; and receiving seventy thousand drachmas, they allowed some of them to slip away.

**10:21** When they reported to Maccabeus concerning what had happened, he assembled the leaders of the people and brought an accusation that those men had sold their brethren for silver by releasing their enemies against them.

**10:22** So he put these men to death for becoming traitors, and immediately captured the two towers.

**10:23** And succeeding in everything with weapons in his hands, he destroyed more than twenty thousand in the two strongholds.

**10:24** Now Timothy, who had previously been defeated by the Jews, gathered together a vast multitude of foreign forces and assembled no small number of horses from Asia, and he came as though to take Judea captive by the spear.

**10:25** As he drew near, those with Maccabeus turned to supplication to God, sprinkling earth upon their heads and girding their loins with sackcloth,

**10:26** and falling down upon the step opposite the altar, they implored Him to be gracious to them, to be an enemy to their enemies and an adversary to their adversaries, just as the law also declares.

**10:27** When they had finished their prayer, they took up their weapons and advanced a considerable distance from the city; and when they drew near to the enemy, they kept to themselves.

**10:28** Just as the dawn was spreading abroad, both sides joined battle: the one having as a pledge of success and victory, along with valor, their refuge in the Lord, while the other appointed their fury as the leader of the conflict.

**10:29** When the battle grew fierce, there appeared to the adversaries out of heaven five resplendent men upon horses with golden bridles; and two of them, leading the Jews,

**10:30** took Maccabeus into their midst and, shielding him with their own full armor, kept him unharmed, while upon the enemies they hurled arrows and thunderbolts; therefore, confounded with blindness, they were cut to pieces, filled with panic.

**10:31** And twenty thousand five hundred were slaughtered, and six hundred horsemen.

**10:32** But Timothy himself fled for refuge into a stronghold called Gazara, a very strong fortress, where Chaereas was commander.

**10:33** And those with Maccabeus eagerly besieged the fortress for forty days.

**10:34** And those within, trusting in the strength of the place, blasphemed exceedingly and uttered profane words.

**10:35** Now as the twenty-fifth day was dawning, young men from among those with Maccabeus, inflamed in their anger because of the blasphemies, assaulted the wall, and manfully and with savage fury cut down everyone who fell in their way.

**10:36** And others likewise climbed up during the diversion against those within, set fire to the towers, and kindling fires, burned the blasphemers alive; while others broke down the gates, and admitting the rest of the force, took possession of the city,

**10:37** and they slaughtered Timothy, who had hidden himself in a certain cistern, along with his brother Chaereas and Apollophanes.

**10:38** When they had accomplished these things, with hymns and thanksgiving they blessed the Lord, who bestows great benefits upon Israel and grants them the victory.

## Translator Notes

- **10:4** — The Greek text omits a verb after "with gentleness," so "dealt with" is supplied to complete the clause; other Greek manuscripts read "be disciplined" (paideuesthai).
- **10:8** — The Greek text reads "the tenth days" (tas dekatas hemeras), whereas other manuscripts read "these days" (tasde tas hemeras).
- **10:10** — The Greek text reads "the supreme Antiochus" (ton hypaton Antiochon) and "of the cities" (ton poleon), whereas other manuscripts read "Antiochus Eupator" (as in v. 13) and "of the wars" (ton polemon).
- **10:14** — The Greek text reads "against his own people" (pros tous idious), whereas other manuscripts read "against the Jews" (pros tous Ioudaious).
- **10:33** — The Greek text reads "forty days" here and "the twenty-fifth day" (without a number before "young men") in v. 35; other manuscripts read "four days" here and "the fifth day, twenty young men" in v. 35.
