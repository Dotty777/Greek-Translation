## Text

**1:1** To the brethren, the Jews throughout Egypt, the brethren, the Jews in Jerusalem and those in the country of Judea, send greetings and good peace.

**1:2** And may God do good to you, and remember His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, His faithful servants.

**1:3** And may He give to all of you a heart to worship Him and to do His will with a great heart and a willing soul.

**1:4** And may He open your heart in His law and in His statutes, and make peace.

**1:5** And may He hear your supplications, and be reconciled to you, and not forsake you in an evil time.

**1:6** And now we are here praying for you.

**1:7** In the reign of Demetrius, in the one hundred and sixty-ninth year, we the Jews wrote to you in the tribulation and in the extremity that came upon us in these years, from the time that Jason and those with him revolted from the holy land and the kingdom,

**1:8** and burned the gate, and shed innocent blood. Then we pleaded with the Lord and were heard; we offered sacrifice and fine flour, and brought forth the lamps, and set out the loaves.

**1:9** And now see that you keep the days of the Feast of Tabernacles of the month of Chislev.

**1:10** In the eighty-eighth year, in Jerusalem. And those in Judea, and the council of elders, and Judas, to Aristobulus, teacher of King Ptolemy, who is of the lineage of the anointed priests, and to the Jews in Egypt: greeting and good health.

**1:11** Having been saved by God out of great perils, we give great thanks to Him, as those who stood arrayed in battle against a king.

**1:12** For He Himself drove out into Persia those who were arrayed in battle in the holy city.

**1:13** For when the leader arrived with the force around him that seemed irresistible, they were cut to pieces in the temple of Nanea, through deceit employed by the priests of Nanea.

**1:14** For as though to marry her, Antiochus and the friends with him came to the place, in order to take the great treasures by way of a dowry.

**1:15** And when the priests of the temple of Nanea had set them forth, and he had entered with a few men into the enclosure of the sanctuary, they closed the temple as soon as Antiochus came in,

**1:16** and opening the secret door in the ceiling, they hurled stones and struck down the leader as with thunderbolts; and cutting them to pieces, they threw the heads out to those outside.

**1:17** Blessed in all things be our God, who gave over those who acted impiously.

**1:18** Since we are about to celebrate the purification of the temple on the twenty-fifth day of Chislev, we have thought it necessary to inform you, so that you yourselves also may keep the Feast of Tabernacles and of the fire, as when Nehemiah, who built the temple and the altar at that time, offered sacrifices.

**1:19** For indeed, when our fathers were being led away into Persia, the devout priests secretly took some of the fire of the altar and hid it in the hollow of a waterless pit resembling a cistern, where they made it secure, so that the place was unknown to all.

**1:20** Now after many years had passed, when it pleased God, Nehemiah, having been sent by the king of Persia, sent the descendants of the priests who had hidden it for the fire.

**1:21** When they reported to us that they had not found fire, but thick water, he commanded them to draw it up and bring it. And when the things pertaining to the sacrifices were brought up, Nehemiah commanded the priests to sprinkle with the water both the wood and what was laid upon it.

**1:22** And when this was done and some time had passed, and the sun, which was overcast, shone forth, a great fire blazed up, so that all marveled.

**1:23** And the priests offered prayer while the sacrifice was being consumed—both the priests and all—with Jonathan leading and the rest responding, as did Nehemiah.

**1:24** And the prayer was in this manner: "O Lord God, Creator of all things, awesome and strong and righteous and merciful, the only King and gracious One,

**1:25** the only Provider, the only righteous and Almighty and eternal One, who delivers Israel from every evil, who made the fathers chosen and sanctified them:

**1:26** accept the sacrifice on behalf of all Your people Israel, and guard Your portion and consecrate it.

**1:27** Gather together our dispersion, set free those who are in bondage among the nations, look upon the despised and abhorred, and let the nations know that You are our God.

**1:28** Torment those who oppress and deal insolently in arrogance;

**1:29** plant Your people in Your holy place, just as Moses said."

**1:30** Then the priests sang the hymns.

**1:31** And when the things of the sacrifice were consumed, Nehemiah commanded that the remaining water also be poured over large stones.

**1:32** And when this was done, a flame was kindled; but when the light shone back from the altar, it was consumed.

**1:33** Now when the matter became known, and it was reported to the king of the Persians that in the place where the priests who had been carried away had hidden the fire, the water had appeared, with which Nehemiah and those with him also purified the sacrifices,

**1:34** the king, having examined the matter, enclosed the place and made it sacred;

**1:35** and from these things the king received many revenues, and also gave a share of them.

**1:36** Now Nehemiah and those with him called this Nephthar, which is interpreted "Purification"; but by most people it is called Nephthar.

## Translator Notes

- **1:8** — The Greek text reads "brought forth" (exegagomen) rather than "lighted" or "kindled" (exepsamen) found in some manuscripts.
- **1:10** — The Greek text reads "eighty-eighth" (without "one hundred"), whereas other manuscripts read "one hundred and eighty-eighth" (Seleucid era, 124 BC); it also places a period after "in Jerusalem" rather than opening the salutation with "Those in Jerusalem."
- **1:21** — The Greek text shifts unexpectedly to the first-person plural ("reported to us"), reflecting the style of the underlying letter.
- **1:35** — The Greek text omits the verb "favored" (echarizeto) found in some manuscripts after the relative pronoun, creating an elliptical clause rendered here as referring to the revenues of the sacred enclosure.
- **1:36** — The Greek text reads "Nephthar" in both places, whereas some manuscripts read "Nephthai" (akin to "naphtha") in the second occurrence.
