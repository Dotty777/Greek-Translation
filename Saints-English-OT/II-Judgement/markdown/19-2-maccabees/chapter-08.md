## Text

**8:1** Now Judas, who is also called Maccabeus, and those with him entered secretly into the villages and summoned their kinsmen; and gathering to themselves those who had remained in Judaism, they assembled about six thousand men.

**8:2** And they called upon the Lord to look upon the people who were oppressed by all, and also to have compassion on the temple that had been profaned by ungodly men;

**8:3** and to have mercy also on the city that was being destroyed and about to be leveled to the ground, and to hear the blood that cried out to Him;

**8:4** and also to remember the lawless destruction of the innocent infants, and the blasphemies committed against His name, and to show His hatred of evil.

**8:5** Now once Maccabeus had assembled an organized force, he became irresistible to the Gentiles, for the wrath of the Lord had turned to mercy.

**8:6** Coming unexpectedly upon cities and districts, he set them on fire; and seizing strategic places and routing not a few of the enemies,

**8:7** he especially chose the nights as allies for such assaults; and the report of his valor resounded everywhere.

**8:8** Now when Philip saw that the man was gaining ground little by little and advancing more frequently in his successes, he wrote to Ptolemy, the governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, to come to the aid of the king's affairs.

**8:9** And he promptly appointed Nicanor the son of Patroclus, one of the chief Friends, and sent him out, placing under his command no fewer than twenty thousand Gentiles of all nations to wipe out the entire race of Judea; and with him they also appointed Gorgias, a general and a man well experienced in matters of war.

**8:10** Now Nicanor determined to make up for the king the tribute owed to the Romans, amounting to two thousand talents, from the enslavement of the Jews.

**8:11** Immediately he sent to the coastal cities, inviting them to purchase Jewish slaves, promising to deliver ninety slaves for a talent—not expecting the judgment from the Almighty that was about to overtake him.

**8:12** Word reached Judas concerning the advance of Nicanor; and when he informed those with him of the approach of the army,

**8:13** the cowardly and those who distrusted the justice of God fled and removed themselves from the region;

**8:14** while the others sold all that remained to them, and at the same time implored the Lord to deliver those who had been sold by the impious Nicanor before they had even met him;

**8:15** and if not for their own sake, yet for the sake of the covenants made with their fathers, and because His venerable and majestic name had been called upon them.

**8:16** Then Maccabeus gathered those who were with him, six thousand in number, and exhorted them not to be terrified by the captives nor to fear the vast multitude of the Gentiles who had come unjustly against them, but to contend nobly,

**8:17** keeping before their eyes the outrage lawlessly perpetrated by them against the holy place, and the cruel abuse of the mocked city, and moreover the overthrow of their ancestral way of life.

**8:18** "For they trust in weapons and in deeds of daring," he said, "but we trust in the Almighty God, who is able with a single nod to overtake both those who are coming against us and the whole world."

**8:19** Moreover, he recounted to them the acts of deliverance granted in the days of their forefathers, both that in the time of Sennacherib, when one hundred and eighty-five thousand perished,

**8:20** and the battle that took place in Babylonia against the Galatians, how all who came to the engagement were eight thousand together with four thousand Macedonians, and when the Macedonians were hard pressed, the six thousand destroyed the one hundred and twenty thousand because of the help that came to them from heaven, and they took great plunder.

**8:21** Having by these words made them courageous and ready to die for the laws and their country, he divided the army into four parts.

**8:22** He appointed his brothers as leaders of each division—Simon, Joseph, and Jonathan—placing one thousand five hundred men under each of them,

**8:23** and also Eleazar. Then, having read aloud the sacred book and given the watchword, "God's Help," he himself took the lead of the first division and engaged Nicanor in battle.

**8:24** And with the Almighty becoming their ally, they slew more than nine thousand of the enemies, wounded and maimed in their limbs the greater part of Nicanor's army, and forced them all to flee.

**8:25** They seized the money of those who had come to buy them; and after pursuing them for a considerable distance, they turned back from the region, being constrained;

**8:26** for it was the observance before the Sabbath, for which reason they did not prolong their pursuit of them.

**8:27** When they had collected their weapons and stripped the spoils from the enemies, they occupied themselves with the Sabbath, exceedingly blessing and giving thanks to the Lord who had preserved them to that day, having appointed for them a beginning of mercy.

**8:28** After the Sabbath, having distributed a portion of the spoils to those who had been tortured, and to the widows and the orphans, they divided the rest among themselves and their children.

**8:29** When they had done these things and offered a common supplication, they implored the merciful Lord to be wholly reconciled to His servants.

**8:30** And engaging in battle with the forces of Timothy and Bacchides, they slew more than twenty thousand of them, gained complete mastery of very high fortresses, and divided abundant plunder, making equal shares for themselves and for the tortured, the orphans, the widows, and also the aged.

**8:31** Having collected their weapons, they carefully stored all of them in strategic places, and brought the rest of the spoils to Jerusalem.

**8:32** They also slew the commander of Timothy's forces, a most wicked man who had inflicted many griefs upon the Jews.

**8:33** And while celebrating the victory in their homeland, they burned those who had set fire to the sacred gates, including Callisthenes, who had fled into a small house; and thus he received the fitting reward for his impiety.

**8:34** Now the thrice-accursed Nicanor, who had brought three thousand merchants for the sale of the Jews,

**8:35** having been humbled through the help of the Lord by those whom he considered to be the least of all, laid aside his glorious apparel and, making his way alone like a runaway slave through the inland country, arrived at Antioch, having prospered beyond all measure in the destruction of his army.

**8:36** And he who had undertaken to pay the tribute to the Romans from the captives of those in Jerusalem now proclaimed that the Jews had a Champion, and that for this reason the Jews were invulnerable, because they followed the laws ordained by Him.

## Translator Notes

- **8:16** — The Greek source text reads "by the captives" (tois desmiois), whereas other manuscripts read "by the enemies" (tois polemiois).
- **8:20** — The Greek source text names eight thousand entering the battle and then refers to "the six thousand" who defeated the one hundred and twenty thousand; other witnesses read "the eight thousand" in both places.
- **8:23** — With the nominative participle paranagnous, the Greek source text presents Judas himself as reading the sacred book, whereas other witnesses use an infinitive assigning the reading to Eleazar.
- **8:25** — The Greek source text reads "from the region, being constrained" here and "the observance (dike) before the Sabbath" in verse 26, where other manuscripts read "constrained by the hour" and "the hour before the Sabbath."
