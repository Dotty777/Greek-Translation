## Text

**2:1** And it came to pass in the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, that wine was before me; and I took the wine and gave it to the king, and there was no other before him.

**2:2** And the king said to me, "Why is your countenance sad, when you are not unwell? This is nothing other than sorrow of heart." And I was exceedingly afraid,

**2:3** and I said to the king, "May the king live forever! Why should my countenance not be sad, since the city, the house of the tombs of my fathers, has been laid waste, and its gates have been devoured by fire?"

**2:4** And the king said to me, "Concerning what do you make this request?" So I prayed to the God of heaven.

**2:5** And I said to the king, "If it seems good to the king, and if your servant is pleasing before you, send him to Judah, to the city of the tombs of my fathers, and I will rebuild it."

**2:6** And the king said to me—the concubine also sitting beside him—"How long will your journey be, and when will you return?" So it pleased the king, and he sent me; and I gave him an appointed time.

**2:7** And I said to the king, "If it seems good to the king, let him give me letters to the governors beyond the River, that they may conduct me through until I come to Judah,

**2:8** and a letter to Asaph, the keeper of the park that belongs to the king, that he may give me timber to roof the gates, and for the wall of the city, and for the house into which I shall enter." And the king granted them to me, according to the good hand of God.

**2:9** Then I came to the governors beyond the River, and gave them the king's letters. Now the king had sent with me captains of the army and horsemen.

**2:10** And Sanballat the Horonite heard of it, and it was grievous to them that a man had come to seek good for the sons of Israel.

**2:11** So I came to Jerusalem, and was there three days.

**2:12** And I arose in the night, I and a few men with me; and I told no one what God was putting into my heart to do with Israel, nor was there any beast with me except the beast on which I rode.

**2:13** And I went out by the Gate of Golela, and toward the mouth of the Spring of the Figs, and to the Dung Gate; and I was breaking at the wall of Jerusalem which they were tearing down, and its gates had been devoured by fire.

**2:14** Then I passed on to the Gate of Aina and to the King's Pool, and there was no room for the beast under me to pass through.

**2:15** And I went up along the wall of the brook by night, and I was breaking at the wall; and I was at the Valley Gate, and returned.

**2:16** And the guards did not know why I had gone and what I was doing; nor had I yet told the Jews, or the priests, or the nobles, or the rest who were doing the works.

**2:17** Then I said to them, "You see the distress in which we are, how Jerusalem lies desolate and its gates have been given to fire. Come, and let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, that we may no longer be a reproach."

**2:18** And I told them of the hand of God which is good upon me, and also the king's words which he had spoken to me; and I said, "Let us arise and build." And their hands were strengthened for good.

**2:19** And Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the Ammonite servant, and Geshem the Arab heard of it; and they laughed us to scorn, and came upon us and said, "What is this thing that you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?"

**2:20** And I returned an answer to them and said to them, "The God of heaven Himself will prosper us, and we are His pure servants, and we will build; but you have no portion, nor righteousness, nor memorial in Jerusalem."

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Greek text reads "before me" and "there was no other before him," differing from the Hebrew ("before him" and "I had not been sad in his presence").
- **2:6** — Greek pallake literally means "concubine," rendering the Hebrew term for a royal consort.
- **2:13** — Greek transliterates the Valley Gate as "Golela," renders the spring as "the Spring of the Figs," and uses syntribon ("breaking/shattering," also in 2:15) where the Hebrew root means "inspecting."
- **2:20** — Greek reads katharoi ("pure/clean"), whereas the Hebrew reads "we will arise."
