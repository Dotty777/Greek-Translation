## Text

**8:1** And when the seventh month arrived, while the children of Israel were in their cities, all the people assembled as a single man in the broad square before the Water Gate; and they told Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses, which He had commanded Israel.

**8:2** So Ezra the priest brought the law before the congregation, from man to woman and everyone able to comprehend what was heard, on the first day of the seventh month.

**8:3** And he read from it from the hour of the sun's breaking forth until midday in the presence of the men and the women, and they understood; and the ears of all the people were turned toward the book of the law.

**8:4** Now Ezra the scribe stood upon a wooden platform, and he stationed next to him Mattathias, Shemaiah, Ananiah, Uriah, Hilkiah, and Maaseiah on his right hand, and on his left Pedaiah, Mishael, Malchijah, and Zechariah.

**8:5** And Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people, because he stood higher than the people; and it came to pass that when he opened it, all the people stood up.

**8:6** And he blessed the Lord, the great God; and all the people answered and declared, "Amen," and they bowed low and worshiped the Lord with their faces to the earth.

**8:7** And Jeshua, Benaiah, and Sherebiah were instructing the people in the law, while the people remained in their station.

**8:8** So they read from the book of the law of God, and Ezra taught and expounded in the knowledge of the Lord, and the people grasped the meaning of the reading.

**8:9** Then Nehemiah spoke, while Ezra the priest and scribe and the Levites were instructing the people, and they declared to all the people, "This day is holy to the Lord our God; do not mourn nor weep." For all the people were weeping as they heard the words of the law.

**8:10** And he said to them, "Go forth, eat rich foods and drink sweet beverages, and send a portion to those who have nothing, for this day is holy to our Lord; and do not falter, for He is our strength."

**8:11** Likewise the Levites quieted the people, saying, "Be still, for the day is holy, and do not be downcast."

**8:12** So all the people departed to eat and drink, to dispatch portions, and to celebrate with exceeding gladness, because they understood the words which he had made known to them.

**8:13** And on the second day the chiefs of the ancestral houses gathered together with all the people, the priests, and the Levites to Ezra the scribe, in order to give careful heed to all the words of the law.

**8:14** And they discovered written in the law which the Lord had commanded Moses that the children of Israel should dwell in booths during the feast in the seventh month,

**8:15** and that they should sound trumpets throughout all their cities and in Jerusalem. Therefore Ezra said, "Go out to the mountain and gather leaves of olive, and leaves of cypress wood, and leaves of myrtle, and leaves of palms, and leaves of thick-leaved trees, to build booths according to what is written."

**8:16** So the people went out and brought them, and constructed booths for themselves, each man upon his own rooftop, and in their courtyards, and in the courts of the house of God, and in the broad squares of the city, even as far as the Gate of Ephraim.

**8:17** Thus the entire congregation of those who had returned from captivity constructed booths and dwelt in booths; for from the days of Joshua the son of Nun until that day the children of Israel had not done so. And there was great gladness.

**8:18** And he read from the book of the law of God day by day, from the first day until the final day; and they kept the feast seven days, and on the eighth day held a closing solemnity.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — The Septuagint does not name "the Lord" explicitly as the subject of "commanded," leaving the clause simply "which He commanded Israel" (or "which he commanded Israel," referring to Moses).
- **8:4** — The Septuagint uses an active verb ("he stationed") and lists fewer men standing beside Ezra (six on his right and four on his left) than the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **8:8** — Distinctively, the Septuagint names Ezra as teaching and expounding "in the knowledge of the Lord" during the public reading.
- **8:10** — Where the Hebrew text reads "the joy of the Lord is your strength," the Septuagint reads concisely "for He is our strength" (or "for it is our strength").
- **8:15** — The Septuagint specifies sounding trumpets across the cities and explicitly identifies Ezra as the speaker commanding the people to gather branches.
