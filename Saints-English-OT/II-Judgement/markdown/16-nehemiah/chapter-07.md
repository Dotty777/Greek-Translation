## Translator Notes

- **7:2** — The Greek text names both Nehemiah's brother and the ruler of the citadel "Hananiah" (where the Hebrew text distinguishes "Hanani" and "Hananiah"); "citadel" translates the Greek loanword beira.
- **7:3** — "Until the sun is up" translates the Greek phrase meaning literally "until together with the sun," where the Hebrew text reads "until the sun is hot."
- **7:5** — Greek synodia ("company" or "caravan") stands here where the Hebrew text refers to enrollment by genealogy.
- **7:7** — The Greek list includes Ezra and treats Hebrew mispar ("the number of") as a personal name, Masphar, while also reading Naamiah, Maspheran, and Batoi, and concluding with "the men of the son of Israel" where the Hebrew has "the men of the people of Israel."
- **7:33** — In verses 33–34, the Greek text transliterates the Hebrew word for "other" as aar ("Nabia-Aar" and "Elam-Aar"); throughout this register, the translation likewise preserves distinct Greek names and totals (such as Jobab in 7:11; Zathuia and Zathu in 7:13–14; Atopha with separate totals in 7:26; Tamal in 7:30; Halia in 7:32; and 1,250 in 7:34).
- **7:40** — The Greek text omits the priestly family name here ("Immer" in the Hebrew text), reading simply "The sons, one thousand and fifty-two." In 7:41, the Greek alphabetic numeral represents one thousand two hundred, yielding one thousand two hundred and forty-seven.
- **7:64** — The Greek passive verb (literally "were treated as next-of-kin" away from the priesthood) reflects reading the Hebrew root for "excluded as defiled" as its homonym meaning "to act as kinsman-redeemer."
- **7:65** — Hasersatha transliterates the Persian title for governor ("the Tirshatha"); "to give light" renders the Greek future participle photison, translating Hebrew "Urim" by its sense of "light" without mentioning "Thummim."
- **7:66** — In verses 66–69, the Greek text preserves shorter totals and omits verse 68 (reading 42,308 for the assembly, 337 for the servants, and 2,700 donkeys, where the Hebrew text has 42,360; 7,337; and lists horses, mules, camels, and 6,720 donkeys).
- **7:70** — In verses 70–72, Greek mechonoth transliterates a Hebrew word for "stands" or "bases" where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "priests' garments"; the Greek text also reads "to Nehemiah" (7:70), "of the year" rather than "of the work" (7:71), and a shorter text in 7:72.
