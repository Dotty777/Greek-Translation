## Text

**4:1** Now Gorgias took five thousand men and a thousand chosen horsemen, and the army departed by night,

**4:2** so as to fall upon the camp of the Jews and strike them suddenly; and the men of the citadel were his guides.

**4:3** And Judas heard of it, and he himself and the mighty men departed to strike the army of the king that was in Emmaus,

**4:4** while the forces were still scattered away from the camp.

**4:5** And Gorgias came to the camp of Judas by night, and found no one; so he searched for them in the mountains, for he said, "These men are fleeing from us."

**4:6** And at daybreak Judas appeared in the plain with three thousand men; yet they did not have shields and swords such as they desired.

**4:7** And they saw a strong camp of the Gentiles clad in armor, with cavalry encircling it; and these were men trained in war.

**4:8** And Judas said to the men who were with him, "Do not fear their multitude, and do not be dismayed at their onslaught.

**4:9** Remember how our fathers were saved at the Red Sea, when Pharaoh pursued them with an army.

**4:10** And now let us cry out to Heaven, whether He will favor us, and remember the covenant of the fathers, and crush this army before our face today.

**4:11** Then all the nations shall know that there is One who redeems and saves Israel."

**4:12** And the foreigners lifted up their eyes and saw them positioned close opposite them,

**4:13** and they went out from the camp to battle, and those who were with Judas sounded the trumpets.

**4:14** And they joined battle, and the Gentiles were crushed and fled into the plain.

**4:15** And all those in the rear fell by the sword; and they pursued them as far as Gazara, and to the plains of Judea, and of Azotus and Jamnia, and about three thousand of their men fell.

**4:16** Then Judas and the army returned from pursuing after them,

**4:17** and he said to the people, "Do not covet the spoils, for a battle is before us,

**4:18** and Gorgias and the army are in the mountain near us; rather, stand now against our enemies and fight them, and after these things take spoils, and with confidence."

**4:19** While Judas was still finishing these words, a certain detachment appeared peering out from the mountain.

**4:20** And they saw that the army had been put to flight and that the camp was being set on fire; for the smoke that was seen revealed what had happened.

**4:21** Now when they perceived these things, they were exceedingly afraid; and seeing also the army of Judas in the plain ready for battle,

**4:22** they all fled into the land of the Philistines.

**4:23** Then Judas turned back to plunder the camp, and he took much gold and silver, and blue, and purple, and sea-purple, and great riches.

**4:24** And as they returned, they sang hymns and blessed Heaven: "For His mercy is good forever."

**4:25** And a great deliverance came to Israel in that day.

**4:26** Now as many of the foreigners as escaped came and reported to Lysias all the things that had happened.

**4:27** And when he heard it all, he was confounded and disheartened, because things had not happened to Israel such as he desired, nor had events turned out as the king had commanded him.

**4:28** And in the following year he mustered sixty thousand chosen men and five thousand horsemen, so as to subdue them in war.

**4:29** And they came into Idumea and encamped at Bethzur, and Judas met them with ten thousand men.

**4:30** And he saw the mighty army, and he prayed and said, "Blessed are You, O Savior of Israel, who crushed the onslaught of the mighty one by the hand of Your servant David, and delivered the camp of the Philistines into the hands of Jonathan the son of Saul and his armorbearer.

**4:31** Shut up this army into the hands of Your people Israel, and let them be put to shame in their army and their horsemen.

**4:32** Give them cowardice, and melt the boldness of their strength, and let them be shaken at their destruction.

**4:33** Cast them down by the sword of those who love You, and let all who have seen Your name praise You with hymns."

**4:34** And they clashed with one another, and about five thousand men fell from the army of Lysias; indeed, they fell right before them.

**4:35** Now when Lysias saw the rout that had come upon his own ranks, and the boldness that had come upon those of Judas, and how ready they were either to live or to die nobly, he departed to Antioch; and he recruited mercenaries, intending, once he had grown greater in number, to come again into Judea.

**4:36** Then Judas and his brothers said, "Behold, our enemies are crushed; let us go up to cleanse the sanctuary and dedicate it."

**4:37** So all the army was gathered together, and they went up to Mount Zion.

**4:38** And they saw our sanctuary laid desolate, and the altar profaned, and the gates burned down, and shrubs growing in the courts as in a forest or as on one of the mountains, and the priests' chambers torn down.

**4:39** And they tore their garments, and made a great lamentation, and put ashes upon their heads.

**4:40** And they fell on their faces to the ground, and sounded the signal trumpets, and cried out to Heaven.

**4:41** Then Judas appointed men to fight against those who were in the citadel, until he should cleanse the sanctuary.

**4:42** And he chose blameless priests who delighted in the law;

**4:43** and they cleansed the sanctuary, and carried away the stones of defilement to an unclean place.

**4:44** And they took counsel concerning the altar of burnt offering that had been profaned, as to what they should do with it.

**4:45** And a good counsel came to them, to tear it down, lest it should ever become a reproach to them because the Gentiles had defiled it; so they tore down the altar.

**4:46** And they stored away the stones on the mountain of the house in a fitting place, until a prophet should come to give an answer concerning them.

**4:47** And they took whole stones according to the law, and built a new altar like the former one.

**4:48** And they built the sanctuary and the inner parts of the house, and he consecrated the courts.

**4:49** And they made new holy vessels, and brought the lampstand, and the altar of burnt offerings and of incense, and the table into the temple.

**4:50** And they burned incense upon the altar, and lit the lamps that were upon the lampstand, and they gave light in the temple.

**4:51** And they set loaves upon the table, and spread out the curtains; and they finished all the works that they had done.

**4:52** And they rose early in the morning on the twenty-fifth day of the ninth month, which is the month of Chislev, in the one hundred and forty-eighth year.

**4:53** And they offered sacrifice according to the law upon the new altar of burnt offerings which they had made.

**4:54** At the very season and on the very day on which the Gentiles had profaned it, on that day it was dedicated with songs, and harps, and lutes, and cymbals.

**4:55** And all the people fell upon their faces, and worshiped, and blessed toward Heaven the One who had prospered them.

**4:56** And they celebrated the dedication of the altar for eight days, and offered burnt offerings with gladness, and sacrificed a sacrifice of deliverance and praise.

**4:57** And they adorned the front of the temple with golden crowns and small shields, and he dedicated the gates and the priests' chambers, and they fitted them with doors.

**4:58** And there was exceedingly great gladness among the people, and the reproach of the Gentiles was turned away.

**4:59** And Judas and his brothers and all the assembly of Israel ordained that the days of the dedication of the altar should be kept in their seasons, year by year, for eight days, from the twenty-fifth day of the month of Chislev, with gladness and joy.

**4:60** And at that time they built up Mount Zion with high walls and strong towers all around, lest the Gentiles should ever come and trample them down, as they had done before.

**4:61** And they stationed a force there to guard it, and they fortified it to guard Bethzur, so that the people might have a stronghold facing Idumea.

## Translator Notes

- **4:12** — The Greek text reads echomenous ("positioned close") rather than the variant erchomenous ("coming"), depicting Judas's forces already drawn up directly opposite the enemy camp.
- **4:15** — The Greek text reads "the plains of Judea" (Ioudaias), whereas some manuscripts read "Idumea" (Idoumaias).
- **4:22** — Greek allophyloi literally means "foreigners" and is the standard Septuagint designation for the Philistines, whose coastal plain lay adjacent to Azotus and Jamnia.
- **4:23** — The Greek text has a conjunction separating "purple" and "sea-purple" (kai porphyran kai thalassian), whereas some manuscripts omit the conjunction and read "sea-purple" as a single phrase.
- **4:24** — The Greek text reads literally "For His mercy is good forever" (Hoti kalon eis ton aiona to eleos autou), a shorter liturgical refrain than witnesses that include a second clause ("for He is good, for His mercy endures forever").
- **4:33** — The Greek text reads hoi idontes ("those who have seen") rather than the variant hoi eidotes ("those who know").
- **4:38** — The Greek text reads "our sanctuary" (to hagiasma hemon), preserving the first-person plural possessive pronoun omitted in some manuscripts.
- **4:49** — The Greek text reads "the altar of burnt offerings and of incense" (to thysiasterion ton holokautomaton kai thymiamaton), whereas some manuscripts omit "of burnt offerings and," referencing only the altar of incense.
- **4:61** — The Greek text reads literally "and they fortified it to guard Bethzur" (kai ochyrosan auto terein ten Bethsoura), where the pronoun "it" likely anticipates Bethzur as a fortified stronghold.
