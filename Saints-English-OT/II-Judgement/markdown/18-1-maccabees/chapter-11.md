## Text

**11:1** And the king of Egypt gathered great forces, like the sand along the shore of the sea, and many ships; and he sought to seize the kingdom of Alexander by deceit, and to add it to his own kingdom.

**11:2** And he went forth into Syria with words of peace, and the people of the cities opened their gates to him and went out to meet him; for it was the command of King Alexander to meet him, because he was his father-in-law.

**11:3** Now as he entered into the cities of Ptolemais, he stationed forces as a garrison in each city.

**11:4** And when he drew near to Azotus, they showed him the temple of Dagon burned with fire, and Azotus and its surrounding towns demolished, and the bodies cast about, and those who had been burned, whom he had burned in the war; for they had made heaps of them along his way.

**11:5** And they recounted to the king what Jonathan had done, in order to cast blame upon him; but the king kept silent.

**11:6** And Jonathan met the king at Joppa with splendor, and they greeted one another and lodged there.

**11:7** And Jonathan journeyed with the king as far as the river called Eleutherus, and returned to Jerusalem.

**11:8** Now King Ptolemy took mastery over the cities of the coast as far as Seleucia by the sea, and devised wicked schemes concerning Alexander.

**11:9** And he sent ambassadors to King Demetrius, saying, "Come, let us make a covenant with one another, and I will give you my daughter whom Alexander has, and you shall reign over the kingdom of your father;

**11:10** for I regret that I gave my daughter to him, for he sought to slay me."

**11:11** And he cast blame upon him because he coveted his kingdom.

**11:12** And taking his daughter away from him, he gave her to Demetrius; and the countenance of Alexander was changed, and their enmity became manifest.

**11:13** Then Ptolemy entered Antioch and put on the diadem of Asia; thus he placed two diadems around his head, that of Asia and of Egypt.

**11:14** Now King Alexander was in Cilicia during those times, because the people of those regions were in revolt.

**11:15** And Alexander heard of it and came against him in war; and Ptolemy marched out and met him with a strong hand, and put him to flight.

**11:16** And Alexander fled into Arabia to be sheltered there; and King Ptolemy was exalted.

**11:17** And Zabdiel the Arab cut off the head of Alexander and sent it to Ptolemy.

**11:18** And King Ptolemy died on the third day, and those who were in his strongholds perished at the hands of those in the strongholds.

**11:19** And Demetrius became king in the one hundred and sixty-seventh year.

**11:20** In those days Jonathan gathered the men of Judea to assault the citadel that was in Jerusalem, and they built many engines of war against it.

**11:21** And certain men who hated their own nation, lawless men, went to the king and reported to him that Jonathan was besieging the citadel.

**11:22** And when he heard it, he was angry; and as soon as he heard it, he immediately broke camp and came to Ptolemais, and wrote to Jonathan that he should not besiege it, and that he should meet him to confer with him at Ptolemais with all speed.

**11:23** Yet when Jonathan heard this, he gave orders to continue the siege; and he chose some of the elders of Israel and of the priests, and put himself in peril.

**11:24** And taking silver and gold and garments and many other gifts, he went to the king at Ptolemais, and found favor in his sight.

**11:25** And certain lawless men from among the nation brought accusations against him.

**11:26** Yet the king dealt with him just as those before him had dealt with him, and exalted him in the presence of all his friends.

**11:27** And he confirmed to him the high priesthood and whatever other honors he had formerly possessed, and appointed him to be foremost among his first friends.

**11:28** And Jonathan requested the king to make Judea exempt from tribute, together with the three districts and Samaria, and they promised him three hundred talents.

**11:29** And the king consented, and wrote letters to Jonathan concerning all these things, after this manner:

**11:30** "King Demetrius to his brother Jonathan, greetings, and to the Jewish nation.

**11:31** The copy of the letter which we wrote to our kinsman Lasthenes concerning you, we have written to you also, that you may see it:

**11:32** 'King Demetrius to his father Lasthenes, greetings.

**11:33** We have determined to do good to the nation of the Jews, our friends, who observe what is right toward us, because of their goodwill toward us.

**11:34** Therefore we have confirmed to them the borders of Judea and the three districts of Aphaerema, Lydda, and Rathamin—they have been added to Judea from Samaria—and all that belongs to them, for all who offer sacrifice in Jerusalem, instead of the royal dues which the king formerly received from them year by year from the produce of the earth and the fruit of the trees.

**11:35** And as for the other things belonging to us from this time forward—the tithes and the tolls belonging to us, and the salt lakes, and the crowns belonging to us—we will grant all of them to them.

**11:36** And not one of these things shall be annulled from this time forward for all time.

**11:37** Now therefore, take care to make a copy of these things, and let it be given to Jonathan, and let it be placed on the holy mountain in a fitting and conspicuous place.'"

**11:38** And King Demetrius saw that the land was quiet before him and that nothing opposed him; so he dismissed all his forces, each man to his own place, except the foreign troops whom he had recruited from the islands of the nations. Therefore all the forces from the days of the fathers became hostile toward them.

**11:39** Now Trypho was formerly one of those who had followed Alexander; and seeing that all the forces were murmuring against Demetrius, he went to Sinmalcue the Arab, who was bringing up Antiochus, the young child of Alexander.

**11:40** And he pressed him persistently that he might hand the child over to him, so that he might reign in place of his father; and they reported to him all that Demetrius had done and the hatred which his forces held against him; and he remained there many days.

**11:41** And Jonathan sent word to King Demetrius that he should remove those in the citadel from Jerusalem and those in the strongholds, for they were waging war against Israel.

**11:42** And Demetrius sent word to Jonathan, saying, "Not only will I do these things for you and your nation, but I will indeed honor you and your nation with great honor, if I obtain an opportunity.

**11:43** Now therefore, you will do well to send us men who will fight alongside me, because all my forces have revolted."

**11:44** So Jonathan sent three thousand men, mighty in their strength, to Antioch; and they came to the king, and the king rejoiced at their arrival.

**11:45** And the people of the city gathered together into the midst of the city, about one hundred and twenty thousand men, and they wanted to slay the king.

**11:46** And the king fled into the palace, while the people of the city seized the thoroughfares of the city and began to fight.

**11:47** Then the king called the Jews for help, and they all rallied to him together; and they spread out through the city and slew in the city on that day about one hundred thousand.

**11:48** And they set the city on fire, and took much spoil on that day, and saved the king.

**11:49** And when the people of the city saw that the Jews had gained mastery over the city as they pleased, their courage failed, and they cried out to the king with supplication, saying,

**11:50** "Grant us the right hand of peace, and let the Jews cease fighting against us and the city."

**11:51** So they threw down their weapons and made peace; and the Jews were glorified before the king and in the sight of all who were in his kingdom, and they returned to Jerusalem carrying much spoil.

**11:52** Thus King Demetrius sat upon the throne of his kingdom, and the land was quiet before him.

**11:53** Yet he proved false to all that he had said, and became estranged from Jonathan, and did not repay the favors which he had rendered to him, but afflicted him greatly.

**11:54** Now after these things Trypho returned, and with him Antiochus, a very young child; and he became king and put on a diadem.

**11:55** And all the forces whom Demetrius had dismissed in disgrace rallied to him; and they fought against him, and he fled and was routed.

**11:56** And Trypho captured the elephants and took possession of Antioch.

**11:57** Then young Antiochus wrote to Jonathan, saying, "I confirm to you the high priesthood, and appoint you over the four districts, and to be one of the friends of the king."

**11:58** And he sent him golden vessels and a table service, and gave him authority to drink from golden vessels, to wear purple, and to have a golden clasp.

**11:59** And he appointed his brother Simon commander from the Ladder of Tyre to the borders of Egypt.

**11:60** Then Jonathan went forth and marched through the region beyond the river and among the cities, and all the forces of Syria rallied to him as allies; and he came to Ashkelon, and the people of the city met him with great honor.

**11:61** And he went from there to Gaza, and the people of Gaza shut their gates against him; so he laid siege around it, and burned its surrounding towns with fire and plundered them.

**11:62** Then the people of Gaza appealed to Jonathan, and he granted them the right hand of peace; and he took the sons of their rulers as hostages and sent them to Jerusalem, and he passed through the country as far as Damascus.

**11:63** And Jonathan heard that the commanders of Demetrius had arrived at Kedesh in Galilee with a great force, intending to remove him from his office.

**11:64** So he went to meet them, but he left his brother Simon in the country.

**11:65** And Simon encamped against Beth-zur, and fought against it for many days, and shut it up.

**11:66** And they asked him to receive the right hand of peace, and he granted it to them; then he expelled them from there, took possession of the city, and set a garrison over it.

**11:67** Now Jonathan and his army encamped by the water of Gennesar, and they rose early in the morning and marched into the plain of Nasor.

**11:68** And behold, an army of foreigners met him in the plain, and they had set an ambush against him in the mountains while they themselves met him face to face.

**11:69** Then those in ambush rose up out of their places and joined battle; and all those who were with Jonathan fled.

**11:70** Not even one of them remained, except Mattathias the son of Absalom and Judas the son of Chalphi, commanders of the host of the forces.

**11:71** Then Jonathan tore his garments, put earth upon his head, and prayed.

**11:72** And he turned back against them in battle and routed them, and they fled.

**11:73** When those who had fled from him saw this, they returned to him and pursued with him as far as Kedesh, even to their camp, and encamped there.

**11:74** And about three thousand men of the foreigners fell on that day; and Jonathan returned to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — The Greek text reads "into the cities of Ptolemais" (genitive Ptolemaidos); other manuscripts read the nominative Ptolemaios ("as Ptolemy entered the cities").
- **11:12** — The Greek text reads "the countenance of Alexander was changed"; other manuscripts read "he became estranged from Alexander."
- **11:18** — The repetition refers to Ptolemy's garrison soldiers stationed inside the fortified cities being slain by the local inhabitants of those strongholds.
- **11:28** — The Greek text includes "and Samaria" alongside "the three districts" and uses the plural "they promised"; other manuscripts read "the three districts of Samaria" (as in verse 34) with the singular "he promised."
- **11:35** — "Crowns" (Greek stephanous) refers to the royal crown tax, a customary gold tribute paid to Hellenistic rulers.
- **11:39** — Sinmalcue is the distinct Greek spelling in this text (Greek Sinmalchoue); other manuscripts read Imalkue.
- **11:50** — "Grant us the right hand of peace" renders the Greek idiom literally meaning "give us right hands," a standard Hellenistic expression for granting a pledge or terms of peace (also in verses 62 and 66).
- **11:56** — "The elephants" translates Greek ta theria (literally "the beasts"), the regular term in 1 Maccabees for war elephants.
- **11:67** — Nasor is the distinct Greek form in this text representing the biblical Hazor in Upper Galilee.
