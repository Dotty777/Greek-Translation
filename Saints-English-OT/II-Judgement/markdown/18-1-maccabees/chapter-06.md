## Text

**6:1** Now King Antiochus was passing through the upper provinces, and he heard that in Elymais in Persia there was a city renowned for wealth, silver, and gold;

**6:2** and that the temple in it was exceedingly rich, and there were golden shields, breastplates, and weapons, which Alexander son of Philip, the Macedonian king who first reigned over the Greeks, had left there.

**6:3** So he came and sought to capture the city and plunder it, but he was not able, because the matter became known to the people of the city.

**6:4** And they rose up against him for battle, so he fled and departed from there with great grief to return to Babylon.

**6:5** And someone came to him in Persia reporting that the armies that had gone into the land of Judah had been routed;

**6:6** that Lysias had marched first with a strong force and had been driven back before them, and that they had grown strong through the weapons they had taken from the armies they had cut down;

**6:7** and that they had torn down the abomination he had built upon the altar in Jerusalem, and had surrounded the sanctuary with high walls as before, and also Beth-zur, his city.

**6:8** Now it came to pass, when the king heard these words, that he was astounded and exceedingly shaken; and he fell upon his bed and sank into sickness from grief, because things had not turned out for him as he had intended.

**6:9** And he remained there many days, because great grief was renewed upon him, and he reckoned that he was dying.

**6:10** So he summoned all his friends and said to them, "Sleep departs from my eyes, and my heart has sunk from anxiety.

**6:11** And I said in my heart, 'To what tribulation have I come, and to what a great flood, in which I now am? For I was kind and beloved in my authority.'

**6:12** But now I remember the evils that I did in Jerusalem; and I took all the silver and gold vessels that were in it, and I sent forth to destroy the inhabitants of Judah without cause.

**6:13** I know that on account of these things these evils have found me; and behold, I am perishing in great grief in a foreign land."

**6:14** Then he summoned Philip, one of his friends, and appointed him over all his kingdom.

**6:15** And he gave him the diadem, his robe, and the signet ring, to guide Antiochus his son and raise him up to reign.

**6:16** And King Antiochus died there in the one hundred and forty-ninth year.

**6:17** And Lysias learned that the king had died, and he appointed Antiochus his son, whom he had raised from his youth, to reign in his place; and he called his name Eupator.

**6:18** Now those from the citadel were hemming Israel in around the sanctuary, continually seeking evil and providing support for the Gentiles.

**6:19** So Judas resolved to destroy them, and he assembled all the people to lay siege against them

**6:20** in the one hundred and fiftieth year; and he built catapult platforms and siege engines against them.

**6:21** And some went out from it, from the blockade, and some of the ungodly from Israel joined themselves to them.

**6:22** And they went to the king and said, "How long will you not execute judgment and avenge our brethren?

**6:23** We were well pleased to serve your father, to walk by the words he spoke, and to follow his decrees;

**6:24** for on account of this those of our own people became estranged from us; moreover, as many of us as were found were put to death, and our inheritances were plundered.

**6:25** And they did not stretch out their hand against us only, but also against all their borders.

**6:26** And behold, today they have encamped against the citadel in Jerusalem to capture it; and they have fortified the sanctuary and Beth-zur.

**6:27** And if you do not forestall them swiftly, they will do greater things than these, and you will not be able to restrain them."

**6:28** And the king was angry when he heard this; and he gathered all his friends, the commanders of his army, and those in charge of the cavalry;

**6:29** and from other kings and from the islands of the seas, mercenary forces came to him.

**6:30** And the number of his forces was one hundred thousand foot soldiers, and twenty thousand horsemen, and thirty-two elephants trained for war.

**6:31** And they came through Idumea and encamped against Beth-zur, and fought for many days and built siege engines; but those within came out and burned them with fire, and fought valiantly.

**6:32** Then Judas departed from the citadel and encamped at Beth-zechariah, opposite the camp of the king.

**6:33** And the king rose early in the morning and led the army forth in fierce eagerness along the road to Beth-zechariah; and the forces arrayed themselves for battle, and they sounded the trumpets.

**6:34** And to the elephants they showed the blood of grapes and mulberries, to arouse them for battle.

**6:35** And they distributed the beasts among the phalanxes, and stationed beside each elephant one thousand men armored in chain mail, with bronze helmets upon their heads; and five hundred chosen horsemen were assigned to each beast.

**6:36** These were stationed beforehand wherever the beast was; and wherever it went, they went along with it, and did not depart from it.

**6:37** And upon them were strong, covered wooden towers, on every beast, fastened upon them with harnesses; and on each were thirty-two men fighting upon them, along with its Indian driver.

**6:38** And the rest of the cavalry he stationed on this side and on that, on the two flanks of the army, harassing the enemy while protected among the phalanxes.

**6:39** Now when the sun shone upon the golden shields, the mountains glistened from them and blazed like torches of fire.

**6:40** And part of the king's army was spread out upon the high mountains, and some upon the low ground; and they advanced steadily and in order.

**6:41** And all who heard the sound of their multitude, the marching of the multitude, and the clashing of weapons were shaken; for the army was exceedingly great and mighty.

**6:42** Then Judas and his army drew near for battle, and six hundred men of the king's army fell.

**6:43** And Eleazar, called Savaran, saw one of the beasts armored with royal breastplates, and it was taller than all the other beasts, and it appeared that the king was upon it.

**6:44** So he gave himself to save his people and to win for himself an everlasting name.

**6:45** And he ran boldly toward it into the midst of the phalanx, slaying to the right and to the left, so that they split apart from before him on this side and on that.

**6:46** And he crept beneath the elephant, and thrust from beneath it and slew it; and it fell to the ground upon him, and he died there.

**6:47** And they saw the strength of the kingdom and the fierce onset of the forces, and they turned away from them.

**6:48** Now troops from the king's army went up to meet them toward Jerusalem, and the king encamped in Judea and against Mount Zion.

**6:49** And he made peace with those of Beth-zur, and they went out of the city, because they had no food there to endure a siege in it, since it was a sabbath for the land.

**6:50** So the king took possession of Beth-zur and stationed a garrison there to guard it.

**6:51** And he encamped against the sanctuary for many days, and set up there catapult platforms, siege engines, fire-throwers, stone-throwers, scorpions for shooting arrows, and slings.

**6:52** And they also built siege engines to match their siege engines, and fought for many days.

**6:53** But there was no food in the sanctuary, because it was the seventh year, and those who had been brought safely into Judea from among the Gentiles had consumed the remainder of the provisions;

**6:54** and only a few men were left in the sanctuary, because the famine prevailed over them; and they were scattered, each man to his own place.

**6:55** Now Lysias heard that Philip, whom King Antiochus while still alive had appointed to raise his son Antiochus to reign,

**6:56** had returned from Persia and Media, along with the king's forces that had marched with him, and that he was seeking to take control of the affairs of state.

**6:57** So they made haste and urged one another to depart; and he said to the king, the commanders of the army, and the men, "We are growing weaker day by day, our food is scarce, the place we are besieging is strong, and the affairs of the kingdom press upon us.

**6:58** Now therefore let us give the right hand to these men, and make peace with them and with all their nation,

**6:59** and confirm to them that they may walk in their laws as before; for on account of their laws, which we abolished, they became angry and did all these things."

**6:60** And the word was pleasing in the sight of the king and the rulers, so he sent to them to make peace, and they accepted it.

**6:61** And the king and the rulers swore an oath to them upon these terms, and they came out of the stronghold.

**6:62** Then the king entered Mount Zion and saw the fortification of the place; and he broke the oath that he had sworn, and gave command and tore down the wall all around.

**6:63** And they departed in haste, and he returned to Antioch, and found Philip in control of the city; and he fought against him and took the city by force.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — The Greek text reads "in Elymais" (ἐν Ἐλύμαις), treating Elymais as a district in Persia containing the wealthy city; some manuscripts omit the preposition and read Elymais as the name of the city itself.
- **6:2** — "Golden shields" translates Greek καλύμματα χρυσᾶ (lit. "golden coverings"), likely referring to decorative shields or shield-covers.
- **6:21** — The Greek text reads ἐξ αὐτοῦ ("from it," referring to the blockade or citadel), whereas some manuscripts read ἐξ αὐτῶν ("some of them").
- **6:26** — "And they have fortified the sanctuary and Beth-zur" construes "the sanctuary" with "fortified" in agreement with 6:7; some punctuate the Greek to read "to capture it and the sanctuary, and they have fortified Beth-zur."
- **6:28** — "Those in charge of the cavalry" renders the Greek idiom τοὺς ἐπὶ τῶν ἡνιῶν (lit. "those over the reins"), referring to cavalry commanders or charioteers.
- **6:37** — The Greek text reads "thirty-two men" (δύο καὶ τριάκοντα), a notably high number for a single elephant tower that many scholars regard as an early textual corruption influenced by the thirty-two elephants in 6:30; "Indian driver" renders ὁ Ἰνδὸς αὐτοῦ (lit. "its Indian").
- **6:43** — The Greek text gives Eleazar's surname as Savaran (Σαυαράν), a variant of Avaran (Αὐαράν) in 2:5.
- **6:49** — The Greek text omits an explicit object after "made" (other manuscripts add "peace") and has singular ἐξῆλθεν ("went out"), rendered collectively in agreement with the following plural "them" (αὐτοῖς).
- **6:53** — The Greek text reads ἐν τοῖς ἁγίοις ("in the sanctuary"), whereas some manuscripts read ἐν τοῖς ἀγγείοις ("in the storage vessels" or "storehouses").
