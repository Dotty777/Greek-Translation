## Text

**16:1** And John went up from Gazara and reported to Simon his father what Cendebeus was doing.

**16:2** Then Simon called his two eldest sons, Judas and John, and said to them, "I and my brothers and my father's house have fought the wars of Israel from youth until this day, and it has prospered in our hands to deliver Israel many times.

**16:3** But now I have grown old, while you, by mercy, are sufficient in years; take my place and that of my brother, and go forth and fight for our nation, and may the help from heaven be with you."

**16:4** And he chose out of the country twenty thousand men of war and horsemen, and they marched against Cendebeus and passed the night in Modein.

**16:5** And rising early in the morning, they marched into the plain; and behold, a great army came to meet them, both foot soldiers and horsemen, and a brook was between them.

**16:6** And he encamped opposite them, he and his people; and he saw that the people were afraid to cross over the brook, so he crossed over first; and the men saw him, and crossed over behind him.

**16:7** Then he divided the people, with the horsemen in the midst of the foot soldiers, for the cavalry of the adversaries was exceedingly numerous.

**16:8** And they sounded the trumpets, and Cendebeus and his camp were put to rout, and many of his men fell wounded; and those who remained fled into the stronghold.

**16:9** At that time Judas, the brother of John, was wounded; but John pursued them until he came to Kedron, which he had built.

**16:10** And they fled into the towers that were in the field of Azotus, and he burned it with fire; and there fell of them about one thousand men, and he returned to Judea in peace.

**16:11** Now Ptolemy, the son of Abubus, had been appointed commander over the plain of Jericho, and he possessed much silver and gold;

**16:12** for he was the son-in-law of the high priest.

**16:13** And his heart was lifted up, and he purposed to seize control of the country, and plotted deceitfully against Simon and against his sons, to do away with them.

**16:14** Now Simon was visiting the cities in the country and attending to their affairs, and he went down to Jericho—he and Mattathias and Judas, even his sons—in the one hundred and seventy-seventh year, in the eleventh month, which is the month of Shebat.

**16:15** And the son of Abubus received them with deceit into the small stronghold called Dok, which he had built, and made a great banquet for them; and he concealed men there.

**16:16** And when Simon and his sons had become drunk, Ptolemy and those who were with him rose up, and took their weapons, and burst in upon Simon in the banquet hall, and slew him and his sons, and some of his servants.

**16:17** Thus he committed great ungodliness, and repaid evil for good.

**16:18** Then Ptolemy wrote these things and sent word to the king, that he should send forces to his aid, so that he might deliver up their country and the cities.

**16:19** And he sent others to Gazara to do away with John; and to the captains of thousands he sent letters to come to him, that he might give them silver and gold and gifts.

**16:20** And he sent others to seize Jerusalem and the mountain of the temple.

**16:21** But one ran ahead and reported to John at Gazara that his father and his brothers had perished, and said, "He has sent to slay you also."

**16:22** And when he heard it, he was greatly astounded; and he seized the men who had come to destroy him and slew them, for he knew that they were seeking to destroy him.

**16:23** Now the rest of the acts of John, and of his wars, and of the valiant exploits that he accomplished, and of the building of the walls that he built, and of his deeds,

**16:24** behold, these are written in the book of the chronicles of his high priesthood, from the time that he became high priest after his father.

## Translator Notes

- **16:3** — 'By mercy' and 'the help from heaven' are reverential circumlocutions referring to God's gracious favor and divine aid.
- **16:10** — The Greek feminine singular pronoun 'it' (auten) refers to Azotus rather than to the towers.
- **16:14** — The Greek text includes 'and' before 'his sons,' rendered epexegetically here ('even his sons') to identify Mattathias and Judas as the sons who accompanied Simon.
- **16:17** — The Greek text reads 'ungodliness' (atheian), whereas some manuscripts read 'treachery' (athesian).
