## Text

**10:1** And in the one hundred and sixtieth year, Alexander the son of Antiochus, Epiphanes, went up and took possession of Ptolemais; and they received him, and he reigned there.

**10:2** And King Demetrius heard of it, and he gathered exceedingly great forces and went out to meet him in battle.

**10:3** And Demetrius sent letters to Jonathan with peaceful words, so as to magnify him.

**10:4** For he said, "Let us be first to make peace with them, before he makes peace with Alexander against us;

**10:5** for he will remember all the evils which we have committed against him, and against his brothers, and against his nation."

**10:6** And he gave him authority to gather forces and to prepare weapons, and to be his ally; and he ordered that the hostages who were in the citadel be delivered to him.

**10:7** And Jonathan came to Jerusalem and read the letters in the ears of all the people and of those from the citadel.

**10:8** And they feared with a great fear when they heard that the king had given them authority to gather forces.

**10:9** And those from the citadel delivered the hostages to Jonathan, and they restored them to their parents.

**10:10** And Jonathan dwelt in Jerusalem, and began to build and renew the city.

**10:11** And he spoke to those who did the work, to build the walls and Mount Zion all around with squared stones for fortification; and they did so.

**10:12** And the foreigners who were in the strongholds which Bacchides had built fled;

**10:13** and each one left his place and departed to his own land.

**10:14** Only in Beth Zur were some left of those who had forsaken the law and the ordinances, for it was a place of refuge for them.

**10:15** Now King Alexander heard of all the promises that Demetrius had sent to Jonathan; and they recounted to him the battles and the valiant deeds which he and his brothers had done, and the toils which they had endured.

**10:16** And he said, "Shall we find even one such man? Now therefore let us make him our friend and ally."

**10:17** So he wrote letters and sent them to him according to these words, saying:

**10:18** "King Alexander to his brother Jonathan, greetings.

**10:19** We have heard concerning you that you are a man valiant in strength and worthy to be our friend.

**10:20** And now today we have appointed you high priest of your nation, and to be called the king's Friend"—and they sent him a purple robe and a golden crown—"and to support our cause and maintain friendship with us."

**10:21** So Jonathan put on the holy robe in the seventh month of the one hundred and sixtieth year, at the Feast of Tabernacles; and he gathered forces and prepared many weapons.

**10:22** And Demetrius heard these words, and he was grieved, and said,

**10:23** "What is this that we have done, that Alexander has forestalled us in securing friendship with the Jews as a support?

**10:24** I also will write to them words of encouragement, and of exaltation, and of gifts, that they may be with me for help."

**10:25** And he sent to them according to these words: "King Demetrius to the nation of the Jews, greetings.

**10:26** Since you have kept the treaties with us, and have continued in our friendship, and have not gone over to our enemies, we have heard of it and rejoiced.

**10:27** And now continue still to keep faith with us, and we will repay you with good things in return for what you do with us.

**10:28** And we will grant you many remissions, and we will give you gifts.

**10:29** And now I release you and exempt all the Jews from the tributes, and from the salt tax, and from the crown levies;

**10:30** and instead of the third part of the sown crop, and instead of the half of the fruit of the trees which falls to me to receive, I remit from this day onward the taking of it from the land of Judah, and from the three districts added to it from Samaria and Galilee, from this day and for all time.

**10:31** And let Jerusalem be holy and exempt, together with its borders, the tithes and the tolls.

**10:32** I also relinquish the authority over the citadel that is in Jerusalem, and give it to the high priest, that he may station in it men whom he himself shall choose, to guard it.

**10:33** And every soul of the Jews who has been taken captive from the land of Judah into all my kingdom I set free without ransom; and let all remit the tributes, even on their livestock.

**10:34** And all the feasts, and the Sabbaths, and new moons, and appointed days, and three days before a feast and three days after a feast—let all of them be days of immunity and release for all the Jews who are in all my kingdom.

**10:35** And no one shall have authority to exact payment from or trouble any of them concerning any matter.

**10:36** And let there be enrolled from the Jews into the forces of the king up to thirty thousand men, and allowances shall be given to them, as is due to all the forces of the king.

**10:37** And some of them shall be stationed in the great strongholds of the king, and some of them shall be appointed over offices of the kingdom that are positions of trust; and let their overseers and their rulers be from among themselves, and let them walk in their own laws, even as the king has commanded in the land of Judah.

**10:38** And as for the three districts that have been added to Judea from the country of Samaria, let them be annexed to Judea so as to be reckoned under one rule, not to obey any other authority except that of the high priest.

**10:39** Ptolemais and the territory adjoining it I have given as a gift to the sanctuary that is in Jerusalem, for the necessary expenses of the sanctuary.

**10:40** And I myself give every year fifteen thousand shekels of silver from the king's revenues, from the appropriate places.

**10:41** And all the surplus which they had not paid from the funds, as in the former years, from now on they shall give for the work of the house.

**10:42** And in addition to this, the five thousand shekels of silver which they used to take from the revenues of the sanctuary, as in the former years, from the annual account—these also are remitted, because they belong to the priests who minister.

**10:43** And whoever flees to the temple that is in Jerusalem and within all its borders, owing royal dues or any other matter, let them be released, together with all that belongs to them in my kingdom.

**10:44** And for the building and the renewing of the works of the sanctuary, the expense also shall be given from the king's revenue.

**10:45** And for the building of the walls of Jerusalem, and fortifying it all around, the expense also shall be given from the king's revenue, and for the building of the walls in Judea."

**10:46** Now when Jonathan and the people heard these words, they did not believe them nor accept them, because they remembered the great evil that he had done in Israel, and how he had greatly afflicted them.

**10:47** And they were well pleased with Alexander, because he had been the author of peaceful words to them; and they fought as his allies all those days.

**10:48** And King Alexander gathered great forces and encamped opposite Demetrius.

**10:49** And the two kings joined battle, and the army of Demetrius fled; and Alexander pursued him and prevailed over them.

**10:50** And he pressed the battle exceedingly until the sun went down, and Demetrius fell on that day.

**10:51** Then Alexander sent ambassadors to Ptolemy, king of Egypt, according to these words, saying:

**10:52** "Since I have returned to my kingdom, and have sat upon the throne of my fathers, and have taken hold of the dominion, and have crushed Demetrius and gained mastery over our country,

**10:53** and I joined battle with him, and he and his army were crushed by us, and we have sat upon the throne of his kingdom—

**10:54** and let us establish friendship with him—now therefore give me your daughter as a wife, and I will become your son-in-law, and I will give to you and to her gifts worthy of you."

**10:55** And King Ptolemy answered, saying, "Good is the day on which you returned to the land of your fathers and sat upon the throne of their kingdom.

**10:56** And now I will do for you what you have written; only come to meet me at Ptolemais, that we may see one another, and I will become your father-in-law, even as you have said."

**10:57** So Ptolemy went out from Egypt, he and his daughter Cleopatra, and came to Ptolemais in the one hundred and sixty-second year.

**10:58** And King Alexander met him, and he gave him his daughter Cleopatra in marriage, and celebrated her wedding in Ptolemais, as kings do, with great glory.

**10:59** Now King Alexander wrote to Jonathan to come to meet him.

**10:60** And he went with glory to Ptolemais and met the two kings; and he gave them and their Friends silver and gold and many gifts, and found favor in their sight.

**10:61** And pestilent men from Israel, lawless men, assembled against him to bring charges against him; yet the king paid no attention to them.

**10:62** And the king gave command to them, and stripped Jonathan of his garments and clothed him in purple; and they did so.

**10:63** And the king seated him beside himself, and said to his officers, "Go out with him into the midst of the city and proclaim that no one shall bring charges against this man concerning any matter, and let no one trouble him for any reason."

**10:64** And it came to pass, when his accusers saw his glory as he had proclaimed it, and him clothed in fine linen, they all fled.

**10:65** And the king honored him, and enrolled him among the Chief Friends, and appointed him general and governor.

**10:66** And Jonathan returned to Jerusalem with peace and gladness.

**10:67** Now in the one hundred and sixty-fifth year, Demetrius, the son of Demetrius, came from Crete into the land of his fathers.

**10:68** And King Alexander heard of it, and he was exceedingly grieved, and returned to Antioch.

**10:69** And Demetrius appointed Apollonius, who was over Coele-Syria; and he gathered a great force and encamped at Jamnia.

**10:70** And he sent to Jonathan the high priest, saying, "You alone lift yourself up against us, and I have become a laughingstock and a reproach because of you. Why then do you exercise authority against us in the mountains?

**10:71** Now therefore, if you trust in your forces, come down to us into the plain, and let us test our strength with one another there; for with me is the strength of the cities.

**10:72** Inquire and learn who I am, and the rest who help us; and they say, 'There is no foothold for you before our face, for twice your fathers were put to flight in their own land.'

**10:73** And now you will not be able to withstand the cavalry and such a force in the plain, where there is neither stone nor pebble, nor any place to flee."

**10:74** Now when Jonathan heard the words of Apollonius, his mind was stirred; and he chose ten thousand men and went out from Jerusalem, and Simon his brother met him to assist him.

**10:75** And he encamped against Joppa; and those of the city shut him out, because a garrison of Apollonius was in Joppa, and they fought against it.

**10:76** And those of the city were afraid and opened to him, and Jonathan gained mastery over Joppa.

**10:77** And Apollonius heard of it, and he marshaled three thousand cavalry and a great force, and marched toward Azotus as though passing through; and at the same time he advanced into the plain, because he had a multitude of cavalry and trusted in it.

**10:78** And he pursued after him to Azotus, and the armies engaged after him in battle.

**10:79** Now Apollonius had left one thousand cavalry secretly behind them.

**10:80** And Jonathan knew that there was an ambush behind him. And they surrounded his army and shot their arrows at the people from morning until evening.

**10:81** But the people stood firm, even as Jonathan had commanded; and their horses grew weary.

**10:82** Then Simon drew out his force and engaged the phalanx, for the cavalry was exhausted; and they were crushed by him and fled.

**10:83** And the cavalry was scattered in the plain; and they fled to Azotus and entered Beth Dagon, the temple of their idol, to save themselves.

**10:84** And Jonathan burned Azotus and the cities around it, and took their spoils; and the temple of Dagon and its sanctuary he burned with fire.

**10:85** And those who fell by the sword, together with those who were burned, amounted to about eight thousand men.

**10:86** And Jonathan departed from there and encamped against Ashkelon; and those of the city came out to meet him with great glory.

**10:87** And Jonathan returned with those who were with him, having many spoils.

**10:88** And it came to pass, when King Alexander heard these words, that he honored Jonathan even more.

**10:89** And he sent him a golden buckle, as is the custom to give to the Kinsmen of the kings; and he gave him Ekron and all its borders as an inheritance.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The one hundred and sixtieth year follows the Seleucid era (153/152 BC). Alexander Balas claimed to be the son of Antiochus IV Epiphanes and assumed the same royal title.
- **10:20** — The Greek embeds a third-person narrative clause ("and they sent him a purple robe and a golden crown," with a plural verb) directly within Alexander's letter. "Friend" denotes an official Seleucid court rank.
- **10:36** — "Allowances" translates Greek xenia (maintenance stipends or provisions), whereas some manuscripts read opsonia ("wages").
- **10:42** — The Greek text includes "as in the former years" within this clause (echoing verse 41) regarding the five thousand shekels previously drawn from the sanctuary's revenues.
- **10:54** — The Greek text reads "with him" (pros auton), whereas many manuscripts read "with one another" (pros allelous).
- **10:64** — The Greek text reads "fine linen" (sindona), whereas other manuscripts read "purple" (porphyran, matching verse 62).
- **10:77** — Azotus is the Hellenistic Greek name for the Old Testament Philistine city of Ashdod.
- **10:84** — The Greek text reads "the temple of Dagon and its sanctuary" (kai to hieron autes), whereas other manuscripts read "and those who had fled into it."
