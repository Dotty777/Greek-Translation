## Text

**8:1** Now Judas heard of the fame of the Romans, that they were mighty in strength, and that they looked with favor upon all who joined themselves to them; and that with as many as joined themselves to them, and with as many as approached them, they established friendship,

**8:2** and that they were mighty in strength. They also recounted to him their wars and the valiant deeds which they carried out among the Galatians, and how they subdued them and brought them under tribute;

**8:3** and all that they did in the land of Spain to gain possession of the mines of silver and gold that were there;

**8:4** and they gained mastery over the whole place by their counsel and patience—even though the place was exceedingly far removed from them—and over the kings who came against them from the end of the earth, until they crushed them and struck them with a great blow, and the rest pay them tribute year by year.

**8:5** Moreover, Philip, and Perseus king of the Kittim, and those who rose up against them—they crushed them in war and subdued them.

**8:6** And Antiochus the Great, king of Asia, who marched against them to war having one hundred and twenty elephants, and cavalry, and chariots, and an exceedingly great force—he too was crushed by them.

**8:7** And they took him alive, and decreed that both he and those who reigned after him should pay them a great tribute, and give hostages and a stipulated partition,

**8:8** as well as the country of India, and Media, and Lydia, and some of their finest lands; and having taken them from him, they gave them to King Eumenes;

**8:9** and how those from Greece took counsel to come and destroy them,

**8:10** and the matter became known to them, and they sent against them a single general and fought against them; and many of them fell wounded, and they took their wives and their children captive, and plundered them, and took possession of their land, and tore down their strongholds, and plundered them, and brought them into bondage to this day.

**8:11** And the remaining kingdoms and the islands, as many as ever rose up against them, they destroyed and brought into subjection to themselves; but with their friends and those who relied upon them, they preserved friendship with them.

**8:12** And they subdued the kingdoms both near and far, and all who heard of their fame were afraid of them.

**8:13** Those whom they wish to help and to make kings, such ones reign; and those whom they wish, they depose; and they were exceedingly exalted.

**8:14** Yet in all these things, not one of them put on a diadem, nor did they clothe themselves in purple to be magnified by it.

**8:15** And they established a senate for themselves, and day by day three hundred and twenty men took counsel, deliberating continually concerning the people, that they might order themselves well.

**8:16** And they trust one man to rule over them year by year and to have dominion over all their land, and all obey that one man; and there is neither envy nor jealousy among them.

**8:17** Then Judas chose Eupolemus the son of John, the son of Accos, and Jason the son of Eleazar, and sent them to Rome to establish friendship and alliance with them,

**8:18** and to take away the yoke from them, for they saw that the kingdom of the Greeks was enslaving Israel into bondage.

**8:19** So they journeyed to Rome, and the way was exceedingly long; and they entered the senate house and spoke:

**8:20** "Judas, who is also called Maccabeus, and his brothers, and the people of the Jews have sent us to you to establish an alliance and peace with you, and that we may be enrolled as your allies and friends."

**8:21** And the proposal was pleasing in their sight.

**8:22** And this is the copy of the writing which they transcribed upon bronze tablets and sent to Jerusalem, to remain with them there as a memorial of peace and alliance:

**8:23** "May it be well with the Romans and with the nation of the Jews at sea and on land forever, and may sword and enemy be far removed from them.

**8:24** But if war should arise against Rome first, or against any of their allies in all their dominion,

**8:25** the nation of the Jews shall fight as their allies, as the occasion may prescribe for them, with a whole heart.

**8:26** And to those who wage war they shall neither give nor supply grain, weapons, silver, or ships, as it has seemed good to Rome; and they shall keep their obligations without receiving anything.

**8:27** Likewise, if war should befall the nation of the Jews first, the Romans shall fight as their allies with all their soul, as the occasion may prescribe for them.

**8:28** And to those who fight as allies there shall not be given grain, weapons, or silver, as it has seemed good to Rome; and they shall keep these obligations, and without deceit.

**8:29** According to these terms, thus have the Romans established it with the people of the Jews.

**8:30** Yet if after these terms both the one party and the other should decide to add or to take away anything, they shall do so of their own choice, and whatever they may add or take away shall be binding.

**8:31** Moreover, concerning the evils which King Demetrius is committing against them, we have written to him, saying, 'Why have you made your yoke heavy upon our friends and allies, the Jews?

**8:32** Therefore, if they appeal against you again, we will execute justice for them, and we will wage war against you by sea and by land.'"

## Translator Notes

- **8:2** — Greek Galatai ("Galatians") refers broadly to Celtic peoples, encompassing here the Gauls conquered by Rome in northern Italy and Europe as well as the Galatians of Asia Minor.
- **8:8** — The Greek text names India, Media, and Lydia—likely an early transmission error for Ionia, Mysia, and Lydia ceded to Eumenes II in the Peace of Apamea—and reads plural "their finest lands."
- **8:10** — The Greek critical text preserves the repeated clause "and plundered them" both before and after the capture of the land and destruction of the strongholds.
- **8:17** — Greek Hakchos ("Accos") corresponds to the Hebrew priestly family name Hakkoz (1 Chronicles 24:10; Ezra 2:61).
- **8:28** — Unlike verse 26, the Greek here reads "to those who fight as allies" (probably referring to the enemy's allies, or an early textual variation for "those who wage war") and omits "ships" from the list of prohibited supplies.
