## Text

**12:1** Now Jonathan saw that the time worked in his favor, and he chose men and sent them to Rome to confirm and renew the friendship with them.

**12:2** He also sent letters to the Spartans and to other places to the same effect.

**12:3** So they went to Rome and entered the senate house, and said, "Jonathan the high priest and the nation of the Jews have sent us to renew the friendship and the alliance for them, as before."

**12:4** And they gave them letters to those in each place, that they should escort them to the land of Judah in peace.

**12:5** And this is a copy of the letters which Jonathan wrote to the Spartans:

**12:6** "Jonathan, high priest of the nation, and the council of elders, and the priests, and the rest of the people of the Jews, to the Spartans their brethren, greetings.

**12:7** Even previously, letters were sent to Onias the high priest from Areius, who reigned among you, stating that you are our brethren, as the copy below sets forth.

**12:8** And Onias received with honor the man who was sent, and accepted the letters in which clear declaration was made concerning alliance and friendship.

**12:9** Therefore, though we have no need of these things, having as our comfort the holy books that are in our hands,

**12:10** we have endeavored to send to renew our brotherhood and friendship with you, that we might not become estranged from you; for many seasons have passed since you sent to us.

**12:11** We therefore, at every season without ceasing, on the feasts and on the other appointed days, remember you in the sacrifices which we offer and in our prayers, as it is right and fitting to be mindful of brethren.

**12:12** And we rejoice in your glory.

**12:13** As for us, many tribulations and many wars have surrounded us, and the kings around us have waged war against us.

**12:14** We did not wish, therefore, to trouble you or the rest of our allies and friends in these wars;

**12:15** for we have the help from heaven aiding us, and we were delivered from our enemies, and our enemies were humbled.

**12:16** Therefore we chose Numenius the son of Antiochus, and Antipater the son of Jason, and have sent them to the Romans to renew our former friendship and alliance with them.

**12:17** We commanded them therefore to go to you also and greet you, and to deliver to you our letters concerning the renewal and our brotherhood.

**12:18** And now you will do well to give us an answer regarding these matters."

**12:19** And this is the copy of the letters which they sent to Onias:

**12:20** "Ares, king of the Spartans, to Onias the great priest, greetings.

**12:21** It has been found in a writing concerning both the Spartans and the Jews that they are brethren, and that they are of the lineage of Abraham.

**12:22** And now, since we have come to know these things, you will do well to write to us concerning your peace.

**12:23** And we also write back to you: 'Your cattle and your possessions are ours, and what is ours is yours.' We therefore command that they report to you in accordance with these same words."

**12:24** Now Jonathan heard that the commanders of Demetrius had returned with a great force, larger than before, to wage war against him.

**12:25** So he departed from Jerusalem and met them in the country of Hamath, for he gave them no respite to set foot in his own land.

**12:26** And he sent spies into his camp; and they returned and reported to him that they were thus preparing to fall upon them during the night.

**12:27** Now when the sun went down, Jonathan commanded his men to keep watch and to remain under arms, being ready for battle throughout the whole night; and he stationed outposts all around the camp.

**12:28** And the adversaries heard that Jonathan and his men were prepared for battle; and they were afraid and trembled in their hearts, and they kindled fires in their camp.

**12:29** But Jonathan and his men did not know it until morning, for they saw the lights burning.

**12:30** Then he pursued after them, yet he did not overtake them, for they had crossed the river Eleutherus.

**12:31** So Jonathan turned aside against the Arabs who are called Zabadeans, and struck them down, and took their spoil.

**12:32** And breaking camp, he came to Damascus and marched through all the country.

**12:33** And Simon went out and marched as far as Ashkelon and the neighboring strongholds, and he turned aside to Joppa and occupied it beforehand.

**12:34** For he had heard that they intended to hand over the stronghold to the men of Demetrius; so he stationed a garrison there to guard it.

**12:35** Now Jonathan returned and assembled the elders of the people, and took counsel with them to build strongholds in Judea,

**12:36** and to raise the walls of Jerusalem higher, and to erect a great height between the citadel and the city to separate it from the city, so that it might stand alone, that they might neither buy nor sell.

**12:37** So they gathered together to build, and part of the wall of the brook on the east fell down, and he repaired that which is called Chaphenatha.

**12:38** And Simon built Adida in the Shephelah, and fortified it with gates and bars.

**12:39** Now Trypho sought to reign over Asia and to put on the diadem, and to lay hands upon Antiochus the king.

**12:40** And he feared lest Jonathan would not permit him, and lest he should make war against him; so he sought a way to seize him to put him to death. And departing, he came to Bethsa.

**12:41** And Jonathan went out to meet him with forty thousand men chosen for battle, and came to Bethsa.

**12:42** Now Trypho saw that he had come with a great force, and he was afraid to lay hands upon him.

**12:43** So he received him with honor and commended him to all his friends, and gave him gifts, and commanded his friends and his forces to obey him in the same way.

**12:44** And he said to Jonathan, "Why have you wearied all this people, when there is no war between us?

**12:45** Now therefore send them away to their homes, and choose for yourself a few men who shall remain with you, and come with me to Ptolemais; and I will hand it over to you, along with the rest of the strongholds and the numerous forces and all those in charge of affairs; then I will turn back and depart, for this is why I have come."

**12:46** And trusting him, he did as he said, and dismissed the forces, and they departed to the land of Judah.

**12:47** But he kept with himself three thousand men; about two thousand he left in Galilee, while one thousand went with him.

**12:48** Now as soon as Jonathan entered Ptolemais, the men of Ptolemais shut the gates and seized him, and all who had come with him they slew with the sword.

**12:49** And Trypho sent forces and cavalry into Galilee, into the Great Plain, to destroy all the men of Jonathan.

**12:50** And they learned that he had been seized and had perished, along with those who were with him; so they encouraged one another and marched in close formation, ready for battle.

**12:51** And their pursuers saw that it was for their very lives, and they turned back.

**12:52** So they all came in peace to the land of Judah, and mourned for Jonathan and those who were with him, and were exceedingly afraid; and Israel mourned with a great mourning.

**12:53** And all the nations around them sought to destroy them completely, for they said, "They have no ruler and helper; now therefore let us make war against them, and we shall blot out their remembrance from among men."

## Translator Notes

- **12:7** — The Greek source spells the Spartan king's name Areius here and Ares in 12:20, referring historically to King Areus I of Sparta.
- **12:26** — The Greek reads "his camp" (singular, referring to Demetrius's camp), whereas other manuscripts read "their camp."
- **12:28** — The Greek source stops at "kindled fires in their camp" as a nocturnal decoy; some other manuscripts add "and withdrew."
- **12:40** — The Greek source reads "Bethsa" here and in 12:41, corresponding to Beth-shan (Beth-shean) in other textual witnesses.
- **12:45** — The Greek source reads "the numerous forces" (pollas), whereas some manuscripts read "the remaining forces" (loipas).
