## Text

**1:1** And it came to pass, after Alexander the son of Philip, the Macedonian, who went forth from the land of Kittim, struck down Darius, king of the Persians and Medes, that he reigned in his stead, having first reigned over Greece.

**1:2** And he waged many wars, captured strongholds, and slew kings,

**1:3** and he passed through to the ends of the earth, and took the spoils of a multitude of nations. And the earth was quiet before him, and he was exalted, and his heart was lifted up.

**1:4** And he gathered an exceedingly mighty army, and ruled over countries, nations, and principalities, and they became tributary to him.

**1:5** And after these things he fell upon his bed, and knew that he was dying.

**1:6** Then he called his honored servants, who had been brought up with him from youth, and divided his kingdom among them while he was still alive.

**1:7** And Alexander reigned twelve years, and died.

**1:8** And his servants took dominion, each in his own place.

**1:9** And they all put diadems upon themselves after his death, and their sons after them for many years; and they multiplied evils in the earth.

**1:10** And there came forth from them a sinful root, Antiochus Epiphanes, son of Antiochus the king, who had been a hostage in Rome; and he began to reign in the one hundred and thirty-seventh year of the kingdom of the Greeks.

**1:11** In those days there went forth from Israel a lawless son, and they persuaded many, saying, "Let us go and make a covenant with the Gentiles who are around us, for since we were separated from them, many evils have found us."

**1:12** And the word was pleasing in their eyes.

**1:13** And some of the people were eager, and went to the king; and they gave them authority to observe the ordinances of the Gentiles.

**1:14** And they built a gymnasium in Jerusalem according to the customs of the Gentiles.

**1:15** And they made themselves uncircumcised, and turned away from the holy covenant; and they yoked themselves to the Gentiles, and sold themselves to do evil.

**1:16** And the kingdom was established before Antiochus, and he purposed to reign over the land of Egypt, that he might reign over the two kingdoms.

**1:17** And he entered into Egypt with a mighty force, with chariots and elephants, and with horsemen, and with a great fleet.

**1:18** And he waged war against Ptolemy, king of Egypt; and Ptolemy turned back before him and fled, and many fell wounded.

**1:19** And they captured the fortified cities in the land of Egypt, and he took the spoils of the land of Egypt.

**1:20** And Antiochus returned after striking down Egypt, in the one hundred and forty-third year; and he went up against Israel and Jerusalem with a mighty force.

**1:21** And he entered into the sanctuary in arrogance, and took the golden altar, and the lampstand of light and all its utensils,

**1:22** and the table of the showbread, and the vessels for drink offerings, and the bowls, and the golden censers, and the veil, and the crowns, and the golden adornment on the front of the temple; and he stripped it all off.

**1:23** And he took the silver and the gold, and the precious vessels; and he took the hidden treasures which he found.

**1:24** And having taken all things, he departed to his own land. And they committed slaughter, and spoke with great arrogance.

**1:25** And there was great mourning upon Israel, in every place of theirs.

**1:26** And rulers and elders groaned; virgins and young men grew faint, and the beauty of the women faded.

**1:27** Every bridegroom took up a lamentation; she who sat in the bridal chamber was in mourning.

**1:28** And the land trembled for those who dwelt in it, and all the house of Jacob clothed itself with shame.

**1:29** After two full years, the king sent a chief collector of tribute to the cities of Judah, and he came to Jerusalem with a mighty force.

**1:30** And he spoke peaceful words to them in deceit, and they trusted him; then he fell upon the city suddenly, and struck it with a great blow, and destroyed many people from Israel.

**1:31** And he took the spoils of the city and burned it with fire, and tore down its houses and its walls all around,

**1:32** and he took the women and the children captive, and they took possession of the livestock.

**1:33** And they fortified the city of David with a great and strong wall, with strong towers, and it became a citadel for them.

**1:34** And they placed there a sinful nation, lawless men, and they strengthened themselves in it.

**1:35** And they stored up weapons and provisions, and having gathered the spoils of Jerusalem, they laid them up there; and it became a great snare,

**1:36** and it became an ambush against the sanctuary, and an evil adversary to Israel continually.

**1:37** And they shed innocent blood all around the sanctuary, and defiled the sanctuary.

**1:38** And the inhabitants of Jerusalem fled because of them, and she became a dwelling of strangers; she became a stranger to her own offspring, and her children forsook her.

**1:39** Her sanctuary was laid waste like a wilderness; her feasts were turned into mourning, her Sabbaths into reproach, her honor into contempt.

**1:40** According to her glory, her dishonor was multiplied, and her exaltation was turned into mourning.

**1:41** And the king wrote to his whole kingdom that all should be one people,

**1:42** and that each should forsake his own customs. And all the nations consented according to the word of the king.

**1:43** And many from Israel gladly accepted his worship, and sacrificed to idols, and profaned the Sabbath.

**1:44** And the king sent letters by the hand of messengers to Jerusalem and the cities of Judah, that they should follow customs foreign to the land,

**1:45** and forbid burnt offerings and sacrifices and drink offering from the sanctuary, and profane Sabbaths and feasts,

**1:46** and defile the sanctuary and the holy ones;

**1:47** to build pagan altars and shrines and idols, and to sacrifice swine and unclean animals,

**1:48** and to leave their sons uncircumcised, to make their souls abominable with every unclean thing and to profane them,

**1:49** so that they might forget the law, and change all the ordinances.

**1:50** And whoever does not act according to the word of the king shall die.

**1:51** According to all these words he wrote to his kingdom, and he appointed overseers over all the people, and commanded the cities of Judah to offer sacrifice, city by city.

**1:52** And many of the people gathered together to them, everyone who forsook the law; and they did evil in the land,

**1:53** and drove Israel into secret places, into every place of refuge of theirs.

**1:54** And on the fifteenth day of Chislev, in the forty-fifth year, they built an abomination of desolation upon the altar; and in the cities of Judah all around they built pagan altars,

**1:55** and they burned incense at the windows of the houses and in the streets.

**1:56** And they tore in pieces the books of the law which they found, and burned them with fire.

**1:57** And wherever a book of the covenant was found with anyone, and if anyone adhered to the law, the decree of the king put him to death.

**1:58** In their might they did thus to Israel, to those who were found month by month in the cities.

**1:59** And on the twenty-fifth day of the month they offered sacrifice upon the pagan altar which was upon the altar.

**1:60** And according to the decree they put to death the women who had circumcised their children,

**1:61** and hanged the infants from their necks, and put to death their households and those who had circumcised them.

**1:62** And many in Israel were hanged. And they stood firm within themselves not to eat unclean things;

**1:63** and they chose to die, so that they might not be defiled by the foods and might not profane the holy covenant; and they died.

**1:64** And there was exceedingly great wrath upon Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — "Kittim" (Greek Chettiim), used in the Old Testament for Cyprus and the western Mediterranean coasts, here refers to Macedonia and Greece.
- **1:10** — The "one hundred and thirty-seventh year of the kingdom of the Greeks" follows the Seleucid era (beginning in 312 BC), placing the accession of Antiochus IV Epiphanes in 175 BC.
- **1:11** — The Greek text shifts from a singular subject ("a lawless son") used collectively to plural verbs ("and they persuaded ... saying"). The Greek phrase "separated from them" literally uses a preposition (hypo) normally meaning "by."
- **1:13** — The Greek verb "they gave" is plural, referring to the king together with his royal court, whereas some later manuscripts read singular "he gave."
- **1:29** — "Two full years" reflects a Hebraism (literally "two years of days"). "Chief collector of tribute" translates a Greek rendering of a Hebrew title that may also reflect "commander of the Mysians" (compare 2 Maccabees 5:24).
- **1:48** — The Greek text reads the infinitive "and to profane" (bebelosai), sharing "their souls" as its object, whereas some manuscripts read the adjective "and profane" (bebelo) modifying "every unclean thing."
- **1:54** — The Greek text reads "the forty-fifth year," an abbreviated reference to (or omission of "one hundred" from) the one hundred and forty-fifth year of the Seleucid era (167 BC). "Abomination of desolation" echoes Daniel 9:27; 11:31; 12:11.
- **1:59** — The Greek distinguishes between the "pagan altar" (bomos) erected for idolatrous worship and the temple "altar" (thysiasterion) upon which it was built.
- **1:62** — The Greek text reads "were hanged" (ekremasthesan), continuing the account of verse 61, whereas many manuscripts read "stood firm" (ekrataiothesan).
