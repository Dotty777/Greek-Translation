## Text

**1:1** There was a certain man in the land of Ausitis whose name was Job; and that man was true, blameless, righteous, and God-fearing, abstaining from every evil deed.

**1:2** And seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

**1:3** And his livestock was seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen, and five hundred grazing female donkeys. He also had an exceedingly large household of servants and great works upon the earth; and that man was noble among those from the rising of the sun.

**1:4** Now his sons would gather together with one another and hold a banquet each day, bringing along their three sisters as well to eat and drink with them.

**1:5** And whenever the days of the banquet were completed, Job would send and purify them, rising up in the morning, and he would offer a sacrifice on their behalf according to their number, and one calf for a sin offering for their souls. For Job said, "Perhaps in their minds my sons have conceived evil thoughts toward God." Therefore, Job did thus all the days.

**1:6** And as this day came, behold, the angels of God came to stand before the Lord, and the devil came with them.

**1:7** And the Lord said to the devil, "From where have you come?" And the devil answered the Lord and said, "Having compassed the earth and walked through all that is under heaven, I am here."

**1:8** And the Lord said to him, "Have you turned your mind toward My servant Job? For there is none like him among those upon the earth—a blameless, true, God-fearing man, abstaining from every evil deed."

**1:9** Then the devil answered and said before the Lord, "Does Job revere the Lord for nothing?

**1:10** Have You not fenced about what is outside him, and what is inside his house, and what is around all that belongs to him on every side? You have blessed the works of his hands, and You have multiplied his livestock upon the earth.

**1:11** Yet send forth Your hand and touch all that he has—truly he will bless You to Your face!"

**1:12** Then the Lord said to the devil, "Behold, all that he has I deliver into your hand; only do not touch him." And the devil went out from the Lord.

**1:13** And it came to pass when this day arrived that the sons of Job and his daughters were drinking wine in the house of their eldest brother.

**1:14** And behold, a messenger came to Job and said to him, "The yoke of oxen were plowing, and the female donkeys were grazing near them;

**1:15** and raiders came and carried them away captive, and slew the servants with swords; and I alone was spared and have come to report to you."

**1:16** While he was yet speaking, another messenger came and said to Job, "Fire fell from heaven and burned up the sheep, and devoured the shepherds likewise; and I alone was spared and have come to report to you."

**1:17** While he was yet speaking, another messenger came and said to Job, "The horsemen formed three companies against us, and surrounded the camels and carried them away captive, and slew the servants with swords; and I alone was spared and have come to report to you."

**1:18** While he was yet speaking, another messenger came, saying to Job, "While your sons and your daughters were eating and drinking with their eldest brother,

**1:19** suddenly a great wind rushed out of the wilderness and struck the four corners of the house; and the house collapsed upon your children, and they perished; and I alone was spared and have come to report to you."

**1:20** Thereupon Job arose, tore his garments, and shaved the hair of his head; and falling to the ground, he worshiped,

**1:21** and said, "Naked I myself came forth from my mother's womb, and naked I shall also depart there. The Lord gave; the Lord took away. As it seemed good to the Lord, so it has come to pass; may the name of the Lord be blessed."

**1:22** In all these things that befell him, Job did not sin in anything before the Lord, nor did he impute folly to God.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — "Ausitis" preserves the Septuagint regional name (Αὐσῖτις), corresponding to Uz in the Hebrew text.
- **1:5** — The Septuagint uniquely specifies "one calf for a sin offering for their souls" alongside the sacrifices Job offered for his children.
- **1:6** — The Greek reads "the angels of God" (οἱ ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ) and "the devil" (ὁ διάβολος, lit. "the slanderer"), corresponding to "the sons of God" and "the adversary" (Satan) in the Hebrew text.
- **1:11** — The Greek verb εὐλογήσει ("will bless") preserves the ancient scribal euphemism for cursing God (also in 2:5).
