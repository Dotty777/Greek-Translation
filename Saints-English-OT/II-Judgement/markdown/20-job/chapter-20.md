## Text

**20:1** Then Zophar the Minaean answered and said:

**20:2** "I did not suppose that you would answer these things thus, and you do not understand more than I also.

**20:3** I shall hear the reproof of my shame, and a spirit out of understanding shall answer me.

**20:4** Have you not known these things from of old, since man was placed upon the earth?

**20:5** For the gladness of the ungodly is a dreadful downfall, and the rejoicing of the lawless is destruction;

**20:6** even if his gifts should ascend to heaven, and his sacrifice touch the clouds.

**20:7** For when he supposes that he is now firmly established, then he shall utterly perish; and those who knew him shall say, 'Where is he?'

**20:8** Like a dream that has flown away, he shall by no means be found, and he has taken flight like a vision of the night;

**20:9** An eye glanced upon him, and shall do so no more, and his place shall behold him no longer.

**20:10** May lesser men destroy his sons, and may his hands burn with pains.

**20:11** His bones were filled with his youth, and with him it shall lie down upon the dust.

**20:12** Though wickedness is sweet in his mouth, he will hide it beneath his tongue,

**20:13** he will not spare it and will not forsake it, and he will gather it in the midst of his throat,

**20:14** and he shall by no means be able to help himself; the gall of an asp is within his belly.

**20:15** Wealth unjustly gathered shall be vomited up; an angel shall drag him out of his house.

**20:16** May he suck the venom of dragons, and may the tongue of a serpent slay him.

**20:17** May he not behold the milking of the flocks, nor the pastures of honey and butter.

**20:18** For empty and vain things he has labored, for wealth of which he shall not taste—like tough gristle, unchewed, unswallowed.

**20:19** For he has crushed the houses of many mighty men; he has seized a dwelling, and did not establish it.

**20:20** There is no safety for his possessions; in his desire he shall not be saved.

**20:21** There is no remnant left of his provisions; therefore his good things shall not flourish.

**20:22** And when he supposes that he is now filled, he shall be afflicted, and every distress shall come upon him.

**20:23** If by any means he should fill his belly, may He send forth upon him the fury of wrath; may He rain pains upon him;

**20:24** and he shall by no means be saved from the hand of iron; may a bow of bronze wound him;

**20:25** and may an arrow pass through his body, and may stars walk about in his dwellings; terrors are upon him,

**20:26** and may all darkness await him; an unkindled fire shall devour him, and may a stranger afflict his house.

**20:27** And may heaven uncover his iniquities, and may the earth rise up against him.

**20:28** May destruction utterly drag away his house; may a day of wrath come upon him.

**20:29** This is the portion of an ungodly man from the Lord, and the possession of what belongs to him from the Overseer."

## Translator Notes

- **20:1** — The Septuagint identifies Zophar as "the Minaean" (Greek Meinaíos, associated with the Minaean people of Arabia), rather than the Masoretic "Naamathite."
- **20:6** — The Greek text reads "his gifts" and "his sacrifice," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to the wicked man's height and his head.
- **20:15** — The Septuagint uniquely reads "an angel shall drag him out of his house" rather than God casting wealth out of the man's belly; the Greek pronoun "him" could alternatively refer to "wealth."
- **20:29** — "The Overseer" translates Greek ho epískopos, a distinctive Septuagint divine title emphasizing God's watchful governance.
