## Text

**21:1** Then Job answered and said:

**21:2** "Hear, hear my words, so that this consolation may not be mine from you.

**21:3** Bear with me, and I will speak; then you shall not mock me.

**21:4** What then? Is my reproof against a man? Or why shall I not be moved to wrath?

**21:5** Looking upon me, you shall be astonished, putting your hand upon your cheek.

**21:6** For even when I remember, I am dismayed, and pains lay hold upon my flesh.

**21:7** Why do the ungodly live, and have also grown old in wealth?

**21:8** Their seed is according to their soul's desire, and their children are before their eyes.

**21:9** Their houses prosper, and fear is nowhere, and there is no scourge upon them from the Lord.

**21:10** Their cow has not miscarried, and their one with young has come safely through and has not failed.

**21:11** And they abide like everlasting sheep, and their little children play about;

**21:12** taking up the psaltery and the harp, they rejoice at the sound of a psalm.

**21:13** And they have completed their life in good things, and in the rest of Hades they have fallen asleep.

**21:14** Yet he says to the Lord, 'Depart from me; I do not desire to know Your ways;

**21:15** What is the Sufficient One, that we shall serve Him? And what profit is there that we shall meet Him?'

**21:16** For their good things were in their hands, and He does not look upon the works of the ungodly.

**21:17** Nevertheless, the lamp of the ungodly shall also be extinguished, and overthrow shall come upon them, and pangs shall seize them from wrath.

**21:18** And they shall be like chaff driven by the wind, or like dust which a whirlwind has snatched away.

**21:19** May his possessions fail his sons; He shall repay him, and he shall know.

**21:20** May his eyes behold his own slaughter, and may he not be saved from the Lord;

**21:21** for his desire is in his house with him, and the numbers of his months have been divided.

**21:22** Is it not the Lord who teaches understanding and knowledge? And He Himself shall judge murders.

**21:23** This one shall die in the strength of his simplicity, altogether in comfort and prospering,

**21:24** and his inward parts are full of fat, and his marrow is diffused;

**21:25** while another dies from bitterness of soul, having eaten no good thing;

**21:26** yet together they lie down upon the earth, and corruption has covered them.

**21:27** Therefore I know you, that with presumption you press upon me,

**21:28** so that you will say, 'Where is the house of a ruler? And where is the shelter of the tents of the ungodly?'

**21:29** Ask those who pass by along the way, and you shall not disown their signs;

**21:30** for the wicked man is spared for the day of destruction; to the day of His wrath they shall be led away.

**21:31** Who shall declare his way before his face? And what he himself has done, who shall repay him?

**21:32** And he himself was carried away to the tombs, and he himself kept watch upon the mounds.

**21:33** The pebbles of the torrent have been sweet to him, and every man shall depart after him, and those before him are innumerable.

**21:34** How then do you comfort me in vain? And as for my finding rest from you, it is nothing."

## Translator Notes

- **21:2** — The Septuagint includes a negative particle ("so that this consolation may not be mine from you"), rejecting the friends' comfort, whereas the Masoretic Text invites their listening to serve as their consolation.
- **21:13** — The Greek reads "in the rest of Hades they have fallen asleep," depicting a peaceful end for the ungodly rather than the sudden descent to Sheol in the Masoretic Text.
- **21:15** — "The Sufficient One" translates Greek Hikanós, the Septuagint of Job's characteristic rendering of the Hebrew divine name Shaddai.
- **21:22** — The Septuagint reads "He Himself shall judge murders" (phónous), differing from the Masoretic Text's reference to judging those on high.
