## Text

**42:1** Then Job responded and said to the Lord:

**42:2** "I know that You are able to do all things, and nothing is impossible with You.

**42:3** For who is he who hides counsel from You? And sparing words, does he think to hide from You also? And who will declare to me the things I did not know, things great and marvelous which I did not understand?

**42:4** Now hear me, O Lord, that I also may speak; I will ask, and You teach me.

**42:5** By the hearing of the ear I heard of You before, but now my eye has seen You.

**42:6** Therefore I have despised myself and melted away, and I regard myself as earth and ashes."

**42:7** Now it came to pass, after the Lord had spoken all these words to Job, that the Lord said to Eliphaz the Temanite, "You have sinned, you and your friends; for you have not spoken anything true before Me, as My servant Job has.

**42:8** Now therefore, take seven calves and seven rams, and go to My servant Job, and he shall offer burnt offerings for you. And My servant Job shall pray for you, for only his person will I accept; for were it not for his sake, I would have destroyed you, because you have not spoken what is true concerning My servant Job."

**42:9** So Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Minaean went and did as the Lord had commanded them; and He remitted their sin on account of Job.

**42:10** And the Lord increased Job; and when he prayed for his friends as well, He forgave them their sin; and the Lord gave Job double of all that he had before, in twofold measure.

**42:11** Then all his brothers and his sisters heard all the things that had happened to him, and came to him, together with all who had known him from the first; and they ate and drank with him, and comforted him, and marveled at all that the Lord had brought upon him; and each one gave him one ewe lamb and a tetradrachm of uncoined gold.

**42:12** And the Lord blessed the latter days of Job more than the former; and his livestock was fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, one thousand yoke of oxen, and one thousand grazing female donkeys.

**42:13** And seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

**42:14** And he called the first Day, the second Cassia, and the third Horn of Amalthea.

**42:15** And none were found like the daughters of Job, nor better than they, in the earth under heaven; and their father gave them an inheritance among their brothers.

**42:16** And Job lived after his affliction one hundred and seventy years, and all the years that he lived were two hundred and forty; and Job saw his sons and his sons' sons, to the fourth generation.

**42:17** And Job died, old and full of days. And it is written that he will rise again with those whom the Lord raises up. This man is interpreted from the Syriac book as dwelling in the land of Ausitis, on the borders of Idumea and Arabia; and his former name was Jobab. And taking an Arabian wife, he fathered a son whose name was Ennon; and he himself was of his father Zerah, a son of the sons of Esau, and of his mother Bozrah, so that he was the fifth from Abraham. And these are the kings who reigned in Edom, over which land he also ruled: first, Balak the son of Beor, and the name of his city was Dinhabah; and after Balak, Jobab, who is called Job; and after him, Husham, who was governor from the Temanite land; and after him, Hadad the son of Barad, who cut down Midian in the plain of Moab, and the name of his city was Geththaim. And the friends who came to him were Eliphaz, of the sons of Esau, king of the Temanites; Bildad, sovereign of the Shuhites; and Zophar, king of the Minaeans.

## Translator Notes

- **42:3** — The Greek differs markedly from the Hebrew, asking who hides counsel from God or spares words thinking to hide from Him, and asking who will declare the great and marvelous things Job did not know or understand.
- **42:6** — The Greek renders Job's confession as despising himself, melting away, and regarding himself as earth and ashes, rather than repenting in dust and ashes as in the Hebrew.
- **42:9** — The Greek identifies Zophar as "the Minaean" (Greek Meinaîos) rather than "the Naamathite" of the Hebrew text; similarly in 42:11 the friends' gifts appear as a ewe lamb and a tetradrachm of uncoined gold.
- **42:14** — The Greek renders the names of Job's daughters as Day, Cassia, and Horn of Amalthea (alluding to the Greek mythological horn of plenty), corresponding to Hebrew Jemimah, Keziah, and Keren-Happuch.
- **42:17** — Following the Greek lifespan figures in 42:16 (one hundred and seventy years after his affliction and two hundred and forty in total), this verse adds a Septuagint epilogue on Job's resurrection and genealogy from "the Syriac book" identifying him with Jobab in Ausitis (Uz), preserving Greek-distinct names such as Ennon, Balak (for Bela), Barad (for Bedad), and Geththaim (for Avith), and treating Bozrah as his mother's name.
