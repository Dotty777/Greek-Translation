## Text

**40:1** And the Lord God answered Job and said,

**40:2** "Will anyone turn aside judgment with the Sufficient One? And he who reproves God, shall he answer it?"

**40:3** Then Job answered and said to the Lord,

**40:4** "Why am I still judged, being admonished and reproving the Lord, hearing such things though I am nothing? And what answer shall I give to these things? I will lay my hand upon my mouth.

**40:5** Once I have spoken, but a second time I will not continue."

**40:6** Then the Lord answered Job again out of the cloud and said,

**40:7** "No, rather gird up your loins like a man; I will question you, and you answer Me.

**40:8** Do not cast aside My judgment. Do you think that I have dealt with you in any other way than that you might be shown righteous?

**40:9** Or do you have an arm against the Lord, or do you thunder with a voice against Him?

**40:10** Take up now loftiness and power, and clothe yourself with glory and honor.

**40:11** Send forth angels in wrath, and humble every insolent one,

**40:12** and extinguish the proud; cause the ungodly to decay at once,

**40:13** and hide them in the earth together, and fill their faces with dishonor.

**40:14** Then I will confess that your right hand is able to save.

**40:15** Yet now, behold wild beasts beside you; they eat grass like oxen.

**40:16** Behold now, his strength is in his loins, and his power is in the navel of his belly.

**40:17** He sets up his tail like a cypress, and his sinews are intertwined.

**40:18** His ribs are ribs of bronze, and his backbone is cast iron.

**40:19** This is the beginning of the Lord's creation, made to be mocked by His angels.

**40:20** And going up upon a steep mountain, he brings joy to the four-footed beasts in Tartarus.

**40:21** He lies down beneath trees of every kind, beside the papyrus and reed and sedge.

**40:22** And great trees cast shade over him with their shoots, and the branches of the field.

**40:23** If a flood arises, he will by no means notice it; he is confident that the Jordan will dash against his mouth.

**40:24** Will anyone take him before his eye? Acting craftily, will one pierce his nose?

**40:25** And will you draw a dragon with a hook, and put a halter around his nose?

**40:26** Will you bind a ring in his nostril, and pierce his lip with a clasp?

**40:27** Will he speak to you with supplication, softly with petition?

**40:28** Will he make a covenant with you? And will you take him as a perpetual servant?

**40:29** Will you play with him as with a bird, or bind him like a sparrow for a child?

**40:30** Do the nations feed upon him, and do the nations of the Phoenicians divide him up?

**40:31** Every sailing vessel gathered together could by no means carry one hide of his tail, nor his head in the boats of fishermen.

**40:32** And you will lay a hand upon him, remembering the battle that takes place in his body."

## Translator Notes

- **40:2** — Greek Hikanos ("the Sufficient One") is the Septuagint's characteristic title rendering Hebrew Shaddai ("the Almighty") throughout Job.
- **40:8** — Unlike the Masoretic Text ("condemn Me that you may be justified"), the Greek asks whether God has dealt with Job for any other purpose than that he might be shown righteous.
- **40:15** — The Greek renders Hebrew Behemoth as the common plural noun theria ("wild beasts") with a plural verb, before shifting to singular pronouns in verse 16.
- **40:19** — Literally "made to be mocked by His angels," a distinctive Septuagint reading that recurs in 41:25.
- **40:20** — Greek tartaros ("Tartarus"), a cosmological term for the deepest abyss, is used here and again in 41:24.
