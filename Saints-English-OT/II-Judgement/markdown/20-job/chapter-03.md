## Text

**3:1** After this Job opened his mouth,

**3:2** and cursed his day, saying:

**3:3** "May the day perish on which I was born, and that night in which they said, 'Behold, a male!'

**3:4** May that night be darkness, and may the Lord not seek after it from above, nor may light shine into it;

**3:5** rather, may darkness and the shadow of death seize it, and may thick gloom come upon it.

**3:6** May that day and that night be accursed; may darkness carry it away; may it not belong to the days of the year, nor be numbered among the days of the months.

**3:7** Instead, may that night be anguish, and may neither gladness nor rejoicing come upon it;

**3:8** rather, may the one who curses that day curse it, the one who is about to subdue the great sea monster.

**3:9** May the stars of that night be darkened; may it wait and never come to illumination, and may it not behold the morning star rising,

**3:10** because it did not shut the gates of my mother's womb; for it would have removed trouble from my eyes.

**3:11** For why did I not die in the womb? And why did I come forth from the belly and not immediately perish?

**3:12** And why did knees receive me? And why did I nurse at breasts?

**3:13** Now, having lain down, I would have been quiet; having fallen asleep, I would have found rest

**3:14** with kings and counselors of the earth, who prided themselves upon swords,

**3:15** or with rulers whose gold was abundant, who filled their houses with silver,

**3:16** or like an untimely birth coming forth from a mother's womb, or like infants who did not see light.

**3:17** There the ungodly have burned out the fury of their wrath; there those weary in body have found rest;

**3:18** and together the eternal ones have not heard the voice of the tax collector.

**3:19** Small and great are there, and the servant who fears his master.

**3:20** For why is light given to those in bitterness, and life to souls in anguish,

**3:21** who long for death and do not obtain it, digging for it as for treasures,

**3:22** and who grow exceedingly glad if they attain it?

**3:23** Death is rest for a man, for God has shut him in.

**3:24** For groaning comes before my food, and I weep, gripped by fear.

**3:25** For the fear about which I was anxious has come upon me, and that which I dreaded has met me.

**3:26** I have neither been at peace, nor been quiet, nor found rest; rather, wrath has come upon me."

## Translator Notes

- **3:8** — "The great sea monster" translates τὸ μέγα κῆτος, the Septuagint rendering corresponding to Leviathan in the Hebrew text.
- **3:14** — The Septuagint reads "who prided themselves upon swords" (ἐπὶ ξίφεσιν), reflecting a reading of the Hebrew consonants as "sword" rather than "ruins."
- **3:18** — "The eternal ones" (οἱ αἰώνιοι) designates the dead in the eternal abode, where the Hebrew text reads "the prisoners"; similarly, "tax collector" (φορολόγου) corresponds to "taskmaster."
- **3:23** — The Septuagint opens the verse with "Death is rest for a man," whereas the Hebrew text reads "to a man whose way is hidden."
