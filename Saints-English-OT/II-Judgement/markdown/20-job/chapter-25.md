## Text

**25:1** Then Bildad the Shuhite answered, saying,

**25:2** "For what prelude or dread comes from Him, who makes the whole universe in the highest?

**25:3** For should anyone suppose that there is any delay for raiders? And upon whom will an ambush from Him not come?

**25:4** For how shall a mortal be righteous before the Lord? Or who, born of a woman, could cleanse himself?

**25:5** Indeed, He commands the moon, and it does not shine forth; and the stars are not pure in His sight.

**25:6** Alas, then, man is decay, and a son of man is a worm."

## Translator Notes

- **25:2** — Greek "prooimion" ("prelude" or "opening") reflects reading the Hebrew root for "proverb/discourse" rather than "dominion," and "the whole universe" ("ten sympasan") appears where the Masoretic Text has "peace."
- **25:3** — The Septuagint reads "delay for raiders" and "an ambush from Him," differing notably from the Masoretic Text's "number to his armies" and "his light."
- **25:6** — Greek "ea de" is an emphatic interjection ("alas, then" or "let be"), rendering the Hebrew "how much less" expression.
