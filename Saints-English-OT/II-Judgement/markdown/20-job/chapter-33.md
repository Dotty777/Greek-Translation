## Text

**33:1** "Nevertheless, hear my words, O Job, and give ear to my speech;

**33:2** for behold, I have opened my mouth, and my tongue has spoken.

**33:3** My heart is pure in words, and the understanding of my lips shall conceive pure things.

**33:4** The divine Spirit is what made me, and the breath of the Almighty is what teaches me.

**33:5** If you are able, give me an answer to these things; stand firm, take your place facing me, and I facing you.

**33:6** Out of clay you have been formed, even as I also; out of the same we have been formed.

**33:7** Dread of me shall not terrify you, nor shall my hand be heavy upon you.

**33:8** Yet you have spoken in my ears, and I have heard the sound of words;

**33:9** because you say, 'I am pure, not having sinned; I am blameless, for I have not committed iniquity.

**33:10** Yet He has found fault against me, and He has regarded me as an adversary;

**33:11** He has put my foot in the stocks, and He has watched all my ways.'

**33:12** For how do you say, 'I am righteous, and He has not listened to me'? For He who is above mortals is eternal.

**33:13** And you say, 'Why has He not listened to every word of my cause?'

**33:14** For the Lord speaks once, and a second time:

**33:15** in a dream, or in a nightly meditation, as when a dreadful fear falls upon men, during slumberings upon a bed;

**33:16** then He uncovers the mind of men; with such visions of dread He terrifies them,

**33:17** to turn a man away from unrighteousness; and He delivers his body from a fall.

**33:18** And He spares his soul from death, and from falling in war.

**33:19** Again He chastens him with sickness upon his bed, and the multitude of his bones grows numb;

**33:20** and he is by no means able to receive any food of grain, though his soul will crave food;

**33:21** until his flesh rots away, and he displays his bones empty.

**33:22** And his soul draws near to death, and this life of his is in Hades;

**33:23** if there are a thousand death-bearing angels, not one of them shall wound him. If he determines in his heart to turn back to the Lord, and declares to man his own fault, and shows his folly,

**33:24** He will uphold him so that he does not fall into death; and He will renew his body like plaster upon a wall, and He will fill his bones with marrow;

**33:25** and He will make his flesh tender like that of a babe, and He will restore him grown to manhood among men.

**33:26** And when he has prayed to the Lord, his prayer shall be acceptable to Him, and he shall enter with a cheerful countenance with open confession; and He will render righteousness to men.

**33:27** Then at that time a man shall reproach himself, saying, 'What manner of things was I doing? And He has not chastened me according to what my sins deserved.

**33:28** Save my soul from going into corruption, and my life shall see the light.'

**33:29** Behold, the Mighty One works all these things in three ways with a man;

**33:30** yet He has delivered my soul from death, that my life may praise Him in the light.

**33:31** Give ear, O Job, and listen to me; keep silent, and I myself will speak.

**33:32** If there is a word, answer me; speak, for I desire that you be justified.

**33:33** If not, you listen to me; keep silent, and I will teach you."

## Translator Notes

- **33:14** — The Greek syntax is elliptical, omitting the Hebrew clause "yet man does not perceive it" and linking "a second time" directly to the dream and nightly meditation in verse 15.
- **33:23** — The Septuagint reads "a thousand death-bearing angels" followed by a call to repentance, diverging markedly from the Masoretic Hebrew's "a mediator, one of a thousand."
- **33:24** — The Greek simile "like plaster upon a wall" for the renewal of the body is distinctive to the Septuagint tradition.
- **33:30** — The Greek shifts to the first person ("my soul ... my life") and reads "praise Him," differing from the third-person wording of the Masoretic Hebrew.
