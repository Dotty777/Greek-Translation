## Text

**9:1** Then Job answered and said:

**9:2** "In truth I know that it is so; for how shall a mortal be righteous before the Lord?

**9:3** For if he desires to contend in judgment with Him, He will by no means answer him, so that he may not reply against one word of His out of a thousand.

**9:4** For He is wise in understanding, both mighty and great; who has become stubborn before Him and endured?

**9:5** He who wears away the mountains, and they do not know it; He who overturns them in wrath;

**9:6** He who shakes the earth beneath heaven from its foundations, and its pillars tremble;

**9:7** He who speaks to the sun, and it does not rise, and who seals up the stars;

**9:8** He who stretched out the heaven alone, walking upon the sea as upon firm ground;

**9:9** He who makes the Pleiades and Hesperus and Arcturus, and the chambers of the South;

**9:10** He who does great and unsearchable things, both glorious and marvelous things, of which there is no number.

**9:11** If He passes over me, I shall by no means see Him; and if He passes by me, not even then do I perceive it.

**9:12** If He takes away, who will turn Him back? Or who will say to Him, 'What have You done?'

**9:13** For He Himself has turned aside wrath; by Him the sea monsters beneath heaven were bowed down.

**9:14** And if He will listen to me, surely He will judge my words.

**9:15** For even if I am righteous, He will not hear me; I will make supplication for His judgment.

**9:16** And if I call and He does not answer, I do not believe that He has heard me.

**9:17** Will He crush me with thick darkness? Yet He has made my bruises many without cause.

**9:18** For He does not allow me to draw breath, but He has filled me with bitterness.

**9:19** For indeed, because He is strong, He prevails; who then shall withstand His judgment?

**9:20** For if I am righteous, my mouth will speak impiously; and if I am blameless, I shall turn out crooked.

**9:21** For even if I have acted impiously, I do not know it in my soul; yet my life is taken away.

**9:22** Therefore I said, 'Wrath destroys the great and the mighty,'

**9:23** for the worthless are in an extraordinary death, yet the righteous are mocked;

**9:24** for they have been delivered into the hands of an ungodly man. He covers the faces of its judges; and if it is not He, who is it?

**9:25** Now my life is swifter than a runner; they fled away, and they did not see.

**9:26** Or is there indeed a track of a way for ships, or of a flying eagle seeking prey?

**9:27** For even if I speak, I shall forget while speaking; bowing down my face, I shall groan.

**9:28** I tremble in all my limbs, for I know that You will not leave me guiltless.

**9:29** And since I am ungodly, why did I not die?

**9:30** For if I wash myself with snow, and cleanse myself with clean hands,

**9:31** You have plunged me thoroughly in filth, and my robe has abhorred me.

**9:32** For You are not a man like me with whom I may contend, that we should come together with one accord into judgment.

**9:33** Oh, that there were a mediator for us, both reproving and hearing between us both;

**9:34** let Him remove the rod from me, and let His terror not confound me,

**9:35** and I shall by no means be afraid, but I will speak; for I am not conscious of being so."

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — The Greek reads 'wears away' or 'makes old' (palaion) rather than the Masoretic Hebrew 'removes'.
- **9:9** — The Greek names Hesperus (the Evening Star) alongside the Pleiades and Arcturus, whereas the Hebrew lists the Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades.
- **9:13** — Unlike the Masoretic Hebrew ('God will not turn back His anger'), the Greek lacks a negative particle ('He Himself has turned aside wrath') and renders the Hebrew 'helpers of Rahab' as 'the sea monsters beneath heaven'.
- **9:22** — The Greek reads 'Wrath destroys the great and the mighty', differing from the Masoretic Hebrew ('He destroys the blameless and the wicked').
- **9:24** — The Greek opens with a plural verb referring to the righteous of verse 23 ('they have been delivered'), yet retains the feminine singular pronoun 'its' (autes, reflecting 'the earth') in 'its judges'.
