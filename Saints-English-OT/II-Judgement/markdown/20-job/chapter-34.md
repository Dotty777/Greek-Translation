## Text

**34:1** Then Elihu answered and said:

**34:2** "Hear me, you wise men; you who have knowledge, give ear;

**34:3** for the ear tests words, and the palate tastes food.

**34:4** Let us choose judgment for ourselves; let us know among ourselves what is good.

**34:5** For Job has said, 'I am righteous; the Lord has removed my judgment,

**34:6** and He has dealt falsely with my judgment; violent is my arrow without injustice.'

**34:7** What man is like Job, drinking scorn like water,

**34:8** having not sinned nor acted ungodly, or shared a way with those who practice lawlessness, to walk with the ungodly?

**34:9** For do not say, 'There shall be no visitation for a man,' yet there is a visitation for him from the Lord.

**34:10** Therefore, you of understanding heart, hear me: Far be it from me to act ungodly before the Lord, and before the Almighty to confound justice;

**34:11** rather, He renders to a man according to what each of them does, and in the path of a man He shall find him.

**34:12** Now do you suppose that the Lord will do wrong? Or will the Almighty confound judgment,

**34:13** He who made the earth? And who is He who makes the world under heaven and all things that are in it?

**34:14** For if He should choose to hold back, and to retain the spirit with Himself,

**34:15** all flesh shall perish together, and every mortal shall depart to the earth from which he was also formed.

**34:16** Now if you are not admonished, hear these things; give ear to the voice of words.

**34:17** Behold Him who hates lawless deeds and destroys the wicked, who is eternally righteous.

**34:18** Ungodly is he who says to a king, 'You act lawlessly,' and to rulers, 'Most ungodly one!'—

**34:19** who does not feel shame before the face of an honorable man, nor knows how to bestow honor upon the great, so that their persons might be respected.

**34:20** And in vain shall it turn out for them to cry out and to implore a man; for they dealt lawlessly when the powerless were turned aside.

**34:21** For He Himself is the beholder of the works of men, and nothing of what they do is hidden from Him;

**34:22** nor shall there be any place for those who do lawless deeds to hide themselves;

**34:23** for He will no longer lay anything upon a man.

**34:24** For the Lord looks upon all—He who comprehends unsearchable things, both glorious and wondrous things, of which there is no number;

**34:25** He who knows their works; and He shall overturn the night, and it shall be brought low.

**34:26** And He has extinguished the ungodly, and they are visible before Him;

**34:27** because they turned aside from the law of God, and they did not acknowledge His ordinances,

**34:28** so as to bring upon Him the cry of the needy, and He shall hear the cry of the poor.

**34:29** And He Himself shall grant quietness, and who shall condemn? And He shall hide His face, and who shall behold Him—both against a nation and against a man alike,

**34:30** making a hypocritical man king because of the perversity of the people.

**34:31** For there is one who says to the Mighty One, 'I have received; I will not take a pledge;

**34:32** apart from myself I shall see; You show me; if I have wrought iniquity, I will surely do so no more.'

**34:33** Will He repay it from you, because you will reject it? For you shall choose, and not I; therefore speak what you know.

**34:34** Therefore those of understanding heart shall say these things, and a wise man has heard my word.

**34:35** But Job has not spoken with understanding; his words are not with knowledge.

**34:36** Nevertheless, learn, O Job: do not give an answer any longer like the foolish,

**34:37** lest we add to our sins, and lawlessness be reckoned against us while we speak many words before the Lord."

## Translator Notes

- **34:6** — In the Greek text, the verb is third-person singular ("He has dealt falsely with my judgment"), attributing the charge to the Lord, and "my arrow" (belos) preserves the Greek metaphor for an inflicted wound.
- **34:8** — The Septuagint uniquely includes "having not sinned nor acted ungodly," continuing Elihu's ironic rhetorical question about Job in contrast to the Masoretic Text.
- **34:10** — The Greek reads "Far be it from me to act ungodly before the Lord," expressing Elihu's own recoil from impiety, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Far be it from God to do wickedness."
- **34:23** — The Greek text of this verse is notably brief and elliptical compared with the longer Hebrew Masoretic Text, leaving the object of "lay" unexpressed.
- **34:30** — Unlike the Masoretic Text ("that a godless man should not reign"), the Septuagint states positively that God sets a hypocritical man as king because of the people's perversity.
