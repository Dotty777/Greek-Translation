## Text

**4:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:

**4:2** "Have you often been spoken to in weariness? Yet who shall endure the force of your words?

**4:3** For if you instructed many, and encouraged the hands of the weak,

**4:4** and raised up the feeble with words, and bestowed courage upon powerless knees;

**4:5** yet now distress has come upon you and touched you, and you are dismayed.

**4:6** Is not your fear in folly, and your hope, and the wickedness of your way?

**4:7** Remember, then: who, being pure, perished, or when did the true perish root and branch?

**4:8** Just as I have seen those who plow wicked deeds, so those who sow them shall reap sorrows for themselves.

**4:9** By the command of the Lord they shall perish, and by the breath of His wrath they shall vanish away.

**4:10** The strength of the lion, and the voice of the lioness, and the exultation of dragons have been quenched.

**4:11** The ant-lion has perished for having no prey, and the cubs of lions have forsaken one another.

**4:12** Now if any true word had been in your speeches, none of these evils would have befallen you. Shall not my ear receive extraordinary things from Him?

**4:13** Now terror and a sound in the night, fear falling upon men—

**4:14** shuddering met me, and trembling, which greatly shook my bones;

**4:15** and a spirit came upon my face, and my hair and flesh shuddered.

**4:16** I arose, and I did not recognize it; I looked, and there was no form before my eyes, only I heard a breeze and a voice:

**4:17** 'What then? Shall a mortal be pure before the Lord, or a man blameless from his works?'

**4:18** If He does not put trust in His servants, and has perceived something crooked against His angels,

**4:19** then those who dwell in houses of clay—of whom we ourselves also are from the same clay—He has struck them like a moth.

**4:20** And from morning until evening they are no more; because they are not able to help themselves, they have perished.

**4:21** For He breathed upon them, and they withered away; they perished because they had no wisdom."

## Translator Notes

- **4:6** — The Greek reads 'in folly' and 'the wickedness of your way,' turning Eliphaz's question into a direct reproach of Job's piety and conduct, in contrast to the Hebrew Masoretic Text's reference to 'your confidence' and 'the integrity of your ways.'
- **4:10** — The Greek speaks of the exultation of 'dragons' (drakontōn) rather than the teeth of young lions found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **4:11** — The Greek renders the rare poetic term for a lion with myrmēkoleōn ('ant-lion'), a composite creature known in ancient natural lore.
- **4:16** — The Greek uses the first-person verb 'I arose' (anestēn) describing Eliphaz's reaction, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text describes the apparition standing still; the voice's quotation in 4:17 closes before Eliphaz speaks in the first-person plural ('we ourselves') in 4:19.
