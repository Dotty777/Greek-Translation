## Text

**31:1** I have made a covenant with my eyes, and I will not turn my thoughts upon a virgin.

**31:2** And moreover God has apportioned from above, and an inheritance of the Almighty from the highest places.

**31:3** Woe! Destruction is for the unrighteous, and estrangement for those who practice lawlessness.

**31:4** Will He not behold my way, and shall not all my steps be numbered?

**31:5** If I had walked with mockers, or if my foot also has hastened toward deceit—

**31:6** for I have been weighed in a just balance, and the Lord knows my innocence—

**31:7** if my foot has turned aside from the way, or if my heart also has followed after the eye, or if with my hands also I have touched bribes—

**31:8** then may I sow and let others eat, and may I become rootless upon the earth.

**31:9** If my heart has followed after the wife of another man, or if I also have lain in wait at her doors—

**31:10** then may my wife also please another, and may my little children be brought low.

**31:11** For an unrestrainable fury of wrath is the defiling of a man's wife;

**31:12** for it is a fire burning in all parts, and wherever it comes, it destroys from the roots.

**31:13** And if I also despised the cause of my manservant or maidservant when they contended against me—

**31:14** for what shall I do if the Lord makes an examination of me? And if He also makes a visitation, what answer shall I give?

**31:15** Just as I also was formed in the womb, were they not also formed? Indeed, we were formed in the same womb.

**31:16** And the helpless did not fail to receive whatever need they had, nor did I cause the eye of the widow to waste away.

**31:17** And if I also ate my morsel alone, and did not share it with the orphan—

**31:18** for from my youth I nourished them as a father, and from my mother's womb I guided them—

**31:19** and if I also overlooked a naked man perishing, and did not clothe him;

**31:20** and if the helpless did not bless me, while their shoulders were warmed from the shearing of my lambs;

**31:21** if I lifted up my hand against an orphan, trusting that abundant help was at my disposal,

**31:22** then may my shoulder fall away from the collarbone, and may my arm be shattered from the elbow.

**31:23** For dread seized me, and by reason of His burden I cannot endure.

**31:24** If I appointed gold as my dust, or if I also trusted in a precious stone,

**31:25** or if I also rejoiced when my wealth became abundant, or if I also laid my hand upon countless things;

**31:26** Or do we not see the shining sun failing, and the moon waning? For it is not in their power.

**31:27** And if my heart was secretly deceived, and if, laying my hand upon my mouth, I kissed it—

**31:28** then may this also be reckoned to me as the greatest lawlessness, because I have lied before the Lord Most High.

**31:29** And if I also gloated over the downfall of my enemies, and my heart said, "Well done!"—

**31:30** then may my ear hear my curse, and may I be made a byword by my people while afflicted.

**31:31** And if my maidservants also often said, "Who would grant us to be filled with his flesh?"—though I was exceedingly kind—

**31:32** and no stranger lodged outside, for my door stood open to everyone who came;

**31:33** and if also, having sinned unwittingly, I concealed my sin—

**31:34** for I was not abashed before the throng of the multitude so as not to confess before them—and if I also allowed a helpless man to go out of my door with an empty bosom—

**31:35** Who would grant me one who hears me? And if I had not feared the hand of the Lord, the written bond that I held against anyone

**31:36** putting it upon my shoulders as a crown, I would have read it aloud;

**31:37** and if I did not tear it up and return it, taking nothing from the debtor—

**31:38** if the land has ever groaned against me, or if its furrows also have wept with one accord;

**31:39** or if I also ate its strength alone without price, or if I also took the life of the owner of the land and grieved him—

**31:40** then instead of wheat may nettles come forth for me, and instead of barley, brambles. And Job ceased from his words.

## Translator Notes

- **31:2** — This Greek text reads kai eti ("and moreover") as a declarative statement rather than a question; Hikanou (literally "of the Sufficient One") is the Septuagint's standard title for Shaddai ("the Almighty").
- **31:23** — Greek lemma ("burden" or "weight") denotes God's overwhelming charge or majesty, while phobos ("dread") stands without an explicit genitive "of God" in this Greek text.
- **31:24** — This Greek text reads eis choun mou ("as my dust," or "mound"), whereas some other Greek witnesses read eis ischyn mou ("as my strength").
- **31:33** — Greek akousios ("unwittingly" or "involuntarily") is the Septuagint reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "like Adam" (or "like men").
- **31:35** — In verses 35–37 the Septuagint frames Job's words around a written debt-bond (syngraphe) he held against a debtor (chreophiletou), which out of fear of the Lord he tore up and returned without taking payment.
