## Text

**15:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:

**15:2** "Will a wise man give an answer of windy understanding, and fill up pain of the belly,

**15:3** reproving with words that are not fitting, with speeches in which there is no profit?

**15:4** Have you not also cast off fear, and consummated such words before the Lord?

**15:5** You are guilty by the words of your mouth, and you have not discerned the words of the mighty.

**15:6** May your own mouth convict you, and not I; and your lips shall testify against you.

**15:7** What then? Were you born the first of men? Or were you fashioned before the hills?

**15:8** Or have you heard the decree of the Lord? And has wisdom come to you?

**15:9** For what do you know that we do not know? Or what do you understand that we also do not?

**15:10** Indeed, both the elder and the very aged are among us, weightier in days than your father.

**15:11** For little of what you have sinned have you been scourged; you have spoken greatly beyond measure.

**15:12** What has your heart dared? Or what have your eyes aimed at,

**15:13** that you have broken out in wrath before the Lord, and brought forth such words from your mouth?

**15:14** For what is a mortal, that he should be blameless, or one born of a woman, that he should be righteous?

**15:15** If He does not put trust in holy ones, and heaven is not clean before Him,

**15:16** how much less one who is abominable and unclean, a man who drinks iniquities like a drink!

**15:17** But I will declare to you, hear me; what indeed I have seen I will declare to you,

**15:18** what wise men shall tell, and did not conceal their fathers;

**15:19** to them alone the land was given, and no foreigner came upon them.

**15:20** All the life of an ungodly man is in anxiety, and numbered years are given to a tyrant,

**15:21** and his terror is in his ears; when he seems already to be at peace, his overthrow shall come.

**15:22** Let him not trust that he will return from darkness, for he is already appointed into the hands of iron,

**15:23** and he is assigned as food for vultures; and he knows within himself that he remains for a carcass. And a dark day shall whirl him about,

**15:24** and distress and tribulation shall seize him like a commander falling in the front rank;

**15:25** because he has lifted up his hands against the Lord, and has stiffened his neck before the Lord Almighty,

**15:26** and has run against Him in insolence, with the thickness of the back of his shield;

**15:27** because he has covered his face with his fat, and has made a fold upon his thighs.

**15:28** May he lodge in desolate cities, and may he enter into uninhabited houses; and what they prepared, others shall carry away.

**15:29** Neither shall he become rich, nor shall his possessions remain; he shall not cast a shadow upon the earth,

**15:30** nor shall he escape the darkness; may the wind wither his shoot, and may his blossom fall away.

**15:31** Let him not trust that he will endure, for empty things shall turn out for him;

**15:32** his cutting shall perish before its season, and his branch shall never grow thick;

**15:33** and may he be gathered like an unripe grape before its season, and may he fall away like the blossom of an olive tree.

**15:34** For the testimony of the ungodly is death, and fire shall burn the houses of those who receive bribes.

**15:35** And he shall conceive pains, and empty things shall turn out for him, and his belly shall bring forth deceit."

## Translator Notes

- **15:2** — The Greek reads "windy understanding" and "pain of the belly," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of "windy knowledge" and filling the belly with the "east wind."
- **15:11** — The Septuagint reads "for little of what you have sinned have you been scourged," differing markedly from the Hebrew text's question concerning the "consolations of God."
- **15:18** — The Greek places "their fathers" in the accusative ("did not conceal their fathers"), rather than the Hebrew prepositional phrase "from their fathers."
- **15:34** — The Greek reads "testimony" (martyrion), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonantal root rendered "company" in the Masoretic Text.
