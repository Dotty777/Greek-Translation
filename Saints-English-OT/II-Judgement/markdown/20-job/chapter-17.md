## Text

**17:1** I am perishing, carried away by the wind; I plead for burial, yet I do not obtain it.

**17:2** I implore in my weariness—and what have I done?

**17:3** Strangers have stolen my possessions. Who is this man? Let him be bound to my hand.

**17:4** Because You have hidden their heart from understanding, therefore You will by no means exalt them.

**17:5** He will report evils for a portion, and eyes have wasted away over sons.

**17:6** You have made me a byword among the nations, and I have become a laughingstock to them.

**17:7** For my eyes have grown dim from wrath, and I have been greatly besieged by all.

**17:8** Astonishment has seized the upright at this, and may the righteous rise up against the lawless.

**17:9** May the faithful hold fast to his own way, and may the one clean of hands take courage.

**17:10** Nevertheless, all of you press on, and come now, for I do not find anything true among you.

**17:11** My days have passed away in tumult, and the joints of my heart have been broken.

**17:12** I have turned night into day; light is near before the face of darkness.

**17:13** For if I wait, Hades is my house, and in thick darkness my bed is spread.

**17:14** I have called upon death to be my father, and corruption to be my mother and my sister.

**17:15** Where then is my hope any longer? Or shall I see my good things?

**17:16** Will they go down with me into Hades, or shall we go down together to the dust?

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — The Septuagint diverges markedly from the Masoretic Text's plea for a pledge, reading instead that strangers have stolen Job's possessions and demanding that the offender be bound to his hand.
- **17:5** — The Greek reads "evils" rather than "friends," reflecting a different vocalization of the underlying Hebrew consonants, followed by the image of eyes wasting away over sons.
- **17:11** — "The joints of my heart" translates Greek arthra ("joints" or "sinews"), rendering the Hebrew imagery of broken heartstrings or desires.
