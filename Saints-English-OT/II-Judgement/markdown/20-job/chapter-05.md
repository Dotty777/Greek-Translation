## Text

**5:1** "Now call out, if indeed anyone will answer you, or if you shall see any of the holy angels.

**5:2** For indeed, wrath slays a foolish man, and jealousy puts to death one who has gone astray.

**5:3** I myself have seen fools taking root, but immediately their habitation was devoured.

**5:4** May their sons be far from deliverance, and may they be mocked at the doors of inferiors, and there shall be no one to rescue them.

**5:5** For what those men gathered, the righteous shall eat, and they themselves shall not be delivered from evils; may their strength be drained dry.

**5:6** For trouble shall by no means come forth from the earth, nor shall distress sprout up from the mountains;

**5:7** but man is born to trouble, and the young of the vulture fly in the heights.

**5:8** Nevertheless, I will implore the Lord, and I will call upon the Lord, the Master of all,

**5:9** who does great and unsearchable things, glorious and extraordinary things, of which there is no number;

**5:10** who gives rain upon the earth, sending water upon the land under heaven;

**5:11** who sets the lowly on high, and raises up those who are lost;

**5:12** who overturns the counsels of the crafty, and their hands shall by no means accomplish anything true.

**5:13** He is the One who seizes the wise in shrewdness, and He has confounded the counsel of the devious;

**5:14** by day darkness shall meet them, and at noonday may they grope as in the night.

**5:15** And may they perish in war, and may the powerless go forth from the hand of the powerful;

**5:16** and may there be hope for the powerless, and may the mouth of the unjust be stopped.

**5:17** Now blessed is the man whom the Lord has reproved; and do not reject the admonition of the Almighty.

**5:18** For He Himself causes pain, and again He restores; He has struck, and His hands have healed.

**5:19** Six times He shall deliver you out of distresses, and in the seventh, evil shall by no means touch you.

**5:20** In famine He shall rescue you from death, and in war He shall release you from the hand of iron.

**5:21** From the scourge of the tongue He shall hide you, and you shall by no means be afraid of coming evils.

**5:22** You shall laugh at the unjust and the lawless, and you shall by no means be afraid of wild beasts;

**5:23** for wild beasts shall be at peace with you.

**5:24** Then you shall know that your house shall be at peace, and the habitation of your tent shall by no means fail.

**5:25** And you shall know that your seed is abundant, and your children shall be like all the herbage of the field.

**5:26** And you shall come to the grave like ripe grain reaped in due season, or like a stack of the threshing floor gathered in at its proper time.

**5:27** Behold, thus we have searched out these things; these are the things we have heard; now know for yourself whether you have done anything."

## Translator Notes

- **5:4** — The rare Greek verb kolabrizō denotes treating with mocking contempt or humiliation, and the Greek specifies 'the doors of inferiors' rather than the city gate of the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **5:5** — The rare Greek verb eksiphōnizō literally means to draw out with a siphon, vividly picturing the strength or wealth of the wicked being drained dry.
- **5:7** — The Greek renders the poetic Hebrew phrase for sparks or winged flames as 'the young of the vulture' (neossoi gypos) flying in the heights.
- **5:23** — The Greek omits the clause concerning a covenant with the stones of the field present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text, preserving a concise statement of peace with wild beasts.
