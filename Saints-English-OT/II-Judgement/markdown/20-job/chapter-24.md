## Text

**24:1** "And why have seasons escaped the notice of the Lord,

**24:2** while the ungodly have overstepped a boundary, seizing a flock together with its shepherd?

**24:3** They led away the beast of burden of orphans, and took the widow's ox as a pledge.

**24:4** They turned the weak aside from the righteous way, and with one accord the meek of the earth hid themselves.

**24:5** And they departed like donkeys in a field, having gone out of their own order beyond me; bread became sweet to him for the young.

**24:6** They reaped before its season a field that was not theirs; the weak worked the vineyards of the ungodly without wages and without food.

**24:7** They made many lie down naked without garments, and they took away the covering of their soul;

**24:8** they are drenched by the showers of the mountains; because they have no shelter for themselves, they have embraced a rock.

**24:9** They snatched the orphan from the breast, and they humbled the one who had fallen.

**24:10** And they made them lie down naked unjustly, and they took away the morsel of the hungry.

**24:11** In narrow places they lay in ambush unjustly, and they did not know the way of the righteous.

**24:12** They were cast out from the city and their own houses, and the soul of infants groaned aloud.

**24:13** And why has He Himself not made a visitation of these things? While they were upon the earth, they did not recognize it; they did not know the way of righteousness, nor did they walk in their paths.

**24:14** Yet knowing their works, He delivered them over to darkness, and by night he shall be as a thief.

**24:15** And the eye of the adulterer watched for darkness, saying, 'No eye will notice me,' and he put a concealment upon his face.

**24:16** In darkness he dug through houses; by day they sealed themselves in; they did not recognize the light;

**24:17** for to them all together the morning is the shadow of death, for he will recognize the terror of the shadow of death.

**24:18** He is light upon the face of the water; may their portion upon the earth be cursed,

**24:19** and may their plants appear withered upon the earth; for they have snatched the sheaf of orphans.

**24:20** Then his sin was brought to remembrance, and like a mist of dew he vanished away; may what he has done be repaid to him, and may every unrighteous one be shattered like an incurable tree.

**24:21** For he did not do good to the barren woman, and he showed no mercy to the one without a spouse,

**24:22** and in wrath he overthrew the weak. Therefore, when he rises up, he shall have no assurance for his own life;

**24:23** when he grows weak with sickness, let him not hope to be healed, but he shall fall by disease.

**24:24** For his exaltation afflicted many; and he withered like a mallow in the burning heat, or like an ear of grain falling of its own accord from the stalk.

**24:25** And if it is not so, who is he who says that I speak falsehood, and will bring my words to nothing?"

## Translator Notes

- **24:5** — The Greek clause 'having gone out of their own order beyond me' (hyper emou exelthontes ten heauton taxin) is an obscure Septuagint rendering that diverges sharply from the Hebrew text.
- **24:17** — Greek tarachos is translated here as a Koine neuter accusative ('the terror'); if read as masculine nominative, the clause means 'for the terror of the shadow of death will recognize them.'
- **24:21** — Greek agynaion literally denotes an unmarried or wifeless person, used here where the Hebrew text refers to a widow.
