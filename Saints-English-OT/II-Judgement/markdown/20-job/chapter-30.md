## Text

**30:1** But now they have laughed me to scorn; the least of men now admonish me in turn, whose fathers I held in contempt, whom I did not deem worthy of the dogs of my flocks.

**30:2** Indeed, of what use to me is the strength of their hands? Upon them completeness has perished.

**30:3** Barren in want and famine, those who fled yesterday a waterless land, distress, and misery;

**30:4** those who go around gathering saltwort by what resounds, whose food was salt herbs—dishonored and despised, lacking every good thing.

**30:5** Thieves have risen up against me,

**30:6** whose dwellings were holes in the rocks;

**30:7** in the midst of resonant places they shall cry out, who made their dwelling beneath wild brushwood;

**30:8** sons of fools and a name of dishonorable men, and renown extinguished from the earth.

**30:9** And now I am their harp, and they hold me as a byword;

**30:10** they have abhorred me, standing far aloof, and they did not withhold spittle from my face.

**30:11** For He has opened His quiver and afflicted me, and He has cast away the bridle of my face.

**30:12** At the right hand of the shoot they rose up; they stretched out their foot, and they prepared against me the paths of their destruction.

**30:13** My paths have been worn away, for they have stripped off my robe;

**30:14** With His arrows He has struck me down; He has dealt with me as He wills; I am overwhelmed in pains.

**30:15** My pains turn back upon me; my hope has vanished like a wind, and like a cloud my deliverance.

**30:16** And now my soul shall be poured out upon me, and days of pains hold me fast;

**30:17** and by night my bones are confounded, and my sinews are loosened.

**30:18** With great strength He has taken hold of my robe; like the collar of my tunic He has clasped me round.

**30:19** And You have reckoned me equal to clay; in earth and ashes is my portion.

**30:20** I have cried out to You, and You do not hear me; and they stood and gazed closely upon me;

**30:21** they came upon me without mercy; with a mighty hand You have scourged me;

**30:22** and You have set me in pains, and cast me away from deliverance.

**30:23** For I know that death shall crush me, for the earth is a house for every mortal.

**30:24** Oh, that I were able to lay violent hands upon myself, or at least to implore another, and he would do this for me!

**30:25** Yet I wept over every helpless one, and I groaned when I saw a man in distress.

**30:26** And while I waited for good things, behold, days of evils came upon me instead.

**30:27** My inward parts have boiled over and shall not be silent; days of poverty have overtaken me.

**30:28** Groaning I have walked without a muzzle, and I have stood in the assembly crying out.

**30:29** I have become a brother of sirens, and a companion of ostriches.

**30:30** And my skin has been greatly darkened, and my bones from burning heat.

**30:31** And my harp has turned into grief, and my song into weeping for me.

## Translator Notes

- **30:4** — Greek epi echounti ("by what resounds") and halima/alima ("saltwort" / "salt herbs") preserve the Septuagint's distinctive interpretation of the underlying Hebrew plant imagery.
- **30:12** — Greek epi dexion blastou ("at the right hand of the shoot") renders the Hebrew term for a rabble or brood with blastos ("shoot" or "sprout").
- **30:24** — The Septuagint distinctively expresses a wish to lay violent hands upon himself (emauton cheirosasthai) or ask another to do so, differing markedly from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **30:28** — Greek aneu phimou ("without a muzzle") is a distinctive Septuagint reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "without the sun."
