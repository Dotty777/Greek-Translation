## Text

**39:1** "Do you know the time of birth of the wild mountain goats of the rock, and have you watched over the birth pangs of the deer?

**39:2** Have you numbered their full months until birth, and have you loosed their birth pangs?

**39:3** Have you reared their young ones free from fear? Will you send away their birth pangs?

**39:4** Their offspring will break away, they will multiply in increase; they will go forth, and will by no means return to them.

**39:5** And who is it who has set the wild donkey free, and who has loosed his bonds?

**39:6** I have appointed the wilderness as his habitation, and the salt land as his dwellings.

**39:7** Laughing at the thronging multitude of the city, and not hearing the rebuke of the tax collector,

**39:8** he will survey the mountains, his pasture, and he searches after every green thing.

**39:9** Will the unicorn be willing to serve you, or to lie down by your manger?

**39:10** Will you bind his yoke with straps, or will he draw your furrows in the plain?

**39:11** Do you trust in him because his strength is great, and will you leave your labors to him?

**39:12** Will you trust that he will return the seed to you, and bring in your threshing floor?

**39:13** The wing of those who delight—if the hasida and the nessa should conceive;

**39:14** for she will leave her eggs in the earth, and warm them upon the dust;

**39:15** and they have forgotten that a foot will scatter them, and the wild beasts of the field will trample them.

**39:16** She has dealt harshly with her own young, as though they were not her own; in vain she has labored without fear.

**39:17** For God has kept wisdom silent from her, and has not given her a share in understanding.

**39:18** In her season she will lift herself up on high; she will laugh at the horse and his rider.

**39:19** Or have you invested the horse with strength, and clothed his neck with terror?

**39:20** Have you clothed him with full armor, and the glory of his breast with boldness?

**39:21** Pawing in the plain, he exults, and goes forth into the plain in strength;

**39:22** meeting a king, he laughs, and will by no means turn back from iron.

**39:23** Upon him the bow and the sword exult,

**39:24** and in wrath he will make the earth vanish; and he will by no means believe until a trumpet sounds.

**39:25** When the trumpet sounds, he says, 'Aha!' And from afar he catches the scent of war, with leaping and shouting.

**39:26** Is it by your knowledge that the hawk stands poised, spreading out its wings motionless, gazing toward the south?

**39:27** And at your command does the eagle soar on high, and does the vulture, settling upon its nest, abide

**39:28** upon a crag of the rock and in a hidden place?

**39:29** Being there, it seeks its food; from afar its eyes keep watch;

**39:30** and its young wallow in blood, and wherever the dead are, immediately they are found."

## Translator Notes

- **39:7** — The Septuagint renders the Hebrew "driver" as "tax collector" (phorologos), portraying the wild donkey as untouched by civic tribute and reproach.
- **39:9** — Greek monokeros literally denotes a "one-horned beast" (traditionally rendered "unicorn"), the standard Septuagint translation of the Hebrew re'em (wild ox).
- **39:13** — The Greek text leaves two Hebrew terms transliterated as "hasida" and "nessa" (in Hebrew referring to the stork and plumage), producing an elliptical clause rendered faithfully as it stands in Greek.
- **39:20** — Where the Hebrew text compares the warhorse to a leaping locust, the Septuagint describes him as outfitted in "full armor" (panoplia) with boldness adorning his breast.
