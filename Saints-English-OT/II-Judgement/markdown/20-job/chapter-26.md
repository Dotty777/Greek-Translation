## Text

**26:1** Then Job answered, saying,

**26:2** "To whom are you attached, or whom are you about to help? Is it not the One who has great strength, and the One whose arm is mighty?

**26:3** To whom have you given counsel? Is it not the One who has all wisdom? Whom will you follow? Is it not the One who has the greatest power?

**26:4** To whom have you declared words? And whose breath is it that has come forth from you?

**26:5** Shall giants be brought to birth beneath the water and its neighbors?

**26:6** Hades is naked before Him, and there is no covering for Destruction.

**26:7** He stretches out the north upon nothing, and suspends the earth upon nothing.

**26:8** He binds up water in His clouds, and no cloud is torn open beneath it.

**26:9** He holds fast the face of the throne, spreading out His cloud over it.

**26:10** He drew a decree in a circle upon the face of the water, even to the end of light with darkness.

**26:11** The pillars of heaven flew apart, and were astounded at His rebuke.

**26:12** By strength He stilled the sea, and by knowledge the sea monster has been laid low.

**26:13** And the bars of heaven fear Him; and by a decree He slew the rebellious dragon.

**26:14** Behold, these are parts of His way, and we shall hear a mere drop of a word concerning Him; but the might of His thunder, who knows when He will make it?"

## Translator Notes

- **26:2** — In verses 2-3, the Septuagint turns the questions into pointed irony, asking whether Bildad is rushing to aid and counsel One who already possesses great strength, all wisdom, and the greatest power.
- **26:5** — Greek "gigantes" ("giants") renders the Hebrew Rephaim (shades of the dead), and "maiothesontai" literally means "be brought to birth" or "delivered as by a midwife."
- **26:10** — Greek "prostagma" ("decree" or "ordinance") translates a Hebrew noun that can mean either "boundary" or "statute."
- **26:13** — The Septuagint reads "bars of heaven" ("kleithra ouranou") and "rebellious dragon" ("drakonta apostaten"), where the Masoretic Text has "the heavens were made fair" and "the fleeing serpent."
