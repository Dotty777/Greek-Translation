## Text

**36:1** And Elihu continued further and said:

**36:2** "Wait for me a little longer, that I may teach you; for there is still speech within me.

**36:3** Taking up my knowledge from afar, in my works I will speak righteous things in truth,

**36:4** and not unrighteous words; you understand unrighteously.

**36:5** Yet know that the Lord will by no means reject the innocent; mighty in strength of heart,

**36:6** He will by no means keep the ungodly alive, and He will grant justice to the poor.

**36:7** He will not withdraw His eyes from the righteous, and with kings upon a throne He will seat them in victory, and they shall be exalted.

**36:8** And those who are bound in fetters shall be held fast in cords of poverty;

**36:9** and He will declare to them their works and their transgressions, because they will grow strong.

**36:10** But He will heed the righteous; and He said that they shall turn back from unrighteousness.

**36:11** If they hear and serve, they shall complete their days in good things, and their years in splendor.

**36:12** But the ungodly He does not preserve, because they were unwilling to know the Lord, and because, when admonished, they were disobedient.

**36:13** And the hypocrites in heart shall marshal wrath; they shall not cry out because He has bound them.

**36:14** Therefore let their soul die in youth, and their life be wounded by angels,

**36:15** because they afflicted the weak and the powerless; yet He will set forth the judgment of the meek.

**36:16** And an abyss has further deceived you from the mouth of an enemy, an outpouring beneath it, and your table went down full of fatness.

**36:17** Yet justice shall not be lacking from the righteous,

**36:18** but wrath shall be upon the ungodly, because of the ungodliness of the gifts which they received for unrighteous deeds.

**36:19** Do not let your mind willingly turn you aside from the supplication of the powerless who are in distress,

**36:20** and all those who exert strength. Do not draw out the night, for peoples to go up in their place;

**36:21** but take heed that you do not practice unrighteous things; for on this account you have chosen out of poverty.

**36:22** Behold, the Mighty One shall act mightily in His strength; for who is a sovereign like Him?

**36:23** And who is he who examines His works? Or who is he who has said, 'He has done unrighteous things'?

**36:24** Remember that His works are great, over which men have ruled;

**36:25** every man has seen within himself, as many mortals as are wounded.

**36:26** Behold, the Mighty One is great, and we shall not know Him; the number of His years is indeed boundless.

**36:27** And the drops of rain are numbered by Him, and they shall be poured out in rain into a cloud;

**36:28** The ancient things shall flow, and the clouds have cast a shadow over countless mortals. He has appointed a season for the beasts, and they know the order of their resting place. At all these things is your mind not astounded, nor is your heart parted from your body?

**36:29** And if anyone understands the outspreadings of a cloud, the evenness of His tabernacle,

**36:30** behold, the song shall stretch out upon Him, and He has covered the roots of the sea.

**36:31** For by these He shall judge peoples; He shall give food to the one who hears.

**36:32** Upon His hands He has covered the light, and He has given command concerning it against the one who meets it;

**36:33** He will declare His friend concerning Him: the Lord is a possession, even concerning unrighteousness."

## Translator Notes

- **36:14** — The Greek reads "wounded by angels," a distinctive Septuagint rendering where the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to ritual figures.
- **36:28** — Greek palaiomata ("ancient things," also in 37:18, 21) renders the Hebrew word for skies/clouds by etymological association with growing old; the second half of this verse is a Septuagint expansion absent from the Masoretic Text.
- **36:30** — The Greek reads "the song" (he ode), a distinctive Septuagint reading where the Hebrew text refers to His light or mist.
- **36:33** — The Greek clause is elliptical and reflects a different reading of the Hebrew consonants, yielding "His friend" and "a possession, even concerning unrighteousness."
