## Text

**11:1** Then Zophar the Minaean answered and said:

**11:2** "He who speaks many things shall also hear in reply; or does the man of fluent speech suppose he is righteous?

**11:3** Blessed is the short-lived man born of woman. Do not be abundant in words, for there is no one answering against you.

**11:4** For do not say, 'I am pure in my works, and blameless before Him.'

**11:5** Yet how might the Lord speak to you, and open His lips with you?

**11:6** Then He will declare to you the power of wisdom, that it shall be double that which pertains to you; and then you shall know that what has befallen you from the Lord is worthy of the sins you have committed.

**11:7** Will you find the trace of the Lord? Or have you arrived at the uttermost bounds that the Almighty has made?

**11:8** Heaven is high, and what will you do? Deeper are they than the things in Hades—what do you know?

**11:9** Or longer than the measure of the earth, or than the breadth of the sea.

**11:10** And if He overturns all things, who will say to Him, 'What have You done?'

**11:11** For He Himself knows the works of lawless men, and seeing wicked deeds, He will not overlook them.

**11:12** Yet man idly swims in words, and a mortal born of woman is like a donkey of the desert.

**11:13** For if you have made your heart pure, and stretch out your hands toward Him;

**11:14** if there is anything lawless in your hands, put it far away from you, and do not let unrighteousness lodge in your dwelling.

**11:15** For thus your face shall shine forth like pure water, and you shall put off filth and shall by no means be afraid;

**11:16** and you shall forget your trouble like a wave that has passed by, and you shall not be terrified;

**11:17** and your prayer shall be like the morning star, and out of noonday life shall rise for you;

**11:18** and you shall be confident that there is hope for you, and out of anxiety and care, peace shall appear to you.

**11:19** For you shall be at rest, and there shall be no one making war against you; and turning around, many shall make supplication to you.

**11:20** Yet salvation shall forsake them; for their hope is destruction, and the eyes of the ungodly shall melt away."

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — The Greek identifies Zophar as "the Minaean" (ho Meinaios), a distinct Septuagint gentilic compared to "the Naamathite" of the Hebrew text.
- **11:3** — The opening line "Blessed is the short-lived man born of woman" is distinctive to the Greek text, replacing the Hebrew rhetorical question about idle talk.
- **11:6** — The Greek states that what has befallen Job from the Lord is "worthy of the sins you have committed," contrasting with the Hebrew statement that God exacts less than Job's iniquity deserves.
