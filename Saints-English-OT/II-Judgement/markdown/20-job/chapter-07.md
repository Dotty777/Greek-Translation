## Text

**7:1** "Is not the life of man upon the earth a trial, and his existence like that of a day laborer?

**7:2** Or like a servant who fears his master and has found shade, or like a hired worker awaiting his wages?

**7:3** So I also have endured empty months, and nights of pain have been given to me.

**7:4** When I lie down, I say, 'When will it be day?' And when I rise up, again, 'When will it be evening?' And I am filled with pains from evening until morning.

**7:5** And my body is defiled with the decay of worms, and I dissolve clods of earth as I scrape away the discharge.

**7:6** And my life is swifter than speech, and it has perished in empty hope.

**7:7** Remember therefore that my life is a breath, and my eye shall no more return to see good.

**7:8** The eye of him who sees me shall look upon me no more; Your eyes are upon me, and I am no more,

**7:9** like a cloud cleared away from the sky. For if a man goes down to Hades, he shall never again come up,

**7:10** nor shall he ever return to his own house, nor shall his place ever recognize him again.

**7:11** Therefore neither will I restrain my mouth; I will speak while in distress; being hard pressed, I will open the bitterness of my soul.

**7:12** Am I a sea, or a dragon, that You have set a watch over me?

**7:13** I said, 'My bed will comfort me, and I will hold private discourse with myself upon my couch;'

**7:14** You terrify me with dreams, and dismay me through visions.

**7:15** You will separate my soul from my spirit, and my bones from death.

**7:16** For I shall not live forever, that I should be patient; depart from me, for my life is empty.

**7:17** For what is man, that You have magnified him? Or that You set Your mind upon him?

**7:18** Or that You will visit him until the morning, and judge him until rest?

**7:19** How long will You not leave me alone, nor let me go, until I swallow my spittle in pain?

**7:20** If I have sinned, what shall I be able to do, O You who know the mind of men? Why have You set me as Your accuser, and why am I a burden upon You?

**7:21** And why have You not made a forgetting of my iniquity and a cleansing of my sin? For now I shall depart into the earth, and rising at dawn, I am no more."

## Translator Notes

- **7:5** — The Greek depicts Job dissolving clods of earth while scraping away watery discharge (ichor) from his sores.
- **7:6** — The Greek reads "swifter than speech" (lalias), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "swifter than a weaver's shuttle."
- **7:15** — Whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text expresses Job's preference for strangling and death over his bones, the Greek addresses God directly: "You will separate my soul from my spirit, and my bones from death."
- **7:18** — The Greek renders the Hebrew idiom for "every moment" as eis anapausin ("until rest"), connecting the Hebrew root with resting.
- **7:20** — Greek katenteukten means "accuser" or "opponent" (rather than "target"), and "a burden upon You" preserves a well-known Septuagint reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "a burden to myself."
