## Text

**23:1** Then Job answered and said:

**23:2** "Indeed I know that argument is out of my hand, and His hand has become heavy upon my groaning.

**23:3** And who indeed could know that I might find Him, and come to the end?

**23:4** Then I would speak my own case, and my mouth would fill with arguments;

**23:5** and I would know the remedies which He will speak to me, and perceive what things He will declare to me.

**23:6** And in great strength He will come upon me; then He will not deal with me with a threat.

**23:7** For truth and reproof are from Him, and may He bring forth my judgment to the end.

**23:8** For I shall go to the first things, and I am no more; and as for the last things, what do I know?

**23:9** When He worked on the left, I did not lay hold of Him; He will turn to the right, and I shall not see Him.

**23:10** For He already knows my way, and He has tested me as gold.

**23:11** And I shall go forth in His commandments, for I have kept His ways, and I shall never turn aside; from His commandments,

**23:12** I shall never depart, and in my bosom I have hidden His words.

**23:13** And if He Himself has judged thus, who is he who contradicts Him? For whatever He Himself willed, He also did.

**23:15** Therefore I have been alarmed at Him; and being admonished, I took thought of Him. For this reason I am dismayed before His presence; I shall consider, and I shall be terrified because of Him.

**23:16** And the Lord has made my heart faint, and the Almighty has dismayed me.

**23:17** For I did not know that darkness would come upon me, and thick darkness has covered before my face."

## Translator Notes

- **23:5** — The Greek reads iamata ('remedies' or 'healings'), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ('words') and possibly reflecting an early Greek shift from rhemata ('words').
- **23:8** — The Greek has the first-person verb 'I am no more' (ouk eti eimi), rather than the third-person 'He is not there' of the Hebrew text.
- **23:15** — Verse 14 of the Hebrew numbering is absent from the Greek text, and verse 15 preserves a doubled rendering of Job's dismay before God.
