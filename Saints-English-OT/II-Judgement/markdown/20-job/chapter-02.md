## Text

**2:1** Now it came to pass when this day arrived that the angels of God came to stand before the Lord, and the devil came in the midst of them to stand before the Lord.

**2:2** And the Lord said to the devil, "From where do you come?" Then the devil said before the Lord, "Having journeyed through the realm under heaven and walked throughout the whole of it, I am here."

**2:3** And the Lord said to the devil, "Have you then given heed to My servant Job? For there is none like him among those upon the earth—a guileless, true, blameless, God-fearing man, abstaining from all evil. Still he holds fast to his innocence, though you spoke to destroy his possessions for nothing."

**2:4** And answering, the devil said to the Lord, "Skin for skin! All that a man possesses he will pay in full for his life.

**2:5** Nevertheless, send forth Your hand and touch his bones and his flesh—truly he will bless You to Your face!"

**2:6** And the Lord said to the devil, "Behold, I deliver him over to you; only preserve his life."

**2:7** So the devil went out from the Lord and struck Job with a grievous sore from his feet to his head.

**2:8** And he took a potsherd to scrape off the discharge, and he sat upon the dunghill outside the city.

**2:9** Now when much time had passed, his wife said to him, "How long will you hold out, saying, 'Behold, I wait yet a little while, awaiting the hope of my deliverance'? For behold, your memorial has vanished from the earth—sons and daughters, the birth pangs and labors of my womb, for whom I toiled in vain with hardships. And you yourself sit in the corruption of worms, passing the night in the open air, while I wander about as a hired servant from place to place and from house to house, watching for when the sun will set, that I may rest from my toils and from the pains that now grip me. Rather, speak some word against the Lord, and die!"

**2:10** But looking intently at her, he said to her, "You have spoken like one of the foolish women. If we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, shall we not endure evil things?" In all these things that befell him, Job did not sin in anything with his lips before God.

**2:11** Now when his three friends heard of all the evils that had come upon him, each came to him from his own land: Eliphaz, king of the Temanites; Bildad, ruler of the Shuhites; and Zophar, king of the Minaeans. And they came to him with one accord to comfort and to visit him.

**2:12** And when they saw him from afar, they did not recognize him; and crying out with a loud voice, they wept, each tearing his own robe and sprinkling earth upon himself.

**2:13** They sat beside him seven days and seven nights, and none of them spoke, for they saw that his affliction was grievous and exceedingly great.

## Translator Notes

- **2:8** — The Septuagint specifies that Job sat "upon the dunghill outside the city" and uses ἰχώρ ("discharge") for the fluid scraped from his sores.
- **2:9** — The Septuagint includes a substantial expansion of Job's wife's lament, recounting her grief over their lost children and her wandering as a hired servant before urging Job to speak against the Lord and die.
- **2:11** — The Septuagint assigns royal titles ("king," "ruler," "king") to Job's three friends and identifies Zophar as king of the "Minaeans" (Μειναίων), where the Hebrew text reads "the Naamathite."
