## Text

**27:1** And Job continued his discourse further, saying,

**27:2** "As God lives, who has judged me thus, and the Almighty, who has embittered my soul,

**27:3** surely while my breath is still in me, and the divine spirit remains in my nostrils,

**27:4** my lips shall not speak lawless things, nor shall my soul meditate on unrighteous things.

**27:5** Far be it from me to declare you righteous until I die, for I will not put away my innocence;

**27:6** holding fast to righteousness, I will by no means abandon it, for I am not conscious within myself of having done anything wrong.

**27:7** Nevertheless, may my enemies be like the overthrow of the ungodly, and those who rise up against me like the destruction of the lawless.

**27:8** For what hope has an ungodly man, because he holds on? Trusting in the Lord, shall he indeed be saved?

**27:9** Or will God hear his supplication? Or when distress comes upon him,

**27:10** does he have any boldness before Him? Or when he calls upon Him, will He hear him?

**27:11** Now then, I will declare to you what is in the hand of the Lord; what is with the Almighty I will not falsify.

**27:12** Behold, you all know that you heap empty things upon empty things.

**27:13** This is the portion of an ungodly man from the Lord, and the possession of tyrants shall come upon them from the Almighty.

**27:14** If their sons become many, they shall be for slaughter; and even if they grow to manhood, they shall beg;

**27:15** and no one shall have mercy on their widows.

**27:16** If he gathers silver like earth, and prepares gold like clay,

**27:17** all these things the righteous shall acquire, and true men shall possess his wealth.

**27:18** And his house has turned out like moths and like a spider.

**27:19** A rich man lies down to sleep and shall not do so again; he opens his eyes, and he is no more.

**27:20** Pains have met him like water, and by night thick darkness has snatched him away.

**27:21** A scorching wind shall take him up, and he shall depart, and it shall winnow him out of his place.

**27:22** And it shall hurl him down and will not spare; from its hand he shall flee headlong.

**27:23** They shall clap their hands at him, and hiss him out of his place."

## Translator Notes

- **27:6** — Greek "ou gar synoida emauto atopa praxas" ("I am not conscious within myself of having done anything wrong") uses an idiom of conscience where the Masoretic Text speaks of Job's heart not reproaching any of his days.
- **27:8** — The Septuagint asks whether the ungodly man will be saved by trusting in the Lord, diverging from the Masoretic Text's reference to God cutting off and taking away his life.
- **27:15** — The Septuagint lacks the first half of the Masoretic verse ("those who survive him are buried by pestilence") and reads "no one shall have mercy on their widows" instead of "his widows do not weep."
- **27:23** — The Greek pairs singular future verbs ("krotesei" and "syriei") with the plural phrase "their hands" ("cheiras auton"), reflecting an impersonal or collective subject rendered here as "They."
