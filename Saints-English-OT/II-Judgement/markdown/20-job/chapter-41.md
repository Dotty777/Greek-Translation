## Text

**41:1** "Have you not even marveled at the things spoken? And do you not fear that he is prepared for Me?

**41:2** Do you not fear that he is prepared for Me? For who is he who has withstood Me?

**41:3** For who is he who has withstood Me? Or who will withstand Me and endure? If all that is under heaven is Mine,

**41:4** I will not be silent because of him, and as for a word of power, he will pity his equal.

**41:5** Who will uncover the face of his garment? And who could enter into the fold of his breastplate?

**41:6** Who will open the gates of his face? Around his teeth is terror.

**41:7** His inward parts are shields of bronze, and his bond is like an emery stone.

**41:8** One is joined fast to another, and wind shall by no means pass through him.

**41:9** Each shall cleave to his brother; they hold fast together and shall by no means be torn apart.

**41:10** In his sneezing a radiance shines forth, and his eyes are the appearance of the morning star.

**41:11** Out of his mouth go forth burning torches, and braziers of fire are hurled abroad.

**41:12** Out of his nostrils goes forth the smoke of a furnace burning with a fire of coals.

**41:13** His breath is coals, and a flame goes forth out of his mouth.

**41:14** In his neck strength abides, and destruction runs before him.

**41:15** The flesh of his body is joined fast together; one pours down upon him, yet he shall not be shaken.

**41:16** His heart is hardened like a stone, and stands firm like an unyielding anvil.

**41:17** And when he turns, terror comes upon the four-footed beasts leaping upon the earth.

**41:18** If spears meet him, they shall accomplish nothing—neither lance nor breastplate.

**41:19** For he regards iron as chaff, and bronze as rotten wood.

**41:20** A bow of bronze shall by no means wound him; he counts a stone-thrower as grass,

**41:21** hammers are reckoned as stubble, and he laughs at the shaking of a firebrand.

**41:22** His bed is sharp spikes, and all the gold of the sea beneath him is like untold mire.

**41:23** He makes the abyss boil like a cauldron; he regards the sea as an ointment pot,

**41:24** and the Tartarus of the abyss as a captive; he has reckoned the abyss as a walkway.

**41:25** There is nothing upon the earth like him, made to be mocked by My angels.

**41:26** He beholds every lofty thing, and he is king over all things in the waters."

## Translator Notes

- **41:1** — Verses 1 through 3 preserve a textual overlap characteristic of the Greek manuscript tradition, repeating the rhetorical questions "Do you not fear that he is prepared for Me?" and "For who is he who has withstood Me?"
- **41:4** — The Greek syntax is compressed, placing "a word of power" in the accusative as a complement of respect or cause before "he will pity his equal."
- **41:22** — Greek "gold of the sea" (chrysos thalasses) reflects a distinct reading of the Hebrew word for a sharp threshing sledge, which shares the same consonants as a poetic term for gold.
- **41:24** — Where the Masoretic Text pictures the deep as white-haired, the Greek portrays the dragon treating "the Tartarus of the abyss as a captive" and reckoning the abyss "as a walkway."
