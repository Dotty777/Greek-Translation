## Text

**19:1** And the king was deeply troubled, and went up to the upper chamber of the gate and wept. And thus he spoke as he went: "My son, my son Absalom! Who would grant my death instead of you? I instead of you, Absalom, my son, my son!"

**19:2** And it was reported to Joab, saying, "Behold, the king is weeping and mourning over Absalom."

**19:3** And the deliverance on that day became mourning for all the people, because the people heard on that day, saying, "The king is grieved over his son."

**19:4** And the people stole away on that day to enter into the city, just as people who are ashamed steal away when they flee in battle.

**19:5** And the king hid his face; and the king cried out with a loud voice, saying, "My son Absalom, Absalom my son!"

**19:6** And Joab came to the king into the house and said, "You have put to shame today the countenance of all your servants who delivered you today, and the life of your sons and of your daughters, and the life of your wives and of your concubines,

**19:7** in loving those who hate you and hating those who love you; and you have declared today that your commanders and servants are nothing. For I know today that if Absalom were alive and all of us today were dead, then it would have been right in your eyes.

**19:8** Now therefore arise, go out, and speak to the heart of your servants; for I have sworn by the Lord that if you do not go out today, not a single man shall lodge with you this night. And recognize for yourself that this will be worse for you than all the evil that has come upon you from your youth until now."

**19:9** Then the king arose and sat in the gate. And all the people reported, saying, "Behold, the king is sitting in the gate." So all the people came before the king. Now Israel had fled, every man to his tents.

**19:10** And all the people were disputing among all the tribes of Israel, saying, "King David rescued us from the hand of all our enemies, and he himself delivered us from the hand of the foreigners; and now he has fled from the land and from his kingdom and from Absalom,

**19:11** whom we anointed over us—he has died in battle. Now therefore, why are you silent about returning to the king?" And the word of all Israel came to the king.

**19:12** And King David sent to Zadok and to Abiathar the priests, saying, "Speak to the elders of Judah, saying, 'Why are you the last to return to the king to his house? And the word of all Israel has come to the king.

**19:13** You are my brethren, you are my bones and my flesh; why then are you the last to bring back the king to his house?'

**19:14** And to Amasa you shall say, 'Are you not my bone and my flesh? Now may God do so to me, and more also, if you shall not be commander of the army before me all the days in place of Joab.'"

**19:15** And he inclined the heart of every man of Judah, even to a single man; and they sent to the king, saying, "Return, you and all your servants."

**19:16** So the king returned and came to the Jordan. And the men of Judah came to Gilgal to go to meet the king, to bring the king across the Jordan.

**19:17** And Shimei the son of Gera, the son of the Jeminite, from Bahurim, hastened and came down with the men of Judah to meet King David,

**19:18** and a thousand men of Benjamin were with him, and Ziba the servant of the house of Saul, and his fifteen sons with him and his twenty servants with him; and they made straight across the Jordan before the king,

**19:19** and they performed the service of bringing the king across; and the ferry crossed over to rouse the household of the king and to do what was right in his eyes. And Shimei the son of Gera fell on his face before the king as he was crossing the Jordan,

**19:20** and said to the king, "Do not let my lord reckon iniquity, and do not remember all the wrong that your servant committed on the day when my lord went out from Jerusalem, that the king should take it to his heart;

**19:21** for your servant knows that I have sinned, and behold, I have come today first of all the house of Joseph to go down to meet my lord the king."

**19:22** And Abishai the son of Zeruiah answered and said, "Shall Shimei not be put to death for this, because he cursed the anointed of the Lord?"

**19:23** And David said, "What have I to do with you, sons of Zeruiah, that you should become an adversary to me today? Today not a single man of Israel shall be put to death; for do I not know that today I reign as king over Israel?"

**19:24** And the king said to Shimei, "You shall surely not die." And the king swore to him.

**19:25** And Mephibosheth the son of the son of Saul came down to meet the king; and he had not tended his feet, nor pared his nails, nor trimmed his mustache, and had not washed his garments from the day the king departed until the day he arrived in peace.

**19:26** And it came to pass, when he came into Jerusalem to meet the king, that the king said to him, "Why did you not go with me, Mephibosheth?"

**19:27** And Mephibosheth said to him, "My lord the king, my servant deceived me; for your servant said to him, 'Saddle the donkey for me, that I may mount it and go with the king,' because your servant is lame.

**19:28** And he dealt craftily against your servant before my lord the king; yet my lord the king has done what is good openly, like an angel of God. Therefore do what is good in your eyes.

**19:29** For all the house of my father were nothing but men worthy of death to my lord the king, yet you set your servant among those who eat at your table. What just claim, therefore, do I still have, even to cry out any further to the king?"

**19:30** And the king said to him, "Why do you still speak your words? I have said, 'You and Ziba shall divide the field.'"

**19:31** And Mephibosheth said to the king, "Indeed, let him take it all, now that my lord the king has come in peace to his own house."

**19:32** And Barzillai the Gileadite came down from Rogelim and crossed the Jordan with the king, to escort him over the Jordan.

**19:33** Now Barzillai was an exceedingly aged man, eighty years old; and he had sustained the king while he dwelt in Mahanaim, for he was a very great man.

**19:34** And the king said to Barzillai, "Cross over with me, and I will sustain your old age with me in Jerusalem."

**19:35** And Barzillai said to the king, "How many are the days of the years of my life, that I should go up with the king to Jerusalem?

**19:36** I am eighty years old today. Can I truly discern between good and bad, even to what is evil? Can your servant still taste what I eat or what I drink? Or can I still hear the voice of singing men and singing women? Why then should your servant still be a burden upon my lord the king?

**19:37** Your servant will go just a little way across the Jordan with the king; and why should the king repay me with such a reward?

**19:38** Let your servant remain, I pray, and I shall die in my own city beside the grave of my father and my mother. But behold, your servant Chimham will cross over with my lord the king; and do for him what is good in your eyes."

**19:39** And the king said, "Let Chimham cross over with me, and I will do for him what is good in your eyes; and all that you choose of me, I will do for you."

**19:40** And all the people crossed over the Jordan, and the king crossed over. And the king kissed Barzillai and blessed him, and he returned to his own place.

**19:41** And the king crossed over to Gilgal, and Chimham crossed over with him; and all the people of Judah were crossing over with the king, and also half the people of Israel.

**19:42** And behold, all the men of Israel came to the king and said to the king, "Why have our brethren, the men of Judah, stolen you away, and brought the king and his household across the Jordan, and all the men of David with him?"

**19:43** And all the men of Judah answered the men of Israel and said, "Because the king is near of kin to me. Why then are you so angry over this matter? Have we eaten anything at all from the king, or has he given a gift, or bestowed a portion upon us?"

**19:44** And the men of Israel answered the men of Judah and said, "I have ten shares in the king, and I am the firstborn rather than you, and indeed I have more in David than you. Why then have you insulted me in this, and why was my word not counted first for me before Judah, to bring back my king?" Yet the words of the men of Judah were harsher than the words of the men of Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **19:1** — In the Septuagint (as in the Hebrew versification), David's lament over Absalom opens chapter nineteen rather than closing chapter eighteen, and the Greek places "my son, my son" twice before "Absalom."
- **19:10** — The Greek text regularly designates the Philistines as "foreigners" in First and Second Kingdoms, and here adds "and from his kingdom" to the account of David's flight.
- **19:25** — The Septuagint explicitly identifies Mephibosheth as "the son of the son of Saul" (Saul's grandson) and includes the additional detail that he had not pared his nails.
- **19:44** — The Septuagint includes the claim "and I am the firstborn rather than you," reflecting a longer reading absent from the Masoretic Text.
