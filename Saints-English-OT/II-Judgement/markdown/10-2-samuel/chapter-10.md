## Text

**10:1** Now it came to pass after this that the king of the sons of Ammon died, and Hanun his son reigned in his place.

**10:2** And David said, "I will show mercy to Hanun the son of Nahash, in the same manner as his father showed mercy to me." So David sent by the hand of his servants to comfort him concerning his father. And the servants of David came into the land of the sons of Ammon.

**10:3** And the princes of the sons of Ammon said to Hanun their lord, "Is it because David honors your father in your sight that he has sent comforters to you? Rather, has David not sent his servants to you so that they may search the city, spy it out, and inspect it?"

**10:4** So Hanun took the servants of David, and shaved their beards, and cut off their garments in the middle as far as their hips, and sent them away.

**10:5** And they reported to David concerning the men, and he sent to meet them, for the men were greatly dishonored. And the king said, "Remain in Jericho until your beards have grown, and then you shall return."

**10:6** And the sons of Ammon saw that the people of David had been put to shame. So the sons of Ammon sent and hired Syria and Rehob, twenty thousand foot soldiers, and the king of Amalek, one thousand men, and Istob, twelve thousand men.

**10:7** And David heard of it, and he sent Joab and all the army, the mighty men.

**10:8** And the sons of Ammon came out and drew up in battle array at the entrance of the gate, while those of Syria of Zobah, and Rehob, and Istob, and Amalek were by themselves in the field.

**10:9** And Joab saw that the front of the battle was against him both before him and behind him. So he chose from all the young men of Israel, and they drew up in battle array against Syria.

**10:10** And the rest of the people he delivered into the hand of Abishai his brother, and they drew up in battle array against the sons of Ammon.

**10:11** And he said, "If Syria becomes too strong for me, then you shall be for my deliverance; and if the sons of Ammon become too strong for you, then we will come to deliver you.

**10:12** Be courageous, and let us be strong for our people and for the cities of our God; and the Lord will do what is good in His sight."

**10:13** So Joab and his people with him drew near to battle against Syria, and they fled from before him.

**10:14** And the sons of Ammon saw that Syria had fled, and they fled from before Abishai and entered into the city. Then Joab turned back from the sons of Ammon, and they came to Jerusalem.

**10:15** And Syria saw that they had been defeated before Israel, and they gathered themselves together.

**10:16** And Hadarezer sent and gathered Syria from beyond the river Chalamak, and they came to Helam; and Shobach, the commander of the army of Hadarezer, went before them.

**10:17** And it was reported to David, and he gathered all Israel, and crossed over the Jordan, and they came to Helam. And David drew up in battle array against Syria, and they fought with him.

**10:18** And Syria fled from before Israel; and David destroyed from Syria seven hundred chariots and forty thousand horsemen, and he struck down Shobach the commander of his army, and he died there.

**10:19** And all the kings who were servants of Hadarezer saw that they had been defeated before Israel, and they went over to Israel and served them. And Syria was afraid to deliver the sons of Ammon anymore.

## Translator Notes

- **10:3** — The Greek uses three verbs of reconnaissance ("search... spy it out... inspect it"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "overthrow it" for the third verb.
- **10:4** — The Greek states simply that Hanun shaved "their beards," without the word "half" found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **10:6** — The Greek preserves several distinctive readings here: "the people of David had been put to shame" (MT: "had become odious to David"), "the king of Amalek" (MT: "king of Maacah"), and the proper name "Istob" (transliterating Hebrew Ish-tob, "men of Tob").
- **10:16** — The Greek names the river "Chalamak" (where the Hebrew Masoretic Text simply reads "the River," referring to the Euphrates) and uses the spelling "Hadarezer" rather than "Hadadezer."
