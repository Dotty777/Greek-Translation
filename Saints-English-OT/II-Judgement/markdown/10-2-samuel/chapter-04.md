## Text

**4:1** Now Mephibosheth the son of Saul heard that Abner had died in Hebron, and his hands became feeble, and all the men of Israel grew faint.

**4:2** And Mephibosheth the son of Saul had two men who were leaders of raiding bands: the name of the one was Baanah, and the name of the second was Rechab, the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, of the sons of Benjamin; for Beeroth was reckoned to the sons of Benjamin.

**4:3** And the Beerothites fled to Gittaim, and have been sojourning there to this day.

**4:4** Now Jonathan the son of Saul had a son who was stricken in his feet. He was five years old when the report of Saul and Jonathan his son came from Israel; and his nurse took him up and fled. And it came to pass, as he hastened to flee, that he fell and became lame; and his name was Mephibosheth.

**4:5** Then the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, Rechab and Baanah, went and came in the heat of the day to the house of Mephibosheth, while he was sleeping on his bed at midday.

**4:6** And behold, the doorkeeper of the house was cleaning wheat, and she grew drowsy and fell asleep; so Rechab and Baanah the brothers slipped past unnoticed,

**4:7** and they entered the house, while Mephibosheth was sleeping on his bed in his bedchamber; and they struck him and put him to death, and removed his head. And they took his head and went by the way toward the west all night long.

**4:8** And they brought the head of Mephibosheth to David in Hebron, and said to the king, "Behold, the head of Mephibosheth, the son of Saul your enemy, who sought your life! And the Lord has given to the lord king vengeance on his enemies as on this day, from Saul your enemy and from his seed."

**4:9** But David answered Rechab and Baanah his brother, the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, and said to them, "As the Lord lives, who has redeemed my soul from all affliction, as for the one who told me that Saul was dead,

**4:10** and he was as one bringing good tidings before me, I seized him and slew him in Ziklag, to whom I ought to have given a reward for good tidings.

**4:11** And now wicked men have slain a righteous man in his own house upon his bed! Now therefore I will require his blood from your hand, and I will utterly destroy you from the earth."

**4:12** Then David commanded his young men, and they slew them, and cut off their hands and their feet, and hanged them by the spring in Hebron. And they buried the head of Mephibosheth in the tomb of Abner the son of Ner.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — The Greek reads "from Israel" (where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "from Jezreel") and uses a masculine noun (tithenos) for Mephibosheth's nurse or attendant.
- **4:6** — The Greek preserves the narrative of the doorkeeper who grew drowsy while cleaning wheat so that the brothers slipped past unnoticed, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text describes them entering as though to fetch wheat.
- **4:7** — The Greek reads "the way toward the west," interpreting the Hebrew geographic term for the Jordan Valley (ha-arabah, "the Arabah") as "the west."
