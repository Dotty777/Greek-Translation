## Text

**16:1** Now David had passed a little beyond Ros, and behold, Ziba the servant of Mephibosheth came to meet him with a pair of saddled donkeys, and upon them were two hundred loaves of bread, one hundred clusters of raisins, one hundred dates, and a skin of wine.

**16:2** And the king said to Ziba, "What are these for?" And Ziba said, "The beasts of burden are for the household of the king to ride on, and the loaves of bread and the dates are for the young men to eat, and the wine is for those who grow faint in the wilderness to drink."

**16:3** And the king said, "And where is the son of your master?" And Ziba said to the king, "Behold, he remains in Jerusalem, for he said, 'Today the house of Israel will restore to me the kingdom of my father.'"

**16:4** Then the king said to Ziba, "Behold, all that belongs to Mephibosheth is yours." And Ziba said, "I bow down; may I find favor in your eyes, my lord the king."

**16:5** Now King David came as far as Bahurim; and behold, from there a man came out from the kindred of the house of Saul, and his name was Shimei the son of Gera; he came forth, coming out and cursing,

**16:6** and throwing stones at David and at all the servants of King David; and all the people and all the mighty men were on his right hand and on the left hand of the king.

**16:7** And thus Shimei spoke while he cursed: "Get out, get out, you man of blood and lawless man!

**16:8** The Lord has brought back upon you all the blood of the house of Saul, because you reigned in his place, and the Lord has delivered the kingdom into the hand of Absalom your son; and behold, you are in your calamity, because you are a man of blood."

**16:9** Then Abishai the son of Zeruiah said to the king, "Why should this dead dog curse my lord the king? Let me cross over now and take off his head."

**16:10** And the king said, "What have I to do with you, you sons of Zeruiah? Let him alone, and so let him curse, because the Lord has told him to curse David; and who shall say, 'Why have you done thus?'"

**16:11** And David said to Abishai and to all his servants, "Behold, my own son who came forth from my body seeks my life; and how much more now this Benjamite! Let him alone to curse, for the Lord has spoken to him.

**16:12** Perhaps the Lord will look upon my affliction, and will return good to me in place of his cursing this day."

**16:13** So David and his men went along the road; and Shimei walked along the side of the mountain opposite him, going along and cursing, and throwing stones from beside him and flinging dust.

**16:14** And the king and all his people arrived weary, and they refreshed themselves there.

**16:15** Now Absalom and all the men of Israel entered into Jerusalem, and Ahithophel was with him.

**16:16** And it came to pass, when Hushai the Archite, David's friend, came to Absalom, that Hushai said to Absalom, "Long live the king!"

**16:17** And Absalom said to Hushai, "Is this your kindness toward your friend? Why did you not depart with your friend?"

**16:18** And Hushai said to Absalom, "No, but following the one whom the Lord and this people and all the men of Israel have chosen, his I will be, and with him I will abide.

**16:19** And secondly, whom shall I serve? Should it not be before his son? Just as I served before your father, so shall I be before you."

**16:20** Then Absalom said to Ahithophel, "Give counsel among yourselves as to what we should do."

**16:21** And Ahithophel said to Absalom, "Go in to your father's concubines, whom he left to keep the house; and all Israel will hear that you have disgraced your father, and the hands of all who are with you will be strengthened."

**16:22** So they pitched the tent for Absalom upon the housetop, and Absalom went in to his father's concubines in the sight of all Israel.

**16:23** Now the counsel of Ahithophel, which he gave in those former days, was as when one would inquire at the word of God; so was all the counsel of Ahithophel, both to David and to Absalom.

## Translator Notes

- **16:1** — The Greek transliterates the Hebrew word for the summit (ha-rosh) as the place name Ros, specifies dates (phoinikes) where the Hebrew has summer fruits, and uses the loanword nebel for the wine skin.
- **16:16** — The Greek records Hushai's acclamation once, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text repeats it twice.
- **16:23** — The Greek reads 'in those former days,' whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'in those days.'
