## Text

**11:1** Now it came to pass at the return of the year, at the time when kings go forth, that David sent Joab and his servants with him, and all Israel; and they destroyed the sons of Ammon and laid siege to Rabbah. But David remained in Jerusalem.

**11:2** And it came to pass toward evening that David arose from his bed and walked upon the roof of the king's house; and from the roof he saw a woman bathing, and the woman was exceedingly beautiful in appearance.

**11:3** So David sent and inquired about the woman, and one said, "Is this not Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliab, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?"

**11:4** Then David sent messengers and took her; and he went in to her and lay with her, and she was purifying herself from her uncleanness. And she returned to her house.

**11:5** And the woman conceived; so she sent and told David, and said, "I am with child."

**11:6** Then David sent to Joab, saying, "Send me Uriah the Hittite." And Joab sent Uriah to David.

**11:7** And Uriah arrived and came in to him; and David inquired concerning the welfare of Joab, and the welfare of the people, and the welfare of the war.

**11:8** And David said to Uriah, "Go down to your house and wash your feet." So Uriah went out from the king's house, and a portion from the king went out after him.

**11:9** But Uriah slept at the king's door with the servants of his lord, and did not go down to his house.

**11:10** And they reported to David, saying, "Uriah did not go down to his house." So David said to Uriah, "Do you not come from a journey? Why is it that you did not go down to your house?"

**11:11** And Uriah said to David, "The ark and Israel and Judah dwell in tents, and my lord Joab and the servants of my lord are encamped upon the face of the field. Shall I then go into my house to eat and to drink, and to lie with my wife? How? As your soul lives, I will not do this thing."

**11:12** Then David said to Uriah, "Remain here today also, and tomorrow I will send you away." So Uriah remained in Jerusalem on that day and the next.

**11:13** And David invited him, and he ate and drank before him; and he made him drunk. And in the evening he went out to lie upon his bed with the servants of his lord, and he did not go down to his house.

**11:14** And it came to pass in the morning that David wrote a letter to Joab, and sent it by the hand of Uriah.

**11:15** And he wrote in the letter, saying, "Set Uriah opposite the fiercest battle, and you shall turn back from behind him, and he shall be struck down and die."

**11:16** And it came to pass, as Joab was keeping watch over the city, that he placed Uriah in the place where he knew that men of valor were.

**11:17** And the men of the city came out and fought with Joab; and some of the people, of the servants of David, fell and died, and Uriah the Hittite also.

**11:18** Then Joab sent and reported to King David all the matters of the war, to speak them to the king.

**11:19** And he commanded the messenger, saying, "When you have finished speaking all the matters of the war to the king,

**11:20** then it shall be, if the wrath of the king rises and he says to you, 'Why is it that you drew near to the city to fight? Did you not know that they would shoot arrows from atop the wall?

**11:21** Who struck down Abimelech the son of Jeroboam the son of Ner? Did not a woman cast a piece of a millstone upon him from atop the wall, so that he died in Thamasi? Why did you draw near to the wall?' then you shall say, 'Your servant Uriah the Hittite has died also.'"

**11:22** So the messenger of Joab went to the king in Jerusalem; and he came and reported to David all that Joab had told him, all the matters of the war. And David was angry with Joab, and said to the messenger, "Why did you draw near to the city to fight? Did you not know that you would be struck from the wall? Who struck down Abimelech the son of Jeroboam? Did not a woman cast a piece of a millstone upon him from the wall, so that he died in Thamasi? Why did you draw near to the wall?"

**11:23** And the messenger said to David, "Because the men prevailed against us and came out against us into the field, and we pressed upon them as far as the entrance of the gate;

**11:24** and the archers shot at your servants from atop the wall, and some of the king's servants died; and your servant Uriah the Hittite has died also."

**11:25** Then David said to the messenger, "Thus you shall say to Joab: 'Do not let this matter be evil in your sight, for the sword devours now in this way and now in that. Strengthen your battle against the city and pull it down.' And strengthen him."

**11:26** And the wife of Uriah heard that Uriah her husband had died, and she mourned for her husband.

**11:27** And when the mourning had passed, David sent and brought her into his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. But the thing that David had done appeared evil in the sight of the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — The Greek identifies Bathsheba's father as "Eliab," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Eliam."
- **11:4** — The Greek makes David the subject who "went in to her," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "she came to him."
- **11:21** — The Greek has the distinctive names "Jeroboam the son of Ner" (MT: "Jerubbesheth," another name for Jerubbaal/Gideon) and "Thamasi" (MT: "Thebez").
- **11:22** — The Greek contains a substantial expansion explicitly recounting David's angry rebuke to Joab's messenger, fulfilling Joab's prediction in verses 20-21; this portion is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
