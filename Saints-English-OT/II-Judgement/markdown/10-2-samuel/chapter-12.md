## Text

**12:1** And the Lord sent Nathan the prophet to David; and he came to him and said to him, "There were two men in one city, one rich and one poor.

**12:2** And the rich man had exceedingly many flocks and herds;

**12:3** but the poor man had nothing except one little ewe lamb, which he had bought and preserved and nourished; and it grew up together with him and with his sons. It ate of his bread and drank from his cup, and slept in his bosom, and was to him as a daughter.

**12:4** And a traveler came to the rich man, and he was unwilling to take from his own flocks and from his own herds to prepare for the wayfaring stranger who had come to him; instead he took the poor man's ewe lamb and prepared it for the man who had come to him."

**12:5** And David was greatly kindled with wrath against the man; and David said to Nathan, "As the Lord lives, surely the man who has done this is a son of death,

**12:6** and he shall restore the ewe lamb sevenfold, because he has done this thing and because he showed no pity."

**12:7** And Nathan said to David, "You are the man who has done this! For thus says the Lord God of Israel: 'I am He who anointed you king over Israel, and I am He who delivered you from the hand of Saul;

**12:8** and I gave you the house of your master and the wives of your master into your bosom, and I gave you the house of Israel and of Judah; and if this is a small thing, I will add to you according to these things.

**12:9** Because you have despised the word of the Lord to do what is evil in His eyes—you have struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword, and have taken his wife to be your wife, and have slain him with the sword of the sons of Ammon—

**12:10** therefore now the sword shall not depart from your house forever, because you have shown contempt and have taken the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your wife.'

**12:11** Thus says the Lord: 'Behold, I am raising up evil against you out of your own house, and I will take your wives before your eyes and give them to your neighbor, and he shall lie with your wives in the sight of this sun.

**12:12** For you acted in secret, but I will do this thing before all Israel and in the full sight of this sun.'"

**12:13** And David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against the Lord." And Nathan said to David, "The Lord also has put away your sin; you shall not die.

**12:14** However, because by this deed you have greatly provoked the enemies of the Lord, the son also who has been born to you shall surely die."

**12:15** Then Nathan departed to his house. And the Lord struck the child that the wife of Uriah bore to David, and it became ill.

**12:16** And David sought God on behalf of the child; and David kept a fast, and went in and spent the night upon the ground.

**12:17** And the elders of the house stood over him to raise him up from the ground, but he was unwilling, nor did he eat bread with them.

**12:18** And it came to pass on the seventh day that the child died. And the servants of David were afraid to report to him that the child was dead, for they said, "Behold, while the child was still alive, we spoke to him, and he did not listen to our voice. How then shall we tell him that the child is dead? He will do harm."

**12:19** And David perceived that his servants were whispering, and David understood that the child was dead. And David said to his servants, "Is the child dead?" And they said, "He is dead."

**12:20** Then David arose from the ground, washed and anointed himself, and changed his garments; and he went into the house of God and worshiped Him. Then he went into his own house and asked for bread to eat; and they set bread before him, and he ate.

**12:21** And his servants said to him, "What is this thing that you have done? For the sake of the child while he was still alive, you fasted and wept and kept vigil; yet when the child died, you arose and ate bread and drank."

**12:22** And David said, "While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept; for I said, 'Who knows whether the Lord will have mercy on me, and the child shall live?'

**12:23** But now he is dead; why do I fast like this? Can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me."

**12:24** Then David comforted Bathsheba his wife, and went in to her and lay with her; and she conceived and bore a son, and called his name Solomon. And the Lord loved him,

**12:25** and He sent by the hand of Nathan the prophet, and he called his name Jedidiah, because of the Lord.

**12:26** Now Joab fought against Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, and captured the royal city.

**12:27** And Joab sent messengers to David and said, "I have fought against Rabbah and have captured the city of waters.

**12:28** Now therefore gather the rest of the people, and encamp against the city and capture it first, lest I capture the city first and my name be called upon it."

**12:29** So David gathered all the people and went to Rabbah, and fought against it and captured it.

**12:30** And he took the crown of Melchol their king from his head; its weight was a talent of gold with precious stone, and it was on David's head. And he brought out exceedingly much spoil from the city.

**12:31** And he brought out the people who were in it, and set them to the saw and to iron threshing sledges, and made them pass through the brickkiln; and thus he did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **12:6** — The Greek text reads 'sevenfold' (heptaplasiona), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'fourfold.'
- **12:21** — The Greek text adds 'and kept vigil' and 'and drank,' details not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **12:30** — The Greek text preserves the distinct proper name 'Melchol' alongside 'their king,' whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has only 'their king' (or 'Milcom').
