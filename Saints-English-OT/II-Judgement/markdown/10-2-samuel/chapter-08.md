## Text

**8:1** And it came to pass after this that David struck the Philistines and put them to flight; and David took the set-apart territory from the hand of the Philistines.

**8:2** And David struck Moab, and measured them with cords, making them lie down on the ground; and the two lines were to be put to death, and the two lines he kept alive. And Moab became servants to David, bringing tribute.

**8:3** And David struck Hadarezer the son of Rehob, king of Zobah, as he went to establish his hand upon the river Euphrates.

**8:4** And David captured from him one thousand chariots and seven thousand horsemen and twenty thousand footmen; and David disabled all the chariots, yet reserved for himself one hundred chariots.

**8:5** And Syria of Damascus came to help Hadarezer king of Zobah, and David struck among the Syrians twenty-two thousand men.

**8:6** Then David placed a garrison in Syria around Damascus, and the Syrians became servants to David, bringing tribute. And the Lord saved David wherever he went.

**8:7** And David took the golden bracelets which he had made for the servants of Hadarezer king of Zobah, and brought them to Jerusalem; and Shishak king of Egypt took them when he went up to Jerusalem in the days of Jeroboam the son of Solomon.

**8:8** And from Masbak, from the choice cities of Hadarezer, King David took exceedingly much bronze; with it Solomon made the bronze sea, and the pillars, and the lavers, and all the vessels.

**8:9** And Tou king of Hamath heard that David had struck all the army of Hadarezer,

**8:10** so Tou sent Jedduran his son to King David, to inquire after his peace and to bless him because he had struck Hadarezer and struck him down, for he was opposed to Hadarezer; and in his hands were vessels of silver, vessels of gold, and vessels of bronze.

**8:11** These also the king consecrated to the Lord, together with the silver and the gold which he had consecrated from all the cities that he had subdued:

**8:12** from Idumea, and from the land of Moab, and from the sons of Ammon, and from the Philistines, and from Amalek, and from the spoil of Hadarezer the son of Rehob, king of Zobah.

**8:13** And David made a name for himself; and as he returned, he struck Idumea in Gebelem, to the number of eighteen thousand.

**8:14** And he placed a garrison in Idumea, throughout all Idumea, and all the Idumeans became servants to the king. And the Lord saved David wherever he went.

**8:15** And David reigned over Israel; and he executed judgment and righteousness over all his people.

**8:16** And Joab the son of Zeruiah was over the army, and Jehoshaphat the son of Ahijah was over the records,

**8:17** and Zadok the son of Ahitub and Ahimelech the son of Abiathar were priests, and Asa was the scribe,

**8:18** and Benaiah the son of Janak was counselor, and the Cherethites and the Pelethites; and the sons of David were chiefs of the court.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Greek οἱ ἀλλόφυλοι ("foreigners") is the standard Septuagint term for the Philistines in Kingdoms, and τὴν ἀφωρισμένην ("the set-apart territory") translates the Hebrew place-name Metheg-ammah.
- **8:2** — The Septuagint specifies "the two lines" for those kept alive as well as those put to death, whereas the Masoretic Text has two lines put to death and one full line kept alive.
- **8:7** — Greek χλίδωνας means "bracelets" (for Masoretic "shields"). The closing clause about Shishak is a Septuagint addition that names "Jeroboam the son of Solomon" where 1 Kings 14:25 names Rehoboam.
- **8:8** — "Masbak" (Μασβὰκ) and "the choice cities" correspond to Betah and Berothai in the Masoretic Text; the closing clause about Solomon's bronze furnishings is a Septuagint addition parallel to 1 Chronicles 18:8.
- **8:10** — "Jedduran" (Ἰεδδουρὰν) is a distinct Septuagint form of the son's name (compare Joram in Masoretic 2 Samuel 8:10 and Hadoram in 1 Chronicles 18:10).
- **8:13** — "Gebelem" (Γεβέλεμ) is a Septuagint transliteration of the Hebrew for "Valley of Salt," and "Idumea" stands here and in 8:12 where the Masoretic Text reads Aram (Syria).
- **8:18** — In 8:16-18 the Septuagint has the distinct personal names Ahijah (for Ahilud), Asa (for Seraiah), and Janak (for Jehoiada); it also styles Benaiah as "counselor" (σύμβουλος) and David's sons as "chiefs of the court" (αὐλάρχαι, for Masoretic "priests").
