## Text

**18:1** And David inspected the people who were with him, and set over them captains of thousands and captains of hundreds.

**18:2** And David sent forth the people: a third under the hand of Joab, and a third under the hand of Abishai the son of Zeruiah, the brother of Joab, and a third under the hand of Ittai the Gittite. And David said to the people, "Surely I also will go forth with you."

**18:3** And they said, "You shall not go forth; for if we flee away, they will not set their heart upon us; and if half of us die, they will not set their heart upon us, for you are as ten thousand of us. And now it is good that you be a help to us in the city to aid us."

**18:4** And the king said to them, "Whatever is pleasing in your eyes I will do." And the king stood by the side of the gate, and all the people marched out by hundreds and by thousands.

**18:5** And the king commanded Joab, and Abishai, and Ittai, saying, "Spare for my sake the young man Absalom." And all the people heard as the king gave charge to all the commanders concerning Absalom.

**18:6** And all the people went out into the forest against Israel, and the battle took place in the forest of Ephraim.

**18:7** And the people of Israel were defeated there before the servants of David, and the slaughter was great on that day, twenty thousand men.

**18:8** And the battle there was spread out over the face of all the land; and the forest devoured more of the people than those whom the sword devoured among the people on that day.

**18:9** And Absalom met the servants of David; and Absalom was riding upon his mule, and the mule went under the thick boughs of the great oak, and his head was caught in the oak, and he was suspended between heaven and earth, while the mule beneath him passed on.

**18:10** And a certain man saw it, and reported to Joab, and said, "Behold, I have seen Absalom hanging in the oak."

**18:11** And Joab said to the man who reported it, "And behold, you saw him! Why did you not strike him to the ground? Then I would have given you ten pieces of silver and one belt."

**18:12** But the man said to Joab, "Even if I were weighing out a thousand shekels of silver upon my hands, I would by no means lay my hand upon the son of the king; for in our hearing the king commanded you and Abishai and Ittai, saying, 'Guard for me the young man Absalom,

**18:13** so as not to do wrong against his life.' And no matter will be hidden from the king, and you yourself will stand in opposition."

**18:14** And Joab said, "I will begin this; I will not wait thus before you." And Joab took three darts in his hand, and they thrust them into the heart of Absalom while he was still alive in the heart of the oak.

**18:15** And ten young men who bore Joab's armor surrounded him, and struck Absalom and put him to death.

**18:16** And Joab blew the horn, and the people turned back from pursuing after Israel, for Joab spared the people.

**18:17** And he took Absalom and cast him into a great chasm in the forest, into the great pit, and raised up over him a very great heap of stones. And all Israel fled, each man to his tent.

**18:18** Now Absalom, while he was still alive, had set up for himself the pillar in which he was taken, and had erected it to take for himself—the pillar that is in the Valley of the King—for he said he had no son to bring his name to remembrance; and he called the pillar the Hand of Absalom, to this day.

**18:19** Then Ahimaaz the son of Zadok said, "Let me run now and bring good tidings to the king, that the Lord has vindicated him from the hand of his enemies."

**18:20** And Joab said to him, "You are not a man of good tidings on this day, and you shall bear good tidings on another day; but on this day you shall not bear good tidings, because the son of the king is dead."

**18:21** Then Joab said to the Cushite, "Go, report to the king all that you have seen." And the Cushite bowed down to Joab and went out.

**18:22** And Ahimaaz the son of Zadok spoke yet again and said to Joab, "Let it be that I also run after the Cushite." And Joab said, "Why do you run for this, my son? Come, you have no good tidings to profit you in going."

**18:23** And he said, "For what if I run?" And Joab said to him, "Run." So Ahimaaz ran by the way of Kechar, and outran the Cushite.

**18:24** Now David was sitting between the two gates. And the watchman went up to the roof of the gate by the wall, and lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, a man running alone before him.

**18:25** And the watchman cried out and reported to the king. And the king said, "If he is alone, there are good tidings in his mouth." And he came on steadily and drew near.

**18:26** And the watchman saw another man running, and the watchman called out at the gate and said, "Behold, another man running alone!" And the king said, "He also brings good tidings."

**18:27** And the watchman said, "I see the running of the first like the running of Ahimaaz the son of Zadok." And the king said, "He is a good man, and indeed he will come with good tidings."

**18:28** And Ahimaaz called out and said to the king, "Peace!" And he bowed down to the king with his face to the earth, and said, "Blessed be the Lord your God, who has delivered up the men who hated their hand against my lord the king."

**18:29** And the king said, "Is it peace with the young man Absalom?" And Ahimaaz said, "I saw the great multitude at the sending of the king's servant Joab and your servant, and I did not know what was there."

**18:30** And the king said, "Turn aside, stand fast here." And he turned aside and stood.

**18:31** And behold, the Cushite arrived and said to the king, "Let my lord the king receive good tidings, for the Lord has vindicated you today from the hand of all who rose up against you."

**18:32** And the king said to the Cushite, "Is it peace with the young man Absalom?" And the Cushite said, "May the enemies of my lord the king, and all who have risen up against him for evil, become like the young man."

## Translator Notes

- **18:13** — The Greek links the opening infinitive clause ("so as not to do wrong against his life") as part of King David's command in verse 12, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text begins a new statement by the soldier.
- **18:14** — The Septuagint preserves Joab's distinctive opening clause ("I will begin this; I will not wait thus before you") and uses a plural verb ("they thrust them"), whereas the Hebrew has a singular verb.
- **18:18** — The Greek contains a distinctive doublet ("the pillar in which he was taken, and had erected it to take for himself") and reports Absalom's words in indirect speech; "the Hand of Absalom" literally renders the Greek name of the monument.
- **18:23** — "Kechar" follows the Greek transliteration of the Hebrew term for the Jordan Valley plain.
- **18:28** — The Greek reads "who hated their hand against my lord the king," reflecting a Hebrew textual variant ("hated") rather than the Masoretic Text's "lifted up."
