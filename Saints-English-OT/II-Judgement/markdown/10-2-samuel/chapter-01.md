## Text

**1:1** Now it came to pass after the death of Saul, when David had returned from striking down Amalek, that David remained in Ziklag two days.

**1:2** And it came to pass on the third day that, behold, a man came from the camp, from the people of Saul, with his garments torn and earth upon his head. And when he came in to David, he fell to the ground and bowed down before him.

**1:3** And David said to him, "Where have you come from?" And he said to him, "I have escaped from the camp of Israel."

**1:4** Then David said to him, "What is this matter? Tell me." And he answered, "The people have fled from the battle, and many of the people have fallen and died; and Saul also has died, and Jonathan his son has died."

**1:5** And David said to the young man who brought him the report, "How do you know that Saul and Jonathan his son are dead?"

**1:6** Then the young man who reported to him said, "By chance I happened to be on Mount Gilboa, and behold, Saul was leaning upon his spear; and behold, the chariots and the commanders of the horsemen were closing in on him.

**1:7** And he looked behind him, and saw me and called me; and I said, 'Here I am.'

**1:8** And he said to me, 'Who are you?' And I answered, 'I am an Amalekite.'

**1:9** And he said to me, 'Stand over me now and put me to death, for a dreadful darkness has seized me, for all my life is still in me.'

**1:10** So I stood over him and put him to death, because I knew that he would not live after he had fallen. And I took the crown that was on his head and the armlet that was on his arm, and I have brought them here to my lord."

**1:11** Then David took hold of his own garments and tore them, and all the men who were with him tore their garments.

**1:12** And they mourned and fasted and wept until evening for Saul and for Jonathan his son, and for the people of Judah and for the house of Israel, because they had been struck down by the sword.

**1:13** Then David said to the young man who had brought him the report, "Where are you from?" And he answered, "I am the son of an Amalekite sojourner."

**1:14** And David said to him, "How were you not afraid to put forth your hand to destroy the anointed of the Lord?"

**1:15** Then David called one of his young men and said, "Draw near and fall upon him!" And he struck him, and he died.

**1:16** And David said to him, "Your blood be upon your own head, for your own mouth has testified against you, saying, 'I have put to death the anointed of the Lord.'"

**1:17** Then David lamented with this lamentation over Saul and over Jonathan his son,

**1:18** and he gave word to teach it to the sons of Judah; behold, it is written in the Book of the Upright:

**1:19** "Set up a monument, O Israel, for those who have died, slain upon your high places! How the mighty have fallen!

**1:20** Do not declare it in Gath, and do not proclaim good tidings in the streets of Ashkelon, lest the daughters of the foreigners rejoice, lest the daughters of the uncircumcised exult.

**1:21** O mountains that are in Gilboa, let neither dew nor rain descend upon you, nor fields of firstfruits; for there the shield of the mighty was despised—the shield of Saul was not anointed with oil.

**1:22** From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, the bow of Jonathan did not turn back empty, and the sword of Saul did not return empty.

**1:23** Saul and Jonathan, beloved and fair, were not parted; comely in their life, and in their death they were not separated. They were swifter than eagles, and they were mightier than lions.

**1:24** O daughters of Israel, weep over Saul—weep for him who clothed you in scarlet with your adornment, who placed ornaments of gold upon your garments.

**1:25** How the mighty have fallen in the midst of the battle! O Jonathan, upon your high places lie the slain!

**1:26** I grieve for you, my brother Jonathan; you were exceedingly pleasant to me. Your love to me was wondrous, surpassing the love of women.

**1:27** How the mighty have fallen, and the weapons of war have perished!"

## Translator Notes

- **1:9** — The Greek reads "a dreadful darkness" (skotos deinon), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to anguish or convulsion.
- **1:12** — The Greek reads "the people of Judah" (where the Hebrew has "the people of the LORD") and places "fasted" before "wept."
- **1:18** — The Greek does not include the Hebrew reference to "the Bow," and translates the Hebrew "Book of Jashar" as "the Book of the Upright."
- **1:19** — The Greek reads the imperative "Set up a monument" (Steloson), whereas the Hebrew reads "The gazelle" or "The beauty of Israel."
- **1:20** — The Greek uses "foreigners" (allophyloi), the Septuagint's standard designation for the Philistines throughout the books of Kingdoms.
