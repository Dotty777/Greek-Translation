## Text

**1:1** And it came to pass, when the judges were judging, that a famine arose in the land; and a man departed from Bethlehem of Judah to sojourn in the countryside of Moab, he and his wife and his sons.

**1:2** And the name of the man was Abimelech, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion, Ephrathites from Bethlehem of Judah; and they came into the countryside of Moab and remained there.

**1:3** And Abimelech, Naomi's husband, died, and she and her two sons were left behind.

**1:4** And they took for themselves Moabite wives; the name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the second was Ruth. And they dwelt there about ten years.

**1:5** Then both of them also died, Mahlon and Chilion; and the woman was left bereft of her husband and of her two sons.

**1:6** Then she arose, she and her two daughters-in-law, and they returned from the countryside of Moab, for they had heard in the countryside of Moab that the Lord had visited His people to give them bread.

**1:7** So she went forth from the place where she had been, and her two daughters-in-law with her; and they walked along the way to return to the land of Judah.

**1:8** And Naomi said to her daughters-in-law, "Go now, return each of you to her mother's house. May the Lord show mercy to you, even as you have done with the dead and with me.

**1:9** May the Lord grant it to you, and may you find rest, each in the house of her husband." Then she kissed them, and they lifted up their voices and wept.

**1:10** And they said to her, "With you we will return to your people."

**1:11** But Naomi said, "Turn back now, my daughters; why will you go with me? Are there still sons in my womb, that they should become husbands for you?

**1:12** Turn back now, my daughters, for I have grown too old to belong to a husband. Even if I should say that I have hope of belonging to a husband and that I shall bear sons,

**1:13** would you wait for them until they are grown? Or would you hold yourselves back for them from belonging to a husband? No indeed, my daughters, for it has become more bitter for me than for you, because the hand of the Lord has gone forth against me."

**1:14** And they lifted up their voices and wept again; and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law and returned to her people, but Ruth followed her.

**1:15** And Naomi said to Ruth, "Behold, your sister-in-law has returned to her people and to her gods; you also turn back now after your sister-in-law."

**1:16** But Ruth said, "May it not befall me to forsake you or to turn away from following after you; for wherever you go, I will go, and wherever you abide, I will abide; your people shall be my people, and your God shall be my God.

**1:17** And where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. Thus may the Lord do to me, and thus may He add to it, that death alone shall divide between me and you."

**1:18** And when Naomi saw that she was steadfastly resolved to go with her, she ceased speaking to her any further.

**1:19** So the two of them went on until they arrived at Bethlehem. And all the city was astir with clamor over her, and they said, "Is this Naomi?"

**1:20** And she said to them, "Do not call me Naomi; call me Bitter, for the Sufficient One has dealt very bitterly with me.

**1:21** I went out full, and the Lord has brought me back empty. Why then do you call me Naomi, when the Lord has humbled me and the Sufficient One has afflicted me?"

**1:22** So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter-in-law, returning from the countryside of Moab; and they arrived in Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — The Greek text reads Abimelech (matching 1:3, 2:1, 2:3) rather than the Hebrew Elimelech, and does not mention Naomi's name until 1:3.
- **1:14** — The Greek includes "and returned to her people" and renders Ruth's action as "followed her" rather than the Hebrew "clung to her."
- **1:16** — The Greek renders Ruth's opening plea as an optative wish, "May it not befall me to forsake you," reflecting a distinct reading of the Hebrew verb.
- **1:20** — The Greek translates the name Mara as "Bitter" (Pikran) and renders Hebrew Shaddai as "the Sufficient One" (ho hikanos, also in 1:21).
