## Text

**10:1** Now the foreigners fought against Israel, and they fled from before the foreigners and fell wounded on Mount Gilboa.

**10:2** And the foreigners pursued closely after Saul and after his sons; and the foreigners struck down Jonathan, and Amminadab, and Malchishua, the sons of Saul.

**10:3** And the battle pressed heavily upon Saul; and the archers found them with bows and pains, and they suffered pain from the bows.

**10:4** Then Saul said to his armorbearer, "Draw your sword and thrust me through with it, lest these uncircumcised men come and mock me." But his armorbearer would not, for he was exceedingly afraid. Therefore Saul took the sword and fell upon it.

**10:5** And when his armorbearer saw that Saul was dead, he also fell upon his own sword.

**10:6** So Saul died, and his three sons on that day, and all his house died together.

**10:7** And when every man of Israel who was in the valley saw that Israel had fled, and that Saul and his sons were dead, they forsook their cities and fled; and the foreigners came and dwelt in them.

**10:8** And it came to pass on the next day that the foreigners came to strip the wounded, and they found Saul and his sons fallen on Mount Gilboa.

**10:9** And they stripped him, and took his head and his armor, and sent them throughout the land of the foreigners all around, to proclaim the good news to their idols and to the people.

**10:10** And they placed their armor in the house of their god, and his head they placed in the house of Dagon.

**10:11** And all who dwelt in Gilead heard everything that the foreigners had done to Saul and to Israel.

**10:12** And every valiant man arose from Gilead, and they took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons, and brought them to Jabesh, and buried their bones under the oak in Jabesh; and they fasted seven days.

**10:13** So Saul died in his iniquities which he committed against God concerning the word of the Lord, because he did not keep it, and because Saul inquired of a medium to seek counsel, and Samuel the prophet answered him,

**10:14** and he did not seek the Lord. Therefore He slew him, and turned the kingdom over to David the son of Jesse.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — Throughout this chapter the Septuagint designates the Philistines as "the foreigners" (ἀλλόφυλοι, literally "people of another tribe") and renders the battle casualties as "wounded" (τραυματίαι).
- **10:3** — The Greek shifts to plural pronouns ("found them... they suffered pain") and includes the distinctive phrase "with bows and pains" (ἐν τόξοις καὶ πόνοις).
- **10:10** — The Greek reads plural "their armor" (τὰ σκεύη αὐτῶν) and singular "their god" (θεοῦ αὐτῶν); in 10:11–12 it also reads "Gilead" rather than "Jabesh-gilead" and adds "and to Israel."
- **10:13** — The Septuagint preserves the explicit clause "and Samuel the prophet answered him" (καὶ ἀπεκρίνατο αὐτῷ Σαμουὴλ ὁ προφήτης), which is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
