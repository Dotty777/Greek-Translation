## Text

**25:1** And King David and the captains of the army appointed for the service the sons of Asaph, and of Heman, and of Jeduthun, who spoke forth with harps, with lutes, and with cymbals; and their number was reckoned head by head of those who labored in their works.

**25:2** The sons of Asaph: Sacchus, Joseph, Nathalias, and Erael—sons of Asaph next to the king.

**25:3** Of Jeduthun, the sons of Jeduthun: Tuna, Surei, Saia, Shimei, Hashabiah, and Mattathias, six, after their father Jeduthun, who struck up on the harp thanksgiving and praise to the Lord.

**25:4** Of Hemani, the sons of Heman: Bukkias, Manthanias, Azarael, Subael, Amsu, Jeremoth, Hananiah, Eliathath, Godollathi, and Romi; the sons of Od, and Jeibasaka, Manthi, Hothiri, and Melzoth.

**25:5** All these were sons to Heman, who played for the king in the words of God, to exalt the horn; and God gave to Heman fourteen sons and three daughters.

**25:6** All these were with their father singing hymns in the house of God with cymbals, with lutes, and with harps, next to the king, together with Asaph, Jeduthun, and Hemani.

**25:7** And their number, along with their brethren who were instructed to sing to the Lord, every one who was skillful, was two hundred and eighty-eight.

**25:8** And they also cast lots for their courses, both the small and the great, the accomplished and the learners.

## Translator Notes

- **25:3** — The Greek text names all six sons of Jeduthun (including Shimei) to match the stated total of six, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text names only five; the forms Tuna, Surei, and Saia also differ from the Hebrew.
- **25:4** — The Greek list of Heman's sons includes Amsu and divides the Hebrew name Romamti-Ezer into "Romi; the sons of Od," preserving a distinct Septuagint transmission of these names.
- **25:5** — The Greek reads "who played for the king" (literally, striking up music), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text describes Heman as "the king's seer."
- **25:9** — The Greek inserts "Heneia" after "Galouia the second" (likely from a Hebrew pronoun read as a proper name); throughout verses 9–31, several lot names such as Nathan, Emei, Haria, and Bakata reflect distinctive Septuagint forms.
