## Text

**20:1** And it came to pass in the following year, at the going forth of the kings, that Joab led out the whole force of the army, and they laid waste the land of the sons of Ammon; and he came and besieged Rabbah. Now David remained in Jerusalem; and he struck Rabbah and tore it down.

**20:2** And David took the crown of Molchol their king from his head, and its weight was found to be a talent of gold, and in it was a precious stone, and it was upon the head of David; and he brought out very great spoil from the city.

**20:3** And he brought out the people who were in it, and sawed them with saws and with iron adzes; and thus David did to the servants, the sons of Ammon. Then David and all his people returned to Jerusalem.

**20:4** And it came to pass after these things that there was again war in Gezer with the foreigners; at that time Sobochai the Thosathite struck down Saphut, who was of the sons of the giants, and humbled him.

**20:5** And there was again war with the foreigners, and Ellan the son of Jair struck down Elemee the brother of Goliath the Gittite, and the shaft of his spear was like a weavers' beam.

**20:6** And there was again war in Gath, and there was a man of exceeding stature, and his fingers and toes were six and six, twenty-four; and he also was a descendant of the giants.

**20:7** And he reproached Israel, and Jonathan the son of Shammah, the son of David's brother, struck him down.

**20:8** This one was born to Rapha in Gath; all of them were four giants, and they fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — The Septuagint reads the proper name Molchol ("Molchol their king"), where the Hebrew text is variously read as Milcom or "their king," and has singular "a precious stone."
- **20:3** — "To the servants, the sons of Ammon" renders Greek tois paisin huiois Ammon, likely an early scribal variant for pasin ("all"), whereas the Hebrew reads "all the cities of the sons of Ammon."
- **20:4** — "Foreigners" translates Greek allophylon, the Septuagint's regular designation for the Philistines; verses 4-5 also preserve the distinct Septuagint names Sobochai the Thosathite, Saphut, Ellan, and Elemee.
- **20:8** — The Septuagint explicitly states "all of them were four giants," a clause absent from the Masoretic Text of 1 Chronicles 20:8 though paralleling 2 Samuel 21:22.
