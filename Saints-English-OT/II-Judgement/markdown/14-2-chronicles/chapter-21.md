## Text

**21:1** And Jehoshaphat slept with his fathers, and was buried in the City of David; and Jehoram his son reigned in his place.

**21:2** And he had six brothers, sons of Jehoshaphat: Jehiel, and Zechariah, and Mishael, and Shephatiah; all these were the sons of Jehoshaphat king of Judah.

**21:3** And their father gave them many gifts—silver, and gold, and weapons—along with fortified cities in Judah; but the kingdom he gave to Jehoram, because he was the firstborn.

**21:4** Now Jehoram arose over his kingdom and strengthened himself, and he slew all his brothers with the sword, and also some of the rulers of Israel.

**21:5** When he was thirty-two years old, Jehoram was established over his kingdom, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.

**21:6** And he walked in the way of the king of Israel, just as the house of Ahab had done, for the daughter of Ahab was his wife; and he did what was evil in the sight of the Lord.

**21:7** Yet the Lord was not willing to destroy the house of David, because of the covenant that He had made with David, and as He had promised him to give a lamp to him and to his sons all the days.

**21:8** In those days Edom revolted from Judah, and set a king to reign over themselves.

**21:9** So Jehoram marched forth with the commanders, and all the cavalry with him; and it came to pass that he arose by night and struck Edom who surrounded him, and the commanders of the chariots; and the people fled to their tents.

**21:10** Thus Edom revolted from Judah to this day. Then Libnah revolted at that time from his hand, because he had forsaken the Lord God of his fathers.

**21:11** Moreover, he himself made high places in the cities of Judah, and they caused the inhabitants of Jerusalem to commit harlotry, and he led Judah astray.

**21:12** And a writing came to him from Elijah the prophet, saying, "Thus says the Lord God of your father David: 'Because you have not walked in the way of Jehoshaphat your father, nor in the ways of Asa king of Judah,

**21:13** but have walked in the ways of the king of Israel, and have caused Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem to commit harlotry just as the house of Ahab caused harlotry, and have also slain your brothers, the sons of your father, who were better than yourself,

**21:14** behold, the Lord will strike you with a great plague in your people, and in your sons, and in your wives, and in all your possessions;

**21:15** and you yourself shall be in a grievous sickness, with a disease of your bowels, until your bowels come out by reason of the sickness from day to day.'"

**21:16** And the Lord stirred up against Jehoram the Philistines, and the Arabians, and those who bordered the Ethiopians;

**21:17** and they came up against Judah and oppressed it, and carried away all the possessions that they found in the king's house, and his sons and his daughters, so that no son was left to him except Ahaziah, the youngest of his sons.

**21:18** And after all these things the Lord struck him in his bowels with a sickness for which there is no cure.

**21:19** And it came to pass from day to day; and when the appointed time of the days came, two days, his bowels came out with the disease, and he died in a grievous sickness. And his people did not make a funeral for him like the funeral of his fathers.

**21:20** He was thirty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem; and he departed without praise, and was buried in the City of David, yet not in the tombs of the kings.

## Translator Notes

- **21:2** — Although the Greek text states that Jehoram had six brothers, it lists only four names (Jehiel, Zechariah, Mishael, and Shephatiah), omitting the two occurrences of Azariah found in the Hebrew text.
- **21:9** — The Greek text reads "all the cavalry" (rather than "all the chariots") and includes the closing clause "and the people fled to their tents," paralleling 2 Kings 8:21.
- **21:17** — The Greek text reads "his daughters" (where the Hebrew has "his wives") and names the surviving son "Ahaziah" (rather than "Jehoahaz").
- **21:19** — The Greek renders the Hebrew idiom for "two years" literally as "two days," and refers to a "funeral" (ekphora) rather than a ritual burning.
