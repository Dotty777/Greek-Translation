## Text

**36:1** Then the people of the land took Jehoahaz the son of Josiah, and they anointed him and appointed him as king in place of his father.

**36:2** Jehoahaz was twenty-three years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. And the name of his mother was Abital, the daughter of Jeremiah from Libnah. And he did evil in the sight of the Lord, according to all that his fathers had done. And Pharaoh Necho bound him in Dablatha in the land of Hamath, so that he would not reign in Jerusalem.

**36:3** And the king removed him to Egypt, and imposed a tribute upon the land of one hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold.

**36:4** And Pharaoh Necho appointed Eliakim the son of Josiah, king of Judah, in place of Josiah his father, and changed his name to Jehoiakim. And Pharaoh Necho took his brother Jehoahaz and brought him into Egypt, and he died there. And he gave the silver and the gold to Pharaoh; at that time the land began to be taxed to give the silver at the command of Pharaoh, and each one according to his ability exacted the silver and the gold from the people of the land to give to Pharaoh Necho.

**36:5** Jehoiakim was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem; and the name of his mother was Zechora, the daughter of Neriah from Ramah. And he did evil before the Lord, according to all that his fathers had done. In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came into the land, and he served him three years, and rebelled against him. And the Lord sent against him the Chaldeans, and raiding bands of Syrians, and raiding bands of Moabites, and of the sons of Ammon, and of Samaria; and they departed after this word, according to the word of the Lord by the hand of His servants the prophets. Yet the wrath of the Lord was upon Judah, to remove him from His presence because of Manasseh, for all that he had done, and for the innocent blood which Jehoiakim shed; he filled Jerusalem with innocent blood; and the Lord was not willing to destroy them.

**36:6** And Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up against him, and bound him in bronze fetters, and led him away to Babylon.

**36:7** And he carried away a portion of the vessels of the house of the Lord to Babylon, and placed them in his temple in Babylon.

**36:8** Now the rest of the acts of Jehoiakim and all that he did, behold, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah? And Jehoiakim slept with his fathers, and was buried in Ganozae with his fathers. And Jeconiah his son reigned in his place.

**36:9** Jeconiah was eight years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months and ten days in Jerusalem, and he did evil in the sight of the Lord.

**36:10** And at the return of the year, King Nebuchadnezzar sent and brought him into Babylon with the precious vessels of the house of the Lord; and he made Zedekiah, his father's brother, king over Judah and Jerusalem.

**36:11** Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem.

**36:12** And he did evil in the sight of the Lord his God; he did not humble himself before Jeremiah the prophet, nor at the mouth of the Lord,

**36:13** in that he set aside his obligations toward King Nebuchadnezzar, which he had made him swear by God; and he stiffened his neck and hardened his heart, so that he would not return to the Lord God of Israel.

**36:14** And all the nobles of Judah, and the priests, and the people of the land, transgressed exceedingly with the faithless deeds of the abominations of the nations, and defiled the house of the Lord which was in Jerusalem.

**36:15** And the Lord God of their fathers sent by hand, rising early and sending His messengers, because He had compassion on His people and His sanctuary.

**36:16** But they mocked His messengers, and despised His words, and scoffed at His prophets, until the wrath of the Lord arose against His people, until there was no healing.

**36:17** And He brought against them the king of the Chaldeans, and he slew their young men with the sword in the house of His sanctuary; and he did not spare Zedekiah, and they showed no mercy to their virgins, and they led away their elders; He delivered all things into their hands.

**36:18** And all the vessels of the house of God, both great and small, and the treasures, and all the treasures of the king and of the nobles—all of them he brought into Babylon.

**36:19** And he burned the house of the Lord, and tore down the wall of Jerusalem, and burned her palaces with fire, and brought every beautiful vessel to destruction.

**36:20** And he carried the remnant away into Babylon, and they became servants to him and to his sons until the reign of the Medes,

**36:21** to fulfill the word of the Lord by the mouth of Jeremiah, until the land received its Sabbaths to keep Sabbath, to the completion of seventy years.

**36:22** In the first year of the king of the Persians, after the word of the Lord by the mouth of Jeremiah was fulfilled, the Lord stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of the Persians, and he commanded a proclamation to be made throughout all his kingdom in writing, saying,

**36:23** "Thus says Cyrus, king of the Persians, to all the kingdoms of the earth: The Lord God of heaven has given them to me, and He Himself has commanded me to build a house for Him in Jerusalem in Judea. Who is among you of all His people? His God shall be with him, and let him go up."

## Translator Notes

- **36:2** — The Greek text includes material parallel to 2 Kings 23:31–33 and preserves the distinct proper names Abital (Hebrew Hamutal) and Dablatha (Hebrew Riblah).
- **36:5** — The Greek text expands Jehoiakim's reign with material parallel to 2 Kings 23:36–24:4, naming his mother Zechora, daughter of Neriah from Ramah, attributing the innocent blood to Jehoiakim, and concluding that the Lord was not willing to destroy them (contrast Hebrew "to pardon").
- **36:8** — "Ganozae" is a Greek transliteration of the Hebrew phrase gan-Uzza ("the garden of Uzza"; cf. 2 Kings 21:18, 26), and the Greek names Jehoiakim's successor Jeconiah (Hebrew Jehoiachin).
- **36:17** — The Greek text reads "he did not spare Zedekiah" (where the Hebrew has "young man") and shifts to plural verbs for the Chaldeans before returning to the Lord as subject of "He delivered."
- **36:23** — The Greek places "to all the kingdoms of the earth" in the dative as the addressees of Cyrus's decree, leaving the object of "has given" implicit (supplied as "them"); likewise in 36:20 the Greek reads "of the Medes" where the Hebrew reads "of Persia."
