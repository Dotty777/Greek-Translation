## Text

**18:1** Now Jehoshaphat still had riches and honor in great abundance, and he allied himself by marriage with the house of Ahab.

**18:2** And after some years he went down to Ahab in Samaria; and Ahab slaughtered sheep and calves in abundance for him and for the people who were with him, and treated him affectionately so that he would go up with him to Ramoth of Gilead.

**18:3** And Ahab king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, "Will you go with me to Ramoth of Gilead?" And he said to him, "As I am, so also are you; as my people are, so also are your people, with you to battle."

**18:4** And Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "Please inquire today of the Lord."

**18:5** Then the king of Israel gathered the prophets together, four hundred men, and said to them, "Shall I go to Ramoth Gilead to battle, or shall I refrain?" And they said, "Go up, and God will deliver it into the hands of the king."

**18:6** And Jehoshaphat said, "Is there not still a prophet of the Lord here, that we may inquire of him?"

**18:7** And the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "There is still one man by whom we may inquire of the Lord, yet I hate him, because he does not prophesy good concerning me, for all his days are for evil; this is Micaiah the son of Jemaa." And Jehoshaphat said, "Do not let the king speak so."

**18:8** Then the king called a certain eunuch and said, "Bring Micaiah the son of Jemaa quickly."

**18:9** Now the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were sitting, each on his throne, and clothed in robes; they were sitting in the open space at the entrance of the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets were prophesying before them.

**18:10** And Zedekiah the son of Canaan made for himself horns of iron and said, "Thus says the Lord: 'With these you shall gore Syria until it is utterly destroyed.'"

**18:11** And all the prophets prophesied in the same way, saying, "Go up to Ramoth Gilead, and you shall prosper, and the Lord will deliver it into the hands of the king."

**18:12** And the messenger who had gone to call Micaiah spoke to him, saying, "Behold, the prophets have spoken good concerning the king with one mouth; please let your words be like those of one of them, and speak what is good."

**18:13** And Micaiah said, "As the Lord lives, whatever God says to me, that I will speak."

**18:14** So he came to the king; and the king said to him, "Micaiah, shall I go to Ramoth Gilead to battle, or shall I refrain?" And he said, "Go up, and you shall prosper; and they shall be delivered into your hands."

**18:15** And the king said to him, "How many times do I put you under oath that you speak nothing to me except the truth in the name of the Lord?"

**18:16** And he said, "I saw Israel scattered upon the mountains, like sheep that have no shepherd. And the Lord said, 'They have no leader; let each one return to his house in peace.'"

**18:17** And the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Did I not tell you that he does not prophesy good concerning me, but only evil?"

**18:18** And he said, "Not so! Hear the word of the Lord: I saw the Lord sitting on His throne, and all the host of heaven stood on His right hand and on His left.

**18:19** And the Lord said, 'Who will deceive Ahab king of Israel, that he may go up and fall at Ramoth Gilead?' And one spoke in this manner, and another spoke in that manner.

**18:20** Then the spirit came forward and stood before the Lord, and said, 'I will deceive him.' And the Lord said, 'By what means?'

**18:21** And he said, 'I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.' And He said, 'You shall deceive him, and you shall prevail; go out and do so.'

**18:22** Now therefore, behold, the Lord has put a lying spirit in the mouth of these prophets of yours, and the Lord has spoken evil against you."

**18:23** Then Zedekiah the son of Canaan drew near and struck Micaiah on the cheek, and said to him, "By which way did a spirit go from me—a spirit to you, the Spirit of the Lord from me—to speak to you?"

**18:24** And Micaiah said, "Behold, you shall see on that day; he shall enter chamber from chamber to hide himself."

**18:25** And the king of Israel said, "Take Micaiah and return him to Emer the governor of the city, and to Joash the ruler, the king's son,

**18:26** and you shall say, 'Thus has the king said: Put this man into the prison house, and let him eat bread of affliction and water of affliction until I return in peace.'"

**18:27** And Micaiah said, "If you truly return in peace, the Lord has not spoken by me; hear, all peoples!"

**18:28** So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth Gilead.

**18:29** And the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Disguise me, and I will enter into the battle, and you put on my apparel." So the king of Israel disguised himself and entered into the battle.

**18:30** Now the king of Syria had commanded the captains of the chariots who were with him, saying, "Do not fight with small or great, but with the king of Israel alone."

**18:31** And it came to pass, when the captains of the chariots saw Jehoshaphat, that they said, "It is the king of Israel!" And they surrounded him to fight; but Jehoshaphat cried out, and the Lord saved him, and God turned them away from him.

**18:32** And it came to pass, when the captains of the chariots saw that he was not the king of Israel, that they turned back from him.

**18:33** And a certain man drew a bow with sure aim and struck the king of Israel between the lung and the breastplate; and he said to the charioteer, "Turn your hand around and take me out of the battle, for I am wounded."

**18:34** And the battle turned on that day; and the king of Israel remained standing upon the chariot until evening opposite Syria, and he died as the sun was setting.

## Translator Notes

- **18:7** — The Greek reads the distinct name "Jemaa" (Greek Iemaas here and Iemaa in 18:8) where the Hebrew reads "Imlah."
- **18:10** — The Greek reads the distinct name "Canaan" (here and in 18:23) where the Hebrew reads "Chenaanah."
- **18:23** — The repetitive phrasing in Zedekiah's question ("a spirit from me—a spirit to you, the Spirit of the Lord from me") reflects a textual doublet in the Septuagint tradition, where the finite verb of motion is ellipsed.
- **18:25** — The Greek reads the distinct name "Emer" where the Hebrew reads "Amon," and adds the title "the ruler" for Joash.
- **18:29** — The Greek reads "Disguise me ... put on my apparel," whereas the Hebrew reads "I will disguise myself ... put on your robes."
- **18:33** — The Greek reads "with sure aim" and "between the lung and the breastplate," whereas the Hebrew reads "at random" and "between the joints of the armor."
