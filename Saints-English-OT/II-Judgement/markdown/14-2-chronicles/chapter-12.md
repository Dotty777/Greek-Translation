## Text

**12:1** Now it came to pass, when the kingdom of Rehoboam was established and strengthened, that he forsook the commandments of the Lord, and all Israel with him.

**12:2** And it happened in the fifth year of the reign of Rehoboam that Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, because they had sinned before the Lord,

**12:3** with one thousand two hundred chariots and sixty thousand horses; and the multitude that came with him out of Egypt was without number—Libyans, Troglodytes, and Ethiopians.

**12:4** And they captured the fortified cities that were in Judah, and he came to Jerusalem.

**12:5** Then Shemaiah the prophet came to Rehoboam and to the rulers of Judah who had gathered at Jerusalem because of Shishak, and said to them, "Thus has the Lord said: 'You have forsaken Me, and I also will forsake you into the hand of Shishak.'"

**12:6** And the rulers of Israel and the king were ashamed, and said, "The Lord is righteous."

**12:7** Now when the Lord saw that they were humbled, the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah, saying, "They were humbled; I will not destroy them, and I will grant them a small measure of deliverance, and My wrath shall by no means drip upon Jerusalem,

**12:8** for they shall become servants, and they shall know My service and the service of the kingdom of the earth."

**12:9** So Shishak king of Egypt came up, and took away the treasures that were in the house of the Lord and the treasures that were in the house of the king; he took away everything. He also took away the shields of gold that Solomon had made.

**12:10** And Rehoboam made shields of bronze in their place; and Shishak appointed over him captains of the runners, those who guarded the gate of the king.

**12:11** And it came to pass, whenever the king entered into the house of the Lord, that the guards and the runners went in, and those who returned to meet the runners.

**12:12** And when he was humbled, the wrath of the Lord turned away from him, and not to utter destruction; for indeed in Judah there were good matters.

**12:13** So Rehoboam grew strong in Jerusalem and reigned. Now Rehoboam was forty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city that the Lord had chosen out of all the tribes of the sons of Israel to name His name there. And his mother's name was Naamah the Ammonitess.

**12:14** And he did evil, because he did not direct his heart to seek the Lord.

**12:15** Now the acts of Rehoboam, first and last, behold, are they not written in the records of Shemaiah the prophet and of Iddo the seer, along with his deeds? And Rehoboam waged war against Jeroboam all the days.

**12:16** And Rehoboam died and was buried with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David; and Abijah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — The Greek reads sixty thousand horses (Hebrew: horsemen) and identifies the second allied people as Troglodytes (Hebrew: Sukkiim).
- **12:10** — In the Greek text, Shishak appoints captains of the runners over Rehoboam, whereas the Hebrew states that Rehoboam committed the bronze shields to the captains of the guard.
- **12:11** — The Greek describes the guards and runners entering and returning to meet the runners, without the Hebrew mention of bearing the shields and returning them to the guardroom.
- **12:15** — The Greek reads "and his deeds" where the Hebrew reads "according to genealogies."
