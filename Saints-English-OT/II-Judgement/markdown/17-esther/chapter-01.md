## Text

**1:1** In the second year of the reign of Artaxerxes the great king, on the first day of Nisan, Mordecai the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish, of the tribe of Benjamin, saw a dream—a Jewish man dwelling in the city of Susa, a great man, serving in the court of the king. Now he was from the captivity which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried away captive from Jerusalem with Jeconiah the king of Judea. And this was his dream: Behold, cries and uproar, thunderings and earthquake, tumult upon the earth. And behold, two great dragons came forth, both ready to wrestle; and their cry was great, and at their cry every nation made ready for war, to fight against a nation of the righteous. And behold, a day of darkness and gloom, tribulation and anguish, affliction and great tumult upon the earth; and all the righteous nation was troubled, fearing their own evils, and they prepared to perish; and they cried out to God. And from their cry there came, as it were from a small spring, a great river, abundant water; light and the sun rose, and the lowly were exalted and devoured the glorious. And when Mordecai awoke, having seen this dream and what God had purposed to do, he kept it in his heart, and in every way he desired to understand it until the night. And Mordecai rested in the court with Gabatha and Tharra, the two eunuchs of the king who guarded the court, and he heard their reasonings and searched out their designs, and learned that they were preparing to lay hands on Artaxerxes the king. And he informed the king concerning them; and the king examined the two eunuchs, and when they confessed, they were led away. And the king wrote these matters for a memorial, and Mordecai also wrote concerning these matters; and the king commanded Mordecai to serve in the court, and gave him gifts for these things. Now Haman the son of Hammedatha, a Bougaean, was honored before the king, and he sought to harm Mordecai and his people because of the two eunuchs of the king. And it came to pass after these things, in the days of Artaxerxes—this is the Artaxerxes who ruled from India over one hundred and twenty-seven provinces.

**1:2** In those very days, when King Artaxerxes was enthroned in the city of Susa,

**1:3** in the third year of his reign, he made a banquet for his friends and for the other nations, and for the nobles of the Persians and Medes, and for the rulers of the satraps.

**1:4** And after these things, after showing them the riches of his kingdom and the glory of the gladness of his riches for one hundred and eighty days,

**1:5** when the days of the marriage feast were fulfilled, the king made a drinking feast for the nations found in the city for six days in the court of the king's house,

**1:6** adorned with fine linen and cotton hangings stretched upon cords of fine linen and purple, upon blocks of gold and silver, upon pillars of Parian marble and stone; couches of gold and silver were upon a pavement of emerald stone, and mother-of-pearl, and Parian marble, and transparent coverings variously embroidered with flowers, with roses strewn all around;

**1:7** cups of gold and silver, and a small carbuncle goblet set forth, worth thirty thousand talents; abundant and sweet wine, such as the king himself drank.

**1:8** Now this drinking feast was not according to an established law; rather, the king so desired, and he commanded the stewards to do his will and the will of the men.

**1:9** And Queen Astin also made a drinking feast for the women in the royal palace where King Artaxerxes was.

**1:10** And on the seventh day, when the king had become merry, he spoke to Aman, Mazan, Tharra, Boraze, Zatholtha, Abataza, and Tharaba, the seven eunuchs who served King Artaxerxes,

**1:11** to bring the queen in to him, that she might reign as queen, and to place the diadem upon her, and to display her to the rulers and her beauty to the nations, for she was fair.

**1:12** But Queen Astin would not obey him to come with the eunuchs; and the king was grieved and grew angry,

**1:13** and he said to his friends, "In this manner Astin has spoken; therefore establish law and judgment concerning this."

**1:14** Then Arcesaeus, Sarsathaeus, and Malesear, the rulers of the Persians and Medes who were near the king, who sat first beside the king, came to him,

**1:15** and declared to him according to the laws what ought to be done to Queen Astin, because she had not done the things commanded by the king through the eunuchs.

**1:16** And Muchaeus said to the king and the rulers, "Queen Astin has wronged not the king alone, but also all the rulers and leaders of the king."

**1:17** For indeed he recounted to them the words of the queen, and how she had contradicted the king. "Therefore, just as she contradicted King Artaxerxes,

**1:18** so today the other noble ladies of the rulers of the Persians and Medes, having heard the things spoken by her to the king, will likewise dare to dishonor their own husbands.

**1:19** If therefore it pleases the king, let him issue a royal decree, and let it be written according to the laws of the Medes and Persians, and let him not do otherwise, nor let the queen come in before him any more, and let the king give her royal estate to a woman better than she.

**1:20** And let the law which the king makes be heard throughout his kingdom; and thus all women will bestow honor upon their own husbands, from poor to rich."

**1:21** And the word pleased the king and the rulers; and the king did just as Muchaeus had spoken,

**1:22** and he sent into all the kingdom, to every province according to its language, that there might be fear of them in their own houses.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The Septuagint opens with the Greek prologue (Addition A) recounting Mordecai's dream and the conspiracy of the eunuchs Gabatha and Tharra; it also identifies the Persian king as Artaxerxes (Hebrew: Ahasuerus) and calls Haman a "Bougaean" (Hebrew: Agagite).
- **1:5** — The Septuagint reads "the days of the marriage feast" and specifies "six days," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "these days" and "seven days."
- **1:9** — The Septuagint renders the queen's name as Astin (Hebrew: Vashti), and likewise gives distinct Greek forms for the names of the seven eunuchs (1:10), the three counselors (1:14), Muchaeus (1:16), and Gai (2:8).
