## Text

**10:1** Now the king wrote upon the kingdom of the land and of the sea.

**10:2** And his might and valor, and the wealth and glory of his kingdom—behold, they are written in the book of the kings of the Persians and Medes for a memorial.

**10:3** Now Mordecai was deputy to King Artaxerxes, and he was great in the kingdom and honored by the Jews; and being beloved, he set forth the way of life to all his nation. And Mordecai said, "From God these things have come to pass. For I remembered the dream which I saw concerning these matters, for not even a word of them has passed away: the little spring that became a river, and there was light and the sun and much water. Esther is the river, whom the king married and made queen; and the two dragons are I and Haman; and the nations are those gathered together to destroy the name of the Jews; and my nation, this is Israel, those who cried out to God and were saved. And the Lord saved His people, and the Lord delivered us from all these evils; and God performed the great signs and wonders which have not come to pass among the nations. Therefore He made two lots, one for the people of God and one for all the nations; and these two lots came to the hour and the appointed time and to the day of judgment before God and for all the nations, and God remembered His people, and He vindicated His own inheritance. And these days shall be for them in the month of Adar, on the fourteenth and the fifteenth of the same month, with assembly and joy and gladness before God, from generation to generation forever among His people Israel." In the fourth year of the reign of Ptolemy and Cleopatra, Dositheus, who said that he was a priest and a Levite, and Ptolemy his son brought in the foregoing letter of Phrourai, which they declared to be genuine and to have been translated by Lysimachus the son of Ptolemy, one of those in Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The Septuagint reads literally "wrote upon" without a direct object; some Greek manuscripts add "taxes," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "laid a tribute."
- **10:2** — The Greek text lists the "Persians and Medes" in this order, reversing the order "Medes and Persians" found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **10:3** — As throughout Greek Esther, the king is named Artaxerxes (Hebrew: Ahasuerus). This verse includes the Septuagint conclusion (Addition F), presenting Mordecai's interpretation of his dream and the translator's colophon naming the festival "Phrourai" (the Greek form of Purim).
