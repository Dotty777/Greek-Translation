## Text

**2:1** And after these things the king ceased from his wrath, and no longer remembered Astin, recalling what she had spoken and how he had condemned her.

**2:2** Then the servants of the king said, "Let uncorrupted maidens, fair in appearance, be sought for the king;

**2:3** and let the king appoint local governors in all the provinces of his kingdom, and let them select virgin maidens, fair in appearance, to be brought to the city of Susa into the women's quarters; and let them be delivered to the king's eunuch, the keeper of the women, and let ointments and all other care be given to them;

**2:4** and the woman who pleases the king shall reign as queen instead of Astin." And the counsel pleased the king, and he did so.

**2:5** Now there was a Jewish man in the city of Susa, and his name was Mordecai, the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish, of the tribe of Benjamin,

**2:6** who had been carried away captive from Jerusalem, which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had taken captive.

**2:7** And he had a foster child, the daughter of Amminadab, his father's brother, and her name was Esther; and when her parents died, he brought her up for himself as a wife; and the maiden was fair in appearance.

**2:8** And when the decree of the king was heard, many maidens were gathered to the city of Susa under the hand of Gai, and Esther was brought to Gai, the keeper of the women.

**2:9** And the maiden pleased him, and she found favor before him; and he hastened to give her the ointments and her portion, and the seven maidens appointed for her from the royal house, and he treated her and her attendants well in the women's quarters.

**2:10** And Esther did not reveal her lineage nor her homeland, for Mordecai had commanded her not to declare it.

**2:11** And every day Mordecai walked about by the court of the women, watching to see what would happen to Esther.

**2:12** Now this was the appointed time for a maiden to go in to the king, when she had completed twelve months; for thus the days of their care were fulfilled: six months anointing themselves with oil of myrrh, and six months with spices and with the ointments of women.

**2:13** And then she would go in to the king; and to whomever she named, he would deliver her to go in with him from the women's quarters to the royal palace.

**2:14** In the evening she would go in, and toward morning she would return to the second women's quarters, where Gai the king's eunuch, the keeper of the women, was; and she would not go in to the king again unless she was called by name.

**2:15** Now when the time was fulfilled for Esther, the daughter of Amminadab the brother of Mordecai's father, to go in to the king, she neglected nothing of the things which the eunuch, the keeper of the women, commanded; for Esther found favor with all who looked upon her.

**2:16** So Esther went in to King Artaxerxes in the twelfth month, which is Adar, in the seventh year of his reign.

**2:17** And the king loved Esther, and she found favor above all the virgins, and he placed the queenly diadem upon her.

**2:18** And the king made a drinking feast for all his friends and his forces for seven days, and he exalted the wedding of Esther, and granted a remission to those under his kingdom.

**2:19** Meanwhile, Mordecai was serving in the court.

**2:20** And Esther did not reveal her homeland; for so Mordecai had commanded her, to fear God and to keep His commandments, just as when she was with him; and Esther did not change her way of life.

**2:21** And the two eunuchs of the king, the chief bodyguards, were grieved because Mordecai had been promoted, and they sought to kill King Artaxerxes.

**2:22** And the matter was made known to Mordecai, and he informed Esther, and she disclosed the details of the plot to the king.

**2:23** Then the king examined the two eunuchs and hanged them; and the king commanded that a memorial be entered in the royal library concerning the goodwill of Mordecai in commendation of him.

## Translator Notes

- **2:7** — The Septuagint names Esther's father as Amminadab (Hebrew: Abihail) and states that Mordecai brought her up "for himself as a wife," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "as his own daughter."
- **2:16** — The Septuagint places Esther's entry before the king in the twelfth month, Adar, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads the tenth month, Tebeth.
- **2:20** — The Septuagint adds Mordecai's charge to Esther "to fear God and to keep His commandments," underscoring her continued religious faithfulness in the royal court.
