## Text

**8:1** And on that same day King Artaxerxes bestowed upon Esther all that belonged to Haman the slanderer, and Mordecai was summoned by the king; for Esther had disclosed that he was related to her.

**8:2** And the king took the signet ring that he had taken away from Haman and gave it to Mordecai; and Esther appointed Mordecai over all that belonged to Haman.

**8:3** And she spoke yet again to the king, and fell down at his feet, and pleaded with him to remove the wickedness of Haman and all that he had done to the Jews.

**8:4** Then the king extended the golden scepter to Esther; so Esther arose to stand before the king.

**8:5** And Esther said, "If it pleases you and I have found favor, let an order be sent to recall the letters dispatched by Haman, which were written that the Jews who are in your kingdom should perish.

**8:6** For how shall I be able to behold the affliction of my people, and how shall I be able to be saved in the destruction of my homeland?"

**8:7** Then the king said to Esther, "If I have given and graciously bestowed upon you all the possessions of Haman, and have hanged him upon a gallows because he laid his hands upon the Jews, what more do you seek?

**8:8** Write also yourselves in my name as seems good to you, and seal it with my signet ring; for all things that are written at the king's command and sealed with my signet ring cannot be contradicted."

**8:9** Then the scribes were summoned in the first month, which is Nisa, on the twenty-third day of that same year; and there was written to the Jews all that he commanded the stewards and the rulers of the satraps from India to Ethiopia, across one hundred and twenty-seven satrapies, province by province, according to their own language.

**8:10** And it was written by the king and sealed with his signet ring, and they sent out the letters by couriers,

**8:11** in which he commanded them to observe their own laws in every city, and to defend themselves, and to deal with their adversaries and their opponents as they wished,

**8:12** on one day throughout all the kingdom of Artaxerxes, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is Adar. Now the copy of the letter is what is written below: "The great King Artaxerxes, to the rulers of the provinces in the one hundred and twenty-seven satrapies from India to Ethiopia, and to those loyal to our interests, greetings. Many who are more frequently honored by the exceedingly great goodness of their benefactors have become arrogant, and not only do they seek to harm our subjects, but being unable to bear their abundance, they even undertake to scheme against their own benefactors; and not only do they take away gratitude from among men, but also, lifted up by the boasts of those ignorant of goodness, they suppose they shall escape the evil-hating justice of God, who continually beholds all things. Moreover, often the persuasion of friends entrusted to administer affairs has made many of those appointed to authority partakers of innocent blood and involved them in incurable calamities, as by the false sophistry of malice they have deceived the guileless goodwill of those in authority. And you may observe this not so much from the more ancient histories we have handed down, as by searching out what lies close at hand—things impiously perpetrated through the pestilence of those who wield power unworthily—and take heed for the future, so that we may render the kingdom undisturbed with peace for all men, making use of changes and always judging the matters that come before our eyes with a more equitable response. For indeed, Haman the son of Hammedatha, a Macedonian, in truth a stranger to the blood of the Persians and far removed from our goodness, having been received as a guest by us, obtained such a share of the benevolence we hold toward every nation that he was proclaimed our father and continually remained the second person to the royal throne, receiving obeisance from all; yet, unable to bear his arrogance, he strove to deprive us of both our dominion and our life, and by intricate and deceitful schemes he demanded for destruction both our savior and continual benefactor Mordecai, and Esther, the blameless partner of our kingdom, together with their entire nation. For by these means he thought, having caught us defenseless, to transfer the dominion of the Persians to the Macedonians. But we find that the Jews, who were delivered over to annihilation by that thrice-wicked wretch, are not evildoers, but are governed by the most righteous laws, and are sons of the Most High, the greatest, living God, who directs the kingdom both for us and for our forefathers in the most excellent order. Therefore you will do well not to act upon the letters sent by Haman the son of Hammedatha, because the very man who devised these things has been crucified at the gates of Susa with his entire household, since God, who rules over all things, has swiftly rendered to him the judgment he deserved. Display the copy of this letter openly in every place, and permit the Jews to observe their own laws, and assist them so that they may defend themselves against those who attack them in the time of affliction, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, Adar, on that very day; for God, who has dominion over all things, has made this day a day of gladness for them instead of a day of destruction for the chosen race. Therefore you also, among your named festivals, keep this as a notable day with all feasting, so that both now and afterward it may be deliverance for us and for the well-disposed Persians, but for those who plot against us a memorial of destruction. And every city or province without exception that does not act according to these instructions shall be consumed in wrath with spear and fire; it shall be made not only impassable for men, but also utterly hateful to wild beasts and birds for all time."

**8:13** And let the copies be set forth in plain sight throughout the entire kingdom, and let all the Jews be ready for that day to fight against their adversaries.

**8:14** So the horsemen went out in haste to carry out the things spoken by the king; and the decree was also posted in Susa.

**8:15** Now Mordecai went out arrayed in the royal robe, wearing a golden crown and a diadem of purple fine linen; and when those in Susa saw him, they rejoiced.

**8:16** And for the Jews there was light and gladness,

**8:17** in every city and province wherever the decree was posted, wherever the proclamation was posted: joy and gladness for the Jews, feasting and gladness. And many of the Gentiles were circumcised and lived as Jews, because of the fear of the Jews.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Throughout Greek Esther the king is named "Artaxerxes" rather than Ahasuerus.
- **8:9** — The Septuagint dates the royal summons to the first month, "Nisa" (Nisan), rather than the third month, Sivan, as in the Hebrew text.
- **8:12** — This verse contains the Greek royal decree known as Addition E, which identifies Haman as a Macedonian rather than an Agagite and explicitly attributes the Jews' deliverance to the living God.
- **8:17** — The Septuagint explicitly adds that many Gentiles "were circumcised" as well as living as Jews (ioudaizon).
