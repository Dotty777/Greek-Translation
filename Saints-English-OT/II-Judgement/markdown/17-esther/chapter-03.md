## Text

**3:1** Now after these things King Artaxerxes glorified Haman the son of Hammedatha, a Bougaean, and exalted him, and he held the first seat among all his friends.

**3:2** And all who were in the court bowed down to him, for so the king had commanded to be done; but Mordecai would not bow down to him.

**3:3** And those who were in the king's court spoke to Mordecai, "Mordecai, why do you disregard the things spoken by the king?"

**3:4** Day by day they spoke to him, and he did not listen to them; so they reported to Haman that Mordecai was opposing the words of the king; and Mordecai had declared to them that he was a Jew.

**3:5** And when Haman perceived that Mordecai did not bow down to him, he was filled with great wrath,

**3:6** and he purposed to destroy all the Jews who were under the kingdom of Artaxerxes.

**3:7** And he made a decree in the twelfth year of the reign of Artaxerxes, and cast lots day by day and month by month, to destroy in one day the race of Mordecai; and the lot fell on the fourteenth day of the month which is Adar.

**3:8** And he spoke to King Artaxerxes, saying, "There is a nation scattered among the nations in all your kingdom, and their laws are different from all the nations, and they disregard the laws of the king, and it is not profitable for the king to leave them alone.

**3:9** If it pleases the king, let him decree to destroy them, and I will pay into the treasury of the king ten thousand talents of silver."

**3:10** Then the king took off his signet ring and gave it into the hand of Haman, to seal what was written against the Jews.

**3:11** And the king said to Haman, "Keep the silver, and deal with the nation as you wish."

**3:12** So the scribes of the king were summoned on the thirteenth day of the first month, and they wrote as Haman commanded to the generals and to the rulers in every province from India to Ethiopia, to the one hundred and twenty-seven provinces, and to the rulers of the nations according to their own language, in the name of King Artaxerxes.

**3:13** And it was sent by couriers into the kingdom of Artaxerxes, to destroy the race of the Jews in one day of the twelfth month, which is Adar, and to plunder their possessions. Now this is the copy of the letter: "The Great King Artaxerxes writes these things to the rulers of the one hundred and twenty-seven provinces from India to Ethiopia, and to the governors subject to him: Having ruled over many nations and gained dominion over the whole inhabited world, I purposed—not being lifted up by the boldness of authority, but always conducting affairs with forbearance and gentleness—to establish the lives of my subjects continually calm and untroubled, and, making the kingdom peaceable and safe for travel to its utmost borders, to restore the peace longed for by all mankind. Now when I inquired of my counselors how this might be brought to fulfillment, Haman, who excels among us in soundness of mind, and has been proven steadfast in unswerving goodwill and firm loyalty, and has attained the second place of honor in the kingdom, pointed out to us that among all the tribes throughout the inhabited world there is mingled a certain hostile people, opposed in their laws to every nation, and continually disregarding the commands of kings, so that the united governance blamelessly directed by us cannot be established. Having therefore understood that this nation alone stands continually in opposition to all mankind, turning aside to a strange manner of life under alien laws, and being ill-disposed toward our affairs, committing the worst evils, so that the kingdom cannot attain stability; we have therefore commanded that those indicated to you in the letters written by Haman, who is appointed over our affairs and is our second father, shall all, together with wives and children, be utterly destroyed root and branch by the swords of their enemies, without any pity or sparing, on the fourteenth day of the twelfth month, Adar, of the present year, so that those who both of old and now are hostile, having gone down violently in one day into Hades, may for the time to come leave our affairs completely stable and untroubled to the end."

**3:14** And copies of the letters were posted in every province; and it was commanded to all the nations to be ready for this day.

**3:15** And the matter was hastened also in Susa; and the king and Haman drank heavily together, while the city was thrown into confusion.

## Translator Notes

- **3:7** — The Septuagint specifies that the lot fell on the fourteenth day of Adar (compare 3:13), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text names only the month here and assigns the thirteenth day of Adar in 3:13.
- **3:13** — This verse includes the full text of King Artaxerxes' decree (Addition B), written in formal Hellenistic royal chancery style and absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
