## Text

**6:1** Now the Lord took sleep away from the king that night, and he told his tutor to bring in the memorial writings of the days to read to him.

**6:2** And he found the records written concerning Mordecai, how he had reported to the king concerning the two eunuchs of the king, while they were standing guard and sought to lay hands on Artaxerxes.

**6:3** Then the king said, "What honor or favor have we done for Mordecai?" And the servants of the king said, "You have done nothing for him."

**6:4** Now while the king was inquiring concerning the loyalty of Mordecai, behold, Haman was in the courtyard. And the king said, "Who is in the courtyard?" For Haman had entered to tell the king to hang Mordecai upon the beam that he had prepared.

**6:5** And the servants of the king said, "Behold, Haman is standing in the courtyard." And the king said, "Call him."

**6:6** And the king said to Haman, "What shall I do for the man whom I wish to honor?" Now Haman said within himself, "Whom does the king wish to honor except me?"

**6:7** And he said to the king, "For the man whom the king wishes to honor,

**6:8** let the servants of the king bring a fine linen robe that the king wears, and a horse upon which the king rides,

**6:9** and let him give them to one of the king's illustrious friends, and let him array the man whom the king loves, and mount him upon the horse, and proclaim through the broad square of the city, saying, 'So shall it be for every man whom the king honors.'"

**6:10** Then the king said to Haman, "You have spoken well; do thus for Mordecai the Jew, who serves in the courtyard, and do not let a single word fail of what you have spoken."

**6:11** So Haman took the robe and the horse, arrayed Mordecai, mounted him upon the horse, and passed through the broad square of the city, proclaiming and saying, "So shall it be for every man whom the king wishes to honor."

**6:12** Then Mordecai returned to the courtyard, while Haman went back to his own house, grieving with his head covered.

**6:13** And Haman recounted what had befallen him to Zosara his wife and to his friends. And his friends and his wife said to him, "If Mordecai is of the race of the Jews, you have begun to be humbled before him—you shall surely fall! You will by no means be able to defend yourself against him, because the living God is with him."

**6:14** While they were still speaking, the eunuchs arrived, hurrying Haman to the wine banquet that Esther had prepared.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — The Septuagint explicitly attributes the king's sleeplessness to "the Lord" and refers to the court reader as his "tutor" (διδάσκαλος).
- **6:2** — "Artaxerxes" preserves the Septuagint name of the Persian king (Ἀρταξέρξης), corresponding to "Ahasuerus" in the Hebrew text.
- **6:13** — The Septuagint explicitly adds "because the living God is with him," an affirmation absent from the Hebrew text.
