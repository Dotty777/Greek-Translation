## Text

**7:1** Now the king and Haman went in to drink with the queen.

**7:2** And on the second day, at the drinking feast, the king said to Esther, "What is it, Queen Esther, and what is your petition, and what is your request? Let it be yours, even to half of my kingdom."

**7:3** And answering, she said, "If I have found favor before the king, let my life be given at my petition, and my word at my request.

**7:4** For we have been sold, both I and my people, to destruction and plunder and bondage, we and our children to be menservants and maidservants, and I paid no heed; for the slanderer is not worthy of the king's court."

**7:5** Then the king said, "Who is this man who has dared to do this thing?"

**7:6** And Esther said, "A man who is an enemy—this wicked Haman!" And Haman was dismayed before the king and the queen.

**7:7** Now the king arose from the banquet into the garden; but Haman pleaded with the queen, for he saw himself in dire straits.

**7:8** And the king returned from the garden, and Haman had fallen upon the couch, pleading with the queen. Then the king said, "Will you even assault my wife in my house?" And when Haman heard this, he was confounded in his countenance.

**7:9** Then Bugathan, one of the eunuchs, said to the king, "Behold, Haman has also prepared a gallows for Mordecai, who spoke concerning the king, and a gallows fifty cubits high stands erected at Haman's house." And the king said, "Let him be crucified upon it."

**7:10** So Haman was hanged upon the gallows that had been prepared for Mordecai. And then the king ceased from his wrath.

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — The Greek text reads logos ("word" or "matter"), where some manuscripts and the Hebrew read "my people" (laos).
- **7:4** — Greek diabolos ("slanderer" or "accuser") is applied to Haman here and in 8:1.
- **7:9** — "Bugathan" is the Septuagint name corresponding to Harbonah in the Hebrew text; the Greek verb stauroo ("crucify" or "impale") is used here and in 8:12 for execution on the fifty-cubit xylon ("gallows" or "timber").
