## Text

**5:1** Now it came to pass on the third day, when she ceased praying, that she took off the garments of worship and arrayed herself in her splendor. And having become resplendent, after calling upon God, the Overseer of all and Savior, she took with her the two maidservants; upon the one she leaned delicately, while the other followed behind, lifting up her robe. And she was radiant in the prime of her beauty, and her countenance was cheerful and lovely, yet her heart was constricted with fear. Having passed through all the doors, she stood before the king; and he was seated upon his royal throne, clothed in all the array of his majesty, wholly adorned with gold and precious stones, and he was exceedingly terrifying. Lifting up his countenance, blazing with splendor, he looked at her in the fierceness of wrath; and the queen fell down, her color changed as she fainted, and she sank down upon the head of the maidservant who went before her. Then God changed the spirit of the king into gentleness, and in anxiety he sprang up from his throne and took her into his arms until she recovered, and he comforted her with words of peace and said to her, "What is it, Esther? I am your brother. Take courage; you shall not die, for our decree is a general one. Come near."

**5:2** And lifting up the golden scepter, he laid it upon her neck, embraced her, and said, "Speak to me." And she said to him, "I saw you, my lord, like an angel of God, and my heart was troubled in fear of your glory; for you are wondrous, my lord, and your countenance is full of grace." Yet as she was speaking, she fell down from faintness; and the king was distressed, and all his attendants comforted her.

**5:3** Then the king said, "What do you wish, Esther, and what is your petition? Even up to half of my kingdom, it shall be yours."

**5:4** And Esther said, "Today is a special day for me; if therefore it pleases the king, let both him and Haman come to the banquet that I will prepare today."

**5:5** And the king said, "Hasten Haman, that we may carry out the word of Esther." So both of them came to the banquet of which Esther had spoken.

**5:6** Now during the drinking, the king said to Esther, "What is it, Queen Esther? Whatever you request shall be granted."

**5:7** And she said, "My request and my petition:

**5:8** if I have found favor before the king, let the king and Haman come again tomorrow to the banquet that I will prepare for them, and tomorrow I will do the same."

**5:9** So Haman went out from the king overjoyed and glad; yet when Haman saw Mordecai the Jew in the courtyard, he became exceedingly angry.

**5:10** And entering his own house, he called together his friends and Zosara his wife,

**5:11** and he displayed to them his wealth and the glory with which the king had invested him, and how he had appointed him to hold first place and to lead the kingdom.

**5:12** And Haman said, "The queen has invited no one with the king to the banquet except me; and for tomorrow also I am invited.

**5:13** Yet these things do not please me whenever I see Mordecai the Jew in the courtyard."

**5:14** Then Zosara his wife and his friends said to him, "Let a beam of fifty cubits be hewn for you, and at dawn speak to the king, and let Mordecai be hanged upon the beam; then go in to the banquet with the king and rejoice." And the saying pleased Haman, and the beam was made ready.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Verses 5:1–2 incorporate Septuagint Addition D, expanding Esther's audience before the king; the phrase "a general one" (κοινόν) means the royal ordinance applies to ordinary subjects rather than to the queen.
- **5:8** — The Septuagint reads "tomorrow I will do the same," differing from the Hebrew text, which has Esther promise to do as the king has said.
- **5:10** — "Zosara" preserves the Septuagint name (Ζωσάρα) for Haman's wife, corresponding to "Zeresh" in the Hebrew text.
