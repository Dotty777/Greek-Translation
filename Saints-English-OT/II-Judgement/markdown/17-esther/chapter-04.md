## Text

**4:1** Now Mordecai, learning of what was being done, tore his garments, put on sackcloth, and sprinkled ashes upon himself; and rushing out through the broad square of the city, he cried out with a loud voice, "A nation that has done no wrong is being swept away!"

**4:2** And he came as far as the king's gate and stood there, for it was not lawful for him to enter the courtyard wearing sackcloth and ashes.

**4:3** And in every province where the letters were posted, there was outcry, wailing, and great mourning among the Jews; they spread sackcloth and ashes for themselves.

**4:4** And the maidservants and the eunuchs of the queen came in and told her; and she was deeply troubled upon hearing what had happened, and sent to clothe Mordecai and take away his sackcloth, but he would not consent.

**4:5** Then Esther summoned Achrathaeus her eunuch, who stood in attendance upon her, and sent him to learn the exact truth for her from Mordecai.

**4:7** And Mordecai showed him what had happened and the promise of ten thousand talents that Haman had promised the king for the treasury in order to destroy the Jews.

**4:8** He also gave him the copy posted in Susa for their destruction, to show to Esther, and told him to charge her to go in, plead with the king, and petition him on behalf of the people: "Remember the days of your lowliness, how you were reared by my hand; for Haman, who holds second place to the king, has spoken against us for death. Call upon the Lord, and speak to the king for us, and deliver us from death."

**4:9** So Achrathaeus went in and spoke all these words to her.

**4:10** Then Esther said to Achrathaeus, "Go to Mordecai and say,

**4:11** 'All the nations of the kingdom know that for any man or woman who goes in to the king into the inner courtyard unsummoned, there is no deliverance—only the one to whom the king extends the golden scepter shall be saved; and I myself have not been summoned to go in to the king these thirty days.'"

**4:12** And Achrathaeus reported to Mordecai all the words of Esther.

**4:13** And Mordecai said to Achrathaeus, "Go and say to her, 'Esther, do not say to yourself that you alone will be saved in the kingdom above all the Jews;

**4:14** for if you fail to heed at this time, help and protection will come to the Jews from another quarter, while you and the house of your father shall perish. And who knows whether you became queen for this very time?'"

**4:15** Then Esther sent the one who had come to her back to Mordecai, saying,

**4:16** "Go, assemble the Jews who are in Susa, and fast on my behalf; neither eat nor drink for three days, night and day, and I and my maidservants will likewise abstain from food. Then I will go in to the king contrary to the law, even if it be that I must perish."

**4:17** So Mordecai went and did all that Esther had commanded him. And he prayed to the Lord, calling to remembrance all the works of the Lord, and said, "O Lord, Lord, King ruling over all things, for all things are in Your power, and there is no one who can oppose You when You will to save Israel; for You made heaven and earth and every wondrous thing beneath heaven, and You are Lord of all, and there is no one who shall withstand You, the Lord. You know all things; You know, O Lord, that it was not in insolence, nor in pride, nor in love of glory that I did this—refusing to bow down before the arrogant Haman; for I would gladly have kissed the soles of his feet for the salvation of Israel. Rather, I did this so that I might not set the glory of a man above the glory of God; and I will not bow down to anyone except You, my Lord, nor will I do these things in pride. And now, O Lord God, the King, the God of Abraham, spare Your people; for they look upon us for destruction, and they have desired to destroy Your inheritance from the beginning. Do not overlook Your portion, which You redeemed for Yourself out of the land of Egypt. Hear my supplication and be merciful to Your allotted heritage, and turn our mourning into feasting, that living we may sing praise to Your name, O Lord; and do not blot out the mouth of those who praise You." And all Israel cried out with all their might, because their death was before their eyes. And Esther the queen fled for refuge to the Lord, seized with the agony of death; and taking off the garments of her splendor, she put on garments of distress and mourning, and instead of exquisite perfumes she filled her head with ashes and dung; and she humbled her body exceedingly, and every place of her joyful adornment she covered with her tangled hair. And she prayed to the Lord God of Israel and said, "My Lord, our King, You are the only One; help me, alone as I am and having no helper except You, for my peril is in my hand. From my birth I have heard in the tribe of my father's house that You, O Lord, took Israel out of all the nations, and our fathers out of all their ancestors, for an everlasting inheritance, and that You did for them all that You spoke. And now we have sinned before You, and You have delivered us into the hands of our enemies because we gave glory to their gods. Righteous are You, O Lord! Yet now they are not satisfied with the bitterness of our bondage, but they have set their hands to abolish the decree of Your mouth, and to blot out Your inheritance, and to shut the mouth of those who praise You, and to quench the glory of Your house and Your altar, and to open the mouth of the nations in praise of worthless things, and that a king of flesh should be marveled at forever. Do not surrender Your scepter, O Lord, to those who do not exist, and do not let them mock at our downfall; but turn their counsel back upon themselves, and make a public example of the one who began this against us. Remember, O Lord; make Yourself known in the time of our affliction, and give me courage, O King of the gods and Sovereign over every dominion. Put well-ordered speech into my mouth before the lion, and turn his heart to hatred of the one who makes war against us, to the utter ruin of him and of those who are of one mind with him. But deliver us by Your hand, and help me, alone as I am and having no one except You, O Lord. You have knowledge of all things, and You know that I have hated the glory of the lawless, and I abhor the bed of the uncircumcised and of every foreigner. You know my necessity—that I abhor the emblem of my high station which is upon my head on the days of my public appearance; I abhor it like a menstrual cloth, and I do not wear it on the days of my quietness. And Your handmaid has not eaten at Haman's table, nor have I honored the king's banquet, nor have I drunk the wine of drink offerings. And Your handmaid has not rejoiced from the day of my change of fortune until now, except in You, O Lord, the God of Abraham. O God, mighty over all, hear the voice of those in despair, and deliver us out of the hand of evildoers, and deliver me from my fear."

## Translator Notes

- **4:5** — "Achrathaeus" preserves the Septuagint name (Ἁχραθαῖος), corresponding to "Hathach" in the Hebrew text; verse 4:6 is absent in the Septuagint.
- **4:8** — The Greek shifts directly from indirect instructions into Mordecai's direct appeal to Esther and explicitly includes the command to "call upon the Lord," which is absent from the Hebrew text.
- **4:17** — In the Septuagint, verse 4:17 incorporates Addition C, containing the prayers of Mordecai and Esther; the critical Greek text reads simply "they have set their hands" without mentioning the hands of idols.
