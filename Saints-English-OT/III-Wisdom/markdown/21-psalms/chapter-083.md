# Psalm 83 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**83:1** For the end, concerning the winepresses; a Psalm for the sons of Korah.

**83:2** How beloved are Your dwellings, O Lord of hosts!

**83:3** My soul yearns and faints for the courts of the Lord; my heart and my flesh have rejoiced in the living God.

**83:4** For indeed the sparrow has found a house for itself, and the turtledove a nest for herself, where she shall lay her young—Your altars, O Lord of hosts, my King and my God.

**83:5** Blessed are all who dwell in Your house; forever and ever they shall praise You. Interlude.

**83:6** Blessed is the man whose help is from You, O Lord; he has set ascents in his heart,

**83:7** in the valley of weeping, to the place which he has appointed. For indeed the Lawgiver shall give blessings;

**83:8** they shall go from strength to strength; the God of gods shall be seen in Zion.

**83:9** O Lord, the God of hosts, listen to my prayer; give ear, O God of Jacob. Interlude.

**83:10** See, O God our defender, and look upon the face of Your anointed.

**83:11** For one day in Your courts is better than thousands; I have chosen to be cast aside in the house of God rather than to dwell in the tents of sinners.

**83:12** For the Lord God loves mercy and truth; He shall give grace and glory; the Lord shall not withhold good things from those who walk in innocence.

**83:13** O Lord of hosts, blessed is the man who hopes in You.

## Translator Notes

- **83:1** — "For the end" is the standard Septuagint superscription corresponding to the Hebrew dedication to the choirmaster, and "concerning the winepresses" renders the Hebrew term al-haggittit by associating it with the word for winepress.
- **83:7** — The Greek translates the Hebrew place name Baca as "weeping" and renders the Hebrew term for early rain (moreh) as "the Lawgiver."
- **83:11** — Greek pararripteisthai ("to be cast aside") renders the Hebrew verb for standing at the threshold, and "thousands" is plural in the Greek.
- **83:12** — The Greek reads "the Lord God loves mercy and truth," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the LORD God is a sun and shield."
