## Text

**51:1** For the end; of understanding, of David,

**51:2** when Doeg the Edomite came and reported to Saul, and said to him, "David has come into the house of Abimelech."

**51:3** Why do you boast in wickedness, O mighty man, of iniquity all day long?

**51:4** Your tongue has devised unrighteousness; like a sharpened razor, you have practiced deceit.

**51:5** You have loved wickedness more than goodness, unrighteousness more than speaking righteousness. Interlude.

**51:6** You have loved all words of ruin, a deceitful tongue.

**51:7** Therefore God shall tear you down forever; may He pluck you up and banish you from the dwelling, and your root from the land of the living. Interlude.

**51:8** And the righteous shall see and fear, and shall laugh at him, and say,

**51:9** "Behold, a man who did not make God his helper, but set his hope upon the abundance of his riches, and grew strong in his vanity."

**51:10** But I am like a fruitful olive tree in the house of God; I have set my hope upon the mercy of God forever and ever.

**51:11** I will give thanks to You forever, because You have done it; and I will wait upon Your name, for it is good before Your holy ones.

## Translator Notes

- **51:2** — The Septuagint genuinely reads "Abimelech" here, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Ahimelech."
- **51:3** — The Septuagint reads "of iniquity all day long" as a further object of the mighty man's boasting, contrasting with the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "the steadfast love of God endures all the day."
- **51:5** — "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew musical or liturgical term Selah.
