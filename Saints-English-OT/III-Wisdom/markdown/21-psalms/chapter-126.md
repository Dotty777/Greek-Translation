## Text

**126:1** A Song of Ascents. Unless the Lord builds a house, those who build it toil in vain; unless the Lord guards a city, the watchman keeps vigil in vain.

**126:2** It is in vain for you to rise early, to rise up after sitting down, you who eat the bread of sorrow, when He gives sleep to His beloved ones.

**126:3** Behold, the heritage of the Lord is sons, the reward of the fruit of her womb.

**126:4** Like arrows in the hand of a mighty man, so are the sons of those who have been shaken out.

**126:5** Blessed is he who shall fulfill his desire from them; he shall not be put to shame when they speak to their enemies in the gate.

## Translator Notes

- **126:2** — Greek egeiresthai meta to kathesthai ("to rise up after sitting down") and hotan do tois agapetois autou ("when He gives... to His beloved ones") differ in sense and number from the Hebrew text.
- **126:3** — Greek reads the genitive tou karpou tes gastros autes ("of the fruit of her womb"), linking "the reward" directly to the offspring in apposition.
- **126:4** — Greek ton ektetinagmenon ("of those who have been shaken out") derives the underlying Hebrew word from the root meaning "to shake out" rather than "youth."
- **126:5** — Greek ten epithymian autou ("his desire") stands in place of the Hebrew "his quiver," and the Greek shifts from singular ("he shall not be put to shame") to plural ("when they speak to their enemies").
