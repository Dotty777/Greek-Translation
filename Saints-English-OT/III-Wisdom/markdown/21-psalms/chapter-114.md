## Text

**114:1** Hallelujah. I have loved, because God will hear the voice of my supplication,

**114:2** because He inclined His ear to me, and in His days I called out.

**114:3** The pangs of death encompassed me, the perils of Hades found me; I found affliction and sorrow.

**114:4** And I called upon the name of the Lord: "O Lord, deliver my soul."

**114:5** Merciful and righteous is the Lord, and the Lord our God shows mercy.

**114:6** The Lord guards the little ones; I was brought low, and He saved me.

**114:7** Return, my soul, to your rest, because the Lord has done good to you;

**114:8** because He rescued my soul from death, my eyes from tears, and my feet from slipping.

**114:9** I will be well-pleasing before the Lord in the land of the living.

## Translator Notes

- **114:1** — This Greek text reads "God" (ho theos) rather than "the Lord" as in other Septuagint witnesses, omits an explicit object after "I have loved," and uses the future tense "will hear."
- **114:2** — The Greek text reads "in His days" (en tais hemerais autou), differing from manuscripts and the Hebrew tradition that read "in my days."
- **114:6** — Greek ta nepia literally means "the infants" or "the little ones," rendering the Hebrew term for "the simple."
- **114:9** — Greek euaresteso ("I will be well-pleasing") renders the Hebrew idiom of walking before the Lord, echoing Septuagint usage in passages such as Genesis 5:22 and 5:24.
