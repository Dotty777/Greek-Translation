## Text

**62:1** A Psalm of David, when he was in the wilderness of Idumea.

**62:2** O God, my God, to You I rise at dawn; my soul has thirsted for You, how manifoldly my flesh also for You, in a desert land, trackless and waterless,

**62:3** thus in the sanctuary I appeared before You, to behold Your power and Your glory.

**62:4** For Your mercy is better than lives; my lips shall praise You.

**62:5** Thus I will bless You in my life; in Your name I will lift up my hands.

**62:6** As with marrow and richness may my soul be filled, and lips of exultation shall praise Your name.

**62:7** If I remembered You upon my bed, at my dawn I meditated upon You;

**62:8** for You have become my helper, and in the shelter of Your wings I will exult.

**62:9** My soul has clung closely behind You; Your right hand has upheld me.

**62:10** Yet they have sought my soul in vain; they shall enter into the lowest parts of the earth;

**62:11** they shall be delivered over into the hands of the sword; they shall be portions for foxes.

**62:12** But the king shall be glad in God; everyone who swears by Him shall be praised, for the mouth of those who speak unjust things has been stopped.

## Translator Notes

- **62:1** — The Septuagint reads "Idumea" (Edom), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Judah."
- **62:2** — Greek "posaplos" ("how manifoldly") reflects the Septuagint reading the Hebrew verb for "yearns" or "faints" as the interrogative "how many times."
- **62:3** — The Greek passive "I appeared before You" differs from the Masoretic Text's active "I have looked upon You."
- **62:7** — The Septuagint reads "at my dawn" ("en to orthro mou"), whereas the Masoretic Text has "in the night watches."
