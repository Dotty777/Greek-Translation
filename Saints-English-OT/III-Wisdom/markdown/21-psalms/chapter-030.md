## Text

**30:1** To the end: a Psalm of David, of alarm.

**30:2** In You, O Lord, I have hoped; may I never be put to shame; in Your righteousness deliver me and rescue me.

**30:3** Incline Your ear to me, hasten to rescue me; be to me a defending God and a house of refuge to save me.

**30:4** For You are my stronghold and my refuge, and for the sake of Your name You shall guide me and nourish me.

**30:5** You shall bring me out of this snare which they have hidden for me, for You are my defender.

**30:6** Into Your hands I shall commit my spirit; You have redeemed me, O Lord, the God of truth.

**30:7** You have hated those who regard vanities to no purpose, but I have hoped in the Lord.

**30:8** I will exult and rejoice in Your mercy, for You have looked upon my lowliness; You have saved my soul from distresses,

**30:9** and You have not shut me into the hands of an enemy; You have set my feet in a spacious place.

**30:10** Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am in distress; my eye is troubled with indignation, my soul and my belly.

**30:11** For my life has failed in sorrow, and my years in groanings; my strength has grown weak in poverty, and my bones are troubled.

**30:12** Beyond all my enemies I have become a reproach, and exceedingly so to my neighbors, and a dread to my acquaintances; those who saw me outside fled from me.

**30:13** I have been forgotten like a dead man from the heart; I have become like a lost vessel.

**30:14** For I have heard the reproach of many dwelling all around; while they gathered together against me, they conspired to take my life.

**30:15** But I have hoped in You, O Lord; I said, "You are my God."

**30:16** My lots are in Your hands; deliver me from the hand of my enemies and from those who pursue me.

**30:17** Cause Your face to shine upon Your servant; save me in Your mercy.

**30:18** O Lord, may I not be put to shame, for I have called upon You; let the ungodly be shamed and brought down to Hades.

**30:19** Let the deceitful lips become mute, which speak lawlessness against the righteous in pride and contempt.

**30:20** How great is the multitude of Your goodness, O Lord, which You have hidden for those who fear You; You have accomplished it for those who hope in You before the sons of men.

**30:21** You shall conceal them in the secret place of Your countenance from the turmoil of men; You shall shelter them in a tabernacle from the contradiction of tongues.

**30:22** Blessed is the Lord, for He has made His mercy marvelous in a fortified city.

**30:23** And I said in my alarm, "Then I have been cast away from the sight of Your eyes." Therefore You heard, O Lord, the voice of my supplication when I cried out to You.

**30:24** Love the Lord, all His holy ones, for the Lord seeks out truths, and He repays those who act with excessive pride.

**30:25** Be courageous, and let your heart be strengthened, all you who hope in the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — The Septuagint superscription adds "of alarm" (Greek ekstaseos, also translated "astonishment" or "ecstasy"), echoing the psalmist's cry "in my alarm" in verse twenty-three.
- **30:7** — The Septuagint has the second-person verb "You have hated" (Greek emisesas), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew reads first-person "I hate."
- **30:11** — Greek en ptochia ("in poverty") reflects a different reading of the Hebrew consonants from the Masoretic text's "because of my iniquity."
- **30:16** — The Septuagint reads "my lots" (Greek hoi kleroi mou, meaning one's appointed portions or destiny), where the Masoretic Hebrew has "my times."
- **30:23** — The Septuagint joins the clauses with "Therefore" (Greek dia touto) rather than "Yet" or "Nevertheless," presenting the Lord's hearing as the direct response to the psalmist's alarmed plea.
