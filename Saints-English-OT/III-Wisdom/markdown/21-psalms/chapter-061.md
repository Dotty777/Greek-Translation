## Text

**61:1** For the end, for Jeduthun; a psalm of David.

**61:2** Shall not my soul be subject to God? For from Him is my salvation.

**61:3** For He indeed is my God and my Savior, my protector; I shall by no means be shaken any further.

**61:4** How long will you assail a man? All of you slay, as against a leaning wall and a pushed-in fence.

**61:5** Yet they took counsel to thrust away my honor; they ran in thirst; with their mouth they blessed, and in their heart they cursed. Interlude.

**61:6** Yet be subject to God, O my soul, for from Him is my endurance.

**61:7** For He is my God and my Savior, my protector; I shall by no means be moved away.

**61:8** In God is my salvation and my glory; the God of my help, and my hope is in God.

**61:9** Hope in Him, every congregation of people; pour out your hearts before Him; God is our helper. Interlude.

**61:10** Yet the sons of men are vain; the sons of men are false in the balances to do injustice; they are of vanity altogether.

**61:11** Do not hope in injustice, and do not long for plunder; if wealth flows in, do not set your heart upon it.

**61:12** Once God has spoken; these two things I have heard: that power belongs to God,

**61:13** and to You, O Lord, belongs mercy; for You will render to each one according to his works.

## Translator Notes

- **61:2** — The Greek opens with a rhetorical question using "be subject to God" (hypotagesetai), contrasting with the Hebrew Masoretic Text's declarative "Truly my soul waits in silence."
- **61:5** — The Greek reads "they ran in thirst" (edramon en dipsei), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("they delight in lies").
- **61:9** — The Greek "every congregation of people" (pasa synagoge laou) reflects a different reading of the Hebrew consonants rendered "at all times" in the Masoretic Text.
