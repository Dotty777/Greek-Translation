## Text

**21:1** For the end, concerning the morning help: a psalm of David.

**21:2** O God, my God, give heed to me; why have You forsaken me? Far from my salvation are the words of my transgressions.

**21:3** O my God, I will cry out by day to You, and You will not hear; and by night, and it is not to my folly.

**21:4** Yet You dwell among the holy ones, O praise of Israel.

**21:5** In You our fathers hoped; they hoped, and You delivered them.

**21:6** To You they cried out and were saved; in You they hoped and were not put to shame.

**21:7** But I am a worm and not a man, a reproach of man and the scorn of the people.

**21:8** All who look upon me have mocked me; they spoke with their lips, they shook their head:

**21:9** "He hoped in the Lord; let Him deliver him; let Him save him, because He delights in him."

**21:10** For You are the One who drew me forth from the womb, my hope from the breasts of my mother.

**21:11** Upon You I was cast from the womb; from my mother's belly You are my God.

**21:12** Do not depart from me, for affliction is near, for there is none to help.

**21:13** Many young bulls have encircled me; fat bulls have hemmed me in.

**21:14** They opened their mouth against me, like a ravenous and roaring lion.

**21:15** Like water I was poured out, and all my bones were scattered; my heart became like wax melting in the midst of my belly.

**21:16** My strength dried up like a potsherd, and my tongue clings to my throat; and into the dust of death You have brought me down.

**21:17** For many dogs have surrounded me; an assembly of evildoers has enclosed me; they pierced my hands and feet.

**21:18** They numbered all my bones; and they themselves observed and looked upon me.

**21:19** They divided my garments among themselves, and upon my apparel they cast a lot.

**21:20** But You, O Lord, do not put my help far away; attend to my aid.

**21:21** Deliver my soul from the sword, and my only one from the hand of the dog.

**21:22** Save me from the mouth of the lion, and my lowliness from the horns of the unicorns.

**21:23** I will declare Your name to my brethren; in the midst of the assembly I will sing hymns to You.

**21:24** You who fear the Lord, praise Him; all the seed of Jacob, glorify Him; let all the seed of Israel fear Him.

**21:25** For He has neither despised nor disdained the supplication of the poor, nor has He turned His face away from me; and when I cried out to Him, He heard me.

**21:26** From You comes my praise in the great assembly; my vows I will fulfill before those who fear Him.

**21:27** The needy shall eat and be filled, and those who diligently seek Him shall praise the Lord; their hearts shall live forever and ever.

**21:28** All the ends of the earth shall remember and turn back to the Lord, and all the families of the nations shall worship before Him;

**21:29** for the kingdom is the Lord's, and He Himself exercises dominion over the nations.

**21:30** All the prosperous of the earth ate and worshiped; before Him shall fall down all who descend into the earth; and my soul lives for Him.

**21:31** And my seed shall serve Him; the coming generation shall be proclaimed to the Lord,

**21:32** and they shall proclaim His righteousness to a people yet to be born, whom the Lord has made.

## Translator Notes

- **21:2** — Greek includes "give heed to me" (προσχες μοι) and reads "the words of my transgressions" (οι λογοι των παραπτωματων μου), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew reads "the words of my groaning."
- **21:4** — Greek εν αγιοις ("among the holy ones") may also be rendered "in the holy places" (the sanctuary), corresponding to Hebrew "holy one."
- **21:17** — Greek ωρυξαν literally means "they dug" (traditionally rendered "pierced"), representing a famous Septuagint divergence from the Masoretic Hebrew "like a lion."
- **21:18** — Greek has third-person plural "They numbered" (εξηριθμησαν), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew reads first-person singular "I can count."
- **21:22** — Greek μονοκερωτων literally means "unicorns" (one-horned beasts, rendering Hebrew "wild oxen") and concludes with "my lowliness" (την ταπεινωσιν μου), where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "You have answered me."
