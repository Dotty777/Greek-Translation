## Text

**11:1** For the end, concerning the eighth; a psalm of David.

**11:2** Save me, O Lord, for the godly one has failed, for truths have diminished from among the sons of men.

**11:3** Each one has spoken vain things to his neighbor; deceitful lips have spoken with a heart and a heart.

**11:4** May the Lord utterly destroy all deceitful lips and the boastful tongue,

**11:5** those who have said, "We will magnify our tongue; our lips are our own; who is lord over us?"

**11:6** "Because of the misery of the poor and because of the groaning of the needy, now I will arise," says the Lord; "I will set him in salvation; I will act boldly on his behalf."

**11:7** The oracles of the Lord are pure oracles, silver tried by fire, tested in the earth, purified sevenfold.

**11:8** You, O Lord, shall guard us, and You shall preserve us from this generation and forever.

**11:9** On every side the ungodly walk; according to Your height, You have greatly cared for the sons of men.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — "Concerning the eighth" (hyper tes ogdoes) renders the Hebrew musical heading al-hasheminith, often understood as an eight-stringed instrument or octave, or in early Christian interpretation as the eschatological eighth day.
- **11:3** — "With a heart and a heart" literally reproduces the Greek Hebraism (en kardia kai en kardia) denoting a double or duplicitous heart.
- **11:6** — The Greek verbs thesomai ("I will set [him]") and parresiasomai en auto ("I will act boldly on his behalf," or "speak openly concerning him") assume a singular implied object referring to the poor and needy person just named.
- **11:9** — Differing sharply from the Hebrew ("when vileness is exalted among the sons of men"), the Greek reads "according to Your height, You have greatly cared for (epolyoresas) the sons of men."
