## Text

**13:1** For the end; a psalm of David. A foolish man has said in his heart, "There is no God." They have become corrupt and abominable in their practices; there is no one who does good, there is not even one.

**13:2** The Lord looked down from heaven upon the sons of men, to see whether there is anyone who understands or who earnestly seeks God.

**13:3** All have turned aside; together they have become worthless; there is no one who does good, there is not even one. Their throat is an open tomb; with their tongues they have practiced deceit; the venom of asps is beneath their lips, whose mouth is full of cursing and bitterness. Their feet are swift to shed blood; ruin and wretchedness are in their ways, and they have not known the way of peace. There is no fear of God before their eyes.

**13:4** Will all who work unrighteousness not come to know, who devour My people as they eat bread? They have not called upon the Lord.

**13:5** There they trembled with fear where there was no fear, for God is in a righteous generation.

**13:6** You have put to shame the counsel of the poor, because the Lord is his hope.

**13:7** Who will give from Zion the salvation of Israel? When the Lord turns back the captivity of His people, let Jacob exult and let Israel be glad.

## Translator Notes

- **13:1** — The Greek superscription "For the end" (eis to telos) renders the Hebrew musical heading often translated "To the choirmaster" and was frequently read in an eschatological sense. Both here and in verse 3, the Septuagint adds "there is not even one."
- **13:3** — The Septuagint includes an extended indictment of human depravity gathered from other scriptural passages (cf. Psalms 5:10; 139:4; 9:28; Isaiah 59:7-8; Psalm 35:2) that is quoted by the apostle Paul in Romans 3:13-18; the Hebrew Masoretic Text ends the verse after "not even one."
- **13:5** — The Septuagint includes the clause "where there was no fear" (paralleling Psalm 52:6 LXX / 53:5 MT), which is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text of Psalm 14:5.
