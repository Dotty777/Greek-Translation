## Text

**143:1** Of David, against Goliath. Blessed be the Lord my God, who teaches my hands for battle array, my fingers for war,

**143:2** my mercy and my refuge, my protector and my deliverer, my shield—and in Him I have hoped—who subdues my people under me.

**143:3** O Lord, what is man, that You were made known to him, or a son of man, that You take account of him?

**143:4** Man has been likened to vanity; his days pass by like a shadow.

**143:5** O Lord, incline Your heavens and come down; touch the mountains, and they shall smoke.

**143:6** Flash forth lightning, and You shall scatter them; send forth Your arrows, and You shall confound them.

**143:7** Send forth Your hand from on high; rescue me and deliver me out of many waters, out of the hand of foreign sons,

**143:8** whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of unrighteousness.

**143:9** O God, I will sing a new song to You; on a ten-stringed psaltery I will sing praises to You,

**143:10** who gives salvation to kings, who redeems David His servant. From an evil sword

**143:11** deliver me, and rescue me out of the hand of foreign sons, whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of unrighteousness;

**143:12** whose sons are like young plants grown sturdy in their youth, their daughters beautified, adorned all around after the likeness of a temple;

**143:13** their storehouses are full, overflowing from this into that; their sheep bear abundantly, multiplying in their goings out,

**143:14** their oxen are fat; there is no collapse of a fence, nor breaking out, nor outcry in their courtyards.

**143:15** They called blessed the people to whom these things belong; blessed is the people whose God is the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **143:1** — The superscription "against Goliath" is unique to the Septuagint; the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads only "Of David."
- **143:3** — The Greek passive verb literally reads "that You were made known to him," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "that You know him."
- **143:10** — In the Greek text, "From an evil sword" begins the petition completed by "deliver me" in verse 11, rather than modifying "redeems David His servant" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **143:12** — The Septuagint reads "whose sons" and continues with third-person plural pronouns through verse 14, connecting to verse 15 ("They called blessed"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text shifts to first-person plural ("that our sons").
