## Text

**35:1** For the end; of the servant of the Lord, David.

**35:2** The lawless man speaks within himself of sinning; there is no fear of God before his eyes.

**35:3** For he has acted deceitfully in his own sight, to discover his iniquity and to hate it.

**35:4** The words of his mouth are iniquity and deceit; he was unwilling to understand so as to do good.

**35:5** He devised iniquity upon his bed; he took his stand on every way that is not good, and he did not abhor wickedness.

**35:6** O Lord, Your mercy is in heaven, and Your truth reaches to the clouds.

**35:7** And Your righteousness is like the mountains of God; Your judgments are like a great deep. Men and beasts You shall save, O Lord,

**35:8** as You have multiplied Your mercy, O God; and the sons of men shall hope in the shelter of Your wings.

**35:9** They shall drink their fill from the fatness of Your house, and You shall give them to drink from the torrent of Your delight.

**35:10** For with You is the fountain of life; in Your light we shall see light.

**35:11** Extend Your mercy to those who know You, and Your righteousness to the upright in heart.

**35:12** Do not let the foot of pride come against me, and do not let the hand of sinners shake me.

**35:13** There all the workers of iniquity have fallen; they have been thrust out and shall by no means be able to stand.

## Translator Notes

- **35:1** — "For the end" is the standard Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew liturgical heading, understood in Greek tradition as pointing toward fulfillment or consummation.
- **35:3** — The Greek phrase rendered "in his own sight" may alternatively be read as "before Him" (referring to God), and the following infinitive clause ("to discover his iniquity and to hate it") preserves the compact and debated syntax of the source text.
- **35:8** — The Septuagint reads "as You have multiplied Your mercy," linked syntactically to the end of verse seven (though the opening particle may also be read exclamatorily as "How You have multiplied..."), where the Masoretic Text has "How precious is your steadfast love."
