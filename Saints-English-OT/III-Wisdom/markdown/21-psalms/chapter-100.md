## Text

**100:1** A Psalm of David. Of mercy and judgment I will sing to You, O Lord.

**100:2** I will sing praises and have understanding in a blameless song; when will You come to me? I walked in the innocence of my heart, in the midst of my house.

**100:3** I did not set before my eyes any lawless thing; I hated those who commit transgressions; a crooked heart did not cling to me.

**100:4** When the wicked one turned aside from me, I did not know him.

**100:5** The one who secretly slandered his neighbor, him I drove out; one of haughty eye and insatiable heart, with him I did not eat.

**100:6** My eyes were upon the faithful of the land, that they might sit with me; the one who walked in a blameless way, he ministered to me.

**100:7** He who practiced arrogance did not dwell in the midst of my house; he who spoke unjust things did not prosper before my eyes.

**100:8** Morning by morning I slew all the sinners of the land, that I might utterly destroy from the city of the Lord all who work iniquity.

## Translator Notes

- **100:2** — The Greek text here reads "in a blameless song" (en ode amomo, as in Codex Vaticanus), whereas verse 6 and other Greek witnesses read "in a blameless way" (en hodo amomo). Beginning with "I walked," the Septuagint renders the royal declarations of verses 2–8 in past (imperfect and aorist) tenses rather than the future tenses typical of Masoretic translations.
- **100:4** — In the Greek versification, "a crooked heart did not cling to me" concludes verse 3, while verse 4 opens with a genitive absolute ("When the wicked one turned aside from me") in which tou ponerou may be taken as either masculine ("the wicked one") or neuter ("evil").
- **100:5** — The Greek "I did not eat" (ou synesthion) reflects a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonantal root from the Masoretic reading usually translated "I will not endure."
- **100:7** — The Greek reads "practiced arrogance" (poion hyperephanian), where the Masoretic Hebrew (Psalm 101:7) reads "practices deceit."
