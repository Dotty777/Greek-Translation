## Text

**15:1** A pillar inscription of David. Guard me, O Lord, for I have put my hope in You.

**15:2** I said to the Lord, "You are my Lord."

**15:3** For the saints who are in His land, He has made wondrous all His desires in them.

**15:4** Their infirmities were multiplied; after these things they hastened. I will by no means gather their assemblies of blood, nor will I ever make mention of their names through my lips.

**15:5** The Lord is the portion of my inheritance and of my cup; You are the One who restores my inheritance to me.

**15:6** Measuring lines have fallen to me in the finest places; for indeed my inheritance is finest to me.

**15:7** I will bless the Lord, who has given me understanding; moreover, even until night my inward parts have instructed me.

**15:8** I continually beheld the Lord before me, because He is at my right hand, so that I may not be shaken.

**15:9** For this reason my heart was glad and my tongue exulted; furthermore, my flesh also shall dwell in hope.

**15:10** For You will not abandon my soul to Hades, nor will You permit Your Holy One to see decay.

**15:11** You have made known to me the paths of life; You will fill me with gladness before Your countenance; delights are in Your right hand to the end.

## Translator Notes

- **15:1** — The Greek superscription stelographia ("pillar inscription") is the Septuagint rendering of the obscure Hebrew musical or literary term miktam.
- **15:2** — The critical Greek text provided here consists solely of "I said to the Lord, 'You are my Lord,'" without the additional clause ("for You have no need of my goods") present in some Septuagint manuscripts and corresponding to the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **15:3** — Whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks in the first person of the psalmist's delight in the saints, the Septuagint uses third-person forms ("His land," "He has made wondrous," "His desires"), making the Lord the subject.
- **15:4** — The Septuagint renders the difficult Hebrew as "Their infirmities were multiplied; after these things they hastened" and "gather their assemblies of blood," differing notably from the Masoretic Text's reference to the sorrows of those who hasten after another god and pouring out drink offerings of blood.
- **15:9** — The Septuagint reads "my tongue" (he glossa mou) and "in hope" (ep' elpidi) rather than the Masoretic Text's "my glory" and "in security"; verses 8-11 are quoted in this Greek form in Acts 2:25-28.
