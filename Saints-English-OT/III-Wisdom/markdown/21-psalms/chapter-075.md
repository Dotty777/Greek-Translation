## Text

**75:1** For the end, with hymns; a psalm for Asaph, a song, concerning the Assyrian.

**75:2** God is known in Judea; in Israel His name is great.

**75:3** And His place was in peace, and His dwelling place in Zion;

**75:4** there He shattered the powers of the bows, shield and sword and battle; there He will break the horns. Interlude.

**75:5** You shine forth wondrously from the everlasting mountains.

**75:6** All the foolish of heart were troubled; they slept their sleep, and all the men of wealth found nothing in their hands.

**75:7** At Your rebuke, O God of Jacob, those who mounted the horses slumbered.

**75:8** You are to be feared, and who shall withstand You because of Your wrath?

**75:9** From heaven You hurled forth judgment; the earth feared and was still,

**75:10** when God arose to judgment, to save all the meek in heart. Interlude.

**75:11** For the thought of man shall give thanks to You, and the remnant of thought shall keep a feast to You.

**75:12** Make vows and pay them to the Lord our God; all who are around Him shall bring gifts

**75:13** to Him who is to be feared and who takes away the spirits of princes, feared among the kings of the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **75:1** — The Greek superscription adds "concerning the Assyrian," a historical heading absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text that links the psalm to the deliverance of Jerusalem from Sennacherib.
- **75:3** — The Greek renders Hebrew be-Shalem ("in Salem") as "in peace" (en eirene).
- **75:4** — This Greek text includes the additional final clause "there He will break the horns" (echoing 74:11), which is absent from the Masoretic Text and some Septuagint manuscripts.
- **75:9** — The Greek text reads ekontisas ("You hurled forth," as with a javelin), a notable early manuscript reading alongside the variant ekoutisas ("You caused to be heard").
- **75:11** — The Greek reads "the thought of man shall give thanks to You, and the remnant of thought shall keep a feast to You," differing markedly from the Masoretic Text's reference to human wrath.
