## Text

**136:1** Of David. By the rivers of Babylon, there we sat down, and we wept as we remembered Zion.

**136:2** Upon the willows in its midst we hung our instruments.

**136:3** For there those who took us captive asked of us words of songs, and those who led us away said, "Sing hymns to us from the songs of Zion."

**136:4** How shall we sing the song of the Lord upon a foreign land?

**136:5** If I forget you, O Jerusalem, may my right hand be forgotten;

**136:6** may my tongue cling to my throat if I do not remember you, if I do not set Jerusalem foremost at the head of my gladness.

**136:7** Remember, O Lord, the sons of Edom on the day of Jerusalem, who said, "Empty it, empty it, even to the foundation in it."

**136:8** O wretched daughter of Babylon, blessed is he who will repay you the repayment that you repaid to us;

**136:9** blessed is he who will seize and dash your infants against the rock.

## Translator Notes

- **136:1** — The Greek includes the superscription "Of David," which is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **136:2** — The Greek organa refers to musical instruments generally rather than specifically harps or lyres as in the Hebrew text.
- **136:5** — The Greek uses the passive optative ("may my right hand be forgotten"), differing from the active sense in the Hebrew text ("let my right hand forget [its skill]").
- **136:8** — The Greek calls Babylon "wretched" (he talaiporos), rendering the underlying Hebrew as "miserable" or "devastated" rather than "doomed to destruction."
