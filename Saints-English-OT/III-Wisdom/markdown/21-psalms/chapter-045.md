## Text

**45:1** For the end; for the sons of Korah, a psalm concerning hidden things.

**45:2** Our God is a refuge and strength, a helper in the tribulations that have found us exceedingly.

**45:3** Therefore we shall not fear when the earth is troubled, and mountains are removed into the hearts of the seas.

**45:4** Their waters roared and were troubled; the mountains were troubled in His might. Interlude.

**45:5** The rushing streams of the river make glad the city of God; the Most High has sanctified His tabernacle.

**45:6** God is in her midst; she shall not be shaken; God shall help her with His countenance.

**45:7** Nations were troubled, kingdoms gave way; He gave forth His voice, the earth was shaken.

**45:8** The Lord of hosts is with us; our protector is the God of Jacob. Interlude.

**45:9** Come, see the works of the Lord, what wonders He has set upon the earth,

**45:10** doing away with wars to the ends of the earth; He shall shatter the bow and break the weapon in pieces, and shields He shall burn up in fire.

**45:11** "Rest, and know that I am God; I shall be exalted among the nations, I shall be exalted in the earth."

**45:12** The Lord of hosts is with us; our protector is the God of Jacob.

## Translator Notes

- **45:1** — The phrase "concerning hidden things" (hyper ton kryphion) reflects the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew musical term alamoth from a root meaning "to hide" or "conceal."
- **45:2** — The Greek active participle "that have found us exceedingly" takes "tribulations" as its subject overtaking the psalmist, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of God as "a very present help."
- **45:6** — The reading "with His countenance" (to prosopo) is preserved in major Septuagint uncials including Vaticanus and Sinaiticus, whereas other Greek witnesses read to pros proi ("at the break of dawn"), aligning with the Hebrew.
- **45:10** — The Septuagint reads thyreous ("shields," referring to large oblong shields), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "chariots."
