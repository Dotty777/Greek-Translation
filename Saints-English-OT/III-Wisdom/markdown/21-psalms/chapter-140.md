## Text

**140:1** A Psalm of David. O Lord, to You I have cried out; hear me; attend to the voice of my supplication when I cry out to You.

**140:2** Let my prayer be directed as incense before You, the lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice.

**140:3** Place a guard, O Lord, over my mouth, and a door of enclosure around my lips.

**140:4** Do not turn my heart aside to words of wickedness, to devise excuses in sins with men who work lawlessness; and by no means shall I join with their chosen ones.

**140:5** A righteous man shall chasten me in mercy and reprove me, but do not let the oil of a sinner anoint my head; for yet again and again my prayer is in their good pleasures.

**140:6** Their mighty ones have been swallowed up beside a rock; they shall hear my words, for they have prevailed.

**140:7** As a clod of earth is broken apart upon the earth, our bones have been scattered beside Hades.

**140:8** For toward You, O Lord, Lord, are my eyes; in You I have put my hope; do not take away my soul.

**140:9** Keep me from the snare which they have set for me, and from the stumbling blocks of those who work lawlessness.

**140:10** Sinners shall fall into his net; I am alone until I pass by.

## Translator Notes

- **140:4** — The verb translated "join with" reflects Greek syndoiaso (a spelling of syndyazo, "associate or consort with"), and "their chosen ones" renders ton eklekton auton, which can also denote "their choice delicacies."
- **140:5** — Literally "for yet again and again my prayer is in their good pleasures," preserving the Septuagint reading eudokiais ("good pleasures") rather than harmonizing to "evil deeds."
- **140:6** — The Greek text reads edynethesan ("they have prevailed" or "were able"), a textual variant alongside hedynthesan ("they were sweetened" or "pleasant").
- **140:10** — The singular pronoun in "his net" (autou) follows the Greek wording literally and may refer either to the sinner's own snare or to a net of divine judgment.
