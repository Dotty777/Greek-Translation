## Text

**7:1** A psalm of David, which he sang to the Lord concerning the words of Chusi, the son of Jemini.

**7:2** O Lord my God, in You I have put my hope; save me from all those who pursue me, and deliver me,

**7:3** lest he snatch away my soul like a lion, while there is no one to redeem or to save.

**7:4** O Lord my God, if I have done this, if there is unrighteousness in my hands,

**7:5** if I have repaid evil to those who repay me evil, then indeed let me fall away empty from my enemies;

**7:6** then let the enemy pursue my soul and overtake it, and trample my life down to the earth, and make my glory dwell in the dust. Interlude.

**7:7** Arise, O Lord, in Your wrath; be exalted in the borders of my enemies; awake, O Lord my God, in the decree which You have commanded.

**7:8** And the assembly of peoples shall surround You; and above it, return on high.

**7:9** The Lord shall judge the peoples; judge me, O Lord, according to my righteousness and according to my innocence that is upon me.

**7:10** Let the wickedness of sinners come to an end, and You shall guide the righteous straight; God examines hearts and inward parts.

**7:11** Righteous is my help from God, who saves the upright in heart.

**7:12** God is a righteous Judge, strong and longsuffering, not bringing wrath every day.

**7:13** If you do not turn back, He will burnish His sword; He has bent His bow and made it ready;

**7:14** and with it He has prepared weapons of death; He has fashioned His arrows for those who burn.

**7:15** Behold, he travailed with lawlessness; he conceived trouble and brought forth unrighteousness.

**7:16** He dug a pit and hollowed it out, and he shall fall into the ditch which he has made.

**7:17** His trouble shall return upon his own head, and upon the crown of his head his unrighteousness shall descend.

**7:18** I will give thanks to the Lord according to His righteousness, and I will sing praise to the name of the Lord Most High.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The Greek superscription names "Chusi, the son of Jemini," corresponding to "Cush, a Benjamite" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **7:5** — The Greek reads "then indeed let me fall away empty from my enemies," whereas the Hebrew text speaks of plundering or rescuing an adversary without cause.
- **7:6** — Greek diapsalma denotes a musical interlude or pause in the psalm, corresponding to Hebrew Selah.
- **7:12** — The Greek includes "strong and longsuffering" and adds the negation "not bringing wrath every day," contrasting with the Hebrew text's "indignant every day."
- **7:14** — Greek tois kaiomenois ("for those who burn") reflects a Hebrew participle that can denote either fiery shafts or hot pursuers.
