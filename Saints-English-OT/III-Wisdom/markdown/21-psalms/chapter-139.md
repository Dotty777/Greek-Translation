## Text

**139:1** For the end; a psalm of David.

**139:2** Deliver me, O Lord, from an evil man; from an unrighteous man rescue me,

**139:3** who devised injustices in the heart; all the day long they arrayed battles;

**139:4** they sharpened their tongue like that of a serpent; the venom of asps is under their lips. Interlude.

**139:5** Keep me, O Lord, from the hand of a sinner; from unrighteous men deliver me, who have plotted to trip up my steps;

**139:6** the proud have hidden a snare for me, and stretched out cords, snares for my feet; alongside the path they have set a stumbling block for me. Interlude.

**139:7** I said to the Lord, "You are my God"; give ear, O Lord, to the voice of my supplication.

**139:8** O Lord, Lord, strength of my salvation, You have overshadowed my head in the day of battle.

**139:9** Do not deliver me up, O Lord, from my desire to a sinner; they have plotted against me; do not forsake me, lest they ever be exalted. Interlude.

**139:10** The head of their encirclement—the toil of their lips shall cover them.

**139:11** Coals of fire shall fall upon them on the earth, and You shall cast them down. In miseries they shall by no means endure;

**139:12** evils shall hunt down an unrighteous man to destruction.

**139:13** I know that the Lord will execute the judgment of the poor and the justice of the needy.

**139:14** Surely the righteous shall give thanks to Your name, and the upright shall dwell in Your presence.

## Translator Notes

- **139:4** — "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma (also in verses 6 and 9), the Septuagint rendering of Hebrew selah, denoting a musical or liturgical pause.
- **139:9** — The Greek reads "from my desire" (apo tes epithymias mou), taking the noun as first-person singular rather than "the desires of the wicked" as in the Masoretic Text.
- **139:12** — In this Greek text (as in Codex Vaticanus), "on the earth" stands in verse 11 and verse 12 omits the opening clause "a talkative man shall not be established" found in other Greek witnesses and the Masoretic Text.
