## Text

**91:1** A Psalm of a Song, for the day of the Sabbath.

**91:2** It is a good thing to give thanks to the Lord, and to sing psalms to Your name, O Most High,

**91:3** to proclaim Your mercy in the morning, and Your truth by night,

**91:4** upon a ten-stringed psaltery, with song upon the lyre.

**91:5** For You have made me glad, O Lord, in Your handiwork, and in the works of Your hands I will exult.

**91:6** How magnified are Your works, O Lord! Exceedingly weighty are Your thoughts.

**91:7** A foolish man shall not know, and one without understanding shall not understand these things.

**91:8** When sinners spring up like grass, and all who work iniquity have peered forth, it is so that they may be utterly destroyed forever and ever.

**91:9** But You are Most High forever, O Lord.

**91:10** For behold, Your enemies shall perish, and all who work iniquity shall be scattered abroad.

**91:11** And my horn shall be exalted like that of a unicorn, and my old age shall be in rich oil.

**91:12** And my eye has looked upon my enemies, and my ear shall hear concerning the evildoers who rise up against me.

**91:13** A righteous man shall blossom like a palm tree; like the cedar that is in Lebanon he shall be multiplied.

**91:14** Planted in the house of the Lord, they shall blossom forth in the courts of our God;

**91:15** then they shall be multiplied in a rich old age, and they shall be thriving,

**91:16** to proclaim that the Lord my God is upright, and there is no unrighteousness in Him.

## Translator Notes

- **91:6** — This Greek text reads "weighty" (ebarynthesan) rather than the variant "deep" (ebathynthesan) found in other Greek manuscripts and the Masoretic Text.
- **91:8** — The verb "peered forth" (diekysan) literally denotes peeping or looking out, reflecting a different sense of the Hebrew root translated "blossom" or "flourish" in the Masoretic Text.
- **91:11** — "Unicorn" translates Greek monokerotos ("one-horned beast"), the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew wild ox; "my old age" also reflects a distinct Septuagint reading where the Masoretic Text has "I have been anointed."
- **91:15** — This Greek text begins with "then" (tote) rather than the variant "still" (eti), and verse 16 reads "the Lord my God" where the Masoretic Text reads "my rock."
