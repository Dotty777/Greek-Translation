## Text

**27:1** Of David. To You, O Lord, I have cried aloud; O my God, do not remain silent toward me, lest You remain silent toward me and I become like those who go down into the pit.

**27:2** Hear the voice of my supplication when I make supplication to You, when I lift up my hands toward Your holy temple.

**27:3** Do not drag away my soul with sinners, nor destroy me together with those who work injustice, who speak peace with their neighbors, while evil things are in their hearts.

**27:4** Give to them according to their works and according to the wickedness of their practices; according to the works of their hands give to them; render to them their recompense.

**27:5** Because they did not understand the works of the Lord and the works of His hands, You shall tear them down and never build them up.

**27:6** Blessed be the Lord, for He has heard the voice of my supplication.

**27:7** The Lord is my helper and my defender; in Him my heart hoped, and I was helped, and my flesh blossomed anew; and of my own will I will give praise to Him.

**27:8** The Lord is the strength of His people, and He is the defender of the salvations of His anointed.

**27:9** Save Your people and bless Your inheritance, and shepherd them and lift them up forever.

## Translator Notes

- **27:1** — The Greek addresses God as "O my God," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "my rock."
- **27:5** — The Greek shifts to the second person ("You shall tear them down and never build them up"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text uses the third person ("He").
- **27:7** — "My flesh blossomed anew" and "of my own will" are distinctive Septuagint readings where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "my heart exults" and "with my song."
- **27:8** — The Greek reads "of His people," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "for them"; "salvations" reflects the Greek plural soterion.
