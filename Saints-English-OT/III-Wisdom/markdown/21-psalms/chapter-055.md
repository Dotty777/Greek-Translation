## Text

**55:1** For the end, concerning the people removed far from the holy places; for David, for a pillar inscription, when the foreigners seized him in Gath.

**55:2** Have mercy on me, O Lord, for man has trampled me down; warring all the day long, he has afflicted me.

**55:3** My enemies have trampled me down all the day long from the height of the day; for many who make war against me

**55:4** shall be afraid, but as for me, I will hope in You.

**55:5** In God I will praise my words all the day long; in God I have hoped; I will not fear what flesh shall do to me.

**55:6** All the day long they abhorred my words; against me are all their thoughts for evil.

**55:7** They shall dwell near and hide themselves; they shall watch my heel, just as I waited in my soul;

**55:8** For nothingness will You save them? In wrath You shall bring down peoples. O God,

**55:9** I have declared my life to You; You have set my tears before You, even as in Your promise.

**55:10** My enemies shall turn backward in whatever day I call upon You; behold, I know that You are my God.

**55:11** In God I will praise an utterance; in God I will praise a word.

**55:12** In God I have hoped; I will not fear what man shall do to me.

**55:13** In me, O God, are the vows of praise that I will pay;

**55:14** for You have delivered my soul from death, and my feet from slipping, that I may be well-pleasing before God in the light of the living.

## Translator Notes

- **55:1** — "The foreigners" translates Greek "hoi allophyloi," the standard Septuagint term for the Philistines; "pillar inscription" translates "stelographian," the Greek rendering of Hebrew "miktam."
- **55:3** — The Greek text places "from the height of the day" ("apo hypsous hemeras") here and links "for many who make war against me" directly to the third-person plural verb "shall be afraid" ("phobethesontai") in 55:4.
- **55:7** — The Greek text reads first-person singular "just as I waited in my soul" ("kathaper hypemeina te psyche mou"), differing from the third-person plural ("they waited for my soul") in some witnesses and the Hebrew.
- **55:8** — "For nothingness will You save them?" renders the Greek "hyper tou methenos soseis autous" as a rhetorical question, reflecting an emphatic denial that God will rescue the wicked.
- **55:9** — The Greek reads "my life," "before You," and "in Your promise," where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "my wanderings," "in Your bottle," and "in Your book."
