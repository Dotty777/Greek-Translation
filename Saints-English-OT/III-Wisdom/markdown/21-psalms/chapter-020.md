## Text

**20:1** For the end: a psalm of David.

**20:2** O Lord, in Your strength the king shall be glad, and in Your salvation he shall exult exceedingly.

**20:3** You have given him the desire of his soul, and You have not deprived him of the supplication of his lips. Interlude.

**20:4** For You went before him with blessings of goodness; You placed upon his head a crown of precious stone.

**20:5** He asked life of You, and You gave it to him—length of days forever and ever.

**20:6** Great is his glory in Your salvation; glory and majesty You shall lay upon him;

**20:7** for You shall give him blessing forever and ever; You shall gladden him in joy with Your countenance.

**20:8** For the king hopes in the Lord, and in the mercy of the Most High he shall never be shaken.

**20:9** May Your hand be found upon all Your enemies; may Your right hand find all who hate You.

**20:10** You shall make them like a furnace of fire at the time of Your countenance; O Lord, in Your wrath You shall confound them, and fire shall devour them.

**20:11** Their fruit You shall destroy from the earth, and their seed from among the sons of men.

**20:12** For they turned evil things against You; they devised a counsel which they shall by no means be able to establish.

**20:13** For You shall make them turn their back; among Your remnants You shall prepare their face.

**20:14** Be exalted, O Lord, in Your strength; we shall sing and chant Your mighty deeds.

## Translator Notes

- **20:4** — Greek reads "a crown of precious stone" rather than the Masoretic Text's "a crown of pure gold."
- **20:10** — The Septuagint addresses the Lord directly here ("O Lord, in Your wrath You shall confound them"), treating verses 9–14 as a unified address to God, whereas the Masoretic Text shifts to the third person.
- **20:13** — "Make them turn their back" renders the literal idiom "make them a back," meaning to put them to flight. "Among Your remnants" translates Greek en tois periloipois sou, reflecting the Hebrew root for "remnant" rather than the Masoretic Text's "on Your bowstrings."
