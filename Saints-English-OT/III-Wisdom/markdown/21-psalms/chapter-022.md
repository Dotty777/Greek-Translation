## Text

**22:1** A Psalm for David. The Lord shepherds me, and nothing shall cause me to want.

**22:2** In a place of tender grass, there He made me dwell; beside water of rest He nurtured me;

**22:3** He restored my soul. He guided me along paths of righteousness for His name's sake.

**22:4** For even though I walk in the midst of the shadow of death, I will fear no evils, for You are with me; Your rod and Your staff, they have comforted me.

**22:5** You have prepared before me a table in the presence of those who afflict me; You have anointed my head with oil, and Your cup is intoxicating as the choicest.

**22:6** And Your mercy shall pursue me all the days of my life, and my dwelling shall be in the house of the Lord for length of days.

## Translator Notes

- **22:1** — Numbered Psalm 22 in the Septuagint (Psalm 23 in Hebrew and most English Bibles). Greek poimainei is a verb ('shepherds'), followed by 'nothing shall cause me to want.'
- **22:2** — Greek kateskenosen ('made to dwell/encamp') and exethrepsen ('nurtured/reared') are aorist narrative verbs carried through verses 2-4.
- **22:4** — The Greek reads 'in the midst of the shadow of death' (en meso skias thanatou) without the word 'valley,' and plural kaka ('evils').
- **22:5** — Greek methyskon hos kratiston literally reads 'intoxicating/gladdening as the mightiest (or choicest),' reflecting the Septuagint's rendering of the Hebrew for an overflowing cup.
- **22:6** — The Septuagint reads 'Your mercy' alone (without 'goodness'), uses the forceful verb katadioxetai ('shall pursue'), and renders the closing clause with the articular infinitive 'my dwelling.'
