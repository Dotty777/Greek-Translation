# Psalm 107 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**107:1** A song of a psalm of David.

**107:2** My heart is ready, O God, my heart is ready; I will sing and make melody in my glory.

**107:3** Awake, psaltery and lyre; I will awake at dawn.

**107:4** I will give thanks to You among the peoples, O Lord, and I will sing praise to You among the nations;

**107:5** for Your mercy is great above the heavens, and Your truth reaches to the clouds.

**107:6** Be exalted above the heavens, O God, and let Your glory be over all the earth.

**107:7** That Your beloved ones may be delivered, save with Your right hand and hear me.

**107:8** God spoke in His sanctuary: "I will be exalted and will divide Shechem, and I will measure out the valley of tabernacles.

**107:9** Gilead is Mine, and Manasseh is Mine, and Ephraim is the support of My head; Judah is My king;

**107:10** Moab is the basin of My hope; upon Idumea I will stretch out My sandal; to Me the foreigners have been subjected."

**107:11** Who will bring me into a fortified city? Who will guide me as far as Idumea?

**107:12** Is it not You, O God, who cast us off? And will You not go forth, O God, with our armies?

**107:13** Give us help out of affliction, and vain is the salvation of man.

**107:14** In God we shall act mightily, and He Himself shall bring our enemies to nothing.

## Translator Notes

- **107:3** — The Greek reads "I will awake at dawn" (exegerthesomai orthrou), whereas the Masoretic Text reads "I will awaken the dawn."
- **107:8** — The Greek renders Hebrew Succoth as "the valley of tabernacles" (ton skenomaton) and reads "I will be exalted" (Hypsothesomai) rather than "I will exult."
- **107:10** — The Greek reads "the basin of My hope" (tes elpidos mou) where the Masoretic Text has "My washbasin," uses Idumea (Idoumaia) for Edom, and renders the Philistines as "the foreigners" (hoi allophyloi).
