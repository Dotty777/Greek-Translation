# Psalm 96 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**96:1** Of David, when his land is being established. The Lord reigns; the earth shall exult; let many isles be glad.

**96:2** Cloud and thick darkness are around Him; righteousness and judgment are the firm establishment of His throne.

**96:3** Fire shall go before Him, and shall set His enemies aflame on every side.

**96:4** His lightnings shone upon the world; the earth saw and was shaken.

**96:5** The mountains melted like wax before the presence of the Lord, before the presence of the Lord of all the earth.

**96:6** The heavens announced His righteousness, and all the peoples saw His glory.

**96:7** Let all be put to shame who worship carved images, who boast in their idols; worship Him, all His angels.

**96:8** Zion heard and was glad, and the daughters of Judea exulted, because of Your judgments, O Lord.

**96:9** For You are the Lord, the Most High over all the earth; You are exceedingly exalted above all the gods.

**96:10** You who love the Lord, hate evil; the Lord guards the souls of His holy ones; from the hand of sinners He shall deliver them.

**96:11** Light has dawned for the righteous, and gladness for the upright in heart.

**96:12** Be glad in the Lord, you righteous, and give thanks at the remembrance of His holiness.

## Translator Notes

- **96:1** — The Septuagint superscription ("Of David, when his land is being established") has no counterpart in the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Psalm 97); "his land" refers to David's realm.
- **96:7** — The Greek reads "all His angels" (pantes hoi angeloi autou), the wording cited in Hebrews 1:6, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "all gods."
- **96:8** — The Septuagint uses the Greek regional name "Judea" (Ioudaias) corresponding to "Judah" in the Hebrew text.
- **96:11** — The Greek verb aneteilen ("has dawned" or "has risen") reflects a different Hebrew vocalization or reading than the Masoretic Text's "is sown."
