## Text

**17:1** For the end; for David, the servant of the Lord, the words of this song which he spoke to the Lord in the day when the Lord delivered him from the hand of all his enemies and from the hand of Saul.

**17:2** And he said, "I will love You, O Lord, my strength.

**17:3** The Lord is my firmament and my refuge and my deliverer; my God is a helper, and I will hope in Him; my protector and the horn of salvation, my upholder.

**17:4** Praising, I will call upon the Lord, and I shall be saved from my enemies.

**17:5** The pangs of death encompassed me, and the torrents of lawlessness terrified me;

**17:6** the pangs of Hades encircled me; the snares of death confronted me.

**17:7** And in my distress I called upon the Lord, and cried aloud to my God; He heard my voice from His holy temple, and my cry before Him shall enter into His ears.

**17:8** And the earth was shaken and trembled, and the foundations of the mountains were troubled and shaken, because God was angry with them.

**17:9** Smoke went up in His wrath, and fire blazed forth before Him; coals were kindled from Him.

**17:10** And He bowed heaven and descended, and thick darkness was under His feet.

**17:11** And He mounted upon the cherubim and flew; He flew upon the wings of the winds.

**17:12** And He made darkness His hiding place; all around Him was His tent, dark water in the clouds of the air.

**17:13** From the radiance before Him the clouds passed through—hail and coals of fire.

**17:14** And the Lord thundered from heaven, and the Most High uttered His voice;

**17:15** and He sent forth arrows and scattered them, and He multiplied lightnings and confounded them.

**17:16** And the springs of the waters were seen, and the foundations of the world were uncovered at Your rebuke, O Lord, at the blast of the breath of Your wrath.

**17:17** He sent from on high and took hold of me; He drew me to Himself out of many waters.

**17:18** He will deliver me from my mighty enemies and from those who hate me, for they became stronger than I.

**17:19** They confronted me in the day of my affliction, and the Lord became my support,

**17:20** and He brought me out into a broad place; He will deliver me, because He delighted in me.

**17:21** He will deliver me from my mighty enemies and from those who hate me. And the Lord will recompense me according to my righteousness, and according to the cleanness of my hands He will recompense me.

**17:22** For I have kept the ways of the Lord, and have not acted ungodly in departing from my God;

**17:23** for all His judgments are before me, and His ordinances did not depart from me.

**17:24** And I shall be blameless with Him, and I shall keep myself from my iniquity.

**17:25** And the Lord will recompense me according to my righteousness, and according to the cleanness of my hands before His eyes.

**17:26** With the holy You will show Yourself holy, and with an innocent man You will be innocent;

**17:27** and with the chosen You will be chosen, and with the perverse You will deal perversely.

**17:28** For You will save a humble people, and You will bring low the eyes of the proud;

**17:29** for You will light my lamp, O Lord; my God, You will illumine my darkness.

**17:30** For by You I shall be delivered from a marauding band, and by my God I shall scale a wall.

**17:31** As for my God, His way is blameless; the oracles of the Lord are tried by fire; He is a protector of all who hope in Him.

**17:32** For who is God except the Lord? And who is God except our God?

**17:33** It is God who girds me with strength, and He has made my way blameless;

**17:34** who makes my feet like those of a deer, and sets me upon the high places;

**17:35** who teaches my hands for battle, and You have made my arms a bow of bronze.

**17:36** And You have given me the protection of my salvation, and Your right hand has upheld me; and Your discipline has set me upright to the end, and Your discipline itself shall teach me.

**17:37** You have widened my steps beneath me, and my footsteps have not grown weak.

**17:38** I will pursue my enemies and overtake them, and I will not turn back until they fail;

**17:39** I will crush them, and they shall by no means be able to stand; they shall fall beneath my feet.

**17:40** And You have girded me with strength for battle; You have shackled beneath me all who rise up against me.

**17:41** And You have made my enemies turn their back to me, and those who hate me You have utterly destroyed.

**17:42** They cried aloud, and there was none to save; to the Lord, and He did not hear them.

**17:43** And I will grind them fine like dust before the face of the wind; like mire of the streets I will trample them smooth.

**17:44** Deliver me from the contentions of the people; You shall set me as head of the nations; a people whom I did not know served me,

**17:45** at the hearing of the ear they obeyed me; foreign sons lied to me;

**17:46** foreign sons grew old and went limping from their paths.

**17:47** The Lord lives, and blessed be my God, and let the God of my salvation be exalted—

**17:48** the God who gives vengeance to me, and has subdued peoples under me,

**17:49** my deliverer from wrathful enemies. From those who rise up against me You shall exalt me; from an unrighteous man You shall deliver me.

**17:50** Therefore I will give thanks to You among the nations, O Lord, and to Your name I will sing praises,

**17:51** magnifying the deliverances of His king, and showing mercy to His anointed, to David and to his seed forever."

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — The Greek renders the opening metaphor as "firmament" (stereoma), emphasizing steadfast solidity and unshakable foundation.
- **17:21** — This Greek text repeats the opening clause of verse 18 ("He will deliver me from my mighty enemies and from those who hate me") at the start of verse 21.
- **17:30** — The Greek reads "I shall be delivered from a marauding band" (rhysthesomai apo peirateriou), differing from the Masoretic Text's "I can run against a troop."
- **17:36** — The Greek contains a doubled statement about God's "discipline" (paideia) setting the psalmist upright and teaching him, whereas the Masoretic Text speaks of God's gentleness.
- **17:46** — The Greek reads "went limping from their paths" (echolanan apo ton tribon auton), differing from the Masoretic Text's "came trembling from their strongholds."
