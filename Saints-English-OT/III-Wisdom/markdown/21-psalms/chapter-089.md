## Text

**89:1** A Prayer of Moses, the man of God. Lord, You have become a refuge for us from generation to generation.

**89:2** Before the mountains came into being, and the earth and the inhabited world were formed, even from eternity to eternity, You are.

**89:3** Do not turn man back to lowliness, and have You said, "Return, O sons of men"?

**89:4** For a thousand years in Your eyes are like yesterday, the day that has passed by, and like a watch in the night.

**89:5** Their worthless things shall be years; in the morning may they pass away like green grass.

**89:6** In the morning may they bloom and pass away; in the evening may they fall away, become hardened, and wither.

**89:7** For we have wasted away in Your wrath, and in Your fury we have been dismayed.

**89:8** You have placed our iniquities in Your presence, our lifetime in the illumination of Your countenance.

**89:9** For all our days have faded away, and in Your wrath we have wasted away; our years were occupied as with a spider's web.

**89:10** The days of our years in years are seventy years, and if in strength, eighty years, and the greater part of them is toil and pain; for gentleness has come upon us, and we shall be disciplined.

**89:11** Who knows the might of Your wrath, and from the fear of Your fury to reckon it up?

**89:12** Make known Your right hand, and those who are disciplined in heart in wisdom.

**89:13** Turn back, O Lord! How long? And be comforted concerning Your servants.

**89:14** We were filled in the morning with Your mercy, and we exulted and were glad in all our days,

**89:15** in return for the days in which You humbled us, the years in which we saw evils.

**89:16** And look upon Your servants and Your works, and guide their sons.

**89:17** And let the brightness of the Lord our God be upon us, and direct the works of our hands upon us.

## Translator Notes

- **89:2** — The Greek text ends with "You are" without the word "God" found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text, as the Septuagint appears to have read the corresponding Hebrew consonants as the opening negative of verse 3.
- **89:5** — The Greek reads "Their worthless things shall be years," differing markedly from the Masoretic Text ("You sweep them away; they are like a sleep").
- **89:9** — Literally "our years practiced/meditated as a spider's web," portraying human life as frail, fleeting toil, whereas the Masoretic Text compares our years to a sigh.
- **89:10** — The Greek text reads "in years" (en eniautois) and concludes with "for gentleness has come upon us, and we shall be disciplined," contrasting with the Masoretic Text ("for it is soon gone, and we fly away").
- **89:12** — The Greek reads "Make known Your right hand," while the infinitive "to reckon it up" closes verse 11, differing from the Masoretic division and reading ("So teach us to number our days").
