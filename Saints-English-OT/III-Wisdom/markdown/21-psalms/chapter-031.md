## Text

**31:1** Of understanding, for David. Blessed are those whose iniquities have been forgiven, and whose sins have been covered over.

**31:2** Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will by no means reckon sin, nor is there deceit in his mouth.

**31:3** Because I kept silent, my bones grew old from my crying out all the day long.

**31:4** For day and night Your hand was heavy upon me; I was turned into misery while a thorn was driven in. Interlude.

**31:5** My sin I made known, and my iniquity I did not cover; I said, "I will confess my sin against myself to the Lord," and You forgave the ungodliness of my heart. Interlude.

**31:6** For this cause every devout one shall pray to You in a fitting time; yet in a flood of many waters, they shall not draw near to him.

**31:7** You are my refuge from the affliction that encompasses me; O my exultation, redeem me from those who have surrounded me. Interlude.

**31:8** I will give you understanding and instruct you in this way in which you shall go; I will fix My eyes upon you.

**31:9** Do not be like a horse and a mule, which have no understanding; with bit and bridle you must constrain their jaws—those that do not draw near to you.

**31:10** Many are the scourges of the sinner, but mercy shall encompass the one who hopes in the Lord.

**31:11** Be glad in the Lord and exult, you righteous; and glory, all you upright in heart.

## Translator Notes

- **31:2** — The Greek reads "in his mouth" (en to stomati autou), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "in his spirit."
- **31:4** — The Greek reads "I was turned into misery while a thorn was driven in," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's imagery of summer drought. "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, corresponding to Hebrew selah.
- **31:5** — The Greek reads "the ungodliness of my heart" (tes kardias mou), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the guilt of my sin."
- **31:9** — The Greek verb form anxai functions here as an imperatival infinitive ("constrain"), and "to you" (pros se) refers to the addressee of verse eight as the animals' master, though some readers take it as addressed to God.
