## Text

**146:1** Hallelujah. Of Haggai and Zechariah. Praise the Lord, for a psalm is good; may praise be sweet to our God.

**146:2** The Lord builds Jerusalem, and He shall gather together the dispersions of Israel—

**146:3** He who heals those who are crushed in heart and binds up their wounds,

**146:4** He who numbers the multitudes of stars and calls them all by their names.

**146:5** Great is our Lord, and great is His might, and of His understanding there is no number.

**146:6** The Lord lifts up the meek, but He brings sinners low even to the earth.

**146:7** Begin a song to the Lord with thanksgiving; make melody to our God upon the harp,

**146:8** to Him who clothes heaven with clouds, who prepares rain for the earth, who causes grass to spring up upon the mountains, and green herbage for the service of men,

**146:9** and who gives to the cattle their food, and to the young of the ravens that call upon Him.

**146:10** He will not take pleasure in the might of the horse, nor is He well pleased with the legs of a man.

**146:11** The Lord is well pleased with those who fear Him, and with all those who hope in His mercy.

## Translator Notes

- **146:1** — The Septuagint divides Hebrew Psalm 147 into two psalms (Psalms 146 and 147 here) and prefixes each with the superscription attributing it to the post-exilic prophets Haggai and Zechariah.
- **146:8** — The final clause "and green herbage for the service of men" is distinctive to the Septuagint text (echoing Psalm 103:14 LXX) and is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **146:11** — The Greek explicitly includes "all" ("with all those who hope in His mercy"), a reading characteristic of the Septuagint.
