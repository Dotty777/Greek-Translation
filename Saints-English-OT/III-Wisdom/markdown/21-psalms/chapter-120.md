# Psalm 120 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**120:1** A Song of the Ascents. I lifted up my eyes to the mountains; from where shall my help come?

**120:2** My help is from the Lord, who made the heaven and the earth.

**120:3** Do not give your foot to slipping, nor shall He who guards you slumber.

**120:4** Behold, He who guards Israel shall neither slumber nor fall fast asleep.

**120:5** The Lord shall guard you; the Lord is your shelter upon your right hand;

**120:6** by day the sun shall not scorch you, nor the moon by night.

**120:7** The Lord shall guard you from all evil; may He guard your soul.

**120:8** The Lord shall guard your coming in and your going out from now and forevermore.

## Translator Notes

- **120:3** — The Greek opens with a second-person prohibition (me dos, "Do not give") addressed to the hearer, rather than a third-person statement about God as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **120:4** — This Greek text reads exypnosei (an intensive compound of hypnoo, "fall fast asleep"), where other Septuagint manuscripts read the simple verb hypnosei ("sleep").
- **120:7** — The verb shifts from the future indicative phylaxei ("shall guard") in the first clause to the aorist optative phylaxai ("may He guard") in the second, turning assurance into a benediction.
- **120:8** — The Septuagint reads ten eisodon sou kai ten exodon sou ("your coming in and your going out"), reversing the Hebrew order ("going out and coming in").
