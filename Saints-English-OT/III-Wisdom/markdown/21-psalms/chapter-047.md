## Text

**47:1** A Psalm of a song for the sons of Korah, for the second day of the week.

**47:2** Great is the Lord, and exceedingly worthy to be praised in the city of our God, His holy mountain,

**47:3** well-rooted, the exultation of all the earth; the mountains of Zion, the sides of the north, the city of the great King.

**47:4** God is known within her citadels when He comes to her defense.

**47:5** For behold, the kings assembled; they came together as one.

**47:6** Seeing it thus, they marveled; they were troubled, they were shaken;

**47:7** trembling laid hold of them, and there were pangs as of a woman in childbirth.

**47:8** With a violent wind You will shatter the ships of Tarshish.

**47:9** Just as we heard, so we have seen in the city of the Lord of hosts, in the city of our God; God has established her forever. Interlude.

**47:10** We have received Your mercy, O God, in the midst of Your people.

**47:11** As is Your name, O God, so also is Your praise to the farthest bounds of the earth; full of righteousness is Your right hand.

**47:12** Let Mount Zion be glad, let the daughters of Judea exult because of Your judgments, O Lord.

**47:13** Encircle Zion and encompass her; tell of it among her towers;

**47:14** set your hearts upon her strength, and apportion her citadels, that you may declare it to another generation.

**47:15** For this is our God forever, even forever and ever.

## Translator Notes

- **47:1** — The Greek phrase "for the second day of the week" (literally "of the Sabbath") is a Septuagint liturgical heading designating this psalm for Monday.
- **47:3** — The Septuagint reads "well-rooted" (eurizo) and plural "mountains of Zion," where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "beautiful in elevation, Mount Zion."
- **47:10** — The Septuagint reads "in the midst of Your people" (tou laou sou) rather than "in the midst of Your temple" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **47:13** — The Greek verb diegesasthe with en ("tell of it among her towers") renders the Hebrew consonantal verb as "recount" rather than "count."
- **47:15** — This critical Greek text concludes at "forever and ever," without the closing clause ("He will guide us") found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and some later Greek manuscripts.
