## Text

**147:1** Hallelujah. Of Haggai and Zechariah. Extol the Lord, O Jerusalem; praise your God, O Zion.

**147:2** For He has made strong the bars of your gates; He has blessed your sons within you.

**147:3** He makes your borders peace, and fills you with the fat of wheat.

**147:4** He sends forth His oracle to the earth; His word shall run swiftly.

**147:5** He gives snow like wool; He scatters mist like ashes.

**147:6** He casts forth His ice like morsels; before the face of His cold, who shall stand?

**147:7** He shall send forth His word and melt them; His wind shall blow, and the waters shall flow.

**147:8** He proclaims His word to Jacob, His ordinances and His judgments to Israel.

**147:9** He has not done thus to any nation, and His judgments He has not made known to them.

## Translator Notes

- **147:1** — In the Septuagint numbering, this begins a distinct psalm (corresponding to Hebrew Psalm 147:12–20) with its own superscription of Haggai and Zechariah.
- **147:3** — "Fat of wheat" renders the literal Greek idiom (stear pyrou) denoting the richest and finest grain.
- **147:5** — The Greek shifts here into genitive participles continuing from "His" in verse 4, and reads "mist" (homichle) where the Hebrew has "frost."
- **147:9** — Unlike the Hebrew text ("they have not known them"), the Greek makes God the subject ("He has not made known to them") and ends without a closing "Hallelujah."
