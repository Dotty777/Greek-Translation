# Psalm 148 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**148:1** Hallelujah. Of Haggai and Zechariah. Praise the Lord from the heavens; praise Him in the highest.

**148:2** Praise Him, all His angels; praise Him, all His powers.

**148:3** Praise Him, sun and moon; praise Him, all the stars and the light.

**148:4** Praise Him, O heavens of heavens, and the water that is above the heavens.

**148:5** Let them praise the name of the Lord, for He spoke, and they came into being; He commanded, and they were created.

**148:6** He established them forever and ever; He set a decree, and it shall not pass away.

**148:7** Praise the Lord from the earth: dragons and all deeps;

**148:8** fire, hail, snow, ice, wind of the tempest, which perform His word;

**148:9** the mountains and all hills, fruit-bearing trees and all cedars;

**148:10** wild beasts and all cattle, creeping things and winged birds;

**148:11** kings of the earth and all peoples, rulers and all judges of the earth;

**148:12** young men and maidens, old men with youths.

**148:13** Let them praise the name of the Lord, for His name alone has been exalted; His praise is over earth and heaven.

**148:14** And He shall exalt the horn of His people, for the sons of Israel, a people drawing near to Him.

## Translator Notes

- **148:3** — The Greek coordinates "the light" alongside "all the stars" ("all the stars and the light"), whereas the Hebrew reads "all stars of light" (shining stars).
- **148:5** — The clause "for He spoke, and they came into being" is a distinctive Septuagint reading (paralleling Psalm 32:9 LXX) not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **148:7** — Greek drakontes denotes dragons or great sea creatures inhabiting the ocean depths.
- **148:14** — This Greek text omits both the phrase "a hymn for all His holy ones" (found in some Septuagint manuscripts and the Hebrew text) and a closing "Hallelujah."
