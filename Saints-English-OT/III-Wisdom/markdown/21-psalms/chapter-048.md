## Text

**48:1** To the end: a Psalm for the sons of Korah.

**48:2** Hear these things, all nations; give ear, all who inhabit the world,

**48:3** both the earthborn and the sons of men, rich and poor together.

**48:4** My mouth shall declare wisdom, and the meditation of my heart shall bring forth understanding.

**48:5** I will bend my ear to a parable; upon the psaltery I will unfold my riddle.

**48:6** Why should I fear in an evil day? The iniquity of my heel will encompass me,

**48:7** they who trust in their strength and boast in the abundance of their wealth.

**48:8** A brother does not redeem; shall a man redeem? He shall not give to God his own propitiation,

**48:9** nor the price of the redemption of his soul; and he has toiled forever,

**48:10** and he shall live to the end, for he will not see corruption,

**48:11** when he sees wise men dying. Together the senseless and the fool shall perish, and they shall leave their riches to strangers.

**48:12** And their tombs are their houses forever, their dwelling places from generation to generation; they have called their own names upon their lands.

**48:13** Yet man, being in honor, did not understand; he was compared to the senseless beasts and became like them.

**48:14** This way of theirs is a stumbling block to them, yet afterward they shall give blessing with their mouth. Interlude.

**48:15** Like sheep they are laid in Hades; death shepherds them. The upright shall have dominion over them in the morning, and their help shall grow old in Hades, away from their glory.

**48:16** Nevertheless, God will redeem my soul from the hand of Hades when He receives me. Interlude.

**48:17** Do not be afraid when a man grows rich, and when the glory of his house is multiplied;

**48:18** for when he dies he shall take nothing at all, neither shall his glory descend with him.

**48:19** For his soul shall be blessed in his lifetime; he will give thanks to You when You do good to him.

**48:20** He shall enter even to the generation of his fathers; forever he shall not see light.

**48:21** Man, being in honor, did not understand; he was compared to the senseless beasts and became like them.

## Translator Notes

- **48:1** — "To the end" (eis to telos) is the standard Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew musical superscription often translated "To the choirmaster."
- **48:6** — "The iniquity of my heel" translates the Greek literally, reflecting a reading of the Hebrew consonantal text often understood in the Masoretic tradition as "my supplanters," and connects loosely to the nominative participle in verse 7.
- **48:8** — The Septuagint reads "a brother" (adelphos), interpreting the Hebrew consonants ach as "brother" rather than the emphatic particle "surely."
- **48:12** — "Their tombs" (hoi taphoi auton) reflects a Hebrew source reading qibram ("their grave") rather than the Masoretic Text's qirbam ("their inward thought").
- **48:19** — The Septuagint addresses God in the second person ("he will give thanks to You when You do good to him"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text addresses the prosperous man.
