## Text

**78:1** A Psalm of Asaph. O God, nations have come into Your inheritance; they have defiled Your holy temple; they have made Jerusalem into a fruit-watcher's hut.

**78:2** They have given the dead bodies of Your servants as food to the birds of heaven, the flesh of Your devout ones to the wild beasts of the earth.

**78:3** They have poured out their blood like water around Jerusalem, and there was no one to bury them.

**78:4** We have become a reproach to our neighbors, a scoffing and derision to those around us.

**78:5** How long, O Lord, will You be angry to the end? Will Your jealousy blaze like fire?

**78:6** Pour out Your wrath upon nations that have not known You, and upon kingdoms that have not called upon Your name.

**78:7** For they have devoured Jacob, and have made his place desolate.

**78:8** Do not remember our former iniquities; let Your tender mercies come swiftly to meet us, for we have become exceedingly poor.

**78:9** Help us, O God our Savior, for the sake of the glory of Your name; O Lord, deliver us, and pardon our sins for the sake of Your name.

**78:10** Lest they say among the nations, "Where is their God?" And let the avenging of the blood of Your servants that has been poured out be made known among the nations before our eyes.

**78:11** Let the groaning of the shackled come before You; according to the greatness of Your arm, preserve the sons of those put to death.

**78:12** Repay our neighbors sevenfold into their bosom their reproach with which they have reproached You, O Lord.

**78:13** For we, Your people and the sheep of Your pasture, will give thanks to You forever; from generation to generation we will proclaim Your praise.

## Translator Notes

- **78:1** — Greek oporophylakion means a fruit-watcher's hut or orchard shed, rendering the Hebrew term for heaps of ruins as an image of stark desolation.
- **78:10** — Greek me pote literally reads 'Lest they say,' whereas the Masoretic Hebrew reads 'Why should they say.'
- **78:11** — Greek ton tethanatomenon reads 'of those put to death,' whereas the Masoretic Hebrew refers to those appointed to die.
