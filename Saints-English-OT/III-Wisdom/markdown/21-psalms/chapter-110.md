# Psalm 110 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**110:1** Hallelujah. I will give thanks to You, O Lord, with all my heart, in the council of the upright and in the congregation.

**110:2** Great are the works of the Lord, sought out according to all His desires;

**110:3** Thanksgiving and majesty are His work, and His righteousness abides forever and ever.

**110:4** He has made a memorial of His wondrous works; merciful and compassionate is the Lord.

**110:5** He has given food to those who fear Him; He shall remember His covenant forever.

**110:6** He has declared the might of His works to His people, to give them the inheritance of the nations.

**110:7** The works of His hands are truth and judgment; all His commandments are faithful,

**110:8** established forever and ever, made in truth and uprightness.

**110:9** He has sent redemption to His people; He has commanded His covenant forever; holy and awe-inspiring is His name.

**110:10** The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord; good understanding belongs to all who practice it; His praise abides forever and ever.

## Translator Notes

- **110:2** — "Sought out according to all His desires" follows the Septuagint exezetemena eis panta ta thelemata autou, whereas the Hebrew text reads "studied by all who delight in them."
- **110:3** — The Septuagint translates the opening noun as exomologesis ("thanksgiving" or "confession"), reflecting a different root understanding from the Hebrew "splendor."
- **110:10** — The Greek feminine singular pronoun auten ("it") refers back to "wisdom" (or "understanding") rather than plural "them" (His commandments) as in the Hebrew text.
