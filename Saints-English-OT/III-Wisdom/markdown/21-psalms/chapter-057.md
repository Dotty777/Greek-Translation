## Text

**57:1** To the end: "Do not destroy." Of David, for a pillar inscription.

**57:2** Do you indeed speak righteousness in truth? Do you judge uprightly, O sons of men?

**57:3** For indeed in heart you work iniquities upon the earth; your hands weave together unrighteousness.

**57:4** Sinners have been estranged from the womb; they went astray from the belly; they spoke falsehoods.

**57:5** Their fury is after the likeness of a serpent, like that of a deaf asp that stops up its ears,

**57:6** which will not heed the voice of charmers, nor of a charm prepared by a wise man.

**57:7** God crushed their teeth in their mouth; the Lord shattered the jaw teeth of the lions.

**57:8** They shall be brought to nothing like water passing away; He will bend His bow until they grow weak.

**57:9** Like wax that melts, they shall be taken away; fire fell, and they did not see the sun.

**57:10** Before your thorns understand the bramble, as though alive, as though in wrath, He will swallow you up.

**57:11** The righteous man shall rejoice when he sees vengeance upon the ungodly; he shall wash his hands in the blood of the sinner.

**57:12** And a man shall say, "Is there indeed fruit for the righteous? Surely, then, there is God who judges them upon the earth."

## Translator Notes

- **57:5** — Greek thymos ("fury" or "wrath") reflects the dual sense of the underlying Hebrew term for both "wrath" and "venom."
- **57:6** — Literally "nor of a charm prepared by a wise man," rendering Greek pharmakou pharmakeuomenou para sophou.
- **57:9** — The Greek reads "wax that melts" and "fire fell," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which speaks of a melting snail and the untimely birth of a woman.
- **57:10** — The Greek literally reads "before your thorns understand the bramble," interpreting the difficult Hebrew imagery of pots feeling the heat of burning thorns as a metaphor of swift judgment.
