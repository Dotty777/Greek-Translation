## Text

**41:1** For the end; for understanding, for the sons of Korah.

**41:2** As the deer longs for the springs of waters, so my soul longs for You, O God.

**41:3** My soul has thirsted for the living God; when shall I come and appear before the face of God?

**41:4** My tears have become bread by day and by night, while it is said to me every day, "Where is your God?"

**41:5** These things I remembered, and I poured out my soul upon myself; for I shall pass through in the place of a wondrous tabernacle, even to the house of God, with a voice of exultation and thanksgiving, the sound of those keeping festival.

**41:6** Why are you deeply sorrowful, O soul, and why do you trouble me? Hope in God, for I shall give thanks to Him, the salvation of my countenance,

**41:7** my God. Within myself my soul has been troubled; therefore I will remember You from the land of the Jordan and of Hermoniim, from a small mountain.

**41:8** Deep calls upon deep at the voice of Your cataracts; all Your billows and Your waves have passed over me.

**41:9** By day the Lord will command His mercy, and by night He will declare it; with me is a prayer to the God of my life.

**41:10** I will say to God, "You are my Protector; why have You forgotten me? Why do I go about with a sorrowful countenance while my enemy afflicts me?"

**41:11** In the crushing of my bones, those who afflict me have reproached me, while they say to me every day, "Where is your God?"

**41:12** Why are you deeply sorrowful, O soul, and why do you trouble me? Hope in God, for I shall give thanks to Him, the salvation of my countenance, my God.

## Translator Notes

- **41:1** — Greek Psalm 41 corresponds to Hebrew Psalm 42; "For the end" (eis to telos) is the characteristic Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew musical superscription.
- **41:7** — "Hermoniim" preserves the Septuagint's plural form of Hermon (Hermonieim), and "from a small mountain" renders the Greek literally (apo orous mikrou), where the Hebrew tradition takes the term as a proper name ("Mount Mizar").
- **41:9** — The Greek text reads "and by night He will declare it" (kai nyktos delosei), differing from traditions that read "His song is with me."
- **41:11** — "In the crushing of my bones" renders the Greek verb katathlasai ("to crush" or "shatter"), conveying deep inward torment amid the taunts of enemies.
