## Text

**1:1** Blessed is the man who has not walked in the counsel of the ungodly, and has not stood in the way of sinners, and has not sat upon the seat of pestilent men;

**1:2** but rather his desire is in the law of the Lord, and in His law he shall meditate day and night.

**1:3** And he shall be like the tree planted beside the channels of waters, which shall yield its fruit in its season, and its leaf shall not fall away; and all that he may do shall prosper.

**1:4** Not so are the ungodly, not so; but rather they are like the dust that the wind scatters from the face of the earth.

**1:5** Therefore the ungodly shall not rise up in judgment, nor sinners in the counsel of the righteous;

**1:6** for the Lord knows the way of the righteous, and the way of the ungodly shall perish.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The Greek noun rendered "pestilent men" literally means "plagues" or "pestilences," used figuratively in the Septuagint for corrupt or scornful persons (where the Hebrew text has "scoffers").
- **1:4** — The Septuagint doubles the opening negation ("Not so ... not so") and adds "from the face of the earth," neither of which appears in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **1:5** — The Greek reads "counsel" (the same term used in verse 1) rather than "congregation" as in the Hebrew text, reinforcing the contrast between the counsel of the ungodly and the counsel of the righteous.
