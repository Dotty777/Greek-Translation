# Psalm 74 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**74:1** For the end; do not destroy; a psalm of a song for Asaph.

**74:2** We will give thanks to You, O God, we will give thanks and call upon Your name; I will declare all Your wondrous works,

**74:3** when I take an appointed time; I will judge uprightness.

**74:4** The earth has melted, and all who dwell in it; I have firmly established its pillars. Interlude.

**74:5** I said to those who act lawlessly not to act lawlessly, and to those who sin, "Do not lift up the horn;

**74:6** do not raise your horn on high; do not speak unrighteousness against God."

**74:7** For neither from the east, nor from the west, nor from the deserts,

**74:8** for God is the Judge: this one He humbles, and that one He exalts.

**74:9** For a cup is in the hand of the Lord, of unmixed wine, full of mixture, and He has poured from this into that; yet its dregs have not been emptied out, and all the sinners of the earth shall drink.

**74:10** But I will rejoice forever; I will sing praises to the God of Jacob;

**74:11** and all the horns of the sinners I will break in pieces, and the horns of the righteous one shall be exalted.

## Translator Notes

- **74:1** — The Greek superscription "For the end" (eis to telos) renders the Hebrew musical heading often translated "To the chief musician," and "do not destroy" translates the Hebrew tune title Al-tashcheth.
- **74:2** — The Greek text reads "and call upon Your name," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "for Your name is near." In the punctuation of the Greek text, "when I take an appointed time" (v. 3) may conclude this sentence or introduce the divine declaration of judgment that follows.
- **74:4** — "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, which corresponds to the Hebrew musical term Selah.
- **74:7** — The clause is elliptical in Greek (implying "does exaltation come"); "the east" renders Greek exodon ("goings forth," i.e., sunrisings), and this Greek text lacks the word "mountains" found after "deserts" in some manuscripts and the Masoretic Text.
