## Text

**63:1** For the end; a Psalm of David.

**63:2** Give ear, O God, to my prayer when I make supplication to You; from the dread of the enemy deliver my soul.

**63:3** You have sheltered me from the conspiracy of evildoers, from the multitude of those who work injustice,

**63:4** who have sharpened their tongues like a sword and bent a bow, a bitter thing,

**63:5** to shoot down the blameless in secret places; suddenly they will shoot him down and will not fear.

**63:6** They have strengthened for themselves an evil word; they spoke of hiding snares; they said, "Who will see them?"

**63:7** They searched out lawlessness; they grew faint searching with diligent search; a man shall draw near, and a deep heart,

**63:8** and God shall be exalted. Their blows have become the arrow of infants,

**63:9** and their tongues have brought him to nothing. All who beheld them were troubled,

**63:10** and every man feared; and they declared the works of God and understood His deeds.

**63:11** The righteous man shall be glad in the Lord and shall hope in Him; and all the upright in heart shall be praised.

## Translator Notes

- **63:1** — "For the end" ("eis to telos") is the standard Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew superscription often translated "To the Chief Musician."
- **63:7** — Greek "a man shall draw near, and a deep heart" reflects the Septuagint taking the Hebrew noun for "inward part" as a verb meaning "to draw near."
- **63:8** — The Septuagint reads "and God shall be exalted. Their blows have become the arrow of infants," whereas the Masoretic Text states that God shoots them with an arrow and they are suddenly wounded.
