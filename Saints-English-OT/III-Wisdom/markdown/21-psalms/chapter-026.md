## Text

**26:1** Of David, before he was anointed. The Lord is my light and my Savior; whom shall I fear? The Lord is the defender of my life; of whom shall I be in dread?

**26:2** When evildoers drew near against me to devour my flesh, my oppressors and my enemies—they themselves grew weak and fell.

**26:3** Though an encampment should array itself against me, my heart shall not fear; though war should rise up against me, even in this I hope.

**26:4** One thing I have asked of the Lord, this I will earnestly seek: that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, to behold the loveliness of the Lord and to visit His temple.

**26:5** For He hid me in a tabernacle in the day of my troubles; He sheltered me in the secret place of His tabernacle; upon a rock He lifted me high.

**26:6** And now, behold, He has lifted up my head above my enemies; I went around and offered in His tabernacle a sacrifice of jubilation; I will sing and make melody to the Lord.

**26:7** "Hear, O Lord, my voice with which I have cried aloud; have mercy on me and hear me,"

**26:8** to You my heart has said. I have earnestly sought Your face; Your face, O Lord, I will seek.

**26:9** Do not turn Your face away from me; do not turn aside in wrath from Your servant. Be my helper; do not forsake me and do not overlook me, O God my Savior.

**26:10** For my father and my mother have forsaken me, but the Lord has taken me to Himself.

**26:11** Appoint a law for me, O Lord, in Your way, and guide me in a straight path because of my enemies.

**26:12** Do not deliver me over to the desires of those who afflict me, for unjust witnesses have risen up against me, and injustice has lied to itself.

**26:13** I will believe that I shall see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living.

**26:14** Wait patiently for the Lord; be courageous, and let your heart be strengthened; and wait patiently for the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **26:1** — The phrase "before he was anointed" is a distinctive Septuagint addition to the superscription not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **26:6** — The Greek reads "I went around" (ekyklosa), interpreting the Hebrew phrase "around me" as a verb, and uses past-tense verbs throughout verses 5 and 6.
- **26:8** — The Greek punctuates "to You my heart has said" as concluding the speech of verse 7, and follows with the first-person "I have earnestly sought" (exezetesa) where the Hebrew has the command "Seek My face."
- **26:12** — "Desires" translates Greek psychas (lit. "souls"); the closing clause "and injustice has lied to itself" is a distinctive Septuagint reading where the Hebrew speaks of breathing out violence.
- **26:13** — The Septuagint omits the opening conditional particle ("Unless...") of the Hebrew Masoretic Text, and the Greek critical text here reads the future tense "I will believe" (pisteuso).
