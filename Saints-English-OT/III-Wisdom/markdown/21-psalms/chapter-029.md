## Text

**29:1** To the end: a Psalm of a song for the dedication of the house, of David.

**29:2** I will exalt You, O Lord, for You have upheld me, and have not made my enemies rejoice over me.

**29:3** O Lord my God, I cried to You, and You have healed me.

**29:4** O Lord, You have brought my soul up out of Hades; You have saved me from those who go down into the pit.

**29:5** Sing praises to the Lord, His holy ones, and give thanks at the remembrance of His holiness.

**29:6** For wrath is in His anger, and life is in His will; in the evening weeping shall lodge, and in the morning, exultation.

**29:7** And I said in my prosperity, "I shall never be shaken."

**29:8** O Lord, by Your will grant strength to my beauty; but You turned away Your face, and I became troubled.

**29:9** To You, O Lord, I will cry aloud, and to my God I will make supplication.

**29:10** What gain is there in my blood, when I descend into corruption? Shall dust give thanks to You, or proclaim Your truth?

**29:11** The Lord heard and had mercy on me; the Lord became my helper.

**29:12** You have turned my mourning into joy for me; You have torn off my sackcloth and girded me with gladness,

**29:13** so that my glory may sing praises to You and I may not be pierced with sorrow. O Lord my God, forever I shall give thanks to You.

## Translator Notes

- **29:1** — "To the end" (Greek Eis to telos) is the standard Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew musical heading often translated "To the choirmaster."
- **29:6** — The Septuagint reads "wrath is in His anger," differing from the Masoretic Hebrew "His anger is but for a moment."
- **29:8** — The Greek text uses the imperative paraschou ("grant") and the noun kallei ("to my beauty"), where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "You made my mountain stand strong."
- **29:13** — Greek katanygo means "be pierced with sorrow" or "feel compunction," corresponding to the Masoretic Hebrew "be silent."
