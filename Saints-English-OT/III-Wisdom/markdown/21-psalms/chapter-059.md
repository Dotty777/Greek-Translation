## Text

**59:1** For the end; for those who shall be changed; still for a pillar inscription of David, for instruction,

**59:2** when he set fire to Mesopotamia of Syria and Syria of Sobal, and Joab returned and struck the Valley of Salt, twelve thousand.

**59:3** O God, You have cast us off and torn us down; You were angry, and You had compassion on us.

**59:4** You have shaken the earth and thrown it into turmoil; heal its breaches, for they have been shaken.

**59:5** You have shown Your people hard things; You have made us drink the wine of stupor.

**59:6** You have given a sign to those who fear You, that they may flee from before the bow. Interlude.

**59:7** That Your beloved ones may be delivered, save with Your right hand and hear me.

**59:8** God has spoken in His holy place: "I will greatly rejoice and divide Shechem, and I will measure out the Valley of Tents.

**59:9** Gilead is Mine, and Manasseh is Mine, and Ephraim is the strength of My head; Judah is My king,

**59:10** Moab is the cauldron of My hope; over Idumea I will stretch out My sandal; to Me the foreigners have been subjected."

**59:11** Who will lead me into a fortified city? Who will guide me as far as Idumea?

**59:12** Is it not You, O God, who have cast us off? And will You not go forth, O God, with our armies?

**59:13** Give us help from affliction, and vain is the salvation of man.

**59:14** Yet in God we shall act with might, and He Himself will bring to nothing those who afflict us.

## Translator Notes

- **59:1** — The Greek superscription renders the Hebrew heading terms as "For the end" (eis to telos), "for those who shall be changed" (tois alloioothesomenois), and "a pillar inscription" (stelographian).
- **59:2** — The Greek reads "set fire to" (enepyrisen) and preserves the distinct proper name Sobal (Sobal, corresponding to Hebrew Zobah), followed by "struck the Valley of Salt, twelve thousand."
- **59:3** — The Greek uses the aorist indicative "You had compassion on us" (oikteiresas hemas), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has an imperative plea ("restore us").
- **59:6** — "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, a liturgical marker for a musical pause; the Greek also reads "that they may flee from before the bow" (tou phygein apo prosopou toxou).
- **59:10** — The Greek reads "cauldron of My hope" (lebes tes elpidos mou), uses Idumea (Idoumaia) for Edom, and has "to Me the foreigners have been subjected" (emoi allophyloi hypetagesan).
