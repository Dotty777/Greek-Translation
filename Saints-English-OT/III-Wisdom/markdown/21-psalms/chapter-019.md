## Text

**19:1** For the end: a psalm of David.

**19:2** May the Lord hear you in the day of affliction; may the name of the God of Jacob shield you.

**19:3** May He send forth help to you from the sanctuary, and out of Zion may He uphold you.

**19:4** May He remember every sacrifice of yours, and let Him make your whole burnt offering fat. Interlude.

**19:5** May He grant you according to your heart, and fulfill all your counsel.

**19:6** Let us exult in your salvation, and in the name of our God we shall be magnified. May the Lord fulfill all your petitions.

**19:7** Now I know that the Lord has saved His anointed; He shall hear him from His holy heaven; in mighty deeds is the salvation of His right hand.

**19:8** Some in chariots and some in horses, but we shall be magnified in the name of the Lord our God.

**19:9** They were fettered and fell, but we rose and were set upright.

**19:10** O Lord, save Your king, and hear us in whatever day we call upon You.

## Translator Notes

- **19:4** — "Make your whole burnt offering fat" is a sacrificial idiom for graciously accepting the offering. "Interlude" renders Greek diapsalma, a musical pause corresponding to Hebrew Selah.
- **19:6** — Greek reads "we shall be magnified" (megalynthēsometha, also in verse 8), whereas the Masoretic Text reads "we will set up our banners" here and "we will remember" in verse 8.
- **19:7** — "His anointed" translates Greek ton christon autou, the royal title from which "Christ" is derived.
- **19:10** — The Septuagint includes "Your" with "king" and directs both imperatives to the Lord, whereas the Masoretic Text can be read "O Lord, save! May the King answer us."
