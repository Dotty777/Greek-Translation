## Text

**67:1** For the end; of David, a psalm of a song.

**67:2** Let God arise, and let His enemies be scattered, and let those who hate Him flee from His presence.

**67:3** As smoke vanishes, let them vanish; as wax melts before fire, so may sinners perish from the presence of God.

**67:4** And let the righteous be glad. Interlude. Let them exult before God; let them be delighted in gladness.

**67:5** Sing to God, sing psalms to His name; prepare a way for Him who rides upon the west—the Lord is His name—and exult before Him.

**67:6** They shall be troubled at His presence, the Father of the orphans and Judge of the widows; God is in His holy place.

**67:7** God settles the solitary in a house, leading out the fettered in valor; likewise the rebellious, who dwell in tombs.

**67:8** O God, when You went forth before Your people, when You passed through the wilderness, Interlude.

**67:9** the earth quaked, and indeed the heavens dripped at the presence of the God of Sinai, at the presence of the God of Israel.

**67:10** You shall set apart a voluntary rain, O God, for Your inheritance; and it grew weak, yet You restored it.

**67:11** Your living creatures dwell in it; in Your goodness You have prepared for the poor.

**67:12** God the Lord shall give a word to those who proclaim good tidings with great power,

**67:13** the King of the powers of the beloved, of the beloved, and for the beauty of the house to divide the spoils.

**67:14** If you lie down in the midst of the inheritances, wings of a dove overlaid with silver, and her back in the pale luster of gold. Interlude.

**67:15** When the Heavenly One separates kings upon it, they shall be made white as snow in Zalmon.

**67:16** A mountain of God is a rich mountain; a curdled mountain, a rich mountain.

**67:17** Why do you suppose, O curdled mountains? This is the mountain in which God has been well pleased to dwell; for indeed the Lord shall abide there to the end.

**67:18** The chariot of God is ten-thousandfold, thousands of those who prosper; the Lord is among them in Sinai, in the holy place.

**67:19** Having ascended on high, You led captivity captive; You received gifts in man—indeed, even for the disobedient to dwell.

**67:20** Blessed is the Lord God, blessed is the Lord day by day; and the God of our salvations shall prosper our way. Interlude.

**67:21** Our God is the God who saves, and to the Lord belong the ways out of death.

**67:22** Yet God shall shatter the heads of His enemies, the hairy crown of those who walk on, each in his trespasses.

**67:23** The Lord said, "From Bashan I will turn back; I will turn back in the depths of the sea,

**67:24** so that your foot may be dipped in blood, and the tongue of your dogs dipped in it from the enemies."

**67:25** Your processions have been seen, O God, the processions of my God, of the King who is in the holy place.

**67:26** Princes went before, next to those who sing psalms, in the midst of maidens playing timbrels.

**67:27** In the assemblies bless God, the Lord from the fountains of Israel.

**67:28** There is Benjamin, the youngest, in ecstasy; the princes of Judah, their leaders; the princes of Zebulun, the princes of Naphtali.

**67:29** Command Your strength, O God; strengthen, O God, this which You have established for us.

**67:30** From Your temple at Jerusalem kings shall bring gifts to You.

**67:31** Rebuke the wild beasts of the reed, the assembly of the bulls among the heifers of the peoples, so that those who are tested with silver may not be shut out; scatter the nations that desire wars.

**67:32** Envoys shall come out of Egypt; Ethiopia shall hasten her hand to God.

**67:33** O kingdoms of the earth, sing to God; sing psalms to the Lord. Interlude.

**67:34** Sing psalms to God, who rides upon the heaven of heaven toward the east. Behold, He shall give in His voice a voice of power.

**67:35** Give glory to God; His majesty is over Israel, and His power is in the clouds.

**67:36** Wondrous is God among His devout ones; the God of Israel, He Himself shall give power and might to His people; and blessed be God.

## Translator Notes

- **67:5** — Greek ἐπὶ δυσμῶν ("upon the west") reflects the Septuagint rendering of a Hebrew term that other traditions translate as "clouds" or "deserts."
- **67:13** — The doubled phrase τοῦ ἀγαπητοῦ, τοῦ ἀγαπητοῦ ("of the beloved, of the beloved") reflects the Septuagint reading of a repeated Hebrew verb as a noun from the root meaning "beloved."
- **67:16** — Both ὄρος πῖον ("a rich mountain") and ὄρος τετυρωμένον ("a curdled mountain") are distinctive Septuagint renderings of the Hebrew descriptions of Mount Bashan.
- **67:19** — The closing clause καὶ γὰρ ἀπειθοῦντες τοῦ κατασκηνῶσαι ("indeed, even for the disobedient to dwell") is syntactically elliptical in Greek, while Κύριος ὁ θεός ("the Lord God") opens verse 20 in this critical text.
- **67:31** — The Septuagint renders this difficult clause as a purpose phrase, τοῦ μὴ ἀποκλεισθῆναι τοὺς δεδοκιμασμένους τῷ ἀργυρίῳ ("so that those who are tested with silver may not be shut out").
- **67:36** — The Greek critical text reads ἐν τοῖς ὁσίοις αὐτοῦ ("among His devout ones"), whereas many Septuagint manuscripts read ἐν τοῖς ἁγίοις αὐτοῦ ("among His holy ones").
