## Text

**103:1** Of David. Bless the Lord, O my soul. O Lord, Lord, my God, how exceedingly You have been magnified! You have clothed Yourself with praise and splendor,

**103:2** wrapping Yourself in light as in a garment, stretching out the heaven like a skin curtain;

**103:3** who roofs His upper chambers with waters, who appoints clouds as His ascent, who walks upon wings of winds;

**103:4** who makes His angels spirits, and His ministers a blazing fire.

**103:5** He founded the earth upon its stability; it shall not sway forever and ever.

**103:6** The abyss, like a garment, is His mantle; waters shall stand upon the mountains.

**103:7** At Your rebuke they shall flee; at the sound of Your thunder they shall cower in dread.

**103:8** Mountains ascend and plains descend to the place which You founded for them.

**103:9** You set a boundary which they shall not pass beyond, nor shall they turn back to cover the earth.

**103:10** He sends forth springs in ravines; through the midst of the mountains waters shall pass.

**103:11** They shall give drink to all the wild beasts of the field; wild donkeys shall await them in their thirst.

**103:12** Beside them the birds of heaven shall make their dwelling; from the midst of the rocks they shall sound forth their voice.

**103:13** He waters the mountains from His upper chambers; from the fruit of Your works the earth shall be satisfied.

**103:14** He causes grass to spring up for the cattle, and green herbage for the service of mankind, to bring forth bread from the earth;

**103:15** and wine gladdens the heart of man, to brighten his countenance with oil, and bread strengthens the heart of man.

**103:16** The trees of the plain shall be satisfied, the cedars of Lebanon which He planted.

**103:17** There sparrows shall build their nests; the dwelling of the heron leads the way for it.

**103:18** The high mountains are for the deer; a rock is a refuge for the rock badgers.

**103:19** He made the moon for the seasons; the sun knows its setting.

**103:20** You appointed darkness, and it became night; in it all the wild beasts of the forest shall roam abroad,

**103:21** young lions roaring to seize their prey, and to seek food for themselves from God.

**103:22** The sun rises, and they shall gather together, and in their dens they shall lie down.

**103:23** Man shall go forth to his work, and to his labor until evening.

**103:24** How magnified are Your works, O Lord! All things You have made in wisdom; the earth is filled with Your creation.

**103:25** This is the great and spacious sea; there are creeping things without number, living creatures small and great,

**103:26** this dragon which You fashioned to play in it.

**103:27** All things wait expectantly upon You, to give them their food in due season.

**103:28** When You give to them, they shall gather it; and when You open Your hand, all things together shall be filled with goodness.

**103:29** But when You turn away Your countenance, they shall be troubled; You shall take away their spirit, and they shall expire and return to their dust.

**103:30** You shall send forth Your Spirit, and they shall be created; and You shall renew the face of the earth.

**103:31** May the glory of the Lord be forever; the Lord shall rejoice in His works;

**103:32** who looks upon the earth and makes it tremble, who touches the mountains and they smoke.

**103:33** I will sing to the Lord throughout my life; I will sing praises to my God as long as I exist.

**103:34** May my meditation be sweet to Him; as for me, I will rejoice in the Lord.

**103:35** May sinners vanish from the earth, and the lawless, so that they exist no more. Bless the Lord, O my soul.

## Translator Notes

- **103:1** — The Greek text includes the superscription "Of David," doubles the address "Lord, Lord," and renders the royal attributes as "praise" (exomologesis) and "splendor" (euprepeia).
- **103:6** — The Greek masculine pronoun "His" (autou) grammatically refers back to God—echoing the garment imagery of verse 2—rather than to the feminine noun "earth."
- **103:16** — The Greek reads "the trees of the plain" (tou pediou), reflecting a Septuagint textual tradition distinct from the Masoretic Hebrew "trees of the LORD."
- **103:17** — This Greek critical text reads the singular dative "for it" (auto) with "leads the way" (hegeitai), whereas some other Greek witnesses read the plural genitive "takes the lead over them" (auton).
- **103:26** — This Greek critical text omits the clause concerning ships passing through, moving directly from the sea creatures of verse 25 to the "dragon" (drakon) fashioned to play in the sea.
