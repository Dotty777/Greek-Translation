## Text

**2:1** Why did the nations rage, and the peoples meditate upon vain things?

**2:2** The kings of the earth took their stand, and the rulers were gathered together against the Lord and against His Anointed. Interlude.

**2:3** "Let us burst Their bonds apart, and cast away Their yoke from us."

**2:4** He who dwells in the heavens shall laugh at them, and the Lord shall deride them.

**2:5** Then He shall speak to them in His wrath, and in His fury He shall dismay them.

**2:6** Yet I was appointed by Him upon Zion, His holy mountain,

**2:7** proclaiming the decree of the Lord. The Lord said to Me, "You are My Son; this day I have begotten You.

**2:8** Ask of Me, and I shall give You nations for Your inheritance, and as Your possession the ends of the earth.

**2:9** You shall shepherd them with an iron rod; like a potter's vessel You shall shatter them."

**2:10** And now, O kings, understand; be instructed, all you who judge the earth.

**2:11** Serve the Lord in fear, and rejoice in Him with trembling.

**2:12** Lay hold of instruction, lest the Lord become angry and you perish from the righteous way, when His fury is swiftly kindled. Blessed are all who trust in Him.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Greek term rendered "Interlude" (diapsalma) denotes a musical pause corresponding to the Hebrew "Selah"; it appears here in the Septuagint though absent in the Hebrew text.
- **2:6** — Unlike the Hebrew text ("I have set My King"), the Septuagint uses the passive voice spoken by the Anointed One ("I was appointed by Him"), and this Greek text omits the word "king."
- **2:9** — The Greek verb means "shepherd" (reflecting an alternate vocalization of the Hebrew consonants, as quoted in Revelation 2:27; 12:5; 19:15) rather than "break" as in the Masoretic Text.
- **2:12** — Where the Hebrew text is traditionally rendered "Kiss the Son," the Septuagint reads "Lay hold of instruction," and also specifies "the righteous way" rather than simply "the way."
