## Text

**95:1** When the house is being built after the captivity; a song of David. Sing to the Lord a new song; sing to the Lord, all the earth.

**95:2** Sing to the Lord, bless His name; proclaim the good tidings of His salvation day by day.

**95:3** Announce His glory among the nations, His wondrous deeds among all the peoples.

**95:4** For great is the Lord and exceedingly to be praised; He is to be feared above all the gods.

**95:5** For all the gods of the nations are demons, but the Lord made the heavens.

**95:6** Thanksgiving and beauty are before Him; holiness and magnificence are in His sanctuary.

**95:7** Bring to the Lord, O families of the nations, bring to the Lord glory and honor.

**95:8** Bring to the Lord glory for His name; take up sacrifices and enter into His courts.

**95:9** Worship the Lord in His holy court; let all the earth be shaken before His presence.

**95:10** Say among the nations, "The Lord reigns; indeed, He has firmly established the world, which shall not be shaken; He shall judge peoples in uprightness."

**95:11** Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth exult; let the sea be shaken, and its fullness.

**95:12** The plains shall rejoice, and all things that are in them; then all the trees of the forest shall exult

**95:13** before the presence of the Lord, for He is coming, for He is coming to judge the earth; He shall judge the world in righteousness, and peoples in His truth.

## Translator Notes

- **95:1** — The Septuagint superscription ("When the house is being built after the captivity; a song of David") is not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Psalm 96), linking the psalm to the post-exilic rebuilding of the temple.
- **95:5** — The Greek renders the gods of the nations as daimonia ("demons"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "idols" or "worthless things."
- **95:6** — The Septuagint reads exomologesis ("thanksgiving" or "confession") and "beauty," followed by "holiness and magnificence," differing from the Hebrew terms for splendor, majesty, and strength.
- **95:9** — The Greek reads "in His holy court" (en aule hagia autou), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "in the splendor of holiness."
- **95:10** — The Septuagint uses the active verb katortosen ("He has firmly established") rather than the passive "is established," and the aorist ebasileusen ("reigns") functions as an ingressive aorist proclaiming the Lord's royal enthronement.
