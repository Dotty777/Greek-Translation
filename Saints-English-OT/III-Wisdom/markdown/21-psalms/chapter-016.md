## Text

**16:1** A Prayer of David. Hear my righteousness, O Lord; attend to my supplication. Give ear to my prayer, not from deceitful lips.

**16:2** Let my judgment proceed from Your presence; let my eyes behold uprightness.

**16:3** You have tested my heart; You have visited me in the night; You have tried me with fire, and no unrighteousness was found in me.

**16:4** So that my mouth may not speak of the works of men, because of the words of Your lips I have kept hard ways.

**16:5** Establish my steps in Your paths, so that my steps may not be shaken.

**16:6** I have cried aloud, for You have heard me, O God; incline Your ear to me, and hear my words.

**16:7** Make wondrous Your mercies, O You who save those who hope in You from those who resist Your right hand.

**16:8** Guard me as the pupil of the eye; in the shelter of Your wings You shall cover me

**16:9** from the face of the ungodly who have afflicted me. My enemies have encompassed my soul;

**16:10** they have closed up their fat; their mouth has spoken arrogance.

**16:11** Casting me out, they have now encircled me; they have set their eyes to bend down to the earth.

**16:12** They lay in wait for me like a lion ready for prey, and like a young lion dwelling in secret places.

**16:13** Arise, O Lord, confront them and overthrow them; deliver my soul from the ungodly, Your sword

**16:14** from the enemies of Your hand. O Lord, dismissing them from the earth, divide them in their life; and their belly has been filled with Your hidden things; they have been satisfied with swine's flesh, and they have left the remnants to their infants.

**16:15** But as for me, in righteousness I shall appear before Your face; I shall be satisfied when Your glory appears.

## Translator Notes

- **16:2** — The Greek reads "my eyes" (hoi ophthalmoi mou), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Your eyes."
- **16:4** — The Greek reads "hard ways" (hodous skleras) as the difficult path faithfully kept by the psalmist, differing from the Masoretic Text's "paths of the violent."
- **16:13** — The Greek leaves "Your sword" (rhomphaian sou) in the accusative without a separate verb, linking it directly into verse 14.
- **16:14** — The Greek text here reads "dismissing them from the earth" (apolyon apo ges, a manuscript variant alongside "from the few," apo oligon) and "swine's flesh" (hyeion, rather than "sons," huion).
