## Text

**119:1** A Song of the Ascents. To the Lord I cried out in my affliction, and He heard me.

**119:2** O Lord, deliver my soul from unjust lips and from a deceitful tongue.

**119:3** What shall be given to you, and what shall be added to you, against a deceitful tongue?

**119:4** The arrows of the mighty one, sharpened, together with the coals of the wilderness.

**119:5** Woe to me, for my sojourning has been prolonged; I have encamped among the tents of Kedar.

**119:6** My soul has long sojourned with those who hate peace.

**119:7** I was peaceable; whenever I spoke to them, they made war against me without cause.

## Translator Notes

- **119:3** — The Greek uses the prepositional phrase pros glossan dolian ("against a deceitful tongue") rather than addressing the tongue directly in the vocative as in the Hebrew text.
- **119:4** — The Septuagint translates the desert-shrub coals with the adjective eremikois ("of the wilderness" or "desert"), emphasizing desolation.
- **119:5** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text names the region of Meshech, the Greek reads a verb meaning "prolonged" (he paroikia mou emakrynthe, "my sojourning has been prolonged").
- **119:7** — The Greek adds dorean ("without cause"), heightening the unprovoked hostility of the speaker's adversaries; in this Greek text, "with those who hate peace" is joined to verse 6.
