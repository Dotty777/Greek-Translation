## Text

**85:1** A prayer of David. Incline Your ear, O Lord, and hear me, because I am poor and needy.

**85:2** Guard my soul, for I am devout; save Your servant, O my God, who hopes in You.

**85:3** Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I will cry out to You all the day.

**85:4** Gladden the soul of Your servant, for to You, O Lord, I have lifted up my soul.

**85:5** For You, O Lord, are good and gentle, and rich in mercy toward all who call upon You.

**85:6** Listen, O Lord, to my prayer, and give heed to the voice of my supplication.

**85:7** In the day of my affliction I cried out to You, for You heard me.

**85:8** There is none like You among the gods, O Lord, nor is there anything like Your works.

**85:9** All the nations, as many as You have made, shall come and worship before You, O Lord, and they shall glorify Your name;

**85:10** for You are great and work wonders; You alone are the great God.

**85:11** Guide me, O Lord, in Your way, and I will walk in Your truth; let my heart rejoice to fear Your name.

**85:12** I will give thanks to You, O Lord my God, with my whole heart, and I will glorify Your name forever;

**85:13** for Your mercy toward me is great, and You have rescued my soul from the lowest Hades.

**85:14** O God, lawless men have risen up against me, and an assembly of mighty men has sought my soul; they have not set You before them.

**85:15** Yet You, O Lord God, are compassionate and merciful, longsuffering, abundant in mercy, and true.

**85:16** Look upon me and have mercy on me; give Your might to Your servant, and save the son of Your handmaid.

**85:17** Work a sign with me for good, and let those who hate me see it and be put to shame, because You, O Lord, have helped me and comforted me.

## Translator Notes

- **85:7** — The Greek uses past-tense verbs ("I cried out... You heard me"), recalling answered prayer rather than the future tense found in renderings of the Hebrew text.
- **85:10** — This Greek text concludes with the emphatic epithet "the great [God]" (ho megas) following "You alone are God."
- **85:11** — The Septuagint reads "let my heart rejoice," reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew consonantal root rendered "unite my heart" in the Masoretic Text.
- **85:14** — Greek paranomoi ("lawless men") and krataion ("mighty men") correspond to "proud" and "violent" men in the Hebrew tradition.
