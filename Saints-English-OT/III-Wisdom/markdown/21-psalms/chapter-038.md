## Text

**38:1** For the end, for Jeduthun; a song of David.

**38:2** I said, "I will guard my ways, that I may not sin with my tongue." I set a watch over my mouth while the sinner stood before me.

**38:3** I became mute and was humbled, and kept silent from good things, and my pain was renewed.

**38:4** My heart grew hot within me, and in my meditation a fire shall be kindled; I spoke with my tongue:

**38:5** "Make known to me, O Lord, my end, and what the number of my days is, that I may know what I lack.

**38:6** Behold, You have made my days old, and my substance is as nothing before You; surely all things are vanity, every living man. Interlude.

**38:7** Indeed, man walks about in an image, yet in vain they are troubled; he stores up treasures, and does not know for whom he shall gather them.

**38:8** And now, what is my endurance? Is it not the Lord? And my substance is from You.

**38:9** Deliver me from all my iniquities; You have made me a reproach to the foolish.

**38:10** I became mute and did not open my mouth, because You are the One who made me.

**38:11** Remove Your scourges from me; by the strength of Your hand I have fainted.

**38:12** With rebukes for iniquity You have chastened man, and caused his soul to melt away like a spider; surely every man is troubled in vain. Interlude.

**38:13** Hear my prayer and my supplication; give ear to my tears. Do not pass by in silence, for I am a sojourner in the earth and a stranger, as all my fathers were.

**38:14** Spare me, that I may be refreshed before I depart and am no more."

## Translator Notes

- **38:6** — Greek palaias literally means "old" (whereas the Hebrew reads "handbreadths," possibly echoed by Greek palaiste, "handbreadth"). "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, a musical or liturgical pause corresponding to Hebrew Selah.
- **38:9** — The Greek uses the indicative "You have made me" (edokas me) rather than the negative petition ("do not make me") found in the Masoretic Text.
- **38:12** — Greek hos arakhnen ("like a spider," or possibly "like a spider's web") and "his soul" (ten psykhen autou) reflect a distinct Septuagint reading compared with the Hebrew "like a moth what he treasures."
- **38:13** — The Greek text reads "in the earth" (en te ge), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "with You."
