## Text

**18:1** For the end: a psalm of David.

**18:2** The heavens recount the glory of God, and the firmament announces the work of His hands.

**18:3** Day to day pours forth speech, and night to night announces knowledge.

**18:4** There are no utterances nor words whose voices are not heard;

**18:5** Into all the earth their sound has gone forth, and their words to the ends of the inhabited world.

**18:6** In the sun He has set His tabernacle; and he, like a bridegroom coming forth from his bridal chamber, shall exult like a giant to run his course.

**18:7** From the extremity of heaven is his going forth, and his goal reaches to the extremity of heaven; and there is none who shall be hidden from his heat.

**18:8** The law of the Lord is blameless, restoring souls; the testimony of the Lord is faithful, giving wisdom to babes.

**18:9** The ordinances of the Lord are upright, gladdening the heart; the commandment of the Lord is radiant, giving light to the eyes.

**18:10** The fear of the Lord is pure, abiding forever and ever; the judgments of the Lord are true, justified altogether,

**18:11** more desirable than gold and much precious stone, and sweeter than honey and the honeycomb.

**18:12** For indeed Your servant keeps them; in keeping them there is great recompense.

**18:13** Who shall understand offenses? Cleanse me from my hidden faults,

**18:14** and spare Your servant from strangers; if they do not gain dominion over me, then I shall be blameless, and I shall be cleansed from great sin.

**18:15** And the utterances of my mouth and the meditation of my heart shall be well-pleasing before You continually, O Lord, my Helper and my Redeemer.

## Translator Notes

- **18:5** — Greek reads "their sound" (phthongos), which is quoted in Romans 10:18, whereas the Masoretic Text reads "their measuring line."
- **18:6** — The Septuagint reads "In the sun He has set His tabernacle," whereas the Masoretic Text reads "In them He has set a tent for the sun."
- **18:11** — Greek reads "much precious stone" rather than the Masoretic Text's "much fine gold."
- **18:14** — Greek reads "strangers" (allotriōn), reflecting a Hebrew consonantal reading for "strangers" rather than the Masoretic Text's "presumptuous sins."
