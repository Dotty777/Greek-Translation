## Text

**25:1** Of David. Judge me, O Lord, for I have walked in my innocence, and hoping in the Lord I shall never be shaken.

**25:2** Examine me, O Lord, and test me; refine with fire my inward parts and my heart.

**25:3** For Your mercy is before my eyes, and I have been well pleased in Your truth.

**25:4** I have not sat with a council of vanity, and with transgressors I shall never enter;

**25:5** I have hated the assembly of evildoers, and with the ungodly I shall never sit.

**25:6** I will wash my hands among the innocent, and I will go around Your altar,

**25:7** to hear the voice of praise, and to declare all Your wondrous works.

**25:8** O Lord, I have loved the beauty of Your house and the place of the tabernacle of Your glory.

**25:9** Do not destroy my soul together with the ungodly, nor my life with men of blood,

**25:10** in whose hands are iniquities; their right hand is filled with bribes.

**25:11** But as for me, I have walked in my innocence; redeem me and have mercy on me.

**25:12** For my foot has stood in uprightness; in the assemblies I will bless You, O Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **25:2** — The Greek verb means "refine with fire," and "inward parts" translates Greek nephrous (lit. "kidneys"), representing the seat of one's deepest affections and conscience.
- **25:3** — The Greek reads "I have been well pleased" (euerestesa), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "I have walked."
- **25:6** — Greek en athoois is plural, meaning "among the innocent," though some interpreters take it abstractly as "in innocence."
- **25:7** — The Greek uses the active infinitive "to hear" (akousai), in contrast to the Hebrew Masoretic Text's causative sense, "to proclaim aloud."
