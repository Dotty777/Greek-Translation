## Text

**90:1** A Praise of a Song, of David. He who dwells in the help of the Most High shall abide in the shelter of the God of heaven.

**90:2** He shall say to God, "You are my protector; and my refuge is my God, I will hope in Him."

**90:3** For He Himself shall bring deliverance from the snare of hunters, and from a troubling word.

**90:4** With His shoulders He shall overshadow you, and under His wings you shall hope; with a shield His truth shall surround you.

**90:5** You shall not fear the dread of night, nor an arrow flying by day,

**90:6** nor a thing passing through in darkness, nor calamity and a noonday demon.

**90:7** A thousand shall fall at your side, and ten thousand at your right hand, yet it shall not draw near to you.

**90:8** Only with your eyes shall you observe, and you shall behold the recompense of sinners.

**90:9** For You, O Lord, are my hope; you have set the Most High as your refuge.

**90:10** Evils shall not approach you, and a scourge shall not draw near to your tent.

**90:11** For He shall command His angels concerning you, to guard you carefully in your ways.

**90:12** Upon their hands they shall lift you up, lest at any time you strike your foot against a stone.

**90:13** Upon the asp and the basilisk you shall tread, and you shall trample underfoot the lion and the dragon.

**90:14** "Because he has hoped in Me, I will deliver him; I will shelter him, because he has known My name.

**90:15** He shall call upon Me, and I will hear him; I am with him in affliction, and I will rescue and glorify him.

**90:16** With length of days I will fill him, and I will show him My salvation."

## Translator Notes

- **90:1** — The Septuagint adds the superscription attributing the psalm to David and reads "in the help of the Most High" and "of the God of heaven," where the Masoretic Text reads "in the secret place" and "of the Almighty."
- **90:3** — "A troubling word" reflects the Septuagint reading the Hebrew consonants for "pestilence" as "word"; the same consonantal vocalization underlies "a thing" in verse 6.
- **90:6** — "A noonday demon" is the distinctive Septuagint rendering corresponding to "the destruction that wastes at noonday" in the Masoretic Text.
- **90:13** — The Greek reads "asp and basilisk" in the first clause, where the Masoretic Text reads "lion and cobra."
