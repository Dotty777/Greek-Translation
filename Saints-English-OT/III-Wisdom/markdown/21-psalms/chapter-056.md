## Text

**56:1** To the end: "Do not destroy." Of David, for a pillar inscription, when he fled from the face of Saul into the cave.

**56:2** Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me, for in You my soul has trusted; and in the shadow of Your wings I will hope until iniquity passes by.

**56:3** I will cry aloud to God the Most High, to God who has done good to me. Interlude.

**56:4** He sent forth from heaven and saved me; He gave over to reproach those who trample upon me. God sent forth His mercy and His truth,

**56:5** and He delivered my soul from the midst of young lions. I lay down troubled; the sons of men—their teeth are a weapon and arrows, and their tongue is a sharp sword.

**56:6** Be exalted over the heavens, O God, and may Your glory be over all the earth.

**56:7** They prepared snares for my feet and bowed down my soul; they dug a pit before my face and fell into it. Interlude.

**56:8** My heart is ready, O God, my heart is ready; I will sing and make melody.

**56:9** Awake, my glory! Awake, psaltery and harp! I will awaken at dawn.

**56:10** I will give thanks to You among the peoples, O Lord; I will sing psalms to You among the nations,

**56:11** for Your mercy has been magnified even to the heavens, and Your truth to the clouds.

**56:12** Be exalted over the heavens, O God, and may Your glory be over all the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **56:1** — Greek "for a pillar inscription" (stelographia) renders the Hebrew heading term miktam; this psalm corresponds to Psalm 57 in the Hebrew Masoretic numbering.
- **56:3** — "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, a musical pause or instrumental break corresponding to Hebrew Selah.
- **56:9** — The Greek uses the passive future with a temporal genitive ("I will awaken at dawn"), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("I will awaken the dawn").
