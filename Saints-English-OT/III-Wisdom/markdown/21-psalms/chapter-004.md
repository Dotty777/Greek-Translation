## Text

**4:1** For the end, in psalms; a song of David.

**4:2** When I called, the God of my righteousness heard me; in distress You made room for me. Have compassion on me, and hear my prayer.

**4:3** O sons of men, how long will you be heavy of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek falsehood? Interlude.

**4:4** And know that the Lord has made His holy one wondrous; the Lord will hear me when I cry out to Him.

**4:5** Be angry, and do not sin; for the things you speak in your heart, feel compunction upon your beds. Interlude.

**4:6** Offer a sacrifice of righteousness, and hope in the Lord.

**4:7** Many say, "Who will show us good things?" The light of Your countenance has been marked upon us, O Lord.

**4:8** You have put gladness into my heart; from the fruit of their wheat and wine and oil they have been multiplied.

**4:9** In peace I will at once lie down and sleep; for You alone, O Lord, have made me dwell in hope.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Greek eis to telos ("for the end") is the Septuagint's standard superscription rendering of the Hebrew musical heading often translated "to the choirmaster," giving the psalm a culminating or eschatological sense.
- **4:3** — The Septuagint reads "heavy of heart" (barykardioi) where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "my honor into shame," and marks a musical pause with diapsalma ("Interlude," corresponding to Hebrew selah).
- **4:7** — Greek esemeiothe ("has been marked" or "signed") reflects a reading of the Hebrew verb from the root for "sign" or "banner," rather than the Masoretic "lift up."
- **4:8** — The Septuagint includes "and oil" alongside wheat and wine and reads "from the fruit of" where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "more than in the time when."
