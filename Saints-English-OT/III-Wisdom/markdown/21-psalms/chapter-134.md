## Text

**134:1** Hallelujah. Praise the name of the Lord; praise the Lord, you servants,

**134:2** who stand in the house of the Lord, in the courts of the house of our God.

**134:3** Praise the Lord, for the Lord is good; sing psalms to His name, for it is lovely;

**134:4** for the Lord has chosen Jacob for Himself, Israel for His own treasured possession.

**134:5** For I have come to know that the Lord is great, and that our Lord is above all the gods.

**134:6** All that the Lord willed, He has done, in heaven and on earth, in the seas and in the deeps;

**134:7** bringing up clouds from the farthest end of the earth, He has made lightnings for the rain; He brings forth winds from His treasuries.

**134:8** He struck the firstborn of Egypt, from man even to beast;

**134:9** He sent forth signs and wonders in your midst, O Egypt, upon Pharaoh and upon all his servants.

**134:10** He struck many nations and slew mighty kings—

**134:11** Sihon king of the Amorites, and Og king of Bashan, and all the kingdoms of Canaan—

**134:12** and gave their land as an inheritance, an inheritance to Israel His servant.

**134:13** O Lord, Your name endures forever; O Lord, Your memorial is from generation to generation.

**134:14** The Lord has compassion on His people, and concerning His servants He will be comforted.

**134:15** The idols of the nations are silver and gold, the works of the hands of men.

**134:16** They have a mouth, yet do not speak; they have eyes, yet will not see;

**134:17** they have ears, yet will not give ear; for neither is there any breath in their mouth.

**134:18** May all who make them become like them, and all who put their trust in them.

**134:19** O house of Israel, bless the Lord; O house of Aaron, bless the Lord;

**134:20** O house of Levi, bless the Lord; you who fear the Lord, bless the Lord.

**134:21** Blessed is the Lord from Zion, He who dwells in Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **134:1** — The Greek opens with the transliterated "Hallelujah" and places "Lord" in the accusative as the direct object of "praise," addressing "servants" without the genitive "of the Lord" found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **134:12** — The Greek reads "Israel His servant," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Israel His people."
- **134:14** — This Greek text reads "has compassion on" (oikteirei) rather than "will judge" (krinei / MT yadin), paired with "will be comforted" (paraklethesetai), expressing God's consoling mercy toward His servants.
- **134:18** — The Greek uses the optative mood (genointo), expressing an imprecatory wish ("May ... become") rather than a simple declarative statement.
