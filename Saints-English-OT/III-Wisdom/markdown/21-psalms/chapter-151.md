## Text

**151:1** This psalm is written by David's own hand and is outside the number, when he fought in single combat with Goliath. I was small among my brethren, and the youngest in the house of my father; I tended the sheep of my father.

**151:2** My hands made a musical instrument; my fingers tuned a psaltery.

**151:3** And who will declare it to my Lord? The Lord Himself, He Himself hears.

**151:4** He Himself sent forth His messenger, and took me from the sheep of my father, and anointed me with the oil of His anointing.

**151:5** My brethren were handsome and tall, yet the Lord was not well pleased with them.

**151:6** I went forth to meet the foreigner, and he cursed me by his idols;

**151:7** but I drew the sword from his side and beheaded him, and took away reproach from the sons of Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **151:1** — Psalm 151 is a supernumerary psalm in the Septuagint, standing 'outside the number' of the one hundred fifty psalms; Greek idiographos denotes something written in one's own hand.
- **151:4** — Greek angelon can mean either 'messenger' (referring to the prophet Samuel who anointed David) or 'angel.'
- **151:6** — Greek allophylos literally means 'foreigner' or 'one of another tribe,' and serves as the Septuagint's standard designation for a Philistine.
- **151:7** — Literally 'having drawn the sword from him,' referring to Goliath's own sword at his side.
