## Text

**23:1** A psalm of David, for the first day of the week. To the Lord belongs the earth and its fullness, the inhabited world and all who dwell in it.

**23:2** He Himself founded it upon the seas, and upon the rivers He prepared it.

**23:3** Who shall ascend the mountain of the Lord, and who shall stand in His holy place?

**23:4** He who is innocent in hands and pure in heart, who has not received his soul in vain, and has not sworn with deceit to his neighbor.

**23:5** This man shall receive a blessing from the Lord, and mercy from God his Savior.

**23:6** This is the generation of those who seek Him, of those who seek the face of the God of Jacob. Interlude.

**23:7** Lift up your gates, O rulers, and be lifted up, O everlasting gates, and the King of glory shall enter in.

**23:8** Who is this, the King of glory? The Lord, strong and powerful; the Lord, powerful in war.

**23:9** Lift up your gates, O rulers, and be lifted up, O everlasting gates, and the King of glory shall enter in.

**23:10** Who is this, the King of glory? The Lord of hosts, He Himself is this King of glory.

## Translator Notes

- **23:1** — The Septuagint superscription adds "for the first day of the week" (της μιας σαββατων), reflecting the ancient liturgical use of this psalm on Sunday.
- **23:4** — Greek ελαβεν επι ματαιω την ψυχην αυτου ("received his soul in vain") reflects Septuagint idiom for taking something in vain (cf. Exod 20:7), and adds "to his neighbor" at the close of the verse.
- **23:6** — Greek διαψαλμα ("Interlude") is the standard Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew notation Selah; the verse also reads "the God of Jacob."
- **23:7** — Greek αρατε πυλας, οι αρχοντες υμων literally means "Lift up gates, your rulers" (repeated in v. 9), interpreting the Hebrew "heads" as "rulers" or "princes" rather than "Lift up your heads, O gates."
