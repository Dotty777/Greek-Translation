## Text

**50:1** For the end; a psalm of David,

**50:2** when Nathan the prophet came to him, when he had gone in to Bathsheba.

**50:3** Have mercy on me, O God, according to Your great mercy; and according to the multitude of Your compassions, blot out my transgression.

**50:4** Wash me yet more from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin.

**50:5** For I myself know my iniquity, and my sin is before me continually.

**50:6** Against You alone I have sinned, and I have done what is evil before You, so that You may be justified in Your words, and prevail when You are judged.

**50:7** For behold, in iniquities I was conceived, and in sins my mother conceived me.

**50:8** For behold, You have loved truth; the unseen and hidden things of Your wisdom You have made known to me.

**50:9** You shall sprinkle me with hyssop, and I shall be cleansed; You shall wash me, and I shall be made whiter than snow.

**50:10** You shall make me hear exultation and gladness; the humbled bones shall rejoice.

**50:11** Turn away Your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities.

**50:12** Create a clean heart in me, O God, and renew an upright spirit within my inward parts.

**50:13** Do not cast me off from Your presence, and do not remove Your Holy Spirit from me.

**50:14** Restore to me the exultation of Your salvation, and establish me with a governing spirit.

**50:15** I will teach the lawless Your ways, and the ungodly shall return to You.

**50:16** Deliver me from bloodguilt, O God, the God of my salvation; my tongue shall rejoice in Your righteousness,

**50:17** O Lord. You shall open my lips, and my mouth shall declare Your praise.

**50:18** For if You had desired sacrifice, I would have given it; You will take no pleasure in whole burnt offerings.

**50:19** A sacrifice to God is a broken spirit; a broken and humbled heart God will not despise.

**50:20** Do good, O Lord, in Your good pleasure to Zion, and let the walls of Jerusalem be built.

**50:21** Then You shall be pleased with a sacrifice of righteousness, an offering and whole burnt offerings; then they shall offer up calves upon Your altar.

## Translator Notes

- **50:6** — The Greek passive "when You are judged" (quoted in Romans 3:4) reflects the Septuagint reading, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has the active "when You judge."
- **50:8** — The Septuagint reads "the unseen and hidden things of Your wisdom," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of truth in the inward parts and wisdom in the hidden part.
- **50:14** — Greek hegemoniko ("governing" or "guiding") denotes a ruling, directing spirit, differing from the Hebrew "willing spirit."
- **50:17** — In the Greek text, the vocative "O Lord" begins verse 17 to close the clause from verse 16, followed by the future indicative "You shall open my lips."
