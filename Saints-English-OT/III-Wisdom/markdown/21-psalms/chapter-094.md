## Text

**94:1** A song of praise of David. Come, let us exult to the Lord; let us shout joyfully to God our Savior;

**94:2** let us come before His face with thanksgiving, and with psalms let us shout joyfully to Him.

**94:3** For a great God is the Lord, and a great King over all the gods.

**94:4** For the Lord will not reject His people, because in His hand are the ends of the earth, and the heights of the mountains are His;

**94:5** for His is the sea, and He Himself made it, and His hands fashioned the dry land.

**94:6** Come, let us worship and fall down before Him, and let us weep before the Lord who made us;

**94:7** for He Himself is our God, and we are a people of His pasture and sheep of His hand.

**94:8** Today, if you hear His voice, do not harden your hearts as in the provocation, according to the day of testing in the wilderness,

**94:9** where your fathers tested, proved, and saw My works.

**94:10** Forty years I was indignant with that generation, and I said, "They always go astray in their heart, and they themselves have not known My ways."

**94:11** So I swore in My wrath, "They shall surely not enter into My rest."

## Translator Notes

- **94:4** — The opening clause, "For the Lord will not reject His people," is a distinctive Septuagint reading absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text of Psalm 95:4 (compare 93:14).
- **94:6** — The Septuagint reads klausomen ("let us weep"), a distinctive Greek reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "let us kneel."
- **94:8** — Greek parapikrasmos ("provocation") and peirasmos ("testing") translate the Hebrew place names Meribah and Massah according to their meanings, as also reflected in Hebrews 3:8.
- **94:11** — "They shall surely not enter into My rest" translates the Semitic oath idiom Ei eiseleusontai (literally "If they shall enter"), expressing an emphatic solemn negation.
