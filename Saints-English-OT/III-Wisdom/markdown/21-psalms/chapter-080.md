## Text

**80:1** For the end, concerning the winepresses; a psalm of Asaph.

**80:2** Rejoice greatly in God our helper; shout aloud to the God of Jacob.

**80:3** Take up a psalm and sound the timbrel, the pleasant psaltery with the harp.

**80:4** Blow the trumpet at the new moon, on the notable day of our feast.

**80:5** For it is a statute for Israel, and an ordinance of the God of Jacob.

**80:6** He established it as a testimony in Joseph, when he went forth out of the land of Egypt; he heard a tongue that he did not know.

**80:7** He removed his back from burdens; his hands served at the basket.

**80:8** "In affliction you called upon Me, and I delivered you; I answered you in the secret place of the storm; I tested you at the water of Contradiction." Interlude.

**80:9** "Hear, O My people, and I will speak to you; O Israel, and I will solemnly testify to you. If you listen to Me,

**80:10** there shall be no new god among you, nor shall you worship a foreign god.

**80:11** For I am the Lord your God, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt; open your mouth wide, and I will fill it.

**80:12** Yet My people did not listen to My voice, and Israel did not give heed to Me.

**80:13** So I sent them away according to the practices of their hearts; they shall walk in their own practices.

**80:14** If the people, Israel, had listened to Me, if they had walked in My ways,

**80:15** in a moment I would have humbled their enemies, and upon those who afflict them I would have laid My hand."

**80:16** The enemies of the Lord lied to Him, and their time shall be forever.

**80:17** And He fed them from the choicest of wheat, and with honey from the rock He satisfied them.

## Translator Notes

- **80:1** — Greek hyper ton lenon ('concerning the winepresses') interprets the Hebrew term Gittith by connecting it with gath ('winepress').
- **80:4** — Greek eusemo ('notable' or 'distinguished') renders the Hebrew term commonly understood as the full moon.
- **80:7** — Greek edouleusan ('served' or 'labored') depicts Israel's servitude at the basket, whereas the Masoretic Hebrew speaks of hands being freed from the basket.
- **80:8** — Greek Antilogias ('Contradiction' or 'Strife') translates the Hebrew place name Meribah; Greek diapsalma ('Interlude') corresponds to Hebrew Selah.
