## Text

**108:1** To the end; a psalm of David. O God, do not pass over my praise in silence,

**108:2** for the mouth of a sinner and the mouth of a deceitful man have been opened against me; they have spoken against me with a deceitful tongue;

**108:3** and with words of hatred they surrounded me, and fought against me without cause.

**108:4** Instead of loving me, they slandered me, yet I kept praying;

**108:5** and they set evil against me in return for good, and hatred in return for my love.

**108:6** Appoint a sinner over him, and let an accuser stand at his right hand;

**108:7** when he is judged, may he go forth condemned, and let his prayer become sin.

**108:8** Let his days be few, and may another receive his office;

**108:9** let his sons become orphans, and his wife a widow;

**108:10** tossed about, let his sons wander and beg; let them be cast out from their ruined dwellings.

**108:11** And let a creditor search out all that belongs to him; let strangers plunder all his labors;

**108:12** let there be no helper for him, nor let there be anyone compassionate to his orphans;

**108:13** let his children be given over to utter destruction; in one generation let his name be blotted out.

**108:14** May the sin of his fathers be remembered before the Lord, and may the sin of his mother not be blotted out;

**108:15** let them be before the Lord continually, and may their memorial be utterly destroyed from the earth.

**108:16** Because he did not remember to show mercy, and pursued the needy and the poor and the one pierced in heart, to put him to death.

**108:17** And he loved a curse, and it shall come to him; and he did not desire blessing, and it shall be far removed from him.

**108:18** And he clothed himself with a curse as with a garment, and it entered like water into his inward parts and like oil into his bones;

**108:19** let it be to him like a garment that he wraps around himself, and like a belt with which he girds himself continually.

**108:20** This is the work of those who slander me before the Lord, and of those who speak evil against my soul.

**108:21** Yet You, O Lord, Lord, show mercy to me for the sake of Your name, for Your mercy is good;

**108:22** deliver me; for I am poor and needy, and my heart is troubled within me.

**108:23** As a shadow, when it declines, is taken away, I was shaken off like locusts.

**108:24** My knees have grown weak from fasting, and my flesh has been altered because of oil.

**108:25** And I became a reproach to them; they saw me, they shook their heads.

**108:26** Help me, O Lord my God; save me according to Your great mercy;

**108:27** and let them know that this is Your hand, and that You, O Lord, have done it.

**108:28** They will curse, but You will bless; let those who rise up against me be put to shame, while Your servant shall rejoice.

**108:29** Let those who slander me be clothed with disgrace, and let them wrap themselves with shame as with their cloak.

**108:30** I will give thanks to the Lord greatly with my mouth, and in the midst of many I will praise Him;

**108:31** for He has stood at the right hand of the needy, to save me from those who pursue my soul.

## Translator Notes

- **108:1** — "To the end" renders the Septuagint superscription Eis to telos, corresponding to the Hebrew musical heading often translated "To the Chief Musician."
- **108:4** — The Greek infinitive phrase anti tou agapan me is syntactically ambiguous, meaning either "instead of loving me" (with "me" as object) or "in return for my loving them" (with "me" as subject, paralleling v. 5).
- **108:6** — Greek diabolos means "accuser" or "slanderer," fitting the judicial imagery of v. 7, though later tradition also associated the term with the devil.
- **108:23** — The Greek text reads the third-person passive verb antanerethe ("is taken away") with "shadow" as its subject, followed by the first-person "I was shaken off," and plural "locusts."
- **108:24** — "Because of oil" renders the literal Septuagint prepositional phrase di' elaion, which reflects a Hebrew idiom expressing the lack of oil or fatness.
