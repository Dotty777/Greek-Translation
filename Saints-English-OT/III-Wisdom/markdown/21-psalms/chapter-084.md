## Text

**84:1** For the end; a psalm for the sons of Korah.

**84:2** You have been well pleased with Your land, O Lord; You have turned back the captivity of Jacob.

**84:3** You have forgiven the iniquities of Your people; You have covered their sins. Interlude.

**84:4** You have caused Your wrath to cease; You have turned back from the wrath of Your indignation.

**84:5** Turn us back, O God of our salvation, and turn Your indignation away from us.

**84:6** Will You be angry with us forever? Or will You prolong Your wrath from generation to generation?

**84:7** O God, You will turn and give us life, and Your people shall rejoice in You.

**84:8** Show us Your mercy, O Lord, and may You grant us Your salvation, O Lord.

**84:9** I will hear what the Lord God will speak within me, for He will speak peace to His people and to His holy ones, and to those who turn their heart toward Him.

**84:10** Indeed, His salvation is near to those who fear Him, so that glory may abide in our land.

**84:11** Mercy and truth met one another; righteousness and peace kissed each other.

**84:12** Truth has sprung forth from the earth, and righteousness has looked down from heaven.

**84:13** For the Lord indeed will give goodness, and our land shall yield its fruit.

**84:14** Righteousness shall go before Him, and shall set His footsteps upon the way.

## Translator Notes

- **84:3** — Greek diapsalma denotes a musical interlude or pause in psalmody, corresponding to Hebrew Selah.
- **84:7** — The Greek reads as a confident declaration ("You will turn and give us life") rather than the rhetorical question found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **84:8** — This Greek text repeats the vocative "O Lord" in the second clause and uses the optative mood ("may You grant").
- **84:9** — The Septuagint reads "and to those who turn their heart toward Him," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text warns against turning back to folly.
