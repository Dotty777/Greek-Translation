## Text

**5:1** For the end, concerning her who inherits; a psalm of David.

**5:2** Give ear to my words, O Lord; understand my cry.

**5:3** Give heed to the voice of my supplication, my King and my God; for to You I will pray, O Lord.

**5:4** In the morning You shall hear my voice; in the morning I will stand before You and watch.

**5:5** For You are not a God who desires lawlessness, nor shall an evildoer sojourn with You.

**5:6** Transgressors shall not abide before Your eyes; You have hated, O Lord, all who work iniquity.

**5:7** You shall destroy all who speak falsehood; the Lord abhors a man of bloodshed and deceit.

**5:8** But as for me, in the multitude of Your mercy I will enter into Your house; I will worship toward Your holy temple in fear of You.

**5:9** O Lord, guide me in Your righteousness because of my enemies; make my way straight before You.

**5:10** For there is no truth in their mouth; their heart is vain. Their throat is an open tomb; with their tongues they have dealt deceitfully.

**5:11** Judge them, O God; let them fall away from their counsels. According to the multitude of their impieties cast them out, for they have provoked You to bitterness, O Lord.

**5:12** And let all who hope in You be glad in You; forever they shall exult, and You shall dwell among them. And all who love Your name shall glory in You,

**5:13** for You bless the righteous. O Lord, as with a shield of favor You have crowned us.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Greek hyper tes kleronomouses ("concerning her who inherits") interprets the Hebrew musical heading (ha-nechiloth, often linked to flutes) from the Hebrew root meaning "to inherit."
- **5:9** — The Septuagint reads "make my way straight before You," reversing the possessive pronouns found in the Masoretic Hebrew ("make Your way straight before my face").
- **5:12** — Greek kataskenoseis en autois ("You shall dwell among them," literally "tabernacle among them") renders the Hebrew verb as dwelling rather than covering or defending.
- **5:13** — The Septuagint ends with the first-person plural "You have crowned us" (hemas), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew has the third-person singular "You surround him."
