## Text

**6:1** For the end, in hymns, concerning the eighth; a psalm of David.

**6:2** O Lord, do not reprove me in Your wrath, nor chasten me in Your anger.

**6:3** Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am weak; heal me, for my bones are troubled.

**6:4** And my soul is exceedingly troubled; and You, O Lord—how long?

**6:5** Return, O Lord, deliver my soul; save me for the sake of Your mercy.

**6:6** For in death there is no one who remembers You; and in Hades who will give thanks to You?

**6:7** I have grown weary in my groaning; every night I will wash my bed; with my tears I will drench my couch.

**6:8** My eye is troubled because of wrath; I have grown old among all my enemies.

**6:9** Depart from me, all you who work lawlessness, for the Lord has heard the voice of my weeping.

**6:10** The Lord has heard my supplication; the Lord has received my prayer.

**6:11** Let all my enemies be put to shame and be exceedingly troubled; let them turn back and be put to shame exceedingly and swiftly.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — Greek hyper tes ogdoes ("concerning the eighth") translates the Hebrew musical term ha-sheminith, variously understood as an eight-stringed instrument, a lower octave, or the eighth day.
- **6:6** — Greek Hades is the Septuagint's regular rendering of Hebrew Sheol (the realm of the dead), and exomologesetai denotes grateful confession or praise.
- **6:8** — Greek thymou ("wrath") renders a Hebrew word for grief or vexation, and epalaiothen ("I have grown old") reads the verb as first-person singular rather than third-person singular referring to the eye.
