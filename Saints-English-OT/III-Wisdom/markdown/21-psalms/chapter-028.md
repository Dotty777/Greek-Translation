## Text

**28:1** A Psalm of David, for the closing of the tabernacle. Bring to the Lord, O sons of God, bring to the Lord young rams; bring to the Lord glory and honor.

**28:2** Bring to the Lord glory to His name; worship the Lord in His holy court.

**28:3** The voice of the Lord is upon the waters; the God of glory has thundered, the Lord is upon many waters.

**28:4** The voice of the Lord is in might; the voice of the Lord is in majesty.

**28:5** The voice of the Lord shatters cedars; the Lord shall shatter the cedars of Lebanon.

**28:6** And He shall beat them small like a calf, even Lebanon, and the beloved one like the young of unicorns.

**28:7** The voice of the Lord cleaves a flame of fire.

**28:8** The voice of the Lord shakes the wilderness; the Lord shall shake the wilderness of Kadesh.

**28:9** The voice of the Lord strengthens the deer and shall uncover the forests; and in His temple everyone declares glory.

**28:10** The Lord shall dwell in the flood, and the Lord shall sit as King forever.

**28:11** The Lord shall give strength to His people; the Lord shall bless His people in peace.

## Translator Notes

- **28:1** — The Septuagint superscription adds "for the closing of the tabernacle," referring to the closing solemn assembly of the Feast of Tabernacles, and preserves a double rendering of the underlying Hebrew as both "sons of God" and "young rams" (literally "sons of rams").
- **28:6** — Following the Septuagint, the Lord "beats them small" rather than making them skip, names "the beloved one" where the Masoretic Hebrew has Sirion, and speaks of "unicorns" (Greek monokeroton, literally one-horned beasts).
- **28:9** — Greek katartizomenou means "strengthens," "prepares," or "brings to fullness," diverging from the Masoretic Hebrew reading "makes the deer give birth."
