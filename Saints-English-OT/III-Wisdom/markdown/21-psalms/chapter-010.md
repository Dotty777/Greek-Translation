## Text

**10:1** For the end; a psalm of David. In the Lord I have put my trust; how will you say to my soul, "Flee to the mountains like a sparrow"?

**10:2** For behold, the sinners have bent a bow, they have made ready arrows in a quiver, to shoot down in a moonless night the upright in heart.

**10:3** For the things You have established, they have torn down; and what has the righteous one done?

**10:4** The Lord is in His holy temple; the Lord—His throne is in heaven. His eyes look upon the poor; His eyelids examine the sons of men.

**10:5** The Lord examines the righteous and the ungodly; but he who loves unrighteousness hates his own soul.

**10:6** He shall rain snares upon sinners; fire and brimstone and a tempestuous wind shall be the portion of their cup.

**10:7** For the Lord is righteous, and He has loved righteous deeds; His countenance has beheld uprightness.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The Greek superscription "For the end" (eis to telos) is the standard Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew musical heading, and the Greek reads the plural "mountains" and "sparrow" (strouthion).
- **10:2** — The Greek reads "in a quiver" (eis pharetran) rather than "upon the bowstring," and uses the rare compound skotomene, meaning a dark, moonless night.
- **10:3** — Addressing God directly, the Greek reads "the things You have established, they have torn down" (ha katertiso katheilon), differing from the Hebrew "if the foundations are destroyed."
- **10:5** — Whereas the Hebrew reads that God's soul hates the wicked and the lover of violence, the Greek states that the Lord examines both the righteous and the ungodly, while he who loves unrighteousness hates his own soul.
