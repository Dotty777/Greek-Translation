## Text

**3:1** Aleph. I am the man who sees poverty by the rod of His wrath upon me.

**3:2** He took me and led me away into darkness, and not light.

**3:3** Surely against me He turned His hand all the day.

**3:4** He made my flesh and my skin grow old; He shattered my bones.

**3:5** Beth. He built up against me, and surrounded my head, and labored.

**3:6** In dark places He seated me, like the dead of old.

**3:7** He built up against me, and I shall not go out; He made my bronze heavy.

**3:8** Gimel. Indeed, though I cry out and call aloud, He has blocked up my prayer.

**3:9** Daleth. He walled up my ways; He blocked my paths; He threw them into confusion.

**3:10** A bear lying in wait, He is to me a lion in secret places.

**3:11** He pursued one who had turned aside and brought me to a halt; He made me desolate.

**3:12** He. He stretched His bow and stationed me as a mark for an arrow;

**3:13** He drove into my kidneys the arrows of His quiver.

**3:14** I became a laughingstock to all my people, their song all the day.

**3:15** Vav. He sated me with bitterness; He made me drunk with gall.

**3:16** And He knocked out my teeth with gravel; He fed me ashes.

**3:17** And He cast away my soul from peace; I forgot good things.

**3:18** And my victory perished, and my hope from the Lord.

**3:19** Zayin. I remembered from my poverty and from persecution; my bitterness and gall shall be remembered,

**3:20** and my soul shall brood over me.

**3:21** This I shall set in my heart; therefore I shall wait patiently.

**3:25** Heth. Good is the Lord to those who wait for Him, to the soul that shall seek Him who is good.

**3:26** And he shall wait and be still for the salvation of the Lord.

**3:27** Teth. It is good for a man when he takes up a yoke in his youth;

**3:28** he shall sit alone and keep silent, because he has taken it upon himself.

**3:30** Yod. He shall give his cheek to the one who strikes him; he shall be sated with reproaches,

**3:31** for the Lord will not reject forever.

**3:32** Kaph. For the One who has humbled will show compassion, even according to the multitude of His mercy;

**3:33** He did not answer from His heart and humble the sons of man.

**3:34** Lamed. To humble under his feet all the prisoners of the earth,

**3:35** to turn aside the judgment of a man before the face of the Most High,

**3:36** to condemn a man when he is judged—the Lord has not spoken.

**3:37** Who spoke thus, and it came to pass? The Lord did not command it.

**3:38** Out of the mouth of the Most High, shall not evil things and good come forth?

**3:39** Mem. Why should a living mortal murmur, a man concerning his sin?

**3:40** Nun. Our way has been searched out and examined; and let us turn back even to the Lord;

**3:41** let us lift up our hearts upon our hands to the High One in heaven:

**3:42** We have sinned, we have acted ungodly, and You have not forgiven.

**3:43** Samekh. You covered over in wrath and drove us away; You slew, You did not spare.

**3:44** You covered Yourself with a cloud on account of prayer,

**3:45** that I should close my eyes and be cast away. Ayin. You set us in the midst of the peoples;

**3:46** all our enemies opened wide their mouth against us.

**3:47** Fear and wrath have come upon us, lifting up and crushing.

**3:48** My eye shall pour down streams of waters over the shattering of the daughter of my people.

**3:49** Pe. My eye has been swallowed up, and I shall not keep silent, because there is no respite,

**3:50** until the Lord looks down from heaven and beholds.

**3:51** My eye shall glean upon my soul above all the daughters of the city.

**3:52** Tsade. Hunting, they hunted me like a sparrow—all my enemies without cause;

**3:53** they put my life to death in a pit and laid a stone upon me;

**3:54** water overflowed upon my head; I said, "I have been cast away."

**3:55** Qoph. I called upon Your name, O Lord, out of the deepest pit;

**3:56** You heard my voice; do not hide Your ears to my supplication.

**3:57** To my help You drew near; on the day that I called upon You, You said to me, "Do not fear."

**3:58** Resh. You judged, O Lord, the causes of my soul; You redeemed my life.

**3:59** You saw, O Lord, my troubles; You judged my cause.

**3:60** You saw all their vengeance, even into all their devices against me.

**3:61** Shin. You heard their reproach, all their devices against me,

**3:62** the lips of those who rise up against me and their meditations against me all the day,

**3:63** their sitting down and their rising up. Look upon their eyes;

**3:64** You shall render to them a recompense, O Lord, according to the works of their hands.

**3:65** Tav. You shall render to them a shielding, a toil of heart.

**3:66** You shall pursue them in anger and utterly consume them from beneath heaven, O Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Greek text preserves the Hebrew acrostic letter names in Greek transcription (e.g., Aleph, Be, Zai, Labd), rendered here with their standard English spellings; it also reads "poverty" (ptocheian) and attaches "upon me" to the end of verse 1.
- **3:5** — The Septuagint reads "my head, and labored" (kephalen mou kai emochthesen), taking the Hebrew word for "gall/poison" as its homonym "head" and rendering "hardship" as a verb.
- **3:25** — The Greek text omits verses 22–24, places the Heth acrostic heading here, and concludes verse 25 with "good" (agathon), which in the Hebrew opens verse 26.
- **3:30** — The Greek text omits verse 29; in verse 28 it also reads reflexively "he has taken it upon himself" (eph' heauto).
- **3:33** — The Septuagint reads "He did not answer" (ouk apekrithe), translating the Hebrew root as "to answer" rather than its homonym "to afflict."
- **3:40** — The Septuagint uses passive verbs ("has been searched out and examined") with singular "our way," rather than the Hebrew cohortatives ("let us search and examine our ways").
- **3:45** — The Greek attaches "that I should close my eyes and be cast away" (kammysai me kai aposthenai) to verse 44 and places the Ayin acrostic heading mid-verse, differing markedly from the Hebrew ("refuse and rubbish").
- **3:63** — The Septuagint reads "Look upon their eyes" (epiblepson ep' ophthalmous auton), whereas the Hebrew has "Look! I am their taunting song."
- **3:65** — The Greek reads "a shielding, a toil of heart" (hyperaspismon, kardias mochthon), rendering the Hebrew for "obstinacy of heart" literally as a shield-covering and reading "Your curse" as "toil."
