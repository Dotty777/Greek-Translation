## Text

**4:1** Aleph. How shall gold be dimmed, and the good silver be changed? Holy stones have been poured out at the head of all the streets.

**4:2** Beth. The precious sons of Zion, who were exalted in gold—how have they been reckoned as earthen vessels, the works of a potter's hands?

**4:3** Gimel. Even dragons have bared their breasts; their whelps have nursed upon the daughters of my people to an incurable state, like a sparrow in a wilderness.

**4:4** Daleth. The tongue of the nursing infant clung to his throat in thirst; little children asked for bread, yet there is no one breaking it for them.

**4:5** He. Those who ate the provisions have vanished in the streets; those who were brought up upon scarlet have clothed themselves with dunghills.

**4:6** Vav. And the iniquity of the daughter of my people was made greater than the iniquities of Sodom, which was overthrown as in haste, and they did not labor with their hands in her.

**4:7** Zayin. Her Nazirites were purer than snow; they shone brighter than milk; they glowed more fiery than stones; their cutting was of sapphire.

**4:8** Heth. Their appearance has grown darker than soot; they were not recognized in the streets; their skin clung fast upon their bones; they were dried up; they became like wood.

**4:9** Teth. Better were the slain of the sword than the slain of famine; they departed, pierced through, from the produce of the fields.

**4:10** Yod. The hands of compassionate women boiled their own children; they became food for them in the crushing of the daughter of my people.

**4:11** Kaph. The Lord has accomplished His fury; He has poured out the fierceness of His wrath, and kindled a fire in Zion, and it devoured her foundations.

**4:12** Lamed. The kings of the earth, all who dwell in the inhabited world, did not believe that an enemy and oppressor would enter through the gates of Jerusalem.

**4:13** Mem. Because of the sins of her prophets, the injustices of her priests, who shed righteous blood in her midst.

**4:14** Nun. Her watchers staggered in the streets; they were defiled with blood; in their inability, they touched their garments.

**4:15** Samekh. Depart from the unclean; call to them, "Depart, depart, do not touch!" For they were kindled, and indeed they staggered; say among the nations, "They shall no longer sojourn."

**4:16** Ayin. The face of the Lord is their portion; He will no longer look upon them. They did not regard the face of the priests; they had no mercy on the prophets.

**4:17** Pe. While we still existed, our eyes failed, as we watched in vain for our help.

**4:18** Tsade. We watched for a nation that does not save; we hunted our little ones, so as not to walk in our squares.

**4:19** Qoph. Our time has drawn near, our days have been fulfilled, our time has arrived. Those who pursue us became swifter than the eagles of heaven; upon the mountains they flew forth; in the wilderness they lay in wait for us.

**4:20** Resh. The breath of our countenance, the anointed Lord, was captured in their corruptions—of whom we said, "In His shadow we shall live among the nations."

**4:21** Shin. Rejoice and be glad, O daughter of Idumea, who dwells upon the earth; even to you the cup of the Lord shall pass; you shall become drunk and pour yourself out.

**4:22** Tav. Your iniquity has come to an end, O daughter of Zion; He will no longer carry you away into exile. He has visited your iniquities, O daughter of Edom; He has uncovered your impieties.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — The Greek preserves the Hebrew acrostic letter headings (with distinctive Greek spellings such as Be, Labd, Tiade, Rechs, and Chsen), rendered here with standard English letter names. The Greek also reads "the good silver" (to argyrion to agathon) where the Hebrew reads fine gold.
- **4:3** — The Greek presents distinctive vocabulary and syntax here: drakontes ("dragons"), strouthion ("sparrow"), and nominative skymnoi ("their whelps") paired with accusative thygateras ("daughters of my people").
- **4:14** — The Greek reads egregoroi ("watchers") where the Hebrew has "blind," and uses the finite verb hepsanto ("they touched") after "in their inability."
- **4:18** — The Greek begins verse 18 with "We watched for a nation that does not save" (shifting the verse boundary with 4:19 relative to the Hebrew) and reads "we hunted our little ones" (mikrous) rather than "they hunted our steps."
- **4:20** — The Greek reads the nominative christos Kyrios ("the anointed Lord" or "Christ the Lord"), differing from the Hebrew genitive "the anointed of the Lord," and renders the Hebrew "pits" as diaphthorais ("corruptions"). In 4:21-22 the Greek likewise distinguishes "Idumea" (4:21) from "Edom" (4:22).
