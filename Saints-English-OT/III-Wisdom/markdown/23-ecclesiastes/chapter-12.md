## Text

**12:1** And remember Him who created you in the days of your youth, before the days of evil come, and the years arrive in which you shall say, "I have no delight in them";

**12:2** before the sun and the light, and the moon and the stars, are darkened, and the clouds return after the rain;

**12:3** in the day when the keepers of the house shall be shaken, and the men of strength shall be bent, and the grinding women cease because they have become few, and those who look through the openings shall grow dark;

**12:4** and they shall shut the doors in the marketplace at the weakness of the sound of her who grinds, and one shall rise up at the voice of the sparrow, and all the daughters of song shall be brought low,

**12:5** and they shall look toward the height, and terrors shall be in the way; and the almond tree shall blossom, and the locust shall grow fat, and the caperberry shall be scattered, because man has gone to his eternal home, and the mourners have gone about in the marketplace;

**12:6** before the cord of silver is overturned, and the golden blossom is crushed, and the pitcher is shattered at the spring, and the wheel rolls down into the cistern,

**12:7** and the dust returns to the earth as it was, and the spirit returns to God who gave it.

**12:8** "Vanity of vanities," said the Preacher, "all things are vanity."

**12:9** And moreover, because the Preacher was wise, he also taught knowledge to man, and the ear shall trace out the orderly arrangement of proverbs.

**12:10** The Preacher sought diligently to find words of delight, and a writing of uprightness, words of truth.

**12:11** The words of the wise are like ox-goads, and like nails firmly planted, those from the collections; they were given from one Shepherd.

**12:12** And beyond these, my son, beware of making many books; there is no end, and much study is a weariness of the flesh.

**12:13** Hear the end of the matter, the whole of it: Fear God and keep His commandments, for this is the whole of man.

**12:14** For God shall bring every work into judgment, along with everything overlooked, whether good or evil.

## Translator Notes

- **12:5** — The Greek reads "they shall look toward the height" (rather than fearing heights), "the locust shall grow fat," and explicitly names the "caperberry" (kapparis).
- **12:6** — Greek anthemion refers to a golden floral ornament or blossom-shaped bowl, and anatrape literally means "is overturned."
- **12:9** — The Greek reads "man" (rather than "the people") and "the ear shall trace out the orderly arrangement of proverbs," taking the Hebrew verb for pondering as the noun "ear."
- **12:14** — Greek pareoramenon means "overlooked" or "passed over," corresponding to "hidden" or "secret" in the Masoretic Hebrew text.
