## Text

**2:1** I said in my heart, "Come now, I will test you with gladness, and look upon good." And behold, this also is vanity.

**2:2** To laughter I said, "Madness!" and to gladness, "What is this that you do?"

**2:3** And I examined whether my heart would draw my flesh like wine—while my heart guided me in wisdom—and to lay hold upon gladness, until I might see what is good for the sons of men, which they should do under the sun for the number of the days of their life.

**2:4** I made my work great; I built houses for myself; I planted vineyards for myself.

**2:5** I made for myself gardens and orchards, and I planted in them every tree of fruit.

**2:6** I made for myself pools of water, to water from them a flourishing grove.

**2:7** I acquired menservants and maidservants, and servants born in my house belonged to me; indeed, a great possession of herds and flocks became mine, beyond all who were before me in Jerusalem.

**2:8** I gathered for myself both silver and gold, and the special treasures of kings and of the provinces; I procured for myself male singers and female singers, the delights of the sons of men, a male cupbearer and female cupbearers.

**2:9** And I grew great and increased beyond all who were before me in Jerusalem; moreover, my wisdom stood by me.

**2:10** And all that my eyes requested I did not withhold from them; I did not keep back my heart from any of my gladness, for my heart rejoiced in all my toil, and this became my portion from all my toil.

**2:11** Then I looked upon all my works which my hands had made, and upon the toil in which I had toiled to perform them; and behold, all things were vanity and a purpose of spirit, and there is no advantage under the sun.

**2:12** And I looked to behold wisdom and madness and folly; for who is the man who will come after counsel, in all that he has done with it?

**2:13** And I saw that wisdom has an advantage over folly, as the advantage of light over darkness.

**2:14** The wise man's eyes are in his head, while the fool walks in darkness; yet I also knew that one event will befall them all.

**2:15** And I said in my heart, "As the event of the fool, even to me also it will befall me; and why then did I become wise?" I spoke exceedingly in my heart that this also is vanity, because the fool speaks out of abundance.

**2:16** For there is no remembrance of the wise man with the fool forever, since already in the coming days all things have been forgotten; and how shall the wise man die along with the fool?

**2:17** Therefore I hated life, because the work that is done under the sun was grievous upon me; for all things are vanity and a purpose of spirit.

**2:18** And I hated all my toil at which I labor under the sun, because I leave it to the man who comes after me.

**2:19** And who knows whether he will be wise or a fool, and whether he will have dominion over all my toil in which I have toiled and acted wisely under the sun? This also is vanity.

**2:20** So I turned to cause my heart to renounce all my toil in which I had toiled under the sun.

**2:21** For there is a man whose toil is in wisdom and in knowledge and in courage, yet to a man who has not toiled in it he will give his portion. This also is vanity and a great evil.

**2:22** For it comes to pass for a man in all his toil and in the purpose of his heart with which he toils under the sun.

**2:23** For all his days are of pains, and his preoccupation is of anger; indeed, even in the night his heart does not sleep. This also is vanity.

**2:24** There is no good for a man that he should eat and drink and show his soul good in his toil; this also I saw, that it is from the hand of God.

**2:25** For who shall eat and who shall drink apart from Him?

**2:26** For to the man who is good before His face He has given wisdom and knowledge and gladness; but to the one who sins He has given preoccupation, to add and to gather, that he may give to the one who is good before the face of God. For this also is vanity and a purpose of spirit.

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — The Greek text reads "draw my flesh like wine" (hōs oinon) and "lay hold upon gladness" (ep' euphrosynē), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "with wine" and "lay hold on folly."
- **2:8** — Greek oinochoon kai oinochoas ("a male cupbearer and female cupbearers") translates an obscure Hebrew expression often rendered "concubines" or "musical instruments."
- **2:12** — The Greek reads "after counsel" (opisō tēs boulēs), interpreting the underlying consonants as Aramaic "counsel" rather than Hebrew "the king."
- **2:15** — The closing clause, "because the fool speaks out of abundance," is an explanatory addition distinctive to the Greek Septuagint.
- **2:24** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("There is nothing better for a man than that he should eat..."), the Greek states that a man possesses no such good of himself, for it comes from the hand of God.
