## Text

**10:1** Deadly flies will corrupt a preparation, the oil of sweet spice; more precious is the word of wisdom than the glory of great folly.

**10:2** The heart of a wise man is toward his right, and the heart of a fool is toward his left.

**10:3** Indeed, even when a fool walks along the way, his heart will be lacking, and all that he considers is folly.

**10:4** If the spirit of the ruler rises against you, do not forsake your place, for healing will put an end to great sins.

**10:5** There is an evil which I saw under the sun, as an unintentional error that went forth from the presence of a ruler.

**10:6** The fool was set in great heights, and the rich shall sit in a lowly place.

**10:7** I saw servants upon horses, and princes walking like servants upon the earth.

**10:8** He who digs a pit shall fall into it, and whoever tears down a hedge, a serpent shall bite him.

**10:9** He who quarries stones shall be hurt by them; he who splits logs shall be endangered by them.

**10:10** If the iron falls off, and he has troubled the face, then he shall strengthen his forces; and the advantage to the man is not wisdom.

**10:11** If a serpent bites without a whispered charm, then there is no advantage to the charmer.

**10:12** The words of a wise man's mouth are grace, but the lips of a fool shall drown him.

**10:13** The beginning of the words of his mouth is folly, and his end is wicked madness.

**10:14** And the fool multiplies words. Man does not know what has been and what shall be; what is after him, who shall declare to him?

**10:15** The toil of fools shall afflict them—one who does not know how to go to the city.

**10:16** Woe to you, O city, whose king is a youth, and whose princes eat in the morning.

**10:17** Blessed are you, O land, whose king is a son of free men, and whose princes shall eat in due season in strength, and shall not be put to shame.

**10:18** Through slothfulness the rafters shall be brought low, and through idleness of hands the house shall leak.

**10:19** For laughter they make bread, and wine and oil to make the living glad; and in submissiveness all things shall obey silver.

**10:20** Do not curse a king even in your conscience, and in the inner recesses of your bedchambers do not curse a rich man; for a bird of the sky shall carry away your voice, and that which has wings shall report your word.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The Greek text reads "more precious is the word of wisdom than the glory of great folly," diverging markedly from the Masoretic Hebrew ("a little folly outweighs wisdom and honor").
- **10:10** — The Greek diverges from the Hebrew, speaking of the iron falling off and ending literally "and the advantage to the man is not wisdom" (some Greek manuscripts read "to the valiant man is wisdom").
- **10:17** — The Greek reads "and shall not be put to shame," whereas the Masoretic Hebrew reads "and not for drunkenness."
- **10:19** — The Greek adds "and oil," and the phrase "in submissiveness... shall obey" reflects a double rendering of the Hebrew verb root that can mean either "humble" or "answer."
