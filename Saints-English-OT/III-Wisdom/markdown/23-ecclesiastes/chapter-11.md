## Text

**11:1** Send forth your bread upon the face of the water, for in a multitude of days you shall find it.

**11:2** Give a portion to seven, and indeed to eight, for you do not know what evil shall be upon the earth.

**11:3** If the clouds are filled with rain, they pour it out upon the earth; and if a tree falls in the south or in the north, in the place where the tree shall fall, there it shall be.

**11:4** He who watches the wind shall not sow, and he who looks at the clouds shall not reap.

**11:5** Just as there is no one who knows what is the way of the spirit, like the bones in the womb of her who is with child, so you shall not know the works of God, all things that He shall do.

**11:6** In the morning sow your seed, and in the evening do not let your hand cease, for you do not know which shall prosper, either this or that, or whether both alike shall be good.

**11:7** And sweet is the light, and good it is for the eyes to behold the sun.

**11:8** For even if a man lives many years, he shall rejoice in them all, and he shall remember the days of darkness, for they shall be many; all that comes is vanity.

**11:9** Rejoice, O young man, in your youth, and let your heart cheer you in the days of your youth; and walk blameless in the ways, and not in the sight of your eyes; and know that for all these things God shall bring you into judgment.

**11:10** And remove wrath from your heart, and put away evil from your flesh, for youth and folly are vanity.

## Translator Notes

- **11:5** — Greek pneuma can mean either "spirit" or "wind," and the Greek uses an impersonal construction ("there is no one who knows") rather than the second-person verb of the Hebrew.
- **11:9** — The Greek reads "walk blameless in the ways, and not in the sight of your eyes," a distinctive Septuagint reading differing from the Masoretic Hebrew ("walk in the ways of your heart and in the sight of your eyes").
- **11:10** — The Greek pairs youth with "folly" (anoia), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew has a rare word usually rendered "prime of life" or "dark hair."
