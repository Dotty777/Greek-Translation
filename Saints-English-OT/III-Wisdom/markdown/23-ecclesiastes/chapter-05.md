## Text

**5:1** Do not be hasty with your mouth, and do not let your heart be quick to utter a word before the face of God; for God is in heaven above, and you are upon the earth; therefore let your words be few.

**5:2** For a dream comes in a multitude of trial, and the voice of a fool in a multitude of words.

**5:3** When you make a vow to God, do not delay to pay it; there is no delight in fools. Therefore, pay whatever you vow.

**5:4** It is better that you should not vow than that you should vow and not pay.

**5:5** Do not allow your mouth to cause your flesh to sin, and do not say before the face of God, "It is an error," lest God become angry at your voice and destroy the works of your hands.

**5:6** For in a multitude of dreams and vanities and many words—rather, as for you, fear God.

**5:7** If you see the oppression of the poor and the seizure of judgment and righteousness in a province, do not marvel at the matter; for a high one is above a high one to keep watch, and high ones are over them.

**5:8** And the abundance of the land—over all is a king of the cultivated field.

**5:9** He who loves silver will not be satisfied with silver; and who has loved yield in their abundance? This also is vanity.

**5:10** In an abundance of goodness, those who eat it are multiplied; and what advantage is there for the one who possesses it, since it is only the beginning of seeing with his eye?

**5:11** Sweet is the sleep of the servant, whether he eats little or much; but for the one who is filled with riches, there is nothing allowing him to sleep.

**5:12** There is a sickness that I have seen under the sun: wealth kept for its owner to his own harm,

**5:13** and that wealth will perish in an evil preoccupation; and he fathers a son, yet there is nothing in his hand.

**5:14** As he came forth naked from his mother's womb, so he will return to depart just as he came, and he will take nothing from his toil that may go in his hand.

**5:15** This also is an evil sickness: for just as he arrived, so also he will depart; and the profit he has, for which he toils, is for the wind.

**5:16** Moreover, all his days are in darkness and in mourning, and in much anger and sickness and bitterness.

**5:17** Behold, I have seen a good thing that is fitting: to eat and to drink and to see goodness in all his toil with which he toils under the sun, throughout the number of the days of his life that God has given him; for this is his portion.

**5:18** Indeed, as for every man to whom God has given wealth and possessions, and granted authority over them to eat of it and to receive his portion and to rejoice in his toil—this is a gift of God.

**5:19** For he will not remember other things throughout the days of his life, because God keeps him occupied in the gladness of his heart.

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — The Greek text reads "trial" (peirasmos) rather than "business" or "preoccupation" as in the Hebrew.
- **5:5** — The Greek text reads "before the face of God" where the Hebrew text reads "before the messenger."
- **5:10** — The Greek phrase "the beginning of seeing" (arche tou horan) reflects a distinctive Septuagint rendering where the Hebrew reads "except to look upon it."
- **5:16** — The Greek text reads "in darkness and in mourning" (en penthei), whereas the Hebrew text reads "in darkness he eats."
- **5:19** — The Greek text reads "not other things" (ouk alla), where the Hebrew text reads "not much."
