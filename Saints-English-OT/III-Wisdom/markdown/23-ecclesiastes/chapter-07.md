## Text

**7:1** A good name is better than good oil, and the day of death than the day of birth.

**7:2** It is better to go to a house of mourning than to go to a house of drinking, because this is the end of every man, and the living will lay what is good to his heart.

**7:3** Anger is better than laughter, for by sadness of countenance it shall be made better.

**7:4** The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, and the heart of fools is in the house of gladness.

**7:5** It is better to hear the rebuke of a wise man than a man who hears the song of fools.

**7:6** Like the sound of thorns beneath the cauldron, so is the laughter of fools; and this also is vanity.

**7:7** For oppression drives a wise man astray and destroys the heart of his nobility.

**7:8** The end of words is better than their beginning; one who is longsuffering is better than one lofty in spirit.

**7:9** Do not be hasty in your spirit to grow angry, for anger shall rest in the bosom of fools.

**7:10** Do not say, "What has happened that the former days were better than these?" For you have not inquired concerning this in wisdom.

**7:11** Wisdom is good along with an inheritance, and an advantage to those who behold the sun;

**7:12** for in its shadow wisdom is like the shadow of silver, and the advantage of the knowledge of wisdom shall give life to the one who belongs to it.

**7:13** Consider the works of God; for who will be able to set in order him whom God has made crooked?

**7:14** In a day of goodness live in good, and consider in a day of adversity. Behold, this also God has made in harmony with that concerning speech, so that man may find nothing after him.

**7:15** I have seen all things in the days of my vanity: there is a righteous man perishing in his righteousness, and there is an ungodly man abiding in his wickedness.

**7:16** Do not become overly righteous, nor make yourself overly wise, lest you be dismayed.

**7:17** Do not act overly ungodly, and do not become hard, lest you die when it is not your time.

**7:18** It is good for you to hold fast to this, and indeed from that do not defile your hand; for to those who fear God all things shall come forth.

**7:19** Wisdom shall help the wise man more than ten rulers who are in the city;

**7:20** for there is no righteous man upon the earth who will do good and will not sin.

**7:21** Moreover, do not set your heart upon all the words that the ungodly shall speak, so that you may not hear your servant cursing you;

**7:22** for very many times he will deal wickedly with you, and on many occasions he will afflict your heart, because you yourself have likewise cursed others.

**7:23** All these things I have tested in wisdom. I said, "I shall be made wise," yet it withdrew far from me,

**7:24** farther than what was, and a deep depth—who will find it?

**7:25** I turned about, and my heart, to know and to examine and to seek wisdom and the reckoning, and to know the folly of the ungodly, and turmoil and madness;

**7:26** and I find it, and I will say, more bitter than death is the woman who is a snare, and her heart is dragnets, a bond upon her hands; he who is good before the face of God shall be delivered from her, but he who sins shall be captured in her.

**7:27** "Behold, this I have found," said the Preacher, "one to another, to find the reasoning

**7:28** which my soul has earnestly sought, yet I have not found: one man out of a thousand I have found, but a woman among all these I have not found.

**7:29** Only behold, this I have found, that God made man upright, yet they have sought out many reasonings." Who knows the wise? And who knows the interpretation of a word?

## Translator Notes

- **7:7** — The Greek reads 'the heart of his nobility' (εὐγενείας αὐτοῦ), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's 'a bribe destroys the heart.'
- **7:14** — The phrase 'concerning speech' (περὶ λαλιᾶς) is a literal Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew idiom meaning 'to the end that / so that.'
- **7:18** — The Greek reads 'do not defile your hand' (μὴ μιάνῃς), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has 'do not withdraw your hand.'
- **7:22** — The Greek reads 'he will deal wickedly with you... and afflict your heart,' reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew consonants than the Masoretic Text's 'your heart knows.'
- **7:29** — The final two questions correspond to the opening of Ecclesiastes 8:1 in the Hebrew Masoretic Text, and the Greek asks 'Who knows the wise?' rather than 'Who is like the wise?'
