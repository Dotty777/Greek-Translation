## Text

**6:1** There is an evil that I have seen under the sun, and it is great under man:

**6:2** a man to whom God gives wealth and possessions and honor, and he lacks nothing for his soul of all that he desires, yet God does not grant him power to eat of it, for a stranger eats it. This is vanity and an evil sickness.

**6:3** If a man fathers a hundred children and lives many years, however abundant the days of his years may be, yet his soul is not satisfied with goodness, and he has no burial as well, I said, "A stillborn child is better than he."

**6:4** For it came in vanity and departs in darkness, and in darkness its name will be covered;

**6:5** moreover, it has not seen the sun, nor has it known; yet there is rest for this one rather than for that one.

**6:6** Even if he lived the returns of a thousand years and saw no goodness, does he not go to one place?

**6:7** All the toil of a man is for his mouth, and yet the soul will not be filled.

**6:8** For there is an advantage to the wise man over the fool, because the poor man knows how to walk before life.

**6:9** Better is the sight of the eyes than that which wanders with the soul; this also is vanity and an inclination of spirit.

**6:10** Whatever has come into being, its name has already been called, and it is known what man is; and he will not be able to contend in judgment with the One who is stronger than he.

**6:11** For there are many words that multiply vanity.

**6:12** What advantage is there for man? For who knows what is good for man in life, throughout the number of the days of his life of vanity? And he has spent them in a shadow; for who will declare to man what will be after him under the sun?

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — The Greek text reads "under man" (hypo ton anthropon), echoing "under the sun," whereas the Hebrew reads "upon man."
- **6:6** — The Greek phrase "the returns of a thousand years" (chilion eton kathodous, also meaning "courses" or "descents") corresponds to the Hebrew "a thousand years twice."
- **6:8** — Whereas the Hebrew frames this verse as two rhetorical questions, the Greek renders it as a declarative statement and reads "before life" (tes zoes) rather than "before the living."
- **6:12** — In the Greek versification, "What advantage is there for man?" opens verse 12 (closing verse 11 in the Hebrew), and "in a shadow" (en skia) appears where the Hebrew has "like a shadow."
