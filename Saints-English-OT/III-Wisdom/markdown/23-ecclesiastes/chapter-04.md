## Text

**4:1** And I turned again and saw all the oppressions that take place under the sun; behold, the tear of those who are oppressed, and there is no one to comfort them; and from the hand of those who oppress them is power, and there is no one to comfort them.

**4:2** And I praised all the dead who have already died more than the living, as many as still live until now.

**4:3** And better than both of these is the one who has not yet come into being, who has not seen all the evil work that has been done under the sun.

**4:4** And I saw all the toil and all the prowess of the work, that it is jealousy for a man from his companion; this also is vanity and an inclination of spirit.

**4:5** The fool clasps his hands together and devours his own flesh.

**4:6** Better is a handful of rest than two handfuls of toil and inclination of spirit.

**4:7** And I turned again and saw vanity under the sun.

**4:8** There is one alone, and there is no second; indeed, he has neither son nor brother. Yet there is no end to all his toil, nor is his eye satisfied with wealth. "And for whom do I toil and deprive my soul of goodness?" This also is vanity and an evil preoccupation.

**4:9** Two are better than one, for they have a good reward in their toil.

**4:10** For if they fall, the one will raise up his partner; but woe to the one who is alone when he falls and there is no second one to raise him up.

**4:11** Moreover, if two lie down, they have warmth; but how shall one alone be warmed?

**4:12** And if one grows strong, the two will stand against him; and the threefold cord will not quickly be torn apart.

**4:13** Better is a poor and wise youth than an elderly and foolish king, who no longer knows how to take heed.

**4:14** For out of the house of prisoners he shall come forth to reign, because even in his kingdom he became poor.

**4:15** I saw all the living who walk under the sun with the second young man, who will stand in his place.

**4:16** There is no end to all the people, to all those who were before them; yet those who come last will not rejoice in him. For this also is vanity and an inclination of spirit.

**4:17** Guard your foot whenever you go to the house of God, and be near to listen; your sacrifice is better than the gift of fools, for they do not know that they do evil.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — The Greek phrase "inclination of spirit" (proairesis pneumatos, literally "choice" or "purpose of spirit") is the Septuagint's characteristic rendering of the Hebrew idiom often translated "striving after wind."
- **4:17** — In the Septuagint, this verse concludes chapter 4 (corresponding to 5:1 in the Hebrew) and reads "your sacrifice is better than the gift of fools," whereas the Hebrew compares drawing near to listen with the sacrifice offered by fools.
