## Text

**9:1** that the righteous and the wise and their works are in the hand of God; neither love nor hatred does man know; all things are before their face,

**9:2** vanity in all things. One event befalls the righteous and the ungodly, the good and the evil, the clean and the unclean, him who sacrifices and him who does not sacrifice. As is the good man, so is the sinner; as is he who swears, so is he who fears an oath.

**9:3** This is an evil in all that is done under the sun, that one event comes to all; moreover, the heart of the sons of men is filled with evil, and madness is in their heart during their life, and after them, to the dead.

**9:4** For who is he who shares with all the living? There is hope; for the living dog itself is better than the dead lion.

**9:5** For the living will know that they shall die, whereas the dead know nothing at all; neither have they any longer a reward, because their remembrance has been forgotten.

**9:6** Indeed, their love, their hatred, and their zeal have already perished, and no longer is there a portion for them forever in all that has been done under the sun.

**9:7** Come, eat your bread in gladness, and drink your wine with a good heart, for God has already been well pleased with your works.

**9:8** At every season let your garments be white, and let oil upon your head not be lacking;

**9:9** and behold life with a wife whom you have loved all the days of your life of vanity that have been given to you under the sun. All are the days of your vapor; for that is your portion in your life and in your toil at which you toil under the sun.

**9:10** Everything that your hand may find to do, do according to your strength; for there is no work, nor reasoning, nor knowledge, nor wisdom in Hades, where you are going.

**9:11** I turned again and beheld under the sun that the race does not belong to the swift, nor battle to the mighty, neither bread to the wise man, nor wealth to those of understanding, nor favor to those who know; for time and occurrence shall meet all of them.

**9:12** For indeed man does not know his season: like the fish that are taken in an evil net, and like birds that are taken in a trap, just like them the sons of men are ensnared in an evil time, when it falls upon them suddenly.

**9:13** This wisdom also I saw under the sun, and it is great to me:

**9:14** a small city and few men in it, and a great king comes against it and surrounds it and builds great siege works against it,

**9:15** and finds in it a poor wise man, and he himself delivers the city by his wisdom—yet no man remembered that poor man.

**9:16** Then I said, "Wisdom is better than strength; yet the wisdom of the poor man is despised, and his words are not heeded."

**9:17** The words of the wise are heard in quietness rather than the outcry of rulers in follies.

**9:18** Wisdom is better than instruments of war, yet one sinner shall destroy much goodness.

## Translator Notes

- **9:2** — The opening words 'vanity in all things' (ματαιότης ἐν τοῖς πᾶσιν) reflect a distinctive Septuagint reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'all things are alike to all'; the Greek also includes 'and the evil.'
- **9:9** — The Greek shifts from 'vanity' (ματαιότητος) in the first clause to 'vapor' (ἀτμοῦ) in 'All are the days of your vapor.'
- **9:10** — Greek ᾅδῃ ('Hades') is the standard Septuagint rendering of Hebrew Sheol, the realm of the dead.
- **9:17** — The Greek reads plural 'rulers in follies' (ἐξουσιαζόντων ἐν ἀφροσύναις) rather than the singular 'a ruler among fools' found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
