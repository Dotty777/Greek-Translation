## Text

**1:1** The words of the Preacher, son of David, king of Israel in Jerusalem.

**1:2** "Vanity of vanities," said the Preacher, "vanity of vanities; all things are vanity."

**1:3** What advantage has a man in all his toil in which he toils under the sun?

**1:4** A generation departs and a generation comes, and the earth stands forever.

**1:5** And the sun rises and the sun goes down, and draws toward its place.

**1:6** Rising there, it goes toward the south and circles toward the north; circling round and round, the wind goes, and upon its circuits the wind returns.

**1:7** All the streams go into the sea, and the sea will not be filled; to the place where the streams go, there they return to go again.

**1:8** All words are wearisome; a man will not be able to speak, and the eye will not be satisfied with seeing, nor will the ear be filled from hearing.

**1:9** What is that which has been? It is the very thing that will be. And what is that which has been done? It is the very thing that will be done. And there is nothing fresh under the sun.

**1:10** He who speaks and says, "Behold, this is new!"—it has already been in the ages that came before us.

**1:11** There is no remembrance of the former ones, nor indeed of the latter ones that come to be; there will be no remembrance of them among those who shall come at the last.

**1:12** I, the Preacher, became king over Israel in Jerusalem.

**1:13** And I gave my heart to search out and to examine by wisdom concerning all things that come to pass under heaven; for an evil preoccupation God has given to the sons of men, to be preoccupied with it.

**1:14** I saw all the works that have been done under the sun, and behold, all things are vanity and a purpose of spirit.

**1:15** That which is crooked will not be able to be set in order, and that which is lacking will not be able to be numbered.

**1:16** I spoke in my heart, saying, "Behold, I have grown great, and I have increased wisdom above all who were before me in Jerusalem."

**1:17** And my heart saw many things, wisdom and knowledge; and I gave my heart to know wisdom and knowledge; parables and understanding I came to know. For indeed this also is a purpose of spirit.

**1:18** For in an abundance of wisdom there is an abundance of knowledge, and he who adds knowledge will add pain.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Greek Ekklēsiastēs means "one who addresses an assembly" (rendered "the Preacher"); the Greek text also specifies "king of Israel in Jerusalem," whereas the Hebrew reads simply "king in Jerusalem."
- **1:6** — In the Greek text, "rising there, it goes toward the south and circles toward the north" refers to the sun (masculine autos) before the verse turns to the wind (pneuma).
- **1:14** — Greek proairesis pneumatos ("purpose of spirit" or "choosing of spirit") renders the Hebrew phrase often translated "grasping for the wind." Here and elsewhere (e.g., 2:17; 3:10, 17), Greek syn mechanically reflects the Hebrew direct-object marker and is left untranslated.
- **1:17** — The Greek text reads "parables and understanding" (parabolas kai epistēmēn) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "madness and folly."
- **1:18** — The Greek text reads "abundance of knowledge" (plēthos gnōseōs) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "much grief" or "vexation."
