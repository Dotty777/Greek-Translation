## Text

**19:1** But upon the ungodly merciless wrath came even to the end; for He knew beforehand even what they were yet to do,

**19:2** that after they themselves had consented for them to depart and had sent them away in haste, they would change their minds and pursue them.

**19:3** For while they still had their mourning in hand and were lamenting at the graves of the dead, they took up another foolish purpose, and those whom they had driven out with pleas, these they pursued as fugitives.

**19:4** For a deserved necessity was drawing them to this end, and cast upon them forgetfulness of the things that had befallen them, so that they might fill up the punishment that was still lacking to their torments,

**19:5** and that Your people might experience a wondrous journey, while those others might find a strange death.

**19:6** For the whole creation in its own kind was fashioned anew from the beginning, serving its own commands, so that Your children might be kept unharmed.

**19:7** The cloud overshadowing the camp, and the emergence of dry land out of water that stood before, were beheld—an unhindered way out of the Red Sea, and a grass-bearing plain out of a violent surge;

**19:8** through which they passed as a whole nation, sheltered by Your hand, beholding marvelous wonders.

**19:9** For like horses they ranged at pasture, and like lambs they leaped about, praising You, O Lord, who delivered them;

**19:10** for they still remembered the events of their sojourn, how instead of producing animals the earth brought forth gnats, and instead of aquatic creatures the river poured forth a multitude of frogs.

**19:11** And afterward they saw also a new generation of birds, when, led on by desire, they asked for delicacies;

**19:12** for to comfort them, quail came up for them from the sea,

**19:13** and punishments came upon the sinners, not without earlier tokens by the force of thunderbolts; for justly they suffered for their own wicked deeds, because they practiced a more grievous hatred of strangers.

**19:14** For the others did not receive unknown visitors when they arrived, whereas these enslaved guests who were their benefactors.

**19:15** And not only so, but surely there shall be some visitation for them, since they received foreigners with hostility;

**19:16** whereas these, after welcoming with festivities those who had already shared in the same rights, afflicted them with grievous toils.

**19:17** They also were struck with blindness, just as those were at the doors of the righteous man, when, enveloped in fathomless darkness, each one sought the passage through his own doors.

**19:18** For the elements changed harmony among themselves, just as on a harp the notes alter the name of the rhythm while always abiding in sound—which may be accurately inferred from beholding the things that came to pass.

**19:19** For land creatures were changed into creatures of the water, and swimming creatures crossed over upon the land;

**19:20** fire prevailed in water beyond its own power, and water forgot its quenching power;

**19:21** flames, on the contrary, did not consume the flesh of perishable living creatures walking among them, nor the melting, easily dissolved, ice-like kind of immortal food.

**19:22** For in all things, O Lord, You magnified Your people and glorified them, and did not neglect them, standing by them in every time and place.

## Translator Notes

- **19:5** — Replaces literal Greek-to-English bare demonstrative 'those' with idiomatic 'those others' to preserve the μὲν...δέ / ἐκεῖνοι contrast cleanly in modern NKJV English.
- **19:6** — The Greek text reads "serving its own commands" (the ordinances appointed to creation), whereas some witnesses and editions read "Your commands."
- **19:15** — The sense of "visitation" (Greek episkope) here is debated: it may mean either that Sodom will face some divine judgment for its hostility or that Sodom will receive some measure of consideration compared with Egypt's greater treachery in verse 16.
- **19:18** — Literally "alter the name of the rhythm while always abiding in sound," a musical analogy describing how the physical elements keep their underlying substance while being transposed to produce new miraculous effects.
- **19:21** — The Greek text reads the double adjective "melting, easily dissolved" (tekton eutekton) governed by "did not consume"; some witnesses read the verb etekon ("nor did they melt") in place of tekton.
