## Text

**12:1** For Your incorruptible Spirit is in all things.

**12:2** Therefore You reprove little by little those who fall away, and You admonish them by reminding them of the things in which they sin, that being freed from wickedness they may believe in You, O Lord.

**12:3** For indeed, the ancient inhabitants of Your holy land,

**12:4** whom You hated for practicing most hateful works of sorceries and unholy rites,

**12:5** both merciless murderers of children, and a feast of devourers of human inward parts, flesh, and blood, initiates from the midst of a mystic revel,

**12:6** and parents who were murderers of helpless souls—these You willed to destroy by the hands of our fathers,

**12:7** that the land most precious of all in Your sight might receive a worthy colony of the children of God.

**12:8** Yet even these You spared as men, and You sent wasps as forerunners of Your host, that they might destroy them little by little.

**12:9** Not that You were unable in battle array to deliver the ungodly into the hands of the righteous, or to wipe them out at once by fierce wild beasts or by a stern word;

**12:10** but judging them little by little, You gave place for repentance, not being unaware that their origin was evil and their wickedness inborn, and that their reasoning would never change forever,

**12:11** for they were an accursed seed from the beginning. Neither did You grant impunity for the things in which they sinned out of fear of anyone.

**12:12** For who shall say, "What have You done?" Or who shall withstand Your judgment? And who shall accuse You concerning the nations that perished, which You made? Or who shall come to confront You as an avenger on behalf of unrighteous men?

**12:13** For neither is there any god besides You, whose care is for all things, that You should show that You did not judge unrighteously;

**12:14** nor shall king or tyrant be able to look You in the face concerning those whom You have punished.

**12:15** Being righteous, You govern all things righteously, deeming it alien to Your power to condemn the one who does not deserve to be punished.

**12:16** For Your strength is the beginning of righteousness, and Your sovereignty over all causes You to spare all.

**12:17** For You show Your strength when Your perfection of power is doubted, and among those who know it You rebuke insolence.

**12:18** Yet You, being Sovereign over strength, judge with gentleness, and with great forbearance You govern us; for power is present with You whenever You will.

**12:19** And by such works You have taught Your people that the righteous man must be a lover of mankind; and You have filled Your sons with good hope, because You grant repentance for sins.

**12:20** For if You punished the enemies of Your children, even those deserving of death, with such great care and entreaty, granting times and place by which they might be delivered from wickedness,

**12:21** with how great carefulness have You judged Your sons, to whose fathers You gave oaths and covenants of good promises?

**12:22** Therefore, while chastening us, You scourge our enemies ten thousandfold, that when we judge we may be mindful of Your goodness, and when we are judged we may look for mercy.

**12:23** Consequently, You also tormented through their own abominations the unrighteous who lived in folly of life.

**12:24** For they went astray far beyond the paths of error, supposing to be gods those creatures that even among animals are despised by their enemies, deceived like senseless infants.

**12:25** Because of this, as to unreasoning children, You sent judgment as a mockery.

**12:26** But those who were not admonished by mocking rebukes shall experience a judgment worthy of God.

**12:27** For in the things through which they suffered and were indignant—being punished by these very creatures whom they supposed to be gods—they saw and recognized as the true God Him whom they had long refused to know; therefore the utmost end of condemnation also came upon them.

## Translator Notes

- **12:5** — The Greek syntax is unusually compressed; "a feast of devourers of human inward parts, flesh, and blood" reflects the rare compound splanchnophagon ("entrail-devourers") with thoinan ("feast"), describing cannibalistic mystery rites (mystas thiasou).
- **12:20** — Translates the Greek text deeseos ("entreaty"), attested in major manuscripts; some modern critical editions emend the word to dieseos ("indulgence" or "remission").
- **12:24** — The Greek phrase ta kai en zoois ton echthron atima is grammatically ambiguous and may also be understood as "the most despised even among hostile animals."
