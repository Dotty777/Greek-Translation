## Text

**4:1** Better is childlessness with virtue, for immortality is in the remembrance of it, because it is acknowledged both before God and before men.

**4:2** When it is present, men imitate it, and when it has departed, they long for it; and throughout eternity it marches in triumph wearing a crown, having won the contest for undefiled prizes.

**4:3** But the prolific multitude of the ungodly will be of no profit, and from illegitimate shoots it will not strike a deep root, nor establish a secure foundation.

**4:4** For even if they flourish in branches for a season, standing unsteadily, they will be shaken by the wind, and by the violence of winds they will be uprooted.

**4:5** Immature twigs will be snapped off, and their fruit will be useless, unripe for eating and fit for nothing.

**4:6** For children born of unlawful unions are witnesses of wickedness against their parents at their examination.

**4:7** But the righteous man, though he die before his time, shall be at rest.

**4:8** For honorable old age is not one of long duration, nor is it measured by the number of years.

**4:9** Rather, understanding is gray hair for men, and an unblemished life is ripe old age.

**4:10** Having become well-pleasing to God, he was loved, and while living among sinners, he was taken away.

**4:11** He was caught away, lest wickedness alter his understanding, or deceit beguile his soul.

**4:12** For the bewitchment of baseness obscures what is good, and the roving of desire undermines an innocent mind.

**4:13** Having been perfected in a short time, he fulfilled long years.

**4:14** For his soul was pleasing to the Lord; therefore it hastened out of the midst of wickedness.

**4:15** Yet the peoples saw and did not understand, nor did they lay such a thing to heart: that grace and mercy are with His chosen ones, and watchful care is with His holy ones.

**4:16** But the righteous man who has died will condemn the ungodly who are still living, and youth quickly perfected will condemn the long old age of the unrighteous.

**4:17** For they will see the end of the wise man, and will not understand what He purposed concerning him, or for what end the Lord kept him safe.

**4:19** They will see and despise him, but the Lord will laugh them to scorn; and after this they shall become a dishonored corpse and an object of reproach among the dead forever. For He will dash them headlong, speechless, and shake them from their foundations; to the uttermost they shall be laid waste, and they shall be in anguish, and their remembrance shall perish.

**4:20** They will come in dread at the reckoning of their sins, and their lawless deeds will convict them to their face.

## Translator Notes

- **4:6** — Greek literally reads "children born of unlawful sleeps," an idiom for illicit sexual unions.
- **4:10** — An unnamed allusion to Enoch, who was well-pleasing to God and was taken away by Him (Genesis 5:24 LXX).
- **4:14** — The verb may be taken intransitively ("it/he hastened out," with his soul or the righteous man as subject) or transitively ("He hurried him out," with the Lord as subject).
- **4:19** — In this critical text, traditional verse 4:18 is combined into 4:19, so 4:18 does not appear as a separate verse.
