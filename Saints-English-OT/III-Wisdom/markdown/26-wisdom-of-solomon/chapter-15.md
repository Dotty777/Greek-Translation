## Text

**15:1** But You, our God, are gracious and true, longsuffering, and governing all things in mercy.

**15:2** For even if we sin, we are Yours, knowing Your might; we will not sin, knowing that we are counted as Yours.

**15:3** For to know You is complete righteousness, and to know Your might is the root of immortality.

**15:4** For neither has the mischievous invention of men led us astray, nor the fruitless labor of painters, a figure stained with varied colors,

**15:5** the sight of which brings reproach upon fools, as one longs for the breathless form of a dead image.

**15:6** Lovers of evil things and worthy of such hopes are both those who make them, and those who long for them, and those who worship them.

**15:7** For a potter also, laboriously pressing soft earth, molds each vessel for our service; yet out of the same clay he fashions both the vessels that serve clean uses and those for the contrary, all alike; but what the use of each of either sort shall be, the worker in clay is the judge.

**15:8** And with misspent toil he molds a vain god out of the same clay—he who a little while before was made from earth, and after a little while returns to that from which he was taken, when the debt of his soul is required of him.

**15:9** Yet his concern is not that he is about to fail, nor that he has a fleeting life, but he competes with goldsmiths and silversmiths, and imitates workers in bronze, and counts it a glory that he molds counterfeits.

**15:10** His heart is ashes, and his hope is of less worth than earth, and his life is of less honor than clay,

**15:11** because he did not know Him who formed him, and who inspired into him an active soul and breathed into him a life-giving spirit;

**15:12** Instead, he reckoned our life to be a sport and our existence a festival for gain; for, he says, one must make profit from every source, even from evil.

**15:13** For this man above all others knows that he sins, fashioning out of earthy matter fragile vessels and graven images.

**15:14** Yet most foolish of all, and more wretched than the soul of an infant, are all the enemies of Your people who oppressed them.

**15:15** For they also reckoned all the idols of the nations to be gods, which have neither the use of eyes to see, nor nostrils to draw in air, nor ears to hear, nor fingers on their hands to feel, and their feet are helpless for walking.

**15:16** For a man made them, and one whose spirit is borrowed molded them; for no man is able to mold a god like himself.

**15:17** Being mortal, he makes a dead thing with lawless hands; for he is better than the objects of his worship, since he indeed lived, whereas they never did.

**15:18** Moreover, they worship even the most hateful beasts; for when compared in senselessness, they are worse than the others.

**15:19** Nor are they even so fair in their appearance among living creatures as to be desired, but they have escaped both the praise of God and His blessing.

## Translator Notes

- **15:5** — The Greek text reads oneidos ("reproach"), followed here; some commentators conjecture orexin ("desire" or "lust") to match "longs for" in the second clause.
- **15:7** — The image of the potter shaping vessels for clean and contrary uses from the same clay echoes Jeremiah 18 and anticipates Paul's illustration in Romans 9:21.
- **15:14** — The critique turns here to the Egyptians, the oppressors of Israel, whose animal worship is condemned in verses 18-19.
- **15:19** — This contrasts the despised creatures worshiped in Egypt with Genesis 1:21-25, where God declared the animals He created good and blessed them.
