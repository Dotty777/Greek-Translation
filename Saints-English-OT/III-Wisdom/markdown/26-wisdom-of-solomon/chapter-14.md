## Text

**14:1** Again, one preparing for a voyage and about to pass through fierce waves calls upon a piece of wood more fragile than the vessel that bears him.

**14:2** For a desire for gain devised that vessel, and wisdom, the master artisan, built it;

**14:3** yet Your providence, O Father, steers it, for You have given a way even in the sea and a safe path among the waves,

**14:4** showing that You are able to save from all things, so that even without skill one may embark.

**14:5** Yet You desire that the works of Your wisdom should not be idle; therefore men entrust their lives even to the slightest piece of wood, and passing through the surging waves on a raft, they are brought safely through.

**14:6** For in the beginning also, when the proud giants were perishing, the hope of the world took refuge upon a raft, and being steered by Your hand, left to the age a seed of a new generation.

**14:7** For blessed is the wood through which righteousness comes;

**14:8** but what is made by hands is accursed, both it and the one who made it—because he indeed fashioned it, while the perishable thing was named a god.

**14:9** For both the ungodly man and his ungodliness are equally hateful to God;

**14:10** for indeed the thing made shall be punished together with the one who made it.

**14:11** Therefore upon the idols of the nations also there shall be a visitation, because within the creation of God they have become an abomination, and stumbling blocks to the souls of men, and a snare to the feet of the foolish.

**14:12** For the devising of idols was the beginning of fornication, and the inventions of them were the corruption of life.

**14:13** For neither did they exist from the beginning, nor shall they remain forever;

**14:14** for by the vainglory of men they entered into the world, and therefore a swift end has been devised for them.

**14:15** For a father, worn down by untimely grief, having made an image of the child swiftly taken away, now honored as a god one who was then a dead mortal, and handed down to those under his authority mysteries and rites;

**14:16** then in the course of time the ungodly custom grew strong and was kept as a law,

**14:17** and by the commands of tyrants graven images were worshiped. When men could not honor them face to face because they dwelt far away, they pictured their distant likeness and made a visible image of the king whom they honored, that by their zeal they might flatter the absent one as though he were present.

**14:18** Moreover, the ambition of the craftsman spurred on even those who did not know him to greater intensity of worship.

**14:19** For he, wishing perhaps to please the ruler, forced the likeness by his skill into greater beauty;

**14:20** and the multitude, drawn by the grace of the workmanship, now reckoned as an object of worship the man who a short time before had been honored.

**14:21** And this became a hidden snare for human life, because men, enslaved either to calamity or to tyranny, bestowed upon stones and wood the incommunicable Name.

**14:22** Then it was not enough that they erred concerning the knowledge of God, but living also in a great war of ignorance, they call such great evils peace.

**14:23** For while celebrating either child-murdering rites, or secret mysteries, or frenzied revelries of strange ordinances,

**14:24** they no longer keep either lives or marriages pure, but one man either lies in ambush and slays another, or grieves him by adultery.

**14:25** And all is mingled together in confusion—blood and murder, theft and deceit, corruption, faithlessness, tumult, perjury,

**14:26** troubling of the good, forgetfulness of kindness, defilement of souls, confusion of nature, disorder in marriages, adultery, and licentiousness.

**14:27** For the worship of nameless idols is the beginning, cause, and end of every evil;

**14:28** for either they are mad in their reveling, or they prophesy falsehoods, or they live unrighteously, or they swiftly swear falsely;

**14:29** for trusting in lifeless idols, they do not expect to suffer harm when they have sworn falsely.

**14:30** Yet just retribution shall overtake them on both counts, because they thought falsely concerning God in giving heed to idols, and they swore unrighteously in deceit, despising holiness.

**14:31** For it is not the power of the things sworn by, but the justice upon sinners that always pursues the transgression of the unrighteous.

## Translator Notes

- **14:6** — An allusion to Noah's ark as a raft preserving humanity during the Flood, when the giants (Genesis 6:4 LXX) perished.
- **14:21** — The "incommunicable Name" (Greek to akoinoneton onoma) refers to the unique divine Name that belongs to God alone and cannot be shared with any creature.
- **14:26** — "Confusion of nature" (Greek geneseos enallage) literally means an interchange of kind or origin, variously understood as unnatural sexual practices or the confounding of human lineage.
- **14:27** — "Nameless idols" (Greek anonymon eidolon) denotes idols unworthy to be named, contrasting with the incommunicable Name of God in verse 21.
