## Text

**7:1** I myself also am a mortal man, equal to all others, and a descendant of the first-formed one born of the earth;

**7:2** and in my mother's womb I was sculpted into flesh in the space of ten months, compacted in blood from the seed of a man and the pleasure that joined with sleep.

**7:3** And when I was born, I also drew in the common air and fell upon the earth of like nature, uttering my first cry in weeping, just as all others do;

**7:4** I was nurtured in swaddling cloths and with cares.

**7:5** For no king had any other beginning of birth;

**7:6** but there is one entrance into life for all, and a like departure.

**7:7** Therefore I prayed, and understanding was given to me; I called out, and a spirit of wisdom came to me.

**7:8** I preferred her above scepters and thrones, and I counted riches as nothing in comparison with her.

**7:9** Neither did I liken any priceless stone to her, for all gold in her sight is a little sand, and silver shall be counted as clay before her.

**7:10** I loved her above health and beauty, and I chose to have her instead of light, because the radiance from her never sleeps.

**7:11** Yet all good things came to me together with her, and countless wealth was in her hands;

**7:12** and I rejoiced in them all, because wisdom leads them, though I did not know that she was the source of them.

**7:13** Without guile I learned, and ungrudgingly I share; I do not conceal her riches.

**7:14** For she is an unfailing treasure to men, and those who make use of it gain friendship with God, being commended by the gifts that come from instruction.

**7:15** May God grant to me to speak with discernment and to conceive thoughts worthy of the things that have been given, for He Himself is both the guide of wisdom and the corrector of the wise.

**7:16** For in His hand are both we and our words, along with all understanding and skill in workmanship.

**7:17** For He Himself gave me unerring knowledge of the things that exist: to know the structure of the world and the working of the elements,

**7:18** the beginning and end and middle of times, the alternations of the solstices and the changes of the seasons,

**7:19** the cycles of years and the positions of the stars,

**7:20** the natures of living creatures and the tempers of wild beasts, the violence of winds and the reasonings of men, the varieties of plants and the powers of roots.

**7:21** All things, both hidden and manifest, I learned,

**7:22** for wisdom, the artisan of all things, taught me. For in her is a spirit that is intelligent, holy, unique, manifold, subtle, nimble, lucid, undefiled, distinct, inviolable, loving the good, keen, unhindered, beneficent,

**7:23** loving mankind, steadfast, sure, free from care, all-powerful, overseeing all, and penetrating through all spirits that are intelligent, pure, and most subtle.

**7:24** For wisdom is more mobile than any motion, and she pervades and penetrates through all things by reason of her purity.

**7:25** For she is a breath of the power of God, and a pure outflow of the glory of the Almighty; therefore nothing defiled steals into her.

**7:26** For she is a radiance of eternal light, a spotless mirror of the working of God, and an image of His goodness.

**7:27** And though she is one, she can do all things; and abiding in herself, she renews all things, and in every generation passing into holy souls, she makes them friends of God and prophets.

**7:28** For God loves no one except him who dwells with wisdom.

**7:29** For she is fairer than the sun, and above every constellation of the stars. Compared with light, she is found superior;

**7:30** for night succeeds that light, but wickedness does not prevail against wisdom.

## Translator Notes

- **7:2** — Ancient Mediterranean reckoning commonly counted human gestation as ten lunar months.
- **7:3** — The Greek adjective homoiopathes ("of like nature" or "sharing the same lot") suggests either the earth's shared condition with mortals or its impartial reception of all alike.
- **7:12** — The Greek noun genesis means "origin" or "source," later rendered "mother" in the Latin Vulgate.
- **7:20** — The Greek pneumaton may mean either "winds" (fitting the natural-science catalog) or "spirits" (paired with human reasonings).
