## Text

**18:1** Yet for Your holy ones there was a very great light. Hearing their voice, yet not seeing their form, they counted them blessed because they also had suffered;

**18:2** and because they did them no harm, though they had been wronged before, they gave thanks, and asked pardon for having been at variance with them.

**18:3** In return for these things, You provided a blazing pillar of fire, a guide for an unknown journey, and a harmless sun for their honorable sojourn.

**18:4** For they indeed were worthy to be deprived of light and imprisoned in darkness, who had kept Your sons shut up in confinement, through whom the imperishable light of the law was to be given to the world.

**18:5** When they had plotted to slay the infants of the holy ones, and a single child was cast out and saved, for their reproof You took away the multitude of their own children, and destroyed them all together in violent water.

**18:6** That night was made known beforehand to our fathers, so that, knowing with certainty the oaths in which they had trusted, they might be of good cheer.

**18:7** The salvation of the righteous and the destruction of their enemies were awaited by Your people.

**18:8** For just as You punished the adversaries, by that very means You called us to Yourself and glorified us.

**18:9** For in secret the holy children of good men were offering sacrifice, and with one accord they ordained the divine law, that the saints would share alike in the same blessings and the same perils, while the fathers were already singing beforehand the hymns of praise.

**18:10** Yet the discordant cry of their enemies echoed back, and a pitiful wail was borne abroad for the children being lamented.

**18:11** With a like penalty the servant was punished together with his master, and the commoner suffered the same things as the king;

**18:12** and all together, under one name of death, had dead without number; for the living were not even sufficient to bury them, since in a single moment their noblest offspring was destroyed.

**18:13** For though they disbelieved all things because of their sorceries, at the destruction of the firstborn they confessed that this people was God's son.

**18:14** For while peaceful silence encompassed all things, and night in its own swift course was midway through all things,

**18:15** Your all-powerful word leaped from the heavens, out of the royal throne, a stern warrior into the midst of the doomed land, bearing as a sharp sword Your unfeigned command,

**18:16** and standing, filled all things with death; it touched heaven, yet stood upon the earth.

**18:17** Then at once visions of dreams dreadfully troubled them, and unexpected terrors came upon them,

**18:18** and one here and another there, cast down half-dead, declared the reason for which he was dying;

**18:19** for the dreams that troubled them had forewarned them of this, lest they perish without knowing why they were suffering so grievously.

**18:20** Yet the experience of death touched the righteous also, and a plague came upon the multitude in the wilderness; but the wrath did not abide for long.

**18:21** For a blameless man made haste to stand in their defense, bringing the weapon of his own ministry—prayer and the atonement of incense; he withstood the fury and put an end to the calamity, showing that he is Your servant.

**18:22** He overcame the turmoil not by strength of body, nor by force of arms, but by a word he subdued the one who was punishing, calling to remembrance the oaths and covenants of the fathers.

**18:23** For when the dead had already fallen in heaps one upon another, he stood between and beat back the wrath, and cut off its way to the living.

**18:24** For upon his full-length robe was the whole world, and the glories of the fathers were upon the four rows of carved stones, and Your majesty was upon the diadem of his head.

**18:25** To these the destroyer yielded, and these they feared; for the mere trial of the wrath was sufficient.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — The Greek text lacks the negative particle "not" (reading literally "because they also had suffered"), referring to Israel's earlier affliction; some later witnesses and editors insert "not" to read "because they had not suffered as well."
- **18:14** — The Greek text repeats "all things" at the end of the clause ("was midway through all things"), whereas some manuscripts omit the second occurrence.
- **18:22** — The Greek text reads "the turmoil" (Greek ochlon, which can also mean "the multitude"); many commentators emend the word to cholon ("the anger" or "wrath").
- **18:25** — The Greek shifts from the singular "the destroyer" to the plural verb "they feared," apparently viewing the instruments of destruction collectively; some manuscripts smooth this to the singular "he feared."
