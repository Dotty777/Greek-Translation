## Text

**11:1** She prospered their works by the hand of a holy prophet.

**11:2** They journeyed through an uninhabited wilderness, and pitched tents in untrodden places;

**11:3** they withstood foes, and repelled enemies.

**11:4** They thirsted and called upon You, and water was given to them from flinty rock, and a remedy for thirst from hard stone.

**11:5** For through the very things by which their enemies were punished, through these they themselves were benefited in their need.

**11:6** Whereas instead of the spring of an ever-flowing river, those were troubled with foul blood

**11:7** in rebuke of the infant-slaying decree, to these You gave abundant water beyond expectation,

**11:8** showing through the thirst of that time how You punished their adversaries.

**11:9** For when they were tested, though chastened in mercy, they came to know how the ungodly were tormented when judged in wrath;

**11:10** for these You tested as a father who admonishes, but those You scrutinized as a stern king who condemns.

**11:11** And whether absent or present, they were alike afflicted;

**11:12** for a twofold grief seized them, and a groaning over memories of things past.

**11:13** For when they heard that through their own punishments the others were being benefited, they perceived the Lord.

**11:14** For him who long ago was cast out in exposure they rejected with mockery; at the end of the events they marveled at him, having thirsted in a manner unlike the righteous.

**11:15** In return for the foolish reasonings of their unrighteousness, by which they were led astray to worship irrational reptiles and worthless vermin, You sent upon them a multitude of irrational creatures for vengeance,

**11:16** that they might know that through the very things by which one sins, through these he is punished.

**11:17** For Your all-powerful hand, which created the world out of formless matter, did not lack the means to send upon them a multitude of bears or fierce lions,

**11:18** or newly created unknown beasts full of fury, either breathing forth a fiery breath, or scattering roaring blasts of smoke, or flashing dreadful sparks from their eyes,

**11:19** whose harm could not only utterly crush them, but whose very sight could terrify and destroy them.

**11:20** Yet even apart from these, they could have fallen by a single breath, pursued by justice and scattered by the breath of Your power; but You have ordered all things by measure and number and weight.

**11:21** For great strength is always present with You, and who shall withstand the might of Your arm?

**11:22** For the whole world before You is like a slight tip of the scales, and like a drop of morning dew descending upon the earth.

**11:23** Yet You have mercy on all, because You can do all things, and You overlook the sins of men for the sake of repentance.

**11:24** For You love all things that exist, and You abhor nothing that You have made; for if You had hated anything, You would not have formed it.

**11:25** And how would anything have endured if You had not willed it, or how would what was not called by You have been preserved?

**11:26** Yet You spare all things, because they are Yours, O Sovereign Lord, lover of souls.

## Translator Notes

- **11:6** — The Greek text has the masculine plural participle tarachthentes ('troubled'), an abrupt construction referring to the Egyptians in contrast with Israel in verse 7; some manuscripts read the genitive singular tarachthentos, modifying 'river.'
- **11:14** — Alludes to Moses, who was exposed in a basket on the Nile (Exodus 2:3) and later mocked before Pharaoh, yet finally held in awe by the Egyptians.
- **11:17** — The phrase 'out of formless matter' (ex amorphou hyles) draws on Hellenistic philosophical terminology echoing the unformed earth of Genesis 1:2 LXX.
- **11:26** — Greek philopsyche means 'lover of souls' or 'lover of life,' a rare epithet applied here to God as the compassionate preserver of all living beings.
