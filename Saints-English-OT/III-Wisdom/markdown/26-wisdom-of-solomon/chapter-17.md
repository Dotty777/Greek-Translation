## Text

**17:1** For great are Your judgments and hard to describe; therefore uninstructed souls went astray.

**17:2** For when lawless men supposed that they oppressed a holy nation, they themselves—prisoners of darkness and captives fettered by a long night, shut in beneath their roofs—lay exiled from eternal providence.

**17:3** For thinking that they were hidden in their secret sins, they were scattered beneath a dark veil of forgetfulness, terribly dismayed and deeply troubled by apparitions.

**17:4** For not even the inner chamber that held them kept them free from fear; terrifying sounds resounded around them, and gloomy specters with unsmiling faces appeared.

**17:5** No force of fire had power to give light, nor did the brilliant flames of the stars endure to illuminate that dismal night.

**17:6** Only a self-kindled blaze full of terror shone through to them, and terrified by that unseen apparition, they deemed the things they saw to be even worse.

**17:7** The illusions of magic art lay defeated, and their boast of wisdom received a humiliating rebuke;

**17:8** for those who promised to drive away terrors and disorders from a sick soul were themselves sick with a laughable dread.

**17:9** For even if nothing dreadful frightened them, yet startled by the passing of noxious creatures and the hissing of reptiles, they perished in trembling, refusing even to look upon the air, which cannot on any side be escaped.

**17:10** For wickedness, being condemned, bears witness by its own nature that it is cowardly, and pressed hard by conscience it always imagines the worst.

**17:11** For fear is nothing other than the surrender of the aids that come from reason;

**17:12** and the weaker the expectation from within, the heavier it counts ignorance of the cause that brings the torment.

**17:13** But throughout that truly powerless night, which had come upon them from the recesses of powerless Hades, sleeping the same sleep,

**17:14** at one moment they were driven by monstrous apparitions, and at another they were paralyzed by the surrender of their soul; for a sudden and unexpected terror came upon them.

**17:15** Thus, whoever fell down there was kept under guard, shut up in that prison without iron bars;

**17:16** for whether a man was a farmer, or a shepherd, or a laborer toiling in the wilderness, he was overtaken and endured that inescapable necessity.

**17:17** For with one chain of darkness they were all bound. Whether it was a whistling wind, or the melodious sound of birds among spreading branches, or the rhythm of water rushing with force, or the harsh crash of tumbling rocks,

**17:18** or the unseen running of bounding animals, or the voice of the fiercest roaring beasts, or an echo rebounding from the hollows of the mountains—these terrified and paralyzed them.

**17:19** For the whole world was illuminated with brilliant light and continued in unhindered labors;

**17:20** over them alone was spread a heavy night, an image of the darkness that was about to receive them; yet to themselves they were heavier than the darkness.

## Translator Notes

- **17:6** — The Greek phrase "that unseen apparition" (or "that sight no longer seen") conveys that in their terror over what they could not clearly discern, the Egyptians imagined the things they did see to be even worse.
- **17:10** — The critical Greek text reads idios ... martyrei ("bears witness by its own nature"), whereas some manuscripts read idio ... martyri ("condemned by its own witness"). The verb proseliphen ("imagines") literally means "has always taken on" or "added" grievous things.
- **17:12** — The compressed Greek clause may be construed either with "of the cause" as an objective genitive ("counts ignorance of the cause that brings the torment heavier") or as a genitive of comparison ("counts ignorance worse than the cause that brings the torment").
