## Text

**8:1** She stretches mightily from one end to the other, and she governs all things graciously.

**8:2** Her I loved and sought out from my youth, and I sought to take her as a bride for myself, and I became a lover of her beauty.

**8:3** She glorifies her noble birth by living in fellowship with God, and the Master of all has loved her;

**8:4** for she is an initiate into the knowledge of God, and a chooser of His works.

**8:5** And if wealth is a desirable possession in life, what is richer than wisdom, who works all things?

**8:6** And if understanding works, who more than she is the artisan of the things that exist?

**8:7** And if anyone loves righteousness, her labors are virtues; for she teaches self-control and prudence, righteousness and courage, and nothing in life is more profitable for men than these.

**8:8** And if anyone also longs for wide experience, she knows the things of old and discerns the things to come; she understands the subtleties of speeches and the solutions of riddles; she foreknows signs and wonders, and the outcomes of seasons and times.

**8:9** Therefore I resolved to take her to dwell with me, knowing that she would be a counselor of good things to me, and a comfort in cares and sorrow.

**8:10** Because of her I shall have glory among the multitudes, and honor in the presence of elders, though I am young;

**8:11** I shall be found keen in judgment, and in the sight of mighty men I shall be admired.

**8:12** When I am silent, they will wait for me; and when I speak, they will give heed; and when I speak at greater length, they will lay their hand upon their mouth.

**8:13** Because of her I shall have immortality, and I shall leave an everlasting remembrance to those who come after me.

**8:14** I shall govern peoples, and nations shall be subjected to me;

**8:15** dread tyrants will fear me when they hear of me; among the multitude I shall appear good, and in war courageous.

**8:16** When I enter my house, I shall find rest with her; for companionship with her has no bitterness, nor does living with her bring grief, but gladness and joy.

**8:17** When I had pondered these things within myself, and considered in my heart that in kinship with wisdom there is immortality,

**8:18** and in her friendship there is noble delight, and in the labors of her hands unfailing wealth, and in the discipline of her companionship understanding, and renown in the fellowship of her words, I went about seeking how I might take her to myself.

**8:19** Now I was a gifted child, and I received a good soul;

**8:20** or rather, being good, I entered an undefiled body.

**8:21** Yet perceiving that I would not otherwise possess her unless God gave her—and this too was a mark of understanding, to know whose gift she is—I appealed to the Lord and pleaded with Him, and I said with all my heart:

## Translator Notes

- **8:7** — These four virtues—self-control, prudence, righteousness, and courage—represent the classical Hellenistic canon of the four cardinal virtues.
- **8:19** — Verses 19–20 use Hellenistic-Jewish philosophical phrasing regarding the soul's goodness in relation to the body, while affirming in verse 20 that the body itself is undefiled.
- **8:21** — Greek enkrates here denotes "having possession of" wisdom, though in other contexts it can mean "self-controlled."
