## Text

**19:3** The foolishness of a man ruins his ways, yet he blames God in his heart.

**19:4** Wealth adds many friends, but the poor man is forsaken even by the friend he has.

**19:5** A false witness shall not go unpunished, and he who brings accusations unjustly shall not escape.

**19:6** Many court the favor of kings, but every wicked one becomes a reproach to a man.

**19:7** Everyone who hates a poor brother shall also be far from friendship; good insight shall draw near to those who know it, and a prudent man shall find it. He who does much evil brings wickedness to completion, and he who provokes words shall not be saved.

**19:8** He who acquires prudence loves himself, and he who keeps prudence shall find good things.

**19:9** A false witness shall not go unpunished, and whoever kindles wickedness shall perish by it.

**19:10** Luxury is not fitting for a fool, nor if a household servant begins to rule with insolence.

**19:11** A merciful man is longsuffering, and his boast comes upon transgressors.

**19:12** The threat of a king is like the roaring of a lion, but like dew upon the grass, so is his cheerfulness.

**19:13** A foolish son is a shame to a father; vows from the hire of a harlot are not pure.

**19:14** Fathers apportion house and possessions to children, but from God a wife is joined to a man.

**19:15** Cowardice holds fast an effeminate man, and the soul of an idle person shall hunger.

**19:16** He who keeps a commandment guards his own soul, but he who despises his own ways shall perish.

**19:17** He who has mercy on a poor man lends to God, and according to his gift it shall be repaid to him.

**19:18** Discipline your son, for thus he shall be of good hope, yet do not be lifted up in your soul to outrage.

**19:19** An evil-minded man shall be penalized; and if he acts wickedly, he shall add his own soul as well.

**19:20** Hear, son, the instruction of your father, that you may become wise in your latter days.

**19:21** Many thoughts are in the heart of a man, but the counsel of the Lord abides forever.

**19:22** Mercy is fruit to a man, and a righteous poor man is better than a lying rich man.

**19:23** The fear of the Lord leads to life for a man, but he who is without fear shall lodge in places where knowledge does not visit.

**19:24** He who hides his hands unjustly in his bosom shall by no means even bring them to his mouth.

**19:25** When a pestilent man is scourged, a fool becomes more shrewd; and if you reprove a prudent man, he will understand discernment.

**19:26** He who dishonors his father and drives away his mother shall be put to shame and become a reproach.

**19:27** A son who ceases to keep the instruction of a father shall meditate on evil sayings.

**19:28** He who becomes surety for a foolish youth mocks a righteous decree, and the mouth of the ungodly shall swallow down judgments.

**19:29** Scourges are prepared for the undisciplined, and punishments likewise for fools.

## Translator Notes

- **19:7** — The Greek text expands significantly beyond the Hebrew here, adding two further couplets on good insight and the consequences of persistent wrongdoing.
- **19:11** — Literally 'his boast comes upon transgressors,' reflecting a Septuagint rendering that differs from the Hebrew sense of overlooking an offense.
- **19:13** — The second clause ('vows from the hire of a harlot are not pure') is a distinctive Septuagint reading that diverges sharply from the Hebrew.
- **19:15** — The Greek term androgynaios denotes an effeminate or fainthearted man, yielding a distinct moral image from the Hebrew.
