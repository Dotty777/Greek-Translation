## Text

**8:1** You shall proclaim wisdom, that prudence may obey you.

**8:2** For she is upon the lofty heights, and she stands in the midst of the paths;

**8:3** for beside the gates of rulers she sits, and at the entrances she is praised:

**8:4** "To you, O men, I call, and I send forth my voice to the sons of men.

**8:5** Understand shrewdness, you innocent ones, and you who are uninstructed, lay up a heart within yourselves.

**8:6** Listen to me, for I will speak noble things, and from my lips I will bring forth what is right.

**8:7** For my throat shall meditate upon truth, and lying lips are an abomination before me;

**8:8** all the words of my mouth are with righteousness; nothing in them is crooked or twisted;

**8:9** all of them are plain before those who understand, and upright to those who find knowledge.

**8:10** Receive instruction and not silver, and knowledge rather than tested gold;

**8:11** for wisdom is better than costly stones, and no precious thing is worthy of her.

**8:12** I, wisdom, have made counsel my dwelling, and I have called upon knowledge and discretion; for wisdom is better than costly stones.

**8:13** The fear of the Lord hates unrighteousness, both insolence and arrogance and the ways of wicked men; and I have hated the perverted ways of evil men.

**8:14** Mine are counsel and security; mine is prudence, and mine is strength;

**8:15** by me kings reign, and rulers inscribe righteousness;

**8:16** by me great men are magnified, and sovereigns rule the earth by me.

**8:17** I love those who cherish me, and those who seek me shall find.

**8:18** Wealth and glory belong to me, even the possession of many things and righteousness.

**8:19** It is better to reap fruit from me than gold and precious stone, and my produce is better than choice silver.

**8:20** I walk in the ways of righteousness, and I move about in the midst of the paths of justice,

**8:21** that I may apportion substance to those who love me, and fill their treasuries with good things. If I declare to you the things that happen day by day, I will remember to recount the things from eternity.

**8:22** The Lord created me as the beginning of His ways for His works;

**8:23** before the age He founded me in the beginning, before He made the earth,

**8:24** and before He made the deeps, before the springs of the waters came forth,

**8:25** before the mountains were settled, and before all hills He begets me.

**8:26** The Lord made lands and uninhabited regions, and the inhabited heights of what lies beneath the heavens.

**8:27** When He was preparing the heaven, I was present with Him, and when He was setting apart His own throne upon the winds;

**8:28** and when He was making the clouds above strong, and when He was making secure the springs of what lies beneath heaven,

**8:29** and when He was making strong the foundations of the earth,

**8:30** I was beside Him, fitting things together; I was the one in whom He took delight, and day by day I rejoiced before His face at every season,

**8:31** when He was glad after bringing the inhabited world to completion, and rejoiced in the sons of men.

**8:32** Now therefore, son, listen to me.

**8:34** Blessed is the man who will listen to me, and the one who will guard my ways, keeping vigil at my doors day by day, watching over the doorposts of my entrances.

**8:35** For my goings forth are goings forth of life, and favor is prepared from the Lord.

**8:36** But those who sin against me act wickedly against their own souls, and those who hate me love death."

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Rather than the Hebrew rhetorical question ("Does not wisdom cry out?"), the Septuagint opens with a direct second-person statement: "You shall proclaim wisdom, that prudence may obey you."
- **8:21** — The Septuagint concludes this verse with an additional couplet promising that if Wisdom declares daily occurrences, she will also recount the things from eternity.
- **8:22** — The Septuagint renders the verb as "created" (ektisen) rather than "possessed," a reading central to early Christian Christological debates; compare the present-tense "begets" (genna) in 8:25.
- **8:27** — Where the Hebrew speaks of inscribing a circle on the face of the deep, the Greek reads "setting apart His own throne upon the winds."
- **8:30** — The participle harmozousa ("fitting things together" or "arranging in harmony") portrays Wisdom as a harmonizing artisan beside the Lord; verse 8:33 is absent from the Septuagint text.
