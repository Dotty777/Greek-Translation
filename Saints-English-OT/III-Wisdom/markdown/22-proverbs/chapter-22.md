## Text

**22:1** A good name is more desirable than great wealth, and good favor is above silver and gold.

**22:2** The rich and the poor meet one another, and the Lord made them both.

**22:3** A shrewd man, seeing a wicked man severely punished, is himself instructed, but the foolish pass on and suffer punishment.

**22:4** The offspring of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and wealth, and glory, and life.

**22:5** Briars and snares are in crooked ways, but he who guards his own soul shall keep away from them.

**22:7** The rich shall rule over the poor, and household servants shall lend to their own masters.

**22:8** He who sows worthless things shall reap evils, and he shall complete the stroke of his works. God blesses a cheerful man and a giver, and He shall bring to an end the vanity of his works.

**22:9** He who shows mercy to a poor man shall himself be nourished, for he has given of his own bread to the poor. He who gives gifts gains victory and honor, yet he takes away the soul of those who possess them.

**22:10** Cast out a pestilent man from the council, and strife shall go out with him; for whenever he sits in the council, he dishonors all.

**22:11** The Lord loves holy hearts, and all the blameless are acceptable to Him; a king shepherds with his lips.

**22:12** And the eyes of the Lord preserve discernment, but a lawless man despises words.

**22:13** A sluggard makes excuses and says, "There is a lion in the roads, and murderers are in the broad streets."

**22:14** The mouth of a lawless man is a deep pit, and he who is hated by the Lord shall fall into it. There are evil ways before a man, and he does not love to turn away from them; yet it is necessary to turn away from a crooked and evil way.

**22:15** Folly is fastened to the heart of a youth, and the rod and discipline are far from him.

**22:16** He who oppresses a poor man multiplies his own possessions, yet he gives to a rich man only to have less.

**22:17** Incline your ear to the words of the wise and hear my word, and apply your heart, that you may know that they are good.

**22:18** And if you put them into your heart, they shall gladden you together upon your lips;

**22:19** so that your hope may be upon the Lord, and He may make known to you your way.

**22:20** And you also, write them down for yourself threefold for counsel and knowledge upon the breadth of your soul.

**22:21** Therefore I teach you a true word and good knowledge to heed, so as to answer words of truth to those who question you.

**22:22** Do not oppress a poor man by violence, for he is destitute, and do not dishonor the weak in the gates.

**22:23** For the Lord shall judge his cause, and you shall deliver your inviolate soul.

**22:24** Do not be a companion to a hot-tempered man, and do not lodge with a wrathful friend,

**22:25** lest you learn of his ways and take snares for your soul.

**22:26** Do not give yourself as surety out of deference to someone's face;

**22:27** for if he has nothing with which to repay, they shall take the bedding from beneath your sides.

**22:28** Do not move the everlasting boundaries which your fathers set.

**22:29** A keen-sighted man who is prompt in his works ought to stand before kings, and not stand before sluggish men.

## Translator Notes

- **22:7** — The Septuagint reverses the second clause relative to the Hebrew Masoretic Text, stating that household servants will lend to their own masters rather than that the borrower serves the lender.
- **22:8** — The Septuagint includes an additional couplet (often numbered 22:8a) beginning "God blesses a cheerful man and a giver," echoed by Paul in Second Corinthians nine seven.
- **22:15** — Unlike the Hebrew text, the Septuagint omits a verb for driving folly away and states simply that "the rod and discipline are far from him," with Greek paidia representing the common spelling of paideia ("discipline").
- **22:20** — "Threefold" renders the Septuagint's understanding of a debated Hebrew term, and "upon the breadth of your soul" amplifies the scribal image of inscribing counsel within.
- **22:23** — The Septuagint reads "you shall deliver your inviolate soul," promising security to the one who refrains from oppressing the poor rather than describing the despoiling of oppressors as in the Hebrew text.
