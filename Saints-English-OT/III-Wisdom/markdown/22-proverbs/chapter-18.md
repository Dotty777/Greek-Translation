## Text

**18:1** A man who wishes to separate himself from friends seeks pretexts, and at every season he shall be subject to reproach.

**18:2** He who lacks understanding has no need of wisdom, for he is led rather by folly.

**18:3** Whenever an ungodly man comes into a depth of evils, he shows contempt, and dishonor and reproach come upon him.

**18:4** A word in the heart of a man is deep water, and a river and a fountain of life spring forth.

**18:5** To respect the person of an ungodly man is not good, nor is it holy to turn aside justice in judgment.

**18:6** The lips of a fool lead him into evils, and his bold mouth calls upon death.

**18:7** The mouth of a fool is destruction to him, and his lips are a snare to his soul.

**18:8** Fear casts down the slothful, and the souls of the effeminate shall hunger.

**18:9** He who does not heal himself in his works is a brother to him who ruins himself.

**18:10** From greatness of strength is the name of the Lord, and the righteous who run to it are exalted.

**18:11** The substance of a rich man is a fortified city, and its glory casts a great shadow.

**18:12** Before destruction the heart of a man is lifted up, and before glory it is humbled.

**18:13** He who answers a word before hearing, it is folly and reproach to him.

**18:14** A prudent servant calms the wrath of a man, but who shall endure a fainthearted man?

**18:15** The heart of a prudent man acquires perception, and the ears of the wise seek understanding.

**18:16** A man's gift enlarges him and seats him beside rulers.

**18:17** A righteous man is his own accuser in the opening speech; and when the adversary falls upon him, he is put to the test.

**18:18** A silent man puts an end to contentions, and among powers he makes decisions.

**18:19** A brother helped by a brother is like a fortified and lofty city, and he is strong as a firmly founded palace.

**18:20** From the fruits of the mouth a man fills his belly, and from the fruits of his lips he shall be filled.

**18:21** Death and life are in the hand of the tongue, and those who master it shall eat its fruits.

**18:22** He who has found a good wife has found favors and has received gladness from God; he who casts out a good wife casts out good things, and he who keeps an adulteress is foolish and ungodly.

## Translator Notes

- **18:8** — The Greek differs substantially from the Masoretic Text's saying about a whisperer, presenting instead a proverb on fear and slothfulness.
- **18:10** — The Greek reads "From greatness of strength," reflecting a different reading of the consonants underlying "tower of strength" in the Masoretic Text; "to it" refers grammatically to the name of the Lord.
- **18:17** — The Greek presents a courtroom maxim in which the righteous person begins by accusing himself, and "he is put to the test" (or "exposed") may refer to either the speaker or the adversary.
- **18:18** — The Greek reads "a silent man" where the Masoretic Text has "the lot."
- **18:22** — The Greek adds a second couplet contrasting the dismissal of a good wife with keeping an adulteress, and ends chapter 18 here without the two verses found at 18:23-24 in the Masoretic Text.
