## Text

**9:1** Wisdom built a house for herself, and set up seven pillars;

**9:2** she slew her offerings, mixed her wine in a mixing bowl, and made ready her table.

**9:3** She sent forth her servants, calling together with a lofty proclamation to the mixing bowl, saying,

**9:4** "Whoever is foolish, let him turn aside to me," and to those who lack sense she said,

**9:5** "Come, eat of my loaves, and drink wine which I have mixed for you.

**9:6** Forsake foolishness, that you may reign forever, and seek prudence, and set understanding upright in knowledge."

**9:7** He who instructs evil men shall receive dishonor for himself, and he who reproves the ungodly man shall bring reproach upon himself.

**9:8** Do not reprove evil men, lest they hate you; reprove a wise man, and he will love you.

**9:9** Give an occasion to a wise man, and he will become wiser; impart knowledge to a righteous man, and he will increase in receiving.

**9:10** The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and the counsel of the holy ones is understanding; for to know the law belongs to a good mind.

**9:11** For in this manner you shall live a long time, and years of your life shall be added to you.

**9:12** Son, if you become wise for yourself, you shall also be wise for your neighbors; but if you turn out evil, you alone shall endure evils. He who relies upon falsehoods, this man shepherds winds, and the same shall pursue flying birds; for he has forsaken the ways of his own vineyard, and has strayed from the tracks of his own field; he journeys through a waterless wilderness and a land appointed to parched places, and with his hands he gathers barrenness.

**9:13** A foolish and brazen woman comes to lack a morsel of bread, she who does not know shame;

**9:14** she sat at the doors of her own house upon a seat, openly in the streets,

**9:15** calling to those who pass by and go straight along their ways,

**9:16** "Whoever among you is most foolish, let him turn aside to me; and to those who lack prudence I give this charge, saying,

**9:17** 'Gladly partake of hidden loaves, and of sweet stolen water.'"

**9:18** But he does not know that the earthborn perish with her, and he meets the trap of Hades. Rather, leap away, do not linger in the place, nor fix your eye upon her; for thus you shall pass across strange water. Abstain from strange water, and do not drink from a strange fountain, that you may live a long time, and years of life may be added to you.

## Translator Notes

- **9:6** — The Septuagint reads "that you may reign forever" (βασιλεύσητε) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "and live."
- **9:10** — The Greek reads "counsel of the holy ones" (βουλὴ ἁγίων) rather than the Hebrew "knowledge of the Holy One," and adds the concluding line concerning knowledge of the law.
- **9:12** — The Septuagint expands this verse with an extended poetic warning about one who relies on falsehoods, shepherds winds, and abandons his own vineyard (often numbered 9:12a-c).
- **9:18** — Greek γηγενεῖς ("the earthborn") renders the Hebrew Rephaim (shades of the dead), and πέτευρον denotes a springboard or trap-plank leading down into Hades. The Septuagint also appends the concluding exhortation to flee strange water (9:18a-d).
