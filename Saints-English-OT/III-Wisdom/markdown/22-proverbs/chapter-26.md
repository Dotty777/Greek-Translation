## Text

**26:1** As dew in harvest and as rain in summer, so there is no honor for a fool.

**26:2** As birds and sparrows fly, so a vain curse shall not come upon anyone.

**26:3** As a whip for a horse and a goad for a donkey, so is a rod for a lawless nation.

**26:4** Do not answer a fool in response to his folly, lest you become like him;

**26:5** but answer a fool according to his folly, lest he appear wise to himself.

**26:6** Out of his own ways he brings reproach upon himself who sends a word through a foolish messenger.

**26:7** Take away the walking of legs, and lawlessness from the mouth of fools.

**26:8** He who binds a stone in a sling is like one who gives glory to a fool.

**26:9** Thorns grow in the hand of a drunkard, and servitude in the hand of fools.

**26:10** All the flesh of fools is greatly tempest-tossed, for their consternation is crushed.

**26:11** As a dog when he returns to his own vomit and becomes detestable, so is a fool in his own wickedness who turns back to his own sin. There is a shame that brings on sin, and there is a shame that is glory and grace.

**26:12** I saw a man who seemed wise to himself; yet a fool had more hope than he.

**26:13** A sluggard, when sent on a way, says, "There is a lion in the ways."

**26:14** As a door revolves upon its hinge, so does a sluggard upon his bed.

**26:15** Having hidden his hand in his bosom, a sluggard will not be able to bring it to his mouth.

**26:16** A sluggard appears wiser to himself than one who brings back a message in fullness.

**26:17** As one who seizes a dog's tail, so is one who presides over another's dispute.

**26:18** As those who heal put forth words to men, and the first one who meets the word shall be tripped up;

**26:19** so are all who lie in ambush for their own friends, and when they are seen, they say, "I did it in jest."

**26:20** With much wood a fire flourishes, and where there is no double-minded man, strife grows quiet.

**26:21** A hearth for coals and wood for fire, and a reviling man for the tumult of strife.

**26:22** The words of knaves are soft, yet they strike into the inner chambers of the inward parts.

**26:23** Silver given with deceit must be reckoned as a potsherd; smooth lips conceal a grievous heart.

**26:24** He who weeps over enemies consents to all things with his lips, yet in his heart he contrives words.

**26:25** If your enemy pleads with you with a loud voice, do not be persuaded; for seven evils are in his soul.

**26:26** He who conceals enmity frames deceit, and he covers up his own sins, though well known in councils.

**26:27** He who digs a pit for his neighbor shall fall into it; and he who rolls a stone rolls it upon himself.

**26:28** A lying tongue hates truth, and an unguarded mouth produces disorders.

## Translator Notes

- **26:3** — The Greek reads "a lawless nation" (ethnei paranomo), reflecting a Hebrew reading of "nation" (goy) rather than the Masoretic "back [of fools]" (gev).
- **26:11** — The second sentence ("There is a shame that brings on sin...") is a Septuagint addition paralleling Sirach 4:21 and absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **26:18** — The Greek reads "those who heal" (hoi iomenoi), departing markedly from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's picture of a madman hurling firebrands and arrows.
- **26:24** — Translated directly from the Greek text as given ("weeping over enemies," apoklaiomenos echthrous, and "words," logous); some Greek manuscripts read "a weeping enemy" (echthros) and "deceits" (dolous).
