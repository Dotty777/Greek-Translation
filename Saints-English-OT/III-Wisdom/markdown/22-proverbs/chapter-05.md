## Text

**5:1** Son, give heed to my wisdom, and incline your ear to my words,

**5:2** that you may keep good understanding; and the discernment of my lips commands you.

**5:3** Do not give heed to a worthless woman; for honey drips from the lips of a harlot woman, who for a season makes your throat smooth;

**5:4** yet afterward you shall find it more bitter than gall, and sharper than a two-edged sword.

**5:5** For the feet of folly lead down those who associate with her with death into Hades, and her footsteps are not firmly established;

**5:6** for she does not walk in the ways of life, and her paths are slippery and not easily known.

**5:7** Now therefore, son, hear me, and do not make my words of no effect.

**5:8** Make your way far from her; do not draw near to the doors of her houses,

**5:9** lest you surrender your life to others, and your living to the merciless;

**5:10** lest strangers be filled with your strength, and your labors enter into the houses of strangers;

**5:11** and you shall feel remorse at the last, when the flesh of your body is worn away,

**5:12** and you shall say, "How I hated instruction, and my heart turned aside from reproofs;

**5:13** I did not listen to the voice of him who instructed me and taught me, nor did I incline my ear;

**5:14** I was almost in all evil in the midst of the assembly and congregation."

**5:15** Drink waters from your own vessels, and from the spring of your own wells.

**5:16** Do not let waters overflow for you from your spring, but let your waters flow out into your own streets;

**5:17** let them belong to you alone, and let no stranger share with you.

**5:18** Let your spring of water be your own, and rejoice with the wife of your youth.

**5:19** Let a doe of friendship and a colt of your graces keep company with you, and let your own wife lead you and be with you at all times; for walking continually in her friendship, you shall be greatly multiplied.

**5:20** Do not be much with a strange woman, nor come into the embrace of her who is not your own;

**5:21** for the ways of a man are before the eyes of God, and He looks upon all his paths.

**5:22** Transgressions ensnare a man, and each one is tightly bound with the cords of his own sins.

**5:23** This man dies with the uninstructed; from the abundance of his own living he is cast out, and he perishes through folly.

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — Greek 'the discernment of my lips commands you' differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ('your lips may keep knowledge'), attributing the authoritative counsel directly to the teacher's speech.
- **5:3** — The Septuagint opens with the additional warning 'Do not give heed to a worthless woman' before describing the harlot's smooth speech.
- **5:16** — Unlike the Hebrew text, which is commonly read as a rhetorical question, the Septuagint introduces a direct prohibition ('Do not let waters overflow') and specifies 'your own streets.'
- **5:19** — The Septuagint expands the imagery of conjugal fidelity and uses the rare term pollostos ('greatly multiplied' or 'of great account') to describe the blessing of abiding in one's wife's friendship.
