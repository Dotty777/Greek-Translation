## Text

**20:1** Wine is unrestrained and strong drink is insolent, and every fool is entangled with such things.

**20:2** The threat of a king does not differ from the wrath of a lion, and he who provokes him sins against his own soul.

**20:3** It is a glory for a man to turn away from revilings, but every fool is entangled with such things.

**20:4** When reproached, a sluggard is not ashamed, and likewise he who borrows grain in harvest.

**20:5** Counsel in the heart of a man is deep water, and a prudent man shall draw it out.

**20:6** A great thing is a man, and a precious thing is a merciful man, yet it is a hard task to find a faithful man.

**20:7** He who walks blameless in righteousness shall leave his children blessed.

**20:8** When a righteous king sits upon a throne, no evil thing withstands before his eyes.

**20:9** Who will boast that he has a pure heart? Or who will boldly claim to be clean from sins? The lamp of him who speaks evil of father or mother shall be extinguished, and the pupils of his eyes shall see darkness. A portion hastily gained at the first shall not be blessed at the last. Do not say, "I will take vengeance on the enemy," but wait for the Lord, that He may help you.

**20:10** A great and a small weight, and double measures—both of them are unclean before the Lord;

**20:11** and he who makes them shall be fettered in his practices; a young man is with a devout one, and his way is straight.

**20:12** The ear hears and the eye sees; both of them are works of the Lord.

**20:13** Do not love to slander, lest you be taken away; open your eyes and be filled with bread.

**20:23** A double weight is an abomination to the Lord, and a deceitful balance is not good before Him.

**20:24** From the Lord the steps of a man are directed, but how could a mortal understand his ways?

**20:25** It is a snare for a man hastily to consecrate something of his own, for after vowing comes regret.

**20:26** A wise king is a winnower of the ungodly, and he shall bring a wheel upon them.

**20:27** The breath of men is a light of the Lord, who searches the inner chambers of the belly.

**20:28** Mercy and truth are a guard for a king, and they shall encompass his throne in righteousness.

**20:29** Wisdom is an ornament to young men, and gray hairs are the glory of elders.

**20:30** Bruises and fractures befall the wicked, and stripes into the inner chambers of the belly.

## Translator Notes

- **20:9** — In the Septuagint arrangement, the sayings corresponding to Hebrew 20:20–22 are placed here within verse 9, while verses 14–19 are omitted.
- **20:11** — The Greek syntax is notably terse, stating that the maker of false weights shall be fettered in his practices before abruptly contrasting a young man who associates with a devout person.
- **20:13** — The Septuagint reads 'to slander' (katalalein) rather than 'sleep,' and warns against being removed rather than coming to poverty.
- **20:27** — The masculine relative pronoun ('who') refers grammatically to 'the Lord' rather than to the neuter 'light' or the feminine 'breath.'
