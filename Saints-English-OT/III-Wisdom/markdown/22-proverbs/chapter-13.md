## Text

**13:1** A prudent son is obedient to his father, but a disobedient son is in destruction.

**13:2** From the fruits of righteousness a good man shall eat, but the souls of the lawless shall perish before their time.

**13:3** He who guards his own mouth preserves his own soul, but he who is rash with his lips shall bring terror upon himself.

**13:4** Every idle man is full of desires, but the hands of the valiant are in diligence.

**13:5** A righteous man hates an unjust word, but an ungodly man is put to shame and shall have no boldness.

**13:7** There are those who make themselves rich, yet have nothing; and there are those who humble themselves in great wealth.

**13:8** The ransom of a man's soul is his own wealth, but a poor man does not endure a threat.

**13:9** Light belongs to the righteous continually, but the light of the ungodly is extinguished. Deceitful souls go astray in sins, but the righteous have compassion and show mercy.

**13:10** An evil man commits evil deeds with insolence, but those who are judges of themselves are wise.

**13:11** Substance hurriedly gained with lawlessness diminishes, but he who gathers for himself with godliness shall be multiplied; a righteous man has compassion and lends.

**13:12** Better is he who helps from the heart those who are beginning than he who promises and leads into hope; for a good desire is a tree of life.

**13:13** He who despises a matter shall be despised by it; but he who fears a commandment, that man is sound in health. For a deceitful son there shall be nothing good; but for a wise servant his deeds shall prosper, and his way shall be directed straight.

**13:14** The law of the wise is a fountain of life, but the senseless man shall die by a snare.

**13:15** Good understanding gives favor, and to know the law belongs to a good mind, but the ways of despisers are in destruction.

**13:16** Every prudent man acts with knowledge, but the fool spreads open his own wickedness.

**13:17** A rash king shall fall into evils, but a wise messenger shall deliver him.

**13:18** Instruction takes away poverty and dishonor, and he who keeps reproofs shall be honored.

**13:19** The desires of the godly sweeten the soul, but the works of the ungodly are far from knowledge.

**13:20** By walking with the wise you shall be wise, but he who walks with fools shall be known.

**13:21** Evils shall pursue those who sin, but good things shall overtake the righteous.

**13:22** A good man shall leave an inheritance to his sons' sons, and the wealth of the ungodly is stored up for the righteous.

**13:23** The righteous shall spend many years in wealth, but the unjust shall perish swiftly.

**13:24** He who spares the rod hates his son, but he who loves him disciplines him diligently.

**13:25** A righteous man eats and fills his soul, but the souls of the ungodly are in want.

## Translator Notes

- **13:9** — The second couplet ("Deceitful souls go astray...") is a distinctive Septuagint expansion not present in the Masoretic Text.
- **13:12** — The Septuagint renders the opening clause distinctively, contrasting wholehearted practical aid for those starting out with empty promises that merely lead into hope.
- **13:13** — The second sentence ("For a deceitful son...") is an additional Septuagint proverb absent from the Masoretic Text.
- **13:17** — The Greek reads "a rash king" (basileus), differing from the Masoretic Text's "wicked messenger."
