## Text

**31:1** My words have been spoken by God: the oracle of a king whom his mother instructed.

**31:2** What, child, will you keep? What? The utterances of God. Firstborn, I speak to you, O son: what, child of my womb? What, child of my vows?

**31:3** Do not give your wealth to women, nor your mind and livelihood to remorse.

**31:4** Do all things with counsel; drink wine with counsel. Rulers are prone to wrath; let them not drink wine,

**31:5** lest they drink and forget wisdom, and be altogether unable to judge the weak rightly.

**31:6** Give strong drink to those in sorrow, and wine to drink to those in anguish,

**31:7** that they may forget their poverty, and remember their toils no more.

**31:8** Open your mouth with the word of God, and judge all soundly;

**31:9** open your mouth and judge righteously, and decide the cause of the poor and the weak.

**31:10** Who shall find a valiant woman? For such a one is more precious than costly stones.

**31:11** The heart of her husband is confident in her; such a one shall not lack fine spoils;

**31:12** for she works good for her husband all her life.

**31:13** Winding wool and flax, she makes what is useful with her hands.

**31:14** She is like a ship trading from afar, and she gathers her livelihood,

**31:15** and rises while it is still night. She gives food to her household and tasks to her maidservants.

**31:16** Considering a field, she buys it; and from the fruits of her hands she plants a possession.

**31:17** Girding her loins strongly, she braces her arms for work.

**31:18** And she tastes that it is good to work, and her lamp is not extinguished all the night.

**31:19** She stretches out her forearms to what is profitable, and braces her hands to the spindle.

**31:20** She opens wide her hands to the poor, and stretches out fruit to the needy.

**31:21** Her husband is not anxious about those in the house whenever he tarries anywhere, for all those with her are clothed.

**31:22** She makes double cloaks for her husband, and garments of fine linen and purple for herself.

**31:23** And her husband becomes distinguished in the gates whenever he sits in council with the elders, the inhabitants of the land.

**31:25** She makes fine linen cloths and sells them, and girdles to the Canaanites. She opens her mouth heedfully and lawfully, and sets order upon her tongue.

**31:26** She clothes herself with strength and dignity, and rejoices in the latter days.

**31:27** The ways of her households are well-kept, and she does not eat the food of idleness.

**31:28** She opens her mouth to the wise in lawful order; her mercy raises up her children and they grow rich, and her husband praises her:

**31:29** "Many daughters have acquired wealth, many have done mighty deeds; but you surpass and excel them all."

**31:30** Charms are deceitful and the beauty of a woman is vain; for a discerning woman is blessed, and let her praise the fear of the Lord.

**31:31** Give to her of the fruits of her lips, and let her husband be praised in the gates.

## Translator Notes

- **31:1** — The Greek text does not name King Lemuel, instead introducing the passage as words spoken by God and the oracle of an unnamed king instructed by his mother.
- **31:20** — The Greek noun karpon ("fruit") can also mean "wrist" (an anatomical parallel to "hands" in the first clause), denoting either the produce she shares or the hand she extends to the needy.
- **31:21** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text mentions snow and scarlet clothing, the Greek text states that her husband is not anxious about those in the house when he tarries away because all with her are clothed.
- **31:25** — In the Septuagint verse arrangement, material corresponding to Hebrew 31:24 and 31:26 is combined in verse 25 (with verse 24 omitted), and the traders are rendered specifically as "the Canaanites."
- **31:31** — The Greek text reads "fruits of her lips" (rather than "hands") and concludes with "let her husband be praised in the gates" (rather than "her works"), following verse 30's distinct exhortation that the discerning woman praise the fear of the Lord.
