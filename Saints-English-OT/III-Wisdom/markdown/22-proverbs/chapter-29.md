# Proverbs 29 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**29:1** Better is a man who reproves than a stiff-necked man, for when he is suddenly set aflame, there is no healing.

**29:2** When the righteous are praised, peoples shall rejoice; but when the ungodly rule, men groan.

**29:3** The father of a man who loves wisdom rejoices, but he who feeds harlots shall destroy wealth.

**29:4** A righteous king raises up a land, but a lawless man tears it down.

**29:5** He who prepares a net before the face of his own friend casts it around his own feet.

**29:6** For a man who sins there is a great snare, but the righteous shall be in joy and in gladness.

**29:7** A righteous man knows how to judge for the needy, but the ungodly does not understand knowledge, and the poor man has no discerning mind.

**29:8** Lawless men set a city ablaze, but the wise turn away wrath.

**29:9** A wise man judges nations, but a worthless man, when angry, is laughed to scorn and does not cower.

**29:10** Men who partake in bloodshed hate a devout man, but the upright shall seek out his soul.

**29:11** A fool brings forth all his fury, but a wise man holds it in reserve little by little.

**29:12** When a king listens to an unjust word, all those under him are lawless.

**29:13** When a creditor and a debtor meet one another, the Lord keeps watch over them both.

**29:14** When a king judges the poor in truth, his throne shall be established for a testimony.

**29:15** Blows and reproofs impart wisdom, but a straying child puts his parents to shame.

**29:16** When the ungodly are many, sins multiply; but when those fall, the righteous become greatly afraid.

**29:17** Discipline your son, and he shall give you rest, and he shall bestow adornment upon your soul.

**29:18** There shall by no means be an interpreter for a lawless nation, but he who guards the law is counted blessed.

**29:19** A stubborn servant will not be disciplined by words; for even if he understands, yet he will not obey.

**29:20** If you see a man hasty in words, know that a fool has more hope than he.

**29:21** He who lives in luxury from childhood shall become a servant, and in the end he shall grieve over himself.

**29:22** A wrathful man stirs up strife, and an angry man digs up sins.

**29:23** Arrogance humbles a man, but the Lord upholds the humble-minded with glory.

**29:24** He who shares with a thief hates his own soul; and if, when an oath is put forth, they hear and do not report it,

**29:25** fearing and being ashamed before men, they are tripped up; but he who trusts in the Lord shall rejoice. Ungodliness brings a downfall upon a man, but he who trusts in the Master shall be saved.

**29:26** Many wait upon the faces of rulers, but from the Lord comes justice for a man.

**29:27** A righteous man is an abomination to the unrighteous, and a straight path is an abomination to the lawless.

## Translator Notes

- **29:1** — The Septuagint reads 'Better is a man who reproves than a stiff-necked man, for when he is suddenly set aflame, there is no healing,' differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which speaks of a man often reproved who stiffens his neck and is suddenly broken.
- **29:7** — The Septuagint includes a third clause not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text: 'and the poor man has no discerning mind.'
- **29:14** — The Septuagint reads 'for a testimony' (Greek eis martyrion), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonantal text than the Masoretic Text's 'forever.'
- **29:25** — The Septuagint presents a double rendering of this verse, joining the first clause seamlessly to the oath-breaking scenario of verse 24 and adding a second parallel saying that refers to God as 'the Master' (Greek despotes).
