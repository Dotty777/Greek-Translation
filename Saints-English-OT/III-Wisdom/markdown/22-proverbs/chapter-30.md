## Text

**30:1** Fear my words, son, and having received them, repent. Thus says the man to those who believe in God, and I cease.

**30:2** For I am the most foolish of all men, and the discernment of men is not in me.

**30:3** God has taught me wisdom, and I have known the knowledge of the holy ones.

**30:4** Who has ascended into heaven and descended? Who has gathered winds in a bosom? Who has wrapped up water in a garment? Who has laid hold of the ends of the earth? What is His name, or what is the name of His children?

**30:5** For all the words of God are refined by fire, and He Himself shields those who revere Him.

**30:6** Do not add to His words, lest He reprove you and you become a liar.

**30:7** Two things I ask of You; do not take away favor from me before I die:

**30:8** put far from me a vain and false word; do not give me wealth or poverty, but appoint for me what is needful and sufficient;

**30:9** lest, being filled, I become false and say, "Who sees me?" or, becoming poor, I steal and swear by the name of God.

**30:10** Do not deliver a servant into the hands of a master, lest he curse you and you be destroyed.

**30:11** An evil offspring curses his father and does not bless his mother.

**30:12** An evil offspring judges himself righteous, yet has not washed away his going forth.

**30:13** An evil offspring has lofty eyes, and lifts himself up with his eyelids.

**30:14** An evil offspring has swords as his teeth and knives as his jaw-teeth, so as to consume and devour the humble from the earth and their needy from among men.

**30:15** The leech had three daughters loved with love, and these three did not satisfy her; and the fourth was not content to say, "Enough!"

**30:16** Hades, and the passion of a woman, and Tartarus, and earth that is not satisfied with water, and water and fire shall by no means say, "It is enough!"

**30:17** The eye that mocks a father and dishonors the old age of a mother—may the ravens from the valleys pluck it out, and may the young of eagles devour it.

**30:18** Now there are three things impossible for me to understand, and the fourth I do not know:

**30:19** the tracks of a flying eagle, and the ways of a serpent upon a rock, and the paths of a ship sailing the sea, and the ways of a man in youth.

**30:20** Such is the way of an adulterous woman, who, when she has acted, washes herself and says she has done nothing wrong.

**30:21** On account of three things the earth is shaken, and the fourth it cannot bear:

**30:22** if a servant becomes king, and a fool is filled with food,

**30:23** and if a maidservant casts out her own mistress, and if a hated woman obtains a good husband.

**30:24** Now four things are the smallest upon the earth, yet these are wiser than the wise:

**30:25** the ants, which have no strength, yet they prepare their food in the summer;

**30:26** and the rock badgers, a tribe not strong, who make their own homes in the rocks;

**30:27** the locust is without a king, yet it marches forth in good order at a single command;

**30:28** and the lizard, supporting itself with its hands and easily caught, dwells in the strongholds of kings.

**30:29** Now there are three things that go smoothly on their way, and a fourth that strides well:

**30:30** a lion's whelp, mightier than the beasts, which does not turn aside nor cower before any beast;

**30:31** and a rooster walking boldly among the hens, and a he-goat leading a herd of goats, and a king addressing a nation.

**30:32** If you give yourself over to mirth and stretch out your hand with strife, you shall be dishonored.

**30:33** Press out milk, and there shall be butter; and if you squeeze nostrils, blood shall come forth; and if you draw out words, judgments and strifes shall come forth.

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text opens with the proper names Agur, Jakeh, Ithiel, and Ucal, the Septuagint interprets these forms as common nouns and verbs, yielding an exhortation to fear the speaker's words and an address to those who believe in God.
- **30:4** — The Septuagint reads the plural 'His children' (Greek tois teknois autou), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has the singular 'his son.'
- **30:12** — Greek exodos ('going forth') functions here as a euphemism for bodily filth or excrement, corresponding to the Hebrew term for filth.
- **30:15** — The Septuagint counts 'three daughters' and a 'fourth' rather than the Hebrew Masoretic Text's 'two daughters,' and in verse 16 expands the list of insatiable things to include Tartarus and water.
- **30:19** — The Septuagint reads 'in youth' (Greek en neoteti), reflecting an abstract noun vocalization of the Hebrew consonants rendered 'with a maiden' in the Masoretic Text.
