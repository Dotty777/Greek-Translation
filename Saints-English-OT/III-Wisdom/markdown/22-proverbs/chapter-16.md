## Text

**16:2** All the works of the humble man are manifest before God, but the ungodly shall perish in an evil day.

**16:5** Everyone who is haughty in heart is unclean before God, and he who joins hand to hand unjustly shall not be held guiltless.

**16:7** The beginning of a good way is to do righteous things, and these are more acceptable before God than to offer sacrifices.

**16:8** He who seeks the Lord shall find knowledge with righteousness, and those who seek Him aright shall find peace.

**16:9** All the works of the Lord are with righteousness, and the ungodly man is reserved for an evil day.

**16:10** An oracle is upon the lips of a king, and in judgment his mouth shall by no means err.

**16:11** The turn of the scales is righteousness before the Lord, and His works are just weights.

**16:12** He who does evil is an abomination to a king, for with righteousness a throne of dominion is prepared.

**16:13** Righteous lips are acceptable to a king, and he loves upright words.

**16:14** The fury of a king is a messenger of death, but a wise man will appease it.

**16:15** In the light of life is the son of a king, and those acceptable to him are like a latter cloud.

**16:16** The nests of wisdom are more desirable than gold, and the nests of prudence are more desirable than silver.

**16:17** The paths of life turn aside from evils, and the ways of righteousness are length of life. He who receives instruction shall be among good things, and he who keeps reproofs shall be made wise. He who guards his own ways preserves his own soul, and he who loves his life will spare his mouth.

**16:18** Arrogance goes before ruin, and evil-mindedness before a fall.

**16:19** Better is a gentle-spirited man with humility than he who divides spoil with the arrogant.

**16:20** He who is understanding in affairs is a finder of good things, and he who trusts in God is most blessed.

**16:21** They call the wise and understanding worthless, yet those who are sweet in speech shall hear more.

**16:22** Insight is a fountain of life to those who possess it, but the instruction of fools is evil.

**16:23** The heart of a wise man will understand what comes from his own mouth, and upon his lips he will bear discernment.

**16:24** Good words are combs of honey, and its sweetness is healing for the soul.

**16:25** There are ways that seem upright to a man, yet their end looks toward the depth of Hades.

**16:26** A man in toils labors for himself and drives away his own destruction; the crooked man, however, bears destruction upon his own mouth.

**16:27** A foolish man digs up evils for himself, and upon his own lips he treasures up fire.

**16:28** A crooked man sends forth evils, and he will kindle a torch of deceit for the wicked, and he separates friends.

**16:29** A lawless man tempts friends and leads them away along ways that are not good.

**16:30** And fixing his eyes, he devises perverse things, and with his lips he decrees all evils; this man is a furnace of wickedness.

**16:31** Old age is a crown of glorying, and it is found in the ways of righteousness.

**16:32** Better is a longsuffering man than a mighty one, and he who masters wrath is better than one who captures a city.

**16:33** All things come into the laps of the unrighteous, yet all righteous things are from the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **16:2** — The Septuagint begins chapter 16 at verse 2, omitting counterparts to Masoretic 16:1, 3, 4, and 6 here and arranging several proverbs differently across chapters 15 and 16.
- **16:15** — The Greek reads "the son of a king" rather than "the king's countenance," and compares those acceptable to him to a "latter cloud" bringing late-season rain.
- **16:16** — The Septuagint translates the repeated noun as "nests" (nossiai) rather than "acquiring," reflecting a different reading of the underlying Hebrew consonants.
- **16:21** — Differing from the Hebrew, the Septuagint states that the wise and understanding are called "worthless" (phaulous), while those sweet in speech gain a greater hearing.
- **16:33** — Rather than the Hebrew saying about casting a lot into the lap, the Septuagint contrasts all things coming into the laps of the unrighteous with all righteous things that come from the Lord.
