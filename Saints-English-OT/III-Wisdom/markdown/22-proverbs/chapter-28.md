## Text

**28:1** An ungodly man flees when no one pursues, but a righteous man is confident as a lion.

**28:2** Because of the sins of the ungodly, disputes are stirred up, but a shrewd man shall quench them.

**28:3** A man bold in ungodly deeds oppresses the poor. Just as a violent and unprofitable rain,

**28:4** so those who forsake the law commend ungodliness; but those who love the law place a wall around themselves.

**28:5** Evil men shall not understand judgment, but those who seek the Lord shall understand in all things.

**28:6** Better is a poor man who walks in truth than a rich man who is false.

**28:7** A discerning son keeps the law, but he who shepherds debauchery dishonors his father.

**28:8** He who multiplies his wealth by interest and excessive gains gathers it for him who shows mercy to the poor.

**28:9** He who turns away his ear so as not to hear the law, he himself also has made his prayer abominable.

**28:10** He who leads the upright astray in an evil way shall himself fall into ruin, and the lawless shall pass through good things and shall not enter into them.

**28:11** A rich man is wise in his own estimation, but an understanding poor man shall condemn him.

**28:12** Through the help of the righteous there is great glory, but in the places of the ungodly men are captured.

**28:13** He who covers over his own ungodliness shall not prosper, but he who declares reproofs shall be loved.

**28:14** Blessed is the man who fears in all things out of reverence, but he who is hard of heart shall fall into evils.

**28:15** A hungry lion and a thirsty wolf is he who, being poor, rules as a tyrant over a needy nation.

**28:16** A king lacking in revenues is a great oppressor, but he who hates unrighteousness shall live a long time.

**28:17** He who becomes surety for a man under a charge of murder shall be a fugitive and not in safety. Discipline a son and he shall love you, and he shall give adornment to your soul; you shall by no means obey a lawless nation.

**28:18** He who walks righteously is helped, but he who walks in crooked ways shall be entangled.

**28:19** He who tills his own land shall be filled with bread, but he who pursues idleness shall be filled with poverty.

**28:20** A trustworthy man shall be greatly blessed, but the evil man shall not go unpunished.

**28:21** He who does not respect the faces of the righteous is not good; such a one will sell a man for a morsel of bread.

**28:22** An envious man hastens to become rich, and he does not know that a merciful man shall prevail over him.

**28:23** He who reproves a man's ways shall have greater favor than he who flatters with the tongue.

**28:24** He who casts off father or mother and thinks he does not sin, this one is a partner of an ungodly man.

**28:25** An unfaithful man judges rashly, but he who trusts in the Lord shall be well cared for.

**28:26** He who trusts in a presumptuous heart, such a one is a fool; but he who walks in wisdom shall be saved.

**28:27** He who gives to the poor shall not be in want, but he who turns away his eye shall be in great distress.

**28:28** In the places of the ungodly the righteous groan, but in their destruction the righteous shall be multiplied.

## Translator Notes

- **28:3** — In the Septuagint, the closing clause of verse 3 ('Just as a violent and unprofitable rain,') forms the first half of a comparison completed in verse 4.
- **28:15** — The Septuagint reads 'a thirsty wolf' and 'being poor,' likely reflecting a variant Hebrew reading of 'poor' (rash) rather than 'wicked' (rasha).
- **28:17** — The opening clause warns against becoming surety for someone charged with murder, while the second sentence ('Discipline a son... lawless nation') is an addition distinctive to the Septuagint.
- **28:22** — The Septuagint reads 'a merciful man' (eleemon) where the Hebrew has 'want' (cheser), likely reflecting a reading of the closely similar Hebrew word for mercy (chesed).
