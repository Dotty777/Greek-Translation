## Text

**25:1** These are the unarranged instructions of Solomon, which the friends of Hezekiah, king of Judea, copied out.

**25:2** The glory of God conceals a matter, but the glory of a king honors affairs.

**25:3** Heaven is high, and the earth is deep, and the heart of a king is unsearchable.

**25:4** Beat impure silver, and all of it shall be purified clean;

**25:5** slay the ungodly from the presence of a king, and his throne shall prosper in righteousness.

**25:6** Do not boast before a king, nor take your stand in the places of rulers;

**25:7** for it is better for you that it be said, "Come up to me," than that you should be humbled in the presence of a ruler. Speak the things which your eyes have seen.

**25:8** Do not plunge hastily into strife, lest you regret it in the end, whenever your own friend reproaches you.

**25:9** Withdraw backward, do not be contemptuous,

**25:10** lest your friend indeed reproach you, and your strife and enmity will not depart, but shall be to you equal to death. Grace and friendship deliver; guard them for yourself, lest you become subject to reproach, but keep your ways peaceably.

**25:11** A golden apple in a necklace of sardius, so is it to speak a word.

**25:12** A precious sardius is bound upon a golden earring, and a wise word into an attentive ear.

**25:13** As a going out in harvest during the heat brings benefit, so does a faithful messenger to those who sent him; for he benefits the souls of those who employ him.

**25:14** As winds and clouds and rains are most conspicuous, so is he who boasts of a false gift.

**25:15** In longsuffering there is prosperity for kings, and a soft tongue crushes bones.

**25:16** Having found honey, eat what is sufficient, lest being filled you vomit it up.

**25:17** Bring your foot seldom to your own friend, lest having had his fill of you he hate you.

**25:18** A club and a sword and a pointed arrow, so also is a man who bears false witness against his friend.

**25:19** The way of an evil man and the foot of a lawless man shall perish in an evil day.

**25:20** As vinegar is harmful to a wound, so an affliction falling upon the body grieves the heart. As a moth in a garment and a worm to wood, so the grief of a man harms the heart.

**25:21** If your enemy hungers, feed him; if he thirsts, give him to drink;

**25:22** for in doing this you shall heap coals of fire upon his head, and the Lord shall repay you good things.

**25:23** The north wind rouses clouds, and a shameless countenance provokes a tongue.

**25:24** It is better to dwell upon a corner of a housetop than with a reviling woman in a shared house.

**25:25** As cold water is pleasant to a thirsty soul, so is a good message from a land far off.

**25:26** As if someone should stop up a spring and spoil an outlet of water, so it is unseemly for a righteous man to have fallen before an ungodly man.

**25:27** To eat much honey is not good, but one ought to honor glorious words.

**25:28** As a city with its walls cast down and unwalled, so is a man who does anything without counsel.

## Translator Notes

- **25:1** — The Greek reads "unarranged instructions" (paideiai adiakritoi) and "the friends" (hoi philoi, a Hellenistic court title for royal counselors), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "proverbs" and "men" of Hezekiah king of Judah.
- **25:10** — The closing sentence ("Grace and friendship deliver...") is a Septuagint expansion not found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **25:13** — The Greek text reads simply "a going out" (exodos, perhaps a respite from field labor or an outlet of water); some Greek manuscripts add "of snow," closer to the Hebrew "cold of snow."
- **25:19** — The Greek text reads "way" (hodos), differing by a single letter from "tooth" (odous, corresponding to the Hebrew "broken tooth").
