## Text

**2:1** Son, if you receive the utterance of my commandment and hide it within yourself,

**2:2** your ear shall heed wisdom, and you shall incline your heart to understanding, and you shall apply it to the admonition of your son.

**2:3** For if you call upon wisdom and give your voice to understanding,

**2:4** and if you seek her as silver and search thoroughly for her as for treasures,

**2:5** then you shall understand the fear of the Lord, and find the knowledge of God.

**2:6** For the Lord gives wisdom, and from His countenance come knowledge and understanding;

**2:7** and He stores up salvation for those who walk uprightly; He shall shield their journey

**2:8** so as to guard the ways of righteous ordinances, and He shall preserve the way of those who revere Him.

**2:9** Then you shall understand righteousness and judgment, and you shall direct aright all good paths.

**2:10** For if wisdom enters into your mind, and perception seems good to your soul,

**2:11** good counsel shall guard you, and holy discretion shall keep you,

**2:12** that it may deliver you from an evil way, and from a man who speaks nothing trustworthy.

**2:13** O those who forsake straight ways to walk in ways of darkness,

**2:14** who delight in evil things and rejoice in wicked perversity,

**2:15** whose paths are crooked and whose courses are winding,

**2:16** so as to make you far from a straight way and estranged from righteous judgment. Son, do not let evil counsel overtake you,

**2:17** which forsakes the teaching of youth and has forgotten the divine covenant.

**2:18** For it has set its house beside death, and its tracks beside Hades with the earthborn;

**2:19** none of those who walk in it shall return, nor shall they attain straight paths; for they are not overtaken by the years of life.

**2:20** For if they had walked in good paths, they would have found the paths of righteousness smooth.

**2:21** For the upright shall dwell in the land, and the holy shall remain in it;

**2:22** the ways of the ungodly shall perish from the land, and the lawless shall be driven out from it.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Septuagint includes an additional third clause directing the hearer to apply the heart's understanding "to the admonition of your son."
- **2:16** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text warns against a "foreign woman," the Septuagint renders the figure as "evil counsel" (kake boule), sustaining this metaphor through verse 19.
- **2:18** — "The earthborn" (ton gegenon) is the Septuagint's rendering of the Hebrew Rephaim, the departed shades of the underworld.
- **2:20** — Unlike the purpose clause in the Hebrew Masoretic Text, the Septuagint employs a contrary-to-fact conditional looking back at those who perished in verse 19.
