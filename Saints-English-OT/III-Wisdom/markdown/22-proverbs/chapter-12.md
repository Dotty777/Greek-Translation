## Text

**12:1** He who loves discipline loves discernment, but he who hates reproofs is a fool.

**12:2** Better is he who has found favor with the Lord, but a lawless man shall be passed over in silence.

**12:3** A man shall not prosper by what is lawless, but the roots of the righteous shall not be uprooted.

**12:4** A valiant woman is a crown to her husband; but like a worm in wood, so a woman who works evil destroys a husband.

**12:5** The thoughts of the righteous are judgments, but the ungodly direct deceits.

**12:6** The words of the ungodly are deceitful, but the mouth of the upright shall deliver them.

**12:7** Wherever he turns, an ungodly man vanishes away, but the houses of the righteous endure.

**12:8** The mouth of a discerning man is praised by a man, but the dull-hearted is mocked.

**12:9** Better is a man in dishonor who serves himself than one who puts honor upon himself and lacks bread.

**12:10** A righteous man has compassion on the lives of his livestock, but the tender affections of the ungodly are merciless.

**12:11** He who tills his own land shall be filled with bread, but those who pursue vain things are lacking in understanding. He who finds pleasure in lingering over wine shall leave dishonor in his own strongholds.

**12:12** The desires of the ungodly are evil, but the roots of the godly are in strongholds.

**12:13** Through the sin of his lips a sinner falls into snares, but a righteous man escapes from them. He whose look is gentle shall receive mercy, but he who confronts others in the gates shall afflict souls.

**12:14** From the fruits of the mouth a man's soul shall be filled with good things, and the recompense of his lips shall be given to him.

**12:15** The ways of fools are right before them, but a wise man heeds counsel.

**12:16** A fool declares his wrath the very same day, but a prudent man conceals his own dishonor.

**12:17** A righteous man declares manifest faithfulness, but the witness of the unrighteous is deceitful.

**12:18** There are those whose words wound like swords, but the tongues of the wise bring healing.

**12:19** Truthful lips establish testimony, but a hasty witness has an unrighteous tongue.

**12:20** Deceit is in the heart of him who devises evil, but those who desire peace shall rejoice.

**12:21** Nothing unrighteous shall please the righteous man, but the ungodly shall be filled with evils.

**12:22** False lips are an abomination to the Lord, but he who performs faithful deeds is acceptable with Him.

**12:23** An understanding man is a throne of discernment, but the heart of fools shall meet with curses.

**12:24** The hand of the chosen shall rule with ease, but the deceitful shall be for plunder.

**12:25** A fearful word troubles the heart of a righteous man, but a good message makes him glad.

**12:26** A discerning righteous man shall be a friend to himself, but evils shall pursue those who sin, and the way of the ungodly shall lead them astray.

**12:27** A deceitful man shall not catch prey, but a pure man is a precious possession.

**12:28** In the ways of righteousness is life, but the ways of those who hold grudges lead to death.

## Translator Notes

- **12:9** — The Greek describes a humble man working for himself ("serving himself"), contrasting with the Hebrew reading of a man who has a servant.
- **12:11** — The Greek text includes an additional two-line proverb at the end of this verse warning against lingering over wine.
- **12:13** — The Septuagint adds a second couplet here; "whose look is gentle" literally renders the Greek idiom "who looks smooth things."
- **12:18** — The Greek text reads the nominative plural "swords" in vivid apposition to those who wound by speaking, rendered "like swords" for clarity.
