## Text

**24:1** Son, do not envy evil men, nor desire to be with them;

**24:2** for their heart meditates on falsehoods, and their lips speak of troubles.

**24:3** With wisdom a house is built, and with understanding it is established;

**24:4** with discernment storehouses are filled with all precious and fine wealth.

**24:5** A wise man is better than a strong man, and a man possessing prudence than a great tilled field.

**24:6** With guidance war is waged, and help is with a heart of counsel.

**24:7** Wisdom and good understanding are in the gates of the wise; the wise do not turn aside from the mouth of the Lord,

**24:8** but they deliberate in councils. Death meets the undisciplined,

**24:9** and a fool dies in sins, and uncleanness shall be stained into a pestilent man

**24:10** in an evil day and in a day of affliction, until he fails.

**24:11** Deliver those who are being led to death, and ransom those who are being slain; do not spare.

**24:12** But if you say, "I do not know this man," know that the Lord knows the hearts of all; and He who formed breath for all, He Himself knows all things, who renders to each according to his works.

**24:13** Eat honey, son, for the honeycomb is good, that your throat may be sweetened.

**24:14** Thus you shall perceive wisdom for your soul; for if you find it, your end shall be good, and hope shall not forsake you.

**24:15** Do not bring an ungodly man to the pasture of the righteous, nor be deceived by the filling of the belly.

**24:16** For seven times a righteous man shall fall and rise again, but the ungodly shall grow weak in evils.

**24:17** If your enemy falls, do not rejoice over him, and at his stumbling do not be lifted up;

**24:18** for the Lord will see it and it will not please Him, and He will turn away His wrath from him.

**24:19** Do not rejoice over evildoers, nor envy sinners;

**24:20** for there shall certainly be no offspring for a wicked man, and the lamp of the ungodly shall be extinguished.

**24:21** Fear God, son, and the king, and do not disobey either of them;

**24:22** for suddenly they shall punish the ungodly, and who shall know the punishments of them both? A son who keeps the word shall be free from destruction, and receiving it, he has accepted it. Let no falsehood from the tongue be spoken to a king, and let no falsehood go forth from his tongue. The tongue of a king is a sword, and not of flesh; and whoever is delivered over shall be crushed. For if his wrath is sharpened, he consumes men along with their sinews, and devours the bones of men, and burns them up like a flame, so that they are inedible for the young of eagles.

**24:23** Now these things I speak to you who are wise, to recognize: To show partiality in judgment is not good.

**24:24** He who says of the ungodly, "He is righteous," shall be accursed by peoples and hated among the nations;

**24:25** but those who reprove shall appear better, and upon them blessing shall come.

**24:26** And they shall kiss lips that answer good words.

**24:27** Prepare your works for going forth, and make yourself ready for the field, and follow after me, and you shall rebuild your house.

**24:28** Do not be a false witness against your fellow citizen, nor exaggerate with your lips.

**24:29** Do not say, "Just as he treated me, so I will treat him, and I will repay him for the wrongs he did to me."

**24:30** Like a tilled field is a foolish man, and like a vineyard is a man lacking sense;

**24:31** if you leave him alone, he shall lie waste and become wholly overgrown with weeds, and he becomes abandoned, and his stone walls are torn down.

**24:32** Afterward I repented; I looked attentively to choose instruction.

**24:33** A little I doze, and a little I sleep, and a little I clasp my chest with my hands;

**24:34** but if you do this, your poverty shall come marching ahead, and your want like a good runner.

## Translator Notes

- **24:5** — The Septuagint contrasts a prudent man with "a great tilled field," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of a man of knowledge increasing strength.
- **24:9** — Verses 9–10 in the Septuagint diverge substantially from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, linking the fool's death in sins with uncleanness staining the pestilent man until he fails in a day of affliction.
- **24:22** — The Septuagint includes an extended addition here warning against speaking falsehood to a king and depicting the king's tongue as a sword not of flesh and his wrath as an all-consuming flame.
- **24:27** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "afterward," the Septuagint reads "follow after me," linking the rebuilding of one's house to following wisdom's instruction.
- **24:34** — The Septuagint compares oncoming want to "a good runner," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's image of an armed man.
