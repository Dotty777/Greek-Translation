## Text

**11:1** Deceitful scales are an abomination before the Lord, but a just weight is acceptable to Him.

**11:2** Wherever arrogance enters, there also is dishonor; but the mouth of the humble meditates on wisdom.

**11:3** When a righteous man dies, he leaves regret behind, but the destruction of the ungodly is swift and rejoiced over.

**11:5** Righteousness cuts straight blameless ways, but ungodliness falls into unrighteousness.

**11:6** The righteousness of upright men delivers them, but the lawless are caught in their own destruction.

**11:7** When a righteous man dies, hope does not perish, but the boast of the ungodly perishes.

**11:8** A righteous man escapes from the snare, and in his place the ungodly man is delivered up.

**11:9** In the mouth of the ungodly is a snare for citizens, but the discernment of the righteous is prosperous.

**11:10** Through the good things of the righteous a city prospers,

**11:11** but by the mouths of the ungodly it is overthrown.

**11:12** He who is lacking in understanding mocks citizens, but a prudent man keeps silence.

**11:13** A double-tongued man reveals counsels in the assembly, but one faithful in spirit conceals matters.

**11:14** Those who have no guidance fall like leaves, but safety is in much counsel.

**11:15** A wicked man does harm whenever he associates with a righteous man, and he hates the sound of security.

**11:16** A gracious woman raises up glory for her husband, but a woman who hates righteous things is a throne of dishonor. The slothful become destitute of wealth, while the valiant are supported by wealth.

**11:17** A merciful man does good to his own soul, but the merciless man destroys his own body.

**11:18** An ungodly man performs unrighteous works, but the seed of the righteous is a reward of truth.

**11:19** A righteous son is born for life, but the pursuit of an ungodly man leads to death.

**11:20** Perverse ways are an abomination to the Lord, but all who are blameless in their ways are acceptable to Him.

**11:21** He who joins hands unjustly shall not go unpunished, but he who sows righteousness shall receive a faithful reward.

**11:22** Like an earring in a swine's snout, so is beauty to an ill-minded woman.

**11:23** Every desire of the righteous is good, but the hope of the ungodly shall perish.

**11:24** There are those who sow their own possessions and gain more; and there are also those who gather in and are diminished.

**11:25** Every sincere soul is blessed, but a wrathful man is unseemly.

**11:26** He who withholds grain, may he leave it behind to the nations; but blessing is upon the head of him who shares it.

**11:27** He who devises good things seeks good favor, but as for him who earnestly seeks evil, it shall overtake him.

**11:28** He who trusts in wealth, he shall fall, but he who upholds the righteous, he shall flourish.

**11:29** He who does not deal considerately with his own household shall inherit wind, and a fool shall serve a prudent man.

**11:30** From the fruit of righteousness grows a tree of life, but the souls of the lawless are taken away before their time.

**11:31** If the righteous man is saved with difficulty, where shall the ungodly and the sinner appear?

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — The Greek text speaks of regret left behind when a righteous man dies and the swift, welcomed ruin of the ungodly; verse 4 is absent from the Septuagint.
- **11:10** — In the Septuagint, verses 10 and 11 form a single two-line proverb, omitting the middle two clauses found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **11:26** — The Greek optative expresses a wish that the grain-hoarder may leave his store behind to the nations, differing from the Hebrew reading where the people curse him.
- **11:31** — This Septuagint rendering is quoted nearly verbatim in the New Testament at 1 Peter 4:18.
