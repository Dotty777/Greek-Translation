## Text

**10:1** A wise son gladdens a father, but a foolish son is a grief to his mother.

**10:2** Treasures shall not profit the lawless, but righteousness shall deliver from death.

**10:3** The Lord will not starve a righteous soul, but He will overturn the life of the ungodly.

**10:4** Poverty humbles a man, but the hands of the valiant make rich. A disciplined son shall be wise, and he shall use the fool as a servant.

**10:5** A prudent son is saved from the scorching heat, but a lawless son becomes wind-blighted in the harvest.

**10:6** The blessing of the Lord is upon the head of a righteous man, but untimely mourning shall cover the mouth of the ungodly.

**10:7** The memory of the righteous is with praises, but the name of the ungodly man is extinguished.

**10:8** The wise in heart shall receive commandments, but he who is unrestrained with his lips, going crookedly, shall be tripped up.

**10:9** He who walks with sincerity walks in confidence, but he who perverts his ways shall be known.

**10:10** He who winks with his eyes deceitfully gathers griefs for men, but he who reproves with boldness makes peace.

**10:11** A fountain of life is in the hand of a righteous man, but destruction shall cover the mouth of the ungodly man.

**10:12** Hatred stirs up strife, but friendship covers all who are not contentious;

**10:13** with a rod it strikes a senseless man.

**10:14** The wise shall conceal knowledge, but the mouth of the rash draws near to ruin.

**10:15** The possession of the rich is a fortified city, but the ruin of the ungodly is poverty.

**10:16** The works of the righteous produce life, but the fruits of the ungodly produce sins.

**10:17** Instruction keeps the righteous ways of life, but instruction left unreproved goes astray.

**10:18** Righteous lips cover enmity, but those who bring forth revilings are most foolish.

**10:19** From much speaking you shall not escape sin, but by sparing your lips you shall be prudent.

**10:20** The tongue of a righteous man is silver refined by fire, but the heart of the ungodly man shall fail.

**10:21** The lips of the righteous know lofty things, but fools come to their end in want.

**10:22** The blessing of the Lord is upon the head of a righteous man; it makes rich, and grief shall by no means be added to it in the heart.

**10:23** With laughter a fool practices evil, but wisdom brings forth prudence for a man.

**10:24** The ungodly man is swept about in destruction, but the desire of a righteous man is acceptable.

**10:25** When a tempest passes by, the ungodly man vanishes away, but the righteous man turns aside and is saved forever.

**10:26** As an unripe grape is harmful to the teeth and smoke to the eyes, so is lawlessness to those who practice it.

**10:27** The fear of the Lord adds days, but the years of the ungodly shall be diminished.

**10:28** Gladness abides long with the righteous, but the hope of the ungodly shall perish.

**10:29** The fear of the Lord is a stronghold for the devout, but ruin for those who work evil.

**10:30** A righteous man shall never give way, but the ungodly shall not dwell in the land.

**10:31** The mouth of a righteous man distills wisdom, but the tongue of an unrighteous man shall utterly perish.

**10:32** The lips of righteous men distill graces, but the mouth of the ungodly turns aside.

## Translator Notes

- **10:4** — The Septuagint begins with "Poverty humbles a man" (rather than the Hebrew "a slack hand") and includes an additional couplet contrasting a disciplined son with a fool.
- **10:10** — The second clause ("he who reproves with boldness makes peace") is distinctive to the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text repeats the closing line of verse 8.
- **10:13** — Following Codex Vaticanus and the main Septuagint critical text, this verse omits the opening clause ("He who brings forth wisdom from his lips...") found in other witnesses and the Hebrew, leaving the verb "strikes" syntactically linked to verse 12.
- **10:26** — The Septuagint reads "unripe grape" (ὄμφαξ) and "lawlessness" (παρανομία) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "vinegar" and "the sluggard."
