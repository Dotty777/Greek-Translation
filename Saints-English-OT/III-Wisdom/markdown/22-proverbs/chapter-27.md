## Text

**27:1** Do not boast about the things of tomorrow, for you do not know what the coming day will bring forth.

**27:2** Let a neighbor commend you, and not your own mouth; a stranger, and not your own lips.

**27:3** A stone is heavy, and sand is hard to bear, but the wrath of a fool is heavier than both.

**27:4** Merciless is fury, and sharp is wrath, yet jealousy endures no one.

**27:5** Open reproofs are better than hidden friendship.

**27:6** More trustworthy are the wounds of a friend than the willing kisses of an enemy.

**27:7** A soul that is full scoffs at honeycombs, but to a soul in want even bitter things appear sweet.

**27:8** Just as when a bird flies down from its own nest, so a man is brought into bondage when he is estranged from his own places.

**27:9** By ointments and wines and incense the heart is gladdened, but the soul is shattered by calamities.

**27:10** Do not forsake your friend or your father's friend, and do not enter the house of your brother when you suffer misfortune; better is a friend nearby than a brother dwelling afar.

**27:11** Become wise, son, that your heart may rejoice, and turn away from yourself words of reproach.

**27:12** A shrewd man hides himself when evils come on, but fools press forward and shall pay the penalty.

**27:13** Take away his garment, for he has passed by; an insolent man is he who ruins what belongs to others.

**27:14** Whoever blesses a friend in the morning with a loud voice shall be thought to differ in nothing from one who curses.

**27:15** Drops drive a man out of the house on a wintry day; likewise also a reviling woman out of his own house.

**27:16** The north wind is a harsh wind, yet by name it is called favorable.

**27:17** Iron sharpens iron, and a man sharpens the countenance of a companion.

**27:18** He who plants a fig tree shall eat its fruits, and he who watches over his own master shall be honored.

**27:19** Just as faces are not like faces, so neither are the minds of men.

**27:20** Hades and destruction are not filled; in like manner also the eyes of men are insatiable. An abomination to the Lord is one who fixes his eye, and the undisciplined are unrestrained in tongue.

**27:21** A test for silver and gold is refining by fire, and a man is tested through the mouth of those who commend him. The heart of a lawless man seeks out evil things, but an upright heart seeks knowledge.

**27:22** Even if you scourge a fool in the midst of an assembly, putting him to dishonor, you shall by no means remove his folly.

**27:23** Thoroughly know the souls of your flock, and set your heart upon your herds;

**27:24** for a man does not have power and strength forever, nor does he pass them on from generation to generation.

**27:25** Care for the green growth in the field, and you shall cut the grass; and gather the mountain hay,

**27:26** that you may have sheep for clothing; honor the field, that you may have lambs.

**27:27** Son, from me you have strong sayings for your life and for the life of your servants.

## Translator Notes

- **27:4** — In the Greek text, 'jealousy' is the grammatical subject with 'no one' as object ('jealousy endures no one'), contrasting with the Hebrew rhetorical question about who can stand before jealousy.
- **27:11** — The Septuagint reads 'your heart' and 'turn away from yourself words of reproach,' whereas the Hebrew reads 'my heart' and answering one who reproaches the speaker.
- **27:16** — The Septuagint renders this verse with a Greek proverbial euphemism in which the harsh north wind is called 'favorable' (epidexios), differing markedly from the Hebrew text.
- **27:27** — The Septuagint concludes the chapter with 'strong sayings' for the son and his servants, whereas the Hebrew refers to sufficient goats' milk for sustenance.
