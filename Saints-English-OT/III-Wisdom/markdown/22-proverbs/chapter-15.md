## Text

**15:1** Wrath destroys even the prudent, but a submissive answer turns away fury, while a grievous word stirs up wrath.

**15:2** The tongue of the wise knows good things, but the mouth of fools will declare evil.

**15:3** In every place are the eyes of the Lord; they keep watch over both the evil and the good.

**15:4** The healing of the tongue is a tree of life, and he who guards it shall be filled with spirit.

**15:6** A fool mocks a father's instruction, but he who keeps commandments is more shrewd. In abounding righteousness there is great strength, but the ungodly shall perish completely uprooted from the earth; in the houses of the righteous there is great strength, but the fruits of the ungodly shall perish.

**15:7** The lips of the wise are bound with discernment, but the hearts of fools are not secure.

**15:8** The sacrifices of the ungodly are an abomination to the Lord, but the prayers of those who walk uprightly are acceptable before Him.

**15:9** The ways of an ungodly man are an abomination to the Lord, but He loves those who pursue righteousness.

**15:10** The discipline of the guileless is recognized by those who pass by, but those who hate reproofs die shamefully.

**15:11** Hades and destruction are manifest before the Lord; how could the hearts of men not be also?

**15:12** An undisciplined man will not love those who reprove him, nor will he keep company with the wise.

**15:13** When the heart rejoices, the countenance flourishes, but when it is in sorrows, it grows downcast.

**15:14** An upright heart seeks discernment, but the mouth of the undisciplined will know evil things.

**15:15** At all times the eyes of the wicked expect evil things, but the good shall abide in quietness continually.

**15:16** Better is a small portion with the fear of the Lord than great treasures without fear.

**15:17** Better is hospitality with herbs in friendship and grace than a serving of calves with enmity.

**15:18** A wrathful man prepares strife, but a longsuffering man calms even that which is impending; a longsuffering man will quench disputes, while the ungodly man stirs them up all the more.

**15:19** The ways of the idle are strewn with thorns, but those of the valiant are worn smooth.

**15:20** A wise son gladdens a father, but a foolish son mocks his mother.

**15:21** The paths of a senseless man are lacking in understanding, but a prudent man walks directing his course aright.

**15:22** Those who do not honor councils postpone their plans, but in the hearts of those who take counsel, counsel abides.

**15:23** The wicked man will by no means heed it, nor will he speak anything timely and good for the community.

**15:24** The thoughts of an understanding man are ways of life, that turning aside from Hades he may be saved.

**15:25** The Lord tears down the houses of the arrogant, yet He establishes the boundary of a widow.

**15:26** An unrighteous thought is an abomination to the Lord, but the sayings of the pure are honorable.

**15:27** He who takes bribes utterly destroys himself, but he who hates the receiving of bribes is saved. By acts of mercy and faithfulness sins are cleansed away, and by the fear of the Lord everyone turns aside from evil.

**15:28** The hearts of the righteous meditate on faithfulness, but the mouth of the ungodly answers evil things. The ways of righteous men are acceptable before the Lord, and through them even enemies become friends.

**15:29** God is far removed from the ungodly, but He heeds the prayers of the righteous. Better is a small gain with righteousness than abundant revenues with unrighteousness. And let the heart of a man ponder righteous things, that his steps may be directed aright by God.

**15:30** An eye that beholds good things gladdens the heart, and a good report gives fatness to the bones.

**15:32** He who rejects instruction hates himself, but he who observes reproofs loves his own soul.

**15:33** The fear of God is instruction and wisdom, and the beginning of glory shall answer to it.

## Translator Notes

- **15:1** — The Septuagint opens with an additional clause ("Wrath destroys even the prudent") and uses the Greek participle hypopiptousa ("submissive" or "yielding") for the gentle answer.
- **15:6** — In the Septuagint, this verse incorporates the counterpart to Masoretic 15:5 together with an expanded doublet contrasting the strength of the righteous with the ruin of the ungodly.
- **15:27** — The Septuagint places the saying on mercy, faithfulness, and the fear of the Lord here, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text locates it at 16:6 (and similarly places counterparts to 16:7-9 in 15:28-29).
- **15:33** — The Greek reads "the beginning of glory shall answer to it" (referring to wisdom and instruction), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "before honor is humility."
