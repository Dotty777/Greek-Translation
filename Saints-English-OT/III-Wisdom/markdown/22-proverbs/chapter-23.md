## Text

**23:1** If you sit down to dine at the table of rulers, consider attentively the things set before you,

**23:2** and put forth your hand, knowing that you must prepare such things; yet if you are overly greedy,

**23:3** do not crave his delicacies, for these belong to a deceitful life.

**23:4** Do not measure yourself, being poor, against a rich man, but by your own understanding hold back.

**23:5** If you fix your eye upon him, nowhere will he alight; for wings have been fashioned for him like those of an eagle, and he returns to the house of him who presides over him.

**23:6** Do not dine with an envious man, nor desire his foods;

**23:7** for just as if someone should swallow a hair, so he eats and drinks. And do not bring him in to yourself and eat your morsel with him;

**23:8** for he will vomit it up and ruin your fair words.

**23:9** Speak nothing in the ears of a fool, lest he sneer at your discerning words.

**23:10** Do not move everlasting boundaries, and do not enter into the possession of orphans;

**23:11** for He who redeems them is the Lord; He is mighty, and He will judge their cause against you.

**23:12** Give your heart to instruction, and prepare your ears for words of discernment.

**23:13** Do not hold back from disciplining a child, for if you strike him with a rod, he will certainly not die;

**23:14** for you shall strike him with a rod, yet you shall deliver his soul from death.

**23:15** Son, if your heart becomes wise, you will gladden my heart also,

**23:16** and your lips will linger in words with my lips, if they are upright.

**23:17** Do not let your heart envy sinners, but remain in the fear of the Lord all the day.

**23:18** For if you keep these things, you shall have offspring, and your hope shall not depart.

**23:19** Listen, son, and become wise, and make straight the thoughts of your heart.

**23:20** Do not be a drinker of wine, nor stretch yourself out at banquets and the buying of meats;

**23:21** for every drunkard and fornicator shall come to poverty, and every drowsy man shall be clothed in torn and ragged garments.

**23:22** Listen, son, to the father who fathered you, and do not show contempt because your mother has grown old.

**23:24** A righteous father nurtures well, and over a wise son his soul rejoices.

**23:25** Let your father and mother be glad over you, and let her who bore you rejoice.

**23:26** Give me your heart, son, and let your eyes observe my ways.

**23:27** For a strange house is a pierced jar, and a strange well is narrow.

**23:28** For such a one shall swiftly perish, and every lawless man shall be consumed.

**23:29** Who has woe? Who has tumult? Who has lawsuits? And who has vexation and idle chatter? Who has bruises in vain? Whose eyes are livid?

**23:30** Is it not those who linger long over wines? Is it not those who seek out where drinking-bouts are held? Do not become drunk with wines, but converse with righteous men, and converse as you walk about;

**23:31** for if you give your eyes to the bowls and the cups, afterward you shall walk about more naked than a pestle;

**23:32** and at the last he is stretched out like one struck by a serpent, and as from a horned viper the venom spreads through him.

**23:33** When your eyes look upon a strange woman, then your mouth shall speak perverse things,

**23:34** and you shall lie down as in the heart of the sea, and like a helmsman in a great surge.

**23:35** And you shall say, "They strike me, and I felt no pain, and they mocked me, yet I did not know it. When will dawn come, that I may go and seek those with whom I may gather?"

## Translator Notes

- **23:2** — The Septuagint instructs the guest to put forth his hand knowing he must reciprocate in kind, differing markedly from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's warning to put a knife to one's throat.
- **23:5** — The Greek text pictures the rich man (or his wealth) fitted with eagle's wings, alighting nowhere and returning to the house of his overseer or patron.
- **23:7** — The Septuagint likens the envious man's eating and drinking to swallowing a hair, reflecting a different reading of the underlying Hebrew consonants from the Masoretic Text.
- **23:24** — Verse 23 of the Hebrew Masoretic Text is absent from the Septuagint text of Proverbs.
- **23:31** — The vivid Greek idiom "more naked than a pestle" warns against gazing upon wine bowls and cups, differing from the Masoretic Text's description of wine sparkling in the cup.
