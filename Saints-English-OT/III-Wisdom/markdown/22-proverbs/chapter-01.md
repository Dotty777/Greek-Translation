## Text

**1:1** The proverbs of Solomon, the son of David, who reigned in Israel:

**1:2** to know wisdom and instruction, and to perceive words of prudence,

**1:3** and to receive subtleties of speech, to understand true righteousness, and to direct judgment aright;

**1:4** to give shrewdness to the innocent, and to a young lad both perception and discretion.

**1:5** For having heard these things, a wise man shall become wiser, and the discerning man shall acquire guidance,

**1:6** and he shall understand a parable and an obscure saying, both the utterances of the wise and riddles.

**1:7** The beginning of wisdom is the fear of God, and good understanding belongs to all who practice it; piety toward God is the beginning of perception, but the ungodly shall despise wisdom and instruction.

**1:8** Hear, son, the instruction of your father, and do not cast away the ordinances of your mother;

**1:9** for you shall receive a crown of graces upon your head, and a golden chain about your neck.

**1:10** Son, do not let ungodly men lead you astray, nor consent

**1:11** if they urge you, saying, "Come with us, share in bloodshed, and let us unjustly hide a righteous man in the earth;

**1:12** let us swallow him up alive like Hades, and remove his remembrance from the earth;

**1:13** let us seize his costly wealth, and fill our houses with spoils;

**1:14** cast your lot among us, let us all acquire a common purse, and let there be one pouch for us."

**1:15** Do not walk in the way with them, but turn your foot aside from their paths;

**1:17** for nets are not spread out unjustly for winged creatures.

**1:18** For those very ones who partake of murder store up evils for themselves, and the overthrow of lawless men is evil.

**1:19** These are the ways of all who carry out lawless deeds; for by ungodliness they take away their own life.

**1:20** Wisdom is praised in the thoroughfares, and in the broad squares she speaks with boldness;

**1:21** on the tops of the walls she is proclaimed, at the gates of rulers she takes her seat, and at the gates of the city she boldly says:

**1:22** "For as long as the innocent hold fast to righteousness, they shall not be put to shame; but the foolish, being lovers of insolence, have become ungodly and hated perception,

**1:23** and have become liable to reproofs. Behold, I will pour forth to you an utterance of my breath, and I will teach you my word.

**1:24** Because I called and you did not heed, and I extended words and you paid no attention,

**1:25** but you made my counsels void and disobeyed my reproofs,

**1:26** therefore I also will laugh at your destruction, and I will exult when ruin comes upon you,

**1:27** and when tumult arrives upon you suddenly, and overthrow is at hand like a tempest, and when affliction and siege come upon you, or when ruin comes upon you.

**1:28** For it shall come to pass that when you call upon me, I will not listen to you; wicked men shall seek me, and shall not find me.

**1:29** For they hated wisdom, and did not choose the word of the Lord;

**1:30** neither were they willing to attend to my counsels, but they derided my reproofs.

**1:31** Therefore they shall eat the fruits of their own way, and shall be filled with their own ungodliness;

**1:32** for because they wronged the simple, they shall be slain, and scrutiny shall destroy the ungodly.

**1:33** But he who listens to me shall dwell in hope, and shall rest without fear from every evil."

## Translator Notes

- **1:7** — The Septuagint expands this verse with two additional clauses echoing Psalm 110:10 (LXX) ("good understanding belongs to all who practice it" and "piety toward God is the beginning of perception").
- **1:17** — Verse 1:16 is absent from the Greek critical text of Proverbs, and here the Septuagint reads "not unjustly" rather than the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "in vain."
- **1:22** — Instead of the Hebrew question ("How long, O simple ones..."), the Septuagint presents a promise that the innocent shall not be put to shame for as long as they hold fast to righteousness.
- **1:29** — The Septuagint reads "the word of the Lord" where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "the fear of the Lord."
