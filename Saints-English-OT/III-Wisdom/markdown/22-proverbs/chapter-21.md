## Text

**21:1** As a rush of water, so is a king's heart in the hand of God; wherever He desires to turn, there He inclines it.

**21:2** Every man appears righteous to himself, but the Lord directs hearts.

**21:3** To do righteous deeds and to speak truth are more pleasing before God than the blood of sacrifices.

**21:4** A high-minded man is bold-hearted in insolence, and the lamp of the ungodly is sin.

**21:6** He who accumulates treasures by a lying tongue pursues vain things into the snares of death.

**21:7** Destruction shall lodge with the ungodly, for they do not desire to practice what is righteous.

**21:8** To the crooked God sends crooked ways, for His works are pure and upright.

**21:9** It is better to dwell on a corner of an open rooftop than in whitewashed rooms with unrighteousness and in a shared house.

**21:10** The soul of an ungodly man shall not receive mercy from any among men.

**21:11** When an undisciplined man is punished, the evil man becomes more shrewd; and a wise man who understands shall receive knowledge.

**21:12** A righteous man understands the hearts of the ungodly, and he despises the ungodly in their evils.

**21:13** He who stops his ears so as not to heed the weak, he also shall cry out, and there shall be no one who listens.

**21:14** A secret gift overturns anger, but he who withholds gifts arouses strong wrath.

**21:15** The gladness of the righteous practices justice, but a holy man is unclean among evildoers.

**21:16** A man who wanders from the way of righteousness shall rest in the assembly of giants.

**21:17** A needy man loves gladness, loving wine and oil for wealth.

**21:18** But a lawless man is the ransom for a righteous man.

**21:19** It is better to dwell in the wilderness than with a sharp-tongued, quarrelsome, and wrathful woman.

**21:20** A desirable treasure shall rest upon the mouth of a wise man, but foolish men shall swallow it up.

**21:21** The way of righteousness and mercy shall find life and glory.

**21:22** A wise man scaled fortified cities and tore down the stronghold in which the ungodly had trusted.

**21:23** He who guards his mouth and his tongue preserves his soul from affliction.

**21:24** A bold, self-willed, and boastful man is called a pestilent fellow, and he who bears a grudge is a lawless man.

**21:25** Desires slay a sluggard, for his hands are not willing to do anything.

**21:26** An ungodly man craves evil desires all the day long, but the righteous man shows mercy and compassion unsparingly.

**21:27** The sacrifices of the ungodly are an abomination to the Lord, for indeed they offer them lawlessly.

**21:28** A false witness shall perish, but an obedient man shall speak with caution.

**21:29** An ungodly man stands shamelessly in countenance, but the upright man himself understands his ways.

**21:30** There is no wisdom, there is no courage, there is no counsel for the ungodly man.

**21:31** A horse is prepared for the day of battle, yet help comes from the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **21:9** — The Septuagint reads "whitewashed rooms with unrighteousness" rather than the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "contentious woman," depicting luxurious yet corrupt shared quarters.
- **21:16** — "Assembly of giants" translates the Greek phrase used across the Septuagint for the Hebrew Rephaim, the shadowy departed of the underworld.
- **21:18** — "Ransom" translates Greek perikatharma, denoting a purification offering or scapegoat given in place of another.
- **21:30** — The Septuagint reads "for the ungodly man," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "against the Lord."
