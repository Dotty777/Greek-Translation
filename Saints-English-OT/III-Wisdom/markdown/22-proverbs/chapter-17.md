# Proverbs 17 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**17:1** Better is a morsel with delight in peace than a house of many good things and unjust sacrifices with strife.

**17:2** A discerning servant shall rule over foolish masters, and among brethren he shall divide portions.

**17:3** Just as silver and gold are tested in a furnace, so are chosen hearts before the Lord.

**17:4** An evil man listens to the tongue of transgressors, but a righteous man does not give heed to lying lips.

**17:5** He who mocks a poor man provokes Him who made him, and he who rejoices over one who is perishing shall not go unpunished; but he who shows compassion shall obtain mercy.

**17:6** Children's children are the crown of old men, and the boast of children is their fathers. To the faithful man belongs the whole world of wealth, but to the unfaithful man not even an obol.

**17:7** Faithful lips will not befit a fool, nor lying lips a righteous man.

**17:8** Instruction is a reward of graces to those who use it, and wherever one turns, he shall prosper.

**17:9** He who conceals offenses seeks friendship, but he who hates to conceal them separates friends and kindred.

**17:10** A threat crushes the heart of a prudent man, but a fool, though scourged, does not perceive it.

**17:11** Every evil man stirs up contentions, and the Lord shall send out a merciless messenger against him.

**17:12** Anxiety shall fall upon a discerning man, but fools shall devise evil things.

**17:13** He who repays evil for good—evil shall not be removed from his house.

**17:14** The beginning of righteousness gives authority to words, but sedition and strife go before want.

**17:15** He who judges the unrighteous man to be righteous, and the righteous man to be unrighteous, is unclean and abominable before God.

**17:16** For what purpose did wealth belong to a fool? For a senseless man will not be able to acquire wisdom. He who makes his own house lofty seeks ruin, and he who turns perversely from learning shall fall into evils.

**17:17** Let a friend be yours at every season, and let brethren be helpful in times of distress, for on account of this they are born.

**17:18** A foolish man claps his hands and rejoices in himself, as also does he who pledges surety for his own friends.

**17:19** A lover of sin rejoices in quarrels,

**17:20** and the hard-hearted man does not meet with good things. A man changeable in tongue shall fall into evils,

**17:21** and the heart of a fool is sorrow to him who possesses it. A father does not rejoice over an undisciplined son, but a prudent son gladdens his mother.

**17:22** A glad heart brings good health, but the bones of a sorrowful man dry up.

**17:23** The ways of him who unjustly takes gifts into his bosom do not prosper, and an ungodly man turns aside the paths of righteousness.

**17:24** The countenance of a wise man is discerning, but the eyes of the fool are upon the ends of the earth.

**17:25** A foolish son is anger to his father, and sorrow to her who bore him.

**17:26** To penalize a righteous man is not good, nor is it holy to plot against righteous rulers.

**17:27** He who refrains from uttering a harsh word is perceptive, and a longsuffering man is prudent.

**17:28** To a senseless man who inquires after wisdom, wisdom shall be reckoned, and he who makes himself mute shall seem to be prudent.

## Translator Notes

- **17:6** — The Greek adds a second proverb not present in the Masoretic Text; an obol was a small ancient Greek coin of minimal value.
- **17:12** — The Greek differs markedly from the Masoretic Text's image of a bear robbed of her cubs, contrasting a discerning man's anxiety with the evil schemes of fools.
- **17:16** — The second half of the Greek verse corresponds to material placed later in the Masoretic Text (17:19b-20).
- **17:19** — In the Greek text, verses 19-21 are divided across clause boundaries, and verse 21 adds a closing contrast regarding a prudent son gladdening his mother.
