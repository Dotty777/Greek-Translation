## Text

**6:1** Son, if you become surety for your friend, you shall deliver your hand to an enemy.

**6:2** For a man's own lips are a strong snare to him, and he is caught by the lips of his own mouth.

**6:3** Do, son, what I command you, and deliver yourself; for you have come into the hands of evil men on account of your friend. Do not grow faint, and also stir up your friend for whom you became surety.

**6:4** Do not give sleep to your eyes, nor let your eyelids slumber,

**6:5** that you may be delivered like a gazelle from snares, and like a bird from a trap.

**6:6** Go to the ant, O sluggard, and emulate him when you see his ways, and become wiser than he;

**6:7** for though he has no farmland, nor has anyone to compel him, nor is under a master,

**6:8** he prepares his food in the summer, and makes an abundant store in the harvest. Or go to the bee and learn what a worker she is, and how noble a work she performs; whose labors kings and common people partake of for health, and she is desired by all and held in honor; though she is weak in strength, by honoring wisdom she has been exalted.

**6:9** How long, sluggard, do you lie down? And when will you rise from sleep?

**6:10** A little you sleep, and a little you sit, a short while you slumber, and a little you clasp your chest with your hands;

**6:11** then poverty comes upon you like an evil wayfarer, and want like a good runner. Yet if you are not slothful, your harvest shall come like a spring, and want shall flee away like a poor runner.

**6:12** A foolish and lawless man walks in ways that are not good;

**6:13** that same man winks with his eye, signals with his foot, and teaches with gestures of his fingers;

**6:14** with a perverse heart he devises evil things at all times; such a man stirs up tumults in a city.

**6:15** Therefore his destruction comes suddenly, a cutting off and an incurable shattering;

**6:16** because he rejoices in all things that God hates, and he is shattered because of uncleanness of soul:

**6:17** the eye of an insolent man, an unrighteous tongue, hands that shed the blood of a righteous man,

**6:18** and a heart devising evil thoughts, and feet hastening to do evil.

**6:19** An unrighteous witness kindles falsehoods, and sends disputes among brethren.

**6:20** Son, keep the laws of your father, and do not reject the ordinances of your mother;

**6:21** bind them upon your soul continually, and fasten them like a collar around your neck.

**6:22** Whenever you walk, bring it along, and let it be with you; and when you sleep, let it guard you, that when you awake it may converse with you.

**6:23** For the commandment of the law is a lamp, and the way of life and reproof and instruction are a light,

**6:24** to preserve you from a married woman, and from the slander of a strange tongue.

**6:25** Do not let desire for beauty overcome you, nor be ensnared by your eyes, nor be carried away by her eyelids.

**6:26** For the price of a harlot is as much as one loaf of bread, yet a woman hunts the precious souls of men.

**6:27** Will anyone bind fire in his bosom, and not burn up his garments?

**6:28** Or will anyone walk upon coals of fire, and not burn his feet?

**6:29** So is he who goes in to a married woman; he shall not be held guiltless, nor shall anyone who touches her.

**6:30** It is no wonder if someone is caught stealing, for he steals to fill his soul when he is hungry;

**6:31** yet if he is caught, he shall repay sevenfold, and by giving all his possessions he shall deliver himself;

**6:32** but the adulterer, through lack of understanding, procures destruction for his own soul;

**6:33** he endures both pains and dishonors, and his reproach shall not be blotted out forever.

**6:34** For the wrath of her husband is full of jealousy; he shall not spare in the day of judgment.

**6:35** He will not exchange his enmity for any ransom, nor will he be reconciled by many gifts.

## Translator Notes

- **6:6** — The Greek form isthi here represents the imperative ithi ('go'), a standard Septuagint spelling; in verse 7 the ant is further described as having 'no farmland' rather than 'no chief.'
- **6:8** — The Septuagint includes an extended passage praising the bee's noble labor, health-giving honey, and exaltation through wisdom, which has no counterpart in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **6:11** — After comparing poverty to an evil wayfarer and want to a good runner, the Septuagint adds a contrasting promise of an abundant harvest for the diligent.
- **6:16** — Instead of the Hebrew numerical saying ('six things... yes, seven'), the Septuagint links the following list directly to the lawless man of verses 12–15, who rejoices in all that God hates.
