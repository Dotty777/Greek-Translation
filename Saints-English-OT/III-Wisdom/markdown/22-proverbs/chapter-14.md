# Proverbs 14 — Saints Traditional English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**14:1** Wise women build houses, but the foolish woman demolishes hers with her hands.

**14:2** He who walks uprightly fears the Lord, but he who is crooked in his ways shall be dishonored.

**14:3** Out of the mouth of fools comes a rod of insolence, but the lips of the wise guard them.

**14:4** Where there are no oxen, the mangers are clean; but where there are abundant crops, the strength of an ox is evident.

**14:5** A faithful witness does not lie, but an unjust witness kindles falsehoods.

**14:6** You shall seek wisdom among evil men and shall not find it, but discernment is readily found among the prudent.

**14:7** All things are contrary to a foolish man, but wise lips are weapons of discernment.

**14:8** The wisdom of the prudent shall discern their ways, but the senselessness of fools is in error.

**14:9** The houses of the lawless shall owe purification, but the houses of the righteous are acceptable.

**14:10** A man's heart is sensitive, and his soul is sorrowful; yet when he rejoices, he does not mingle with insolence.

**14:11** The houses of the ungodly shall be destroyed, but the tents of the upright shall stand.

**14:12** There is a way that seems right among men, but its end comes to the depths of Hades.

**14:13** In times of gladness sorrow is not mingled, yet the end of joy comes to mourning.

**14:14** A presumptuous-hearted man shall be filled with his own ways, and a good man from his own thoughts.

**14:15** The guileless man believes every word, but the prudent man comes to repentance.

**14:16** A wise man fears and turns aside from evil, but the fool, trusting in himself, mingles with the lawless.

**14:17** A quick-tempered man acts without counsel, but a prudent man endures many things.

**14:18** Fools shall share wickedness as their portion, but the prudent shall lay hold of discernment.

**14:19** Evil men shall slip before the good, and the ungodly shall wait upon the doors of the righteous.

**14:20** Friends shall hate poor friends, but the friends of the rich are many.

**14:21** He who dishonors the needy sins, but he who shows mercy to the poor is blessed.

**14:22** Those who go astray craft evil, but the good craft mercy and truth; craftsmen of evil do not know mercy and faithfulness, but deeds of mercy and faithfulness dwell with good craftsmen.

**14:23** In everyone who takes diligent care there is abundance, but the pleasure-loving and unfeeling man shall be in want.

**14:24** The crown of the wise is a prudent man, but the pastime of fools is evil.

**14:25** A faithful witness shall deliver a soul from evils, but a deceitful man kindles falsehoods.

**14:26** In the fear of the Lord is a hope of strength, and for His children He leaves a support.

**14:27** The ordinance of the Lord is a fountain of life, and it causes one to turn aside from the snare of death.

**14:28** In a numerous nation is the glory of a king, but in the failing of a people is the ruin of a ruler.

**14:29** A longsuffering man is great in prudence, but the impatient man is a mighty fool.

**14:30** A gentle-spirited man is a physician of the heart, but a sensitive heart is a moth to the bones.

**14:31** He who oppresses the needy provokes the One who made him, but he who honors Him shows mercy to the poor.

**14:32** In his wickedness the ungodly man shall be thrust away, but he who trusts in his own holiness is righteous.

**14:33** In the good heart of a man is wisdom, but in the heart of fools it is not discerned.

**14:34** Righteousness exalts a nation, but sins diminish tribes.

**14:35** An understanding servant is acceptable to a king, and by his own resourcefulness he takes away dishonor.

## Translator Notes

- **14:10** — The Greek portrays a sensitive heart whose soul feels sorrow yet avoids insolence in gladness, diverging from the Masoretic Text's statement about private bitterness and joy.
- **14:15** — The Greek word metanoia ("repentance") carries here the sense of thoughtful reconsideration or a wise change of mind.
- **14:26** — The Greek pronoun "His" and verb "leaves" naturally look back to "the Lord," though some take them as referring to the person who fears the Lord.
- **14:30** — The Greek likens a sensitive or easily agitated heart to a "moth" (ses) to the bones, whereas the Masoretic Text reads "envy is rottenness to the bones."
