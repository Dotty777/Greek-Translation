## Text

**42:1** Do not be ashamed of these things, and do not show partiality so as to sin:

**42:2** of the law of the Most High and the covenant, and of judgment to justify the ungodly,

**42:3** of settling accounts with a partner and fellow travelers, of sharing an inheritance with companions,

**42:4** of accuracy of scales and weights, of acquiring much and little,

**42:5** of impartiality in selling and with merchants, and of much discipline of children, and of making the side of a wicked servant bleed.

**42:6** Over a wicked wife a seal is good, and where there are many hands, lock things up.

**42:7** Whatever you deliver, let it be by number and weight, and let giving and receiving with everyone be in writing;

**42:8** of the correction of the senseless and the foolish, and of one in extreme old age contending with the young; then you shall be truly disciplined and approved before every living person.

**42:9** A daughter is a secret cause of wakefulness to a father, and anxiety for her drives away sleep: in her youth, lest she pass her prime, and when married, lest she be hated;

**42:10** in her virginity, lest she be defiled and become pregnant in her father's house; when with a husband, lest she transgress, and when married, lest she be barren.

**42:11** Keep a strict watch over a headstrong daughter, lest she make you a laughingstock to enemies, a byword in the city and a notoriety among the people, and put you to shame before a great multitude.

**42:12** Do not gaze upon any person because of beauty, and do not sit in the midst of women;

**42:13** for from garments comes forth a moth, and from a woman comes a woman's wickedness.

**42:14** Better is the wickedness of a man than a woman who does good, and a woman who brings shame leading to reproach.

**42:15** Now I will call to remembrance the works of the Lord, and what I have seen I will declare: by the words of the Lord are His works.

**42:16** The sun giving light looks down upon everything, and His work is full of His glory.

**42:17** The Lord has not enabled the holy ones to declare all His marvelous works, which the Lord Almighty has established, so that the universe might stand firm in His glory.

**42:18** He has searched out the abyss and the heart, and upon their subtle devices I have pondered; for the Lord knows all knowledge, and He looks upon the sign of the age,

**42:19** declaring the things that are past and the things that are to come, and uncovering the traces of hidden things.

**42:20** No thought has escaped Him, and not a single word has been hidden from Him.

**42:21** He has set in order the mighty works of His wisdom; and while He exists from before the age and forever, He has neither been increased nor diminished, and He has had no need of any counselor.

**42:22** How desirable are all His works, and how like a spark they are to behold!

**42:23** All these things live and abide forever for every need, and all things obey.

**42:24** All things are twofold, one opposite the other, and He has made nothing incomplete.

**42:25** One thing establishes the good things of the other, and who shall be satisfied with beholding His glory?

## Translator Notes

- **42:2** — The Greek literally reads 'to justify the ungodly,' either continuing the warning against partiality in verse 1 or referring to rendering impartial justice even when an ungodly person is in the right.
- **42:5** — The Greek adiaphorou literally means 'impartial' or 'indifferent' (a textual variant alongside diaphoros, 'profit' or 'margin'), pointing to fair dealing in commerce.
- **42:12** — The Greek text literally reads 'do not gaze upon any person because of beauty,' differing from the Hebrew tradition that warns against exposing a daughter's beauty.
- **42:18** — The Greek text preserves the first-person verb dienoethen ('I have pondered'), whereas other manuscripts read third-person dienoethe ('He considered') referring to the Lord.
- **42:21** — The Greek reads kai heos estin ('and while He exists'), a textual variant for hos estin ('as He is'), affirming God's eternal, unchanging self-sufficiency.
