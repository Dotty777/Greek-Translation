## Text

**32:1** Have they appointed you as leader? Do not exalt yourself; be among them as one of them. Take care of them, and then sit down.

**32:2** And when you have fulfilled all your duty, recline, that you may rejoice on their account and receive a crown for good order.

**32:3** Speak, O elder, for it is fitting for you—with exact knowledge—and do not hinder the music.

**32:4** Where there is entertainment, do not pour out speech, and do not make a show of wisdom out of season.

**32:5** A signet of carbuncle upon an ornament of gold, such is a concert of music at a banquet of wine.

**32:6** A signet of emerald in a setting of gold, such is a melody of music with sweet wine.

**32:7** Speak, O young man, if there is need of you, yet scarcely twice, if you are questioned.

**32:8** Sum up your speech, much in few words; be as one who knows and at the same time keeps silent.

**32:9** In the midst of great men do not make yourself their equal, and when another is speaking, do not chatter much.

**32:10** Before thunder the lightning hastens, and before a modest man favor shall go forth.

**32:11** Rise up at the proper hour and do not lag behind; hasten home and do not linger.

**32:12** There take your pleasure and do what you have in mind, yet do not sin by an arrogant word.

**32:13** And for these things bless Him who made you, and who satisfies you abundantly from His good things.

**32:14** He who fears the Lord will accept discipline, and those who rise early shall find favor.

**32:15** He who seeks the law shall be filled with it, but he who plays the hypocrite shall stumble in it.

**32:16** Those who fear the Lord shall find justice, and shall kindle righteous decrees like a light.

**32:17** A sinful man will turn aside from reproof, and according to his own will he will find a precedent.

**32:18** A man of counsel will by no means overlook a thought, but a strange and arrogant man will not shrink back in fear, even after acting on his own without counsel.

**32:19** Without counsel do nothing, and once you have acted, do not regret it.

**32:20** Do not walk on a path of stumbling, and do not stumble in stony places.

**32:21** Do not put your trust in an unobstructed road,

**32:22** and guard yourself against your children.

**32:23** In every work trust your own soul, for this also is the keeping of the commandments.

**32:24** He who trusts in the law gives heed to the commandments, and he who relies upon the Lord shall suffer no loss.

## Translator Notes

- **32:17** — Greek synkrima here means a comparison or precedent, referring to a self-serving justification or ruling found to excuse one's conduct.
- **32:18** — The closing clause is syntactically obscure in Greek (literally, 'even after doing with him without counsel'), conveying that the arrogant man remains fearless even after acting on his own without advice.
- **32:23** — The Greek text reads 'trust' (pisteue) rather than the Hebrew reading 'guard' your soul, emphasizing conscientious integrity in one's actions.
