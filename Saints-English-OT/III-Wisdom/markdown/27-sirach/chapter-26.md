## Text

**26:1** Blessed is the husband of a good wife, and the number of his days is twofold.

**26:2** A virtuous wife gladdens her husband, and he shall fulfill his years in peace.

**26:3** A good wife is a good portion; she shall be given in the portion of those who fear the Lord.

**26:4** Whether rich or poor, his heart is glad, and at every season his countenance is cheerful.

**26:5** Of three things my heart has been fearful, and before a fourth countenance I have made supplication: the slander of a city, and the assembly of a mob, and a false accusation—all these are more grievous than death.

**26:6** Grief of heart and mourning is a wife jealous of another wife, and a scourge of the tongue that makes it known to all.

**26:7** A shaking ox-yoke is a wicked wife; he who takes hold of her is like one who grasps a scorpion.

**26:8** Great wrath is a drunken wife, and she will not conceal her shame.

**26:9** A woman's harlotry is in the lifting up of her eyes, and by her eyelids it shall be known.

**26:10** Over a daughter strengthen the watch unswervingly, lest, finding liberty, she make use of it for herself.

**26:11** Keep watch behind a shameless eye, and do not marvel if she transgresses against you.

**26:12** As a thirsty traveler opens his mouth and drinks from any water near at hand, she will sit down before every stake, and before an arrow she will open her quiver.

**26:13** A wife's grace shall delight her husband, and her understanding shall fatten his bones.

**26:14** A gift of the Lord is a silent wife, and there is no exchange for a disciplined soul.

**26:15** Grace upon grace is a modest wife, and no weight is worthy of a self-controlled soul.

**26:16** Like the sun rising in the heights of the Lord, so is the beauty of a good wife in the ordering of his house.

**26:17** Like a lamp shining forth upon a holy lampstand, so is the beauty of a face upon a stately stature.

**26:18** Like golden pillars upon a silver base, so are beautiful feet upon the breast of a steadfast woman.

**26:28** At two things my heart has been grieved, and at a third wrath has come upon me: a warrior suffering want through poverty, and men of understanding if they are held in contempt, and one who turns back from righteousness to sin—the Lord shall prepare him for the sword.

**26:29** Scarcely shall a merchant keep himself free from wrongdoing, and a tradesman shall not be acquitted of sin.

## Translator Notes

- **26:5** — The Greek phrase "before a fourth countenance I have made supplication" is unusual and likely reflects a Hebrew expression for being terrified before a fourth thing, which is introduced in verse 6.
- **26:10** — The Greek text reads the adverb "unswervingly" modifying the command to strengthen the watch; some manuscripts read the dative adjective "headstrong" modifying "daughter."
- **26:18** — The Greek text reads "breast" (literally plural, "breasts/bosom"), which many scholars regard as an early copyist variant for "heels" matching the pillar-and-base imagery.
- **26:28** — Verses 19–27 are absent from the primary Greek manuscript tradition (GI) and appear only in expanded secondary witnesses (GII).
