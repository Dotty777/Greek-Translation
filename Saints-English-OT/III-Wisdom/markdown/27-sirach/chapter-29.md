## Text

**29:1** He who shows mercy will lend to his neighbor, and he who strengthens with his hand keeps the commandments.

**29:2** Lend to your neighbor in the time of his need, and in turn repay your neighbor at the appointed time.

**29:3** Keep your word firm and deal faithfully with him, and at every time you shall find what you need.

**29:4** Many have regarded a loan as a windfall, and have brought trouble upon those who helped them.

**29:5** Until he receives it, he will kiss his hand, and over his neighbor's wealth he will humble his voice; yet at the time of repayment he will draw out the time, and return words of weariness, and blame the times.

**29:6** If he is able, he will scarcely recover half, and will reckon it as a windfall; but if not, he has defrauded him of his money, and has gained him as an enemy not for nothing; curses and revilings he will repay him, and instead of honor he will repay him dishonor.

**29:7** Many have turned away on account of wickedness; they feared being defrauded for nothing.

**29:8** Nevertheless, be patient with a humble man, and you shall draw him toward mercy.

**29:9** For the sake of the commandment, help the poor man, and according to his need do not turn him away empty.

**29:10** Lose silver for a brother and a friend, and do not let it rust under a stone to ruin.

**29:11** Lay up your treasure according to the commandments of the Most High, and it shall profit you more than gold.

**29:12** Lock up almsgiving in your storehouses, and it shall deliver you from every affliction;

**29:13** more than a shield of might and more than a spear of strength, it shall fight for you against an enemy.

**29:14** A good man will become surety for his neighbor, and he who has lost shame will forsake him.

**29:15** Do not forget the favors of a surety, for he has given his life for you.

**29:16** A sinner will overturn the goods of his surety, and one who is ungrateful in mind will forsake his deliverer.

**29:17** Suretyship has ruined many who were prospering and tossed them like a wave of the sea; it has uprooted mighty men, and they wandered among foreign nations.

**29:19** A sinner who falls into suretyship and pursues business contracts shall fall into lawsuits.

**29:20** Help your neighbor according to your ability, and take heed to yourself lest you fall.

**29:21** The first essential of life is water and bread and a garment, and a house covering shame.

**29:22** Better is the life of a poor man under a shelter of rafters than sumptuous fare among strangers.

**29:23** With little or with much, be content.

**29:24** It is a miserable life to go from house to house, and wherever he sojourns he will not open his mouth;

**29:25** you will entertain and give drink without thanks, and in addition to these things you shall hear bitter words:

**29:26** "Come forward, sojourner, set the table, and if there is anything in your hand, feed me."

**29:27** "Depart, sojourner, from the presence of honor; my brother has come as my guest, and there is need of the lodging."

**29:28** These things are grievous to a man who has understanding: the rebuke of a household and the reproach of a creditor.

## Translator Notes

- **29:6** — The Greek text reads 'not for nothing' (ou dorean), meaning the lender has acquired an enemy at his own expense; some other manuscripts omit the negative to read 'without cause.'
- **29:8** — The Greek text lacks the negative particle present in some manuscripts ('do not keep him waiting for alms'), reading positively that one shall draw the humble person toward mercy.
- **29:17** — The second half of this verse ('it has uprooted mighty men...') is numbered separately as verse 18 in some editions, so the verse numbering here proceeds directly from 29:17 to 29:19.
- **29:23** — The Greek text preserves the shorter reading here, without the additional clause ('and you shall not hear the reproach of your sojourning') found in some later manuscripts.
