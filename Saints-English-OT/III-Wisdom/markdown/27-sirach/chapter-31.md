## Text

**31:1** Sleeplessness over wealth wastes away the flesh, and anxiety over it drives away sleep.

**31:2** Wakeful anxiety banishes slumber, and a severe sickness dispels sleep.

**31:3** The rich man toils in the gathering of riches, and when he rests, he is filled with his luxuries;

**31:4** the poor man toils in scarcity of livelihood, and when he rests, he becomes needy.

**31:5** He who loves gold will not be justified, and he who pursues corruption will himself be filled.

**31:6** Many have been given over to ruin for the sake of gold, and their destruction came before their face.

**31:7** It is a stumbling block to those who sacrifice to it, and every fool will be caught in it.

**31:8** Blessed is the rich man who is found blameless, and who has not walked after gold.

**31:9** Who is he, and we will call him blessed? For he has done wondrous things among his people.

**31:10** Who has been tested by it and proven perfect? Let it be a cause for boasting! Who was able to transgress and did not transgress, and to do evil and did not do it?

**31:11** His goods will be established, and the assembly will declare his charitable deeds.

**31:12** Have you sat down at a great table? Do not open your throat wide over it, and do not say, "Many indeed are the things upon it!"

**31:13** Remember that an evil eye is a wicked thing; what has been created more evil than an eye? Therefore it sheds tears from every face.

**31:14** Wherever he looks, do not stretch out your hand, and do not crowd with him in the dish.

**31:15** Understand the concerns of your neighbor from yourself, and be thoughtful in every matter.

**31:16** Eat like a man what is set before you, and do not chew greedily, lest you be despised.

**31:17** Be the first to stop for the sake of discipline, and do not be insatiable, lest you give offense.

**31:18** And if you are seated in the midst of many, do not stretch out your hand before them.

**31:19** How sufficient is a little to a well-disciplined man! Upon his bed he does not gasp for breath.

**31:20** Healthy sleep comes with a moderate stomach; he rises early, and his soul is with him. The distress of sleeplessness, bilious sickness, and griping pain are with an insatiable man.

**31:21** And if you have been pressed too hard with foods, rise up and step away, and you will find relief.

**31:22** Listen to me, child, and do not despise me, and in the end you will find my words true. In all your works be diligent, and no sickness shall ever come upon you.

**31:23** Lips will bless him who is generous with bread, and the testimony to his nobility is trustworthy;

**31:24** against him who is grudging with bread the city will murmur, and the testimony to his stinginess is exact.

**31:25** Do not show your valor in wine, for wine has destroyed many.

**31:26** The furnace tests the tempered steel in the quenching; so does wine test hearts in the strife of the proud.

**31:27** Wine is equal to life for a man, if you drink it in its measure. What is life to one who is deprived of wine? For it was created for the gladness of men.

**31:28** Exultation of heart and gladness of soul is wine drunk at the right time and in moderate measure;

**31:29** bitterness of soul is wine drunk in excess, with provocation and downfall.

**31:30** Drunkenness increases the wrath of a fool to his stumbling, diminishing strength and adding wounds.

**31:31** At a banquet of wine do not rebuke your neighbor, and do not despise him in his gladness; do not speak a word of reproach to him, and do not distress him by demanding a debt.

## Translator Notes

- **31:2** — The verbs rendered "banishes" and "dispels" literally mean "demands back" and "wakes soberly from," reflecting unusual Greek renderings of the underlying proverb.
- **31:5** — The Greek text here reads "will himself be filled"; other Greek manuscripts read "will go astray in it."
- **31:13** — "From every face" literally renders a Greek phrase reflecting a Hebrew idiom meaning "because of everything" or "in the presence of all."
- **31:21** — "Rise up and step away" translates the Greek phrase literally meaning "rise up going in the midst"; other Greek witnesses read "vomit outside," matching the Hebrew text.
