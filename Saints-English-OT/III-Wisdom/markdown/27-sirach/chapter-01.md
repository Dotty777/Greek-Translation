## Prologue

Since many and great things have been given to us through the Law and the Prophets and the others who followed after them—for which Israel ought to be praised for instruction and wisdom, and since not only ought the readers themselves to become knowledgeable, but lovers of learning should also be able to be useful to those outside, both in speaking and in writing—my grandfather Jesus, after devoting himself extensively to the reading of the Law and the Prophets and the other ancestral books, and having acquired considerable proficiency in them, was himself also led to compose something pertaining to instruction and wisdom, so that lovers of learning, becoming committed to these writings as well, might make much greater progress in living according to the Law. You are urged, therefore, to read with goodwill and attention, and to show forbearance wherever we may seem to fall short in some of the expressions over which we have labored in translation; for things spoken in Hebrew do not have the same force in themselves when carried over into another language. And not only these, but the Law itself, and the prophecies, and the rest of the books have no small difference when spoken in their original tongue. For when I came into Egypt in the thirty-eighth year under King Euergetes and remained for a time, I found a copy of no small instruction; I therefore judged it most necessary myself to apply some diligence and labor to translating this book. Indeed, I have devoted much sleepless effort and skill over that span of time to bringing the book to completion and publishing it also for those living abroad who wish to love learning, preparing their character to live according to the Law.

## Text

**1:1** All wisdom comes from the Lord, and is with Him forever.

**1:2** The sand of the seas, the drops of rain, and the days of eternity—who shall number them?

**1:3** The height of heaven, the breadth of the earth, the abyss, and wisdom—who shall trace them out?

**1:4** Before all things wisdom has been created, and the understanding of prudence is from everlasting.

**1:6** To whom has the root of wisdom been revealed? And who has known her skillful designs?

**1:8** There is One who is wise, greatly to be feared, seated upon His throne.

**1:9** The Lord Himself created her, and saw and numbered her, and poured her out upon all His works,

**1:10** with all flesh according to His gift, and He bestowed her generously upon those who love Him.

**1:11** The fear of the Lord is glory and exultation, and gladness, and a crown of rejoicing.

**1:12** The fear of the Lord shall delight the heart, and shall give gladness, and joy, and length of days.

**1:13** With him who fears the Lord it shall be well at the last, and in the day of his death he shall find favor.

**1:14** The beginning of wisdom is to fear God, and she was created together with the faithful in the womb.

**1:15** Among men she made an everlasting foundation her nest, and with their offspring she shall be trusted.

**1:16** The fullness of wisdom is to fear the Lord, and she satiates them from her fruits.

**1:17** She shall fill all her house with desirable things, and the storehouses with her produce.

**1:18** The crown of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, causing peace and the health of healing to flourish.

**1:19** He saw her and numbered her; He rained down skill and discerning knowledge, and exalted the glory of those who hold fast to her.

**1:20** The root of wisdom is to fear the Lord, and her branches are length of days.

**1:22** Unrighteous wrath cannot be justified; for the weight of his wrath is his downfall.

**1:23** A patient man will endure until the proper time, and afterward gladness shall spring up for him.

**1:24** Until the proper time he will conceal his words, and the lips of the faithful shall declare his understanding.

**1:25** In the treasuries of wisdom is a proverb of knowledge, but godliness is an abomination to a sinner.

**1:26** If you desire wisdom, keep the commandments, and the Lord will generously bestow her upon you.

**1:27** For the fear of the Lord is wisdom and instruction, and His good pleasure is faithfulness and meekness.

**1:28** Do not be disobedient to the fear of the Lord, and do not draw near to Him with a double heart.

**1:29** Do not be a hypocrite in the mouths of men, and keep watch over your lips.

**1:30** Do not exalt yourself, lest you fall and bring dishonor upon your soul; and the Lord will uncover your secrets and cast you down in the midst of the assembly, because you did not draw near in the fear of the Lord, and your heart was full of deceit.

## Translator Notes

- **Prologue** — The Greek translator's prologue by the grandson of Jesus ben Sira, dated to the thirty-eighth year of Ptolemy VIII Euergetes II (132 BC), is the earliest witness to the threefold division of Israel's Scriptures ('the Law and the Prophets and the other ancestral books') and reflects on the inherent challenges of translating Hebrew into Greek.
- **1:6** — The Greek critical text follows the shorter ancient Greek tradition (GI) and therefore omits verses 5 and 7 (as well as verse 21), which appear in expanded Greek manuscripts. The noun translated "skillful designs" (panourgeumata) carries a positive sense here of wisdom's subtle craftsmanship.
- **1:13** — The Greek text reads "he shall find favor"; several later manuscripts read "he shall be blessed."
- **1:15** — The vivid Greek phrase "made an everlasting foundation her nest" (enosseusen) reflects the Greek translator's rendering of the underlying Hebrew, picturing wisdom's lasting dwelling among humanity.
- **1:19** — The opening clause "He saw her and numbered her" echoes verse 9 and establishes the Lord as the subject of the actions that follow; it is omitted in some manuscripts.
- **1:29** — Literally "in the mouths of men," an idiom referring to putting on a false front before the speech and opinion of others.
