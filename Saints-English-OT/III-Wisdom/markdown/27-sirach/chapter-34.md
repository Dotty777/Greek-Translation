## Text

**34:1** Empty and false hopes belong to a man without understanding, and dreams give wings to fools.

**34:2** Like one who grasps at a shadow and pursues the wind, so is he who pays heed to dreams.

**34:3** The vision of dreams is this over against that, the likeness of a face over against a face.

**34:4** From what is unclean, what shall be cleansed? And from what is false, what shall prove true?

**34:5** Divinations and omens and dreams are vain, and like one in travail the heart forms imaginations.

**34:6** Unless they are sent from the Most High in a visitation, do not give your heart to them.

**34:7** Dreams have led many astray, and those who put their hope in them have fallen.

**34:8** Without falsehood the law shall be fulfilled, and wisdom in a faithful mouth is perfection.

**34:9** A well-instructed man knows many things, and a man of much experience will declare understanding.

**34:10** He who has not been tested knows little, but he who has traveled widely will multiply shrewdness.

**34:11** I have seen many things in my travels, and my understanding is greater than my words.

**34:12** Many times I was in peril even to death, and I was delivered on account of these things.

**34:13** The spirit of those who fear the Lord shall live, for their hope is in Him who saves them.

**34:14** He who fears the Lord shall never be terrified and shall never lose heart, for He is his hope.

**34:15** Blessed is the soul of him who fears the Lord! To whom does he look, and who is his support?

**34:16** The eyes of the Lord are upon those who love Him—a mighty defense and a strong support, a shelter from scorching heat and a shade from noonday, a guard against stumbling and a help against falling.

**34:17** He lifts up the soul and enlightens the eyes, giving healing, life, and blessing.

**34:18** If one sacrifices from what is unjustly gained, the offering is a mockery, and the mockeries of the lawless are not acceptable.

**34:19** The Most High is not pleased with the offerings of the ungodly, nor does He forgive sins for a multitude of sacrifices.

**34:20** Like one who slays a son before his father is he who brings a sacrifice from the goods of the poor.

**34:21** The bread of the needy is the life of the poor; he who deprives them of it is a man of blood.

**34:22** He murders his neighbor who takes away his livelihood, and he sheds blood who deprives a hired laborer of his wages.

**34:23** When one builds and another tears down, what have they profited beyond toil?

**34:24** When one prays and another curses, whose voice will the Master hear?

**34:25** If a man washes himself because of a dead body and touches it again, what has he profited by his washing?

**34:26** So it is with a man who fasts for his sins and goes again and does the same things: who will hear his prayer, and what has he profited by humbling himself?

## Translator Notes

- **34:9** — The Greek text reads "well-instructed" (pepaideumenos), whereas some critics conjecture "traveled" (peplanemenos) to parallel verse ten.
- **34:18** — The Greek reads "mocked" (memokemene) and "mockeries" (mokemata), standing alongside the variant "blemished" (memomemene) attested in some manuscripts.
