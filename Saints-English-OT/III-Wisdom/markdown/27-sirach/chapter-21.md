## Text

**21:1** Child, have you sinned? Do not do so any more, and pray concerning your former sins.

**21:2** Flee from sin as from the face of a serpent, for if you draw near, it will bite you; its teeth are the teeth of a lion, slaying the souls of men.

**21:3** All lawlessness is like a two-edged sword; for its wound there is no healing.

**21:4** Terror and arrogance will lay waste riches; thus the house of the proud shall be laid waste.

**21:5** The prayer of a poor man goes from his mouth to His ears, and His judgment comes swiftly.

**21:6** He who hates reproof is in the track of a sinner, but he who fears the Lord will repent in his heart.

**21:7** He who is mighty in tongue is known from afar, but the discerning man knows whenever he slips.

**21:8** He who builds his house with the money of others is like one who gathers his stones for winter.

**21:9** The assembly of the lawless is tow gathered together, and their end is a flame of fire.

**21:10** The way of sinners is paved smooth with stones, yet at its end is the pit of Hades.

**21:11** He who keeps the law masters his thoughts, and the perfection of the fear of the Lord is wisdom.

**21:12** He who is not shrewd will not be instructed; yet there is a shrewdness that multiplies bitterness.

**21:13** The knowledge of a wise man shall abound like a flood, and his counsel is like a fountain of life.

**21:14** The inward parts of a fool are like a broken vessel, and he will retain no knowledge at all.

**21:15** If a man of understanding hears a wise word, he will praise it and add to it; the self-indulgent man hears it and it displeases him, and he casts it behind his back.

**21:16** The discourse of a fool is like a burden on a journey, but grace shall be found upon the lip of the discerning.

**21:17** The mouth of a prudent man shall be sought in the assembly, and they will ponder his words in their heart.

**21:18** Like a house laid in ruins, so is wisdom to a fool, and the knowledge of the senseless is unexamined words.

**21:19** Instruction is fetters on the feet of the foolish, and like shackles upon the right hand.

**21:20** A fool raises his voice in laughter, but a shrewd man will scarcely smile quietly.

**21:21** Like a golden ornament is instruction to a prudent man, and like a bracelet upon the right arm.

**21:22** The foot of a fool is swift to enter a house, but a man of much experience will show modesty before another's presence.

**21:23** A fool peers from the doorway into a house, but a well-instructed man will stand outside.

**21:24** It is lack of discipline in a man to listen at the door, and the prudent man shall be weighed down with disgrace.

**21:25** The lips of strangers shall be weighed down with these things, but the words of the prudent shall be weighed in a balance.

**21:26** In the mouth of fools is their heart, but the heart of the wise is their mouth.

**21:27** When an ungodly man curses Satan, he curses his own soul.

**21:28** A whisperer defiles his own soul, and he shall be hated in his neighborhood.

## Translator Notes

- **21:5** — The Greek pronoun "His" implicitly refers to God despite no prior explicit antecedent; in the second clause, it may alternatively be read as "his judgment," meaning the poor man's vindication.
- **21:8** — The Greek literally reads "for winter," an image of futile or self-defeating labor (some scholars suspect an underlying Hebrew wordplay on a burial mound or tomb).
- **21:25** — The Greek text reads "shall be weighed down" (echoing v. 24), contrasting the burdensome talk of meddlesome strangers with the measured words of the prudent weighed in a balance.
- **21:27** — Greek "ton satanan" transliterates the Hebrew title meaning "the adversary" (Satan), which may refer either to the demonic adversary or to an opponent whom the ungodly man blames for his own guilt.
