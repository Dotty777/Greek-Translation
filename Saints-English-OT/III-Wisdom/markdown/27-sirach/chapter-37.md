## Text

**37:1** Every friend will say, "I also have been a friend to him"; yet there is a friend who is a friend in name only.

**37:2** Is there not a grief even to death when a companion and friend turns to enmity?

**37:3** O wicked imagination, from where were you rolled in to cover the dry land with deceit?

**37:4** A companion delights in a friend's gladness, and in a time of affliction he will stand against him;

**37:5** a companion labors with a friend for the sake of the belly; in the face of battle he will take up a shield.

**37:6** Do not forget a friend in your soul, and do not be unmindful of him in your wealth.

**37:7** Every counselor extols counsel, yet there is one who gives counsel for himself.

**37:8** Guard your soul from a counselor, and know beforehand what his need is—for he also will counsel for himself—lest he cast a lot against you,

**37:9** and say to you, "Your way is good," and he will stand opposite to see what will befall you.

**37:10** Do not take counsel with him who looks suspiciously at you, and hide counsel from those who envy you:

**37:11** with a woman concerning her rival, and with a coward concerning war, and with a merchant concerning exchange, and with a buyer concerning a sale, with a grudging man concerning gratitude, and with a merciless man concerning kindness, with a slothful man concerning any work, and with an outside hireling concerning the completion of a task, or with an idle household servant concerning much labor—do not rely on these for any counsel.

**37:12** Rather, remain continually with a godly man, whom you recognize as keeping the commandments, who in his soul is like your own soul, and if you stumble, he will grieve with you.

**37:13** And establish the counsel of your heart, for there is no one more faithful to you than it;

**37:14** for a man's soul is sometimes accustomed to report better than seven watchmen sitting on high upon a watchtower.

**37:15** And above all these things, pray to the Most High that He may direct your way in truth.

**37:16** The beginning of every work is a word, and before every deed is counsel.

**37:17** The trace of alteration is in the heart:

**37:18** four parts spring forth—good and evil, life and death—and that which continually rules over them is the tongue.

**37:19** There is a shrewd man and an instructor of many, and yet to his own soul he is useless.

**37:20** There is one who makes a show of wisdom in words and is hated; this man shall be destitute of all nourishment,

**37:21** for grace was not given to him from the Lord, because he was deprived of all wisdom.

**37:22** There is one who is wise for his own soul, and the fruits of his understanding upon the mouth are trustworthy.

**37:23** A wise man will instruct his own people, and the fruits of his understanding are trustworthy.

**37:24** A wise man shall be filled with blessing, and all who behold him will call him blessed.

**37:25** The life of a man is in a number of days, and the days of Israel are innumerable.

**37:26** The wise man among his people shall inherit confidence, and his name shall live forever.

**37:27** Child, in your life test your soul, and see what is harmful to it, and do not give that to it;

**37:28** for not all things are profitable for all, and not every soul takes pleasure in everything.

**37:29** Do not be insatiable in any delicacy, and do not pour yourself out over foods;

**37:30** for in many foods there shall be pain, and insatiability will draw near even to bilious sickness;

**37:31** because of insatiability many have died, but he who takes heed will add to his life.

## Translator Notes

- **37:3** — Greek ἐνθύμημα ("imagination" or "evil impulse") denotes a deceitful inclination, and ἐνεκυλίσθης literally means "were rolled in," picturing deceit engulfing the earth.
- **37:11** — Greek μισθίου ἀφεστίου literally designates a hired laborer living away from the hearth or household ("an outside hireling"), contrasted with the resident household servant that follows.
- **37:17** — Greek ἴχνος ἀλλοιώσεως καρδίᾳ literally reads "a trace of alteration is in the heart," introducing the four contrasting outcomes in verse 18 that spring forth and are governed by the tongue.
- **37:30** — Greek χολέρας refers to severe bilious or intestinal illness resulting from overindulgence.
