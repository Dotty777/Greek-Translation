## Text

**50:1** Simon the son of Onias was the high priest, who in his life repaired the house and in his days strengthened the temple.

**50:2** And by him was laid the foundation of the lofty double wall, the high retaining wall of the temple precinct.

**50:3** In his days the reservoir of waters was diminished, bronze like the sea in its circumference.

**50:4** He cared for his people against downfall and fortified the city to withstand siege.

**50:5** How he was glorified when surrounded by the people, as he came forth from the house of the veil!

**50:6** Like the morning star in the midst of a cloud, like the full moon in its days;

**50:7** like the sun shining forth upon the temple of the Most High, and like the rainbow gleaming in clouds of glory;

**50:8** like the blossom of roses in the days of new growth, like lilies by the streams of water, like a shoot of Lebanon in the days of summer;

**50:9** like fire and frankincense upon the censer, like a vessel of solid beaten gold adorned with every precious stone;

**50:10** like an olive tree putting forth fruit, and like a cypress towering into the clouds.

**50:11** When he put on the robe of glory and clothed himself in the perfection of splendor, as he ascended to the holy altar, he made glorious the enclosure of the sanctuary.

**50:12** And when he received the portions from the hands of the priests, while standing himself by the hearth of the altar, with a garland of brethren all around him, he was like a young cedar in Lebanon, and they encircled him like trunks of palm trees.

**50:13** And all the sons of Aaron were in their glory, with the offering of the Lord in their hands before the whole assembly of Israel.

**50:14** And completing the service at the altars, to arrange the offering of the Most High, the Almighty,

**50:15** he stretched out his hand to the libation cup and poured a drink offering of the blood of the grape; he poured it out at the foundations of the altar, a sweet-smelling aroma to the Most High, the King of all.

**50:16** Then the sons of Aaron shouted aloud; they sounded the trumpets of beaten work; they caused a mighty sound to be heard for a remembrance before the Most High.

**50:17** Then all the people hastened together and fell upon their faces to the ground to worship their Lord, the Almighty, God Most High.

**50:18** And the singers sang praises with their voices; throughout the vast house the melody was made sweet.

**50:19** And the people implored the Lord Most High in prayer before the Merciful One, until the order of the Lord was completed and they finished His service.

**50:20** Then he came down and lifted up his hands over the whole assembly of the sons of Israel, to give blessing to the Lord from his lips and to glory in His name.

**50:21** And he bowed down in worship a second time to show forth the blessing from the Most High.

**50:22** And now, all of you, bless God, who does great things everywhere, who exalts our days from the womb and deals with us according to His mercy.

**50:23** May He grant you gladness of heart, and may there be peace in our days in Israel as in the days of old.

**50:24** May His mercy abide faithfully with us, and in His days may He redeem us.

**50:25** My soul abhors two nations, and the third is not a nation:

**50:26** those who sit on the mountain of Samaria, the Philistines, and the foolish people who dwell in Shechem.

**50:27** Instruction in understanding and knowledge I have inscribed in this book, I, Jesus the son of Sirach Eleazar of Jerusalem, who poured forth wisdom from his heart.

**50:28** Blessed is he who shall walk in these things, and he who lays them upon his heart shall become wise.

**50:29** For if he does them, he shall be strong for all things; for the light of the Lord is his path.

## Translator Notes

- **50:3** — The Greek text reads elattothe ("was diminished"), generally regarded as an early scribal variant for elatomethe ("was quarried" or "hewn out"), referring to the digging of a temple cistern.
- **50:18** — The Greek text reads "the vast house" (en pleisto oiko), referring to the temple precincts, whereas some manuscripts read "the fullest sound" (en pleisto echo).
- **50:20** — The Greek text reads the dative Kyrio ("to the Lord"), whereas other manuscripts read the genitive Kyriou ("the blessing of the Lord").
- **50:21** — The Greek text has a singular verb ("he bowed down a second time") and epideixasthai ("to show forth"), referring to the high priest, whereas other manuscripts read a plural verb and epidexasthai ("to receive"), referring to the people.
- **50:26** — The Greek text reads "the mountain of Samaria," whereas the Hebrew text reads "Mount Seir," referring to the Edomites.
