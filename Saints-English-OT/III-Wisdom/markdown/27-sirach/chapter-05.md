## Text

**5:1** Do not rely on your riches, and do not say, "They are sufficient for me."

**5:2** Do not follow after your soul and your strength, to walk in the desires of your heart;

**5:3** and do not say, "Who shall have dominion over me?" For the Lord will surely execute vengeance upon you.

**5:4** Do not say, "I have sinned, and what has happened to me?" For the Lord is longsuffering.

**5:5** Concerning atonement, do not become fearless, to add sin upon sins;

**5:6** and do not say, "His compassion is great; He will atone for the multitude of my sins." For mercy and wrath are from Him, and His fury shall rest upon sinners.

**5:7** Do not delay to turn back to the Lord, and do not put it off from day to day; for suddenly the wrath of the Lord shall go forth, and in the time of vengeance you shall perish.

**5:8** Do not rely on unrighteous riches; for you shall profit nothing in the day of calamity.

**5:9** Do not winnow in every wind, and do not walk in every path; so is the double-tongued sinner.

**5:10** Be steadfast in your understanding, and let your word be one.

**5:11** Be quick in your listening, and with patience utter an answer.

**5:12** If you have understanding, answer your neighbor; but if not, let your hand be upon your mouth.

**5:13** Glory and dishonor are in speech, and the tongue of a man is his downfall.

**5:14** Do not be called a whisperer, and do not be ensnared by your tongue; for shame is upon the thief, and evil condemnation upon the double-tongued.

**5:15** In great matters and in small, do not act ignorantly, and instead of a friend do not become an enemy; for an evil name is shame, and it shall inherit reproach; so is the double-tongued sinner. Do not exalt yourself in the counsel of your soul, lest your soul be torn apart like a bull.

## Translator Notes

- **5:14** — The Greek reads the passive verb ἐνεδρευθῇς ("be ensnared" or "ambushed"), whereas some manuscripts read the active ἐνέδρευε ("lay snares").
- **5:15** — In this Septuagint versification, verse 5:15 encompasses the text numbered 5:15–6:2 in some editions. The closing clause faithfully renders ὡς ταῦρος ("like a bull"), a well-known Septuagint reading where the Hebrew may have had a different image.
