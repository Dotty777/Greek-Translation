## Text

**9:1** Do not be jealous of the wife of your bosom, nor teach an evil lesson against yourself.

**9:2** Do not give your soul to a woman, that she should tread upon your strength.

**9:3** Do not go to meet a loose woman, lest you fall into her snares.

**9:4** Do not keep constant company with a woman who plays music, lest you be captured by her schemes.

**9:5** Do not gaze intently upon a virgin, lest you stumble into the penalties for her.

**9:6** Do not give your soul to harlots, lest you lose your inheritance.

**9:7** Do not look around in the streets of a city, nor wander about in its deserted places.

**9:8** Turn your eye away from a comely woman, and do not gaze intently upon beauty that belongs to another; by the beauty of a woman many have been led astray, and from this, love is kindled like fire.

**9:9** Do not sit at all with a married woman, nor revel with her in wine, lest your soul incline toward her, and in your spirit you slip into destruction.

**9:10** Do not forsake an old friend, for the new one is not equal to him; new wine is a new friend: when it has grown old, you shall drink it with gladness.

**9:11** Do not envy the glory of a sinner, for you do not know what his overthrow will be.

**9:12** Do not delight in the delight of the ungodly; remember that even until Hades they shall by no means be justified.

**9:13** Keep far away from a man who has authority to slay, and you shall by no means entertain the fear of death; and even if you approach him, do not offend, lest he take away your life. Know well that you pass through the midst of snares, and you walk upon the battlements of cities.

**9:14** According to your strength, take the measure of your neighbors, and take counsel with the wise.

**9:15** And let your discourse be with those of understanding, and all your conversation be in the law of the Most High.

**9:16** Let righteous men be your table companions, and let your glorying be in the fear of the Lord.

**9:17** By the hand of craftsmen a work shall be praised, and the ruler of a people is wise in his speech.

**9:18** A loose-tongued man is dreaded in his city, and he who is rash in his speech shall be hated.

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — The Greek phrase literally refers to her penalties or fines, alluding to the legal penalties incurred under the Law for seducing or wronging a virgin (cf. Exodus 22:16-17; Deuteronomy 22:28-29).
- **9:13** — The Greek verb literally means to suspect or apprehend, warning against placing oneself in constant apprehension of death before a ruler who holds power over life.
