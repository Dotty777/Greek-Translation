## Text

**18:1** He who lives forever created all things together.

**18:2** The Lord alone shall be justified.

**18:4** To no one has He granted to declare His works, and who shall trace out His mighty deeds?

**18:5** Who shall reckon the might of His majesty? And who shall proceed to recount His mercies?

**18:6** It is not possible to diminish nor to add, and it is not possible to trace out the wondrous works of the Lord.

**18:7** When a man has finished, then he begins, and when he ceases, then he shall be perplexed.

**18:8** What is man, and what is his usefulness? What is his good, and what is his evil?

**18:9** The number of a man's days is at most one hundred years.

**18:10** As a drop of water from the sea and a grain of sand, so are a few years in a day of eternity.

**18:11** Therefore the Lord was longsuffering toward them, and poured out His mercy upon them.

**18:12** He saw and perceived their downfall, that it is evil; therefore He multiplied His forgiveness.

**18:13** The mercy of man is upon his neighbor, but the mercy of the Lord is upon all flesh—reproving and chastening and teaching, and bringing back as a shepherd his flock.

**18:14** He has mercy on those who receive discipline, and on those who hasten after His judgments.

**18:15** Child, in good deeds do not give reproach, nor in any gift grief of words.

**18:16** Will not dew relieve the scorching heat? So is a word better than a gift.

**18:17** Behold, is not a word better than a good gift? And both are with a gracious man.

**18:18** A fool will ungraciously reproach, and the gift of a grudging man wastes away the eyes.

**18:19** Before you speak, learn, and before sickness, seek treatment.

**18:20** Before judgment examine yourself, and in the hour of visitation you shall find forgiveness.

**18:21** Before you fall sick, humble yourself, and in the time of sins, show repentance.

**18:22** Do not be hindered from paying a vow in due time, and do not wait until death to be justified.

**18:23** Before making a vow, prepare yourself, and do not become like a man who tests the Lord.

**18:24** Remember wrath in the days of the end, and the time of vengeance in the turning away of the face.

**18:25** Remember the time of famine in the time of abundance, poverty and want in the days of wealth.

**18:26** From morning until evening the time changes, and all things are swift before the Lord.

**18:27** A wise man shall be cautious in everything, and in days of sins he shall guard himself from trespass.

**18:28** Every man of understanding knows wisdom, and to him who finds her he shall give praise.

**18:29** Those who have understanding in words have themselves also become wise, and have poured forth accurate proverbs.

**18:30** Self-control of the soul. Do not go after your desires, and restrain yourself from your appetites.

**18:31** If you grant your soul the satisfaction of desire, it will make you a laughingstock to your enemies.

**18:32** Do not delight in great luxury; do not be bound to its expense.

**18:33** Do not become poor by carousing on borrowed money, when you have nothing in your purse.

## Translator Notes

- **18:12** — "Forgiveness" renders Greek exilasmos (also in 18:20), which in the Septuagint denotes both atonement/propitiation and divine pardon.
- **18:18** — "Wastes away the eyes" renders Greek ektekei ophthalmous, a vivid idiom for causing bitter grief or weeping over a grudgingly given gift.
- **18:23** — Greek euxasthai can mean either "to make a vow" or "to pray"; following the mention of vows in 18:22, it refers primarily to making a vow.
- **18:30** — The section heading "Self-control of the soul" (Greek Enkrateia psyches) stands in the Greek manuscript tradition at the beginning of this verse.
