## Text

**19:1** A drunken workman shall not become rich; he who despises small things shall fall little by little.

**19:2** Wine and women will turn men of understanding astray, and he who clings to harlots shall become more reckless.

**19:3** Decay and worms shall inherit him, and a reckless soul shall be cut off.

**19:4** He who trusts quickly is light of heart, and he who sins shall offend against his own soul.

**19:5** He who rejoices in heart shall be condemned.

**19:6** And he who hates babbling has less of evil.

**19:7** Never repeat a word, and you shall suffer no loss at all.

**19:8** Whether to friend or to enemy, do not recount it; and unless it is a sin for you, do not reveal it.

**19:9** For he has heard you and has guarded himself against you, and in due time he shall hate you.

**19:10** Have you heard a word? Let it die with you. Take courage—it shall by no means burst you.

**19:11** Because of a word a fool shall be in travail, as a woman giving birth because of an infant.

**19:12** An arrow stuck fast in a thigh of flesh—so is a word in the belly of a fool.

**19:13** Confront a friend—perhaps he did not do it; and if he did anything, that he may not do it again.

**19:14** Confront your friend—perhaps he did not say it; and if he has said it, that he may not repeat it.

**19:15** Confront a friend, for often there is slander, and do not believe every word.

**19:16** There is one who slips, and not from his soul; and who has not sinned with his tongue?

**19:17** Confront your neighbor before threatening him, and give place to the law of the Most High.

**19:20** All wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and in all wisdom is the doing of the law.

**19:22** And the knowledge of wickedness is not wisdom, nor is the counsel of sinners anywhere prudence.

**19:23** There is wickedness, and it is an abomination; and there is a fool who is lacking in wisdom.

**19:24** Better is one who is inferior in understanding yet filled with fear, than one who abounds in prudence and transgresses the law.

**19:25** There is a meticulous craftiness, and it is unjust; and there is one who perverts favor to bring forth a judgment.

**19:26** There is one who does evil, bowed down in gloom, yet his inward parts are full of deceit.

**19:27** Bowing down his face and feigning deafness, where he has not been recognized he will overtake you.

**19:28** And if through lack of strength he is hindered from sinning, if he finds an opportunity, he will do evil.

**19:29** By his appearance a man shall be known, and by the meeting of his countenance a discerning man shall be known.

**19:30** The attire of a man, and the laughter of his teeth, and a man's steps declare what concerns him.

## Translator Notes

- **19:5** — The critical Greek text reads kardia ("in heart"); some Greek manuscripts read kakia ("in wickedness"), yielding "he who rejoices in wickedness shall be condemned."
- **19:13** — Greek elenxon (repeated in 19:14, 19:15, and 19:17) encompasses confronting, questioning, and reproving, culminating in the allusion to Leviticus 19:17 in 19:17.
- **19:23** — Greek poneria literally means "wickedness" (echoing 19:22), whereas the underlying Hebrew tradition contrasts malicious "cleverness" with simple folly.
- **19:27** — "Feigning deafness" renders the rare Greek compound heterokophon, literally pretending to be deaf on one side while covertly watching for an advantage.
