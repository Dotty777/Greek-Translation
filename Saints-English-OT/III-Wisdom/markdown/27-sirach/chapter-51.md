## Text

**51:1** A Prayer of Jesus, the son of Sirach. I will give thanks to You, O Lord, King, and I will praise You, God my Savior; I give thanks to Your name,

**51:2** for You have become a protector and helper to me, and You have redeemed my body from destruction and from the snare of a slanderous tongue, from lips that work falsehood, and before those who stood by You became a helper,

**51:3** and You redeemed me, according to the abundance of Your mercy and Your name, from gnashing teeth when I was ready to become food, from the hand of those who sought my life, from the many afflictions which I endured,

**51:4** from the choking of a blazing fire all around, and from the midst of a fire which I did not kindle,

**51:5** from the depth of the belly of Hades, and from an unclean tongue and a lying word.

**51:6** Slander of an unrighteous tongue before a king—my soul gave praise even to death, and my life was near to Hades below.

**51:7** They surrounded me on every side, and there was no one to help; I looked for the support of men, and there was none.

**51:8** Then I remembered Your mercy, O Lord, and Your work from of old, that You will deliver those who wait for You, and You save them from the hand of the nations.

**51:9** And I lifted up my supplication upon the earth, and I pleaded for deliverance from death.

**51:10** I called upon the Lord, the Father of my lord, not to forsake me in the days of affliction, in the time of arrogance and helplessness. I will praise Your name continually, and I will sing hymns in thanksgiving. And my prayer was heard.

**51:12** For You saved me from destruction and delivered me from an evil time; therefore I will give thanks and praise You, and I will bless the name of the Lord.

**51:13** While I was still young, before I wandered, I sought wisdom openly in my prayer.

**51:14** Before the temple I prayed for her, and to the very end I will seek her out.

**51:15** From the blossom, as of a ripening grape, my heart rejoiced in her; my foot trod in uprightness, and from my youth I followed her tracks.

**51:16** I inclined my ear a little and received her, and I found for myself much instruction.

**51:17** Progress came to me through her; to Him who gives me wisdom I will give glory.

**51:18** For I resolved to practice her, and I was zealous for what is good, and I shall never be put to shame.

**51:19** My soul has wrestled for her, and in the practice of hunger I was scrupulous; I stretched out my hands on high, and I lamented my ignorance of her.

**51:20** I directed my soul toward her, I gained a heart with them from the beginning, and in purification I found her; therefore I shall never be forsaken.

**51:21** And my inmost being was stirred to seek her out; therefore I have acquired a good possession.

**51:22** The Lord gave me a tongue as my reward, and with it I will praise Him.

**51:23** Draw near to me, you who are uninstructed, and lodge in the house of instruction.

**51:24** And because you say that you are lacking in these things, and your souls thirst exceedingly,

**51:25** I opened my mouth and spoke, "Acquire her for yourselves without silver;

**51:26** put your neck under the yoke, and let your soul receive instruction; she is near to be found.

**51:27** See with your own eyes that I labored but a little, and found for myself much rest.

**51:28** Partake of instruction with a great sum of silver, and acquire much gold through her.

**51:29** May your soul rejoice in His mercy, and may you not be ashamed in His praise.

**51:30** Do your work before the time, and He will give your reward in His time."

## Translator Notes

- **51:6** — The Greek text reads enesen ("gave praise"), an early manuscript reading (as in Codex Vaticanus), whereas other witnesses read engisen ("drew near").
- **51:10** — The title "the Lord, the Father of my lord" translates the exact Greek Kyrion patera kyriou mou, a distinctive Septuagint rendering; the final clause ("And my prayer was heard") is counted as verse 11 in some editions.
- **51:19** — The Greek text reads en poiesei limou ("in the practice of hunger/famine"), where other Greek manuscripts read nomou ("of the law").
- **51:20** — The Greek text preserves the plural met' auton ("with them") in this clause order, where other manuscripts read the singular met' autes ("with her").
- **51:28** — The Greek text reads en pollo arithmo argyriou ("with a great sum of silver"), creating a rhetorical contrast with "without silver" in verse 25 and differing from traditions that read "a small sum."
