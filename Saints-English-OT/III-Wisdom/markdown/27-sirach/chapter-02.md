## Text

**2:1** Child, if you come forward to serve the Lord God, prepare your soul for trial.

**2:2** Set your heart aright and be steadfast, and do not make haste in the time of calamity.

**2:3** Cling to Him and do not depart, so that you may be increased at your end.

**2:4** Accept whatever is brought upon you, and in the reversals of your humiliation be longsuffering.

**2:5** For gold is tested in the fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of humiliation.

**2:6** Trust in Him, and He will uphold you; make straight your ways, and hope in Him.

**2:7** You who fear the Lord, wait for His mercy, and do not turn aside, lest you fall.

**2:8** You who fear the Lord, trust in Him, and your reward shall by no means fail.

**2:9** You who fear the Lord, hope for good things, and for everlasting gladness and mercy.

**2:10** Look to the generations of old and see: who ever trusted in the Lord and was put to shame? Or who remained steadfast in His fear and was forsaken? Or who called upon Him, and He disregarded him?

**2:11** For the Lord is compassionate and merciful, and forgives sins, and saves in the time of affliction.

**2:12** Woe to timid hearts and to slack hands, and to the sinner who walks upon two paths.

**2:13** Woe to a faint heart, for it does not trust; therefore it shall not be sheltered.

**2:14** Woe to you who have lost endurance! And what will you do when the Lord visits?

**2:15** Those who fear the Lord will not disobey His words, and those who love Him will keep His ways.

**2:16** Those who fear the Lord will seek His good pleasure, and those who love Him shall be filled with the law.

**2:17** Those who fear the Lord will prepare their hearts, and before Him they will humble their souls.

**2:18** Let us fall into the hands of the Lord, and not into the hands of men; for as is His greatness, so also is His mercy.

## Translator Notes

- **2:9** — Literally "for gladness of eternity and of mercy," where both genitives depend on "gladness" in the Greek.
- **2:14** — The verb "visits" (episkeptetai) carries the biblical sense of God's decisive inspection and judgment.
- **2:18** — The Greek uses the future indicative ("We will fall") with a cohortative force, echoing David's words in 2 Samuel 24:14.
