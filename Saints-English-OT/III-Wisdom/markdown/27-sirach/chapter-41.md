## Text

**41:1** O death, how bitter is the remembrance of you to a man who lives in peace among his possessions, to a man free from distraction and prospering in all things, and still strong enough to receive food!

**41:2** O death, good is your decree to a man who is needy and diminishing in strength, in extreme old age and anxious about all things, who is defiant and has lost endurance.

**41:3** Do not dread the decree of death; remember those who went before you and those who come after. This is the decree from the Lord for all flesh,

**41:4** and why do you refuse the good pleasure of the Most High? Whether ten, or a hundred, or a thousand years, there is no reckoning of life in Hades.

**41:5** The children of sinners become abominable children, and frequent the habitations of the ungodly.

**41:6** The inheritance of the children of sinners shall perish, and with their offspring reproach shall continually abide.

**41:7** Children will blame an ungodly father, because on account of him they will suffer reproach.

**41:8** Woe to you, ungodly men, who have forsaken the law of the Most High God!

**41:9** And if you are born, to a curse you shall be born; and if you die, to a curse you shall be apportioned.

**41:10** All things that come from the earth shall go back to the earth; so the ungodly go from a curse to destruction.

**41:11** The mourning of men is over their bodies, but the name of sinners, which is not good, shall be blotted out.

**41:12** Take care for your name, for it shall endure for you longer than a thousand great treasures of gold.

**41:13** A good life has its number of days, but a good name shall endure forever.

**41:14** Keep instruction in peace, O children; yet hidden wisdom and unseen treasure—what profit is in either of them?

**41:15** Better is a man who hides his folly than a man who hides his wisdom.

**41:16** Therefore have a proper sense of shame at my word; for it is not good to retain every kind of shame, and not all things are faithfully approved by all.

**41:17** Be ashamed before father and mother of fornication, and before a ruler and a mighty man of a lie,

**41:19** before a judge and a prince of a trespass, before an assembly and a people of lawlessness, before a partner and a friend of injustice, and before the place where you sojourn of theft,

**41:20** and before the truth of God and the covenant, of leaning an elbow upon the bread, of scornful dealing in receiving and giving,

**41:21** of silence before those who greet you, of gazing upon a harlot,

**41:23** of turning away the face of a kinsman, of taking away a portion and a gift, of looking intently upon a married woman,

**41:25** of meddling with his maidservant—and do not approach her bed; before friends of words of reproach—and after you have given, do not reproach;

**41:27** of repeating and uttering a word you have heard, and of disclosing secret words. Then you shall be truly modest, and you shall find favor before every man.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

## Translator Notes

- **41:4** — The Greek word elegmos ('reckoning' or 'reproof') conveys that in Hades no one disputes or calls to account the length of a mortal life.
- **41:9** — The Greek text reads gennethete ('be born'), rather than the Hebrew reading 'increase' or 'multiply,' portraying the entire existence of the ungodly from birth to death as allotted to a curse.
- **41:20** — Leaning an elbow upon the bread reflects a breach of table etiquette, while skorakismos ('scornful dealing') denotes contemptuous or insolent behavior in financial exchanges.
- **41:23 *(additional)*** — Parallels the opening clauses of 41:27 in the site numbering.
