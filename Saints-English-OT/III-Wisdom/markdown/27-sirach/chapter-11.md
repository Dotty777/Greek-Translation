## Text

**11:1** The wisdom of the humble lifts up his head, and shall seat him in the midst of great men.

**11:2** Do not praise a man for his beauty, and do not abhor a man for his appearance.

**11:3** Small among flying creatures is the bee, yet the chief of sweet things is her fruit.

**11:4** Do not boast in the wearing of garments, and in the day of glory do not exalt yourself; for wondrous are the works of the Lord, and hidden are His works among men.

**11:5** Many rulers have sat upon the ground, while one never thought of has worn a diadem.

**11:6** Many mighty men have been exceedingly dishonored, and men of renown have been delivered into the hands of companions.

**11:7** Before you examine, do not find fault; consider first, and then rebuke.

**11:8** Do not answer before you have heard, and in the midst of words do not interrupt.

**11:9** Do not contend concerning a matter that does not concern you, and in the judgment of sinners do not sit in council.

**11:10** Child, do not let your undertakings be about many things; if you multiply them, you shall not be held guiltless; and if you pursue, you shall by no means overtake, nor shall you escape by fleeing.

**11:11** There is one who labors and toils and hastens, and is all the more in want.

**11:12** There is one who is sluggish and in need of help, lacking in strength and abounding in poverty; yet the eyes of the Lord looked upon him for good, and He raised him up from his low estate,

**11:13** and He lifted up his head, and many marveled at him.

**11:14** Good things and evil, life and death, poverty and riches are from the Lord.

**11:17** The gift of the Lord abides with the godly, and His good pleasure shall prosper forever.

**11:18** There is one who grows rich through his carefulness and stinginess, and this is the portion of his reward:

**11:19** When he says, "I have found rest, and now I will eat of my good things," he does not know what time will pass, and he shall leave them to others and die.

**11:20** Stand firm in your covenant and attend to it, and grow old in your work.

**11:21** Do not marvel at the works of a sinner; trust in the Lord and abide in your toil; for it is an easy thing in the eyes of the Lord swiftly and suddenly to make a poor man rich.

**11:22** The blessing of the Lord is in the reward of the godly, and in a swift hour He causes His blessing to flourish.

**11:23** Do not say, "What is my need, and what good things shall be mine from now on?"

**11:24** Do not say, "I have enough, and what harm shall I suffer from now on?"

**11:25** In the day of prosperity there is forgetfulness of adversity, and in the day of adversity prosperity will not be remembered;

**11:26** for it is an easy thing before the Lord in the day of death to repay a man according to his ways.

**11:27** The affliction of an hour causes forgetfulness of delight, and at the end of a man is the revelation of his works.

**11:28** Before his death do not call anyone blessed, and by his children a man shall be known.

**11:29** Do not bring every man into your house, for many are the snares of the deceitful.

**11:30** A decoy partridge in a basket—so is the heart of the proud, and like a spy he watches for a downfall.

**11:31** For turning good things into evil he lies in wait, and upon admirable things he will lay blame.

**11:32** From a spark of fire a heap of burning coals is multiplied, and a sinful man lies in wait for blood.

**11:33** Beware of an evildoer, for he contrives wicked things, lest he bring upon you a blemish forever.

**11:34** Lodge a stranger in your house, and he will overturn you with troubles and estrange you from your own.

## Translator Notes

- **11:6** — The Greek critical text reads "companions" (hetairon), whereas some manuscripts read "others" (heteron).
- **11:17** — Verses 15 and 16 are absent from the main Greek critical text (appearing only in later expanded witnesses), so the verse numbering moves directly from verse 14 to verse 17.
- **11:20** — Greek diatheke ("covenant") here reflects the Hebrew sense of one's allotted obligation or appointed task.
- **11:30** — A "decoy partridge in a basket" refers to a caged bird used by hunters to lure other birds into a trap.
