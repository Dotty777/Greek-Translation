## Text

**7:1** Do not do evil deeds, and evil shall by no means overtake you.

**7:2** Depart from injustice, and it shall turn aside from you.

**7:3** Son, do not sow upon the furrows of unrighteousness, and you shall by no means reap them sevenfold.

**7:4** Do not seek authority from the Lord, nor a seat of honor from a king.

**7:5** Do not justify yourself before the Lord, and do not pretend to be wise before a king.

**7:6** Do not seek to become a judge, lest you lack the strength to remove injustices; lest you shrink in fear before the presence of a mighty man, and place a stumbling block in your uprightness.

**7:7** Do not sin against the multitude of a city, and do not cast yourself down in a crowd.

**7:8** Do not bind up sin a second time, for even in one you shall not be guiltless.

**7:9** Do not say, "He will look upon the multitude of my gifts, and when I bring an offering to God Most High, He will accept it."

**7:10** Do not be fainthearted in your prayer, and do not neglect to give alms.

**7:11** Do not mock a man who is in the bitterness of his soul, for there is One who humbles and exalts.

**7:12** Do not plow falsehood against your brother, nor do the same to a friend.

**7:13** Do not desire to utter any falsehood, for its continual practice is not for good.

**7:14** Do not prattle in the assembly of elders, and do not repeat a word in your prayer.

**7:15** Do not hate toilsome labor and farming, which was created by the Most High.

**7:16** Do not reckon yourself among the multitude of sinners.

**7:17** Humble your soul exceedingly. Remember that wrath will not delay, for the vengeance upon the ungodly is fire and the worm.

**7:18** Do not exchange a friend for the sake of money, nor a true brother for the gold of Souphir.

**7:19** Do not forgo a wise and good wife, for her grace is above gold.

**7:20** Do not mistreat a servant who labors in truth, nor a hired laborer who gives his soul.

**7:21** Let your soul love a good servant; do not deprive him of freedom.

**7:22** Do you have livestock? Look after them, and if they are useful to you, let them remain with you.

**7:23** Do you have children? Discipline them, and bend their neck from youth.

**7:24** Do you have daughters? Watch over their body, and do not make your countenance cheerful toward them.

**7:25** Give a daughter in marriage, and you will have accomplished a great work; and bestow her upon a man of understanding.

**7:26** Do you have a wife dear to your soul? Do not cast her out.

**7:27** With all your heart honor your father, and do not forget the birth pangs of your mother.

**7:28** Remember that through them you were born, and what will you repay them as they have done for you?

**7:29** With all your soul revere the Lord, and hold His priests in honor.

**7:30** With all your strength love Him who made you, and do not forsake His ministers.

**7:31** Fear the Lord and honor the priest, and give him his portion as you have been commanded from the beginning: the offering for trespass, and the gift of the shoulders, and the sacrifice of sanctification, and the firstfruits.

**7:32** Extend your hand also to the poor, that your blessing may be made complete.

**7:33** Let the grace of a gift be before every living person, and do not withhold grace from the dead.

**7:34** Do not hold back from those who weep, and mourn with those who mourn.

**7:35** Do not hesitate to visit a sick person, for by such deeds you shall be loved.

**7:36** In all your words remember your final end, and you shall never sin.

## Translator Notes

- **7:17** — In this Greek text, the warning "Remember that wrath will not delay" stands in the middle of verse 17 rather than at the end of verse 16 as in some editions.
- **7:18** — "Money" translates Greek adiaphoron (literally "an indifferent thing," used here for material gain); "Souphir" preserves the Septuagint form of Ophir.
- **7:31** — This Greek text reads "from the beginning" (ap' arches) before listing the priestly portions prescribed in the Law, including the "gift of the shoulders" (cf. Deut 18:3).
- **7:36** — The Greek text reads "in all your words" (logois), whereas some textual witnesses and the Hebrew tradition read "works" or "ways."
