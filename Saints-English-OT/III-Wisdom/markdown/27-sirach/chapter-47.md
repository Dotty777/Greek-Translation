## Text

**47:1** And after him Nathan arose to prophesy in the days of David.

**47:2** As the fat set apart from the peace offering, so was David from the sons of Israel.

**47:3** He played with lions as with young goats, and with bears as with lambs of the flock.

**47:4** In his youth did he not slay a giant, and remove reproach from the people, when he lifted up his hand with a slingstone and cast down the boasting of Goliath?

**47:5** For he called upon the Lord, the Most High, and He gave strength to his right hand to strike down a man in war; He shall exalt the horn of His people.

**47:6** Thus He glorified him for ten thousands, and praised him with the blessings of the Lord, when a diadem of glory was brought to him.

**47:7** For he crushed enemies on every side, and brought to nothing the Philistines, the adversaries; even to this day he shattered their horn.

**47:8** In every work of his he gave thanksgiving to the Holy One, the Most High, with a word of glory; with all his heart he sang praises and loved Him who made him.

**47:9** And he placed psalm-singers before the altar, to make sweet melodies by their sound.

**47:10** He gave splendor to the feasts and adorned the appointed seasons to perfection, while they praised His holy name and the sanctuary resounded from early morning.

**47:11** The Lord took away his sins and exalted his horn forever, and gave him a covenant of kings and a throne of glory in Israel.

**47:12** After him arose a son of understanding, and for his sake he dwelt in a broad place.

**47:13** Solomon reigned in days of peace, to whom God gave rest on every side, that he might establish a house in His name and prepare a sanctuary forever.

**47:14** How wise you became in your youth, and you were filled like a river with understanding!

**47:15** Your soul covered the earth, and you filled it with parables of riddles.

**47:16** Your name reached distant islands, and you were beloved in your peace.

**47:17** For songs and proverbs and parables, and for interpretation, the lands marveled at you.

**47:18** In the name of the Lord God, who is called the God of Israel, you gathered gold like tin, and you multiplied silver like lead.

**47:19** You laid your loins beside women, and you were brought under dominion in your body.

**47:20** You brought a blemish upon your glory and profaned your seed, so as to bring wrath upon your children, and I was grieved over your folly,

**47:21** so that the sovereignty was divided in two, and out of Ephraim a disobedient kingdom began to rule.

**47:22** Yet the Lord will by no means forsake His mercy, nor shall any of His works perish; neither will He blot out the descendants of His chosen one, nor will He take away the seed of him who loved Him; and to Jacob He gave a remnant, and to David a root from himself.

**47:23** And Solomon rested with the fathers, and left after him from his seed the folly of the people and one lacking in understanding, Rehoboam, who caused the people to revolt by his counsel; and Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin and gave Ephraim a way of sin.

**47:24** And their sins were multiplied exceedingly, so as to remove them from their land;

**47:25** and they sought out every wickedness, until vengeance should come upon them.

## Translator Notes

- **47:2** — "Peace offering" renders Greek σωτηρίου, the standard Septuagint term for the fellowship or peace offering (cf. Leviticus 3:1–5).
- **47:6** — Major Greek witnesses read singular verbs ("He glorified... and praised"), whereas other witnesses read plural ("they glorified... and praised"), referring to the people honoring David's ten thousands (cf. 1 Samuel 18:7).
- **47:20** — Major Greek witnesses read the first-person verb κατενύγην ("I was grieved"), expressing the author's own lament over Solomon's folly, while other witnesses read the infinitive κατανυγῆναι ("to be grieved").
