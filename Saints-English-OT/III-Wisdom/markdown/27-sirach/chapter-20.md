## Text

**20:1** There is a reproof that is not timely, and there is one who keeps silent, and he is prudent.

**20:2** How much better it is to reprove than to be angry,

**20:3** and he who confesses shall be kept back from loss.

**20:4** Like the lust of a eunuch to deflower a maiden, so is he who executes judgments by violence.

**20:5** There is one who keeps silent and is found wise, and there is one who is hated for much speaking.

**20:6** There is one who keeps silent, for he has no answer; and there is one who keeps silent, knowing the proper time.

**20:7** A wise man will keep silent until the proper time, but the braggart and the fool will overstep the time.

**20:8** He who multiplies words shall be abhorred, and he who takes authority upon himself shall be hated.

**20:9** There is success for a man amid misfortunes, and there is a windfall that turns to loss.

**20:10** There is a gift that will not profit you, and there is a gift whose recompense is twofold.

**20:11** There is loss on account of glory, and there is one who from lowliness has lifted up his head.

**20:12** There is one who buys much for little, yet pays for it sevenfold.

**20:13** The wise man will make himself beloved by his speech, but the favors of fools shall be poured out.

**20:14** The gift of a fool will not profit you, for his eyes are many in place of one.

**20:15** He will give little and reproach much, and he will open his mouth like a herald; today he will lend, and tomorrow he will demand it back—such a man is hateful.

**20:16** A fool will say, "I have no friend, and there is no gratitude for my good deeds; those who eat my bread are wicked of tongue."

**20:17** How often, and how many, will laugh him to scorn?

**20:18** A slip on the pavement is better than a slip of the tongue; so shall the downfall of the wicked come swiftly.

**20:19** An ungracious man is an untimely tale; it will be continually in the mouth of the undisciplined.

**20:20** A proverb from the mouth of a fool shall be rejected, for he will not speak it in its season.

**20:21** There is one who is kept from sinning by want, and in his rest he shall not be pierced with remorse.

**20:22** There is one who destroys his own soul through shame, and because of a foolish person he will destroy it.

**20:23** There is one who out of shame makes a promise to a friend, and he gains him as an enemy for nothing.

**20:24** A lie is a foul blemish in a man; it will be continually in the mouth of the undisciplined.

**20:25** A thief is preferable to one who continually lies, yet both shall inherit destruction.

**20:26** The character of a lying man is dishonor, and his shame is with him continually.

**20:27** Words of proverbs. The wise man will advance himself by words, and a prudent man will please great men.

**20:28** He who tills the ground shall raise high his heap of grain, and he who pleases great men shall atone for wrongdoing.

**20:29** Presents and gifts blind the eyes of the wise, and like a muzzle in the mouth, they turn aside reproofs.

**20:30** Hidden wisdom and unseen treasure—what profit is there in either of them?

**20:31** Better is a man who conceals his folly than a man who conceals his wisdom.

## Translator Notes

- **20:14** — The idiom "his eyes are many in place of one" indicates that the fool gives grudgingly while expecting many favors in return for a single gift.
- **20:22** — "Shame" here denotes misplaced bashfulness or fear of losing face; "because of a foolish person" translates the Greek idiom "from a foolish face," meaning out of undue deference to a fool.
- **20:27** — "Words of proverbs" is an ancient internal section heading preserved directly in the Greek text.
