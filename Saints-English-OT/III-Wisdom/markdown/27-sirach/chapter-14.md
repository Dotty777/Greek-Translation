## Text

**14:1** Blessed is the man who has not slipped with his mouth, and has not been pierced with sorrow for sin.

**14:2** Blessed is he whose own soul does not condemn him, and who has not fallen from his hope.

**14:3** Wealth is not fitting for a stingy man, and of what use are riches to an envious man?

**14:4** He who gathers by denying his own soul gathers for others, and others will revel in his good things.

**14:5** He who is wicked to himself, to whom will he be good? And he shall by no means rejoice in his riches.

**14:6** There is no one worse than he who begrudges himself, and this is the repayment of his own wickedness.

**14:7** Even if he does good, he does it by oversight, and in the end he reveals his wickedness.

**14:8** Wicked is the man who grudges with his eye, turning away his face and despising souls.

**14:9** The eye of a covetous man is not satisfied with a portion, and wicked injustice shall dry up the soul.

**14:10** An evil eye is grudging over bread, and goes wanting at his own table.

**14:11** Child, according to what you have, do good to yourself, and bring worthy offerings to the Lord.

**14:12** Remember that death will not delay, and the covenant of Hades has not been shown to you.

**14:13** Before you die, do good to a friend, and according to your ability reach out and give to him.

**14:14** Do not deprive yourself of a good day, and let no share of a worthy desire pass you by.

**14:15** Will you not leave your toils to another, and your labors to be divided by lot?

**14:16** Give and receive, and indulge your soul, for in Hades there is no seeking of luxury.

**14:17** All flesh grows old like a garment; for the covenant from of old is, "You shall surely die."

**14:18** Like flourishing foliage on a leafy tree—some it casts down, and others it brings forth—so is a generation of flesh and blood: one dies, and another is born.

**14:19** Every decaying work passes away, and he who fashions it shall depart with it.

**14:20** Blessed is the man who shall die in wisdom, and who shall reason in his understanding.

**14:21** He who ponders her ways in his heart shall also gain insight into her hidden things.

**14:22** Go forth after her like a tracker, and lie in wait at her entryways.

**14:23** He who peers through her windows will also listen at her doorways.

**14:24** He who lodges close by her house will also drive a tent peg into her walls.

**14:25** He shall pitch his tent right beside her, and he shall lodge in a lodging place of good things.

**14:26** He shall place his children in her shelter, and under her branches he shall abide.

**14:27** He shall be sheltered by her from the scorching heat, and in her glory he shall dwell.

## Translator Notes

- **14:12** — Greek diatheke ("covenant," also in 14:17) here denotes an appointed decree or fixed term set by Hades.
- **14:16** — Greek apateson (literally "beguile") is used here in the sense of indulging or treating oneself, reflecting the Hebrew sense of pampering oneself.
- **14:20** — The critical Greek text reads teleutesei ("shall die"), whereas the Hebrew text and some Greek witnesses read "meditates" (meletesei).
