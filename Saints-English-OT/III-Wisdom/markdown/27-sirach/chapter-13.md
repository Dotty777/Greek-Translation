## Text

**13:1** He who touches pitch shall be defiled, and he who keeps company with a proud man shall become like him.

**13:2** Do not lift a burden beyond you, and do not keep company with one stronger and wealthier than yourself. What fellowship shall an earthen pot have with a cauldron? The pot will strike against it, and it will be shattered.

**13:3** A rich man has done wrong, and moreover he fumes with indignation; a poor man has been wronged, and moreover he must plead.

**13:4** If you are useful, he will make use of you; and if you come to want, he will forsake you.

**13:5** If you have possessions, he will live with you and empty you out, and he himself will not be troubled.

**13:6** He has need of you, and he will lead you astray; he will smile upon you and give you hope; he will speak fair words to you and say, "What is your need?"

**13:7** And he will put you to shame with his feasts until he has emptied you out twice or three times, and in the end he will mock you; afterward he will see you and forsake you, and he will shake his head over you.

**13:8** Take heed lest you be led astray, and do not be brought low in your gladness.

**13:9** When a mighty man summons you, hold yourself back, and so much the more he will summon you.

**13:10** Do not press forward, lest you be thrust away, and do not stand far off, lest you be forgotten.

**13:11** Do not presume to speak as an equal with him, and do not trust his many words; for with much talk he will test you, and as though smiling upon you he will examine you.

**13:12** Merciless is he who does not guard words, and he will by no means spare you from affliction and chains.

**13:13** Guard yourself and take great heed, for you walk alongside your own downfall.

**13:15** Every living creature loves its own kind, and every man his neighbor.

**13:16** All flesh gathers together according to its kind, and a man will cling to one like himself.

**13:17** What fellowship shall a wolf have with a lamb? So is a sinner with a godly man.

**13:18** What peace is there between a hyena and a dog? And what peace is there between a rich man and a poor man?

**13:19** Wild donkeys in the wilderness are the prey of lions; so the poor are the pasture of the rich.

**13:20** Humility is an abomination to the proud; so is a poor man an abomination to the rich.

**13:21** When a rich man totters, he is steadied by friends; but when a humble man falls, he is thrust further away by friends.

**13:22** When a rich man slips, there are many helpers; he speaks things that ought not to be spoken, and they justify him. A humble man slips, and they moreover rebuke him; he utters understanding, and no place is given to him.

**13:23** A rich man speaks, and all keep silence, and they exalt his word to the clouds; a poor man speaks, and they say, "Who is this?" And if he stumbles, they will overthrow him besides.

**13:24** Wealth is good in which there is no sin, and poverty is evil in the mouth of a godly man.

**13:25** The heart of a man alters his countenance, whether for good or for evil.

**13:26** The mark of a heart in good fortune is a cheerful countenance, and the finding of proverbs is thought with toil.

## Translator Notes

- **13:8** — The Greek text reads "in your gladness" (en euphrosyne sou), whereas the Hebrew text reads "in your folly."
- **13:12** — "He who does not guard words" refers to a powerful ruler who mercilessly fails to keep spoken words in confidence and uses them to punish his guest.
- **13:24** — The critical Greek text reads "in the mouths of a godly man" (en stomasin eusebous), whereas some Greek manuscripts and the Hebrew text read "of the ungodly."
