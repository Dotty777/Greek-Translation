## Text

**43:1** The pride of the height is the firmament of purity, the appearance of heaven in a vision of glory.

**43:2** The sun in its appearing, proclaiming at its going forth, is a marvelous vessel, the work of the Most High.

**43:3** At its midday it parches the land, and before its burning heat who shall stand?

**43:4** One tending a furnace is in works of heat, yet threefold does the sun burn up the mountains; breathing forth fiery vapors, and flashing forth rays, it dims the eyes.

**43:5** Great is the Lord who made it, and by His words He hastened its course.

**43:6** And the moon also is in all things for its season, a showing forth of times and a sign of the age.

**43:7** From the moon is the sign of the feast, a luminary waning at its completion.

**43:8** The month is according to its name, increasing marvelously in its changing; a vessel of parables in the height, shining forth in the firmament of heaven.

**43:9** The beauty of heaven, the glory of the stars, an illuminating adornment; in the highest places is the Lord.

**43:10** By holy words they shall stand according to decree, and they shall never grow faint in their watches.

**43:11** Look upon the rainbow, and bless Him who made it, exceedingly fair in its radiance.

**43:12** It has encircled heaven in a circuit of glory; the hands of the Most High stretched it out.

**43:13** By His command He caused the snow to rest, and He hastens the lightnings of His judgment.

**43:14** Therefore the storehouses were opened, and clouds flew forth like birds.

**43:15** In His majesty He strengthened the clouds, and the hailstones were broken in pieces.

**43:16** And at His appearings the mountains shall be shaken; at His will the south wind shall blow.

**43:17** The voice of His thunder reproached the earth, as did the tempest of the north and the whirling of the wind. Like birds flying down, He scatters the snow, and its descent is like the locust settling down.

**43:18** At the beauty of its whiteness the eye shall marvel, and at its shower the heart shall be astonished.

**43:19** Hoarfrost also He pours out like salt upon the earth, and when it is frozen, it becomes points of thorns.

**43:20** The cold north wind shall blow, and ice shall be congealed from the water; upon every gathering of water it shall abide, and the water shall clothe itself as with a breastplate.

**43:21** It shall devour the mountains and burn up the wilderness, and extinguish the green grass like fire.

**43:22** A mist is a speedy healing of all things; dew coming after the scorching heat shall bring cheer.

**43:23** By His counsel He stilled the deep, and Jesus planted it.

**43:24** Those who sail the sea tell of its peril, and at the hearing of our ears we marvel.

**43:25** And there are the strange and wondrous works, a variety of every living creature, the creation of sea monsters.

**43:26** Because of Him His end is a sweet fragrance, and by His word all things consist.

**43:27** We shall say many things, and yet never reach the end; and the sum of our words is, "He is the all."

**43:28** In glorifying Him, where shall we find strength? For He Himself is great above all His works.

**43:29** Awesome is the Lord and exceedingly great, and marvelous is His power.

**43:30** As you glorify the Lord, exalt Him as much as you are able, for He shall surpass even that; and as you exalt Him, increase in strength; do not grow weary, for you shall never reach the end.

**43:31** Who has seen Him and shall declare Him? And who shall magnify Him as He is?

**43:32** Many hidden things are greater than these, for we have seen but a few of His works.

**43:33** For the Lord has made all things, and to the godly He has given wisdom.

## Translator Notes

- **43:8** — The Greek text reads parabolon ("of parables/comparisons"), where other Greek manuscripts read parembolon ("of armies/encampments").
- **43:13** — The Greek text reads katepausen ("caused to rest/stilled"), whereas other manuscripts read katespeusen ("hastened").
- **43:17** — The Greek text reads oneidisen ("reproached"), where other manuscripts read odinesen ("caused to travail").
- **43:23** — The Greek text reads ephuteusen auten Iesous ("Jesus planted it"), a notable scribal variant in major Greek uncials for ephuteusen en aute nesous ("He planted islands in it").
- **43:26** — The Greek text reads euodia ("sweet fragrance"), a manuscript spelling variant for euodia ("prosperous journey/success").
