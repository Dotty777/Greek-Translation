## Text

**38:1** Honor a physician according to the need of him with his due honors, for the Lord indeed created him;

**38:2** for healing comes from the Most High, and from a king he shall receive a gift.

**38:3** The skill of a physician shall lift up his head, and in the presence of great men he shall be held in wonder.

**38:4** The Lord created medicines out of the earth, and a prudent man will not loathe them.

**38:5** Was not water made sweet by wood, that His power might be made known?

**38:6** And He Himself gave men knowledge, to be glorified in His wondrous works;

**38:7** by them he heals and removes his pain,

**38:8** with these the apothecary will make a compound; and He will never bring His works to an end, and peace from Him is upon the face of the earth.

**38:9** Child, in your sickness do not be negligent, but pray to the Lord, and He Himself will heal you;

**38:10** put away transgression and make your hands upright, and cleanse your heart from all sin;

**38:11** offer a sweet aroma and a memorial of fine flour, and make the offering rich, as one who is no more.

**38:12** And give place to the physician, for the Lord indeed created him; and let him not depart from you, for indeed there is need of him.

**38:13** There is a time when success is indeed in their hands;

**38:14** for they also will pray to the Lord, that He may prosper for them relief and healing for the preservation of life.

**38:15** He who sins before Him who made him—may he fall into the hands of a physician!

**38:16** Child, let tears fall over the dead, and as one suffering grievously begin a lament; and according to his due shroud his body, and do not neglect his burial.

**38:17** Make your weeping bitter and your wailing fervent, and observe mourning according to his worth for one day and two because of slander, and then be comforted on account of grief;

**38:18** for out of grief comes death, and grief of heart will bow down strength.

**38:19** In affliction grief also goes alongside, and the life of a poor man is against the heart.

**38:20** Do not give your heart over to grief; put it away, remembering the end.

**38:21** Do not forget, for there is no returning; you will do him no good, and you will harm yourself.

**38:22** Remember that his sentence is even as yours: "For me yesterday, and for you today."

**38:23** At the resting of the dead, let his remembrance rest, and be comforted concerning him at the departure of his spirit.

**38:24** The wisdom of a scribe comes through opportunity of leisure, and he who has less of business shall become wise.

**38:25** How shall he become wise who holds the plow and boasts in the shaft of the goad, driving oxen and occupied in their works, while their discourse is of young bulls?

**38:26** He will set his heart to turn furrows, and his sleepless care is for the fodder of heifers.

**38:27** So also is every craftsman and master builder, who passes the night as the day; so are those who engrave the carvings of signets, and his perseverance is to vary the intricate pattern; he will set his heart to make a lifelike painting, and his sleepless care is to complete the work.

**38:28** So also is the smith sitting near the anvil and studying the unwrought iron; the vapor of the fire will harden his flesh, and in the heat of the furnace he will contend; the sound of the hammer will continually renew his ear, and his eyes are upon the pattern of the vessel; he will set his heart to finish his works, and his sleepless care is to adorn them to completion.

**38:29** So also is the potter sitting at his work and turning the wheel with his feet, who is continually burdened with anxiety over his work, and all his labor is counted by number;

**38:30** with his arm he will fashion the clay, and before his feet he will bend its stiffness; he will apply his heart to finish the gift, and his sleepless care is to cleanse the kiln.

**38:31** All these have put their trust in their hands, and each one is wise in his own work.

**38:32** Without them a city shall not be inhabited, nor shall men sojourn or walk about;

**38:33** and in the assembly they shall not rise to eminence, nor shall they understand the covenant of judgment; they shall not sit upon the judge's seat, nor shall they declare righteousness and judgment, and among parables they shall not be found;

**38:34** but they shall uphold the fabric of the age, and their prayer is in the practice of their craft. Not so with him who devotes his soul and meditates in the law of the Most High,

## Translator Notes

- **38:8** — The subject of συντελέσῃ ("will bring to an end") may be understood either as God, whose healing works never cease, or as the apothecary, who can never exhaust God's works.
- **38:11** — Greek ὡς μὴ ὑπάρχων literally means "as one who is no more," urging a generous offering as though facing death rather than clinging to worldly possessions.
- **38:19** — The Greek phrase ἐν ἀπαγωγῇ παραβαίνει καὶ λύπη is difficult; ἀπαγωγή ("carrying away" or "affliction") and παραβαίνει ("goes alongside" or "passes by") depict grief accompanying calamity, while κατὰ καρδίας expresses what weighs against the heart.
- **38:28** — Greek καινιεῖ literally means "will renew," describing the hammer's sound ringing perpetually anew in the smith's ear, though some textual witnesses and commentators understand a verb meaning "to deafen."
- **38:30** — The Greek text reads τὸ χάρισμα ("the gift"), a reading found in major early manuscripts, whereas another textual tradition reads τὸ χρίσμα ("the glaze").
