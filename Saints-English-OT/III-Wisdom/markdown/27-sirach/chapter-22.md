## Text

**22:1** A slothful man is compared to a filthy stone, and everyone will hiss at his disgrace.

**22:2** A slothful man is compared to a lump of dung from a dunghill; everyone who picks it up will shake off his hand.

**22:3** A father's shame is in the birth of an undisciplined son, and a daughter is born to his loss.

**22:4** A prudent daughter will obtain a husband for herself, but she who brings shame is a grief to the one who fathered her.

**22:5** A shameless woman brings shame upon both father and husband, and she will be dishonored by both.

**22:6** Like music in mourning is an untimely tale, but scourges and discipline belong to wisdom at every season.

**22:9** He who teaches a fool is gluing together a potsherd, and rousing a sleeper from a deep sleep.

**22:10** He who tells a tale to a fool tells it to one who is drowsy, and at the conclusion he will say, "What is it?"

**22:11** Weep over the dead, for light has failed him; and weep over a fool, for understanding has failed him. Weep more gently over the dead, for he has found rest; but the life of the fool is worse than death.

**22:12** Mourning for the dead is seven days, but for a fool and an ungodly man it is all the days of his life.

**22:13** Do not multiply words with a fool, and do not go to one who lacks understanding. Guard yourself against him, lest you suffer trouble, and you shall not be defiled by his spattering; turn aside from him and you shall find rest, and you shall not be wearied by his senselessness.

**22:14** What is heavier than lead? And what is its name other than a fool?

**22:15** Sand and salt and a mass of iron are easier to bear than a man without understanding.

**22:16** Timber bracing bound into a building will not be loosened in an earthquake; so a heart established upon considered counsel will not grow fearful when the time comes.

**22:17** A heart settled upon thoughtful understanding is like stucco ornament upon a polished wall.

**22:18** Stakes set upon a high place will not stand firm against the wind; so a fearful heart set upon a foolish thought will not stand firm against any terror.

**22:19** He who pricks an eye brings forth tears, and he who pricks a heart reveals its feeling.

**22:20** He who casts a stone at birds drives them away, and he who reproaches a friend will dissolve friendship.

**22:21** If you draw a sword against a friend, do not despair, for there is a way of return.

**22:22** If you open your mouth against a friend, do not be afraid, for there is reconciliation—except for reproach, and arrogance, and the revealing of a secret, and a treacherous blow; from these things every friend will flee away.

**22:23** Gain the trust of your neighbor in his poverty, that you may be filled together with him in his prosperity; remain steadfast with him in a time of affliction, that you may share with him in his inheritance.

**22:24** Before fire come the vapor of a furnace and smoke; so before bloodshed come revilings.

**22:25** I will not be ashamed to shelter a friend, and I will not hide myself from his presence;

**22:26** and if evil things befall me on his account, everyone who hears of it will beware of him.

**22:27** Who will set a guard over my mouth, and a prudent seal upon my lips, that I may not fall through it, and that my tongue may not destroy me?

## Translator Notes

- **22:9** — Verses 22:7–8 are absent from the primary Greek text tradition (G-I) and appear only in later expanded witnesses.
- **22:13** — Greek entinagmos ("spattering" or "shaking off") vividly recalls the image of shaking filth off the hand in 22:2.
- **22:17** — Greek kosmos psammotos refers to a sand-plaster or stucco adornment applied to a polished wall, illustrating durable inner steadiness.
