## Text

**30:1** Concerning children: He who loves his son will continually apply lashes to him, that he may rejoice in his latter end.

**30:2** He who disciplines his son will profit by him, and in the midst of acquaintances he will boast of him.

**30:3** He who teaches his son will provoke the enemy to jealousy, and before friends he will exult in him.

**30:4** His father has died, and it is as though he has not died, for he has left behind one like himself after him.

**30:5** In his lifetime he saw and rejoiced, and at his death he was not grieved;

**30:6** against enemies he left behind an avenger, and to friends one who repays favor.

**30:7** Concerning the lives of sons he will bind up his wounds, and at every cry his inward parts will be troubled.

**30:8** An untamed horse turns out stubborn, and an unrestrained son turns out headstrong.

**30:9** Pamper a child, and he will terrify you; play with him, and he will grieve you.

**30:10** Do not laugh with him, lest you suffer grief with him, and in the end you will gnash your teeth.

**30:11** Do not give him authority in youth.

**30:12** Bruise his sides while he is a young child, lest he become hardened and disobey you.

**30:13** Discipline your son and labor with him, lest he stumble in your disgrace.

**30:14** Better is a poor man who is healthy and strong in constitution than a rich man scourged in his body.

**30:15** Health and vigor are better than all gold, and a strong body than measureless wealth.

**30:16** There is no wealth better than health of body, and there is no gladness above joy of heart.

**30:17** Better is death than a bitter life or a lingering sickness.

**30:18** Good things poured out upon a closed mouth are like offerings of food set upon a grave.

**30:19** What does a burnt offering profit an idol? For it will neither eat nor smell; so is he who is pursued by the Lord,

**30:20** seeing with his eyes and groaning, like a eunuch embracing a virgin and groaning.

**30:21** Do not give your soul over to sorrow, and do not afflict yourself by your own counsel.

**30:22** Gladness of heart is the life of a man, and the exultation of a man is length of days.

**30:23** Love your own soul and comfort your heart, and put sorrow far away from you; for sorrow has slain many, and there is no profit in it.

**30:24** Jealousy and wrath shorten days, and anxiety brings old age before its time.

**30:25** A cheerful and good heart will attend to its food at meals.

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — The opening heading "Concerning children" translates an ancient Greek superscription preserved in the manuscript tradition of Sirach.
- **30:7** — The Greek text here reads "concerning the lives of sons" (as in Codex Vaticanus); other Greek witnesses divide and read the letters as "he who pampers a son."
- **30:13** — The Greek text here reads "lest he stumble in your disgrace"; other Greek manuscripts read "lest you stumble at his disgrace."
- **30:25** — Literally "a bright and good heart will care for its foods at meals," signifying that a cheerful disposition takes healthy nourishment from its food.
