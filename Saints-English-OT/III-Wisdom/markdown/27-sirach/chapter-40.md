## Text

**40:1** Great travail has been created for every man, and a heavy yoke is upon the sons of Adam, from the day of their coming forth from their mother's womb until the day of burial into the mother of all:

**40:2** their anxious thoughts and fear of heart, the brooding of expectation, the day of death—

**40:3** from him who sits upon a throne in glory, even to him who is humbled in earth and ashes,

**40:4** from him who wears blue-purple and a crown, even to him who is wrapped in coarse linen—

**40:5** wrath and jealousy, and turmoil and unrest, and fear of death, and resentment, and strife. And in the time of rest upon his bed, the sleep of night alters his mind.

**40:6** Little, as nothing, is he at rest; and afterward in his sleep he is as on a day of keeping watch, troubled in the vision of his heart, like one who has escaped from the face of battle.

**40:7** At the moment of his deliverance he awakes, marveling that there was nothing to fear.

**40:8** So it is with all flesh, from man to beast, and upon sinners sevenfold more than these:

**40:9** death and bloodshed, and strife and the sword;

**40:10** against the lawless all these things were created, and because of them the flood came to pass.

**40:11** All things that are from the earth return to the earth, and what is from the waters turns back to the sea.

**40:12** Every bribe and injustice shall be blotted out, and faithfulness shall stand forever.

**40:13** The wealth of the unrighteous shall dry up like a river, and like great thunder in rain it shall resound.

**40:14** When he opens his hands, he shall rejoice; even so transgressors shall utterly fail.

**40:15** The offspring of the ungodly shall not multiply branches, and unclean roots are upon a sheer rock.

**40:16** The reed by every water and riverbank shall be plucked up before all grass.

**40:17** Kindness is like a paradise in blessings, and almsgiving shall abide forever.

**40:18** The life of a self-sufficient laborer shall be made sweet, yet above both is he who finds a treasure.

**40:19** Children and the building of a city establish a name, yet above both a blameless wife is esteemed.

**40:20** Wine and music gladden the heart, yet above both is the love of wisdom.

**40:21** The flute and the harp make melodies sweet, yet above both is a sweet tongue.

**40:22** Your eye shall desire grace and beauty, yet above both the green shoots of sown grain.

**40:23** A friend and a companion meet in due season, yet above both is a wife with her husband.

**40:24** Brethren and help are for a time of affliction, yet above both almsgiving shall deliver.

**40:25** Gold and silver shall make the foot stand firm, yet above both a wife is esteemed.

**40:26** Wealth and strength shall lift up the heart, yet above both is the fear of the Lord; in the fear of the Lord there is no lack, and in it there is no need to seek for help.

**40:27** The fear of the Lord is like a paradise of blessing, and above all glory they have covered it.

**40:28** Child, do not live a life of begging; it is better to die than to beg.

**40:29** A man who looks to another's table—his existence is not in the reckoning of life; he shall defile his soul with another's food, but a man of understanding and discipline shall guard himself.

**40:30** In the mouth of the shameless, begging shall be sweet, yet in his belly a fire shall burn.

## Translator Notes

- **40:1** — The Greek text reads "until the day of burial into the mother of all," where some manuscripts read "the day of return to the mother of all."
- **40:9** — The Greek text here lists four calamities; some manuscripts add a second clause naming afflictions, famine, ruin, and scourge.
- **40:14** — The first clause is terse and leaves its subject unspecified in Greek, contrasting the joy of one who opens his hands with the complete ruin of transgressors.
- **40:18** — The Greek text reads "a self-sufficient laborer" without a conjunction, though the following "above both" preserves the paired pattern of the proverb series in this section.
- **40:25** — The Greek text here reads "a wife is esteemed," whereas other Greek manuscripts and the Hebrew text of Ben Sira read "counsel is esteemed."
