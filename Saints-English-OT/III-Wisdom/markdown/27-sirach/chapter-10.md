## Text

**10:1** A wise judge will instruct his people, and the rule of one who has understanding shall be well-ordered.

**10:2** As is the judge of his people, so also are his ministers; and as is the ruler of the city, so are all who dwell in it.

**10:3** An undisciplined king will destroy his people, and a city shall be built up through the understanding of its rulers.

**10:4** In the hand of the Lord is the authority of the earth, and He will raise up the right one over it at the appointed time.

**10:5** In the hand of the Lord is the prosperity of a man, and upon the face of the scribe He will set His glory.

**10:6** Do not harbor anger against your neighbor for every wrong, and do nothing in deeds of arrogance.

**10:7** Pride is hateful before the Lord and men, and against both it will commit unjust deeds.

**10:8** A kingdom is transferred from nation to nation because of injustices, insolence, and wealth.

**10:9** Why are earth and ashes proud? Because even in life I have cast away his inward parts.

**10:10** The physician mocks a lingering illness; he is king today, and tomorrow he shall die.

**10:11** For when a man dies, he shall inherit creeping things and beasts, even worms.

**10:12** The beginning of pride is a man departing from the Lord, and his heart has turned away from Him who made him.

**10:13** For the beginning of pride is sin, and he who holds fast to it shall pour forth abomination. Therefore the Lord made their calamities extraordinary, and overthrew them utterly.

**10:14** The Lord cast down the thrones of rulers, and seated the meek in their place.

**10:15** The Lord plucked up the roots of nations, and planted the humble in their place.

**10:16** The Lord overthrew the lands of nations, and destroyed them to the foundations of the earth.

**10:17** He withered some of them and destroyed them, and caused their remembrance to cease from the earth.

**10:18** Pride was not created for men, nor fierce wrath for those born of women.

**10:19** What offspring is honorable? The offspring of man. What offspring is honorable? Those who fear the Lord. What offspring is dishonorable? The offspring of man. What offspring is dishonorable? Those who transgress commandments.

**10:20** In the midst of brethren their leader is honorable, and those who fear the Lord are honorable in His eyes.

**10:22** The rich, the noble, and the poor—their glorying is the fear of the Lord.

**10:23** It is not right to dishonor a poor man of understanding, nor is it fitting to honor a sinful man.

**10:24** The nobleman, the judge, and the ruler shall be honored, yet not one of them is greater than he who fears the Lord.

**10:25** Free men shall serve a wise servant, and a man of knowledge will not murmur.

**10:26** Do not make a show of wisdom in doing your work, and do not glorify yourself in the time of your distress.

**10:27** Better is he who works in all things than he who strolls about, or he who glorifies himself and lacks bread.

**10:28** Child, in meekness glorify your soul, and give it honor according to its worth.

**10:29** Who will justify him who sins against his own soul? And who will honor him who dishonors his own life?

**10:30** A poor man is honored because of his knowledge, and a rich man is honored because of his wealth.

**10:31** And he who is honored in poverty—how much more in wealth? And he who is without honor in wealth—how much more in poverty?

## Translator Notes

- **10:7** — The Greek clause is syntactically compressed and is rendered here with pride as subject, though the neuter plural for unjust deeds may also be construed as the subject ("unjust deeds will be an offense against both").
- **10:9** — The critical Greek text preserves an abrupt first-person aorist verb ("I have cast away"), referring to God casting away a mortal's inward parts even while alive.
- **10:10** — The Greek reads "the physician mocks" (or scoffs at) a lingering illness, underscoring the suddenness with which even a king's life ends.
- **10:17** — The Greek verb means "He withered" or "dried up," continuing the imagery of uprooting nations from verse 15.
- **10:27** — This Greek text reads directly "works in all things than he who strolls about, or...," lacking the expansion "and abounds in all things" found in some manuscripts.
