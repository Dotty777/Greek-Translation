## Text

**28:1** He who takes vengeance shall find vengeance from the Lord, and He will surely keep a strict account of his sins.

**28:2** Forgive your neighbor his offense, and then, when you pray, your sins shall be loosed.

**28:3** Does one man harbor anger against another, and seek healing from the Lord?

**28:4** He has no mercy upon a man like himself, and does he make supplication for his own sins?

**28:5** Being flesh himself, he harbors wrath; who shall make atonement for his sins?

**28:6** Remember the last things and cease from enmity—corruption and death—and abide in the commandments.

**28:7** Remember the commandments, and do not hold wrath against your neighbor; remember the covenant of the Most High, and overlook ignorance.

**28:8** Abstain from strife, and you shall diminish sins; for a wrathful man will kindle strife,

**28:9** and a sinful man will trouble friends, and casts slander in the midst of those who are at peace.

**28:10** According to the fuel of the fire, so shall it burn; according to the strength of a man shall his fury be; and according to his wealth he will heighten his anger, and according to the intensity of the strife it shall be kindled.

**28:11** A hasty quarrel kindles a fire, and hasty strife sheds blood.

**28:12** If you blow on a spark, it will blaze up; and if you spit upon it, it will be quenched; and both proceed out of your mouth.

**28:13** Curse the whisperer and the double-tongued, for they have destroyed many who were at peace.

**28:14** A third tongue has shaken many and scattered them from nation to nation; it has pulled down fortified cities and overturned the houses of great men.

**28:15** A third tongue has cast out valiant wives and deprived them of their labors.

**28:16** He who gives heed to it shall by no means find rest, nor shall he dwell in quietness.

**28:17** The stroke of a whip leaves welts, but the stroke of a tongue will crush bones.

**28:18** Many have fallen by the edge of the sword, yet not like those who have fallen because of the tongue.

**28:19** Blessed is he who is sheltered from it, who has not passed through its fury, who has not drawn its yoke, and has not been bound in its bonds;

**28:20** for its yoke is a yoke of iron, and its bonds are bonds of bronze.

**28:21** Its death is an evil death, and Hades is more profitable than it.

**28:22** It shall by no means prevail over the godly, and in its flame they shall not be burned;

**28:23** those who forsake the Lord shall fall into it, and it shall blaze among them and never be quenched; it shall be sent forth upon them like a lion, and like a leopard it shall ravage them.

**28:24** Behold, hedge your possession around with thorns; bind up your silver and your gold,

**28:25** and make a balance and a weight for your words, and make a door and a bolt for your mouth.

**28:26** Take heed lest you somehow slip by it, lest you fall before one who lies in wait.

## Translator Notes

- **28:1** — The Greek uses an emphatic participial construction (literally 'firmly fixing, He will fix') to stress that the Lord keeps a strict account of the vengeful person's sins.
- **28:14** — 'A third tongue' translates a Semitic idiom for a slanderous or meddling third party who comes between two others.
- **28:21** — Literally 'and Hades is more profitable than it,' using the genitive of comparison to emphasize that even the grave is preferable to the ruin brought by a slanderous tongue.
