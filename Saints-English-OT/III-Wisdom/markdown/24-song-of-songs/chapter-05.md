## Text

**5:1** Let my kinsman go down into his garden and eat the fruit of his choice trees. I have entered into the garden, my sister, O bride; I have gathered my myrrh with my spices; I have eaten my bread with my honey; I have drunk my wine with my milk. Eat, O companions, and drink, and drink deeply, O brethren.

**5:2** I sleep, yet my heart keeps watch. The voice of my kinsman! He knocks at the door: "Open to me, my sister, my companion, my dove, my perfect one, for my head is filled with dew, and my locks with the drops of the night."

**5:3** I have taken off my tunic; how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet; how shall I defile them?

**5:4** My kinsman sent forth his hand from the opening, and my inward parts were stirred for him.

**5:5** I arose to open to my kinsman; my hands dripped myrrh, and my fingers full myrrh, upon the handles of the bolt.

**5:6** I opened to my kinsman, yet my kinsman had passed by; my soul went forth at his word; I sought him, and did not find him; I called him, and he did not answer me.

**5:7** The watchmen who go around in the city found me; they struck me, they wounded me; the watchmen of the walls took away my shawl from me.

**5:8** I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers and by the strengths of the field: if you find my kinsman, what shall you tell him? That I am wounded with love.

**5:9** What is your kinsman more than another kinsman, O fair one among women? What is your kinsman more than another kinsman, that you have thus adjured us?

**5:10** My kinsman is white and ruddy, chosen out from ten thousands.

**5:11** His head is gold and Phaz; his locks are palm shoots, black as a raven.

**5:12** His eyes are like doves beside full pools of water, washed in milk, sitting beside full pools.

**5:13** His cheeks are like bowls of spice, bringing forth fragrant ointments; his lips are lilies, dripping full myrrh.

**5:14** His hands are wrought gold, filled with Tarshish. His belly is an ivory tablet upon a sapphire stone.

**5:15** His legs are pillars of marble, founded upon golden bases; his appearance is like Lebanon, choice as the cedars.

**5:16** His throat is sweetness, and he is wholly desire. This is my kinsman, and this is my companion, O daughters of Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Throughout chapters 5–6, Greek 'adelphidos' ("kinsman" or "nephew") renders the Hebrew term of endearment 'dodi' as a kinship counterpart to "my sister." The Septuagint also reads "my bread" ('arton mou') rather than "my honeycomb."
- **5:8** — The Septuagint includes "by the powers and by the strengths of the field," translating the oath formula's Hebrew words for gazelles and does by their homonymous senses of "powers" and "strengths."
- **5:11** — Greek 'chrysion kai phaz' ("gold and Phaz") transliterates the Hebrew word for refined gold ('paz') as a separate term joined by "and."
- **5:14** — "Tarshish" ('tharseis') is a Septuagint transliteration of the Hebrew name for a precious gemstone, commonly identified as beryl or chrysolite.
