## Text

**1:1** The Song of Songs, which belongs to Solomon.

**1:2** Let him kiss me with kisses from his mouth; for your breasts are better than wine, and the fragrance of your ointments is above all spices.

**1:3** Your name is ointment poured forth; therefore maidens have loved you,

**1:4** they have drawn you; we will run after you into the fragrance of your ointments. The king has brought me into his inner chamber. Let us exult and be glad in you; we shall love your breasts more than wine; uprightness has loved you.

**1:5** I am black and beautiful, O daughters of Israel, like the tents of Kedar, like the skin curtain of Solomon.

**1:6** Do not gaze upon me because I am blackened, because the sun has looked askance upon me. The sons of my mother fought against me; they appointed me a keeper in the vineyards; my own vineyard I have not kept.

**1:7** Tell me, you whom my soul loves, where you shepherd your flock, where you rest at midday, lest I become like one who veils herself beside the flocks of your companions.

**1:8** If you do not know yourself, O beautiful one among women, go forth in the tracks of the flocks, and pasture your young goats beside the tents of the shepherds.

**1:9** To my mare among the chariots of Pharaoh I have likened you, my companion.

**1:10** How lovely have your cheeks become, like turtledoves, and your neck like necklaces!

**1:11** We shall make for you likenesses of gold, with studs of silver.

**1:12** While the king was upon his couch, my spikenard gave forth its fragrance.

**1:13** A bundle of myrrh is my kinsman to me,

**1:14** in the vineyards of En Gedi.

**1:15** Behold, you are beautiful, my companion; behold, you are beautiful! Your eyes are doves.

**1:16** Behold, you are handsome, my kinsman, yes, indeed lovely; moreover, our couch is shaded,

**1:17** the beams of our houses are cedars, and our paneled ceilings are cypresses.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — The Greek reads "your breasts" (and again in 1:4), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonants than the Masoretic Text ("your love").
- **1:5** — The Greek reads "daughters of Israel," whereas the Hebrew reads "daughters of Jerusalem."
- **1:10** — The Greek reads "like turtledoves," rendering the Hebrew term for ornaments or rows by its homonym for turtledoves.
- **1:13** — "Kinsman" translates Greek adelphidos (literally "nephew"), the Septuagint's consistent rendering throughout this book for the Hebrew word for "beloved."
- **1:14** — This Greek text lacks the clauses between "to me" in 1:13 and "in the vineyards of En Gedi" in 1:14 that appear in the Hebrew and other manuscripts, likely due to a scribal jump between the repeated phrase "my kinsman to me."
