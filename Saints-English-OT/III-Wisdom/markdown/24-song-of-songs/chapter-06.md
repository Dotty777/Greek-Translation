## Text

**6:1** Where has your kinsman gone, O fair one among women? Where has your kinsman turned his gaze? And we will seek him with you.

**6:2** My brother has gone down into his garden, to the bowls of spice, to shepherd in the gardens and to gather lilies.

**6:3** I am my kinsman's, and my kinsman is mine, he who shepherds among the lilies.

**6:4** You are fair, my companion, as good pleasure, lovely as Jerusalem, a dread like ranks set in array.

**6:5** Turn away your eyes from before me, for they have made me take wing. Your hair is like flocks of goats that appeared from Gilead;

**6:6** your teeth are like flocks of the shorn ones that came up from the washing, all of them bearing twins, and there is not one barren among them. Like a scarlet cord are your lips, and your speech is lovely.

**6:7** Like a rind of pomegranate is your cheek, apart from your silence.

**6:8** There are sixty queens, and eighty concubines, and maidens without number;

**6:9** one is my dove, my perfect one; she is the only one to her mother, the choice one to her who bore her. Daughters saw her and shall call her blessed; queens and even concubines, and they shall praise her.

**6:10** Who is she who looks forth like the dawn, fair as the moon, choice as the sun, a dread like ranks set in array?

**6:11** Into the garden of walnut trees I went down to look upon the produce of the brook, to see whether the vine had blossomed and the pomegranates had bloomed; there I will give my breasts to you.

**6:12** My soul did not know; it made me the chariots of Amminadab.

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — Here the Greek text reads 'Adelphos mou' ("My brother") rather than its customary 'adelphidos mou' ("my kinsman").
- **6:4** — Where the Hebrew text names the city Tirzah, the Septuagint translates the name according to its verbal root as "good pleasure" ('eudokia').
- **6:11** — The closing clause "there I will give my breasts to you" is present in the Septuagint text of 6:11 (paralleling 7:12) but does not appear here in the Masoretic Text.
- **6:12** — In this notoriously difficult verse, the Septuagint reads a double accusative ("made me the chariots") and renders the final Hebrew phrase as the proper name "Amminadab."
