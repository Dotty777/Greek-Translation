## Text

**4:1** Behold, you are fair, my companion; behold, you are fair. Your eyes are doves, apart from your silence. Your hair is like flocks of goats that were revealed from Gilead.

**4:2** Your teeth are like flocks of the shorn ones that came up from the washing, all of them bearing twins, and there is not one barren among them.

**4:3** Like a scarlet cord are your lips, and your speech is lovely; like a rind of pomegranate is your cheek, apart from your silence.

**4:4** Like the tower of David is your neck, which was built for Thalpioth. A thousand shields hang upon it, all the darts of the mighty men.

**4:5** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle, feeding among the lilies.

**4:6** Until the day breathes and the shadows move, I will go for myself to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of Lebanon.

**4:7** You are wholly fair, my companion, and there is no blemish in you.

**4:8** Come from Lebanon, O bride, come from Lebanon; you shall come and pass through from the beginning of faith, from the summit of Senir and Hermon, from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards.

**4:9** You have captivated our heart, my sister, O bride; you have captivated our heart with one of your eyes, with one, with a necklace of your necks.

**4:10** How have your breasts become fairer than wine, and the fragrance of your garments above all spices?

**4:11** Your lips drip honeycomb, O bride; honey and milk are under your tongue, and the fragrance of your garments is like the fragrance of Lebanon.

**4:12** A closed garden is my sister, O bride; a closed garden, a sealed spring;

**4:13** your shoots are an orchard with the fruit of choice trees, henna blossoms with spikenards;

**4:14** spikenard and saffron, calamus and cinnamon, with all the trees of Lebanon, myrrh and aloes, with all the finest ointments;

**4:15** a spring of a garden, and a well of living water, even rushing from Lebanon.

**4:16** Awake, O north wind; and come, O south wind, and blow through my garden, and let my spices flow out.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Greek 'ektos tes siopeseos sou' literally means "apart from your silence" (also in 4:3; 6:7), where the Septuagint renders the Hebrew term for "veil" from a root meaning silence.
- **4:4** — "Thalpioth" is a Septuagint transliteration of an obscure Hebrew architectural or military term; Greek 'bolides' specifically means "darts" or "javelins."
- **4:6** — Greek 'Libanos' can denote either Mount Lebanon (as capitalized in the Greek text here, anticipating v. 8) or frankincense (paired with myrrh).
- **4:8** — The Septuagint renders the Hebrew place name Amana ("peak of Amana") according to its root meaning as "from the beginning of faith" ('apo arches pisteos').
- **4:10** — The Septuagint reads 'mastoi sou' ("your breasts"), vocalizing the Hebrew consonants as "breasts" rather than "love."
