## Text

**7:1** Turn back, turn back, O Sumanite; turn back, turn back, and we shall look upon you. What will you look upon in the Sumanite? She who comes like dances of the camps.

**7:2** Your steps have become lovely in sandals, O daughter of Nadab; the proportions of your thighs are like necklaces, the work of a craftsman.

**7:3** Your navel is a wrought mixing bowl, not lacking mixed wine; your belly is a mound of wheat, hedged about with lilies.

**7:4** Your two breasts are like two twin fawns of a gazelle.

**7:5** Your neck is like a tower of ivory; your eyes are like pools in Heshbon, at the gates of the daughter of many; your nose is like a tower of Lebanon, keeping watch toward the face of Damascus.

**7:6** Your head upon you is like Carmel, and the braided hair of your head is like purple; a king is bound in the galleries.

**7:7** How lovely you have become, and how sweet, O love, in your delights!

**7:8** This is your stature: you are likened to the palm tree, and your breasts to the clusters.

**7:9** I said, "I will ascend the palm tree; I will take hold of its heights." And now your breasts shall be like clusters of the vine, and the fragrance of your nose like apples,

**7:10** and your throat like good wine, going smoothly for my kinsman, sufficing for my lips and teeth.

**7:11** I belong to my kinsman, and toward me is his turning.

**7:12** Come, my kinsman, let us go forth into the field; let us lodge in the villages.

**7:13** Let us go early to the vineyards; let us see whether the vine has blossomed, whether the vine-bud has blossomed, whether the pomegranates have blossomed; there I will give my breasts to you.

**7:14** The mandrakes have given forth fragrance, and at our doors are all choice fruits, new with old; my kinsman, I have kept them for you.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — Greek Soumaneitis ("Sumanite") corresponds to "Shulammite" in the Hebrew text; the Greek also includes "she who comes" before "like dances of the camps."
- **7:2** — The Septuagint renders the Hebrew phrase meaning "noble daughter" as a proper name, "daughter of Nadab."
- **7:5** — The Greek translates the gate name Bath-rabbim literally as "the daughter of many."
- **7:10** — Greek adelphidos literally denotes a nephew or close kinsman and serves throughout the Septuagint of Song of Songs as an affectionate term for the beloved; the closing phrase "sufficing for my lips and teeth" differs from the Masoretic Text ("gliding over the lips of sleepers").
- **7:13** — The Greek reads "my breasts" where the Masoretic Text vocalizes "my love"; similarly in 7:11 Greek epistrophe renders "turning" rather than "desire."
