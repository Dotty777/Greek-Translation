## Text

**8:1** Who would grant you to be my kinsman, nursing at the breasts of my mother? Finding you outside, I will kiss you, and indeed they will not despise me.

**8:2** I will take you, I will bring you into the house of my mother and into the chamber of her who conceived me; I will give you to drink of the spiced wine, of the juice of your pomegranates.

**8:3** His left hand is beneath my head, and his right hand shall embrace me.

**8:4** I have adjured you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers and by the strengths of the field, that you neither rouse nor awaken love until it desires.

**8:5** Who is this coming up all in white, leaning upon her kinsman? Beneath an apple tree I awakened you; there your mother was in travail with you, there she who bore you was in travail with you.

**8:6** Place me like a seal upon your heart, like a seal upon your arm; for love is mighty as death, jealousy is unyielding as Hades; its wings are wings of fire, its flames.

**8:7** Much water will not be able to quench love, and rivers shall not overwhelm it. If a man should give all his substance for love, they will utterly despise him.

**8:8** We have a small sister, and she has no breasts. What shall we do for our sister on the day when she is spoken for?

**8:9** If she is a wall, let us build upon her battlements of silver; and if she is a door, let us panel upon her a board of cedar.

**8:10** I am a wall, and my breasts are towers; I was in their eyes as one who finds peace.

**8:11** A vineyard belonged to Solomon in Beethlamon; he gave his vineyard to the keepers; each man shall bring for its fruit a thousand pieces of silver.

**8:12** My own vineyard is before me; the thousand belong to Solomon, and the two hundred to those who keep its fruit.

**8:13** O you who dwell in the gardens, companions are paying heed to your voice; cause me to hear it.

**8:14** Flee, my kinsman, and be like a gazelle or a fawn of the stags upon the mountains of spices.

## Translator Notes

- **8:2** — The Greek includes "and into the chamber of her who conceived me" and reads "your pomegranates" rather than the Masoretic Text's "my pomegranates."
- **8:4** — Preserving a longer Septuagint reading, the Greek adjures "by the powers and by the strengths of the field," and the Greek oath construction with ean conveys a solemn prohibition ("that you neither rouse nor awaken").
- **8:5** — Greek leleukathismene ("all in white" or "made white") diverges from the Masoretic Text's "from the wilderness."
- **8:6** — Greek periptera literally means "wings" or "winged flashes," and phloges autes ("its flames") contains no divine-name element.
- **8:11** — Greek Beethlamon corresponds to the place name "Baal-hamon" in the Hebrew text.
