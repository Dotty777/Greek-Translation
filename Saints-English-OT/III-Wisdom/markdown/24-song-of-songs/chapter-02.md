## Text

**2:1** I am a flower of the plain, a lily of the valleys.

**2:2** Like a lily in the midst of thorns, so is my companion among the daughters.

**2:3** Like an apple tree among the trees of the forest, so is my kinsman among the sons. In his shadow I longed and sat down, and his fruit was sweet in my throat.

**2:4** Bring me into the house of wine; set love in order over me.

**2:5** Strengthen me with ointments, heap me about with apples, for I am wounded with love.

**2:6** His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me.

**2:7** I have adjured you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers and by the forces of the field, that you neither stir up nor awaken love until it pleases.

**2:8** The voice of my kinsman! Behold, he comes leaping upon the mountains, bounding over the hills.

**2:9** My kinsman is like a gazelle or a fawn of stags upon the mountains of Bethel. Behold, he is behind our wall, gazing through the windows, peering through the lattices.

**2:10** My kinsman answers and says to me, "Arise, come, my companion, my beautiful one, my dove.

**2:11** For behold, the winter has passed; the rain has departed, it has gone its way.

**2:12** The flowers have appeared in the land; the time of pruning has arrived; the voice of the turtledove has been heard in our land.

**2:13** The fig tree has put forth its unripe figs; the vines are in blossom, they have given forth fragrance. Arise, come, my companion, my beautiful one, my dove! And come,

**2:14** you, my dove, in the shelter of the rock, next to the outer wall, show me your countenance and let me hear your voice, for your voice is sweet and your countenance is lovely."

**2:15** Catch for us the little foxes that ruin vineyards, and our vineyards are in blossom.

**2:16** My kinsman is mine, and I am his—he who tends his flock among the lilies,

**2:17** until the day breathes and the shadows shift. Turn back, kinsman; be like a gazelle or a fawn of stags upon the mountains of the ravines.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Greek renders the Hebrew place-name Sharon as a common noun, "the plain."
- **2:4** — The Greek has plural imperatives ("Bring me... set love in order") rather than the third-person singular clauses of the Hebrew ("he brought me... his banner").
- **2:7** — The Greek reads "by the powers and by the forces of the field" (also in 3:5), translating the Hebrew words for gazelles and does by their homonyms for hosts and strengths.
- **2:9** — The Greek includes "upon the mountains of Bethel," a phrase absent from the Masoretic Text.
- **2:12** — Greek tome specifically means "pruning," one of two senses of the underlying Hebrew word (the other being "singing").
