## Text

**24:1** And when Balaam saw that it was good before the Lord to bless Israel, he did not go, as was his custom, to meet with omens, but turned his face toward the wilderness.

**24:2** And Balaam lifted up his eyes and beheld Israel encamped according to their tribes; and the Spirit of God came within him.

**24:3** And taking up his parable, he said, "Balaam the son of Beor says, the man who truly sees says,

**24:4** he who hears the oracles of God says, who saw a vision of God in sleep, with his eyes uncovered:

**24:5** How fair are your houses, O Jacob, your tents, O Israel;

**24:6** like shady valleys, and like a garden beside rivers; and like tents which the Lord pitched, like cedars beside waters.

**24:7** A man shall come forth from his seed, and he shall rule over many nations; and the kingdom shall be exalted higher than Gog, and his kingdom shall be increased.

**24:8** God led him out of Egypt; his is as the glory of a unicorn; he shall devour the nations of his enemies, and he shall drain the marrow from their fatness, and with his darts he shall shoot down the enemy.

**24:9** Lying down, he rested like a lion and like a lion's cub; who shall rouse him? Those who bless you have been blessed, and those who curse you have been cursed."

**24:10** And Balak was enraged against Balaam, and he clapped his hands together; and Balak said to Balaam, "I called you to curse my enemy, and behold, you have surely blessed him this third time.

**24:11** Now therefore, flee to your own place. I said, 'I will honor you,' and now the Lord has deprived you of glory."

**24:12** And Balaam said to Balak, "Did I not also speak to your messengers whom you sent to me, saying,

**24:13** 'If Balak should give me his house full of silver and gold, I will not be able to transgress the word of the Lord, to make it evil or good of myself; whatever God may speak, those things I will say'?

**24:14** And now, behold, I am hastening away to my own place; come, I will advise you what this people shall do to your people in the last of the days."

**24:15** And taking up his parable, he said:

**24:16** "Balaam the son of Beor says, the truthful man who sees says, hearing the oracles of God, knowing knowledge from the Most High, and having seen a vision of God in sleep, with his eyes uncovered:

**24:17** I will show to him, yet not now; I call blessed, yet he does not draw near: a star shall rise out of Jacob, and a man shall arise out of Israel, and he shall shatter the leaders of Moab, and he shall plunder all the sons of Seth.

**24:18** And Edom shall be an inheritance, and Esau his enemy shall be an inheritance; and Israel acted with strength.

**24:19** And one shall be roused up out of Jacob, and he shall destroy whoever escapes from the city."

**24:20** And seeing Amalek and taking up his parable, he said, "Amalek is the first of the nations, yet their seed shall perish."

**24:21** And seeing the Kenite and taking up his parable, he said, "Strong is your dwelling place; and even if you set your nest in a rock,

**24:22** and even if Beor should have a nest of craftiness, the Assyrians shall take you captive."

**24:23** And seeing Og and taking up his parable, he said, "Alas, alas! Who shall live when God appoints these things?

**24:24** And one shall come forth from the hand of the Kittim, and they shall afflict Asshur, and they shall afflict the Hebrews, and they themselves shall perish together."

**24:25** And Balaam rose up and departed, returning to his own place; and Balak departed to his own home.

## Translator Notes

- **24:7** — The Septuagint introduces a personal figure ("a man shall come forth from his seed") where the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of water flowing from buckets, and reads "Gog" rather than "Agag."
- **24:17** — The Septuagint reads "I will show to him" and "I call blessed" (MT: "I see him... I behold him") and renders Hebrew "scepter" as "a man" ("a man shall arise out of Israel"), highlighting the messianic character of the oracle.
- **24:22** — The Septuagint reads the Hebrew phrase for being consumed or burned as the proper name "Beor" and translates the clause as "a nest of craftiness."
- **24:23** — The Septuagint explicitly includes the name "Og" ("And seeing Og"), which is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **24:24** — The Septuagint reads "the Hebrews" where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "Eber," and has a singular verb ("shall come forth") from "the hand of the Kittim."
