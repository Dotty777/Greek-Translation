## Translator Notes

- **7:2** — The Greek explicitly specifies "twelve rulers" (absent in the Hebrew Masoretic Text) and uses episkope ("inspection" or "oversight") for the census administration.
- **7:17** — The Greek thysia soteriu ("sacrifice of deliverance") corresponds to the Hebrew peace offering, and uniquely specifies feminine animals—two heifers (damaleis) and five year-old ewe lambs (amnadas)—throughout the chapter's offerings.
- **7:29** — Following the Greek critical text, "five male goats" is omitted in both 7:29 and 7:41, though present in the other days' offering lists.
- **7:30** — The Greek text reads Edisur here, whereas 7:35 reads Sediur (Shedeur).
- **7:42** — The Greek text reads Raguel (Reuel), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has Deuel here.
- **7:84** — The Greek reads "from the sons of Israel," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "from the leaders of Israel."
- **7:86** — The Greek omits the phrase "ten shekels apiece, according to the shekel of the sanctuary," which appears in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **7:87** — The Greek explicitly adds "and their drink offerings" alongside the sacrifices, a clause not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **7:88** — The Greek adds "without blemish" to the sixty year-old ewe lambs and concludes with "after filling his hands and after anointing him" (reflecting the idiom for priestly consecration with a masculine pronoun), whereas the Hebrew reads "after it was anointed."
- **7:89** — The Greek explicitly specifies "the voice of the Lord" speaking from above the mercy seat, whereas the Hebrew leaves the speaker unnamed.
