## Text

**36:1** And Bezalel and Eliab worked, and every man wise in mind to whom wisdom and knowledge were given within them to understand how to perform all the works according to the sacred duties, according to all that the Lord commanded.

**36:2** And Moses called Bezalel and Eliab, and all those who possessed wisdom, to whom God had given knowledge in the heart, and all those willingly desiring to come forward to the works so as to complete them.

**36:3** And they received from Moses all the contributions which the sons of Israel had brought for all the works of the sanctuary to make them; and they continued to receive the offerings from those who brought them in the morning.

**36:4** And all the wise men who were performing the works of the sanctuary came, each according to his own work which they were doing.

**36:5** And he said to Moses, "The people are bringing an abundance for the works which the Lord commanded to do."

**36:6** And Moses gave a command, and proclaimed in the camp, saying, "Let neither man nor woman work any longer for the firstfruits of the sanctuary." And the people were restrained from bringing any more.

**36:7** And the works were sufficient for them to accomplish the construction, and they had some left over.

**36:8** And every wise man among those who did the work made the garments of the sanctuary, which are for Aaron the priest, just as the Lord commanded Moses.

**36:9** And he made the ephod of gold, and blue, and purple, and spun scarlet, and fine twisted linen.

**36:10** And the plates of gold were cut into hair-fine threads, so as to weave them together with the blue, and the purple, and the finely spun scarlet, and with the fine twisted linen; they made it as woven work,

**36:11** shoulder-pieces joining it together on both sides, woven work, intertwined with one another as one piece.

**36:12** Of the same piece they made it, of gold, and blue, and purple, and finely spun scarlet, and fine twisted linen, just as the Lord commanded Moses.

**36:13** And they made both stones of emerald, fastened in clasps and bordered with settings, and engraved with the engraving of a signet according to the names of the sons of Israel;

**36:14** and he placed them upon the shoulders of the ephod, as stones of remembrance for the sons of Israel, just as the Lord commanded Moses.

**36:15** And they made an oracle, woven work of embroidery, like the workmanship of the ephod, of gold, and blue, and purple, and finely spun scarlet, and fine twisted linen;

**36:16** they made the oracle foursquare and doubled, a span in length and a span in width, doubled.

**36:17** And a woven setting of stones in four rows was woven into it, a row of stones: a sardius, a topaz, and an emerald were the first row;

**36:18** and the second row: a carbuncle, a sapphire, and a jasper;

**36:19** and the third row: a ligure, an agate, and an amethyst;

**36:20** and the fourth row: a chrysolite, a beryl, and an onyx, encircled with gold and bound together with gold.

**36:21** And the stones were twelve, according to the names of the sons of Israel, engraved like signets according to their names, each one according to his own name, for the twelve tribes.

**36:22** And they made upon the oracle intertwined fringes, braided work, of pure gold.

**36:23** And they made two small shields of gold and two golden rings, and they placed the two golden rings upon both ends of the oracle;

**36:24** and they placed the braided cords of gold upon the rings on both sides of the oracle, and the two braided cords into the ends,

**36:25** and they placed them upon the two small shields; and they placed them upon the shoulders of the ephod opposite each other on the front.

**36:26** And they made two golden rings, and placed them upon the two corners at the edge of the oracle, and upon the edge of the back of the ephod on the inside.

**36:27** And they made two golden rings, and placed them upon both shoulders of the ephod.

**36:28** And he fastened the oracle tightly from the rings that were upon it to the rings of the ephod, held together by the blue, interwoven into the woven fabric of the ephod, so that the oracle might not become loose from the ephod, just as the Lord commanded Moses.

**36:29** And they made the robe beneath the ephod, woven work, wholly of blue;

**36:30** and the opening of the robe in the middle was woven through and braided together, having a border all around the opening so that it would not come apart.

**36:31** And they made upon the hem of the robe below little pomegranates, like a blossoming pomegranate, of blue, and purple, and spun scarlet, and fine twisted linen.

**36:32** And they made golden bells, and placed the bells upon the hem of the robe all around between the little pomegranates:

**36:33** a golden bell and a little pomegranate upon the hem of the robe all around, for ministering, just as the Lord commanded Moses.

**36:34** And they made tunics of fine linen, woven work, for Aaron and for his sons,

**36:35** and the turbans of fine linen, and the miter of fine linen, and the trousers of fine twisted linen,

**36:36** and their sashes of fine linen, and blue, and purple, and spun scarlet, the work of an embroiderer, in the manner that the Lord commanded Moses.

**36:37** And they made the golden plate, the consecration of the sanctuary, of pure gold; and he inscribed upon it letters carved like a signet: "Holiness to the Lord";

**36:38** and they placed it upon the blue border, so that it rested upon the miter above, in the manner that the Lord commanded Moses.

## Translator Notes

- **36:1** — The Greek text reads Eliab, corresponding to Oholiab in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **36:8** — Beginning here, the Septuagint departs from the order of the Hebrew Masoretic Text, placing the construction of the priestly garments in 36:8–38 (corresponding to Hebrew Exodus 39:1–31).
- **36:15** — Greek logion literally means "oracle," the Septuagint's regular rendering for the high priest's breastpiece.
- **36:24** — The Greek clause eis tas lacks an explicit noun before "the two braided cords" in the manuscript tradition; "the ends" is supplied from the preceding verse to complete the sense.
