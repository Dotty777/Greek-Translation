## Text

**32:1** And when the people saw that Moses delayed to come down from the mountain, the people gathered together against Aaron and said to him, "Arise and make for us gods who shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man who brought us out of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him."

**32:2** And Aaron said to them, "Take off the golden earrings that are in the ears of your wives and daughters, and bring them to me."

**32:3** So all the people took off the golden earrings that were in their ears and brought them to Aaron.

**32:4** And he received them from their hands, fashioned them with an engraving tool, and made them into a molten calf; and he said, "These are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt."

**32:5** And when Aaron saw it, he built an altar before it; and Aaron made proclamation, saying, "Tomorrow is a feast of the Lord."

**32:6** And rising early the next day, he offered up whole burnt offerings and presented a sacrifice of deliverance; and the people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play.

**32:7** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying, "Go quickly from here, go down; for your people whom you brought out of the land of Egypt have acted lawlessly.

**32:8** They have quickly turned aside from the way which you commanded them; they have made for themselves a calf, and have worshiped it, and have said, 'These are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt.'

**32:10** And now leave Me alone, and being kindled with wrath against them I will utterly destroy them, and I will make you into a great nation."

**32:11** Then Moses pleaded before the Lord God and said, "Why, O Lord, are You enraged with wrath against Your people, whom You brought out of the land of Egypt with great strength and with Your uplifted arm?

**32:12** Lest the Egyptians speak, saying, 'With evil intent He brought them out to slay them in the mountains and to consume them from the earth.' Cease from the anger of Your wrath, and be merciful concerning the wickedness of Your people,

**32:13** remembering Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, Your servants, to whom You swore by Yourself and spoke to them, saying, 'I will greatly multiply your seed as the stars of heaven in multitude,' and all this land which You spoke to give to them, and they shall possess it forever."

**32:14** And the Lord was appeased, so as to preserve His people.

**32:15** And Moses turned and went down from the mountain, and the two tablets of the Testimony were in his hands, tablets of stone inscribed on both their sides; on the one side and on the other they were written.

**32:16** And the tablets were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, engraved upon the tablets.

**32:17** And when Joshua heard the sound of the people shouting, he said to Moses, "There is a sound of war in the camp."

**32:18** And he said, "It is not the sound of those leading a song of might, nor the sound of those leading a song of defeat; rather, I hear the sound of those leading a song of wine."

**32:19** And as he drew near to the camp, he saw the calf and the dances; and being enraged with wrath, Moses cast the two tablets from his hands and shattered them beneath the mountain.

**32:20** And taking the calf which they had made, he burned it in the fire, ground it fine, scattered it into the water, and made the sons of Israel drink it.

**32:21** And Moses said to Aaron, "What did this people do to you that you have brought a great sin upon them?"

**32:22** And Aaron said to Moses, "Do not be angry, my lord; for you know the impulse of this people.

**32:23** For they said to me, 'Make for us gods who shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man who brought us out of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him.'

**32:24** And I said to them, 'If anyone has gold ornaments, take them off.' So they gave them to me, and I cast them into the fire, and this calf came out."

**32:25** And Moses saw that the people were scattered—for Aaron had scattered them as an object of derision to their adversaries.

**32:26** Then Moses stood at the gate of the camp and said, "Who is for the Lord? Let him come to me." Therefore all the sons of Levi came together.

**32:27** And he said to them, "Thus says the Lord God of Israel: 'Let every man put his sword upon his thigh, and pass through and return from gate to gate throughout the camp, and let every man slay his brother and every man his nearest neighbor.'"

**32:28** And the sons of Levi did according to what Moses had spoken to them; and there fell of the people on that day about three thousand men.

**32:29** And Moses said to them, "You have filled your hands today for the Lord, each man against his son or his brother, that a blessing may be given upon you."

**32:30** And it came to pass on the following day that Moses said to the people, "You have committed a great sin. And now I will go up to God, that I may make atonement for your sin."

**32:31** Then Moses returned to the Lord and said, "I plead with You, O Lord: this people has committed a great sin, and they have made for themselves gods of gold;

**32:32** and now, if You will forgive them their sin, forgive it; but if not, blot me out of Your book which You have written."

**32:33** And the Lord said to Moses, "If anyone has sinned, I will blot them out of My book.

**32:34** But as for you, go, go down, and lead this people to the place of which I spoke to you; behold, My angel goes before your face; and on whichever day I visit, I will bring their sin upon them."

**32:35** And the Lord struck the people because of the making of the calf which Aaron had made.

## Translator Notes

- **32:4** — The Greek uses a singular verb ("and he said"), attributing the declaration directly to Aaron, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads a plural ("and they said").
- **32:8** — In the Septuagint text, verse 9 of the Hebrew numbering (where the Lord calls Israel a stiff-necked people) is not present, and verse 8 reads "which you commanded them" rather than "which I commanded them."
- **32:14** — The Greek reads "was appeased, so as to preserve His people," whereas the Hebrew states that the Lord relented from the harm He had spoken of doing to His people.
- **32:18** — The Greek reads "leading a song of wine," highlighting drunken revelry in the camp, whereas the Hebrew reads simply "singing."
