## Text

**11:1** Now the Lord said to Moses, "Yet one more plague I will bring upon Pharaoh and upon Egypt, and after these things he will send you away from here; and when he sends you away, with everything he will utterly drive you out.

**11:2** Speak therefore secretly in the ears of the people, and let each one ask from his neighbor articles of silver and of gold, and clothing."

**11:3** And the Lord gave favor to His people in the sight of the Egyptians, and they lent to them; and the man Moses became exceedingly great in the sight of the Egyptians, and in the sight of Pharaoh, and in the sight of all his servants.

**11:4** And Moses said, "Thus says the Lord: 'About midnight I am going into the midst of Egypt;

**11:5** and every firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits upon the throne, even to the firstborn of the maidservant who is beside the mill, and to the firstborn of every beast;

**11:6** and there shall be a great outcry throughout all the land of Egypt, the like of which has never been, and the like of which shall never be repeated.

**11:7** Yet among all the sons of Israel no dog shall growl with its tongue, not even from man to beast, that you may see how wondrously the Lord makes distinction between the Egyptians and Israel.'

**11:8** And all these servants of yours shall come down to me and bow down to me, saying, 'Go out, you and all your people whom you lead!' And after this I will go out." Then Moses went out from Pharaoh in wrath.

**11:9** And the Lord said to Moses, "Pharaoh will not heed you, so that I may exceedingly multiply My signs and My wonders in the land of Egypt."

**11:10** Now Moses and Aaron performed all these signs and wonders in the land of Egypt before Pharaoh; yet the Lord hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he did not consent to send away the sons of Israel from the land of Egypt.

## Translator Notes

- **11:2** — The Greek adds "secretly" (kryphe) and "clothing" (himatismon) to the command, while omitting the separate clause about women asking their neighbors.
- **11:3** — The Greek adds "and they lent to them" (kai echresan autois) and lists three groups before whom Moses became great: the Egyptians, Pharaoh, and all his servants.
- **11:7** — The Greek verb paradoxazei denotes making a wondrous distinction, and "that you may see" (ides) is second-person singular rather than the Hebrew plural "that you may know."
- **11:9** — The Greek uses an emphatic first-person active idiom ("that I may exceedingly multiply My signs and My wonders"), whereas the Hebrew has a passive verb ("that My wonders may be multiplied").
