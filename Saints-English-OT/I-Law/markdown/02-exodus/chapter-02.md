## Text

**2:1** Now there was a certain man of the tribe of Levi who took one of the daughters of Levi.

**2:2** And she conceived and bore a male child; and seeing that he was fair, they hid him three months.

**2:3** And when they could no longer hide him, his mother took a basket for him and coated it with bitumen and pitch, and placed the child in it, and set it in the marsh by the river.

**2:4** And his sister kept watch from afar to learn what would happen to him.

**2:5** Then the daughter of Pharaoh came down to bathe at the river, and her maidservants walked along beside the river; and seeing the basket in the marsh, she sent her maidservant and took it up.

**2:6** And when she opened it, she saw a child weeping in the basket; and the daughter of Pharaoh took pity on him, and said, "This is one of the children of the Hebrews."

**2:7** Then his sister said to the daughter of Pharaoh, "Do you wish that I call for you a nursing woman from among the Hebrews, that she may nurse the child for you?"

**2:8** And the daughter of Pharaoh said to her, "Go." So the young maiden went and called the mother of the child.

**2:9** And the daughter of Pharaoh said to her, "Keep this child safely for me and nurse him for me, and I will give you your wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him.

**2:10** And when the child had grown, she brought him in to the daughter of Pharaoh, and he became a son to her; and she called his name Moses, saying, "Out of the water I took him up."

**2:11** Now it came to pass in those many days, when Moses had grown to manhood, that he went out to his brethren, the children of Israel. And observing their toil, he saw an Egyptian man striking a certain Hebrew, one of his own brethren of the children of Israel.

**2:12** And looking around this way and that, he saw no one; and having struck down the Egyptian, he hid him in the sand.

**2:13** And going out on the second day, he saw two Hebrew men fighting with one another, and he said to the one doing wrong, "Why do you strike your neighbor?"

**2:14** And he said, "Who appointed you ruler and judge over us? Do you intend to slay me in the manner you slew the Egyptian yesterday?" Then Moses feared and said, "Has this matter indeed become so openly known?"

**2:15** Now Pharaoh heard this matter, and sought to slay Moses; but Moses withdrew from the presence of Pharaoh and dwelt in the land of Midian; and when he came into the land of Midian, he sat down by the well.

**2:16** Now the priest of Midian had seven daughters, who tended the sheep of their father Jethro; and coming there, they drew water until they filled the troughs, to water the sheep of their father Jethro.

**2:17** And the shepherds came and drove them away; but Moses rose up and delivered them, and drew water for them, and watered their sheep.

**2:18** Then they came to Reuel their father; and he said to them, "Why have you made haste to return today?"

**2:19** And they said, "An Egyptian man delivered us from the shepherds, and also drew water for us and watered our sheep."

**2:20** And he said to his daughters, "And where is he? Why have you left the man behind? Call him, therefore, that he may eat bread."

**2:21** And Moses took up his abode with the man; and he gave Zipporah his daughter to Moses as a wife.

**2:22** And the woman conceived and bore a son; and Moses called his name Gershom, saying, "Because I am a sojourner in a strange land."

**2:23** Now after those many days, the king of Egypt died; and the children of Israel groaned because of their labors and cried out, and their cry went up to God because of their labors.

**2:24** And God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob.

**2:25** And God looked upon the children of Israel, and He was made known to them.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — Here and in verse 3, the Septuagint uses plural verbs ("seeing... they hid... they could no longer hide"), including both parents (compare Hebrews 11:23), whereas the Masoretic Text uses singular verbs referring to Moses's mother.
- **2:16** — The Septuagint explicitly names the priest of Midian "Jethro" twice in this verse, while verse 18 refers to him as "Reuel."
- **2:25** — The Septuagint renders the final clause as a passive ("and He was made known to them"), whereas the Masoretic Text reads "and God knew."
