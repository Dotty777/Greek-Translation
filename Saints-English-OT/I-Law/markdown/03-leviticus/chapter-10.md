## Text

**10:1** Then the two sons of Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, each took his censer, put fire upon it, laid incense upon it, and offered before the Lord strange fire, which the Lord had not commanded them.

**10:2** And fire came forth from the Lord and consumed them, and they died before the Lord.

**10:3** And Moses said to Aaron, "This is what the Lord spoke, saying, 'Among those who draw near to Me I will be sanctified, and in all the congregation I will be glorified.'" And Aaron was pierced to the heart.

**10:4** And Moses called Misadai and Elisaphan, sons of Aziel, sons of the brother of Aaron's father, and said to them, "Come near and carry your brethren from before the sanctuary, outside the camp."

**10:5** So they came near and carried them in their tunics outside the camp, just as Moses had spoken.

**10:6** And Moses said to Aaron, and to Eleazar and Ithamar his remaining sons, "You shall not take off the turbans from your heads, nor shall you tear your garments, lest you die and wrath come upon all the congregation. Your brethren, all the house of Israel, shall weep over the burning with which they were burned by the Lord.

**10:7** And you shall not go out from the door of the tabernacle of testimony, lest you die; for the anointing oil from the Lord is upon you." And they did according to the word of Moses.

**10:8** Then the Lord spoke to Aaron, saying,

**10:9** "You shall not drink wine or strong drink, you and your sons with you, whenever you enter the tabernacle of testimony, or when you approach the altar, and you shall by no means die; it shall be an everlasting statute throughout your generations,

**10:10** to distinguish between the holy and the profane, and between the unclean and the clean,

**10:11** and you shall instruct the sons of Israel in all the statutes which the Lord has spoken to them by the hand of Moses."

**10:12** And Moses said to Aaron, and to Eleazar and Ithamar, the remaining sons of Aaron, "Take the sacrifice that remains from the offerings of the Lord, and eat it unleavened beside the altar; it is most holy.

**10:13** And you shall eat it in a holy place, for this is an everlasting statute for you and a statute for your sons from the offerings of the Lord; for so it has been commanded me.

**10:14** And the breast of the set-apart offering and the shoulder of the contribution you shall eat in a holy place, you and your sons and your household with you; for it has been given as a statute for you and a statute for your sons from the sacrifices of salvation of the sons of Israel.

**10:15** The shoulder of the contribution and the breast of the set-apart offering they shall bring with the offerings of the fat portions, to set apart as a set-apart offering before the Lord; and it shall be for you and for your sons and for your daughters with you as an everlasting statute, just as the Lord commanded Moses."

**10:16** Then Moses inquired diligently about the goat of the sin offering, and behold, it had been burned up! And Moses was angry with Eleazar and Ithamar, the remaining sons of Aaron, saying,

**10:17** "Why did you not eat the sin offering in a holy place? For because it is most holy, He has given this to you to eat, that you may take away the sin of the congregation and make atonement for them before the Lord.

**10:18** For none of its blood was brought into the sanctuary; you were to eat it directly inside, in a holy place, just as the Lord commanded me."

**10:19** And Aaron spoke to Moses, saying, "If today they have offered their sin offerings and their whole burnt offerings before the Lord, and these things have befallen me, and I eat the sin offerings today, would it be pleasing to the Lord?"

**10:20** And Moses heard, and it pleased him.

## Translator Notes

- **10:3** — The Greek reads "in all the congregation" (Hebrew "before all the people") and renders Aaron's reaction with katenychthe ("was pierced to the heart"), whereas the Hebrew has "kept silent."
- **10:4** — The Greek preserves the Septuagint name forms Misadai (Hebrew Mishael), Elisaphan (Hebrew Elzaphan), and Aziel (Hebrew Uzziel), and describes Misadai and Elisaphan in plural apposition as "sons of the brother of Aaron's father."
- **10:6** — The Greek verb apokidaroo specifically means to remove the priestly turban (kidaris) from the head, and the final clause uses a passive verb ("with which they were burned by the Lord") rather than the Hebrew active ("which the Lord has kindled").
- **10:18** — The Greek phrase kata prosopon eso ("directly inside") stands at the head of the second clause in the Septuagint punctuation, modifying where the priests were to eat the offering.
