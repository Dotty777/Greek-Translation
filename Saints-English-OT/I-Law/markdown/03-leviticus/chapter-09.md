## Text

**9:1** And it came to pass on the eighth day that Moses called Aaron and his sons and the council of elders of Israel.

**9:2** And Moses said to Aaron, "Take for yourself a tender calf from the herd for a sin offering, and a ram for a burnt offering, without blemish, and offer them before the Lord;

**9:3** and speak to the council of elders of Israel, saying, 'Take one male goat from the goats for a sin offering, and a calf and a lamb a year old for a whole burnt offering, without blemish,

**9:4** and a young bull and a ram for a sacrifice of deliverance before the Lord, and fine flour mixed with oil; for today the Lord will appear among you.'"

**9:5** And they took what Moses commanded before the tabernacle of witness; and all the congregation drew near, and stood before the Lord.

**9:6** And Moses said, "Do this thing that the Lord has spoken, and the glory of the Lord will appear among you."

**9:7** And Moses said to Aaron, "Draw near to the altar, and offer your sin offering and your burnt offering, and make atonement for yourself and for your household; and offer the gifts of the people, and make atonement for them, just as the Lord commanded Moses."

**9:8** And Aaron drew near to the altar, and slew the calf for the sin offering.

**9:9** And the sons of Aaron brought the blood to him, and he dipped his finger into the blood and put it upon the horns of the altar, and poured out the blood at the base of the altar.

**9:10** And the fat, and the kidneys, and the lobe of the liver from the sin offering he offered up upon the altar, just as the Lord commanded Moses.

**9:11** And the flesh and the hide he burned completely with fire outside the camp.

**9:12** And he slew the burnt offering; and the sons of Aaron brought the blood also to him, and he poured it upon the altar all around.

**9:13** And as for the burnt offering, they brought it in pieces, both the pieces and the head, and he placed them upon the altar.

**9:14** And he washed the inward parts and the legs with water, and placed them upon the burnt offering on the altar.

**9:15** And they brought forward the gift of the people; and he took the male goat for the sin offering of the people, and slew it, just as he did the first.

**9:16** And he brought forward the burnt offering, and offered it as is fitting.

**9:17** And he brought forward the offering, and filled his hands from it, and placed it upon the altar, besides the morning burnt offering.

**9:18** And he slew the young bull and the ram of the sacrifice of deliverance, which was for the people; and the sons of Aaron brought the blood to him, and he poured it against the altar all around;

**9:19** and the fat from the young bull and from the ram—the loin, and the fat covering the inward parts, and the two kidneys and the fat that was upon them, and the lobe that was upon the liver;

**9:20** and he placed the fat portions upon the breasts, and they offered up the fat portions upon the altar;

**9:21** and the breast and the right shoulder Aaron set apart as a dedicated offering before the Lord, just as the Lord commanded Moses.

**9:22** And Aaron lifted up his hands toward the people and blessed them, and came down after offering the sin offering, and the burnt offerings, and the offerings of deliverance.

**9:23** And Moses and Aaron entered into the tabernacle of witness, and when they came out they blessed all the people; and the glory of the Lord appeared to all the people.

**9:24** And fire went out from the Lord and consumed what was upon the altar, both the burnt offerings and the fat portions; and all the people saw it and were astounded, and fell upon their faces.

## Translator Notes

- **9:4** — The Septuagint renders the Hebrew peace offering (shelamim) as "a sacrifice of deliverance" (thysia soteriou), emphasizing deliverance and well-being before the Lord.
- **9:7** — The Greek reads "for yourself and for your household," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "for yourself and for the people."
- **9:17** — The Greek noun thysian ("offering") here refers to the grain offering of fine flour mixed with oil prescribed in verse 4; the Greek also specifies "his hands" in the plural.
- **9:24** — The Greek verb exeste ("were astounded") describes the people's awe and amazement, whereas the Hebrew text describes them shouting aloud.
