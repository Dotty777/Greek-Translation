## Text

**12:1** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying,

**12:2** "Speak to the sons of Israel, and you shall say to them, 'Any woman who receives seed and bears a male shall be unclean seven days; according to the days of the separation of her menstruation, she shall be unclean.

**12:3** And on the eighth day she shall circumcise the flesh of his foreskin.

**12:4** And for thirty-three days she shall remain in her unclean blood; she shall not touch any vessel, and she shall not enter the sanctuary, until the days of her purification are fulfilled.

**12:5** But if she bears a female, she shall be unclean twice seven days, according to her menstruation; and for sixty-six days she shall remain in her unclean blood.

**12:6** And when the days of her purification are completed, for a son or for a daughter, she shall bring a lamb a year old, without blemish, for a whole burnt offering, and a young pigeon or a turtledove for a sin offering, to the door of the tabernacle of witness, to the priest.

**12:7** And he shall offer them before the Lord, and the priest shall make atonement for her, and he shall cleanse her from the fountain of her blood. This is the law for her who bears a male or a female.

**12:8** But if her hand does not find enough for a lamb, then she shall take two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one for a whole burnt offering and one for a sin offering; and the priest shall make atonement for her, and she shall be cleansed.'"

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — The Greek uses the active verb περιτεμεῖ ("she shall circumcise"), continuing the mother as subject, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text uses a passive verb ("shall be circumcised").
- **12:4** — The Greek text reads ἐν αἵματι ἀκαθάρτῳ αὐτῆς ("in her unclean blood"; likewise in v. 5), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("in the blood of purification"). It also reads ἀγγίου ("vessel"), whereas other Greek manuscripts read ἁγίου ("holy thing").
- **12:6** — The Septuagint consistently renders Hebrew "tent of meeting" as σκηνῆς τοῦ μαρτυρίου ("tabernacle of witness").
- **12:7** — The Greek uses the active verb καθαριεῖ αὐτήν ("he shall cleanse her"), making the priest the subject, whereas the Hebrew reads "she shall be clean" (matching the passive verb used in v. 8).
