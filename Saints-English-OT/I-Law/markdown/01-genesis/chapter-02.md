## Text

**2:1** And heaven and the earth were brought to completion, and all the array of them.

**2:2** And God finished on the sixth day His works which He had made; and He rested on the seventh day from all His works which He had made.

**2:3** And God blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, because on it He rested from all His works which God began to make.

**2:4** This is the book of the generation of heaven and earth, when it came into being, in the day in which the Lord God made heaven and the earth,

**2:5** and every green thing of the field before it came to be upon the earth, and every herb of the field before it sprang up; for God had not sent rain upon the earth, and there was no man to till the earth;

**2:6** but a spring went up out of the earth and watered all the face of the earth.

**2:7** And God formed man of dust from the earth, and breathed into his face the breath of life; and man became a living soul.

**2:8** And the Lord God planted a paradise in Eden toward the east, and there He placed the man whom He had formed.

**2:9** And God caused to spring forth yet out of the earth every tree pleasing to the sight and good for food, and the tree of life in the midst of the paradise, and the tree of knowing what is knowable of good and evil.

**2:10** Now a river goes forth out of Eden to water the paradise; from there it is parted into four heads.

**2:11** The name of the one is Pishon; this is the one that encompasses all the land of Havilah, there where the gold is;

**2:12** and the gold of that land is good; and there is the carbuncle and the green stone.

**2:13** And the name of the second river is Gihon; this is the one that encompasses all the land of Ethiopia.

**2:14** And the third river is Tigris; this is the one that goes over against the Assyrians. And the fourth river, this is Euphrates.

**2:15** And the Lord God took the man whom He had formed and placed him in the paradise, to till it and to keep it.

**2:16** And the Lord God commanded Adam, saying, "From every tree that is in the paradise you shall surely eat;

**2:17** but from the tree of knowing good and evil, you shall not eat of it; for in whatever day you eat of it, you shall surely die."

**2:18** And the Lord God said, "It is not good for man to be alone; let Us make for him a helper answering to him."

**2:19** And God formed yet out of the earth all the wild beasts of the field and all the birds of heaven, and brought them to Adam to see what he would call them; and whatever Adam called it, a living soul, that was its name.

**2:20** And Adam gave names to all the cattle, and to all the birds of heaven, and to all the wild beasts of the field; yet for Adam there was not found a helper like him.

**2:21** And God cast a trance upon Adam, and he slept; and He took one of his ribs and filled up flesh in its place.

**2:22** And the Lord God built the rib which He had taken from Adam into a woman, and brought her to Adam.

**2:23** And Adam said, "This now is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because out of her husband she was taken."

**2:24** For this cause a man shall leave his father and his mother and cleave to his wife; and the two shall become one flesh.

**2:25** And the two were naked, both Adam and his wife, and they were not ashamed.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Septuagint reads 'on the sixth day' (en te hemera te hekte), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew text reads 'on the seventh day.'
- **2:7** — Greek reads 'into his face' (eis to prosopon autou), where the Hebrew specifies 'into his nostrils.'
- **2:8** — Greek paradeisos ('paradise,' an enclosed park or orchard) is the Septuagint's regular term for Eden's garden.
- **2:16** — In the Greek text, the Lord addresses Adam in the second-person singular in verse 16 (phage) and shifts to the second-person plural in verse 17 (phagesthe ... apothaneisthe).
- **2:18** — The Greek reads first-person plural poiesomen ('let Us make'), echoing 1:26, where the Hebrew has singular 'I will make.'
