## Text

**50:1** And Joseph fell upon the face of his father, wept over him, and kissed him.

**50:2** And Joseph commanded his servants the embalmers to embalm his father; so the embalmers embalmed Israel.

**50:3** And he fulfilled forty days for them, for thus the days of embalming are reckoned; and Egypt mourned for him seventy days.

**50:4** Now when the days of mourning had passed, Joseph spoke to the officials of Pharaoh, saying, "If I have found favor before you, speak concerning me in the ears of Pharaoh, saying,

**50:5** 'My father made me swear, saying, "In the tomb which I dug for myself in the land of Canaan, there you shall bury me." Now therefore I will go up and bury my father, and I will return.'"

**50:6** And Pharaoh said, "Go up, bury your father, just as he made you swear."

**50:7** So Joseph went up to bury his father; and with him went up all the servants of Pharaoh and the elders of his house, and all the elders of the land of Egypt,

**50:8** and all the household of Joseph, and his brethren, and all his father's house, and his kindred; and they left behind the sheep and the cattle in the land of Goshen.

**50:9** And both chariots and horsemen went up with him, and the company was exceedingly great.

**50:10** And they came to the threshing floor of Atad, which is beyond the Jordan, and they lamented for him with a great and exceedingly strong lamentation; and they observed mourning for his father seven days.

**50:11** And the inhabitants of the land of Canaan saw the mourning at the threshing floor of Atad, and said, "This is a great mourning for the Egyptians." Therefore its name was called Mourning of Egypt, which is beyond the Jordan.

**50:12** And his sons did thus for him, and buried him there.

**50:13** And his sons carried him up into the land of Canaan, and buried him in the double cave, the cave which Abraham acquired for a burial possession from Ephron the Hittite, opposite Mamre.

**50:14** And Joseph returned to Egypt, he and his brethren and those who had gone up with him to bury his father.

**50:15** Now when the brethren of Joseph saw that their father was dead, they said, "Perhaps Joseph will hold a grudge against us, and will surely repay us for all the evils which we did to him."

**50:16** And they came to Joseph, saying, "Your father gave a sworn charge before his death, saying,

**50:17** 'Thus say to Joseph, "Forgive them their unrighteousness and their sin, for they did wicked things to you."' And now, pardon the unrighteousness of the servants of the God of your father." And Joseph wept while they spoke to him.

**50:18** And coming to him, they said, "Behold, we are household servants."

**50:19** And Joseph said to them, "Do not be afraid, for I belong to God.

**50:20** You purposed against me for evil, but God purposed concerning me for good, so that it might come to pass as it is this day, that a great people might be nourished."

**50:21** And he said to them, "Do not be afraid; I will nourish you and your households." And he comforted them and spoke to their heart.

**50:22** And Joseph dwelt in Egypt, he and his brethren and all his father's household; and Joseph lived one hundred and ten years.

**50:23** And Joseph saw children of Ephraim to the third generation; and the sons of Machir, the son of Manasseh, were born upon the thighs of Joseph.

**50:24** And Joseph spoke to his brethren, saying, "I am dying; but God will surely visit you, and will bring you up out of this land to the land which God swore to our fathers, Abraham and Isaac and Jacob."

**50:25** And Joseph made the sons of Israel swear, saying, "In the visitation with which God will visit you, you shall also carry up my bones from here with you."

**50:26** And Joseph died, being one hundred and ten years old; and they embalmed him, and placed him in the coffin in Egypt.

## Translator Notes

- **50:2** — The Greek designates Joseph's servants specifically as "embalmers" (entaphiastai), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "physicians."
- **50:11** — The Greek translates the place name as "Mourning of Egypt" (Penthos Aigyptou), rendering the Hebrew name Abel-mizraim.
- **50:13** — The Greek renders the Hebrew name Machpelah descriptively as "the double cave" (to spelaion to diploun) and does not mention the field alongside the cave.
- **50:19** — The Greek reads as a direct declaration, "for I belong to God," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text poses a rhetorical question ("Am I in the place of God?").
- **50:26** — The Greek verb used here (thapto, ordinarily "bury") carries its Ptolemaic Egyptian sense of preparing a body by embalming prior to placing it in a coffin, matching "embalming" (taphe) in verse 3.
