## Text

**4:1** Now Adam knew his wife Eve, and she conceived and bore Cain; and she said, "I have obtained a man through God."

**4:2** Then again she bore his brother Abel. Now Abel became a shepherd of sheep, while Cain was tilling the ground.

**4:3** And it came to pass after some days that Cain brought from the fruits of the earth a sacrifice to the Lord.

**4:4** And Abel also brought from the firstborn of his sheep and from their fat portions. And God looked upon Abel and upon his gifts,

**4:5** but to Cain and to his sacrifices He gave no heed. And it grieved Cain exceedingly, and his countenance fell.

**4:6** Then the Lord God said to Cain, "Why have you become deeply grieved, and why has your countenance fallen?

**4:7** If you offer rightly, yet do not divide rightly, have you not sinned? Be still; to you shall be his turning, and you shall rule over him."

**4:8** And Cain said to Abel his brother, "Let us go out into the plain." And it came to pass, while they were in the plain, that Cain rose up against Abel his brother and slew him.

**4:9** Then God said to Cain, "Where is your brother Abel?" And he answered, "I do not know. Am I the keeper of my brother?"

**4:10** And God said, "What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood cries out to Me from the earth.

**4:11** And now cursed are you upon the earth, which has opened wide its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand.

**4:12** For you shall till the earth, and it shall not again yield its strength to you; groaning and trembling you shall be upon the earth."

**4:13** And Cain said to the Lord, "My guilt is too great for me to be forgiven.

**4:14** If You cast me out this day from the face of the earth, I shall also be hidden from Your face, and I shall be groaning and trembling upon the earth; and everyone who finds me will slay me."

**4:15** And the Lord God said to him, "Not so! Everyone who slays Cain shall discharge sevenfold vengeance." And the Lord God set a sign for Cain, lest anyone who found him should kill him.

**4:16** So Cain went out from the presence of God and dwelt in the land of Naid, opposite Eden.

**4:17** And Cain knew his wife, and she conceived and bore Enoch. And he was building a city, and he named the city after the name of his son, Enoch.

**4:18** Now to Enoch was born Gaidad; and Gaidad fathered Maiel, and Maiel fathered Methuselah, and Methuselah fathered Lamech.

**4:19** And Lamech took for himself two wives: the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the second was Zillah.

**4:20** And Adah bore Jabal; he was the father of those who dwell in tents as keepers of livestock.

**4:21** And his brother's name was Jubal; he was the one who introduced the harp and the lyre.

**4:22** And Zillah also bore Tubal; and he was a hammer-forger, a smith of bronze and iron. And the sister of Tubal was Naamah.

**4:23** Then Lamech said to his wives, "Adah and Zillah, hear my voice; wives of Lamech, give ear to my words: for I have slain a man to my wounding, and a young man to my bruising;

**4:24** because sevenfold vengeance has been taken for Cain, yet for Lamech seventy times seven."

**4:25** And Adam knew his wife Eve, and she conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Seth, saying, "For God has raised up for me another seed in place of Abel, whom Cain slew."

**4:26** And to Seth a son was born, and he called his name Enosh. This man hoped to call upon the name of the Lord God.

## Translator Notes

- **4:7** — The Septuagint reads this difficult verse in terms of rightly offering and rightly dividing a sacrifice, followed by "Be still; to you shall be his turning," differing notably from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's imagery of sin crouching at the door.
- **4:16** — The Greek text reads "Naid," corresponding to "Nod" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **4:18** — The Greek text preserves the distinct name forms "Gaidad" (Hebrew "Irad"), "Maiel" (Hebrew "Mehujael"), and "Methuselah" (the same name as in 5:21, where Hebrew has "Methushael").
- **4:22** — The Greek text reads simply "Tubal" (Thobel), without the "-cain" element found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("Tubal-cain").
- **4:26** — The Greek text reads "This man hoped to call upon the name of the Lord God," reflecting a different understanding of the Hebrew verb than the Masoretic Text ("then men began to call").
