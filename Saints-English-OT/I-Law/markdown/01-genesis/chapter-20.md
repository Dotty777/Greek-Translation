## Text

**20:1** And Abraham moved from there to the land toward the south, and he dwelt between Kadesh and Shur; and he sojourned in Gerar.

**20:2** And Abraham said concerning Sarah his wife, "She is my sister," for he feared to say, "She is my wife," lest the men of the city slay him on her account. And Abimelech king of Gerar sent and took Sarah.

**20:3** And God came in to Abimelech in a dream by night, and said to him, "Behold, you are about to die because of the woman whom you have taken, for she is married to a husband."

**20:4** Now Abimelech had not touched her; and he said, "Lord, will You destroy an unwitting and righteous nation?

**20:5** Did he not himself say to me, 'She is my sister'? And she said to me, 'He is my brother.' In a pure heart and in righteousness of hands I have done this."

**20:6** And God said to him in a dream, "I also knew that you did this in a pure heart, and I spared you so that you would not sin against Me; for this reason I did not permit you to touch her.

**20:7** Now therefore restore the wife to the man, for he is a prophet, and he shall pray for you, and you shall live. But if you do not restore her, know that you shall die, you and all that belongs to you."

**20:8** And Abimelech rose early in the morning and called all his servants, and spoke all these words in their ears; and all the men feared exceedingly.

**20:9** And Abimelech called Abraham and said to him, "What is this that you have done to us? Have we sinned in anything against you, that you have brought upon me and upon my kingdom a great sin? You have done to me a deed that no one should do."

**20:10** And Abimelech said to Abraham, "What did you see that you did this?"

**20:11** And Abraham said, "Because I said, 'Surely there is no reverence for God in this place, and they will slay me on account of my wife.'

**20:12** And indeed she is truly my sister, by my father, though not by my mother; and she became my wife.

**20:13** And it came to pass, when God brought me out from the house of my father, that I said to her, 'Show this righteousness toward me: in every place into which we may enter, say of me, "He is my brother."'"

**20:14** And Abimelech took a thousand didrachmas, sheep and calves, and menservants and maidservants, and gave them to Abram; and he restored to him Sarah his wife.

**20:15** And Abimelech said to Abraham, "Behold, my land is before you; dwell wherever it pleases you."

**20:16** And to Sarah he said, "Behold, I have given a thousand didrachmas to your brother; these shall be to you for the honor of your countenance and for all the women with you; and speak the truth in all things."

**20:17** And Abraham prayed to God, and God healed Abimelech, and his wife, and his maidservants, and they bore children;

**20:18** for the Lord had shut up from outside every womb in the house of Abimelech on account of Sarah, Abraham's wife.

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — The Greek text includes an explicit explanatory clause absent from the Masoretic Text: "for he feared to say, 'She is my wife,' lest the men of the city slay him on her account."
- **20:4** — The Septuagint reads "an unwitting and righteous nation" (Greek agnooun), emphasizing Abimelech's innocence through ignorance, where the Masoretic Text reads "even a righteous nation."
- **20:14** — The Greek text adds "a thousand didrachmas" here (anticipating verse 16) and reads the earlier name form "Abram" rather than "Abraham."
- **20:16** — The Septuagint renders Abimelech's words distinctly as "a thousand didrachmas" given "for the honor of your countenance and for all the women with you," closing with the imperative "speak the truth in all things."
