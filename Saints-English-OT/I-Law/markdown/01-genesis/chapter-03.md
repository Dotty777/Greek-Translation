## Text

**3:1** Now the serpent was the most shrewd of all the wild beasts upon the earth which the Lord God had made; and the serpent said to the woman, "Why is it that God has said, 'You shall by no means eat from every tree of the paradise'?"

**3:2** And the woman said to the serpent, "From every tree of the paradise we may eat;

**3:3** but from the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the paradise, God has said, 'You shall not eat of it, nor shall you touch it, lest you die.'"

**3:4** And the serpent said to the woman, "You shall not surely die;

**3:5** for God knew that in whatever day you eat of it, your eyes shall be opened, and you shall be as gods, knowing good and evil."

**3:6** And the woman beheld that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasing to the eyes to look upon and fair to contemplate; and having taken of its fruit, she ate, and gave also to her husband with her, and they ate.

**3:7** And the eyes of the two were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together and made for themselves aprons.

**3:8** And they heard the voice of the Lord God walking in the paradise in the cool of the afternoon; and both Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the Lord God in the midst of the tree of the paradise.

**3:9** And the Lord God called Adam and said to him, "Adam, where are you?"

**3:10** And he said to Him, "I heard Your voice as You walked in the paradise, and I was afraid, because I am naked, and I hid myself."

**3:11** And He said to him, "Who told you that you are naked, unless from the tree of which I commanded you, of this alone not to eat, from it you have eaten?"

**3:12** And Adam said, "The woman whom You gave to be with me, she gave to me from the tree, and I ate."

**3:13** And the Lord God said to the woman, "What is this that you have done?" And the woman said, "The serpent deceived me, and I ate."

**3:14** And the Lord God said to the serpent, "Because you have done this, cursed are you beyond all cattle and beyond all wild beasts of the earth; upon your breast and belly you shall go, and earth you shall eat all the days of your life.

**3:15** And I will set enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed; he shall watch for your head, and you shall watch for his heel."

**3:16** And to the woman He said, "Multiplying I will multiply your sorrows and your groaning; in sorrows you shall bring forth children, and toward your husband shall be your turning, and he shall rule over you."

**3:17** And to Adam He said, "Because you have heeded the voice of your wife, and have eaten from the tree of which I commanded you, of this alone not to eat, yet from it you have eaten, cursed is the earth in your labors; in sorrows you shall eat of it all the days of your life;

**3:18** thorns and thistles it shall bring forth for you, and you shall eat the herb of the field.

**3:19** In the sweat of your face you shall eat your bread until you return to the earth out of which you were taken; for earth you are, and to earth you shall depart."

**3:20** And Adam called the name of his wife Life, because she is the mother of all the living.

**3:21** And the Lord God made for Adam and for his wife tunics of skins, and clothed them.

**3:22** And the Lord God said, "Behold, Adam has become as one of Us, to know good and evil; and now, lest he stretch forth his hand and take of the tree of life and eat, and live forever—"

**3:23** and the Lord God sent him forth out of the paradise of delight, to till the earth out of which he was taken.

**3:24** And He cast Adam out and caused him to dwell over against the paradise of delight, and He stationed the cherubim and the flaming sword that turns every way, to guard the way of the tree of life.

## Translator Notes

- **3:15** — The Greek reads masculine autos ('he') with the verb teresei ('shall watch for' or 'lie in wait for'), distinct from both the Hebrew ('bruise/crush') and the Latin Vulgate ('she shall crush').
- **3:16** — Greek apostrophe means 'turning' (or 'recourse'), where the Hebrew reads 'desire.'
- **3:17** — Greek reads 'in your labors/works' (en tois ergois sou), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonants than Masoretic 'because of you.'
- **3:20** — The Septuagint translates Hebrew Hawwah ('Eve') directly into Greek as Zoe ('Life'), preserving the wordplay with 'all the living.'
