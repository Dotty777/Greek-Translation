## Text

**22:1** Now it came to pass after these things that God tested Abraham, and said to him, "Abraham, Abraham!" And he said, "Behold, here I am."

**22:2** And He said, "Take your beloved son whom you have loved, Isaac, and journey to the high land, and offer him up there for a whole burnt offering upon one of the mountains of which I shall tell you."

**22:3** So Abraham rose early in the morning and saddled his donkey; and he took with himself two young men and his son Isaac. And having split wood for a whole burnt offering, he arose and went, and came on the third day to the place of which God had told him.

**22:4** And Abraham lifted up his eyes and beheld the place afar off.

**22:5** And Abraham said to his servants, "Remain here with the donkey, and I and the lad will go on over there; and after we have worshiped, we shall return to you."

**22:6** Then Abraham took the wood of the whole burnt offering and laid it upon his son Isaac; and he also took the fire in his hand, and the knife, and the two of them went on together.

**22:7** And Isaac spoke to Abraham his father, saying, "Father!" And he said, "What is it, my child?" And he said, "Behold, the fire and the wood; where is the sheep for a whole burnt offering?"

**22:8** And Abraham said, "God will provide for Himself a sheep for a whole burnt offering, my child." And both of them went on together,

**22:9** and came to the place of which God had spoken to him. And Abraham built an altar there and laid the wood upon it; and having bound his son Isaac, he placed him upon the altar, on top of the wood.

**22:10** And Abraham stretched out his hand to take the knife, to slay his son.

**22:11** And the angel of the Lord called to him out of heaven and said to him, "Abraham, Abraham!" And he said, "Behold, here I am."

**22:12** And He said, "Do not lay your hand upon the lad, nor do anything to him; for now I know that you fear God, and have not spared your beloved son for My sake."

**22:13** Then Abraham lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, one ram caught by its horns in a sabek bush. And Abraham went and took the ram, and offered it up for a whole burnt offering in place of his son Isaac.

**22:14** And Abraham called the name of that place, "The Lord Has Seen"; so that they say today, "On the mountain the Lord has appeared."

**22:15** Then the angel of the Lord called to Abraham a second time out of heaven,

**22:16** saying, "By Myself I have sworn, says the Lord: because you have done this thing, and have not spared your beloved son for My sake,

**22:17** surely blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply your seed as the stars of heaven and as the sand along the shore of the sea; and your seed shall inherit the cities of the adversaries.

**22:18** And in your seed all the nations shall be blessed, because you have obeyed My voice."

**22:19** So Abraham returned to his servants, and they arose and went to the Well of the Oath; and Abraham dwelt at the Well of the Oath.

**22:20** Now it came to pass after these things that it was reported to Abraham, saying, "Behold, Milcah also has borne sons to your brother Nahor:

**22:21** Ox his firstborn, and Baux his brother, and Kemuel the father of the Syrians,

**22:22** and Chaszad, and Hazo, and Pildash, and Jeldaph, and Bethuel."

**22:23** And Bethuel fathered Rebekah. These eight were the sons whom Milcah bore to Nahor, the brother of Abraham.

**22:24** And his concubine, whose name was Reera, she also bore Tabek, and Taam, and Tahash, and Maacah.

## Translator Notes

- **22:2** — The Greek renders the land of Moriah descriptively as "the high land" (ten gen ten hypselen) and refers to Isaac as Abraham's "beloved son" (agapeton) rather than his "only son."
- **22:13** — The Greek reads "one ram" (krios heis) and transliterates the Hebrew word for thicket as a plant name, "a sabek bush."
- **22:14** — The place name "The Lord Has Seen" (Kyrios eiden) and the saying "On the mountain the Lord has appeared" (ophthe) echo Abraham's words in v. 8, where the verb rendered "will provide" (opsetai) literally means "will see."
- **22:21** — In vv. 21-24 the Greek preserves several distinct name forms that differ in consonants from the Hebrew: Ox (Uz), Baux (Buz), Chaszad (Chesed), Jeldaph (Jidlaph), Reera (Reumah), Tabek (Tebah), and Taam (Gaham).
