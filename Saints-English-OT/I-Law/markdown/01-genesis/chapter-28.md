## Text

**28:1** Then Isaac called Jacob and blessed him, and commanded him, saying, "You shall not take a wife from the daughters of Canaan.

**28:2** Arise, flee into Mesopotamia of Syria to the house of Bethuel your mother's father, and take for yourself a wife from there from the daughters of Laban your mother's brother.

**28:3** And may my God bless you, and make you increase, and multiply you, and you shall become assemblies of nations;

**28:4** and may He give you the blessing of Abraham your father, to you and to your seed after you, that you may inherit the land of your sojourning, which God has given to Abraham."

**28:5** So Isaac sent Jacob away, and he went to Mesopotamia, to Laban the son of Bethuel the Syrian, the brother of Rebekah, the mother of Jacob and Esau.

**28:6** Now Esau saw that Isaac had blessed Jacob and that he had departed into Mesopotamia of Syria to take a wife for himself, and that while blessing him he had commanded him, saying, "You shall not take a wife from the daughters of Canaan,"

**28:7** and that Jacob had obeyed his father and his mother and had gone to Mesopotamia.

**28:8** And Esau saw that the daughters of Canaan were evil in the sight of Isaac his father;

**28:9** so Esau went to Ishmael and took Mahalath the daughter of Ishmael, the son of Abraham, the sister of Nebaioth, as a wife in addition to his wives.

**28:10** Now Jacob went out from the Well of the Oath and went toward Haran.

**28:11** And he came upon a place and lay down there, for the sun had set; and he took one of the stones of the place and placed it at his head, and he slept in that place.

**28:12** And he dreamed, and behold, a ladder was set firmly on the earth, whose top reached to heaven; and the angels of God were ascending and descending upon it.

**28:13** And the Lord stood firmly upon it and said, "I am the God of Abraham your father and the God of Isaac. Do not be afraid; the land upon which you sleep, to you I will give it and to your seed.

**28:14** And your seed shall be as the sand of the sea, and it shall spread abroad to the west, and to the south, and to the north, and to the east; and in you all the tribes of the earth shall be blessed, and in your seed.

**28:15** And behold, I am with you, guarding you in all the way wherever you go, and I will bring you back to this land; for I will by no means forsake you until I have done all that I have spoken to you."

**28:16** Then Jacob awoke from his sleep and said, "The Lord is in this place, and I did not know it."

**28:17** And he was afraid and said, "How fearful is this place! This is none other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven!"

**28:18** And Jacob rose up in the morning, and took the stone which he had placed there at his head, and set it up as a pillar, and poured oil upon the top of it.

**28:19** And Jacob called the name of that place House of God; yet Oulammaus was the name of the city previously.

**28:20** And Jacob vowed a vow, saying, "If the Lord God is with me, and guards me in this way in which I am going, and gives me bread to eat and a garment to put on,

**28:21** and brings me back in safety to the house of my father, then the Lord shall be my God;

**28:22** and this stone, which I have set up as a pillar, shall be to me a house of God; and of all that You give to me, I will surely give a tenth of them to You."

## Translator Notes

- **28:3** — The Greek reads "my God" (ho theos mou), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "God Almighty" (El Shaddai).
- **28:10** — The Greek translates the place name Beersheba literally as "the Well of the Oath."
- **28:13** — The Greek adds "Do not be afraid" and uses epestērikto ("stood firmly upon it"), echoing esterigmene ("set firmly") in verse 12.
- **28:14** — The Greek reads "the sand of the sea" (instead of the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "dust of the earth") and lists the directions in the order west (literally "sea"), south, north, and east.
- **28:19** — The Greek translates Bethel literally as "House of God" and gives the city's earlier name as Oulammaus, a Septuagint-distinct form combining the Hebrew adverb "however" (ulam) with the place name Luz.
