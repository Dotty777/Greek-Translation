## Text

**40:1** Now it came to pass after these things that the chief cupbearer of the king of Egypt and the chief baker sinned against their lord, the king of Egypt.

**40:2** And Pharaoh was angry with his two eunuchs, with the chief cupbearer and with the chief baker;

**40:3** and he put them in custody with the chief jailer in the prison, in the place where Joseph had been led away.

**40:4** And the chief jailer entrusted them to Joseph, and he attended them; and they were in custody for some days.

**40:5** And both of them saw a dream, each one a dream in one night—a vision of his own dream—the chief cupbearer and the chief baker who belonged to the king of Egypt, who were in the prison.

**40:6** Now Joseph came in to them in the morning, and saw them, and they were troubled.

**40:7** And he asked the eunuchs of Pharaoh, who were with him in custody under his master, saying, "Why are your countenances downcast today?"

**40:8** And they said to him, "We have seen a dream, and there is no one to interpret it." Then Joseph said to them, "Is not the explanation of them through God? Tell me, therefore."

**40:9** So the chief cupbearer related his dream to Joseph and said, "In my sleep, there was a vine before me;

**40:10** and in the vine were three stems, and it was flourishing, having put forth shoots; the clusters of grapes were ripe.

**40:11** And the cup of Pharaoh was in my hand; and I took the grapes and pressed them into the cup, and I gave the cup into the hands of Pharaoh."

**40:12** And Joseph said to him, "This is its interpretation: the three stems are three days;

**40:13** yet three days, and Pharaoh will remember your office and will restore you to your office of chief cupbearer, and you shall give Pharaoh's cup into his hand according to your former office, as when you were cupbearer.

**40:14** But remember me on your own when it goes well with you, and show mercy to me, and make mention of me to Pharaoh, and bring me out of this stronghold;

**40:15** for indeed I was stolen away from the land of the Hebrews; and here I have done nothing, yet they cast me into this pit."

**40:16** Now the chief baker saw that he had interpreted rightly, and he said to Joseph, "I also saw a dream, and I thought I was carrying three baskets of coarse-wheat loaves upon my head;

**40:17** and in the uppermost basket were some of all kinds of foods which King Pharaoh eats, the work of a baker; and the birds of the air were devouring them from the basket upon my head."

**40:18** And Joseph answered and said to him, "This is its interpretation: the three baskets are three days;

**40:19** within three more days Pharaoh will take away your head from you, and will hang you upon a tree, and the birds of the air shall eat your flesh from off you."

**40:20** Now it came to pass on the third day, which was Pharaoh's birthday, that he made a banquet for all his servants; and he remembered the office of the chief cupbearer and the office of the chief baker in the midst of his servants.

**40:21** And he restored the chief cupbearer to his office, and he gave the cup into the hand of Pharaoh;

**40:22** but the chief baker he hanged, just as Joseph had interpreted to them.

**40:23** The chief cupbearer, however, did not remember Joseph, but forgot him.

## Translator Notes

- **40:4** — The Greek uses the rare title ἀρχιδεσμώτης (here rendered "chief jailer") in place of ἀρχιδεσμοφύλαξ used in 39:21–23 and 40:3.
- **40:5** — The Greek reads "a vision of his own dream" (ὅρασις τοῦ ἐνυπνίου αὐτοῦ), contrasting with "according to the interpretation of his dream" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **40:13** — The Greek renders the Hebrew idiom "lift up your head" as "remember your office" (μνησθήσεται ... τῆς ἀρχῆς σου; likewise in 40:20).
- **40:16** — The Greek specifies "coarse-wheat loaves" (χονδριτῶν), bread made from groats, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to white or wicker baskets.
