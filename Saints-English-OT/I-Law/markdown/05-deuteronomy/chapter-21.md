## Text

**21:1** Now if a slain man is found in the land which the Lord your God is giving to you to inherit, fallen in the plain, and they do not know who struck him,

**21:2** your elders and your judges shall go out, and they shall measure to the cities that are around the slain man.

**21:3** And it shall be, for the city that is nearest to the slain man, that the elders of that city shall take a heifer from the herd, which has not been worked and which has not pulled a yoke;

**21:4** and the elders of that city shall bring the heifer down into a rough ravine, which is neither worked nor sown, and they shall hamstring the heifer in the ravine.

**21:5** And the priests, the Levites, shall come near, for the Lord God has chosen them to stand before Him and to bless in His name, and by their mouth shall be every dispute and every assault.

**21:6** And all the elders of that city who are nearest to the slain man shall wash their hands over the head of the heifer that was hamstrung in the ravine,

**21:7** and they shall answer and say, "Our hands have not shed this blood, and our eyes have not seen it.

**21:8** Be merciful to Your people Israel, whom You have redeemed, O Lord, so that innocent blood may not be among Your people Israel." And the blood shall be atoned for them.

**21:9** So you shall remove the innocent blood from among yourselves, if you do what is good and what is pleasing before the Lord your God.

**21:10** Now when you go out to war against your enemies, and the Lord your God delivers them into your hands, and you plunder their spoil,

**21:11** and you see among the spoil a woman beautiful in appearance, and you desire her and take her for yourself as a wife,

**21:12** then you shall bring her inside your house, and you shall shave her head and pare her nails;

**21:13** and you shall remove the garments of captivity from her, and she shall sit in your house and weep for her father and her mother for the days of a month; and after this you shall go in to her and be joined to her in marriage, and she shall be your wife.

**21:14** And it shall be, if you do not want her, that you shall send her away free, and she shall by no means be sold for silver; you shall not deal treacherously with her, because you have humbled her.

**21:15** Now if a man has two wives, one of them loved and one of them hated, and both the loved and the hated give birth for him, and the firstborn son belongs to the hated one,

**21:16** then it shall be, on the day when he apportions his possessions as an inheritance to his sons, that he shall not be able to grant the birthright to the son of the loved one, overlooking the son of the hated one, the firstborn;

**21:17** rather, he shall acknowledge the firstborn son of the hated one by giving him a double portion from all that is found with him; for this one is the beginning of his children, and to him belongs the birthright.

**21:18** Now if anyone has a disobedient and contentious son, who does not obey the voice of his father and the voice of his mother, and they discipline him and he does not listen to them,

**21:19** then his father and his mother shall take hold of him and bring him out to the elders of his city and to the gate of the place,

**21:20** and they shall say to the men of their city, "This son of ours is disobedient and contentious; he does not obey our voice; carousing in revelry, he is given to drunkenness."

**21:21** Then the men of his city shall stone him with stones, and he shall die; and you shall remove the wicked one from among yourselves, and the rest shall hear and fear.

**21:22** Now if there is in anyone a sin whose judgment is death, and he dies, and you hang him upon a tree,

**21:23** his body shall not remain overnight upon the tree, but you shall surely bury it on that day, for everyone who is hanged upon a tree is accursed by God; and you shall not defile the land which the Lord your God is giving to you as an inheritance.

## Translator Notes

- **21:4** — The Septuagint specifies a 'rough ravine' rather than a perennial stream valley, and 'hamstring' (neurokopēsousin, severing the sinews) rather than breaking the heifer's neck as in the Hebrew.
- **21:12** — In verses 12–13 the Greek uses second-person singular verbs ('you shall shave... pare... remove'), assigning these actions to the Israelite man rather than to the captive woman as in the Hebrew.
- **21:17** — The Greek reads 'the beginning of his children' (archē teknōn autou), where the Hebrew has 'the beginning of his strength.'
- **21:23** — The Septuagint explicitly includes 'everyone' (pas) in 'everyone who is hanged upon a tree is accursed by God,' a wording closely echoed in Galatians 3:13.
