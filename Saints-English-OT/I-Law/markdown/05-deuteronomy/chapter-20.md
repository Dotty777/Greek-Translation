## Text

**20:1** Now when you go out to war against your enemies, and you see horse and rider and a people more numerous than you, you shall not be afraid of them; for the Lord your God is with you, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt.

**20:2** And it shall be, when you draw near to the battle, that the priest shall approach and speak to the people,

**20:3** and shall say to them, "Hear, O Israel: you are marching today to the battle against your enemies; do not let your heart grow faint, do not fear, nor be broken, nor turn aside from before their face,

**20:4** for the Lord your God is He who goes forward with you, to fight together with you against your enemies, to deliver you."

**20:5** And the scribes shall speak to the people, saying, "Who is the man who has built a new house and has not dedicated it? Let him go and return to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man dedicate it.

**20:6** And who is the man who has planted a vineyard and has not rejoiced from it? Let him go and return to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man rejoice from it.

**20:7** And who is the man who has betrothed a woman and has not taken her? Let him go and return to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man take her."

**20:8** And the scribes shall speak further to the people and say, "Who is the man who is fearful and cowardly in heart? Let him go and return to his house, lest he make the heart of his brother cowardly like his own."

**20:9** And it shall be, when the scribes finish speaking to the people, that they shall appoint commanders of the army to lead the people.

**20:10** Now when you approach a city to make war against them, then summon them with peace.

**20:11** If they answer you peaceably and open to you, then it shall be that all the people who are found in it shall be tributary to you and obedient to you.

**20:12** But if they do not obey you, and make war against you, you shall besiege it,

**20:13** until the Lord your God delivers it into your hands; and you shall strike every male of it with the slaughter of the sword,

**20:14** except the women and the dependents; and whatever is in the city and all the belongings you shall plunder for yourself, and you shall eat all the plunder of your enemies whom the Lord your God is giving to you.

**20:15** Thus you shall do to all the cities that are very far from you; not from the cities of these nations

**20:16** whose land the Lord your God is giving to you to inherit shall you leave alive anything that breathes,

**20:17** but you shall surely devote them to destruction—the Hittite, and the Amorite, and the Canaanite, and the Perizzite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite—just as the Lord your God has commanded you,

**20:18** lest they teach you to do all their abominations which they have done to their gods, and you sin before the Lord your God.

**20:19** Now if you besiege one city for many days, making war against it to capture it, you shall not destroy its trees by laying iron upon them; rather, you shall eat from it, and you shall not cut it down. Is the tree in the field a man, to enter into the siegework from before your face?

**20:20** Only a tree which you know does not bear edible fruit, that one you shall destroy and cut down, and you shall build a siegework against the city that makes war against you, until it is delivered up.

## Translator Notes

- **20:5** — Where the Hebrew refers to 'officers,' the Septuagint renders the term as 'scribes' (grammateis) here and in verses 8–9.
- **20:14** — The Greek terms aposkeuē ('dependents' or household retinue) and apartia ('belongings' or movable goods) correspond to 'little ones' and 'livestock' in the Hebrew.
- **20:15** — The Greek text connects 'not from the cities of these nations' directly to the prohibition in verse 16, omitting the repeated introductory clause found in the Hebrew.
- **20:19** — The Greek explicitly reads 'one city' and frames the closing clause as a rhetorical question ('Is the tree in the field a man...?') expecting a negative answer.
