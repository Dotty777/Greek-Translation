## Text

**24:1** Now if anyone takes a wife and dwells with her, and it comes to pass that she does not find favor before him because he has found an unseemly thing in her, and he writes for her a bill of divorce and puts it into her hands, and sends her out of his house,

**24:2** and having departed, she becomes another man's wife,

**24:3** and the latter husband hates her, and writes for her a bill of divorce and puts it into her hands and sends her out of his house, and the latter husband who took her for himself as a wife dies,

**24:4** her former husband who sent her away shall not be able to turn back and take her to himself as a wife after she has been defiled, for that is an abomination before the Lord your God; and you shall not defile the land which the Lord your God gives you as an inheritance.

**24:5** Now if anyone has recently taken a wife, he shall not go out to war, and no duty shall be laid upon him; he shall be exempt in his house for one year; he shall gladden his wife whom he has taken.

**24:6** You shall not take as a pledge a millstone nor an upper millstone, for he takes a life as a pledge.

**24:7** And if a man is caught stealing a person from his brethren, the sons of Israel, and having oppressed him, sells him, that thief shall die; and you shall remove the wicked one from among yourselves.

**24:8** Take heed to yourself concerning the plague of leprosy; you shall be exceedingly careful to do according to all the law which the priests, the Levites, declare to you; in the manner I commanded you, be careful to do.

**24:9** Remember all that the Lord your God did to Miriam on the way, as you were coming out of Egypt.

**24:10** If there is a debt with your neighbor, any debt whatever, and you go into his house to take his pledge,

**24:11** you shall stand outside, and the man who holds your loan shall bring the pledge outside to you.

**24:12** And if the man is poor, you shall not sleep in the pledge;

**24:13** you shall surely restore his pledge by the setting of the sun, and he shall sleep in his garment, and he shall bless you, and it shall be mercy for you before the Lord your God.

**24:14** You shall not unjustly withhold the wages of one who is poor and needy from among your brethren, or from among the sojourners who are in your cities;

**24:15** on the same day you shall pay his wages; the sun shall not go down upon it, for he is poor and has his hope in it, and he will cry out against you to the Lord, and there shall be sin in you.

**24:16** Fathers shall not die for children, and sons shall not die for fathers; each one shall die in his own sin.

**24:17** You shall not pervert the justice of the sojourner and the orphan and the widow,

**24:18** for you were a household servant in the land of Egypt, and the Lord your God redeemed you from there; therefore I command you to do this thing.

**24:19** Now when you reap a harvest in your field, and forget a sheaf in your field, you shall not turn back to take it; it shall be for the sojourner and for the orphan and for the widow, that the Lord your God may bless you in all the works of your hands.

**24:20** And when you gather olives, you shall not turn back to glean what is behind you; it shall be for the sojourner and for the orphan and for the widow; and you shall remember that you were a household servant in the land of Egypt; therefore I command you to do this thing.

**24:21** And when you harvest the grapes of your vineyard, you shall not glean it again behind you; it shall be for the sojourner and for the orphan and for the widow,

**24:22** and you shall remember that you were a household servant in the land of Egypt; therefore I command you to do this thing.

## Translator Notes

- **24:4** — The Greek reads "you shall not defile" (mianeite), differing from the Hebrew "you shall not bring sin upon."
- **24:10** — This Greek text omits the negative particle before "enter," forming a continuous conditional clause leading into the command to stand outside in verse 11 (contrast the Hebrew and some Greek manuscripts, "you shall not enter").
- **24:13** — Greek eleemosyne ("mercy" or "charity") renders the Hebrew term for "righteousness."
- **24:17** — This Greek text ends after "and the widow," omitting the clause about taking a widow's garment in pledge found in the Hebrew and some Greek witnesses, and opens verse 18 directly with "for."
