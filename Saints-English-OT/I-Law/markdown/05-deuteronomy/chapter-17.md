## Text

**17:1** You shall not sacrifice to the Lord your God a calf or a sheep in which there is a blemish, any evil defect; for that is an abomination to the Lord your God.

**17:2** Now if there is found within one of your cities, which the Lord your God gives you, a man or a woman who does what is evil before the Lord your God, in transgressing His covenant,

**17:3** and goes and serves other gods and worships them, whether the sun or the moon or any of the host of heaven, which He has not commanded,

**17:4** and it is reported to you, then you shall inquire diligently; and behold, if the matter is indeed true, that this abomination has been committed in Israel,

**17:5** then you shall take out that man or that woman, and you shall stone them with stones, and they shall die.

**17:6** On the testimony of two witnesses or three witnesses, he who is to die shall be put to death; he shall not be put to death on the testimony of one witness.

**17:7** And the hand of the witnesses shall be the first against him to put him to death, and afterward the hand of the people; so you shall put away the evil one from among yourselves.

**17:8** Now if a matter in judgment is too difficult for you—between blood and blood, between judgment and judgment, between stroke and stroke, and between dispute and dispute, matters of judgment in your cities—then you shall arise and go up to the place that the Lord your God chooses there,

**17:9** and you shall come to the judge who shall be in those days; and having inquired, they shall declare the judgment to you.

**17:10** And you shall act according to the decision that they declare to you from the place that the Lord your God chooses, and you shall be careful to do all that is ordained as law for you.

**17:11** According to the law and according to the judgment that they tell you, you shall do; you shall not turn aside from the word that they declare to you, to the right or to the left.

**17:12** And the man who acts presumptuously, so as not to obey the priest who stands to minister in the name of the Lord your God, or the judge who shall be in those days, that man shall die; and you shall put away the evil one from Israel.

**17:13** And all the people shall hear and fear, and shall no longer act ungodly.

**17:14** When you enter into the land which the Lord your God gives you, and inherit it and dwell in it, and say, "I will appoint a ruler over myself, just like the rest of the nations around me,"

**17:15** you shall surely appoint over yourself a ruler whom the Lord God chooses. From among your brethren you shall appoint a ruler over yourself; you may not appoint a foreign man over yourself, because he is not your brother.

**17:16** for he shall not multiply horses for himself, nor shall he turn the people back to Egypt in order to multiply horses for himself; for the Lord has said, "You shall not again return by this way."

**17:17** Neither shall he multiply wives for himself, nor shall his heart turn aside; nor shall he greatly multiply silver and gold for himself.

**17:18** And when he is seated in his rule, he shall write for himself this second law in a book from the priests, the Levites.

**17:19** And it shall be with him, and he shall read in it all the days of his life, that he may learn to fear the Lord your God and to observe all these commandments and these statutes, to do them;

**17:20** that his heart may not be lifted up above his brethren, and that he may not turn aside from the commandments to the right or to the left, so that he may prolong his days in his rule, he and his sons among the sons of Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **17:7** — The Greek reads masculine singular "the evil one" (ton ponēron) rather than abstract "the evil," matching the form quoted in 1 Corinthians 5:13.
- **17:9** — This Greek witness names only "the judge" while keeping plural verbs ("having inquired, they shall declare"), omitting "the Levitical priests" found in the Hebrew text.
- **17:14** — Throughout 17:14-20 the Greek renders Hebrew "king" as "ruler" (archōn) and "kingdom" as "rule" (archē).
- **17:18** — The Greek phrase to deuteronomion touto ("this second law" or "this copy of the law") is the origin of the English title Deuteronomy.
