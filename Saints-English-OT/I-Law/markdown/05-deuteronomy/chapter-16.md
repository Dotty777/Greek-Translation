## Text

**16:1** Observe the month of new fruits, and you shall keep the Passover to the Lord your God; for in the month of new fruits you came out of Egypt by night.

**16:2** And you shall sacrifice the Passover to the Lord your God, sheep and oxen, in the place that the Lord your God chooses for His name to be called upon there.

**16:3** You shall eat no leaven with it; seven days you shall eat unleavened bread with it, the bread of affliction, for you came out of Egypt in haste, that you may remember the day of your departure from the land of Egypt all the days of your life.

**16:4** No leaven shall be seen with you within all your borders for seven days, nor shall any of the flesh that you sacrifice in the evening on the first day remain overnight until the morning.

**16:5** You may not sacrifice the Passover in any of your cities that the Lord your God gives you,

**16:6** but only in the place that the Lord your God chooses for His name to be called upon there; you shall sacrifice the Passover in the evening, at the setting of the sun, at the season when you came out of Egypt.

**16:7** And you shall boil and roast and eat it in the place that the Lord your God chooses; and in the morning you shall turn back and go to your houses.

**16:8** Six days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day shall be a concluding assembly, a feast to the Lord your God; you shall do no work on it, except whatever shall be done for a soul.

**16:9** Seven weeks you shall count off for yourself; from the time you begin to put the sickle to the harvest, you shall begin to count off seven weeks.

**16:10** And you shall keep the Feast of Weeks to the Lord your God according as your hand is able, according to all that the Lord your God gives.

**16:11** And you shall rejoice before your God, both your son and your daughter, your manservant and your maidservant, and the Levite, and the sojourner, and the orphan, and the widow who is among you, in the place that the Lord your God chooses for His name to be called upon there.

**16:12** And you shall remember that you were a servant in the land of Egypt, and you shall observe and do these commandments.

**16:13** You shall keep the Feast of Tabernacles for yourself seven days, when you gather in from your threshing floor and from your winepress.

**16:14** And you shall rejoice in your feast, you and your son and your daughter, your manservant and your maidservant, and the Levite, and the sojourner, and the orphan, and the widow who is in your cities.

**16:15** Seven days you shall keep the feast to the Lord your God in the place that the Lord your God chooses for Himself; and if the Lord your God blesses you in all your produce and in all the work of your hands, then you shall be joyful.

**16:16** Three times in the year all your males shall appear before the Lord your God in the place that the Lord chooses: at the Feast of Unleavened Bread, and at the Feast of Weeks, and at the Feast of Tabernacles. You shall not appear before the Lord your God empty-handed;

**16:17** every man according to the ability of your hands, according to the blessing of the Lord your God which He has given you.

**16:18** You shall appoint judges and recorders for yourself in your cities that the Lord your God gives you, tribe by tribe, and they shall judge the people with righteous judgment.

**16:19** They shall not show partiality, nor shall they accept a bribe; for bribes blind the eyes of the wise and overturn the words of the righteous.

**16:20** Justly you shall pursue what is just, that you may live and enter in and inherit the land which the Lord your God gives you.

**16:21** You shall not plant a sacred grove for yourself; you shall not make for yourself any tree beside the altar of your God.

**16:22** Nor shall you set up a pillar for yourself, things which the Lord your God hates.

## Translator Notes

- **16:1** — The Greek renders Hebrew Abib as "the month of new fruits" (literally "of the new [produce]") and adds "by night" at the end of the verse.
- **16:7** — The Greek reads "boil and roast," harmonizing Exodus 12:8-9 with Deuteronomy, and specifies "your houses" rather than "your tents."
- **16:8** — The Greek adds the closing exception "except whatever shall be done for a soul" (referring to preparing food, as in Exodus 12:16), which is not in the Hebrew Masoretic text.
- **16:18** — The Greek term grammatoeisagōgeis ("recorders") refers to judicial clerks who introduced written records in court, rendering Hebrew shoterim ("officers").
