## Text

**8:1** All the commandments which I command you today you shall take care to do, that you may live and be multiplied, and go in and inherit the land which the Lord your God swore to your fathers.

**8:2** And you shall remember all the way which the Lord your God led you in the wilderness, so that He might afflict you and test you, and that what was in your heart might be discerned, whether you would keep His commandments or not.

**8:3** And He afflicted you and caused you to hunger, and fed you with the manna which your fathers did not know, that He might declare to you that man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds through the mouth of God shall man live.

**8:4** Your garments did not grow old upon you, your sandals did not wear out upon you, your feet did not become calloused—behold, forty years.

**8:5** And you shall know in your heart that just as a man disciplines his son, so the Lord your God will discipline you;

**8:6** and you shall keep the commandments of the Lord your God, to walk in His ways and to fear Him.

**8:7** For the Lord your God is bringing you into a good and vast land, where there are brooks of water, springs of the deeps issuing forth through the plains and through the mountains;

**8:8** a land of wheat and barley, vines, fig trees, pomegranates, a land of olive oil and honey;

**8:9** a land in which you shall not eat your bread in poverty, and you shall lack nothing in it; a land whose stones are iron, and out of whose mountains you shall mine bronze.

**8:10** And you shall eat and be filled, and you shall bless the Lord your God for the good land which He has given to you.

**8:11** Take heed to yourself lest you forget the Lord your God, so as not to keep His commandments and judgments and His statutes, all that I command you today;

**8:12** lest, when you have eaten and are filled, and have built fine houses and dwell in them,

**8:13** and when your cattle are multiplied and your sheep are multiplied, and silver and gold are multiplied to you, and all that you shall have is multiplied to you,

**8:14** you be lifted up in your heart and forget the Lord your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage;

**8:15** who led you through that great and fearful wilderness, where there was the biting serpent and scorpion and thirst, where there was no water; who brought forth for you from the flinty rock a spring of water;

**8:16** who fed you with the manna in the wilderness, which your fathers did not know, that He might afflict you and test you, and do you good at the end of your days.

**8:17** Do not say in your heart, "My strength and the might of my hand have produced for me this great wealth."

**8:18** And you shall remember the Lord your God, for it is He who gives you the strength to produce wealth, and that He may establish the covenant which the Lord swore to your fathers, as it is today.

**8:19** And it shall come to pass, if you utterly forget the Lord your God, and go after other gods and serve them, I solemnly testify to you today that you shall surely perish;

**8:20** just as the rest of the nations which the Lord destroys before your face, so shall you perish, because you did not listen to the voice of the Lord your God.

## Translator Notes

- **8:3** — Following the Septuagint, this verse omits "which you did not know," reads "God" rather than the divine name, and explicitly supplies "word" (rhemati) along with the repeated clause "shall man live"—wording closely echoed in Matthew 4:4.
- **8:4** — The Septuagint adds the clause "your sandals did not wear out upon you," reads "your feet did not become calloused" (instead of the Hebrew "your foot did not swell"), and introduces the forty years with "behold."
- **8:15** — The Greek describes the wilderness creature as a "biting serpent" (ophis daknon) rather than the Hebrew "fiery serpent," and specifies "a spring of water" (pegen hydatos) from the flinty rock.
- **8:17** — The Greek noun dynamis (here paired with the Septuagint addition "great") can mean either "power" or "wealth," reflecting Hebrew chayil in the context of material prosperity (vv. 13, 18).
