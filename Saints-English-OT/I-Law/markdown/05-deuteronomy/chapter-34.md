## Text

**34:1** And Moses went up from Araboth of Moab to Mount Nebo, to the summit of Pisgah, which is opposite Jericho; and the Lord showed him all the land of Gilead as far as Dan,

**34:2** and all the land of Naphtali, and all the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah as far as the farthest sea,

**34:3** and the wilderness, and the surrounding region of Jericho, the city of palm trees, as far as Zoar.

**34:4** And the Lord said to Moses, "This is the land which I swore to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, saying, 'To your seed I will give it.' I have shown it to your eyes, yet you shall not enter there."

**34:5** And Moses the servant of the Lord died in the land of Moab by the word of the Lord.

**34:6** And they buried him in Gai, near the house of Peor; and no one knows his burial place to this day.

**34:7** Now Moses was one hundred and twenty years old when he died; his eyes were not dimmed, nor were they impaired.

**34:8** And the sons of Israel wept for Moses in Araboth of Moab by the Jordan, opposite Jericho, for thirty days; and the days of the mourning of weeping for Moses were completed.

**34:9** And Joshua the son of Nun was filled with a spirit of understanding, for Moses had laid his hands upon him; and the sons of Israel heeded him, and did just as the Lord had commanded Moses.

**34:10** And there has not arisen again a prophet in Israel like Moses, whom the Lord knew face to face,

**34:11** in all the signs and wonders, whom the Lord sent to perform them in the land of Egypt, to Pharaoh, and to his servants, and to all his land,

**34:12** the great marvels and the mighty hand, which Moses performed before all Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **34:1** — "Araboth of Moab" (also in 34:8) preserves the Greek transliteration of Hebrew araboth, meaning "the plains" or "steppes."
- **34:6** — The Greek uses a plural verb ("they buried him"), transliterates Hebrew "in the valley" as the place name "Gai," and renders Beth-peor literally as "the house of Peor."
- **34:7** — In this Greek text, the final verb ("nor were they impaired") continues with "his eyes" as its subject, without the Masoretic Text's reference to his vigor or other Septuagint witnesses' addition of "his cheeks."
- **34:9** — Standard English "Nun" renders the Septuagint Greek form Naue for the name of Joshua's father.
