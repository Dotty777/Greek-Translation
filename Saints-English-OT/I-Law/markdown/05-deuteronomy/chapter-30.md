## Text

**30:1** And it shall come to pass, when all these words come upon you, the blessing and the curse which I have set before your face, and you receive them into your heart among all the nations where the Lord shall scatter you,

**30:2** and you return to the Lord your God and heed His voice according to all that I command you today, with all your heart and with all your soul,

**30:3** then the Lord shall heal your sins, and He shall have mercy on you, and again gather you from all the nations among whom the Lord scattered you.

**30:4** Even if your dispersion is from one end of heaven to the other end of heaven, from there the Lord your God shall gather you, and from there the Lord your God shall take you.

**30:5** And your God shall bring you from there into the land which your fathers inherited, and you shall inherit it; and He shall do good to you and make you more numerous than your fathers.

**30:6** And the Lord shall thoroughly cleanse your heart and the heart of your seed, to love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul, that you may live.

**30:7** And the Lord your God shall put these curses upon your enemies and upon those who hate you, who persecuted you.

**30:8** And you shall return to the Lord and heed the voice of the Lord your God, and you shall do His commandments which I command you today.

**30:9** And the Lord your God shall bless you in every work of your hands, in the offspring of your womb and in the produce of your land and in the offspring of your livestock. For the Lord your God shall turn again to rejoice over you for good, just as He rejoiced over your fathers,

**30:10** if you heed the voice of the Lord your God, to keep His commandments and His statutes and His judgments which are written in the book of this law, if you return to the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul.

**30:11** For this commandment which I command you today is not too heavy, nor is it far from you.

**30:12** It is not in heaven above, that one should say, "Who shall ascend for us into heaven and take it for us, and having heard it we shall do it?"

**30:13** Nor is it beyond the sea, that one should say, "Who shall cross over for us to the other side of the sea, and take it for us and make it heard to us, and we shall do it?"

**30:14** The word is very near you, in your mouth and in your heart and in your hands, to do it.

**30:15** Behold, I have set before your face today life and death, good and evil.

**30:16** If you heed the commandments of the Lord your God which I command you today, to love the Lord your God, to walk in all His ways, and to keep His statutes and His judgments, then you shall live and become many, and the Lord your God shall bless you in all the land into which you are entering to inherit it.

**30:17** But if your heart turns away and you do not heed, and being led astray you worship other gods and serve them,

**30:18** I declare to you today that you shall surely perish, and you shall by no means live many days upon the land into which you are crossing the Jordan to inherit it.

**30:19** I call both heaven and earth as witnesses against you today: I have set before your face life and death, the blessing and the curse; choose life, that both you and your seed may live,

**30:20** to love the Lord your God, to heed His voice, and to hold fast to Him; for this is your life and the length of your days, that you may dwell upon the land which the Lord swore to your fathers, Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, to give to them.

## Translator Notes

- **30:3** — The Septuagint distinctively reads "the Lord shall heal your sins," whereas the Masoretic Text speaks of the Lord restoring Israel's captivity or fortunes.
- **30:6** — The Greek verb perikathariei ("shall thoroughly cleanse") renders the Hebrew metaphor of circumcising the heart in an explicitly purifying sense.
- **30:14** — The Septuagint adds "and in your hands" after "in your mouth and in your heart," highlighting practical obedience alongside speech and inner devotion.
- **30:20** — The Greek uses the neuter pronoun touto ("for this is your life"), pointing to loving, heeding, and holding fast to the Lord rather than the personal pronoun "He."
