## Text

**27:1** And Moses and the council of elders of the Israelites commanded, "Observe all these commandments which I command you today.

**27:2** And it shall be, on whatever day you cross over the Jordan into the land which the Lord your God gives to you, that you shall set up for yourself great stones and coat them with lime.

**27:3** And you shall write upon these stones all the words of this law, when you have crossed over the Jordan, at the time you enter into the land which the Lord, the God of your fathers, gives to you, a land flowing with milk and honey, just as the Lord, the God of your fathers, spoke to you.

**27:4** And it shall be, when you have crossed over the Jordan, you shall set up these stones, which I command you today, on Mount Ebal, and you shall coat them with lime.

**27:5** And you shall build there an altar to the Lord your God, an altar of stones; you shall not lay iron upon it.

**27:6** With whole stones you shall build an altar to the Lord your God, and you shall offer up upon it the burnt offerings to the Lord your God.

**27:7** And you shall sacrifice there a sacrifice of deliverance, and you shall eat and be filled, and you shall rejoice before the Lord your God.

**27:8** And you shall write upon the stones all this law very clearly."

**27:9** And Moses and the priests, the Levites, spoke to all Israel, saying, "Be silent and hear, O Israel; on this day you have become a people to the Lord your God.

**27:10** And you shall obey the voice of the Lord your God, and perform all His commandments and His statutes which I command you today."

**27:11** And Moses commanded the people on that day, saying,

**27:12** "These shall stand to bless the people on Mount Gerizim, when they have crossed over the Jordan: Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Joseph, and Benjamin.

**27:13** And these shall stand for the curse on Mount Ebal: Reuben, Gad and Asher, Zebulun, Dan and Naphtali."

**27:14** And the Levites shall answer and say to all Israel with a loud voice:

**27:15** "Cursed is the man who makes a carved and molten image, an abomination to the Lord, the work of the hands of craftsmen, and sets it in a secret place." And the people shall answer and say, "So be it."

**27:16** "Cursed is the one who dishonors his father or his mother." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:17** "Cursed is the one who moves the boundaries of his neighbor." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:18** "Cursed is the one who leads a blind man astray on the road." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:19** "Cursed is whoever turns aside the justice of a sojourner, an orphan, and a widow." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:20** "Cursed is the one who lies with a wife of his father, because he has uncovered the covering of his father." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:21** "Cursed is the one who lies with any beast." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:22** "Cursed is the one who lies with the sister of his father or his mother." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:23** "Cursed is the one who lies with his daughter-in-law." And all the people shall say, "So be it." "Cursed is the one who lies with the sister of his wife." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:24** "Cursed is the one who strikes his neighbor by deceit." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:25** "Cursed is whoever takes bribes to strike down a life of innocent blood." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:26** "Cursed is every man who does not abide in all the words of this law to do them." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

## Translator Notes

- **27:7** — "A sacrifice of deliverance" renders Greek thysian sōtēriou, the Septuagint's standard term for the peace or fellowship offering. The Septuagint also includes the phrase "and be filled" (kai emplēsthēsē), which does not appear in the Masoretic Hebrew text.
- **27:15** — Here and throughout verses 15–26, the Greek text translates the liturgical response with the optative verb Genoito ("So be it") rather than transliterating the Hebrew "Amen."
- **27:22** — The Greek reads "the sister of his father or his mother" (his aunt), differing from the Masoretic Hebrew, which speaks of "his sister, the daughter of his father or the daughter of his mother."
- **27:23** — The Greek reads "his daughter-in-law" (nymphēs autou) where the Hebrew reads "his mother-in-law," and adds an extra curse concerning a man's wife's sister that is not present in the Masoretic text.
- **27:26** — The Septuagint's wording "every man" (pas anthrōpos) and "in all the words" (en pasin tois logois) is more expansive than the Masoretic Hebrew and aligns closely with the form of the verse cited in Galatians 3:10.
