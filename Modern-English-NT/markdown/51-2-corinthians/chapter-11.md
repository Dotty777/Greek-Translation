## Text

**11:1** I wish you would bear with me in a little foolishness; indeed, do bear with me!

**11:2** For I am jealous for you with God's jealousy; for I betrothed you to one husband, to present a pure virgin to Christ.

**11:3** Yet I fear that somehow, as the serpent deceived Eve by its craftiness, your minds might be corrupted away from the sincerity and the purity toward Christ.

**11:4** For if the one who comes proclaims another Jesus whom we did not proclaim, or you receive a different spirit which you did not receive, or a different gospel which you did not accept, you bear with it well enough.

**11:5** For I consider myself to have fallen short in nothing compared to the super-apostles;

**11:6** and even if I am unskilled in speech, yet not in knowledge; rather, in every way we have made it manifest to you in all things.

**11:7** Or did I commit a sin by humbling myself so that you might be exalted, because I proclaimed the gospel of God to you free of charge?

**11:8** I plundered other churches, taking wages for the ministry to you,

**11:9** and when I was present with you and was in need, I was not a burden to anyone; for the brothers who came from Macedonia supplied my need; and in everything I kept myself from being burdensome to you, and I will keep doing so.

**11:10** As the truth of Christ is in me, this boasting will not be silenced in my case in the regions of Achaia.

**11:11** Why? Because I do not love you? God knows.

**11:12** But what I am doing I will also continue to do, so that I may cut off the opportunity of those wanting an opportunity, in order that in the very thing in which they boast they may be found just as we are.

**11:13** For such people are false apostles, deceitful workers, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ.

**11:14** And no wonder, for Satan himself disguises himself as an angel of light.

**11:15** Therefore it is no great thing if his servants also disguise themselves as servants of righteousness—whose end will be according to their works.

**11:16** Again I say, let no one think me to be foolish—or if not, then accept me even as a foolish person, so that I too may boast a little.

**11:17** What I am speaking, I am not speaking according to the Lord, but as in foolishness, in this confidence of boasting.

**11:18** Since many boast according to the flesh, I too will boast.

**11:19** For you gladly bear with the foolish, being wise yourselves;

**11:20** for you bear with it if someone enslaves you, if someone devours you, if someone takes advantage of you, if someone exalts himself, if someone strikes you in the face.

**11:21** To our disgrace I say it, as though we have been weak! Yet in whatever anyone dares—I speak in foolishness—I dare as well.

**11:22** Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they offspring of Abraham? So am I.

**11:23** Are they servants of Christ? I speak as one out of his mind—I am even more so! In labors far more, in prisons far more, in beatings beyond measure, in deaths often;

**11:24** five times from the Jews I received forty lashes minus one,

**11:25** three times I was beaten with rods, once I was stoned, three times I was shipwrecked, a night and a day I have spent in the deep;

**11:26** on journeys often, in dangers from rivers, in dangers from robbers, in dangers from my own people, in dangers from Gentiles, in dangers in the city, in dangers in the wilderness, in dangers at sea, in dangers among false brothers,

**11:27** in labor and hardship, in sleepless nights often, in hunger and thirst, in fastings often, in cold and nakedness;

**11:28** apart from the other things, there is the daily pressure on me: the anxiety for all the churches.

**11:29** Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is led to stumble, and I do not burn?

**11:30** If it is necessary to boast, I will boast of the things concerning my weakness.

**11:31** The God and Father of the Lord Jesus, He who is blessed forever, knows that I am not lying.

**11:32** In Damascus, the ethnarch under King Aretas was guarding the city of the Damascenes in order to seize me,

**11:33** and through a window I was lowered in a basket through the wall, and I escaped his hands.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — The second anechesthe can be read either as an imperative ("indeed, do bear with me") or as an indicative ("indeed, you are bearing with me").
- **11:3** — Some manuscripts omit "and the purity" (kai tes hagnotetos), though it is strongly attested in early witnesses and echoes the "pure virgin" imagery of 11:2.
- **11:6** — The Greek participle phanerosantes is grammatically elliptical without a finite verb or explicit object; some manuscripts read the passive phanerothentes ("having been made manifest").
- **11:28** — Greek ton parektos can mean "the other things left unmentioned" or "the external troubles," and epistasis conveys a daily "pressure" or crowding of concerns.
