## Text

**13:1** This is the third time I am coming to you. By the mouth of two or three witnesses every matter will be established.

**13:2** I have warned beforehand and I still warn—as when present the second time, so now while absent—those who sinned earlier and all the rest, that if I come again I will not spare them,

**13:3** since you are seeking proof of Christ speaking in me, who is not weak toward you, but is powerful in you.

**13:4** For indeed He was crucified out of weakness, yet He lives by the power of God. For we also are weak in Him, yet we will live with Him by the power of God toward you.

**13:5** Test yourselves as to whether you are in the faith; examine yourselves. Or do you not realize about yourselves that Jesus Christ is in you—unless indeed you are unapproved?

**13:6** But I hope that you will come to know that we are not unapproved.

**13:7** Now we pray to God that you do nothing wrong—not so that we may appear approved, but so that you may do what is right, even though we may be as though unapproved.

**13:8** For we are not able to do anything against the truth, but on behalf of the truth.

**13:9** For we rejoice whenever we are weak while you are strong. This also is what we pray for: your restoration.

**13:10** For this reason I write these things while absent, so that when I am present I may not act severely according to the authority that the Lord gave me, for building up and not for tearing down.

**13:11** Finally, brothers, rejoice, be restored, be encouraged, be of the same mind, live in peace; and the God of love and peace will be with you.

**13:12** Greet one another with a holy kiss. All the saints greet you.

**13:13** The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with all of you.

## Translator Notes

- **13:1** — Echoes Deuteronomy 19:15; "by the mouth of" is an idiom for oral testimony, and the Greek noun rhema can mean either "word" or "matter/charge."
- **13:5** — Greek adokimoi ("unapproved") draws from testing or assaying imagery and means "failing the test" or "disqualified," carrying through verses 6-7 alongside dokimoi ("approved").
- **13:11** — Greek chairete can mean "rejoice" (echoing "we rejoice" in verse 9) or serve as a conventional closing greeting ("farewell").
- **13:13** — Following standard Greek versification, both greetings appear in 13:12 and the benediction is 13:13 (numbered 13:14 in many English versions); the earliest manuscripts do not include a closing "Amen."
