## Text

**1:1** The elder, to Gaius the beloved, whom I love in truth.

**1:2** Beloved, concerning all things I pray that you prosper and be healthy, just as your soul prospers.

**1:3** For I rejoiced greatly as brothers came and testified to your truth, just as you walk in truth.

**1:4** I have no greater joy than these things: to hear that my children are walking in the truth.

**1:5** Beloved, you act faithfully in whatever work you do for the brothers, and strangers at that,

**1:6** who testified to your love before the church. You will do well to send them forward in a way worthy of God.

**1:7** For they went out on behalf of the Name, taking nothing from the Gentiles.

**1:8** We therefore ought to support such people, so that we may become coworkers for the truth.

**1:9** I wrote something to the church, but Diotrephes, who loves to be first among them, does not welcome us.

**1:10** For this reason, if I come, I will call to mind the deeds that he is doing, talking nonsense against us with evil words; and not content with these things, he does not himself welcome the brothers, and he hinders those who wish to do so and throws them out of the church.

**1:11** Beloved, do not imitate evil, but good. The one doing good is from God; the one doing evil has not seen God.

**1:12** Demetrius has been attested by all, and by the truth itself; and we also testify, and you know that our testimony is true.

**1:13** I had many things to write to you, but I do not wish to write to you with ink and reed pen;

**1:14** rather, I hope to see you right away, and we will speak face to face.

**1:15** Peace to you. The friends greet you. Greet each of the friends by name.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — Greek peri panton ("concerning all things") can mean "in every respect" or "above all"; euodoo ("prosper") literally means to have a smooth journey and is used here for both general and spiritual well-being.
- **1:7** — "The Name" (tou onomatos) is an absolute reference to the name of Jesus Christ, as in Acts 5:41; ton ethnikon ("the Gentiles") here denotes non-Christian outsiders.
- **1:9** — Diotrephes is characterized by philoproteuon ("loving to be first"), a word occurring nowhere else in the New Testament; the earlier communication mentioned in "I wrote something" (egrapsa ti) is not preserved.
- **1:14** — "Face to face" renders the literal Greek idiom stoma pros stoma ("mouth to mouth"), meaning direct personal conversation.
