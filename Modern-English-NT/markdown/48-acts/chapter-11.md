## Text

**11:1** Now the apostles and the brothers who were throughout Judea heard that the Gentiles also had received the word of God.

**11:2** And when Peter went up to Jerusalem, those of the circumcision disputed with him,

**11:3** saying, "You went in to uncircumcised men and ate with them."

**11:4** But Peter began and laid it out for them in order, saying,

**11:5** "I was in the city of Joppa praying, and in a trance I saw a vision: a certain object descending, like a large sheet being lowered from heaven by four corners, and it came all the way to me.

**11:6** Gazing intently into it, I examined it and saw the four-footed animals of the earth, and the wild beasts, and the crawling creatures, and the birds of the sky.

**11:7** I also heard a voice saying to me, 'Get up, Peter; slaughter and eat.'

**11:8** But I said, 'By no means, Lord, because nothing common or unclean has ever entered my mouth.'

**11:9** But a voice answered a second time from heaven, 'What God has cleansed, you must not regard as common.'

**11:10** Now this happened three times, and everything was drawn up again into heaven.

**11:11** And look, at that very moment three men arrived at the house in which we were, having been sent from Caesarea to me.

**11:12** And the Spirit told me to go with them, making no distinction. These six brothers also went with me, and we entered the man's house.

**11:13** And he reported to us how he had seen the angel standing in his house and saying, 'Send to Joppa and summon Simon, who is called Peter,

**11:14** who will speak words to you by which you will be saved, you and all your household.'

**11:15** And as I began to speak, the Holy Spirit fell upon them just as He also did upon us at the beginning.

**11:16** And I remembered the word of the Lord, how He said, 'John indeed baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit.'

**11:17** Therefore, if God gave to them the same gift as also to us who believed on the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I to be able to hinder God?"

**11:18** When they heard these things, they fell silent and glorified God, saying, "Then to the Gentiles also God has granted repentance leading to life."

**11:19** Now those who were scattered because of the affliction that arose over Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia and Cyprus and Antioch, speaking the word to no one except Jews alone.

**11:20** But there were some of them, men of Cyprus and Cyrene, who on coming to Antioch spoke to the Hellenists also, proclaiming the good news of the Lord Jesus.

**11:21** And the hand of the Lord was with them, and a large number who believed turned to the Lord.

**11:22** And the report concerning them reached the ears of the church that was in Jerusalem, and they sent Barnabas out as far as Antioch.

**11:23** When he arrived and saw the grace of God, he rejoiced and exhorted them all with purpose of heart to remain faithful to the Lord,

**11:24** for he was a good man, and full of the Holy Spirit and of faith. And a considerable crowd was added to the Lord.

**11:25** Then he departed for Tarsus to search for Saul,

**11:26** and when he found him, he brought him to Antioch. And it happened that for an entire year they met together in the church and taught a considerable crowd, and in Antioch the disciples were first called Christians.

**11:27** Now in these days prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch.

**11:28** And one of them named Agabus stood up and indicated through the Spirit that a great famine was about to occur over the whole inhabited world, which took place under Claudius.

**11:29** And according to each one's means, the disciples determined, every one of them, to send relief to the brothers living in Judea;

**11:30** and this they did, sending it to the elders by the hand of Barnabas and Saul.

## Translator Notes

- **11:12** — The Greek active participle διακρίναντα literally means "making no distinction" (unlike the middle voice in 10:20, "without hesitating"), though some interpreters take it in the sense of "without doubting."
- **11:20** — The Greek text reads Ἑλληνιστάς ("Hellenists," i.e., Greek-speakers), while some manuscripts read Ἕλληνας ("Greeks"); here the term stands in contrast to "Jews" in v. 19.
- **11:28** — Greek οἰκουμένην ("inhabited world") in Luke-Acts typically refers to the Roman world.
