## Text

**8:1** Now Saul was approving of his execution. And on that day a great persecution arose against the church in Jerusalem, and all were scattered throughout the regions of Judea and Samaria, except the apostles.

**8:2** Devout men buried Stephen and made great lamentation over him.

**8:3** Saul, however, was ravaging the church, entering house after house; and dragging off both men and women, he was delivering them into prison.

**8:4** Therefore those who had been scattered went about preaching the good news of the word.

**8:5** Philip went down to the city of Samaria and was proclaiming the Christ to them.

**8:6** And the crowds with one accord were paying attention to the things spoken by Philip as they heard and saw the signs that he was performing.

**8:7** For unclean spirits were coming out of many of those who had them, crying out with a loud voice; and many who were paralyzed and lame were healed.

**8:8** And there was much joy in that city.

**8:9** Now a certain man named Simon had previously been in the city, practicing magic and astonishing the nation of Samaria, saying that he himself was someone great,

**8:10** to whom all, from the least to the greatest, were paying attention, saying, "This man is the Power of God that is called Great."

**8:11** And they were paying attention to him because for a considerable time he had astonished them with his magic arts.

**8:12** But when they believed Philip as he was preaching the good news about the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, they were being baptized, both men and women.

**8:13** And Simon himself also believed; and having been baptized, he was continuing closely with Philip, and as he observed signs and great works of power taking place, he was astonished.

**8:14** Now when the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent Peter and John to them,

**8:15** who came down and prayed for them that they might receive the Holy Spirit.

**8:16** For He had not yet fallen upon any of them; they had only been baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus.

**8:17** Then they were laying their hands on them, and they were receiving the Holy Spirit.

**8:18** Now when Simon saw that through the laying on of the apostles' hands the Spirit was given, he offered them money,

**8:19** saying, "Give me also this authority, so that anyone on whom I lay my hands may receive the Holy Spirit."

**8:20** But Peter said to him, "May your silver go to destruction with you, because you thought to acquire the gift of God through money!

**8:21** You have no part or lot in this matter, for your heart is not right before God.

**8:22** Therefore repent of this wickedness of yours, and plead with the Lord, if perhaps the intent of your heart may be forgiven you;

**8:23** for I see that you are in the gall of bitterness and in the bond of unrighteousness."

**8:24** And Simon answered and said, "Plead with the Lord yourselves on my behalf, so that nothing of the things you have spoken may come upon me."

**8:25** So when they had solemnly testified and spoken the word of the Lord, they were returning to Jerusalem and were preaching the good news to many villages of the Samaritans.

**8:26** Now an angel of the Lord spoke to Philip, saying, "Rise and go toward the south to the road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza." This is deserted.

**8:27** So he rose and went; and look, an Ethiopian man, a eunuch, a high official of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, who was in charge of all her treasury—he had come to Jerusalem to worship,

**8:28** and he was returning and sitting in his chariot, and he was reading the prophet Isaiah.

**8:29** Then the Spirit said to Philip, "Go over and join this chariot."

**8:30** So Philip ran up and heard him reading Isaiah the prophet, and he said, "Do you really understand what you are reading?"

**8:31** And he said, "How indeed could I, unless someone guides me?" And he invited Philip to come up and sit with him.

**8:32** Now the passage of Scripture that he was reading was this: "Like a sheep He was led to slaughter, and like a lamb silent before its shearer, so He does not open His mouth.

**8:33** In humiliation His justice was taken away; who will describe His generation? For His life is taken away from the earth."

**8:34** And the eunuch answered Philip and said, "Please, I ask you, about whom does the prophet say this—about himself or about someone else?"

**8:35** Then Philip opened his mouth, and beginning from this Scripture, he preached to him the good news of Jesus.

**8:36** And as they were going along the road, they came to some water; and the eunuch said, "Look, water! What prevents me from being baptized?"

**8:38** And he ordered the chariot to stop; and they both went down into the water, both Philip and the eunuch, and he baptized him.

**8:39** And when they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord snatched Philip away, and the eunuch saw him no longer, for he went on his way rejoicing.

**8:40** But Philip was found at Azotus; and as he passed through, he was preaching the good news to all the cities until he came to Caesarea.

## Translator Notes

- **8:7** — The Greek exhibits a grammatical shift (anacoluthon), opening with "many of those who had unclean spirits" as nominative before switching to the neuter plural spirits crying out and coming out.
- **8:26** — The phrase kata mesembrian can mean either "toward the south" or "at noon." The closing clause ("This is a desert road," or "This is deserted") may be an editorial aside referring either to the road or to old Gaza.
- **8:33** — The quotation follows the Septuagint of Isaiah 53:8; the earliest manuscripts read "in humiliation" without a possessive pronoun.
- **8:36** — Verse 37, which adds Philip's condition and the eunuch's confession of faith, is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts; the Saints Greek Critical Text prints it within double brackets ⟦ ⟧ (shown as an additional verse).
- **8:37 *(additional)*** — Text-critical double brackets ⟦ ⟧ are preserved from the Saints Greek Critical Text; this verse is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
