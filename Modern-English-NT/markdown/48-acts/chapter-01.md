## Text

**1:1** I made the first account, Theophilus, concerning all that Jesus began both to do and to teach,

**1:2** until the day on which, having given commands through the Holy Spirit to the apostles whom He had chosen, He was taken up.

**1:3** To them He also presented Himself alive after His suffering by many sure proofs, appearing to them during forty days and speaking the things concerning the kingdom of God.

**1:4** And while eating with them, He commanded them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the promise of the Father, "which you heard from Me,

**1:5** for John baptized with water, but you will be baptized in the Holy Spirit not many days from now."

**1:6** So when they had come together, they were asking Him, saying, "Lord, is it at this time that You are restoring the kingdom to Israel?"

**1:7** He said to them, "It is not for you to know times or seasons that the Father has set in His own authority,

**1:8** but you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be My witnesses both in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the farthest part of the earth."

**1:9** And having said these things, while they were watching, He was lifted up, and a cloud took Him up from their eyes.

**1:10** And as they were gazing intently into heaven while He was going, look, two men stood beside them in white garments,

**1:11** who also said, "Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will come in this same manner in which you watched Him going into heaven."

**1:12** Then they returned to Jerusalem from the mount called Olivet, which is near Jerusalem, a Sabbath day's journey away.

**1:13** And when they had entered, they went up to the upper room where they were staying: Peter and John and James and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James son of Alphaeus and Simon the Zealot and Judas son of James.

**1:14** All these were continually devoting themselves with one mind to prayer, along with women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with His brothers.

**1:15** And in these days Peter stood up in the midst of the brothers and said (and the crowd of persons together was about one hundred twenty),

**1:16** "Men and brothers, it was necessary for the Scripture to be fulfilled which the Holy Spirit spoke beforehand through the mouth of David concerning Judas, who became a guide to those who arrested Jesus,

**1:17** for he was numbered among us and obtained his allotted share in this ministry."

**1:18** (Now this man acquired a field with the wages of unrighteousness, and falling headlong, he burst open in the middle and all his inward parts spilled out.

**1:19** And it became known to all those dwelling in Jerusalem, so that in their own language that field was called Akeldama, that is, Field of Blood.)

**1:20** "For it is written in the Book of Psalms, 'Let his homestead become desolate, and let there be no one dwelling in it,' and, 'Let another take his office.'

**1:21** Therefore it is necessary that of the men who accompanied us during all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and went out among us,

**1:22** beginning from the baptism of John until the day on which He was taken up from us, one of these become a witness with us of His resurrection."

**1:23** And they put forward two: Joseph called Barsabbas, who was surnamed Justus, and Matthias.

**1:24** And praying, they said, "You, Lord, who know the hearts of all, show which one of these two You have chosen

**1:25** to take the place of this ministry and apostleship from which Judas turned aside to go to his own place."

**1:26** And they cast lots for them, and the lot fell on Matthias, and he was numbered together with the eleven apostles.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — The phrase "through the Holy Spirit" may modify either "having given commands" (as translated) or "whom He had chosen."
- **1:4** — The Greek participle synalizomenos is debated and may mean either "eating (salt) together with them" or "staying/assembling together with them."
- **1:18** — The Greek phrase prenes genomenos literally means "becoming prone" (i.e., "falling headlong/face down"), though some scholars interpret prenes as "swelling up" based on an alternative ancient usage.
- **1:25** — Some manuscripts read "allotted share" (kleron) instead of "place" (topon), echoing 1:17.
