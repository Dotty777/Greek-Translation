## Text

**26:1** And Agrippa said to Paul, "You are permitted to speak for yourself." Then Paul stretched out his hand and began to make his defense:

**26:2** "Concerning all the things of which I am accused by the Jews, King Agrippa, I consider myself fortunate that I am about to make my defense before you today,

**26:3** especially because you are an expert in all the customs and disputes among the Jews; therefore I beg you to listen to me patiently.

**26:4** My manner of life from youth, which from the beginning was spent among my own nation and in Jerusalem, all the Jews know,

**26:5** having known me from the first, if they are willing to testify, that according to the strictest sect of our religion I lived as a Pharisee.

**26:6** And now I stand on trial for the hope of the promise made by God to our fathers,

**26:7** to which our twelve tribes, earnestly serving night and day, hope to attain. Concerning this hope I am accused by the Jews, O king.

**26:8** Why is it judged incredible among you if God raises the dead?

**26:9** I myself indeed thought that I ought to do many things in opposition to the name of Jesus the Nazarene.

**26:10** And this I also did in Jerusalem; and I myself locked up many of the saints in prisons, having received authority from the chief priests, and when they were being put to death, I cast my vote against them.

**26:11** And throughout all the synagogues, often punishing them, I kept trying to force them to blaspheme; and being furiously enraged against them, I kept pursuing them even to foreign cities.

**26:12** While thus engaged, as I was journeying to Damascus with the authority and commission of the chief priests,

**26:13** at midday along the road I saw, O king, a light from heaven, brighter than the radiance of the sun, shining around me and those who were journeying with me.

**26:14** And when we had all fallen to the ground, I heard a voice saying to me in the Hebrew dialect, 'Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads.'

**26:15** And I said, 'Who are You, Lord?' And the Lord said, 'I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.

**26:16** But rise and stand on your feet; for to this end I have appeared to you, to appoint you as a servant and a witness both of the things in which you have seen Me and of the things in which I will appear to you,

**26:17** delivering you from the people and from the Gentiles, to whom I am sending you,

**26:18** to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the authority of Satan to God, so that they may receive forgiveness of sins and an inheritance among those who have been sanctified by faith in Me.'

**26:19** Therefore, King Agrippa, I did not become disobedient to the heavenly vision,

**26:20** but kept declaring first to those in Damascus, and in Jerusalem, and throughout all the region of Judea, and to the Gentiles, that they should repent and turn to God, practicing deeds worthy of repentance.

**26:21** On account of these things, some Jews seized me in the temple and were trying to put me to death.

**26:22** Therefore, having obtained the help that comes from God, I stand to this day testifying to both small and great, saying nothing beyond what both the prophets and Moses said was going to take place:

**26:23** that the Christ must suffer, and that, as the first from the resurrection of the dead, He is going to proclaim light both to the people and to the Gentiles."

**26:24** And as he was saying these things in his defense, Festus said with a loud voice, "You are out of your mind, Paul! Your great learning is turning you to madness."

**26:25** But Paul said, "I am not out of my mind, most excellent Festus, but I am uttering words of truth and sober reason.

**26:26** For the king knows about these things, to whom I also speak boldly; for I am persuaded that none of these things has escaped his notice, because this has not been done in a corner.

**26:27** Do you believe the prophets, King Agrippa? I know that you believe."

**26:28** And Agrippa said to Paul, "In a short time you are trying to persuade me to act as a Christian."

**26:29** And Paul said, "I would pray to God that whether in a short time or in a long time, not only you, but also all who are listening to me today might become such as I also am, except for these chains."

**26:30** And the king stood up, and the governor and Bernice and those who were sitting with them,

**26:31** and when they had withdrawn, they were speaking to one another, saying, "This man is doing nothing worthy of death or chains."

**26:32** And Agrippa said to Festus, "This man could have been released if he had not appealed to Caesar."

## Translator Notes

- **26:11** — The imperfect enagkazon ("I kept trying to force") is likely conative, describing repeated attempts to compel believers to blaspheme rather than necessarily succeeding.
- **26:14** — Greek te Hebraidi dialekto ("in the Hebrew dialect") commonly refers to Aramaic, the Semitic language spoken in first-century Judea, consistent with the form Saoul ("Saul").
- **26:23** — Greek ei (literally "whether") introduces the propositions at issue, and pathetos literally means "subject to suffering" or "destined to suffer."
- **26:28** — Greek en oligo can mean "in a short time" or "with little effort." The critical text reads poiesai (literally "to make," perhaps "to act the Christian" or "to make [me] a Christian"), which later manuscripts smooth to genesthai ("to become").
