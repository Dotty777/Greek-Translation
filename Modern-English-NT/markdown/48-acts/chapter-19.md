## Text

**19:1** Now it happened that while Apollos was in Corinth, Paul, having passed through the upper regions, came to Ephesus and found some disciples.

**19:2** And he said to them, "Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?" And they said to him, "No, we have not even heard whether there is a Holy Spirit."

**19:3** And he said, "Into what then were you baptized?" And they said, "Into John's baptism."

**19:4** And Paul said, "John baptized with a baptism of repentance, telling the people that they should believe in the One coming after him, that is, in Jesus."

**19:5** And when they heard this, they were baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus.

**19:6** And when Paul had laid hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they were speaking in tongues and prophesying.

**19:7** And there were in all about twelve men.

**19:8** And having entered the synagogue, he spoke boldly for three months, reasoning and persuading concerning the kingdom of God.

**19:9** But when some were becoming hardened and disobedient, speaking evil of the Way before the multitude, he withdrew from them and separated the disciples, reasoning daily in the lecture hall of Tyrannus.

**19:10** And this went on for two years, so that all who lived in Asia heard the word of the Lord, both Jews and Greeks.

**19:11** And God was performing extraordinary miracles through the hands of Paul,

**19:12** so that handkerchiefs or aprons were even carried from his skin to those who were sick, and the diseases left them, and the evil spirits went out.

**19:13** Now some of the traveling Jewish exorcists also attempted to invoke the name of the Lord Jesus over those who had the evil spirits, saying, "I adjure you by Jesus whom Paul proclaims."

**19:14** And seven sons of a certain Sceva, a Jewish chief priest, were doing this.

**19:15** And the evil spirit answered and said to them, "Jesus I recognize, and Paul I know about, but who are you?"

**19:16** And the man in whom the evil spirit was leaped upon them, subdued all of them, and overpowered them, so that they fled out of that house naked and wounded.

**19:17** And this became known to all, both Jews and Greeks, who lived in Ephesus; and fear fell upon them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was being magnified.

**19:18** And many of those who had believed kept coming, confessing and disclosing their practices.

**19:19** And a considerable number of those who had practiced magic arts brought their books together and were burning them before everyone; and they calculated their value and found it to be fifty thousand pieces of silver.

**19:20** In this way the word of the Lord was growing mightily and prevailing.

**19:21** Now when these things had been accomplished, Paul resolved in the spirit to travel to Jerusalem after passing through Macedonia and Achaia, saying, "After I have been there, I must also see Rome."

**19:22** And having sent into Macedonia two of those who assisted him, Timothy and Erastus, he himself stayed in Asia for a time.

**19:23** Now about that time there arose no small disturbance concerning the Way.

**19:24** For a certain man named Demetrius, a silversmith, who made silver shrines of Artemis, was bringing no small business to the craftsmen.

**19:25** Having gathered these together, along with the workers in similar trades, he said, "Men, you know that our prosperity is from this business,

**19:26** and you see and hear that not only in Ephesus, but in almost all of Asia, this Paul has persuaded and turned away a considerable crowd, saying that those made with hands are not gods.

**19:27** And not only is there danger that this trade of ours will fall into disrepute, but also that the temple of the great goddess Artemis will be counted as nothing, and that she is even about to be brought down from her majesty, she whom all Asia and the inhabited world worship."

**19:28** And when they heard this and became full of rage, they were crying out, saying, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"

**19:29** And the city was filled with the confusion, and they rushed with one accord into the theater, having seized Gaius and Aristarchus, Macedonians, Paul's traveling companions.

**19:30** And when Paul wanted to go in among the people, the disciples would not permit him.

**19:31** And some of the Asiarchs as well, who were friends of his, sent to him and urged him not to venture into the theater.

**19:32** So then, some were shouting one thing and some another, for the assembly was in confusion, and the majority did not know for what reason they had come together.

**19:33** And some from the crowd prompted Alexander, since the Jews had put him forward; and Alexander, motioning with his hand, wanted to make a defense to the people.

**19:34** But when they recognized that he was a Jew, a single cry arose from them all as they shouted for about two hours, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"

**19:35** And after quieting the crowd, the town clerk said, "Men of Ephesus, what person is there who does not know that the city of the Ephesians is temple keeper of the great Artemis and of that which fell from heaven?

**19:36** Therefore, since these things are undeniable, you ought to remain quiet and to do nothing rash.

**19:37** For you have brought these men, who are neither temple robbers nor blasphemers of our goddess.

**19:38** So then, if Demetrius and the craftsmen who are with him have a complaint against anyone, the courts are in session and there are proconsuls; let them bring charges against one another.

**19:39** But if you are seeking anything concerning other matters, it will be settled in the lawful assembly.

**19:40** For indeed we are in danger of being charged with rioting concerning today, since there is no cause, and in regard to it we will not be able to give an account for this disorderly gathering." And having said these things, he dismissed the assembly.

## Translator Notes

- **19:16** — The Greek word translated "all of them" (amphoteros) literally means "both of them," though in Koine Greek it can mean "all" of more than two, or indicate that only two of Sceva's seven sons were present here.
- **19:21** — The Greek phrase "en to pneumati" may refer either to Paul's own spirit ("resolved in his spirit") or to the Holy Spirit ("resolved in the Spirit").
- **19:33** — The critical text reads "synebibasan" ("prompted" or "instructed"), which is unusual here; some later manuscripts read "proebibasan" ("pushed forward").
- **19:35** — The Greek "diopetes" ("that which fell from heaven") literally means "fallen from Zeus" or "from the sky," referring to the sacred image or stone associated with Artemis.
- **19:40** — The critical Greek text retains a negative ("peri hou ou dynesometha"), producing a compressed syntax meaning there is no justification and regarding it they will be unable to give an account for the uproar.
