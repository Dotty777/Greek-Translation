## Text

**14:1** Now it happened in Iconium that they entered together into the synagogue of the Jews and spoke in such a way that a great multitude of both Jews and Greeks believed.

**14:2** But the Jews who disbelieved stirred up and embittered the minds of the Gentiles against the brothers.

**14:3** Therefore they stayed for a considerable time, speaking boldly in the Lord, who was bearing witness to the word of His grace, granting signs and wonders to take place through their hands.

**14:4** But the multitude of the city was divided, and some were with the Jews, while others were with the apostles.

**14:5** And when a move was made by both the Gentiles and the Jews, along with their rulers, to mistreat and stone them,

**14:6** becoming aware of it, they fled to the cities of Lycaonia—Lystra and Derbe—and to the surrounding region,

**14:7** and there they continued proclaiming the good news.

**14:8** Now in Lystra a certain man without strength in his feet was sitting, lame from his mother's womb, who had never walked.

**14:9** This man heard Paul speaking; and Paul, fixing his gaze on him and seeing that he had faith to be made well,

**14:10** said with a loud voice, "Stand upright on your feet!" And he leaped up and began walking.

**14:11** When the crowds saw what Paul had done, they lifted up their voice in Lycaonian, saying, "The gods, having become like men, have come down to us!"

**14:12** And they began calling Barnabas Zeus, and Paul Hermes, because he was the chief speaker.

**14:13** And the priest of Zeus, whose temple was in front of the city, brought bulls and garlands to the gates and wanted to offer sacrifice together with the crowds.

**14:14** But when the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard of this, they tore their garments and rushed out into the crowd, crying out

**14:15** and saying, "Men, why are you doing these things? We too are men of the same nature as you, proclaiming the good news to you to turn from these worthless things to a living God, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and all that is in them.

**14:16** In the generations gone by, He permitted all the nations to walk in their own ways;

**14:17** yet He did not leave Himself without witness, doing good by giving you rains from heaven and fruitful seasons, filling your hearts with food and gladness."

**14:18** Even by saying these things, they barely restrained the crowds from offering sacrifice to them.

**14:19** But Jews came from Antioch and Iconium, and after persuading the crowds, they stoned Paul and dragged him outside the city, supposing him to be dead.

**14:20** But while the disciples stood around him, he got up and entered the city. And the next day he went out with Barnabas to Derbe.

**14:21** After proclaiming the good news to that city and making many disciples, they returned to Lystra and to Iconium and to Antioch,

**14:22** strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to continue in the faith, and saying that through many tribulations we must enter the kingdom of God.

**14:23** When they had appointed elders for them in every church, having prayed with fasting, they committed them to the Lord in whom they had believed.

**14:24** Then, passing through Pisidia, they came into Pamphylia,

**14:25** and after speaking the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia.

**14:26** From there they sailed away to Antioch, from which they had been commended to the grace of God for the work that they had completed.

**14:27** When they arrived and gathered the church together, they reported all that God had done with them, and how He had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.

**14:28** And they stayed no little time with the disciples.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — The Greek phrase kata to auto can mean "together" or "in the same manner" (as previously in Pisidian Antioch).
- **14:9** — The Greek verb sozo ("be made well") encompasses both physical healing and spiritual salvation in Luke-Acts.
- **14:13** — Literally "of Zeus who is before the city," referring to a temple or shrine of Zeus located just outside the city gates.
- **14:23** — The verb cheirotoneo originally referred to electing by raising hands and in Hellenistic Greek also came to mean simply "appoint."
