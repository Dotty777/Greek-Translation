## Text

**17:1** Now when they had traveled through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where there was a synagogue of the Jews.

**17:2** And according to Paul's custom, he went in to them, and for three Sabbaths he reasoned with them from the Scriptures,

**17:3** explaining and demonstrating that the Christ had to suffer and rise from the dead, and saying, "This is the Christ, Jesus, whom I am proclaiming to you."

**17:4** And some of them were persuaded and joined Paul and Silas, along with a large multitude of the devout Greeks and not a few of the leading women.

**17:5** But the Jews, becoming jealous and taking along some wicked men from the marketplace, formed a mob and threw the city into an uproar; and descending upon the house of Jason, they were seeking to bring them out to the public assembly.

**17:6** And when they did not find them, they dragged Jason and some brothers before the city officials, shouting, "These men who have thrown the inhabited world into turmoil have come here as well,

**17:7** whom Jason has welcomed; and they all act contrary to the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, Jesus."

**17:8** And they agitated the crowd and the city officials who heard these things,

**17:9** and after taking security from Jason and the rest, they released them.

**17:10** Now the brothers immediately sent both Paul and Silas away by night to Berea, and when they arrived, they went into the synagogue of the Jews.

**17:11** Now these were more noble-minded than those in Thessalonica; they received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see whether these things were so.

**17:12** Therefore many of them believed, along with not a few Greek women of high standing and men.

**17:13** But when the Jews from Thessalonica learned that the word of God had been proclaimed by Paul in Berea also, they came there as well, stirring up and agitating the crowds.

**17:14** Then immediately the brothers sent Paul away to go as far as the sea, while both Silas and Timothy remained there.

**17:15** Now those who escorted Paul brought him as far as Athens; and after receiving a command for Silas and Timothy to come to him as quickly as possible, they departed.

**17:16** Now while Paul was waiting for them in Athens, his spirit was provoked within him as he observed that the city was full of idols.

**17:17** So he was reasoning in the synagogue with the Jews and the devout worshipers, and in the marketplace every day with those who happened to be there.

**17:18** And some of the Epicurean and Stoic philosophers also were debating with him. Some were saying, "What would this babbler wish to say?" Others said, "He seems to be a proclaimer of foreign deities," because he was preaching the good news of Jesus and the resurrection.

**17:19** And taking hold of him, they brought him to the Areopagus, saying, "May we know what this new teaching is that is being spoken by you?

**17:20** For you are bringing some strange things to our ears; therefore we wish to know what these things mean."

**17:21** Now all the Athenians and the foreigners residing there spent their time in nothing other than telling or hearing something new.

**17:22** Then Paul stood in the midst of the Areopagus and said, "Men of Athens, I observe that in every respect you are very religious;

**17:23** for while passing through and closely observing your objects of worship, I also found an altar on which had been inscribed, 'To an Unknown God.' What therefore you worship in ignorance, this I proclaim to you.

**17:24** The God who made the world and everything in it, since He is Lord of heaven and earth, does not dwell in temples made with hands,

**17:25** nor is He served by human hands, as though He needed anything, since He Himself gives to all life and breath and all things.

**17:26** And He made from one man every nation of mankind to live on the entire face of the earth, having determined their appointed times and the boundaries of their habitation,

**17:27** so that they would seek God, if perhaps they might grope for Him and find Him, though indeed He is not far from each one of us;

**17:28** for in Him we live and move and exist, as even some of your own poets have said, 'For we are also His offspring.'

**17:29** Being therefore the offspring of God, we ought not to suppose that the Divine Nature is like gold or silver or stone, an image formed by the craft and imagination of man.

**17:30** Therefore, having overlooked the times of ignorance, God now declares to people that all everywhere should repent,

**17:31** because He has fixed a day on which He will judge the inhabited world in righteousness through a man whom He has appointed, having provided assurance to all by raising Him from the dead."

**17:32** Now when they heard of a resurrection of the dead, some began mocking, while others said, "We will hear you concerning this again."

**17:33** So Paul went out from their midst.

**17:34** But some men joined him and believed, among whom were also Dionysius the Areopagite, a woman named Damaris, and others with them.

## Translator Notes

- **17:6** — "City officials" translates Greek politarchas (politarchs), a civic title confirmed by inscriptions specifically for the magistrates of Thessalonica.
- **17:18** — "Babbler" translates spermologos (literally "seed-picker"), Athenian slang for a scavenger of stray ideas. "Foreign deities" (xenon daimonion) reflects classical Greek usage for divine beings rather than evil spirits.
- **17:22** — Greek deisidaimonesterous is an ambiguous comparative ranging from "very religious" to "rather superstitious," allowing Paul a diplomatic opening before critiquing Athenian idolatry.
- **17:26** — Literally "from one" (ex henos), understood as one man (Adam); some later manuscripts add "blood" (haimatos).
