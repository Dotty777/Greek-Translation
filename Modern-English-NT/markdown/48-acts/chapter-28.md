## Text

**28:1** And once we had been brought to safety, we then learned that the island is called Malta.

**28:2** And the native people showed us no ordinary kindness; for they kindled a fire and welcomed all of us because of the rain that had set in and because of the cold.

**28:3** But when Paul had gathered a bundle of brushwood and laid it on the fire, a viper came out because of the heat and fastened onto his hand.

**28:4** And when the native people saw the creature hanging from his hand, they began saying to one another, "Certainly this man is a murderer; although he was brought to safety from the sea, Justice has not permitted him to live."

**28:5** He, however, shook the creature off into the fire and suffered no harm.

**28:6** But they were expecting that he was about to swell up or suddenly fall down dead. Yet after waiting in expectation for a long time and seeing nothing unusual happening to him, they changed their minds and began saying that he was a god.

**28:7** Now in the region around that place were estates belonging to the leading man of the island, named Publius, who welcomed us and hosted us kindly for three days.

**28:8** And it happened that the father of Publius was lying in bed, afflicted with fevers and dysentery. Paul went in to him, and having prayed and laid his hands on him, he healed him.

**28:9** After this happened, the rest of those on the island who had illnesses also began coming and were being cured.

**28:10** They also honored us with many honors, and when we were setting sail, they put on board the things for our needs.

**28:11** After three months we set sail on an Alexandrian ship that had wintered at the island, with the Twin Brothers as its figurehead.

**28:12** And putting in at Syracuse, we stayed there three days.

**28:13** From there we weighed anchor and arrived at Rhegium. And after one day a south wind sprang up, and on the second day we came to Puteoli.

**28:14** There we found brothers and were urged to stay with them seven days. And in this way we came to Rome.

**28:15** And from there the brothers, when they heard the news about us, came to meet us as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns. When Paul saw them, he thanked God and took courage.

**28:16** When we entered Rome, Paul was permitted to stay by himself, with the soldier who was guarding him.

**28:17** And it happened after three days that he called together the leading men of the Jews; and when they had assembled, he began saying to them, "Men, brothers, though I had done nothing against the people or the ancestral customs, I was handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem into the hands of the Romans,

**28:18** who, when they had examined me, wanted to release me because there was no ground for death in me.

**28:19** But when the Jews spoke against it, I was compelled to appeal to Caesar, not as having anything of which to accuse my nation.

**28:20** For this reason, therefore, I have asked to see you and to speak with you, for on account of the hope of Israel I am wearing this chain."

**28:21** And they said to him, "We have neither received letters concerning you from Judea, nor has any of the brothers arrived and reported or spoken anything evil about you.

**28:22** But we desire to hear from you what you think; for concerning this sect, it is known to us that everywhere it is spoken against."

**28:23** When they had set a day for him, they came to him at his lodging in greater numbers. He explained to them, solemnly testifying about the kingdom of God and seeking to persuade them concerning Jesus from both the Law of Moses and the Prophets, from morning until evening.

**28:24** And some were being persuaded by the things being spoken, while others would not believe.

**28:25** And disagreeing with one another, they began to depart after Paul had made one statement: "The Holy Spirit spoke rightly through Isaiah the prophet to your fathers,

**28:26** saying, 'Go to this people and say, "Hearing, you will hear, yet never understand; and looking, you will look, yet never perceive;

**28:27** for the heart of this people has grown callous, and with their ears they have scarcely heard, and they have shut their eyes; otherwise they might see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart and turn back, and I would heal them."'

**28:28** Therefore let it be known to you that this salvation of God has been sent to the Gentiles; they will also listen."

**28:30** And he remained a full two years in his own rented quarters, welcoming all who came in to him,

**28:31** proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching the things concerning the Lord Jesus Christ with complete boldness, unhindered.

## Translator Notes

- **28:2** — Greek "barbaroi" (also in 28:4), a neutral term for non-Greek speakers—here the native Punic-speaking inhabitants of Malta—rather than "barbarians" in the modern sense.
- **28:7** — Literally "the first man of the island" (Greek "protos"), an official Roman-era title for the chief magistrate of Malta attested in local inscriptions.
- **28:11** — Greek "Dioskurois" (the Dioscuri), the mythological twin sons of Zeus—Castor and Pollux—regarded in antiquity as patron deities of sailors.
- **28:13** — The critical text reads "perielontes" ("having cast off" or "weighed anchor"); some manuscripts read "perielthontes" ("having sailed around" or "made a circuit").
- **28:28** — Verse 29 ("And when he had said these things, the Jews departed, having a great dispute among themselves") is absent from the earliest and best manuscripts and is omitted in the critical text.
