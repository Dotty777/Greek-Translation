## Text

**25:1** Festus then, having arrived in the province, after three days went up to Jerusalem from Caesarea.

**25:2** And the chief priests and the leading men of the Jews brought charges before him against Paul, and they were urging him,

**25:3** asking a favor against him that he would summon him to Jerusalem, while preparing an ambush to kill him along the way.

**25:4** Festus then answered that Paul was being kept in custody at Caesarea, and that he himself was about to depart shortly.

**25:5** "Therefore," he said, "let those among you who are in authority go down with me, and if there is anything wrong in the man, let them accuse him."

**25:6** And after spending among them not more than eight or ten days, he went down to Caesarea; and on the next day, sitting down on the judgment seat, he commanded Paul to be brought.

**25:7** And when he arrived, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood around him, bringing many and serious charges which they were not able to prove,

**25:8** while Paul spoke in his defense, "Neither against the law of the Jews, nor against the temple, nor against Caesar have I committed any offense."

**25:9** But Festus, wishing to do the Jews a favor, answered Paul and said, "Are you willing to go up to Jerusalem and there be tried before me concerning these things?"

**25:10** But Paul said, "I am standing before the judgment seat of Caesar, where I ought to be tried. I have done no wrong to the Jews, as you also know very well.

**25:11** If, then, I am a wrongdoer and have committed anything worthy of death, I do not refuse to die; but if there is nothing to the things of which these men accuse me, no one can hand me over to them as a favor. I appeal to Caesar."

**25:12** Then Festus, having conferred with the council, answered, "To Caesar you have appealed; to Caesar you will go."

**25:13** Now when several days had passed, King Agrippa and Bernice arrived at Caesarea to greet Festus.

**25:14** And while they were spending many days there, Festus laid the matters concerning Paul before the king, saying, "There is a certain man left behind as a prisoner by Felix,

**25:15** concerning whom, when I was in Jerusalem, the chief priests and the elders of the Jews brought charges, asking for a sentence of condemnation against him.

**25:16** To them I answered that it is not the custom of the Romans to hand over any man as a favor before the accused has his accusers face to face and receives an opportunity for a defense concerning the charge.

**25:17** Therefore, when they had assembled here, making no delay, on the next day I sat down on the judgment seat and commanded the man to be brought.

**25:18** Concerning him, when the accusers stood up, they brought no charge of such evil things as I was supposing,

**25:19** but they had certain disputes against him concerning their own religion and concerning a certain Jesus, who had died, whom Paul kept asserting to be alive.

**25:20** And being at a loss as to the inquiry concerning these matters, I asked whether he was willing to go to Jerusalem and there be tried concerning these matters.

**25:21** But when Paul appealed to be kept in custody for the decision of Augustus, I commanded him to be kept until I could send him up to Caesar."

**25:22** And Agrippa said to Festus, "I myself would also like to hear the man." "Tomorrow," he said, "you will hear him."

**25:23** So on the next day, when Agrippa and Bernice came with great pomp and entered the audience hall along with the military tribunes and the prominent men of the city, at the command of Festus Paul was brought in.

**25:24** And Festus said, "King Agrippa, and all you men who are present here with us, you see this man, concerning whom the entire multitude of the Jews petitioned me both in Jerusalem and here, shouting that he ought not to live any longer.

**25:25** Yet I found that he had committed nothing worthy of death; and since he himself appealed to Augustus, I decided to send him.

**25:26** Concerning him I have nothing definite to write to my lord. Therefore I have brought him out before you all, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after the examination has taken place, I may have something to write.

**25:27** For it seems unreasonable to me, in sending a prisoner, not also to indicate the charges against him."

## Translator Notes

- **25:13** — The critical text reads the aorist participle aspasamenoi rather than the future participle aspasomenoi of later manuscripts; in Hellenistic Greek an aorist participle after a verb of motion can express attendant circumstance or purpose.
- **25:19** — Greek deisidaimonia can mean either "religion" (neutral) or "superstition" (pejorative); addressing King Agrippa, Festus likely uses the term politely to mean "religion."
- **25:21** — Greek Sebastos is the standard equivalent of the Latin imperial title Augustus, referring here to Emperor Nero (also in 25:25).
- **25:26** — Literally "to the lord" (to kyrio), a Roman title (dominus) used here by a provincial governor referring to the emperor.
