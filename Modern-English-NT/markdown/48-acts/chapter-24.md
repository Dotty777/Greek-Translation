## Text

**24:1** Now after five days the high priest Ananias came down with some elders and a certain orator named Tertullus, and they presented their case to the governor against Paul.

**24:2** And when he had been summoned, Tertullus began to accuse him, saying, "Since we enjoy great peace through you, and since reforms are taking place for this nation through your foresight,

**24:3** in every way and in every place we acknowledge this, most excellent Felix, with all gratitude.

**24:4** But so that I may not detain you any further, I beg you in your kindness to hear us briefly.

**24:5** For we have found this man to be a pest and one who stirs up dissensions among all the Jews throughout the world, and a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes,

**24:6** who even attempted to profane the temple, and we seized him.

**24:8** By examining him yourself, you will be able to learn from him concerning all these things of which we accuse him."

**24:9** And the Jews also joined in the charge, asserting that these things were so.

**24:10** When the governor nodded to him to speak, Paul answered, "Knowing that for many years you have been a judge to this nation, I gladly make my defense concerning myself,

**24:11** since you are able to verify that it has been no more than twelve days since I went up to Jerusalem to worship;

**24:12** and they did not find me disputing with anyone or stirring up a crowd either in the temple or in the synagogues or throughout the city,

**24:13** nor can they prove to you the things of which they now accuse me.

**24:14** But this I confess to you, that according to the Way, which they call a sect, I worship the God of our fathers, believing everything in accordance with the law and written in the prophets,

**24:15** having a hope in God, which these men themselves also accept, that there is going to be a resurrection of both the righteous and the unrighteous.

**24:16** Because of this, I myself also take pains to have a blameless conscience toward God and toward people at all times.

**24:17** Now after several years I came to bring alms to my nation and to present offerings,

**24:18** in the course of which they found me purified in the temple, with neither crowd nor uproar.

**24:19** But some Jews from Asia—they ought to be present before you and bring charges if they have anything against me.

**24:20** Or let these men themselves say what wrongdoing they found when I stood before the Sanhedrin,

**24:21** other than this one statement that I cried out while standing among them, 'Concerning the resurrection of the dead I am on trial before you today.'"

**24:22** Then Felix, having quite accurate knowledge of the matters concerning the Way, put them off, saying, "When Lysias the commander comes down, I will decide your case."

**24:23** He gave orders to the centurion for him to be kept under guard, yet to have some freedom, and not to prevent any of his own people from serving him.

**24:24** Now after some days, Felix arrived with his wife Drusilla, who was Jewish, and he sent for Paul and heard him concerning faith in Christ Jesus.

**24:25** And as he was reasoning about righteousness, self-control, and the coming judgment, Felix became frightened and answered, "Go away for the present, and when I find an opportunity I will summon you."

**24:26** At the same time he was also hoping that money would be given to him by Paul; therefore he also sent for him rather frequently and conversed with him.

**24:27** When two years had passed, Felix received Porcius Festus as his successor; and wanting to gain favor with the Jews, Felix left Paul in chains.

## Translator Notes

- **24:6** — The earliest manuscripts lack the material traditionally numbered as verse 7 (with portions of verses 6 and 8) describing Lysias intervening by force; the Saints Greek Critical Text prints verse 7 within double brackets ⟦ ⟧ (shown as an additional verse). In the shorter text, "from him" in 24:8 refers directly to Paul.
- **24:19** — The Greek syntax breaks off abruptly (anacoluthon) after introducing "some Jews from Asia," shifting directly to the relative clause stating what they ought to have done.
- **24:7 *(additional)*** — Text-critical double brackets ⟦ ⟧ are preserved from the Saints Greek Critical Text; this verse is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
