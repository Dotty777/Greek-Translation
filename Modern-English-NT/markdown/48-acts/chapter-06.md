## Text

**6:1** Now in these days, as the disciples were increasing in number, a complaint arose from the Hellenists against the Hebrews because their widows were being overlooked in the daily distribution.

**6:2** So the twelve summoned the multitude of the disciples and said, "It is not fitting for us to leave the word of God in order to serve tables.

**6:3** But, brothers, select from among you seven men of good reputation, full of the Spirit and of wisdom, whom we will appoint over this duty;

**6:4** while we will devote ourselves continually to prayer and to the ministry of the word."

**6:5** And this statement pleased the whole multitude, and they chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit, and Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus, a proselyte from Antioch,

**6:6** whom they presented before the apostles; and after praying, they laid their hands on them.

**6:7** And the word of God kept growing, and the number of the disciples in Jerusalem was multiplying greatly, and a large crowd of the priests was becoming obedient to the faith.

**6:8** Now Stephen, full of grace and power, was performing great wonders and signs among the people.

**6:9** But some men rose up from what was called the Synagogue of the Freedmen, and of the Cyrenians and Alexandrians, and some of those from Cilicia and Asia, disputing with Stephen.

**6:10** Yet they were not able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by whom he was speaking.

**6:11** Then they secretly instigated men who said, "We have heard him speaking blasphemous words against Moses and God."

**6:12** And they stirred up the people, the elders, and the scribes, and coming upon him, they seized him and brought him to the Sanhedrin.

**6:13** And they put forward false witnesses who said, "This man never stops speaking words against the holy place and the law,

**6:14** for we have heard him saying that this Jesus the Nazarene will destroy this place and will change the customs that Moses handed down to us."

**6:15** And all who were sitting in the Sanhedrin, gazing intently at him, saw his face like the face of an angel.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — "Hellenists" refers to Greek-speaking Jews (often from the Diaspora), while "Hebrews" refers to Aramaic- or Hebrew-speaking Jews native to Judea and Galilee.
- **6:8** — The critical Greek text reads "grace" (charitos) rather than "faith" (pisteos), which appears in some later manuscripts by assimilation to 6:5.
- **6:9** — "Freedmen" (Libertinon) denotes former Jewish captives freed from Roman slavery or their descendants; the Greek syntax leaves open whether one synagogue or several distinct synagogues are listed.
