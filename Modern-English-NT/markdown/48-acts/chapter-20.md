## Text

**20:1** Now after the uproar ceased, Paul sent for the disciples and encouraged them, and after saying farewell, he departed to travel to Macedonia.

**20:2** After passing through those regions and encouraging them with many words, he came to Greece,

**20:3** and spent three months there. When a plot was made against him by the Jews as he was about to set sail for Syria, he decided to return through Macedonia.

**20:4** Now accompanying him were Sopater son of Pyrrhus, of Berea; and of the Thessalonians, Aristarchus and Secundus; and Gaius of Derbe, and Timothy; and of Asia, Tychicus and Trophimus.

**20:5** These men went ahead and were waiting for us in Troas.

**20:6** But we sailed away from Philippi after the days of Unleavened Bread, and came to them at Troas within five days, where we stayed seven days.

**20:7** Now on the first day of the week, when we were gathered together to break bread, Paul was speaking to them, intending to leave the next day, and he prolonged his speech until midnight.

**20:8** Now there were many lamps in the upper room where we were gathered together.

**20:9** A certain young man named Eutychus, sitting on the windowsill, was sinking into a deep sleep as Paul kept speaking longer and longer; and overcome by sleep, he fell down from the third story and was picked up dead.

**20:10** But Paul went down and fell upon him, and embracing him, he said, "Do not be alarmed, for his life is in him."

**20:11** Then he went back up, broke the bread and ate, and after conversing for a long while until daybreak, he departed.

**20:12** And they took the boy away alive and were comforted beyond measure.

**20:13** But we went ahead to the ship and set sail for Assos, intending to take Paul on board from there; for he had arranged it this way, intending himself to go on foot.

**20:14** When he met us at Assos, we took him on board and came to Mitylene.

**20:15** Sailing away from there, on the following day we arrived opposite Chios; the next day we crossed over to Samos; and on the day after that we came to Miletus.

**20:16** For Paul had decided to sail past Ephesus, so that he would not have to spend time in Asia; for he was hurrying, if it were possible for him, to be in Jerusalem on the day of Pentecost.

**20:17** Now from Miletus he sent to Ephesus and summoned the elders of the church.

**20:18** When they came to him, he said to them, "You yourselves know, from the first day that I set foot in Asia, how I conducted myself with you the whole time,

**20:19** serving the Lord with all humility and with tears and trials that came upon me through the plots of the Jews;

**20:20** how I did not hold back from declaring to you anything that was beneficial and from teaching you publicly and from house to house,

**20:21** solemnly testifying to both Jews and Greeks of repentance toward God and faith in our Lord Jesus.

**20:22** And now, look, bound in spirit, I am going to Jerusalem, not knowing the things that will happen to me there,

**20:23** except that the Holy Spirit solemnly testifies to me in every city, saying that chains and afflictions await me.

**20:24** Yet I hold my life to be of no account, nor dear to myself, so that I may complete my course and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify solemnly to the gospel of the grace of God.

**20:25** And now, look, I know that none of you among whom I went about proclaiming the kingdom will see my face again.

**20:26** Therefore I testify to you this very day that I am innocent of the blood of all,

**20:27** for I did not hold back from declaring to you the entire purpose of God.

**20:28** Watch carefully over yourselves and all the flock, in which the Holy Spirit has appointed you overseers, to shepherd the church of God, which He acquired through His own blood.

**20:29** I know that after my departure fierce wolves will come in among you, not sparing the flock;

**20:30** and from among your own selves men will rise up, speaking distorted things to draw away the disciples after themselves.

**20:31** Therefore be watchful, remembering that for three years, night and day, I did not cease admonishing each one with tears.

**20:32** And now I entrust you to God and to the word of His grace, which is able to build you up and to give the inheritance among all those who have been sanctified.

**20:33** I have coveted no one's silver or gold or clothing.

**20:34** You yourselves know that these hands provided for my needs and for those who were with me.

**20:35** In everything I demonstrated to you that by laboring in this manner we must support the weak, and remember the words of the Lord Jesus, that He Himself said, 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.'"

**20:36** When he had said these things, he knelt down and prayed with all of them.

**20:37** There was much weeping among them all, and falling upon Paul's neck, they kissed him repeatedly,

**20:38** grieving most of all over the word he had spoken, that they would not see his face again. And they accompanied him to the ship.

## Translator Notes

- **20:5** — The critical Greek text reads proselthontes (lit. 'having come forward/arrived'), while other manuscripts read proelthontes ('having gone ahead'); in context it conveys that they traveled ahead to wait at Troas.
- **20:22** — Or 'bound by the Spirit'; the Greek dative to pneumati can refer either to Paul's own spirit (cf. 19:21) or to the Holy Spirit.
- **20:24** — The Greek phrasing blends two idioms ('consider my life of no account' and 'consider my life precious to myself') to emphasize Paul's complete devotion to his mission.
- **20:28** — Some manuscripts read 'the church of the Lord' instead of 'the church of God.' The closing phrase dia tou haimatos tou idiou may be rendered either 'through His own blood' or 'through the blood of His Own [i.e., His Son].'
- **20:35** — This saying of Jesus is not recorded in any of the four Gospels, preserving an independent oral tradition of His teaching.
