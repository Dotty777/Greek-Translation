## Text

**16:1** And he arrived also at Derbe and at Lystra. And look, a certain disciple was there by the name of Timothy, the son of a believing Jewish woman, though his father was a Greek,

**16:2** who was well spoken of by the brothers in Lystra and Iconium.

**16:3** Paul wanted this man to go out with him, and taking him, he circumcised him because of the Jews who were in those places, for they all knew that his father was a Greek.

**16:4** Now as they were traveling through the cities, they were delivering to them the decrees to keep that had been decided by the apostles and elders who were in Jerusalem.

**16:5** So then the churches were being established firmly in the faith and were increasing in number day by day.

**16:6** And they passed through the Phrygian and Galatian region, having been prevented by the Holy Spirit from speaking the word in Asia.

**16:7** And having come up to Mysia, they were trying to go into Bithynia, and the Spirit of Jesus did not permit them.

**16:8** So passing by Mysia, they went down to Troas.

**16:9** And a vision appeared to Paul during the night: a certain Macedonian man was standing and appealing to him and saying, "Cross over into Macedonia and help us."

**16:10** Now when he saw the vision, we immediately sought to depart for Macedonia, concluding that God had summoned us to proclaim the gospel to them.

**16:11** Therefore, setting sail from Troas, we ran a straight course to Samothrace, and on the next day to Neapolis,

**16:12** and from there to Philippi, which is a leading city of the district of Macedonia, a colony. And we were staying in this city for some days.

**16:13** And on the Sabbath day we went outside the gate beside a river, where we supposed there was a place of prayer; and sitting down, we were speaking to the women who had come together.

**16:14** And a certain woman named Lydia, a seller of purple from the city of Thyatira, a worshiper of God, was listening, whose heart the Lord opened to pay attention to what was being spoken by Paul.

**16:15** Now when she and her household were baptized, she appealed, saying, "If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come into my house and stay." And she prevailed upon us.

**16:16** Now it happened as we were going to the place of prayer that a certain slave girl having a spirit of divination met us, who was bringing much profit to her masters by fortune-telling.

**16:17** Following after Paul and us, she kept crying out, saying, "These men are slaves of the Most High God, who are proclaiming to you a way of salvation."

**16:18** And she kept doing this for many days. But Paul, becoming greatly annoyed, turned and said to the spirit, "I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her." And it came out at that very hour.

**16:19** But when her masters saw that their hope of profit had gone out, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the rulers.

**16:20** And having brought them to the chief magistrates, they said, "These men, being Jews, are throwing our city into turmoil,

**16:21** and are proclaiming customs that are not lawful for us to accept or to practice, being Romans."

**16:22** And the crowd rose up together against them, and the chief magistrates tore off their garments and gave orders to beat them with rods.

**16:23** And after inflicting many blows upon them, they threw them into prison, commanding the jailer to guard them securely.

**16:24** Having received such a command, he threw them into the inner prison and secured their feet in the stocks.

**16:25** Now about midnight, Paul and Silas, praying, were singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening intently to them.

**16:26** And suddenly a great earthquake occurred, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken; and immediately all the doors were opened, and everyone's bonds were unfastened.

**16:27** Now when the jailer woke up and saw the prison doors opened, he drew his sword and was about to kill himself, supposing that the prisoners had escaped.

**16:28** But Paul called out with a loud voice, saying, "Do no harm to yourself, for we are all here."

**16:29** And asking for lights, he rushed in and, trembling, fell down before Paul and Silas.

**16:30** And bringing them outside, he said, "Sirs, what must I do in order to be saved?"

**16:31** And they said, "Believe on the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household."

**16:32** And they spoke the word of the Lord to him along with all who were in his house.

**16:33** And taking them at that hour of the night, he washed their wounds, and immediately he and all who were his were baptized.

**16:34** And bringing them up into the house, he set a table before them and rejoiced greatly with his whole household, having believed in God.

**16:35** Now when day came, the chief magistrates sent the officers, saying, "Release those men."

**16:36** And the jailer reported these words to Paul: "The chief magistrates have sent word that you may be released. Now therefore, come out and go in peace."

**16:37** But Paul said to them, "Having beaten us publicly without a trial, men who are Romans, they threw us into prison; and now are they thrusting us out secretly? No indeed! Rather, let them come themselves and bring us out."

**16:38** And the officers reported these words to the chief magistrates; and they became afraid upon hearing that they were Romans.

**16:39** And they came and appealed to them, and after bringing them out, they kept asking them to depart from the city.

**16:40** And having come out of the prison, they went to Lydia's house; and when they saw the brothers, they encouraged them and departed.

## Translator Notes

- **16:7** — The earliest and best manuscripts read 'the Spirit of Jesus,' whereas some later witnesses omit 'of Jesus' to read simply 'the Spirit.'
- **16:10** — The shift from third person ('they') to first person plural ('we') marks the first of the 'we' sections in Acts.
- **16:12** — The Greek phrase translated 'a leading city of the district of Macedonia' is grammatically and textually debated; the attested text supports 'leading city' (or 'first city'), while some interpreters emend it to 'a city of the first district of Macedonia.'
- **16:16** — Literally 'a spirit, a Python,' referring to a spirit of prophecy or divination associated in the Greco-Roman world with Apollo's oracle at Delphi.
- **16:35** — Greek rhabdouchoi, literally 'rod-bearers,' referring to Roman lictors who served as official attendants to the chief magistrates and carried out public beatings.
