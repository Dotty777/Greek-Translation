## Text

**9:1** Now Saul, still breathing threats and murder against the disciples of the Lord, approached the high priest

**9:2** and requested from him letters for Damascus to the synagogues, so that if he found any who were of the Way, both men and women, he might bring them bound to Jerusalem.

**9:3** And as he was traveling, it happened that he drew near to Damascus, and suddenly a light from heaven flashed around him;

**9:4** and falling to the ground, he heard a voice saying to him, "Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?"

**9:5** And he said, "Who are You, Lord?" And He said, "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.

**9:6** But rise and enter the city, and it will be told to you what you must do."

**9:7** Now the men who were traveling with him stood speechless, hearing the voice, yet seeing no one.

**9:8** And Saul got up from the ground, and though his eyes were open, he saw nothing; so leading him by the hand, they brought him into Damascus.

**9:9** And for three days he was without sight, and he neither ate nor drank.

**9:10** Now there was a certain disciple in Damascus named Ananias, and the Lord said to him in a vision, "Ananias." And he said, "Here I am, Lord."

**9:11** And the Lord said to him, "Rise and go to the street called Straight, and inquire at the house of Judas for a man from Tarsus named Saul; for look, he is praying,

**9:12** and in a vision he has seen a man named Ananias coming in and laying hands on him so that he might regain his sight."

**9:13** But Ananias answered, "Lord, I have heard from many about this man, how much harm he has done to Your saints in Jerusalem;

**9:14** and here he has authority from the chief priests to bind all who call on Your name."

**9:15** But the Lord said to him, "Go, for this man is a chosen vessel of Mine to carry My name before Gentiles and kings and the sons of Israel;

**9:16** for I will show him how much he must suffer for the sake of My name."

**9:17** So Ananias departed and entered the house, and laying his hands on him, he said, "Brother Saul, the Lord has sent me, Jesus who appeared to you on the road by which you were coming, so that you may regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit."

**9:18** And immediately something like scales fell from his eyes, and he regained his sight; and getting up, he was baptized,

**9:19** and having taken food, he was strengthened. Now he was with the disciples in Damascus for some days,

**9:20** and immediately in the synagogues he began proclaiming Jesus, that He is the Son of God.

**9:21** And all who were hearing him were amazed and kept saying, "Is this not the man who ravaged in Jerusalem those who call on this name, and who had come here for this purpose, to bring them bound before the chief priests?"

**9:22** But Saul kept growing all the more in strength and was confounding the Jews living in Damascus by proving that this One is the Christ.

**9:23** When many days had elapsed, the Jews plotted together to kill him,

**9:24** but their plot became known to Saul. They were also closely watching the gates both day and night so that they might kill him;

**9:25** but his disciples took him by night and let him down through the wall, lowering him in a basket.

**9:26** When he arrived in Jerusalem, he kept trying to associate with the disciples; and they were all afraid of him, not believing that he was a disciple.

**9:27** But Barnabas took hold of him and brought him to the apostles, and recounted to them how on the road he had seen the Lord, and that He had spoken to him, and how in Damascus he had spoken boldly in the name of Jesus.

**9:28** And he was with them, going in and going out in Jerusalem, speaking boldly in the name of the Lord,

**9:29** and he was speaking and disputing with the Hellenists, but they were attempting to kill him.

**9:30** When the brothers learned of this, they brought him down to Caesarea and sent him off to Tarsus.

**9:31** So the church throughout all Judea and Galilee and Samaria had peace, being built up; and walking in the fear of the Lord and in the encouragement of the Holy Spirit, it continued to multiply.

**9:32** Now it happened that as Peter was traveling through all parts, he came down also to the saints living in Lydda.

**9:33** There he found a certain man named Aeneas, lying on a mat for eight years, who was paralyzed.

**9:34** And Peter said to him, "Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you; rise and make your bed." And immediately he got up.

**9:35** And all who lived in Lydda and Sharon saw him, and they turned to the Lord.

**9:36** Now in Joppa there was a certain female disciple named Tabitha, which translated means Dorcas; she was full of good works and acts of charity that she continually did.

**9:37** And it happened in those days that she became sick and died; and when they had washed her, they laid her in an upper room.

**9:38** Since Lydda was near Joppa, the disciples, hearing that Peter was there, sent two men to him, urging him, "Do not delay in coming over to us."

**9:39** So Peter got up and went with them. When he arrived, they brought him up to the upper room; and all the widows stood beside him weeping and showing the tunics and garments that Dorcas used to make while she was with them.

**9:40** But Peter sent them all outside, and kneeling down, he prayed; and turning toward the body, he said, "Tabitha, rise." And she opened her eyes, and seeing Peter, she sat up.

**9:41** And giving her his hand, he raised her up; and calling the saints and the widows, he presented her alive.

**9:42** And it became known throughout all Joppa, and many believed in the Lord.

**9:43** And it happened that he stayed many days in Joppa with a certain Simon, a tanner.

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — Later manuscripts expand verses 5-6 with words drawn from Acts 26:14 and 22:10; the earliest Greek witnesses contain the shorter text translated here.
- **9:7** — Greek uses the genitive case with akouō ("hearing the voice/sound"), which is often contrasted with the accusative in Acts 22:9 to indicate that Saul's companions heard the sound without understanding the message.
- **9:15** — Literally "a vessel of choice" (skeuos eklogēs), an idiom denoting a chosen instrument or appointed agent.
- **9:25** — The earliest Greek manuscripts read "his disciples" (hoi mathētai autou), likely referring to converts won through Saul's preaching in Damascus; later manuscripts simplify this to "the disciples."
- **9:31** — The earliest manuscripts read the singular "the church" (hē ekklēsia) with singular verbs, emphasizing the unity of the whole Christian community across Judea, Galilee, and Samaria rather than the later plural reading "churches."
