## Text

**12:1** Now at that time Herod the king laid hands on some of those belonging to the church to mistreat them.

**12:2** And he killed James the brother of John with the sword.

**12:3** And seeing that it was pleasing to the Jews, he proceeded to arrest Peter also (now these were the days of Unleavened Bread),

**12:4** and having seized him, he put him in prison, handing him over to four squads of four soldiers to guard him, intending after the Passover to bring him out before the people.

**12:5** So Peter was kept in the prison, but prayer was being made earnestly by the church to God for him.

**12:6** Now when Herod was about to bring him forward, on that night Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains, and guards in front of the door were guarding the prison.

**12:7** And look, an angel of the Lord stood there, and a light shone in the cell; and striking Peter's side, he roused him, saying, "Get up quickly!" And his chains fell off his hands.

**12:8** And the angel said to him, "Fasten your belt and tie on your sandals." And he did so. Then he said to him, "Wrap your cloak around you and follow me."

**12:9** And he went out and was following him, and he did not know that what was happening through the angel was real, but supposed he was seeing a vision.

**12:10** When they had passed the first and second guard posts, they came to the iron gate that leads into the city, which opened for them by itself; and going out, they went along one street, and immediately the angel departed from him.

**12:11** And when Peter came to himself, he said, "Now I truly know that the Lord has sent out His angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod and from all the expectation of the people of the Jews."

**12:12** And when he realized this, he went to the house of Mary, the mother of John who was called Mark, where a considerable number were gathered together and praying.

**12:13** When he knocked at the door of the gate, a servant girl named Rhoda came to answer.

**12:14** And recognizing Peter's voice, out of joy she did not open the gate, but ran inside and reported that Peter was standing in front of the gate.

**12:15** They said to her, "You are out of your mind!" But she kept insisting that it was so. And they kept saying, "It is his angel."

**12:16** Meanwhile Peter kept on knocking; and when they opened it, they saw him and were astounded.

**12:17** Motioning to them with his hand to be silent, he described to them how the Lord had led him out of the prison. And he said, "Report these things to James and to the brothers." Then he went out and departed to another place.

**12:18** Now when day came, there was no small commotion among the soldiers as to what had become of Peter.

**12:19** And when Herod had searched for him and did not find him, he examined the guards and ordered them to be led away to execution. Then he went down from Judea to Caesarea and stayed there.

**12:20** Now he was bitterly angry with the people of Tyre and Sidon; and with one accord they came before him, and having won over Blastus, the one in charge of the king's bedchamber, they were asking for peace, because their country was supplied with food from the king's territory.

**12:21** On an appointed day, Herod put on royal attire, took his seat on the tribunal, and began delivering a public address to them.

**12:22** And the populace kept shouting, "The voice of a god, and not of a man!"

**12:23** And immediately an angel of the Lord struck him, because he did not give the glory to God; and having been eaten by worms, he breathed his last.

**12:24** But the word of God kept growing and multiplying.

**12:25** Now Barnabas and Saul returned, having completed the service to Jerusalem, bringing along with them John, who was called Mark.

## Translator Notes

- **12:4** — Greek τέσσαρσιν τετραδίοις ("four squads of four") denotes four four-man guard detachments (sixteen soldiers in total) rotating through the watches of the night.
- **12:15** — "His angel" reflects a contemporary Jewish belief that a person's guardian angel could appear in their likeness or speak with their voice.
- **12:19** — Greek ἀπαχθῆναι literally means "to be led away," a standard legal idiom in this period for being led away to execution.
- **12:25** — The Greek text reads εἰς Ἰερουσαλήμ ("to/in Jerusalem") rather than the variant ἐξ Ἰερουσαλήμ ("from Jerusalem"); with the break after ὑπέστρεψαν, the phrase modifies πληρώσαντες τὴν διακονίαν ("having completed the service to Jerusalem") upon their return to Antioch.
