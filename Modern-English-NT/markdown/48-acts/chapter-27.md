## Text

**27:1** Now when it was decided that we would sail for Italy, they handed over both Paul and some other prisoners to a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan cohort.

**27:2** Boarding a ship of Adramyttium that was about to sail to the places along Asia, we put out to sea, Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, being with us.

**27:3** The next day we put in at Sidon, and Julius, treating Paul kindly, permitted him to go to his friends to receive care.

**27:4** Putting out to sea from there, we sailed under the lee of Cyprus because the winds were contrary.

**27:5** After sailing across the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came down to Myra in Lycia.

**27:6** There the centurion found an Alexandrian ship sailing for Italy, and he put us aboard it.

**27:7** Sailing slowly for many days and coming with difficulty off Cnidus, since the wind did not permit us to advance, we sailed under the lee of Crete off Salmone;

**27:8** and coasting along it with difficulty, we came to a certain place called Fair Havens, near which was the city of Lasea.

**27:9** When considerable time had passed and the voyage was now dangerous because even the Fast had already gone by, Paul began to advise them,

**27:10** saying to them, "Men, I perceive that the voyage is going to be with damage and much loss, not only of the cargo and the ship, but also of our lives."

**27:11** But the centurion was persuaded more by the pilot and the shipowner than by what was being said by Paul.

**27:12** And because the harbor was unsuitable for wintering, the majority decided to put out to sea from there, if somehow they might reach Phoenix and winter there, a harbor of Crete facing southwest and northwest.

**27:13** When a gentle south wind began to blow, supposing that they had secured their purpose, they weighed anchor and began coasting close along Crete.

**27:14** Not long afterward, however, a tempestuous wind called Euraquilo struck down from it;

**27:15** and when the ship was caught and unable to face into the wind, we gave way and were driven along.

**27:16** Running under the lee of a small island called Clauda, we were able with difficulty to gain control of the ship's boat.

**27:17** After hoisting it up, they used supports to undergird the ship; and fearing that they might be driven onto the Syrtis, they lowered the gear and so were driven along.

**27:18** Since we were being violently storm-tossed, on the next day they began to jettison the cargo,

**27:19** and on the third day they threw out the ship's tackle with their own hands.

**27:20** When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days, and no small storm was pressing upon us, from then on all hope of our being saved was stripped away.

**27:21** When they had gone a long time without food, Paul stood in the midst of them and said, "Men, you ought indeed to have listened to me and not put out to sea from Crete, and so spared yourselves this damage and loss.

**27:22** Yet now I advise you to take courage, for there will be no loss of life among you, but only of the ship.

**27:23** For this very night an angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I also serve stood beside me,

**27:24** saying, 'Do not be afraid, Paul; you must stand before Caesar; and look, God has graciously granted to you all those who are sailing with you.'

**27:25** Therefore take courage, men, for I believe God that it will happen just as it has been spoken to me.

**27:26** However, we must run aground on a certain island."

**27:27** Now when the fourteenth night came, as we were being driven about in the Adriatic Sea, around the middle of the night the sailors began to suspect that some land was drawing near to them.

**27:28** And taking soundings, they found twenty fathoms; and after moving a short distance farther and taking soundings again, they found fifteen fathoms.

**27:29** Fearing that we might run aground somewhere on rocky places, they cast four anchors from the stern and prayed for day to come.

**27:30** But as the sailors were seeking to escape from the ship and had lowered the ship's boat into the sea, under the pretext that they were about to lay out anchors from the bow,

**27:31** Paul said to the centurion and to the soldiers, "Unless these men remain in the ship, you cannot be saved."

**27:32** Then the soldiers cut away the ropes of the ship's boat and let it fall away.

**27:33** Until day was about to come, Paul was urging everyone to take some food, saying, "Today is the fourteenth day that you have continued watching without food, having taken nothing.

**27:34** Therefore I urge you to take some food, for this is for your survival; for not a hair from the head of any one of you will perish."

**27:35** Having said these things, he took bread, gave thanks to God in the presence of all, broke it, and began to eat.

**27:36** All of them took courage and took some food themselves.

**27:37** Now in all, we were two hundred seventy-six souls in the ship.

**27:38** When they had eaten enough food, they began lightening the ship by throwing the wheat into the sea.

**27:39** When day came, they did not recognize the land, but they noticed a certain bay with a beach, onto which they planned to drive the ship ashore, if they could.

**27:40** And casting off the anchors, they left them in the sea, at the same time loosening the lashings of the rudders; and hoisting the foresail to the wind, they made for the beach.

**27:41** But striking a place where two seas met, they ran the vessel aground; and the bow stuck fast and remained immovable, while the stern was being broken up by the violence.

**27:42** Now the soldiers' plan was to kill the prisoners, so that none of them would swim away and escape.

**27:43** But the centurion, wanting to bring Paul safely through, kept them from their purpose, and ordered those who were able to swim to throw themselves overboard first and get to land,

**27:44** and the rest, some on planks and others on various things from the ship. And so it happened that all were brought safely to land.

## Translator Notes

- **27:9** — "The Fast" refers to the Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur) in late September or October, after which open-sea navigation on the Mediterranean became hazardous.
- **27:12** — Literally "looking down the southwest wind and down the northwest wind"; scholars debate whether this describes a harbor opening toward the southwest and northwest, or toward the northeast and southeast (the direction in which those winds blow).
- **27:17** — "Lowered the gear" translates a general nautical term (skeuos) whose exact referent is debated, likely meaning either lowering the main sail and rigging or deploying a sea anchor to slow the ship's drift toward the Syrtis sandbanks.
- **27:27** — In first-century geography, "the Adriatic Sea" (ho Adrias) encompassed the central Mediterranean between Greece, Crete, Sicily, and Malta, not merely the modern Adriatic.
- **27:41** — The text reads simply "by the violence" (hypo tes bias); some later manuscripts add "of the waves," which is absent from the earliest witnesses followed here.
