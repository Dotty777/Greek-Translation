## Text

**23:1** Looking intently at the Sanhedrin, Paul said, "Men, brothers, I have conducted my life before God in all good conscience until this day."

**23:2** And the high priest Ananias ordered those standing beside him to strike his mouth.

**23:3** Then Paul said to him, "God is going to strike you, whitewashed wall! And do you sit judging me according to the law, and yet violating the law command me to be struck?"

**23:4** And those standing by said, "Do you revile God's high priest?"

**23:5** And Paul said, "I did not know, brothers, that he is high priest; for it is written, 'You must not speak evil of a ruler of your people.'"

**23:6** Now knowing that one part was Sadducees and the other Pharisees, Paul cried out in the Sanhedrin, "Men, brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees! Concerning the hope and resurrection of the dead I am on trial."

**23:7** And as he was saying this, a dissension arose between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the assembly was divided.

**23:8** For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, nor angel, nor spirit, but the Pharisees acknowledge both.

**23:9** And a loud clamor arose, and some of the scribes of the party of the Pharisees stood up and were arguing sharply, saying, "We find nothing wrong in this man; and what if a spirit spoke to him, or an angel—"

**23:10** And as a great dissension developed, the commander, fearing that Paul might be torn to pieces by them, commanded the troops to go down and seize him from their midst and bring him into the barracks.

**23:11** On the following night the Lord stood beside him and said, "Take courage, for as you have testified to the things concerning Me in Jerusalem, so you must also testify in Rome."

**23:12** When day came, the Jews formed a conspiracy and bound themselves under a curse, saying that they would neither eat nor drink until they killed Paul.

**23:13** Now there were more than forty who made this sworn conspiracy.

**23:14** They came to the chief priests and the elders and said, "We have solemnly bound ourselves under a curse to taste nothing until we kill Paul.

**23:15** Now therefore, you, along with the Sanhedrin, notify the commander so that he will bring him down to you, as though you were about to investigate the matters concerning him more closely; and as for us, before he comes near, we are ready to kill him."

**23:16** But the son of Paul's sister heard about the ambush, and he came and entered the barracks and reported it to Paul.

**23:17** Then Paul called one of the centurions to him and said, "Lead this young man to the commander, for he has something to report to him."

**23:18** So he took him and brought him to the commander and said, "The prisoner Paul called me to him and asked me to bring this young man to you, since he has something to tell you."

**23:19** And the commander took him by the hand and, withdrawing privately, began to inquire, "What is it that you have to report to me?"

**23:20** And he said, "The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down to the Sanhedrin tomorrow, as though you were going to inquire somewhat more closely concerning him.

**23:21** Therefore do not be persuaded by them, for more than forty men from among them are lying in ambush for him, who have bound themselves under a curse neither to eat nor to drink until they kill him; and now they are ready, waiting for the promise from you."

**23:22** So the commander dismissed the young man, charging him, "Tell no one that you have reported these things to me."

**23:23** And calling two of the centurions to him, he said, "Make ready two hundred soldiers to go as far as Caesarea, and seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen, at the third hour of the night."

**23:24** They were also to provide mounts so that, having mounted Paul, they might bring him safely to Felix the governor.

**23:25** And he wrote a letter having this form:

**23:26** "Claudius Lysias, to the most excellent governor Felix: Greetings.

**23:27** This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be killed by them, when I came upon them with the troops and rescued him, having learned that he is a Roman.

**23:28** And wanting to ascertain the charge for which they were accusing him, I brought him down to their Sanhedrin.

**23:29** I found him to be accused concerning questions of their law, yet having no accusation deserving death or chains.

**23:30** When it was disclosed to me that there would be a plot against the man, I sent him to you at once, also instructing the accusers to speak against him before you."

**23:31** So the soldiers, in accordance with what had been ordered them, took Paul and brought him by night to Antipatris.

**23:32** And on the next day, leaving the horsemen to go on with him, they returned to the barracks.

**23:33** When these entered Caesarea and delivered the letter to the governor, they also presented Paul to him.

**23:34** When he had read it and asked from what province he was, and learned that he was from Cilicia,

**23:35** "I will give you a full hearing," he said, "when your accusers arrive as well," and he ordered him to be kept under guard in Herod's praetorium.

## Translator Notes

- **23:8** — Greek amphotera literally means "both," either grouping "angel" and "spirit" together as one category alongside "resurrection" or reflecting Koine Greek usage meaning "all of them."
- **23:9** — The Greek sentence breaks off abruptly after "or an angel" (aposiopesis); later manuscripts add "let us not fight against God" to complete the clause.
- **23:20** — The critical text reads the singular participle mellon ("as though you were going to inquire"), attributing the pretext to the commander, whereas some manuscripts read plural forms referring to the Sanhedrin.
- **23:23** — The rare Greek term dexiolabos (literally "right-side taker," occurring only here in the New Testament) denotes a class of light-armed infantry, usually understood as spearmen or lancers.
