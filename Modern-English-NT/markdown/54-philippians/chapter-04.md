## Text

**4:1** So then, my beloved and longed-for brothers, my joy and crown, stand firm in this way in the Lord, beloved.

**4:2** I urge Euodia and I urge Syntyche to be of the same mind in the Lord.

**4:3** Yes, I also ask you, genuine companion, assist these women, who contended alongside me in the gospel along with Clement as well and the rest of my fellow workers, whose names are in the book of life.

**4:4** Rejoice in the Lord always; I will say again, rejoice.

**4:5** Let your gentleness be known to all people. The Lord is near.

**4:6** Be anxious about nothing, but in everything, through prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God.

**4:7** And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will stand guard over your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.

**4:8** Finally, brothers, whatever things are true, whatever things are noble, whatever things are righteous, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are admirable, if there is any excellence and if there is any praise, consider these things.

**4:9** The things that you learned and received and heard and saw in me, put these into practice, and the God of peace will be with you.

**4:10** Now I rejoiced greatly in the Lord because now at last you have made your concern for me flourish again; indeed, you were concerned about this, yet you had no opportunity.

**4:11** Not that I speak out of lack, for I have learned in whatever state I am to be content.

**4:12** I know how to be brought low, and I also know how to abound; in every situation and in all things I have learned the secret, both to be well fed and to go hungry, both to abound and to suffer lack.

**4:13** I have strength for all things in the One who empowers me.

**4:14** Nevertheless, you did well by sharing with me in my affliction.

**4:15** And you yourselves also know, Philippians, that in the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no church shared with me in the account of giving and receiving except you alone,

**4:16** for even in Thessalonica you sent to me more than once for my need.

**4:17** Not that I am seeking the gift, but I am seeking the fruit that increases to your account.

**4:18** Now I have received everything in full and I abound; I have been filled, having received from Epaphroditus the things from you, a fragrant aroma, an acceptable sacrifice, pleasing to God.

**4:19** And my God will fill every need of yours according to His riches in glory in Christ Jesus.

**4:20** And to our God and Father be the glory forever and ever. Amen.

**4:21** Greet every saint in Christ Jesus. The brothers with me greet you.

**4:22** All the saints greet you, and especially those from Caesar's household.

**4:23** The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Amen!

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — Greek gnesie syzyge ("genuine companion," literally "true yokefellow") may address an unnamed coworker or treat Syzygos as a personal name ("Syzygus").
- **4:5** — Greek to epieikes ("gentleness") conveys gracious forbearance or reasonableness; "The Lord is near" may refer either to Christ's imminent return or to His continual presence.
- **4:12** — Greek memyemai ("I have learned the secret") literally means "I have been initiated," drawing on terminology associated with ancient mystery cults.
- **4:13** — The critical Greek text reads simply "in the One who empowers me," without the explicit name "Christ" added in later manuscripts.
- **4:23** — The Saints Greek Critical Text includes the closing "Amen!" attested in many manuscripts, though some early witnesses omit it.
