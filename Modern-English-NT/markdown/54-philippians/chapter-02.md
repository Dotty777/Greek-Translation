## Text

**2:1** Therefore, if there is any encouragement in Christ, if any consolation of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit, if any tender affection and compassion,

**2:2** make my joy complete, that you be of the same mind, having the same love, united in soul, thinking the one thing,

**2:3** doing nothing from selfish ambition or vain conceit, but with humility of mind considering one another as surpassing yourselves,

**2:4** not each looking out for his own interests, but each also for the interests of others.

**2:5** Hold this mindset among yourselves, which was also in Christ Jesus,

**2:6** who, existing in the form of God, did not consider being equal with God a thing to be seized,

**2:7** but emptied Himself by taking the form of a slave, coming to be in the likeness of human beings; and being found in outward appearance as a man,

**2:8** He humbled Himself, becoming obedient up to death—indeed, death on a cross.

**2:9** For this reason God also supremely exalted Him and graciously granted Him the name that is above every name,

**2:10** so that at the name of Jesus every knee may bow, of those in heaven and on earth and beneath the earth,

**2:11** and every tongue may openly confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

**2:12** Consequently, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed—not only as in my presence, but now far more in my absence—work out your own salvation with fear and trembling,

**2:13** for God is the One working in you both to will and to work for the sake of His good pleasure.

**2:14** Do everything without grumbling and disputes,

**2:15** so that you may become blameless and pure, unblemished children of God in the midst of a crooked and twisted generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world,

**2:16** holding fast to the word of life, for a ground of boasting for me on the day of Christ, that I did not run in vain nor labor in vain.

**2:17** Yet even if I am being poured out as a drink offering upon the sacrifice and service of your faith, I rejoice and rejoice together with all of you.

**2:18** And in the same way, you also rejoice and rejoice together with me.

**2:19** Now I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, so that I also may be cheered when I learn about your circumstances.

**2:20** For I have no one of kindred soul who will genuinely care for the things concerning you,

**2:21** for they all seek their own interests, not those of Jesus Christ.

**2:22** But you know his proven worth, that as a child with a father, he served with me for the gospel.

**2:23** Therefore I hope to send him at once, as soon as I see how things turn out for me;

**2:24** and I am confident in the Lord that I myself will also come soon.

**2:25** Yet I considered it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus, my brother and fellow worker and fellow soldier, and your messenger and minister to my need,

**2:26** since he was longing for all of you and was deeply distressed because you had heard that he was ill.

**2:27** For indeed he was ill, coming close to death; but God had mercy on him, and not on him only, but also on me, so that I would not have sorrow upon sorrow.

**2:28** Therefore I have sent him all the more eagerly, so that when you see him again you may rejoice, and I may be less sorrowful.

**2:29** Receive him then in the Lord with all joy, and hold such people in honor,

**2:30** because for the work of Christ he came close to death, risking his life to supply what was lacking in your service toward me.

## Translator Notes

- **2:5** — Because the relative clause omits a verb in Greek, it may point either to Christ's own example ("which was also in Christ Jesus") or to the mindset believers share in union with Him ("which is also yours in Christ Jesus").
- **2:6** — The rare Greek noun "harpagmos" ("a thing to be seized") is much debated; it can denote either something not yet possessed to be grasped at or an existing privilege not to be exploited for selfish advantage.
- **2:15** — The Greek verb "phainesthe" has the same form in the indicative and imperative, so it can be read either as a statement ("you shine") or as a command ("shine").
- **2:16** — The Greek participle "epechontes" can mean either "holding fast to" (retaining) or "holding forth" (offering to the world) the word of life.
