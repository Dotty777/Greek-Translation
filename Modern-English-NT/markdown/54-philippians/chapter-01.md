## Text

**1:1** Paul and Timothy, slaves of Christ Jesus, to all the saints in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, together with the overseers and deacons:

**1:2** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:3** I give thanks to my God upon every remembrance of you,

**1:4** always in every petition of mine on behalf of all of you, making this petition with joy,

**1:5** because of your participation in the gospel from the first day until now,

**1:6** being confident of this very thing, that He who began a good work in you will carry it to completion until the day of Christ Jesus;

**1:7** just as it is right for me to think this way concerning all of you, because I hold you in my heart, since both in my chains and in the defense and confirmation of the gospel, all of you are fellow partners with me of grace.

**1:8** For God is my witness how I long for all of you with the deep affection of Christ Jesus.

**1:9** And this I pray, that your love may abound still more and more in full knowledge and all discernment,

**1:10** so that you may approve the things that excel, in order that you may be pure and blameless for the day of Christ,

**1:11** having been filled with the fruit of righteousness that comes through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God.

**1:12** Now I want you to know, brothers, that the things concerning me have turned out rather for the advancement of the gospel,

**1:13** so that my chains have become clearly known in Christ throughout the entire praetorian guard and to all the rest,

**1:14** and most of the brothers, having gained confidence in the Lord through my chains, are far more daring to speak the word of God without fear.

**1:15** Some indeed preach Christ even out of envy and strife, yet others also out of goodwill.

**1:16** The latter do so out of love, knowing that I am appointed for the defense of the gospel;

**1:17** but the former proclaim Christ out of selfish ambition, not sincerely, supposing to stir up affliction in my chains.

**1:18** What then? Only that in every way, whether in pretense or in truth, Christ is proclaimed, and in this I rejoice. Yes, and I will rejoice,

**1:19** for I know that this will turn out for my salvation through your petition and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ,

**1:20** according to my eager expectation and hope that I will be put to shame in nothing, but that with all boldness, as always, so also now Christ will be magnified in my body, whether through life or through death.

**1:21** For to me, living is Christ and dying is gain.

**1:22** But if living in the flesh means fruit of labor for me—then what I will choose, I cannot tell.

**1:23** Yet I am hard-pressed between the two, having the desire to depart and be with Christ, for that is very much better;

**1:24** but to remain on in the flesh is more necessary on your account.

**1:25** And being confident of this, I know that I will remain and continue alongside all of you for your advancement and joy of faith,

**1:26** so that your ground for boasting in Christ Jesus may abound on my account through my coming to you again.

**1:27** Only conduct yourselves as citizens worthy of the gospel of Christ, so that whether I come and see you or am absent, I may hear about you that you are standing firm in one spirit, with one soul striving side by side for the faith of the gospel,

**1:28** and in nothing terrified by those who oppose you (which is to them a proof of destruction, yet of your salvation, and this from God,

**1:29** because it has been graciously granted to you on behalf of Christ not only to believe in Him, but also to suffer on His behalf),

**1:30** having the same struggle that you saw in me and now hear to be in me.

## Translator Notes

- **1:7** — The Greek infinitive phrase is grammatically ambiguous and can be read either as "because I hold you in my heart" or "because you hold me in your heart," though the former best fits the immediate context.
- **1:13** — Greek "praitorion" can refer either to the imperial (praetorian) guard in Rome or to a Roman governor's official headquarters/palace in a province.
- **1:19** — Echoing Job 13:16 (LXX), Greek "soteria" ("salvation") may encompass both Paul's deliverance or vindication before the court and his ultimate salvation before God.
- **1:27** — The verb "politeuesthe" means "conduct yourselves as citizens," a pointed civic metaphor for believers residing in the Roman colony of Philippi.
