## Text

**1:1** Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus, and Timothy our brother, to Philemon, our beloved and fellow worker,

**1:2** and to our sister Apphia, and to our fellow soldier Archippus, and to the church at your house:

**1:3** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:4** I give thanks to my God always, making mention of you in my prayers,

**1:5** hearing of your love and the faith that you have toward the Lord Jesus and for all the saints,

**1:6** so that the sharing of your faith may become effective in the full knowledge of every good thing that is in us for Christ.

**1:7** For I had much joy and encouragement over your love, because the hearts of the saints have been refreshed through you, brother.

**1:8** Therefore, though I have much boldness in Christ to command you to do what is fitting,

**1:9** for the sake of love I appeal instead, being such a person as Paul, an old man, and now also a prisoner of Christ Jesus—

**1:10** I appeal to you concerning my own child, whom I fathered in my chains, Onesimus,

**1:11** who was once useless to you, but now is useful to you and to me,

**1:12** whom I have sent back to you—him, that is, my very heart.

**1:13** I myself wanted to keep him with me, so that on your behalf he might serve me in the chains of the gospel,

**1:14** but without your consent I did not want to do anything, so that your good deed might not be as if by compulsion, but voluntarily.

**1:15** For perhaps for this reason he was separated for a short while, so that you might have him back forever,

**1:16** no longer as a slave, but more than a slave: a beloved brother, especially to me, and how much more to you, both in the flesh and in the Lord.

**1:17** So if you regard me as a partner, receive him as you would me.

**1:18** But if he has wronged you in anything, or owes anything, charge this to my account.

**1:19** I, Paul, have written this with my own hand: I myself will repay it—not to say to you that you owe me even your own self in addition.

**1:20** Yes, brother, may I receive benefit from you in the Lord; refresh my heart in Christ.

**1:21** Being confident in your obedience, I have written to you, knowing that you will do even beyond what I say.

**1:22** And at the same time, also prepare lodging for me, for I hope that through your prayers I will be graciously granted to you.

**1:23** Epaphras, my fellow captive in Christ Jesus, greets you,

**1:24** together with Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, my fellow workers.

**1:25** May the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **1:6** — The phrase "the sharing of your faith" (Greek koinonia) can refer either to generosity arising from faith or to the fellowship created by faith. Some manuscripts read "in you" (hymin) instead of "in us" (hemin).
- **1:9** — Greek presbytes literally means "old man," though some interpreters read it as a wordplay on or alternate spelling of presbeutes ("ambassador").
- **1:11** — Paul plays on the name Onesimus, which means "useful" or "profitable," contrasting "useless" (achrestos) with "useful" (euchrestos).
- **1:20** — The verb "may I receive benefit" (onaimen) plays again on the name Onesimus; "heart" (Greek splanchna, also in vv. 7, 12) literally denotes the inward parts as the seat of deep affection.
