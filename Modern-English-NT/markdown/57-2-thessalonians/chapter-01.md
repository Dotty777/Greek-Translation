## Text

**1:1** Paul and Silvanus and Timothy, to the church of the Thessalonians in God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ:

**1:2** Grace to you and peace from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:3** We ought to give thanks to God always concerning you, brothers, just as it is fitting, because your faith grows exceedingly and the love of each and every one of you toward one another abounds,

**1:4** so that we ourselves boast in you among the churches of God regarding your steadfastness and faith in all your persecutions and the afflictions that you are enduring,

**1:5** which is evidence of the righteous judgment of God, so that you may be counted worthy of the kingdom of God, for which you also suffer,

**1:6** since indeed it is righteous with God to repay affliction to those who afflict you,

**1:7** and to you who are afflicted, relief together with us at the revelation of the Lord Jesus from heaven with the angels of His power

**1:8** in flaming fire, inflicting vengeance on those who do not know God and on those who do not obey the gospel of our Lord Jesus.

**1:9** Such ones will pay the penalty, eternal destruction, away from the presence of the Lord and from the glory of His might,

**1:10** when He comes to be glorified in His saints and to be marveled at among all who have believed—because our testimony to you was believed—on that day.

**1:11** To this end we also pray always for you, that our God may count you worthy of the calling, and fulfill every desire for goodness and work of faith in power,

**1:12** so that the name of our Lord Jesus may be glorified in you, and you in Him, according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ.

## Translator Notes

- **1:8** — The phrase "in flaming fire" (literally "in a fire of flame") can be connected either with the revelation of the Lord Jesus in 1:7 or with the inflicting of vengeance in 1:8.
- **1:9** — The preposition "away from" (Greek apo) may express either eternal separation from the Lord's presence or the source from which the destruction comes.
- **1:12** — The Greek phrase "of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ" shares a single definite article, allowing it to be read either of two persons or of one ("our God and Lord, Jesus Christ").
