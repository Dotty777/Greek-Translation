## Text

**1:1** The elder, to the chosen lady and her children, whom I love in truth, and not I alone, but also all who have come to know the truth,

**1:2** because of the truth that remains in us and will be with us forever.

**1:3** Grace, mercy, and peace from God the Father and from Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, will be with us in truth and love.

**1:4** I rejoiced greatly that I have found some of your children walking in truth, just as we received a commandment from the Father.

**1:5** And now I ask you, lady—not as if writing a new commandment to you, but the one we had from the beginning—that we love one another.

**1:6** And this is love: that we walk according to His commandments. This is the commandment, just as you heard from the beginning, that you walk in it.

**1:7** For many deceivers have gone out into the world, those who do not confess Jesus Christ coming in the flesh. This one is the deceiver and the antichrist.

**1:8** Watch yourselves, so that you do not lose what we worked for, but receive a full reward.

**1:9** Everyone who goes ahead and does not remain in the teaching of Christ does not have God. The one who remains in the teaching—this one has both the Father and the Son.

**1:10** If anyone comes to you and does not bring this teaching, do not take him into your house and do not offer him a greeting,

**1:11** for the one who offers him a greeting shares in his evil works.

**1:12** Although I have many things to write to you, I did not wish to do so with paper and ink; instead, I hope to come to you and speak face to face, so that your joy may be complete.

**1:13** Your chosen sister's children greet you.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The phrase "chosen lady" (Greek eklekte kyria) is widely understood as a figurative reference to a local church and its members ("her children"), though some take it to refer to an individual Christian woman.
- **1:6** — The feminine singular pronoun "it" (aute) can refer back either to "love" or to "the commandment," both of which are feminine nouns in Greek.
- **1:8** — The Greek shifts from second person plural ("you do not lose") to first person plural ("what we worked for") and back to second person plural ("receive"); some manuscripts harmonize all three verbs to the same person.
- **1:9** — The participle "goes ahead" (proagon) denotes moving beyond the apostolic teaching; later manuscripts substitute "transgresses" (parabainon).
- **1:12** — "Face to face" translates the literal Greek idiom "mouth to mouth." While this text reads "your joy" (hymon), a number of manuscripts read "our joy" (hemon).
