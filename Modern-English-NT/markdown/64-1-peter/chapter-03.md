## Text

**3:1** In the same way, wives, be subject to your own husbands, so that even if any disobey the word, they will be won without a word through the conduct of their wives,

**3:2** as they observe your pure conduct in fear.

**3:3** Let your adornment not be the outward kind, of braiding hair, wearing gold ornaments, or putting on garments,

**3:4** but the hidden person of the heart, with the imperishable quality of a gentle and quiet spirit, which in the sight of God is of great worth.

**3:5** For in this way formerly the holy women also, who hoped in God, used to adorn themselves, being subject to their own husbands,

**3:6** just as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him "lord." You have become her children as you do good and do not fear any terror.

**3:7** Husbands, in the same way, live with your wives according to knowledge, showing honor to the woman as to the weaker vessel, since they are also fellow heirs of the grace of life, so that your prayers will not be hindered.

**3:8** Finally, all of you be united in mind, sympathetic, brotherly in love, compassionate, humble-minded,

**3:9** not returning evil for evil or reviling for reviling, but on the contrary blessing, because to this you were called so that you may inherit a blessing.

**3:10** For, "The one who desires to love life and to see good days must keep his tongue from evil and his lips from speaking deceit;

**3:11** he must turn away from evil and do good; he must seek peace and pursue it.

**3:12** For the eyes of the Lord are on the righteous, and His ears are toward their supplication, but the face of the Lord is against those who do evil."

**3:13** And who will harm you if you become zealous for what is good?

**3:14** Yet even if you should suffer for the sake of righteousness, you are blessed. And do not fear their terror, nor be troubled,

**3:15** but in your hearts set apart Christ as Lord, always ready to give a defense to everyone who asks you for an account of the hope that is in you,

**3:16** yet with gentleness and fear, keeping a good conscience, so that in the matter in which you are slandered, those who malign your good conduct in Christ may be put to shame.

**3:17** For it is better, if the will of God should so will, to suffer for doing good rather than for doing evil.

**3:18** For Christ also died once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, so that He might bring you to God, having been put to death in the flesh, yet made alive in the spirit;

**3:19** in which He also went and proclaimed to the spirits in prison,

**3:20** who formerly disobeyed when the patience of God kept waiting in the days of Noah while an ark was being constructed, in which a few, that is, eight souls, were saved through water.

**3:21** Corresponding to that, baptism now saves you also, not the removal of dirt from the flesh, but an appeal to God for a good conscience, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ,

**3:22** who is at the right hand of God, having gone into heaven, with angels and authorities and powers having been subjected to Him.

## Translator Notes

- **3:14** — Echoing Isaiah 8:12, Greek ton phobon auton ("their terror") may mean either the fear they seek to instill ("fear of them") or the things they themselves fear.
- **3:18** — The contrasting datives sarki ... pneumati ("in the flesh ... in the spirit") may denote realms of existence (bodily versus spiritual) or, for pneumati, divine agency ("made alive by the Spirit").
- **3:19** — The identity of "the spirits in prison" and the nature of Christ's proclamation are widely debated, with major views understanding them as disobedient angelic spirits from Genesis 6 or human spirits from the generation of Noah.
- **3:21** — Greek syneideseos agathes eperotema can be understood either as "an appeal to God for a good conscience" (objective genitive) or as "the pledge of a good conscience toward God" (subjective genitive).
