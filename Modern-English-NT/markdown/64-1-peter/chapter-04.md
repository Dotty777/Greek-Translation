## Text

**4:1** Therefore, since Christ suffered in the flesh, arm yourselves likewise with the same mindset, for the one who has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin,

**4:2** so as to live out the remaining time in the flesh no longer for human passions, but for the will of God.

**4:3** For the time that has passed is sufficient to have carried out the purpose of the Gentiles, having walked in debauchery, passions, drunkenness, revelries, drinking bouts, and lawless idolatries.

**4:4** In this regard, they are surprised that you do not run together with them into the same flood of reckless dissipation, slandering you;

**4:5** they will give an account to Him who stands ready to judge the living and the dead.

**4:6** For to this end the gospel was proclaimed also to the dead, so that, though judged in the flesh according to humans, they might live in the spirit according to God.

**4:7** Now the end of all things has drawn near. Therefore, be of sound mind and be sober for prayers.

**4:8** Above all, maintain fervent love for one another, for love covers a multitude of sins.

**4:9** Be hospitable toward one another without grumbling.

**4:10** Just as each one has received a gift, serve one another with it as good stewards of the diverse grace of God.

**4:11** If anyone speaks, let it be as utterances of God; if anyone serves, let it be as from the strength that God supplies; so that in all things God may be glorified through Jesus Christ, to whom belong the glory and the might forever and ever. Amen.

**4:12** Beloved, do not be surprised at the fiery ordeal among you that is taking place to test you, as though something strange were happening to you;

**4:13** but to the extent that you share in the sufferings of Christ, rejoice, so that at the revelation of His glory you may also rejoice exultantly.

**4:14** If you are reproached for the name of Christ, you are blessed, for the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you.

**4:15** For let none of you suffer as a murderer, or a thief, or a wrongdoer, or as a meddler in others' affairs;

**4:16** yet if anyone suffers as a Christian, let him not be ashamed, but let him glorify God in this name.

**4:17** For the time has come for judgment to begin from the household of God; and if it begins first with us, what will be the end of those who disobey the gospel of God?

**4:18** And if the righteous person is saved with difficulty, where will the ungodly and the sinner appear?

**4:19** Therefore, let those who suffer according to the will of God also commit their souls to a faithful Creator in doing good.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Interpreters debate whether "the one who has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin" refers directly to Christ or states a general principle applying to the believer who shares in Christ's resolve to suffer rather than sin.
- **4:6** — The identity of "the dead" is debated, commonly understood either as believers who heard the gospel before dying or as the dead who heard Christ's proclamation after death. The parallel datives "in the flesh" and "in the spirit" (possibly "by the Spirit") contrast mortal human judgment with spiritual life before God.
- **4:15** — The Greek compound allotriepiskopos occurs only here in the New Testament and literally means an "overseer of another's affairs," hence a meddler or busybody.
- **4:16** — The Greek critical text reads "in this name" (en to onomati touto), referring to the name "Christian," whereas some later manuscripts read "in this matter" (en to merei touto).
