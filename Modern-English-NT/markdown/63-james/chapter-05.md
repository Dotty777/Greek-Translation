## Text

**5:1** Come now, you rich, weep, wailing over your miseries that are coming.

**5:2** Your wealth has rotted, and your garments have become moth-eaten,

**5:3** your gold and your silver have corroded, and their corrosion will be for a testimony against you and will eat your flesh; like fire you have stored up treasure in the last days.

**5:4** Look, the pay of the laborers who mowed your fields, which was kept back by you, cries out, and the outcries of those who harvested have entered the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth.

**5:5** You lived in luxury on the earth and gave yourselves to pleasure; you fattened your hearts in a day of slaughter.

**5:6** You condemned, you murdered the righteous one. Does he not oppose you?

**5:7** Therefore be patient, brothers, until the coming of the Lord. Look, the farmer awaits the precious fruit of the earth, being patient over it until it receives the early and late rain.

**5:8** Be patient, you also; strengthen your hearts, because the coming of the Lord has drawn near.

**5:9** Do not groan against one another, brothers, so that you may not be judged; look, the Judge is standing before the doors.

**5:10** Take as an example, brothers, of suffering hardship and of patience, the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord.

**5:11** Look, we consider blessed those who endured. You have heard of the endurance of Job, and you have seen the outcome from the Lord, that the Lord is very compassionate and merciful.

**5:12** Now above all, my brothers, do not swear—neither by heaven, nor by the earth, nor by any other oath; instead, let your "Yes" be yes and your "No" be no, so that you may not fall under judgment.

**5:13** Is anyone among you suffering hardship? Let him pray. Is anyone in good spirits? Let him sing praises.

**5:14** Is anyone among you sick? Let him summon the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord;

**5:15** and the prayer of faith will save the ailing person, and the Lord will raise him up; and if he has committed sins, they will be forgiven him.

**5:16** Therefore confess your sins to one another, and pray on behalf of one another, so that you may be healed. The petition of a righteous person has great power in its working.

**5:17** Elijah was a man of like nature with us, and he prayed earnestly for it not to rain, and it did not rain on the earth for three years and six months;

**5:18** and he prayed again, and the sky gave rain, and the earth brought forth its fruit.

**5:19** My brothers, if anyone among you strays from the truth and someone turns him back,

**5:20** let him know that the one who turns back a sinner from the error of his way will save his soul from death and will cover a multitude of sins.

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — Following the critical text's punctuation, "like fire" modifies "you have stored up treasure," though it is also commonly attached to the preceding clause ("will eat your flesh like fire").
- **5:4** — "Sabaoth" is the Greek transliteration of the Hebrew title meaning "hosts" or "armies."
- **5:6** — The final clause is punctuated as a question in the critical text ("Does he not oppose you?"), though many interpreters read it as a statement ("He does not oppose you").
- **5:11** — "The outcome from the Lord" translates literally "the end of the Lord," referring to the final result the Lord brought about for Job.
- **5:16** — The Greek participle rendered "in its working" is grammatically ambiguous between a middle sense ("as it operates") and a passive sense ("as it is made effective" by God).
