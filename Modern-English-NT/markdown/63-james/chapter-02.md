## Text

**2:1** My brothers, surely you do not hold the faith of our glorious Lord Jesus Christ with acts of partiality?

**2:2** For if a man wearing a gold ring comes into your assembly in fine clothing, and a poor man in dirty clothing also comes in,

**2:3** and you pay special attention to the one wearing the fine clothing and say, "You sit here in a good place," and to the poor man you say, "You stand over there, or sit below my footstool,"

**2:4** have you not drawn distinctions among yourselves and become judges with evil reasoning?

**2:5** Listen, my beloved brothers. Did God not choose those who are poor in the eyes of the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom He promised to those who love Him?

**2:6** Yet you have dishonored the poor man. Do the rich not oppress you, and are they not the ones dragging you into the courts?

**2:7** Do they not blaspheme the noble name that was invoked over you?

**2:8** If, however, you are fulfilling the royal law according to the Scripture, "You shall love your neighbor as yourself," you are doing well.

**2:9** Yet if you show partiality, you commit sin, convicted by the law as transgressors.

**2:10** For anyone who keeps the entire law, yet stumbles in a single point, has become guilty of all.

**2:11** For He who said, "Do not commit adultery," also said, "Do not murder." Now if you do not commit adultery, yet you do murder, you have become a transgressor of the law.

**2:12** Speak in this way and act in this way, as those who are about to be judged through the law of freedom.

**2:13** For judgment is merciless to the one who did not show mercy; mercy triumphs over judgment.

**2:14** What is the benefit, my brothers, if someone says he has faith, yet has no works? Can that faith save him?

**2:15** If a brother or a sister is without clothing and lacking daily food,

**2:16** and one of you says to them, "Go in peace, keep warm and be well fed," yet you do not give them what is needed for the body, what is the benefit?

**2:17** In the same way, faith also, if it does not have works, is dead by itself.

**2:18** Yet someone will say, "You have faith, and I have works." Show me your faith apart from works, and I will show you faith by my works.

**2:19** Do you believe that God is one? You are doing well; the demons also believe, and they shudder.

**2:20** But do you want to know, you empty person, that faith apart from works is useless?

**2:21** Was Abraham our father not justified by works when he offered Isaac his son upon the altar?

**2:22** You see that faith was working together with his works, and by works faith was perfected,

**2:23** and the Scripture was fulfilled that says, "Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness," and he was called a friend of God.

**2:24** You see that a person is justified by works, and not by faith only.

**2:25** And in the same way, was Rahab the prostitute not also justified by works when she welcomed the messengers and sent them out by another road?

**2:26** For just as the body apart from spirit is dead, so too faith apart from works is dead.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The verb echete can be read as a question ("surely you do not hold...?") matching the Greek punctuation here, or as an imperative ("do not hold..."); "our glorious Lord Jesus Christ" is literally "our Lord Jesus Christ of glory." The Greek genitive phrase τὴν πίστιν τοῦ κυρίου ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ ("the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ") can be understood either objectively as "faith in our Lord Jesus Christ" or subjectively as "the faithfulness of our Lord Jesus Christ."
- **2:14** — The Greek definite article before "faith" (he pistis) points back to the claimed, workless faith just mentioned, hence "that faith."
- **2:18** — Commentators debate whether the hypothetical speaker's quotation ends after "I have works" (as punctuated here) or continues through the end of the verse.
- **2:20** — The Greek creates a wordplay between ergon ("works") and arge ("useless" or "barren," literally "workless").
- **2:26** — The Greek noun pneuma can be rendered either "spirit" or "breath."
