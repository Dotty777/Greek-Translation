## Text

**4:1** Where do wars come from, and where do fights among you come from? Is it not from this, from your pleasures that wage war in your members?

**4:2** You desire and do not have; you murder and are envious, and you are not able to obtain; you fight and wage war. You do not have because you do not ask.

**4:3** You ask and do not receive, because you ask wrongly, in order to spend it on your pleasures.

**4:4** Adulteresses, do you not know that friendship with the world is enmity with God? Therefore, whoever intends to be a friend of the world is made an enemy of God.

**4:5** Or do you suppose that the Scripture speaks in vain, "He jealously longs for the spirit that He caused to dwell in us"?

**4:6** But He gives greater grace; therefore it says, "God opposes the proud, yet to the humble He gives grace."

**4:7** Be subject therefore to God, but resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

**4:8** Draw near to God, and He will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, sinners, and purify your hearts, double-minded ones.

**4:9** Be distressed, and mourn, and weep; let your laughter be changed into mourning, and your joy into dejection.

**4:10** Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and He will exalt you.

**4:11** Do not speak against one another, brothers. The one speaking against a brother or judging his brother speaks against the law and judges the law; and if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law, but a judge.

**4:12** There is one Lawgiver and Judge, the One able to save and to destroy; but who are you, the one judging your neighbor?

**4:13** Come now, you who say, "Today or tomorrow we will go to this or that city, and spend a year there, and carry on trade, and make a profit,"

**4:14** you who do not know what belongs to tomorrow, what your life is like—for you are a mist that appears for a short time and then disappears—

**4:15** instead of your saying, "If the Lord wills, we will both live and do this or that."

**4:16** But as it is, you boast in your arrogant pretensions; all boasting of this kind is evil.

**4:17** Therefore, for the one who knows to do what is good and does not do it, to him it is sin.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — "Adulteresses" is feminine plural in Greek, echoing Old Testament prophetic imagery of spiritual unfaithfulness to God.
- **4:5** — Both the Greek syntax and the exact scriptural source are debated; the quotation can also be rendered "The spirit that He caused to dwell in us longs toward envy," and "spirit" may alternatively refer to the Holy Spirit.
- **4:14** — The Greek phrasing is notably abrupt ("what belongs to tomorrow, what your life is like"), and "you are a mist" follows the critical text's second-person plural verb rather than a variant reading "it is."
