## Text

**1:1** James, a slave of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes that are in the Dispersion: Greetings.

**1:2** Regard it as complete joy, my brothers, whenever you fall into various trials,

**1:3** knowing that the testing of your faith brings about endurance.

**1:4** And let endurance have its perfect work, so that you may be perfect and whole, lacking nothing.

**1:5** Now if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask from God, who gives to everyone generously and without rebuking, and it will be given to him.

**1:6** Yet let him ask in faith, doubting nothing, for the one who doubts is like a surge of the sea, driven by the wind and tossed about.

**1:7** For that person must not expect that he will receive anything from the Lord,

**1:8** a double-minded man, unstable in all of his ways.

**1:9** Now let the humble brother boast in his high position,

**1:10** and the rich one in his humbling, because like a blossom of the grass he will pass away.

**1:11** For the sun rises with its scorching heat and dries up the grass, and its blossom falls off, and the beauty of its appearance perishes; in the same way the rich man also will fade away in his pursuits.

**1:12** Blessed is the man who endures a trial, because having been approved, he will receive the crown of life, which He promised to those who love Him.

**1:13** When anyone is tempted, let him not say, "I am tempted by God," for God cannot be tempted by evil, and He Himself does not tempt anyone.

**1:14** Rather, each one is tempted as he is drawn away and lured by his own desire.

**1:15** Then, once desire has conceived, it bears sin; and when sin is fully formed, it brings forth death.

**1:16** Do not be misled, my beloved brothers.

**1:17** Every good giving and every perfect gift is from above, descending from the Father of the lights, with whom there is no variation or shadow of turning.

**1:18** By His own choice He brought us forth by the word of truth, so that we might be a kind of firstfruits of His creatures.

**1:19** Know this, my beloved brothers. And every person must be quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger,

**1:20** for a man's anger does not accomplish God's righteousness.

**1:21** Therefore, laying aside all filthiness and abundance of malice, welcome with gentleness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls.

**1:22** And become doers of the word, and not hearers only, deluding yourselves.

**1:23** For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, this person resembles a man observing his natural face in a mirror;

**1:24** for he observes himself and goes away, and immediately forgets what kind of person he was.

**1:25** But the one who looks intently into the perfect law, the law of freedom, and continues in it, becoming not a forgetful hearer but a doer of the work, this one will be blessed in his doing.

**1:26** If anyone thinks he is religious while not bridling his tongue, but deceiving his own heart, this person's religion is worthless.

**1:27** Pure and undefiled religion in the sight of God the Father is this: to look after orphans and widows in their distress, and to keep oneself unstained from the world.

## Translator Notes

- **1:13** — The Greek verb peirazo (and noun peirasmos in 1:2, 1:12) can mean either "test/trial" or "tempt/temptation" depending on context; here James shifts from outward trials to inward solicitation to evil.
- **1:17** — "Variation" (parallage) and "shadow of turning" (tropes aposkiasma) draw on ancient astronomical terms describing the shifting movements and shadows of heavenly bodies.
- **1:23** — Literally "the face of his birth," a Greek idiom for one's natural face.
