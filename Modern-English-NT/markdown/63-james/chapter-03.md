## Text

**3:1** Do not let many of you become teachers, my brothers, knowing that we will receive a greater judgment.

**3:2** For we all stumble in many ways. If anyone does not stumble in word, this person is a perfect man, able to bridle the whole body as well.

**3:3** Now if we put bits into the mouths of horses so that they obey us, we guide their entire body as well.

**3:4** Look at ships also: though they are so great and are driven by fierce winds, they are guided by a very small rudder wherever the impulse of the pilot wills.

**3:5** In the same way, the tongue also is a small member, yet it boasts of great things. See how small a fire sets ablaze how great a forest!

**3:6** And the tongue is a fire; as the world of unrighteousness, the tongue is placed among our members, staining the entire body and setting ablaze the wheel of existence, while itself being set ablaze by hell.

**3:7** For every kind of wild beast and bird, of reptile and sea creature, is tamed and has been tamed by humankind,

**3:8** but no human being is able to tame the tongue; it is an unstable evil, full of deadly poison.

**3:9** With it we bless the Lord and Father, and with it we curse human beings who have been made according to the likeness of God.

**3:10** From the same mouth come forth blessing and cursing. My brothers, these things ought not to happen this way.

**3:11** Does a spring gush both fresh water and bitter from the same opening?

**3:12** Can a fig tree, my brothers, yield olives, or a vine yield figs? Neither can salt water yield fresh water.

**3:13** Who among you is wise and understanding? Let him demonstrate his works from a good way of life in the gentleness of wisdom.

**3:14** Yet if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your heart, do not boast arrogantly and lie against the truth.

**3:15** This is not wisdom coming down from above; rather, it is earthly, unspiritual, demonic.

**3:16** For wherever jealousy and selfish ambition are, there is disorder and every base deed.

**3:17** But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, considerate, open to persuasion, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial, and without hypocrisy.

**3:18** And fruit of righteousness is sown in peace for those who make peace.

## Translator Notes

- **3:6** — "The wheel of existence" (ton trochon tes geneseos) is a vivid metaphor for the whole cycle of human life; "hell" translates Greek Gehenna.
- **3:15** — "Unspiritual" translates psychike, literally "soulish" or "merely natural," describing wisdom operating on a purely human level apart from God's Spirit.
- **3:18** — The Greek dative tois poiousin eirenen can be understood either as "for those who make peace" (dative of advantage) or "by those who make peace" (dative of agency).
