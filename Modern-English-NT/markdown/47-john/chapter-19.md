## Text

**19:1** So then Pilate took Jesus and flogged Him.

**19:2** And the soldiers twisted together a crown of thorns and set it on His head, and put a purple robe around Him.

**19:3** And they kept coming up to Him and saying, "Hail, King of the Jews!" and they kept slapping Him.

**19:4** And Pilate went outside again and said to them, "Look, I am bringing Him out to you so that you may know that I find no grounds at all for a charge in Him."

**19:5** So Jesus came outside, wearing the crown of thorns and the purple robe. And he said to them, "Here is the man."

**19:6** Therefore, when the chief priests and the officers saw Him, they shouted, saying, "Crucify! Crucify!" Pilate said to them, "Take Him yourselves and crucify Him, for I myself find no grounds for a charge in Him."

**19:7** The Jews answered him, "We have a law, and according to the law He must die, because He made Himself the Son of God."

**19:8** Therefore, when Pilate heard this statement, he became even more afraid.

**19:9** And he entered the Praetorium again and said to Jesus, "Where are You from?" But Jesus did not give him an answer.

**19:10** So Pilate said to Him, "Are You not speaking to me? Do You not know that I have authority to release You, and I have authority to crucify You?"

**19:11** Jesus answered him, "You would have no authority at all against Me unless it had been given to you from above. For this reason, the one who handed Me over to you has greater sin."

**19:12** From then on Pilate kept seeking to release Him, but the Jews shouted, saying, "If you release this man, you are no friend of Caesar. Everyone who makes himself a king speaks against Caesar."

**19:13** Therefore, when Pilate heard these words, he brought Jesus outside and sat down on the judgment seat at a place called the Stone Pavement, and in Hebrew, Gabbatha.

**19:14** Now it was the day of Preparation for the Passover; it was about the sixth hour. And he said to the Jews, "Here is your King!"

**19:15** But they shouted, "Take Him away! Take Him away! Crucify Him!" Pilate said to them, "Should I crucify your King?" The chief priests answered, "We have no king except Caesar."

**19:16** So then he handed Him over to them so that He would be crucified. And they took Jesus.

**19:17** And carrying the cross for Himself, He went out to what is called the Place of a Skull, which in Hebrew is called Golgotha.

**19:18** There they crucified Him, and with Him two others, one on this side and one on that side, with Jesus in the middle.

**19:19** Now Pilate also wrote an inscription and placed it on the cross. And what was written was, "Jesus the Nazarene, the King of the Jews."

**19:20** Therefore many of the Jews read this inscription, because the place where Jesus was crucified was near the city; and it was written in Hebrew, in Latin, and in Greek.

**19:21** So the chief priests of the Jews were saying to Pilate, "Do not write, 'The King of the Jews,' but that He said, 'I am King of the Jews.'"

**19:22** Pilate answered, "What I have written, I have written."

**19:23** Therefore, when the soldiers had crucified Jesus, they took His garments and made four parts, a part for each soldier, and the tunic as well. Now the tunic was seamless, woven from the top all the way through.

**19:24** So they said to one another, "Let us not tear it, but let us cast lots for it as to whose it will be," so that the Scripture would be fulfilled that says, "They divided My garments among themselves, and for My clothing they cast lots." So the soldiers did these things.

**19:25** Now standing beside the cross of Jesus were His mother and His mother's sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene.

**19:26** Therefore, when Jesus saw His mother and the disciple whom He loved standing nearby, He said to His mother, "Woman, here is your son."

**19:27** Then He said to the disciple, "Here is your mother." And from that hour the disciple took her into his own home.

**19:28** After this, Jesus, knowing that all things were already finished, so that the Scripture might be fulfilled, said, "I am thirsty."

**19:29** A jar full of sour wine was sitting there, so they put a sponge full of the sour wine on a hyssop branch and brought it to His mouth.

**19:30** So when Jesus had taken the sour wine, He said, "It is finished." And bowing His head, He yielded up His spirit.

**19:31** Then the Jews, because it was the day of Preparation, so that the bodies would not remain on the cross during the Sabbath (for the day of that Sabbath was a great day), asked Pilate that their legs be broken and that they be taken away.

**19:32** So the soldiers came and broke the legs of the first man, and of the other who was crucified with Him.

**19:33** But upon coming to Jesus, when they saw that He was already dead, they did not break His legs.

**19:34** Instead, one of the soldiers pierced His side with a spear, and immediately blood and water came out.

**19:35** And the one who has seen this has testified, and his testimony is true; and he knows that he speaks the truth, so that you also may believe.

**19:36** For these things took place so that the Scripture might be fulfilled, "No bone of His will be broken."

**19:37** And again another Scripture says, "They will look upon Him whom they pierced."

**19:38** Now after these things, Joseph from Arimathea, who was a disciple of Jesus, though in secret because of fear of the Jews, asked Pilate that he might take away the body of Jesus; and Pilate permitted it. So he came and took away His body.

**19:39** And Nicodemus also came, the one who had first come to Him by night, bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about a hundred pounds.

**19:40** So they took the body of Jesus and bound it in linen cloths with the spices, just as is the custom of the Jews in preparing for burial.

**19:41** Now in the place where He was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden a new tomb in which no one had yet been laid.

**19:42** So there, because of the Jews' day of Preparation, since the tomb was nearby, they laid Jesus.

## Translator Notes

- **19:13** — The verb ἐκάθισεν can be read intransitively ("sat down on the judgment seat") or transitively ("seated Him on the judgment seat"); "in Hebrew" (Ἑβραϊστί) refers here to the Aramaic or Hebrew spoken in Judea.
- **19:14** — "The sixth hour" is about noon by Jewish daylight reckoning from sunrise, or about 6:00 a.m. if Roman reckoning from midnight is used.
- **19:25** — Literally "Mary of Clopas," usually understood as the wife of Clopas; commentators also debate whether three or four women are listed depending on whether she is identified with Jesus' mother's sister.
- **19:39** — A Roman pound (λίτρα) weighed about 12 ounces (327 grams), so a hundred pounds equals roughly 75 modern pounds (34 kilograms).
