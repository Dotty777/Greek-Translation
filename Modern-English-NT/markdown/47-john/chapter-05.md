## Text

**5:1** After these things there was a feast of the Jews, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.

**5:2** Now there is in Jerusalem, by the Sheep Gate, a pool called Bethzatha in Hebrew, having five porticoes.

**5:3** In these lay a multitude of the sick—blind, lame, withered.

**5:5** Now a certain man was there who had been thirty-eight years in his illness.

**5:6** When Jesus saw him lying there and knew that he had already been that way for a long time, He said to him, "Do you want to become well?"

**5:7** The sick man answered Him, "Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up; and while I am coming, another goes down ahead of me."

**5:8** Jesus said to him, "Get up, pick up your mat, and walk."

**5:9** And immediately the man became well, and he picked up his mat and began walking. Now it was a Sabbath on that day.

**5:10** So the Jews were saying to the man who had been cured, "It is the Sabbath, and it is not lawful for you to carry the mat."

**5:11** But he answered them, "The One who made me well, He said to me, 'Pick up your mat and walk.'"

**5:12** They asked him, "Who is the man who said to you, 'Pick it up and walk'?"

**5:13** But the man who had been healed did not know who He was, for Jesus had slipped away, as there was a crowd in that place.

**5:14** After these things Jesus found him in the temple and said to him, "Look, you have become well; do not sin any longer, so that nothing worse may happen to you."

**5:15** The man went away and reported to the Jews that Jesus was the One who had made him well.

**5:16** And for this reason the Jews were persecuting Jesus, because He was doing these things on the Sabbath.

**5:17** But He answered them, "My Father is working until now, and I also am working."

**5:18** For this reason, therefore, the Jews were seeking all the more to kill Him, because He was not only breaking the Sabbath, but was also calling God His own Father, making Himself equal to God.

**5:19** So Jesus answered and said to them, "Truly, truly, I tell you, the Son can do nothing from Himself, unless He sees the Father doing something; for whatever He does, these things the Son also does in the same way.

**5:20** For the Father loves the Son and shows Him all the things that He Himself does; and He will show Him greater works than these, so that you may marvel.

**5:21** For just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom He wills.

**5:22** For not even the Father judges anyone, but He has given all judgment to the Son,

**5:23** so that all may honor the Son just as they honor the Father. The one who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent Him.

**5:24** Truly, truly, I tell you, the one who hears My word and believes Him who sent Me has eternal life, and does not come into judgment, but has crossed over out of death into life.

**5:25** Truly, truly, I tell you, an hour is coming and is now here, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who have heard will live.

**5:26** For just as the Father has life in Himself, so He also gave the Son to have life in Himself,

**5:27** and He gave Him authority to carry out judgment, because He is Son of Man.

**5:28** Do not marvel at this, because an hour is coming in which all who are in the tombs will hear His voice

**5:29** and will come out—those who did good things to a resurrection of life, and those who practiced evil things to a resurrection of judgment.

**5:30** I cannot do anything from Myself; just as I hear, I judge, and My judgment is righteous, because I do not seek My own will, but the will of the One who sent Me.

**5:31** If I testify about Myself, My testimony is not true.

**5:32** There is Another who testifies about Me, and I know that the testimony that He testifies about Me is true.

**5:33** You have sent to John, and he has testified to the truth.

**5:34** Yet I do not receive testimony from a man, but I say these things so that you may be saved.

**5:35** He was the burning and shining lamp, and you were willing to rejoice for an hour in his light.

**5:36** But I have testimony greater than that of John; for the works that the Father has given Me to complete, these very works that I do, testify about Me that the Father has sent Me.

**5:37** And the Father who sent Me, He Himself has testified about Me. You have neither heard His voice at any time nor seen His form,

**5:38** and you do not have His word remaining in you, because you do not believe the One whom He sent.

**5:39** You search the Scriptures because you suppose that in them you have eternal life; and those are the ones that testify about Me,

**5:40** yet you are unwilling to come to Me so that you may have life.

**5:41** I do not receive glory from people,

**5:42** but I know you, that you do not have the love of God in yourselves.

**5:43** I have come in the name of My Father, and you do not receive Me; if another comes in his own name, you will receive him.

**5:44** How are you able to believe, receiving glory from one another, while you do not seek the glory that comes from the only God?

**5:45** Do not suppose that I will accuse you before the Father; the one who accuses you is Moses, in whom you have placed your hope.

**5:46** For if you believed Moses, you would believe Me, for he wrote about Me.

**5:47** But if you do not believe his writings, how will you believe My words?"

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — The Greek reads literally 'by the sheep' (with 'Gate' implied, cf. Neh 3:1). Early manuscripts vary on the pool's name among Bethzatha, Bethesda, and Bethsaida, and 'in Hebrew' refers here to the Semitic vernacular (Aramaic) spoken in Judea.
- **5:3** — The earliest and most reliable Greek manuscripts omit 'waiting for the moving of the water' at the end of v. 3 and all of v. 4 (about an angel stirring the water), so v. 4 is absent from the critical text.
- **5:27** — The Greek phrase 'Son of Man' is uniquely without the definite article here, recalling both the heavenly judge of Daniel 7:13-14 and Jesus' genuine humanity.
- **5:39** — The Greek verb 'eraunate' can be construed as either an indicative ('You search') or an imperative ('Search!'); the indicative fits the flow of the argument best in contrast with v. 40.
