## Text

**1:1** In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

**1:2** This One was with God in the beginning.

**1:3** All things came into being through Him, and apart from Him not even one thing came into being. What has come into being

**1:4** in Him was life, and that life was the light of people.

**1:5** And the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overtake it.

**1:6** There appeared a man sent from God; his name was John.

**1:7** This man came for testimony, so that he might testify concerning the light, so that everyone might believe through him.

**1:8** That man was not the light, but came so that he might testify concerning the light.

**1:9** The true light, which illuminates every person, was coming into the world.

**1:10** He was in the world, and the world came into being through Him, yet the world did not know Him.

**1:11** He came to His own domain, and His own people did not accept Him.

**1:12** Yet to as many as received Him, He gave them authority to become children of God, to those believing in His name,

**1:13** who were born not from bloodlines, nor from the will of flesh, nor from the will of a husband, but from God.

**1:14** And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we gazed upon His glory, glory as of an only-begotten from a father, full of grace and truth.

**1:15** (John testifies concerning Him and has cried out, saying, "This was He of whom I said, 'The One coming after me has come ahead of me, because He was before me.'")

**1:16** Because out of His fullness all of us received, even grace in place of grace;

**1:17** because the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came into being through Jesus Christ.

**1:18** No one has ever seen God; the only-begotten God, the One who is at the Father's side, He has made Him known.

**1:19** And this is the testimony of John, when the Jews sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to ask him, "Who are you?"

**1:20** And he confessed and did not deny, and he confessed, "I am not the Christ."

**1:21** And they asked him, "What then? Are you Elijah?" And he says, "I am not." "Are you the Prophet?" And he answered, "No."

**1:22** Therefore they said to him, "Who are you, so that we may give an answer to those who sent us? What do you say concerning yourself?"

**1:23** He said, "I am a voice of one crying out in the wilderness, 'Make straight the way of the Lord,' just as Isaiah the prophet said."

**1:24** Now they had been sent from the Pharisees.

**1:25** And they asked him and said to him, "Why then are you baptizing, if you are not the Christ, nor Elijah, nor the Prophet?"

**1:26** John answered them, saying, "I baptize in water; among you stands One whom you do not know,

**1:27** the One coming after me, the strap of whose sandal I am not worthy to untie."

**1:28** These things happened in Bethany across the Jordan, where John was baptizing.

**1:29** On the next day he sees Jesus coming toward him and says, "Look, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!

**1:30** This is He on behalf of whom I said, 'After me comes a man who has come ahead of me, because He was before me.'

**1:31** And I myself did not know Him, but so that He might be revealed to Israel, for this reason I came baptizing in water."

**1:32** And John testified, saying, "I have gazed upon the Spirit descending like a dove out of heaven, and He remained upon Him.

**1:33** And I myself did not know Him, but the One who sent me to baptize in water, that One said to me, 'Upon whomever you see the Spirit descending and remaining upon Him, this is the One who baptizes in the Holy Spirit.'

**1:34** And I myself have seen and have testified that this is the Son of God."

**1:35** On the next day again John was standing, along with two of his disciples,

**1:36** and looking closely at Jesus as He was walking, he says, "Look, the Lamb of God!"

**1:37** And the two disciples heard him speaking and followed Jesus.

**1:38** Now Jesus, turning and seeing them following, says to them, "What are you seeking?" And they said to Him, "Rabbi" (which translated means "Teacher"), "where are You staying?"

**1:39** He says to them, "Come and you will see." So they came and saw where He was staying, and they stayed with Him that day; it was about the tenth hour.

**1:40** Andrew, the brother of Simon Peter, was one of the two who heard from John and followed Him.

**1:41** This man first finds his own brother Simon and says to him, "We have found the Messiah" (which translated is "Christ").

**1:42** He brought him to Jesus. Looking closely at him, Jesus said, "You are Simon, the son of John; you will be called Cephas" (which is translated "Peter").

**1:43** On the next day He wanted to go out into Galilee. And Jesus finds Philip and says to him, "Follow Me."

**1:44** Now Philip was from Bethsaida, from the city of Andrew and Peter.

**1:45** Philip finds Nathanael and says to him, "We have found the one of whom Moses wrote in the law, and also the prophets: Jesus, son of Joseph, the one from Nazareth."

**1:46** And Nathanael said to him, "Can anything good be from Nazareth?" Philip says to him, "Come and see."

**1:47** Jesus saw Nathanael coming toward Him and says concerning him, "Look, truly an Israelite in whom there is no deceit!"

**1:48** Nathanael says to Him, "Where do You know me from?" Jesus answered and said to him, "Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you."

**1:49** Nathanael answered Him, "Rabbi, You are the Son of God; You are King of Israel!"

**1:50** Jesus answered and said to him, "Because I told you that I saw you under the fig tree, do you believe? You will see greater things than these."

**1:51** And He says to him, "Truly, truly, I tell you, you will see heaven opened and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of Man."

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — In the final clause (θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος), ὁ λόγος ("the Word") has the article and is the subject, while θεός ("God") is the predicate, placed first without the article. Rendered "the Word was God" to follow English subject-predicate order.
- **1:3** — The Saints Greek Critical Text places a period after οὐδὲ ἕν ("not even one thing"), joining ὃ γέγονεν ("what has come into being") to the start of 1:4 rather than ending 1:3 with "that was made." The translation follows the Greek punctuation.
- **1:5** — κατέλαβεν carries a deliberate double resonance in John: "overtake/overcome" (hostile darkness failing to extinguish the light) and "comprehend/grasp." "Overtake" keeps the primary spatial and conflict sense of the verb.
- **1:9** — The participle ἐρχόμενον ("coming") is grammatically ambiguous: it can modify "the true light" (rendered here) or "every person" ("illuminates every person coming into the world").
- **1:11** — The Greek shifts from neuter plural τὰ ἴδια ("His own things/domain") to masculine plural οἱ ἴδιοι ("His own people"). Both are reflected distinctly.
- **1:13** — ἐξ αἱμάτων uses the plural "bloods" (rendered "bloodlines"), referring to physical descent, and ἀνδρός specifically means a male/husband rather than a generic human being.
- **1:14** — ἐσκήνωσεν literally means "pitched a tent" or "tabernacled" (cognate with σκηνή, "tent, tabernacle"). μονογενοῦς means "only-begotten" or "unique, one of a kind."
- **1:16** — χάριν ἀντὶ χάριτος literally means "grace in place of grace" (ἀντί, "in place of, instead of"), often understood as one gift of grace succeeding another.
- **1:18** — The Saints Greek Critical Text reads μονογενὴς θεός ("only-begotten God" or "the unique God"), rather than the later variant "only-begotten Son." εἰς τὸν κόλπον literally means "into the bosom/embrace," an idiom for closest intimacy, rendered "at the Father's side." ἐξηγήσατο ("explained, narrated, made known") has no explicit object in Greek; "Him" is supplied for English clarity.
- **1:21** — John frequently uses the historical present (λέγει, "he says"; βλέπει, "he sees"; εὑρίσκει, "he finds") throughout 1:21–51. It is retained here to preserve the vividness of the Greek narrative.
- **1:51** — Ἀμὴν ἀμήν (literally "Amen, amen") is rendered "Truly, truly." The "you" in "I tell you" and "you will see" is plural (ὑμῖν, ὄψεσθε), addressing all the disciples present.
