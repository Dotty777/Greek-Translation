## Text

**17:1** Jesus spoke these things, and lifting up His eyes to heaven, He said, "Father, the hour has come; glorify Your Son, so that the Son may glorify You,

**17:2** just as You gave Him authority over all flesh, so that for all that You have given Him, He may give them eternal life.

**17:3** Now this is eternal life: that they may know You, the only true God, and the One whom You sent, Jesus Christ.

**17:4** I glorified You on the earth, having completed the work that You have given Me to do;

**17:5** and now You, Father, glorify Me in Your own presence with the glory that I had with You before the world existed.

**17:6** I revealed Your name to the people whom You gave Me out of the world. They were Yours, and to Me You gave them, and they have kept Your word.

**17:7** Now they have come to know that all the things You have given Me are from You;

**17:8** because the words that You gave Me I have given to them, and they received them and truly knew that I came out from You, and they believed that You sent Me.

**17:9** I am asking on their behalf; I am not asking on behalf of the world, but on behalf of those whom You have given Me, because they are Yours,

**17:10** and all things that are Mine are Yours, and what is Yours is Mine, and I have been glorified in them.

**17:11** And I am no longer in the world, and they are in the world, and I am coming to You. Holy Father, keep them in Your name that You have given Me, so that they may be one, just as We are.

**17:12** While I was with them, I was keeping them in Your name that You have given Me, and I guarded them, and not one of them perished except the son of destruction, so that the Scripture might be fulfilled.

**17:13** But now I am coming to You, and I speak these things in the world so that they may have My joy made full in themselves.

**17:14** I have given Your word to them, and the world hated them, because they are not from the world, just as I am not from the world.

**17:15** I am not asking that You take them out of the world, but that You keep them from the evil one.

**17:16** They are not from the world, just as I am not from the world.

**17:17** Consecrate them in the truth; Your word is truth.

**17:18** Just as You sent Me into the world, I also sent them into the world;

**17:19** and on their behalf I consecrate Myself, so that they also may be consecrated in truth.

**17:20** Now I am not asking on behalf of these alone, but also on behalf of those who believe in Me through their word,

**17:21** so that all of them may be one, just as You, Father, are in Me and I in You, so that they also may be in Us, so that the world may believe that You sent Me.

**17:22** And the glory that You have given Me, I have given to them, so that they may be one, just as We are one,

**17:23** I in them and You in Me, so that they may be brought to completion into one, so that the world may know that You sent Me and loved them just as You loved Me.

**17:24** Father, what You have given Me—I desire that where I am, they also may be with Me, so that they may see My glory that You have given Me, because You loved Me before the foundation of the world.

**17:25** Righteous Father, although the world did not know You, I knew You, and these knew that You sent Me,

**17:26** and I made Your name known to them and will make it known, so that the love with which You loved Me may be in them, and I in them."

## Translator Notes

- **17:2** — The Greek uses a neuter singular collective ("all that You have given Him," similarly in 17:24) resumed by a plural pronoun ("them"), presenting believers both as a unified gift and as individual recipients of eternal life.
- **17:11** — The critical Greek text reads the singular relative pronoun referring back to "Your name" ("Your name that You have given Me," as also in 17:12), whereas some later manuscripts read a plural pronoun referring to the disciples.
- **17:15** — The Greek phrase "from the evil one" is grammatically ambiguous between masculine ("the evil one") and neuter ("evil"), though Johannine usage elsewhere favors the personal adversary.
- **17:17** — The Greek verb rendered "consecrate" (and "consecrated" in 17:19) means to set apart as holy for God's purpose, sharing the same root as "Holy Father" in 17:11.
