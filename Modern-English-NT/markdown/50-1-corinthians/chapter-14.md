## Text

**14:1** Pursue love, and eagerly desire spiritual things, but especially that you may prophesy.

**14:2** For the one who speaks in a tongue does not speak to people, but to God, for no one understands; instead, in spirit he speaks mysteries.

**14:3** But the one who prophesies speaks to people building up, encouragement, and consolation.

**14:4** The one who speaks in a tongue builds himself up, but the one who prophesies builds up the church.

**14:5** Now I wish for all of you to speak in tongues, but even more that you may prophesy; and the one who prophesies is greater than the one who speaks in tongues, unless he interprets, so that the church may receive building up.

**14:6** But now, brothers, if I come to you speaking in tongues, how will I benefit you, unless I speak to you either in revelation or in knowledge or in prophecy or in teaching?

**14:7** Even lifeless things that give a sound, whether flute or harp, if they do not give a distinction in the tones, how will what is played on the flute or what is played on the harp be known?

**14:8** For indeed, if a trumpet gives an unclear sound, who will prepare himself for battle?

**14:9** So also you, unless with the tongue you give intelligible speech, how will what is spoken be known? For you will be speaking into the air.

**14:10** There are, perhaps, so many kinds of languages in the world, and none is without meaning;

**14:11** if then I do not know the meaning of the language, I will be a foreigner to the one who speaks, and the one who speaks will be a foreigner to me.

**14:12** So also you, since you are zealous for spirits, seek to abound for the building up of the church.

**14:13** Therefore, let the one who speaks in a tongue pray that he may interpret.

**14:14** For if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prays, but my mind is unfruitful.

**14:15** What is it then? I will pray with the spirit, and I will pray with the mind also; I will sing praise with the spirit, and I will sing praise with the mind also.

**14:16** Otherwise, if you bless in spirit, how will the one who fills the place of the uninstructed person say the "Amen" at your thanksgiving, since he does not know what you are saying?

**14:17** For you indeed give thanks well, but the other person is not built up.

**14:18** I give thanks to God, I speak in tongues more than all of you;

**14:19** yet in church I would rather speak five words with my mind, so that I may instruct others also, than ten thousand words in a tongue.

**14:20** Brothers, do not become children in your thinking; instead, be infants in evil, and in your thinking become mature.

**14:21** In the law it is written, "By people of other tongues and by the lips of strangers I will speak to this people, and not even so will they listen to Me," says the Lord.

**14:22** So then, tongues are for a sign, not for those who believe but for unbelievers; while prophecy is not for unbelievers but for those who believe.

**14:23** Therefore, if the whole church comes together in one place and all speak in tongues, and uninstructed people or unbelievers come in, will they not say that you are out of your minds?

**14:24** But if all prophesy, and an unbeliever or uninstructed person comes in, he is convicted by all, he is examined by all,

**14:25** the secrets of his heart become manifest, and so, falling on his face, he will worship God, declaring, "Truly God is among you."

**14:26** What is it then, brothers? Whenever you come together, each one has a psalm, has a teaching, has a revelation, has a tongue, has an interpretation. Let all things be done for building up.

**14:27** If anyone speaks in a tongue, let it be two, or at most three, and each in turn, and let one interpret;

**14:28** but if there is no interpreter, let him keep silent in church, and let him speak to himself and to God.

**14:29** And let two or three prophets speak, and let the others discern;

**14:30** but if something is revealed to another who is seated, let the first keep silent.

**14:31** For you can all prophesy one by one, so that all may learn and all may be encouraged

**14:32** (and the spirits of prophets are subject to prophets,

**14:33** for God is not a God of disorder but of peace), as in all the churches of the saints.

**14:34** Let the women keep silent in the churches, for it is not permitted for them to speak; rather, let them be in subjection, just as the law also says.

**14:35** And if they desire to learn anything, let them ask their own husbands at home; for it is disgraceful for a woman to speak in church.

**14:36** Or did the word of God go out from you, or did it reach you alone?

**14:37** If anyone thinks he is a prophet or spiritual, let him recognize that the things I am writing to you are a command of the Lord.

**14:38** But if anyone is ignorant, let him remain ignorant.

**14:39** So then, my brothers, eagerly desire to prophesy, and do not forbid speaking in tongues;

**14:40** but let all things be done properly and in an orderly manner.

## Translator Notes

- **14:2** — Greek anarthrous πνεύματι may refer either to the speaker's human spirit ("in spirit," cf. 14:14) or to the Holy Spirit ("by the Spirit").
- **14:12** — Greek πνευμάτων literally means 'spirits,' referring in this context to spiritual gifts or manifestations.
- **14:33** — The punctuation places verses 32-33a in parentheses, connecting 'as in all the churches of the saints' to verse 31; many editions and interpreters instead attach that clause to the beginning of verse 34.
- **14:34** — Some Western manuscripts place verses 34-35 after verse 40 rather than here, a variation widely discussed in textual criticism.
- **14:37** — The Greek text reads singular ἐντολή ('a command') in the predicate clause κυρίου ἐστίν ἐντολή ('are a command of the Lord'); other manuscripts read plural ἐντολαί ('commands') or omit the noun.
- **14:38** — The Greek text reads the active imperative ἀγνοείτω ('let him remain ignorant'); other manuscripts read the passive indicative ἀγνοεῖται ('he is not recognized').
