## Text

**9:1** Am I not free? Am I not an apostle? Have I not seen Jesus our Lord? You are my work in the Lord, are you not?

**9:2** If to others I am not an apostle, yet at least to you I am; for you are the seal of my apostleship in the Lord.

**9:3** My defense to those who examine me is this.

**9:4** Do we not have the right to eat and to drink?

**9:5** Do we not have the right to take along a sister as a wife, just as the rest of the apostles and the brothers of the Lord and Cephas also do?

**9:6** Or is it only Barnabas and I who do not have the right to refrain from working?

**9:7** Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat its fruit? Or who shepherds a flock and does not eat from the milk of the flock?

**9:8** Am I speaking these things in human terms, or does the law not say these things also?

**9:9** For in the law of Moses it is written, "You shall not muzzle a threshing ox." Is God concerned about oxen,

**9:10** or does He speak altogether for our sake? For it was written for our sake, because the one who plows ought to plow in hope, and the one who threshes, in hope of sharing.

**9:11** If we sowed spiritual things for you, is it a great thing if we reap material things from you?

**9:12** If others share in this right over you, do we not all the more? Yet we did not use this right, but we endure all things so that we may cause no hindrance to the gospel of Christ.

**9:13** Do you not know that those who perform sacred duties eat the things from the temple, and those who attend at the altar share with the altar?

**9:14** In the same way, the Lord also directed those who proclaim the gospel to live from the gospel.

**9:15** But I have made use of none of these things. And I did not write these things so that it might be done this way for me; for it would be better for me to die than—no one will make my boast empty.

**9:16** For if I proclaim the gospel, I have no ground for boasting, for necessity is laid upon me; for woe to me if I do not proclaim the gospel.

**9:17** For if I do this willingly, I have a reward; but if unwillingly, I have been entrusted with a stewardship.

**9:18** What then is my reward? That in proclaiming the gospel I may present the gospel free of charge, so as not to make full use of my right in the gospel.

**9:19** For though I am free from all, I enslaved myself to all, so that I might gain the more.

**9:20** And to the Jews I became as a Jew, so that I might gain Jews; to those under the law, as one under the law—though not myself under the law—so that I might gain those under the law;

**9:21** to those without the law, as one without the law—not being without the law of God, but within the law of Christ—so that I might gain those without the law.

**9:22** To the weak I became weak, so that I might gain the weak; I have become all things to all people, so that by every means I might save some.

**9:23** And I do all things for the sake of the gospel, so that I may become a fellow sharer in it.

**9:24** Do you not know that those who run in a stadium all run, yet one receives the prize? Run in such a way that you may obtain it.

**9:25** And everyone who competes exercises self-control in all things; they do so to receive a perishable wreath, but we an imperishable one.

**9:26** Therefore I run in this way, not aimlessly; I box in this way, not as one beating the air;

**9:27** but I bruise my body and bring it into slavery, so that after proclaiming to others, I myself may not somehow become disqualified.

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — Literally 'a sister, a wife,' referring to a Christian wife accompanying an apostle in his travels.
- **9:15** — The Greek sentence breaks off abruptly after 'than' (anacoluthon), reflecting Paul's emphatic shift to declare that no one will make his boast empty.
- **9:21** — Paul plays on the Greek terms anomos ('without the law') and ennomos ('within the law') to clarify that living apart from the Mosaic law does not mean being lawless toward God, since he lives under Christ's law.
- **9:27** — The verb translated 'bruise' (hypopiazo) literally means 'strike under the eye,' continuing the boxing metaphor from v. 26, and 'proclaiming' (keryxas) can also evoke the herald at the athletic games.
