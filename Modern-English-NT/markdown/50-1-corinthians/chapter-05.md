## Text

**5:1** Sexual immorality is actually being reported among you, and a kind of sexual immorality that does not occur even among the Gentiles: that someone has his father's wife.

**5:2** And you are puffed up, and have you not rather mourned, so that the one who did this deed would be taken away from your midst?

**5:3** For I, absent in body yet present in spirit, have already judged, as though present, the one who has done this in such a way,

**5:4** in the name of our Lord Jesus, when you are assembled together along with my spirit, with the power of our Lord Jesus,

**5:5** to hand such a person over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that the spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord.

**5:6** Your boast is not good. Do you not know that a little leaven leavens the entire batch of dough?

**5:7** Clean out the old leaven, so that you may be a new batch of dough, just as you are unleavened. For indeed our Passover, Christ, has been sacrificed.

**5:8** So then let us observe the festival, not with old leaven nor with the leaven of malice and evil, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

**5:9** I wrote to you in the letter not to associate with sexually immoral people,

**5:10** not at all meaning the sexually immoral of this world, or the greedy and swindlers, or idolaters, for then you would indeed have to go out of the world.

**5:11** But as it is, I am writing to you not to associate with anyone bearing the name of brother if he is sexually immoral, or greedy, or an idolater, or a reviler, or a drunkard, or a swindler—not even to eat with such a one.

**5:12** For what is it to me to judge those outside? Do you not judge those inside,

**5:13** whereas God judges those outside? Drive out the evil person from among yourselves.

## Translator Notes

- **5:4** — "In the name of our Lord Jesus" may grammatically modify Paul's judgment (v. 3), the congregation's gathering (v. 4), or the handing over of the offender (v. 5).
- **5:5** — "Destruction of the flesh" is debated, understood either as severe physical affliction or death, or as the breaking of the person's sinful fleshly impulses so that the spirit may be saved on the day of the Lord.
- **5:13** — Greek krinei is accented as present tense ("judges"), though unaccented manuscripts can also be read as future ("will judge"). The final command echoes Deuteronomy 17:7.
