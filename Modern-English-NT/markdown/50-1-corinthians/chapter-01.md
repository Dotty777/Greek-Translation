## Text

**1:1** Paul, called as an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, and our brother Sosthenes,

**1:2** to the church of God that is in Corinth, to those sanctified in Christ Jesus, called as saints, together with all who call on the name of our Lord Jesus Christ in every place, both theirs and ours:

**1:3** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:4** I give thanks to my God always concerning you for the grace of God that was given to you in Christ Jesus,

**1:5** that in every respect you were enriched in Him, in all speech and in all knowledge,

**1:6** just as the testimony of Christ was confirmed among you,

**1:7** so that you are not lacking in any gift, eagerly awaiting the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**1:8** who will also confirm you to the end, blameless on the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:9** God is faithful, through whom you were called into fellowship with His Son, Jesus Christ our Lord.

**1:10** Now I urge you, brothers, through the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that you all say the same thing and that there be no divisions among you, but that you be fully united in the same mind and in the same judgment.

**1:11** For it has been reported to me concerning you, my brothers, by Chloe's people that there are quarrels among you.

**1:12** Now I mean this, that each one of you says, "I am of Paul," and "I of Apollos," and "I of Cephas," and "I of Christ."

**1:13** Has Christ been divided? Paul was not crucified on your behalf, was he? Or were you baptized into the name of Paul?

**1:14** I give thanks that I baptized none of you except Crispus and Gaius,

**1:15** so that no one may say that you were baptized into my name.

**1:16** Now I also baptized the household of Stephanas; beyond that, I do not know whether I baptized anyone else.

**1:17** For Christ did not send me to baptize, but to proclaim the gospel, not with wisdom of speech, so that the cross of Christ might not be made void.

**1:18** For the word concerning the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, yet to us who are being saved it is God's power.

**1:19** For it is written, "I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and the discernment of the discerning I will set aside."

**1:20** Where is the wise one? Where is the scribe? Where is the debater of this age? Has God not made foolish the wisdom of the world?

**1:21** For since in the wisdom of God the world did not come to know God through wisdom, God was well pleased through the foolishness of the proclamation to save those who believe.

**1:22** For indeed Jews ask for signs and Greeks seek wisdom,

**1:23** but we proclaim Christ crucified, to Jews a stumbling block and to Gentiles foolishness,

**1:24** yet to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God.

**1:25** Because God's foolishness is wiser than people, and God's weakness is stronger than people.

**1:26** For consider your calling, brothers, that not many were wise according to the flesh, not many powerful, not many of noble birth;

**1:27** rather, God chose the foolish things of the world so that He might put the wise to shame, and God chose the weak things of the world so that He might put the strong things to shame,

**1:28** and the lowly things of the world and the despised things God chose, the things that are not, so that He might bring to nothing the things that are,

**1:29** so that no flesh may boast before God.

**1:30** Yet from Him you are in Christ Jesus, who became to us wisdom from God, both righteousness and sanctification and redemption,

**1:31** so that, just as it is written, "Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord."

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — The Greek text places "those sanctified in Christ Jesus" before "that is in Corinth," and "both theirs and ours" at the end of the verse can grammatically modify either "every place" or "our Lord Jesus Christ."
- **1:14** — The critical Greek text reads simply "I give thanks," omitting "to God," which is added in many later manuscripts.
- **1:23** — The critical Greek text reads "Gentiles" (literally "nations") here rather than "Greeks," which appears in some later manuscripts in assimilation to verses 22 and 24.
- **1:30** — The Greek construction links righteousness, sanctification, and redemption closely together as an unfolding of how Christ became wisdom from God for believers.
