## Text

**4:1** In this way let a person consider us: as servants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries.

**4:2** Here, moreover, it is sought in stewards that one be found faithful.

**4:3** To me, however, it is the smallest matter that I should be examined by you or by a human court; in fact, I do not even examine myself.

**4:4** For I am aware of nothing against myself, yet I am not acquitted by this; the One who examines me is the Lord.

**4:5** Therefore do not judge anything before the time, until the Lord comes, who will both illuminate the hidden things of darkness and reveal the counsels of the hearts; and then praise will come to each one from God.

**4:6** Now these things, brothers, I have applied figuratively to myself and Apollos on your account, so that in us you may learn, "Not beyond what is written," so that none of you may be puffed up in favor of one against another.

**4:7** For who makes you distinct? And what do you possess that you did not receive? And if indeed you received it, why do you boast as if you had not received it?

**4:8** Already you are satisfied, already you have become rich, apart from us you have begun to reign! And if only you really had begun to reign, so that we too might reign alongside you.

**4:9** For I suppose God has displayed us apostles as last, like those condemned to death, because we have become a spectacle to the world, both to angels and to people.

**4:10** We are fools on account of Christ, but you are sensible in Christ; we are weak, but you are strong; you are honored, but we are without honor.

**4:11** Up to this very hour we both hunger and thirst, we go poorly clothed, we are struck with fists, we are homeless,

**4:12** and we toil, working with our own hands. When reviled, we bless; when persecuted, we endure;

**4:13** when slandered, we speak kindly. We have become like the refuse of the world, the scrapings of all things up to this moment.

**4:14** I am not writing these things to shame you, but I am admonishing you as my beloved children.

**4:15** For even if you have ten thousand guardians in Christ, yet you do not have many fathers; for in Christ Jesus I fathered you through the gospel.

**4:16** Therefore I urge you: become imitators of me.

**4:17** For this reason I sent Timothy to you, who is my beloved and faithful child in the Lord, who will remind you of my ways in Christ Jesus, just as I teach everywhere in every church.

**4:18** Now some have become puffed up, as though I were not coming to you.

**4:19** Yet I will come to you soon, if the Lord wills, and I will find out not the talk of those who are puffed up, but their power,

**4:20** for the kingdom of God is not in talk, but in power.

**4:21** What do you want? Should I come to you with a rod, or in love and a spirit of gentleness?

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — "Human court" translates Greek anthropines hemeras, literally "a human day," referring to a day of human judicial reckoning in contrast to the coming day of the Lord.
- **4:6** — The phrase "Not beyond what is written" has no verb in the Greek, and its exact reference is debated, most likely pointing back to the Scripture passages cited in chapters 1-3 or to a familiar maxim about keeping within scriptural boundaries.
- **4:15** — Greek paidagogous refers to household guardians who supervised and escorted children rather than formal schoolteachers, contrasted here with a father.
