## Text

**7:1** Now concerning the matters about which you wrote: it is good for a man not to touch a woman.

**7:2** But because of acts of sexual immorality, let each man have his own wife, and let each woman have her own husband.

**7:3** Let the husband fulfill his duty to his wife, and likewise the wife also to her husband.

**7:4** The wife does not exercise authority over her own body, rather the husband does; and in the same way the husband also does not exercise authority over his own body, rather the wife does.

**7:5** Do not deprive one another, except perhaps by agreement for a time, so that you may devote yourselves to prayer and be together again, so that Satan may not tempt you because of your lack of self-control.

**7:6** Now I say this by way of concession, not as a command.

**7:7** Yet I wish that all people were even as I myself am; however, each person has his own gift from God, one in this way, and another in that.

**7:8** Now to the unmarried and to the widows I say: it is good for them if they remain even as I am;

**7:9** but if they do not exercise self-control, let them marry; for it is better to marry than to burn.

**7:10** Now to the married I command, not I, but the Lord, that a wife is not to separate from her husband—

**7:11** yet if she does separate, let her remain unmarried or be reconciled to her husband—and that a husband is not to divorce his wife.

**7:12** But to the rest I say, not the Lord: if any brother has an unbelieving wife, and she consents to live with him, let him not divorce her.

**7:13** And a woman who has an unbelieving husband, and he consents to live with her, let her not divorce her husband.

**7:14** For the unbelieving husband has been sanctified through his wife, and the unbelieving wife has been sanctified through the brother; otherwise your children would be unclean, but now they are holy.

**7:15** Yet if the unbeliever separates, let him separate. The brother or the sister is not bound in such cases; rather, God has called us to peace.

**7:16** For how do you know, wife, whether you will save your husband? Or how do you know, husband, whether you will save your wife?

**7:17** Only, as the Lord has assigned to each one, as God has called each one, in that manner let him walk. And so I direct in all the churches.

**7:18** Was anyone called while circumcised? Let him not undo his circumcision. Has anyone been called in uncircumcision? Let him not be circumcised.

**7:19** Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but rather the keeping of God's commandments.

**7:20** Let each person remain in the calling in which he was called.

**7:21** Were you called as a slave? Do not let it concern you; yet if you are even able to become free, rather make use of it.

**7:22** For the one who was called in the Lord as a slave is the Lord's freedman; likewise the one who was called as a free person is a slave of Christ.

**7:23** You were bought with a price; do not become slaves of human beings.

**7:24** Brothers, in whatever situation each one was called, let him remain in that with God.

**7:25** Now concerning virgins, I have no command from the Lord, yet I give my judgment as one who has been shown mercy by the Lord to be trustworthy.

**7:26** Therefore I consider this to be good because of the present distress: that it is good for a person to be as he is.

**7:27** Are you bound to a wife? Do not seek release. Have you been released from a wife? Do not seek a wife.

**7:28** Yet even if you do marry, you have not sinned; and if a virgin marries, she has not sinned. However, such people will have affliction in the flesh, and I am seeking to spare you.

**7:29** Now this I say, brothers: the time has been shortened. From now on, let even those who have wives be as though they had none,

**7:30** and those who weep as though not weeping, and those who rejoice as though not rejoicing, and those who buy as though not possessing,

**7:31** and those who use the world as though not making full use of it; for the form of this world is passing away.

**7:32** Now I want you to be free from concern. The unmarried man is concerned about the things of the Lord, how he may please the Lord;

**7:33** but the married man is concerned about the things of the world, how he may please his wife, and he is divided. And the unmarried woman, as well as the virgin

**7:34** who is unmarried, is concerned about the things of the Lord, so that she may be holy both in body and in spirit; but the married woman is concerned about the things of the world, how she may please her husband.

**7:35** Now I say this for your own benefit, not to put a restraint on you, but for what is proper and for devoted attendance to the Lord without distraction.

**7:36** But if anyone thinks that he is acting improperly toward his virgin if she is past her prime, and so it ought to be, let him do what he wishes; he does not sin; let them marry.

**7:37** But the one who stands firm in his heart, having no necessity, and has authority over his own will, and has decided this in his own heart, to keep his own virgin, will do well.

**7:38** So then, both the one who gives his own virgin in marriage does well, and the one who does not give her in marriage will do better.

**7:39** A wife is bound for as long as her husband lives; but if her husband falls asleep, she is free to be married to whomever she wishes, only in the Lord.

**7:40** Yet she is more blessed if she remains as she is, according to my judgment; and I think that I also have the Spirit of God.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The clause "it is good for a man not to touch a woman" is widely understood as a slogan quoted from the Corinthians' letter, with "touch" used as a euphemism for sexual relations.
- **7:21** — The Greek imperative translated "make use of it" has no explicit object; interpreters debate whether Paul urges slaves to take the opportunity to become free or to make use of their present condition.
- **7:33** — Manuscript wording and punctuation vary here; the Greek source concludes the sentence about the married man with "and he is divided" and continues with "the unmarried woman" and "the virgin who is unmarried" across vv. 33-34.
- **7:36** — Interpreters debate whether "his virgin" refers to a father's unmarried daughter or a man's betrothed fiancée, and whether the Greek term translated "past her prime" refers to her age or to his strong passions.
