## Text

**8:1** Now concerning things sacrificed to idols, we know that we all have knowledge. Knowledge inflates, but love builds up.

**8:2** If anyone supposes that he has come to know anything, he has not yet known as he ought to know;

**8:3** but if anyone loves God, this person is known by Him.

**8:4** Therefore, concerning the eating of things sacrificed to idols, we know that an idol is nothing in the world, and that there is no God except one.

**8:5** For even if indeed there are so-called gods, whether in heaven or on earth—just as there are many gods and many lords—

**8:6** yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things and we are for Him, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things and we are through Him.

**8:7** However, this knowledge is not in everyone; rather, some, through being accustomed to the idol until now, eat it as something sacrificed to an idol, and their conscience, being weak, is defiled.

**8:8** Now food will not commend us to God; neither do we have an advantage if we eat, nor are we lacking if we do not eat.

**8:9** But take care that this right of yours does not somehow become a stumbling block to the weak.

**8:10** For if someone sees you, who have knowledge, reclining at table in an idol's temple, will not his conscience, since he is weak, be built up to eat things sacrificed to idols?

**8:11** For through your knowledge the one who is weak is destroyed, the brother for whom Christ died.

**8:12** And thus, by sinning against the brothers and wounding their conscience when it is weak, you sin against Christ.

**8:13** Therefore, if food causes my brother to stumble, I will certainly never eat meat, so that I will not cause my brother to stumble.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — The assertion "we all have knowledge" (along with the claims in v. 4) is widely regarded as a Corinthian slogan that Paul quotes and then qualifies.
- **8:8** — The Greek source places the clause regarding eating before the clause regarding not eating, the reverse of the order found in some other manuscripts.
- **8:10** — The verb "be built up" ironically echoes "builds up" in v. 1, indicating that a weak believer's conscience could be emboldened to act contrary to its own convictions.
