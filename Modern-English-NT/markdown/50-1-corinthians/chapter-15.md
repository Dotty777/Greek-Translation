## Text

**15:1** Now I make known to you, brothers, the gospel that I preached to you, which you also received, in which you also stand,

**15:2** and through which you are also being saved, if you hold firmly to the word by which I preached to you—unless you believed in vain.

**15:3** For I handed down to you as of first importance what I also received: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures,

**15:4** and that He was buried, and that He has been raised on the third day according to the Scriptures,

**15:5** and that He appeared to Cephas, then to the Twelve.

**15:6** After that He appeared to more than five hundred brothers at a single time, most of whom remain until now, though some have fallen asleep.

**15:7** After that He appeared to James, then to all the apostles.

**15:8** And last of all, as to one born prematurely, He appeared also to me.

**15:9** For I am the least of the apostles, not fit to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the church of God.

**15:10** But by God's grace I am what I am, and His grace toward me did not turn out empty; instead, I labored more abundantly than all of them—yet not I, but the grace of God with me.

**15:11** Whether then it is I or they, this is how we proclaim, and this is how you believed.

**15:12** Now if Christ is proclaimed that He has been raised from the dead, how do some among you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?

**15:13** But if there is no resurrection of the dead, then neither has Christ been raised;

**15:14** and if Christ has not been raised, then our proclamation is empty, and your faith is also empty.

**15:15** Moreover, we are even found to be false witnesses of God, because we testified against God that He raised Christ, whom He did not raise if indeed the dead are not raised.

**15:16** For if the dead are not raised, then neither has Christ been raised;

**15:17** and if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins.

**15:18** Then those also who fell asleep in Christ have perished.

**15:19** If we have hoped in Christ in this life only, we are more to be pitied than all people.

**15:20** Now, however, Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep.

**15:21** For since death came through a man, through a man also comes the resurrection of the dead.

**15:22** For just as all die in Adam, so also in Christ all will be made alive.

**15:23** But each in his own order: Christ the firstfruits, then those who belong to Christ at His coming.

**15:24** Then comes the end, when He hands over the kingdom to God the Father, once He has abolished every rule and every authority and power.

**15:25** For He must reign until He places all enemies under His feet.

**15:26** The last enemy to be abolished is death;

**15:27** for He has subjected all things under His feet. But when it says, "All things have been subjected," it is clear that this excludes the One who subjected all things to Him.

**15:28** And when all things have been subjected to Him, then the Son Himself will also be subjected to the One who subjected all things to Him, so that God may be all in all.

**15:29** Otherwise, what will those do who are baptized on behalf of the dead? If the dead are not raised at all, why are they even baptized on behalf of them?

**15:30** Why are we also in danger every hour?

**15:31** I die every day, by the boasting about you, brothers, which I have in Christ Jesus our Lord!

**15:32** If in human terms I fought wild beasts in Ephesus, what is the benefit to me? If the dead are not raised, "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die."

**15:33** Do not be deceived: bad associations corrupt good character.

**15:34** Sober up properly and stop sinning, for some have no knowledge of God; I speak this to your shame.

**15:35** But someone will say, "How are the dead raised? And with what kind of body do they come?"

**15:36** Foolish one! What you yourself sow is not made alive unless it dies.

**15:37** And what you sow—you do not sow the body that will come into being, but a bare grain, perhaps of wheat or of some other kind.

**15:38** But God gives it a body just as He willed, and to each of the seeds its own body.

**15:39** Not all flesh is the same flesh, but there is one kind for humans, another flesh for animals, another flesh for birds, and another for fish.

**15:40** There are also heavenly bodies and earthly bodies; yet the glory of the heavenly is one thing, and the glory of the earthly is another.

**15:41** The sun has one glory, the moon another glory, and the stars another glory; for one star differs from another star in glory.

**15:42** So it is with the resurrection of the dead as well. It is sown in perishability; it is raised in imperishability.

**15:43** It is sown in dishonor; it is raised in glory. It is sown in weakness; it is raised in power.

**15:44** It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body. If there is a natural body, there is also a spiritual one.

**15:45** Thus it is also written: "The first man, Adam, became a living soul"; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit.

**15:46** However, the spiritual does not come first, but the natural, and after that the spiritual.

**15:47** The first man is from the earth, made of dust; the second man is from heaven.

**15:48** Just as the one made of dust is, so also are those made of dust; and just as the heavenly One is, so also are those who are heavenly.

**15:49** And just as we have borne the image of the one made of dust, let us also bear the image of the heavenly One.

**15:50** Now I say this, brothers: flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God, nor does perishability inherit imperishability.

**15:51** Look, I tell you a mystery: we will not all fall asleep, but we will all be changed,

**15:52** in an instant, in the blink of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised imperishable, and we will be changed.

**15:53** For this perishable must clothe itself with imperishability, and this mortal must clothe itself with immortality.

**15:54** And when this perishable clothes itself with imperishability, and this mortal clothes itself with immortality, then the saying that is written will come to pass: "Death has been swallowed up in victory."

**15:55** "Where, death, is your victory? Where, death, is your sting?"

**15:56** Now the sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law;

**15:57** but thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.

**15:58** Therefore, my beloved brothers, be firm, immovable, always overflowing in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labor is not empty in the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **15:8** — The Greek term ektrōmati (with the definite article) literally denotes a miscarriage or an abnormally early birth, underscoring the sudden, irregular, and humbling character of Paul's call as an apostle.
- **15:29** — The phrase "baptized on behalf of the dead" (hyper tōn nekrōn) is famously debated, with proposals ranging from vicarious baptism for deceased persons to baptism undertaken in hope of rejoining dead believers. Paul cites the practice simply to expose the inconsistency of denying the resurrection, without explaining or endorsing it.
- **15:44** — Greek psychikon literally means "soulish" (pertaining to the psychē, natural mortal life), contrasted with pneumatikon ("spiritual"), meaning a resurrection body animated by the Spirit rather than an immaterial body.
- **15:49** — The critical Greek text reads the hortatory subjunctive phoresōmen ("let us also bear"), supported by strong early manuscripts; other manuscripts read the future indicative phoresomen ("we will also bear"), which aligns with the predictive statements in the context.
