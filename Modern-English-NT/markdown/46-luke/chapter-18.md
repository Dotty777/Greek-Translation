## Text

**18:1** Now He was telling a parable to them about how they must always pray and not lose heart,

**18:2** saying, "There was a certain judge in a certain city who did not fear God and did not respect man.

**18:3** And there was a widow in that city, and she kept coming to him, saying, 'Give me justice against my adversary.'

**18:4** And for a time he was unwilling, but afterward he said within himself, 'Even though I do not fear God nor respect man,

**18:5** yet because this widow causes me trouble, I will give her justice, so that by continually coming she will not wear me out.'"

**18:6** And the Lord said, "Listen to what the unrighteous judge says.

**18:7** And will God not certainly bring about justice for His chosen ones who cry out to Him day and night, while He is patient over them?

**18:8** I tell you that He will bring about justice for them quickly. However, when the Son of Man comes, will He indeed find the faith on the earth?"

**18:9** He also told this parable to some who were confident in themselves that they were righteous and treated the rest with contempt:

**18:10** "Two men went up into the temple to pray, one a Pharisee and the other a tax collector.

**18:11** The Pharisee, taking his stand, prayed these things to himself: 'God, I thank You that I am not like the rest of people—extortioners, unjust, adulterers—or even like this tax collector.

**18:12** I fast twice a week; I tithe everything that I acquire.'

**18:13** But the tax collector, standing at a distance, was unwilling even to lift up his eyes toward heaven, but kept beating his chest, saying, 'God, be merciful to me, the sinner!'

**18:14** I tell you, this man went down to his house justified rather than that one; because everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, but the one who humbles himself will be exalted."

**18:15** Now they were bringing even infants to Him so that He might touch them; but when the disciples saw it, they were rebuking them.

**18:16** But Jesus called them to Himself, saying, "Allow the little children to come to Me and do not hinder them, for to such as these belongs the kingdom of God.

**18:17** Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a little child will by no means enter into it."

**18:18** And a certain ruler questioned Him, saying, "Good Teacher, by doing what will I inherit eternal life?"

**18:19** And Jesus said to him, "Why do you call Me good? No one is good except One—God.

**18:20** You know the commandments: 'Do not commit adultery,' 'Do not murder,' 'Do not steal,' 'Do not bear false witness,' 'Honor your father and mother.'"

**18:21** And he said, "All these things I have kept from my youth."

**18:22** When Jesus heard this, He said to him, "Still one thing you lack: sell everything that you possess and distribute it to the poor, and you will have treasure in the heavens; and come, follow Me."

**18:23** But when he heard these things, he became deeply grieved, for he was exceedingly wealthy.

**18:24** And seeing him, Jesus said, "With what difficulty those who have wealth enter into the kingdom of God!

**18:25** For it is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God."

**18:26** And those who heard it said, "Then who is able to be saved?"

**18:27** But He said, "Things impossible with men are possible with God."

**18:28** And Peter said, "Look, we have left everything and followed You."

**18:29** And He said to them, "Truly I tell you that there is no one who has left house or wife or brothers or parents or children for the sake of the kingdom of God,

**18:30** who will not receive many times as much in this time, and in the coming age eternal life."

**18:31** Taking the twelve aside, He said to them, "Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and everything written through the prophets for the Son of Man will be accomplished.

**18:32** For He will be handed over to the Gentiles, and will be mocked and shamefully treated and spit upon;

**18:33** and after flogging Him, they will kill Him, and on the third day He will rise."

**18:34** And they themselves understood none of these things, and this saying was hidden from them, and they did not comprehend the things being spoken.

**18:35** Now it happened that as He was drawing near to Jericho, a certain blind man was sitting beside the road, begging.

**18:36** And hearing a crowd passing by, he began inquiring what this might be.

**18:37** And they reported to him that Jesus the Nazarene was passing by.

**18:38** And he cried out, saying, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!"

**18:39** And those going ahead were rebuking him so that he would be silent, but he kept shouting all the more, "Son of David, have mercy on me!"

**18:40** And Jesus stopped and ordered him to be brought to Him. And when he drew near, He asked him,

**18:41** "What do you want Me to do for you?" And he said, "Lord, that I may regain my sight."

**18:42** And Jesus said to him, "Regain your sight; your faith has saved you."

**18:43** And immediately he regained his sight and began following Him, glorifying God. And all the people, when they saw it, gave praise to God.

## Translator Notes

- **18:5** — The Greek verb hypopiazo literally means "to strike under the eye" and is used figuratively here for wearing someone down or exhausting them; eis telos can mean either "continually" or "finally."
- **18:7** — The clause kai makrothymei ep' autois is syntactically debated: makrothymeo can mean "is patient over them" as an accompanying remark or "delays long over them" as a continuation of the rhetorical question.
- **18:11** — The phrase pros heauton ("to himself") stands directly before proseucheto ("was praying") in this text, suggesting silent or self-directed prayer, though some link it with statheis ("standing by himself").
- **18:42** — The Greek verb sesoken literally means "has saved" (matching 18:26) and in Luke's healing narratives carries the dual sense of physical healing ("made well") and spiritual salvation.
