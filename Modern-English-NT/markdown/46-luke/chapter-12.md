## Text

**12:1** Meanwhile, when tens of thousands of the crowd had gathered together so that they were trampling on one another, He began to say to His disciples first, "Be on your guard against the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy.

**12:2** Yet nothing is covered up that will not be uncovered, and nothing is hidden that will not be known.

**12:3** Therefore, whatever you have said in the darkness will be heard in the light, and what you have spoken into the ear in the inner rooms will be proclaimed on the housetops.

**12:4** Now I say to you, My friends, do not be afraid of those who kill the body and after that have nothing further they can do.

**12:5** But I will show you whom to fear: fear the One who, after killing, has authority to throw into Gehenna; yes, I say to you, fear Him.

**12:6** Are not five sparrows sold for two copper coins? Yet not one of them is forgotten before God.

**12:7** Indeed, even the hairs of your head have all been numbered. Do not be afraid; you are worth more than many sparrows.

**12:8** And I say to you, everyone who confesses Me before people, the Son of Man will also confess him before the angels of God;

**12:9** but the one who denies Me before people will be disowned before the angels of God.

**12:10** And everyone who speaks a word against the Son of Man, it will be forgiven him; but to the one who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven.

**12:11** Now when they bring you before the synagogues and the rulers and the authorities, do not be anxious about how or what you should answer in defense, or what you should say,

**12:12** for the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you must say."

**12:13** Then someone from the crowd said to Him, "Teacher, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me."

**12:14** But He said to him, "Man, who appointed Me a judge or a divider over you?"

**12:15** And He said to them, "Watch out and guard yourselves against every kind of greed, for even when someone has an abundance, his life does not come from his possessions."

**12:16** And He spoke a parable to them, saying, "The land of a certain rich man yielded an abundant harvest.

**12:17** And he reasoned within himself, saying, 'What shall I do, because I have nowhere to gather my crops?'

**12:18** Then he said, 'This is what I will do: I will tear down my barns and build larger ones, and there I will gather all the grain and my goods,

**12:19** and I will say to my soul, "Soul, you have many goods stored up for many years; rest, eat, drink, enjoy yourself."'

**12:20** But God said to him, 'Fool! This very night your soul is demanded from you; and the things you have prepared, whose will they be?'

**12:21** So it is with the one who stores up treasure for himself and is not rich toward God."

**12:22** And He said to His disciples, "For this reason I say to you, do not be anxious about your life, what you will eat, nor about your body, what you will put on.

**12:23** For the life is more than food, and the body is more than clothing.

**12:24** Consider the ravens: they neither sow nor reap, they have neither storeroom nor barn, yet God feeds them. How much more are you worth than the birds!

**12:25** And which of you by being anxious can add a cubit to his lifespan?

**12:26** If then you cannot do even the smallest thing, why are you anxious about the rest?

**12:27** Consider the lilies, how they grow: they neither labor nor spin; yet I say to you, not even Solomon in all his glory was clothed like one of these.

**12:28** Now if God so clothes the grass in the field, which is here today and tomorrow is thrown into an oven, how much more will He clothe you, you of little faith!

**12:29** And as for you, do not seek what you will eat and what you will drink, and do not live in anxious suspense,

**12:30** for all the nations of the world eagerly seek these things, but your Father knows that you need these things.

**12:31** Instead, seek His kingdom, and these things will be added to you.

**12:32** Do not be afraid, little flock, for it has pleased your Father to give you the kingdom.

**12:33** Sell your possessions and give alms; make for yourselves money bags that do not wear out, an inexhaustible treasure in the heavens, where no thief approaches and no moth destroys.

**12:34** For where your treasure is, there your heart will also be.

**12:35** Let your waists be belted and your lamps burning,

**12:36** and be like people waiting for their master to return from the wedding banquet, so that when he comes and knocks, they may immediately open to him.

**12:37** Blessed are those slaves whom the master will find staying awake when he comes. Truly I say to you that he will fasten his belt, have them recline at table, and come forward and serve them.

**12:38** Whether he comes in the second watch or in the third watch and finds them so, blessed are they.

**12:39** Now know this: if the homeowner had known at what hour the thief was coming, he would not have allowed his house to be broken into.

**12:40** You also must be ready, because the Son of Man is coming at an hour you do not expect."

**12:41** Then Peter said, "Lord, are You speaking this parable to us, or also to everyone?"

**12:42** And the Lord said, "Who then is the faithful, sensible steward whom the master will put in charge of his household staff, to give them their ration of food at the proper time?

**12:43** Blessed is that slave whom his master will find doing so when he comes.

**12:44** In truth I say to you that he will put him in charge of all his possessions.

**12:45** But if that slave says in his heart, 'My master is delayed in coming,' and begins to beat the male servants and the female servants, and to eat and drink and get drunk,

**12:46** the master of that slave will come on a day he does not expect and at an hour he does not know, and will cut him in two and assign his portion with the unfaithful.

**12:47** And that slave who knew his master's will and did not get ready or act according to his will will be beaten with many blows;

**12:48** but the one who did not know, yet did things deserving blows, will be beaten with few. From everyone to whom much was given, much will be required; and from the one to whom much was entrusted, even more will be asked.

**12:49** I came to cast fire on the earth, and how I wish it were already kindled!

**12:50** But I have a baptism to be baptized with, and how distressed I am until it is accomplished!

**12:51** Do you suppose that I came to give peace on earth? No, I say to you, but rather division.

**12:52** For from now on five in one house will be divided, three against two and two against three;

**12:53** they will be divided, father against son and son against father, mother against daughter and daughter against mother, mother-in-law against her daughter-in-law and daughter-in-law against mother-in-law."

**12:54** And He was also saying to the crowds, "When you see a cloud rising in the west, immediately you say, 'A shower is coming,' and so it happens.

**12:55** And when a south wind is blowing, you say, 'There will be scorching heat,' and it happens.

**12:56** Hypocrites! You know how to discern the appearance of the earth and the sky, yet how is it that you do not know how to discern this present time?

**12:57** And why do you not even judge for yourselves what is right?

**12:58** For as you are going with your opponent before a magistrate, make an effort on the way to settle with him, so that he does not drag you to the judge, and the judge hand you over to the officer, and the officer throw you into prison.

**12:59** I say to you, you will certainly not come out from there until you have paid even the very last coin."

## Translator Notes

- **12:5** — Greek geenna (Gehenna), originally the Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem, used for the place of final punishment.
- **12:20** — Literally "they are demanding," an impersonal third-person plural idiom functioning as a passive.
- **12:25** — Greek pechus (a cubit, about 18 inches) is used figuratively for a small increment, and helikia can mean either "lifespan" or "stature."
- **12:29** — Greek meteorizomai, occurring only here in the New Testament, literally refers to being lifted in mid-air, hence unsettled or held in anxious suspense.
- **12:46** — Greek dichotomeo literally means "cut in two," though some interpreters understand it figuratively as severe punishment or expulsion.
- **12:49** — Literally "what do I wish if it has already been kindled?", a Semitic idiom expressing intense desire.
- **12:59** — Greek lepton, the smallest bronze coin circulating in Judea, worth one-eighth of an assarion (compare 12:6).
