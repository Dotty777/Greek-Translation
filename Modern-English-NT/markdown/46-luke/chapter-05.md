## Text

**5:1** Now it happened, as the crowd was pressing upon Him and hearing the word of God, that He was standing beside the lake of Gennesaret,

**5:2** and He saw two boats lying beside the lake, but the fishermen had stepped out of them and were washing the nets.

**5:3** Stepping into one of the boats, which belonged to Simon, He asked him to put out a little from the land. And sitting down, He was teaching the crowds from the boat.

**5:4** When He ceased speaking, He said to Simon, "Put out into the deep water and let down your nets for a catch."

**5:5** And Simon answered and said, "Master, we labored through the entire night and took nothing; yet at Your word I will let down the nets."

**5:6** And when they had done this, they enclosed a great multitude of fish, and their nets were beginning to tear.

**5:7** So they signaled to their partners in the other boat to come and help them. And they came and filled both boats, so that they were sinking.

**5:8** But when Simon Peter saw this, he fell down at the knees of Jesus, saying, "Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, Lord."

**5:9** For astonishment had gripped him and all those who were with him at the catch of the fish which they had taken,

**5:10** and likewise also James and John, sons of Zebedee, who were associates of Simon. And Jesus said to Simon, "Do not be afraid; from now on you will be catching people alive."

**5:11** And having brought the boats to land, they left everything and followed Him.

**5:12** And it happened while He was in one of the cities, look, there was a man full of leprosy. When he saw Jesus, he fell on his face and implored Him, saying, "Lord, if You are willing, You are able to cleanse me."

**5:13** And stretching out His hand, He touched him, saying, "I am willing; be cleansed." And immediately the leprosy departed from him.

**5:14** And He ordered him to tell no one, "but go and show yourself to the priest, and make an offering for your cleansing just as Moses commanded, as a testimony to them."

**5:15** Yet the report about Him was spreading even more, and large crowds were gathering together to hear and to be healed of their illnesses.

**5:16** But He Himself would withdraw to the desolate places and pray.

**5:17** And it happened on one of the days that He was teaching, and Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there, who had come from every village of Galilee and Judea and from Jerusalem; and the power of the Lord was present for Him to heal.

**5:18** And look, men were carrying on a bed a man who was paralyzed, and they were seeking to bring him in and lay him before Him.

**5:19** And not finding a way to bring him in because of the crowd, they went up onto the roof and lowered him with the cot through the tiles into the middle, in front of Jesus.

**5:20** And seeing their faith, He said, "Man, your sins have been forgiven you."

**5:21** And the scribes and the Pharisees began to reason, saying, "Who is this who speaks blasphemies? Who is able to forgive sins except God alone?"

**5:22** But Jesus, knowing their reasonings, answered and said to them, "Why are you reasoning in your hearts?

**5:23** Which is easier, to say, 'Your sins have been forgiven you,' or to say, 'Get up and walk'?

**5:24** But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on the earth to forgive sins"—He said to the paralyzed man—"I say to you, get up, pick up your cot, and go to your house."

**5:25** And at once rising up before them, he picked up what he had been lying on and went away to his house, glorifying God.

**5:26** And amazement seized everyone, and they were glorifying God; and they were filled with fear, saying, "We have seen extraordinary things today."

**5:27** And after these things He went out and noticed a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax booth, and He said to him, "Follow Me."

**5:28** And leaving everything behind, he got up and began following Him.

**5:29** And Levi gave a great banquet for Him in his house, and there was a large crowd of tax collectors and others who were reclining at table with them.

**5:30** And the Pharisees and their scribes were grumbling at His disciples, saying, "Why do you eat and drink with the tax collectors and sinners?"

**5:31** And Jesus answered and said to them, "Those who are healthy have no need of a physician, but those who are ill;

**5:32** I have not come to call righteous people, but sinners, to repentance."

**5:33** And they said to Him, "The disciples of John fast frequently and offer prayers, and likewise those of the Pharisees, but Yours eat and drink."

**5:34** And Jesus said to them, "You cannot make the sons of the bridal chamber fast while the bridegroom is with them, can you?

**5:35** Yet days will come, and when the bridegroom is taken away from them, then they will fast in those days."

**5:36** And He was also telling a parable to them: "No one tears a patch from a new garment and puts it on an old garment; otherwise, he will both tear the new one, and the patch from the new will not match the old.

**5:37** And no one puts new wine into old wineskins; otherwise, the new wine will burst the wineskins, and it will be spilled out, and the wineskins will be ruined.

**5:38** Rather, new wine must be put into fresh wineskins.

**5:39** And no one, after drinking old wine, desires new; for he says, 'The old is good.'"

## Translator Notes

- **5:10** — Greek zōgrōn literally means "capturing alive," contrasting with the fishermen's ordinary catch of fish for death.
- **5:17** — The Greek text reads singular auton ("for Him to heal," referring to Jesus), whereas some manuscripts read plural autous ("to heal them").
- **5:34** — "The sons of the bridal chamber" is a literal Semitic idiom referring to the wedding guests or attendants of the bridegroom.
- **5:38** — Greek blēteon ("must be put") is the only verbal adjective of obligation in the New Testament; the verse also distinguishes neos ("new" in age, for the wine) from kainos ("fresh" or unused, for the wineskins).
- **5:39** — The critical Greek text reads the positive adjective chrēstos ("good"), while some later manuscripts read the comparative chrēstoteros ("better").
