## Text

**14:1** And it happened, when He went into the house of one of the leaders of the Pharisees on a Sabbath to eat bread, that they were watching Him closely.

**14:2** And look, a certain man suffering from dropsy was in front of Him.

**14:3** And answering, Jesus spoke to the experts in the law and the Pharisees, saying, "Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath, or not?"

**14:4** But they remained silent. And taking hold of him, He healed him and sent him away.

**14:5** And He said to them, "Which of you will have a son or an ox fall into a well, and will not immediately pull him out on a Sabbath day?"

**14:6** And they were not able to reply to these things.

**14:7** Now He began telling a parable to those who had been invited, noticing how they were choosing the places of honor, saying to them,

**14:8** "When you are invited by someone to a wedding feast, do not recline in the place of honor, in case someone more distinguished than you has been invited by him,

**14:9** and the one who invited both you and him will come and say to you, 'Give place to this man,' and then with shame you will begin to take the lowest place.

**14:10** Rather, when you are invited, go and recline in the lowest place, so that when the one who has invited you comes, he will say to you, 'Friend, move up higher.' Then you will have honor in the presence of all who recline at table with you.

**14:11** For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted."

**14:12** And He was also saying to the one who had invited Him, "When you give a lunch or a dinner, do not invite your friends or your brothers or your relatives or rich neighbors, in case they also invite you in return and repayment comes to you.

**14:13** Instead, when you give a feast, invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, the blind;

**14:14** and you will be blessed, because they cannot repay you; for you will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous."

**14:15** When one of those reclining at table heard these things, he said to Him, "Blessed is whoever will eat bread in the kingdom of God!"

**14:16** But He said to him, "A certain man was giving a great banquet, and he invited many;

**14:17** and at the hour of the banquet he sent his slave to say to those who had been invited, 'Come, because everything is ready now.'

**14:18** And they all alike began to make excuses. The first said to him, 'I have bought a field, and I must go out and see it; I ask you, consider me excused.'

**14:19** And another said, 'I have bought five yoke of oxen, and I am on my way to try them out; I ask you, consider me excused.'

**14:20** And another said, 'I have married a wife, and for that reason I cannot come.'

**14:21** And the slave came back and reported these things to his master. Then the master of the house became angry and said to his slave, 'Go out quickly into the streets and alleys of the city, and bring in here the poor and crippled and blind and lame.'

**14:22** And the slave said, 'Master, what you commanded has been done, and still there is room.'

**14:23** And the master said to the slave, 'Go out to the roads and hedges and compel people to come in, so that my house may be filled;

**14:24** for I tell you that none of those men who were invited will taste of my banquet.'"

**14:25** Now large crowds were traveling along with Him, and turning around, He said to them,

**14:26** "If anyone comes to Me and does not hate his own father and mother and wife and children and brothers and sisters, yes, and even his own life, he cannot be My disciple.

**14:27** Whoever does not carry his cross and come after Me cannot be My disciple.

**14:28** For which of you, wanting to build a tower, does not first sit down and calculate the cost, to see whether he has enough for its completion?

**14:29** Otherwise, after he has laid a foundation and is unable to finish, all who look on may begin to mock him,

**14:30** saying, 'This man began to build and was not able to finish.'

**14:31** Or what king, going out to engage another king in war, will not first sit down and take counsel whether he is able with ten thousand to meet the one coming against him with twenty thousand?

**14:32** And if not, while the other is still far away, he sends a delegation and asks for terms of peace.

**14:33** In the same way, therefore, every one of you who does not renounce all his own possessions cannot be My disciple.

**14:34** Salt, therefore, is good; but if even salt becomes tasteless, with what will it be seasoned?

**14:35** It is fit neither for the soil nor for the manure pile; they throw it out. The one who has ears to hear, let him hear."

## Translator Notes

- **14:2** — "Dropsy" translates Greek hydropikos, a condition characterized by abnormal fluid retention and swelling in the body (edema).
- **14:5** — The Greek text reads "a son or an ox" (huios e bous), strongly attested in early witnesses; other manuscripts read "a donkey or an ox" (onos e bous).
- **14:18** — "All alike" renders the elliptical Greek idiom apo mias (literally "from one [mind/voice]"), meaning unanimously or one and all.
- **14:24** — "You" is plural (hymin), shifting from the single slave addressed in v. 23 so that the host's final declaration also speaks directly to Jesus' listeners.
- **14:26** — In Semitic idiom, "hate" (miseo) is used hyperbolically for loving less or subordinating all other allegiances to devotion to Jesus (cf. Matthew 10:37).
