## Text

**4:1** Now Jesus, full of the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan and was being led by the Spirit in the wilderness

**4:2** for forty days, being tempted by the devil. And He ate nothing in those days, and when they were completed, He became hungry.

**4:3** Then the devil said to Him, "If You are the Son of God, speak to this stone that it may become bread."

**4:4** And Jesus replied to him, "It is written, 'Man will not live on bread alone.'"

**4:5** And leading Him up, he showed Him all the kingdoms of the inhabited world in an instant of time.

**4:6** And the devil said to Him, "To You I will give all this authority and their glory, because it has been handed over to me, and I give it to whomever I wish.

**4:7** Therefore, if You worship before me, it will all be Yours."

**4:8** And answering, Jesus said to him, "It is written, 'You will worship the Lord your God, and Him alone you will serve.'"

**4:9** Then he led Him to Jerusalem and set Him on the pinnacle of the temple, and said to Him, "If You are the Son of God, throw Yourself down from here;

**4:10** for it is written, 'He will command His angels concerning You to guard You carefully,'

**4:11** and, 'On their hands they will lift You up, so that You do not strike Your foot against a stone.'"

**4:12** And answering, Jesus said to him, "It has been said, 'You will not put the Lord your God to the test.'"

**4:13** And when the devil had completed every temptation, he departed from Him until an opportune time.

**4:14** And Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit to Galilee. And a report went out through all the surrounding region concerning Him.

**4:15** And He Himself was teaching in their synagogues, being praised by everyone.

**4:16** And He came to Nazareth, where He had been raised, and according to His custom He went into the synagogue on the Sabbath day, and stood up to read.

**4:17** And a scroll of the prophet Isaiah was handed to Him, and unrolling the scroll, He found the place where it was written,

**4:18** "The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me, because He anointed Me to announce good news to the poor. He has sent Me to proclaim liberty to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to send out the oppressed in liberty,

**4:19** to proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord."

**4:20** And rolling up the scroll and returning it to the attendant, He sat down; and the eyes of everyone in the synagogue were gazing intently at Him.

**4:21** Then He began to say to them, "Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your ears."

**4:22** And all were bearing witness to Him and marveling at the words of grace that were coming out of His mouth, and they were saying, "Is this not the son of Joseph?"

**4:23** And He said to them, "Surely you will say this proverb to Me: 'Physician, heal Yourself! Whatever we heard took place in Capernaum, do here also in Your hometown.'"

**4:24** And He said, "Truly I say to you, no prophet is acceptable in his hometown.

**4:25** But in truth I say to you, there were many widows in the days of Elijah in Israel, when the sky was shut up for three years and six months, when a great famine came over all the land,

**4:26** and to none of them was Elijah sent except to Zarephath in the region of Sidon, to a woman who was a widow.

**4:27** And there were many lepers in Israel in the time of Elisha the prophet, and none of them was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian."

**4:28** And all in the synagogue were filled with fury as they heard these things,

**4:29** and rising up, they drove Him out of the city and led Him up to the brow of the hill on which their city had been built, so as to hurl Him down the cliff.

**4:30** But He Himself, passing through the midst of them, went on His way.

**4:31** And He went down to Capernaum, a city of Galilee. And He was teaching them on the Sabbaths;

**4:32** and they were astonished at His teaching, because His word was with authority.

**4:33** And in the synagogue there was a man having a spirit of an unclean demon, and he cried out with a loud voice,

**4:34** "Ah! What is there between us and You, Jesus of Nazareth? Did You come to destroy us? I know who You are: the Holy One of God."

**4:35** And Jesus rebuked it, saying, "Be silenced and come out of him!" And the demon threw him down in the middle and came out of him without harming him at all.

**4:36** And amazement came upon all, and they were talking with one another, saying, "What is this word, that with authority and power He commands the unclean spirits, and they come out?"

**4:37** And news concerning Him was going out into every place in the surrounding region.

**4:38** Now rising up from the synagogue, He entered the house of Simon. And Simon's mother-in-law was gripped by a high fever, and they asked Him concerning her.

**4:39** And standing over her, He rebuked the fever, and it left her; and immediately rising up, she began to serve them.

**4:40** Now while the sun was setting, all those who had any who were sick with various diseases brought them to Him; and laying His hands on each one of them, He was healing them.

**4:41** And demons also were coming out of many, crying out and saying, "You are the Son of God!" And rebuking them, He would not allow them to speak, because they knew Him to be the Christ.

**4:42** Now when day came, He went out and departed to a desolate place; and the crowds were searching for Him, and they came up to Him and tried to keep Him from going away from them.

**4:43** But He said to them, "I must announce the good news of the kingdom of God to the other cities also, because for this purpose I was sent."

**4:44** And He was proclaiming in the synagogues of Judea.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — Some later manuscripts add "but by every word of God" (cf. Matt 4:4; Deut 8:3), which is absent from the earliest Greek witnesses.
- **4:16** — The Greek text spells the town name "Nazara," an early alternate form of Nazareth.
- **4:18** — This reading combines Isaiah 61:1-2 with Isaiah 58:6 ("to send out the oppressed in liberty"); later manuscripts add "to heal the brokenhearted" from Isaiah 61:1.
- **4:44** — The earliest manuscripts read "Judea" (likely used broadly for the whole land of the Jews), whereas some later manuscripts read "Galilee" in harmonization with Mark 1:39.
