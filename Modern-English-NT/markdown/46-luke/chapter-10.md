## Text

**10:1** Now after these things, the Lord also appointed seventy-two others and sent them two by two ahead of Him into every city and place where He Himself was about to come.

**10:2** And He was saying to them, "The harvest indeed is plentiful, but the workers are few; therefore plead with the Lord of the harvest so that He may send out workers into His harvest.

**10:3** Go; look, I am sending you out as lambs in the midst of wolves.

**10:4** Carry no money bag, no travel bag, no sandals; and do not greet anyone along the road.

**10:5** Whatever house you enter, first say, 'Peace to this house.'

**10:6** And if a son of peace is there, your peace will rest upon him; but if not, it will return to you.

**10:7** Remain in that same house, eating and drinking what they provide, for the worker is worthy of his wages. Do not move from house to house.

**10:8** And whatever city you enter and they receive you, eat what is set before you,

**10:9** and heal those in it who are sick, and say to them, 'The kingdom of God has drawn near to you.'

**10:10** But whatever city you enter and they do not receive you, go out into its streets and say,

**10:11** 'Even the dust from your city that clung to our feet we wipe off against you; yet know this, that the kingdom of God has drawn near.'

**10:12** I say to you that it will be more bearable on that day for Sodom than for that city.

**10:13** Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the mighty deeds that were done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago, sitting in sackcloth and ashes.

**10:14** Yet it will be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon in the judgment than for you.

**10:15** And you, Capernaum, will you be exalted to heaven? You will descend to Hades.

**10:16** The one who listens to you listens to Me, and the one who rejects you rejects Me; and the one who rejects Me rejects the One who sent Me."

**10:17** Now the seventy-two returned with joy, saying, "Lord, even the demons are subject to us in Your name!"

**10:18** And He said to them, "I was watching Satan fall like lightning from heaven.

**10:19** Look, I have given you the authority to tread upon serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing will by any means harm you.

**10:20** Nevertheless, do not rejoice in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but rejoice that your names have been written in the heavens."

**10:21** In that same hour He exulted in the Holy Spirit and said, "I praise You, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that You hid these things from the wise and understanding and revealed them to infants; yes, Father, because this was well-pleasing in Your sight.

**10:22** All things have been handed over to Me by My Father, and no one knows who the Son is except the Father, and who the Father is except the Son and anyone to whom the Son wills to reveal Him."

**10:23** And turning to the disciples privately, He said, "Blessed are the eyes that see the things you see.

**10:24** For I say to you that many prophets and kings desired to see the things you see and did not see them, and to hear the things you hear and did not hear them."

**10:25** And look, a certain expert in the law stood up, testing Him and saying, "Teacher, what shall I do to inherit eternal life?"

**10:26** And He said to him, "What is written in the law? How do you read it?"

**10:27** And he answered and said, "You shall love the Lord your God from your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole strength, and with your whole mind, and your neighbor as yourself."

**10:28** And He said to him, "You have answered rightly; do this, and you will live."

**10:29** But wanting to justify himself, he said to Jesus, "And who is my neighbor?"

**10:30** Jesus replied and said, "A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho and fell among robbers, who stripped him and inflicted blows upon him, and went away leaving him half dead.

**10:31** Now by chance a certain priest was going down along that road, and seeing him, he passed by on the opposite side.

**10:32** And likewise a Levite also, when he came to the place and saw him, passed by on the opposite side.

**10:33** But a certain Samaritan who was journeying came upon him, and when he saw him, he was moved with compassion,

**10:34** and approaching, he bound up his wounds, pouring on oil and wine; and placing him on his own animal, he brought him to an inn and took care of him.

**10:35** And on the next day he took out two denarii and gave them to the innkeeper and said, 'Take care of him, and whatever more you spend, I will repay you when I return.'

**10:36** Which of these three seems to you to have become a neighbor to the one who fell among the robbers?"

**10:37** And he said, "The one who showed mercy to him." And Jesus said to him, "Go, and you do likewise."

**10:38** Now as they were going along, He entered a certain village; and a certain woman named Martha welcomed Him.

**10:39** And she had a sister called Mary, who also sat at the feet of Jesus and was listening to His word.

**10:40** But Martha was distracted with much serving; and stepping up, she said, "Lord, do You not care that my sister has left me alone to serve? Tell her therefore to help me."

**10:41** But the Lord answered and said to her, "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things,

**10:42** yet few things are needed, or one; for Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her."

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The critical text reads "seventy-two" (also in 10:17), whereas many manuscripts read "seventy"; both numbers have Old Testament precedents (e.g., the nations in Genesis 10 in the Septuagint vs. Masoretic Text, or the elders in Numbers 11).
- **10:18** — The imperfect verb "I was watching" paired with the aorist participle "fall" depicts a vivid sighting of Satan's sudden downfall, viewed by interpreters either as taking place during the mission of the seventy-two or as a prophetic vision of his defeat.
- **10:38** — The critical text concludes with "welcomed Him," omitting the clarifying addition "into her house" found in many later manuscripts.
- **10:42** — The reading "few things are needed, or one" (combining a literal reference to a simple meal with the single spiritual necessity) is strongly attested in early Alexandrian witnesses, while other manuscripts shorten it to "one thing is needed" or "few things are needed."
