## Text

**7:1** When He had completed all His words in the hearing of the people, He entered Capernaum.

**7:2** Now a certain centurion's slave, who was highly valued by him, was ill and about to die.

**7:3** When he heard about Jesus, he sent elders of the Jews to Him, asking Him to come and heal his slave.

**7:4** When they arrived before Jesus, they earnestly pleaded with Him, saying, "He is worthy for You to grant him this,

**7:5** for he loves our nation, and he himself built the synagogue for us."

**7:6** So Jesus was going with them. And when He was already not far from the house, the centurion sent friends, saying to Him, "Lord, do not trouble Yourself, for I am not worthy for You to come under my roof;

**7:7** for this reason I did not even consider myself worthy to come to You. Instead, speak a word, and let my servant be healed.

**7:8** For I too am a man placed under authority, having soldiers under myself, and I say to this one, 'Go,' and he goes, and to another, 'Come,' and he comes, and to my slave, 'Do this,' and he does it."

**7:9** When Jesus heard these things, He marveled at him, and turning to the crowd following Him, He said, "I tell you, not even in Israel have I found such great faith."

**7:10** And when those who had been sent returned to the house, they found the slave in good health.

**7:11** And it happened soon afterward that He went to a city called Nain, and His disciples and a large crowd were going along with Him.

**7:12** Now as He approached the gate of the city, look, a dead man was being carried out, the only son of his mother, and she was a widow; and a sizable crowd from the city was with her.

**7:13** When the Lord saw her, He was moved with compassion for her and said to her, "Do not weep."

**7:14** And coming forward, He touched the bier, and those carrying it stood still. And He said, "Young man, I say to you, arise."

**7:15** And the dead man sat up and began to speak, and He gave him to his mother.

**7:16** Fear seized everyone, and they were glorifying God, saying, "A great prophet has been raised up among us," and, "God has visited His people."

**7:17** And this report concerning Him went out throughout all Judea and all the surrounding region.

**7:18** John's disciples reported to him about all these things. And calling two of his disciples to himself, John

**7:19** sent them to Jesus, saying, "Are You the One who is to come, or should we look for another?"

**7:20** When the men came to Him, they said, "John the Baptist has sent us to You, saying, 'Are You the One who is to come, or should we look for another?'"

**7:21** In that hour He healed many of diseases, afflictions, and evil spirits, and to many who were blind He graciously granted sight.

**7:22** And answering, He said to them, "Go and report to John what you have seen and heard: the blind receive sight, the lame walk, lepers are cleansed, and the deaf hear, the dead are raised, the poor have the good news proclaimed to them.

**7:23** And blessed is whoever does not stumble because of Me."

**7:24** When John's messengers had departed, He began to speak to the crowds concerning John: "What did you go out into the wilderness to observe? A reed shaken by the wind?

**7:25** But what did you go out to see? A man dressed in soft garments? Look, those who are in splendid clothing and luxury are in royal palaces.

**7:26** But what did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet.

**7:27** This is the one about whom it is written, 'Look, I am sending My messenger before Your face, who will prepare Your way ahead of You.'

**7:28** I tell you, among those born of women there is no one greater than John; yet the one who is least in the kingdom of God is greater than he."

**7:29** (When all the people and the tax collectors heard this, they vindicated God, having been baptized with the baptism of John;

**7:30** but the Pharisees and the experts in the law rejected God's purpose for themselves, not having been baptized by him.)

**7:31** "To what then shall I compare the people of this generation, and what are they like?

**7:32** They are like children sitting in the marketplace and calling out to one another, who say, 'We played the flute for you, and you did not dance; we sang a dirge, and you did not weep.'

**7:33** For John the Baptist has come neither eating bread nor drinking wine, and you say, 'He has a demon.'

**7:34** The Son of Man has come eating and drinking, and you say, 'Look, a gluttonous man and a drunkard, a friend of tax collectors and sinners!'

**7:35** Yet wisdom is vindicated by all her children."

**7:36** Now one of the Pharisees was asking Him to eat with him; and having entered the Pharisee's house, He reclined at table.

**7:37** And look, a woman in the city who was a sinner, when she learned that He was reclining at table in the Pharisee's house, brought an alabaster jar of fragrant oil,

**7:38** and standing behind Him at His feet, weeping, she began to wet His feet with her tears and was wiping them with the hair of her head, kissing His feet and anointing them with the fragrant oil.

**7:39** Now when the Pharisee who had invited Him saw this, he spoke to himself, saying, "If this man were a prophet, He would know who and what sort of woman this is who is touching Him, that she is a sinner."

**7:40** And answering, Jesus said to him, "Simon, I have something to say to you." And he said, "Teacher, say it."

**7:41** "A certain moneylender had two debtors: the one owed five hundred denarii, and the other fifty.

**7:42** Since they had nothing with which to repay, he graciously forgave them both. Which of them, therefore, will love him more?"

**7:43** Simon answered and said, "I suppose the one whom he forgave the larger amount." And He said to him, "You have judged rightly."

**7:44** And turning toward the woman, He said to Simon, "Do you see this woman? I entered your house; you gave Me no water for My feet, yet she has wet My feet with her tears and wiped them with her hair.

**7:45** You gave Me no kiss; yet from the time I entered, she has not ceased kissing My feet.

**7:46** You did not anoint My head with oil; yet she has anointed My feet with fragrant oil.

**7:47** For this reason, I tell you, her sins, which are many, have been forgiven, for she has loved much; but the one to whom little is forgiven loves little."

**7:48** And He said to her, "Your sins have been forgiven."

**7:49** And those who were reclining at table with Him began to say among themselves, "Who is this man who even forgives sins?"

**7:50** And He said to the woman, "Your faith has saved you; go in peace."

## Translator Notes

- **7:19** — The Greek text here reads "to Jesus" (πρὸς τὸν Ἰησοῦν), whereas several early manuscripts read "to the Lord" (πρὸς τὸν κύριον).
- **7:29** — Verses 29–30 are punctuated as a parenthetical comment by the narrator, though some interpreters view them as a continuation of Jesus' speech. "Vindicated God" (ἐδικαίωσαν τὸν θεόν) means they acknowledged God to be righteous.
- **7:35** — "Is vindicated" uses a gnomic aorist (ἐδικαιώθη), echoing "vindicated" in 7:29; the preposition ἀπό ("by") marks the children of wisdom as those through whom wisdom's rightness is demonstrated.
- **7:47** — The conjunction ὅτι ("for") is debated: in light of the parable in 7:41–43, many read it evidentially ("as shown by the fact that she has loved much"), while others take it causally ("because she has loved much").
