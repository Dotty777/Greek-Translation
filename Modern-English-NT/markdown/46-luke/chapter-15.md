## Text

**15:1** Now all the tax collectors and the sinners were drawing near to Him to listen to Him.

**15:2** And both the Pharisees and the scribes were grumbling, saying, "This man welcomes sinners and eats with them."

**15:3** So He spoke this parable to them, saying,

**15:4** "What man among you, having a hundred sheep and having lost one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine behind in the wilderness and go after the lost one until he finds it?

**15:5** And when he has found it, he lays it upon his shoulders, rejoicing.

**15:6** And when he comes into the house, he calls together his friends and his neighbors, saying to them, 'Rejoice with me, because I have found my sheep that was lost.'

**15:7** I tell you that in the same way there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous people who have no need of repentance.

**15:8** Or what woman, having ten drachmas, if she loses one drachma, does not light a lamp and sweep the house and search carefully until she finds it?

**15:9** And when she has found it, she calls together her friends and neighbors, saying, 'Rejoice with me, because I have found the drachma that I lost.'

**15:10** In the same way, I tell you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents."

**15:11** And He said, "A certain man had two sons.

**15:12** And the younger of them said to his father, 'Father, give me the share of the estate that falls to me.' So he divided his livelihood between them.

**15:13** And not many days later, having gathered everything together, the younger son journeyed away to a distant country, and there he squandered his estate by living recklessly.

**15:14** Now when he had spent everything, a severe famine occurred throughout that country, and he himself began to be in need.

**15:15** So he went and attached himself to one of the citizens of that country, and he sent him into his fields to feed pigs.

**15:16** And he was longing to be filled from the carob pods that the pigs were eating, and no one was giving anything to him.

**15:17** But when he came to himself, he said, 'How many of my father's hired workers have an abundance of bread, while I am perishing here from hunger!

**15:18** I will get up and go to my father and say to him, "Father, I have sinned against heaven and in your sight;

**15:19** I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me like one of your hired workers."'

**15:20** And he got up and came to his own father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was moved with compassion, and he ran and fell upon his neck and kissed him affectionately.

**15:21** And the son said to him, 'Father, I have sinned against heaven and in your sight; I am no longer worthy to be called your son.'

**15:22** But the father said to his slaves, 'Quickly bring out the best robe and put it on him, and put a ring on his hand and sandals on his feet,

**15:23** and bring the fattened calf, slaughter it, and let us eat and celebrate,

**15:24** because this son of mine was dead and has come to life again; he was lost and has been found.' And they began to celebrate.

**15:25** Now his older son was in the field; and as he came and drew near to the house, he heard music and dancing.

**15:26** And calling over one of the servants, he began inquiring what these things might be.

**15:27** And he said to him, 'Your brother has arrived, and your father has slaughtered the fattened calf, because he has received him back safe and sound.'

**15:28** But he became angry and was unwilling to go in. So his father came out and was pleading with him.

**15:29** But he answered and said to his father, 'Look, for so many years I have been serving you and have never neglected a command of yours, yet to me you have never given a young goat so that I might celebrate with my friends;

**15:30** but when this son of yours came, who has devoured your livelihood with prostitutes, you slaughtered the fattened calf for him.'

**15:31** And he said to him, 'Child, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours.

**15:32** Yet it was necessary to celebrate and rejoice, because this brother of yours was dead and has come to life, and was lost and has been found.'"

## Translator Notes

- **15:7** — The Greek omits an explicit comparative ("more"), using a Semitic idiom in which "joy ... than" carries the sense "more joy ... than."
- **15:8** — A drachma was a Greek silver coin roughly equivalent to a Roman denarius, about a day's wage for a common laborer.
- **15:21** — Some early manuscripts (including Codex Vaticanus [B], Codex Sinaiticus [א], and Codex Bezae [D]) add "make me like one of your hired workers" at the end of the verse, matching verse 19; the adopted shorter text is attested by 𝔓75, Codex Alexandrinus (A), the Byzantine tradition, and modern critical editions.
