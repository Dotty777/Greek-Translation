## Text

**13:1** Now some people were present at that very time, reporting to Him about the Galileans whose blood Pilate had mixed with their sacrifices.

**13:2** And answering, He said to them, "Do you suppose that these Galileans were greater sinners than all the Galileans because they have suffered these things?

**13:3** No, I say to you, but unless you repent, all of you will perish similarly.

**13:4** Or those eighteen on whom the tower in Siloam fell and killed them—do you suppose that they were greater offenders than all the people dwelling in Jerusalem?

**13:5** No, I tell you, but unless you repent, you will all perish in the same way."

**13:6** And He was telling this parable: "A certain man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard, and he came looking for fruit on it and did not find any.

**13:7** So he said to the vinedresser, 'Look, for three years I have been coming looking for fruit on this fig tree, and I find none. Cut it down! Why does it even waste the ground?'

**13:8** But he answered and said to him, 'Sir, leave it this year also, until I dig around it and put manure on it;

**13:9** and if indeed it bears fruit in the time to come—but if not, you will cut it down.'"

**13:10** Now He was teaching in one of the synagogues on the Sabbath.

**13:11** And look, there was a woman who had a spirit of infirmity for eighteen years, and she was bent over and unable to straighten up at all.

**13:12** When Jesus saw her, He called to her and said to her, "Woman, you have been released from your infirmity,"

**13:13** and He laid His hands on her; and immediately she was made straight, and she began glorifying God.

**13:14** But the synagogue leader, indignant because Jesus had healed on the Sabbath, responded and was saying to the crowd, "There are six days in which it is necessary to work; so come on those days and be healed, and not on the Sabbath day."

**13:15** But the Lord answered him and said, "Hypocrites! Does not each of you untie his ox or his donkey from the feeding trough on the Sabbath and lead it away to give it water?

**13:16** And this woman, being a daughter of Abraham whom Satan has bound—look, for eighteen years—should she not be released from this bond on the Sabbath day?"

**13:17** And as He was saying these things, all those opposing Him were being put to shame, while the entire crowd was rejoicing over all the glorious deeds being done by Him.

**13:18** Therefore He was saying, "What is the kingdom of God like, and to what shall I compare it?

**13:19** It is like a mustard seed, which a man took and cast into his own garden; and it grew and became a tree, and the birds of the sky nested in its branches."

**13:20** And again He said, "To what shall I compare the kingdom of God?

**13:21** It is like leaven, which a woman took and hid in three measures of flour until all of it was leavened."

**13:22** And He was passing through cities and villages, teaching and making His journey toward Jerusalem.

**13:23** And someone said to Him, "Lord, are those who are being saved few?" And He said to them,

**13:24** "Strive to enter through the narrow door, because many, I tell you, will seek to enter and will not be able,

**13:25** once the master of the house gets up and locks the door, and you begin to stand outside and knock on the door, saying, 'Lord, open for us!' Then answering, He will say to you, 'I do not know you—where you are from.'

**13:26** Then you will begin to say, 'We ate and drank in Your presence, and You taught in our streets.'

**13:27** And He will speak, saying to you, 'I do not know where you are from; depart from Me, all you workers of unrighteousness.'

**13:28** In that place there will be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth, when you see Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and all the prophets in the kingdom of God, but yourselves being thrown outside.

**13:29** And they will come from east and west, and from north and south, and will recline at table in the kingdom of God.

**13:30** And look, there are last ones who will be first, and there are first ones who will be last."

**13:31** At that very hour some Pharisees came up, saying to Him, "Leave and go away from here, because Herod wants to kill You."

**13:32** And He said to them, "Go and tell that fox, 'Look, I am casting out demons and completing healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I reach completion.'

**13:33** Nevertheless, I must journey on today and tomorrow and the next day, because it is not possible for a prophet to perish outside Jerusalem.

**13:34** Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the one who kills the prophets and stones those who have been sent to her—how often I wanted to gather your children together, in the way a hen gathers her own brood under her wings, and you were not willing!

**13:35** Look, your house is left to you. And I tell you, you will certainly not see Me until the time comes when you say, 'Blessed is the One who comes in the name of the Lord.'"

## Translator Notes

- **13:4** — "Offenders" translates Greek opheiletai (literally "debtors"), a Semitic idiom denoting moral guilt before God.
- **13:9** — The Greek clause breaks off abruptly after "in the time to come" (aposiopesis), leaving an implied positive outcome such as "well and good."
- **13:21** — "Three measures" renders Greek sata tria (three Hebrew seahs), a large volume of roughly 35 to 40 liters of flour.
- **13:32** — Greek teleioumai can mean "I reach completion" (finishing His appointed course) or "I am brought to perfection" through suffering.
- **13:35** — The Greek text reads simply "your house is left to you," without the word "desolate" found in some later manuscripts and Matthew 23:38.
