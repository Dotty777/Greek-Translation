## Text

**19:1** And having entered, He was passing through Jericho.

**19:2** And look, there was a man called by name Zacchaeus; and he was a chief tax collector, and he was rich.

**19:3** And he was seeking to see who Jesus was, and he was unable because of the crowd, for he was small in stature.

**19:4** So running on ahead, he climbed up into a sycamore-fig tree in order to see Him, because He was about to pass by that way.

**19:5** And when He came to the place, Jesus looked up and said to him, "Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, for today I must stay at your house."

**19:6** And he hurried and came down, and welcomed Him rejoicing.

**19:7** And when they saw it, they all kept grumbling, saying, "He has gone in to lodge with a sinful man."

**19:8** But Zacchaeus stood and said to the Lord, "Look, Lord, half of my possessions I give to the poor, and if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I pay back fourfold."

**19:9** And Jesus said to him, "Today salvation has come to this house, because he too is a son of Abraham;

**19:10** for the Son of Man came to seek and to save what was lost."

**19:11** Now as they were listening to these things, He went on to tell a parable, because He was near Jerusalem and they supposed that the kingdom of God was about to appear immediately.

**19:12** So He said, "A certain man of noble birth journeyed to a distant country to receive a kingdom for himself and to return.

**19:13** And calling ten of his slaves, he gave them ten minas and said to them, 'Conduct business until I come.'

**19:14** But his citizens hated him, and they sent a delegation after him, saying, 'We do not want this man to reign over us.'

**19:15** And it happened when he returned, having received the kingdom, that he ordered these slaves to whom he had given the money to be summoned to him, so that he might know what they had gained by doing business.

**19:16** Then the first came forward, saying, 'Master, your mina has earned ten more minas.'

**19:17** And he said to him, 'Well done, good slave! Because you proved faithful in a very small matter, have authority over ten cities.'

**19:18** And the second came, saying, 'Your mina, master, has made five minas.'

**19:19** And he said to this one also, 'You also be over five cities.'

**19:20** And the other came, saying, 'Master, here is your mina, which I kept put away in a handkerchief;

**19:21** for I was afraid of you, because you are a severe man: you take up what you did not lay down, and you reap what you did not sow.'

**19:22** He says to him, 'Out of your mouth I judge you, wicked slave! You knew that I am a severe man, taking up what I did not lay down and reaping what I did not sow?

**19:23** Then why did you not put my money in the bank? And upon coming, I would have collected it with interest.'

**19:24** And he said to those standing by, 'Take the mina from him and give it to the one who has the ten minas'—

**19:25** and they said to him, 'Master, he has ten minas'—

**19:26** 'I say to you that to everyone who has, it will be given, but from the one who does not have, even what he has will be taken away.

**19:27** But as for these enemies of mine who did not want me to reign over them, bring them here and slaughter them before me.'"

**19:28** And having said these things, He was going on ahead, going up to Jerusalem.

**19:29** And it happened as He drew near to Bethphage and Bethany, at the mount called Olivet, that He sent two of the disciples,

**19:30** saying, "Go into the village opposite, where on entering you will find a colt tied, on which no person has ever yet sat; untie it and bring it.

**19:31** And if anyone asks you, 'Why are you untying it?' you will say this: 'The Lord has need of it.'"

**19:32** So those who had been sent departed and found it just as He had told them.

**19:33** And as they were untying the colt, its owners said to them, "Why are you untying the colt?"

**19:34** And they said, "The Lord has need of it."

**19:35** And they brought it to Jesus, and throwing their cloaks onto the colt, they set Jesus on it.

**19:36** And as He went along, they were spreading their cloaks on the road.

**19:37** And as He was already drawing near to the descent of the Mount of Olives, the whole multitude of the disciples began, rejoicing, to praise God with a loud voice for all the mighty works that they had seen,

**19:38** saying, "Blessed is the King coming in the name of the Lord! Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest!"

**19:39** And some of the Pharisees from the crowd said to Him, "Teacher, rebuke Your disciples."

**19:40** And in reply He said, "I tell you that if these stay silent, the stones will cry out."

**19:41** And as He drew near, seeing the city, He wept over it,

**19:42** saying, "If only you too had known on this day the things that lead to peace—but now they have been hidden from your eyes.

**19:43** For days will come upon you when your enemies will set up a barricade around you, and encircle you, and hem you in on every side,

**19:44** and they will dash you to the ground, and your children within you, and they will not leave one stone upon another in you, because you did not recognize the time of your visitation."

**19:45** And having entered the temple, He began to drive out those who were selling,

**19:46** saying to them, "It is written, 'My house is a house of prayer,' but you have made it a cave of robbers."

**19:47** And He was teaching daily in the temple. But the chief priests and the scribes were seeking to destroy Him, and the leading men of the people also,

**19:48** and they could not find what they might do, for all the people were hanging on Him as they listened.

## Translator Notes

- **19:8** — The Greek present-tense verbs "I give" (didōmi) and "I pay back" (apodidōmi) are debated as either a resolve taking immediate effect or a statement of Zacchaeus's ongoing practice; keeping the simple present preserves both possibilities.
- **19:13** — A mina (mna) was a unit of silver currency equal to 100 drachmas, roughly three to four months' wages for an ordinary laborer.
- **19:42** — The opening clause is an unfinished conditional sentence (aposiopesis) expressing deep grief and an unfulfilled wish, literally "If you had known on this day, even you, the things toward peace—".
- **19:46** — The Greek text here omits the future verb "will be" (estai) found in some manuscripts (cf. Isa 56:7), reading a verbless clause literally "My house [is] a house of prayer."
