## Text

**2:1** And when He entered Capernaum again after some days, it was heard that He was at home.

**2:2** And many gathered together so that there was no longer room, not even around the door, and He was speaking the word to them.

**2:3** And they came bringing to Him a paralyzed man, carried by four men.

**2:4** And not being able to bring him to Him because of the crowd, they removed the roof where He was; and after digging through it, they lowered the mat on which the paralyzed man was lying.

**2:5** And seeing their faith, Jesus said to the paralyzed man, "Child, your sins are forgiven."

**2:6** Now some of the scribes were sitting there and reasoning in their hearts,

**2:7** "Why does this man speak like this? He is blaspheming! Who can forgive sins except God alone?"

**2:8** And immediately Jesus, perceiving in His spirit that they were reasoning this way within themselves, said to them, "Why are you reasoning about these things in your hearts?

**2:9** Which is easier, to say to the paralyzed man, 'Your sins are forgiven,' or to say, 'Get up and pick up your mat and walk'?

**2:10** But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins"—He said to the paralyzed man,

**2:11** "I say to you, get up, pick up your mat, and go to your home."

**2:12** And he got up, and immediately picking up the mat, he went out in front of everyone, so that all were amazed and glorified God, saying, "We have never seen anything like this."

**2:13** And He went out again beside the sea; and the entire crowd was coming to Him, and He was teaching them.

**2:14** And as He passed by, He saw Levi the son of Alphaeus sitting at the tax booth, and He said to him, "Follow Me." And rising up, he followed Him.

**2:15** And it came about that He was reclining at table in his house, and many tax collectors and sinners were reclining with Jesus and His disciples, for there were many, and they were following Him.

**2:16** And when the scribes of the Pharisees saw that He was eating with the sinners and tax collectors, they were saying to His disciples, "Why does He eat with the tax collectors and sinners?"

**2:17** And hearing this, Jesus said to them, "Those who are healthy have no need of a physician, but those who are ill; I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners."

**2:18** And John's disciples and the Pharisees were fasting. And they came and said to Him, "Why do the disciples of John and the disciples of the Pharisees fast, yet Your disciples do not fast?"

**2:19** And Jesus said to them, "Can the sons of the bridal chamber fast while the bridegroom is with them? For as long as they have the bridegroom with them, they cannot fast.

**2:20** Yet days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast in that day.

**2:21** No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth onto an old garment; otherwise, the patch pulls away from it, the new from the old, and a worse tear occurs.

**2:22** And no one pours new wine into old wineskins; otherwise, the wine will burst the wineskins, and the wine is lost along with the skins. Rather, new wine belongs in fresh wineskins."

**2:23** And it happened that He was passing through the grainfields on the Sabbath, and His disciples began to make their way while plucking the heads of grain.

**2:24** And the Pharisees were saying to Him, "Look, why are they doing on the Sabbath what is not lawful?"

**2:25** And He said to them, "Have you never read what David did when he was in need and grew hungry, he and those who were with him,

**2:26** how he entered the house of God in the time of Abiathar the high priest and ate the loaves of the Presence, which it is not lawful for anyone to eat except the priests, and gave some also to those who were with him?"

**2:27** And He was saying to them, "The Sabbath came into being for the sake of man, and not man for the sake of the Sabbath;

**2:28** so the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath."

## Translator Notes

- **2:10** — The mid-sentence shift from addressing the scribes to addressing the paralyzed man reflects an abrupt grammatical break (anacoluthon) present in the Greek text.
- **2:19** — "Sons of the bridal chamber" is a Semitic idiom referring to the bridegroom's attendants or wedding guests.
- **2:23** — "Make their way" renders the Greek expression hodon poiein (literally "make a path"), meaning either to travel along or to clear a path while plucking heads of grain.
- **2:26** — "In the time of Abiathar the high priest" is a noted historical crux because 1 Samuel 21:1-6 names Abiathar's father, Ahimelech, as the priest at Nob; the phrase may point broadly to the era or narrative cycle associated with Abiathar, who became David's prominent high priest.
