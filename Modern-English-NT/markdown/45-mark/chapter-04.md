## Text

**4:1** And again He began to teach beside the sea. And a very large crowd gathered to Him, so that He got into a boat and sat on the sea, and the whole crowd was by the sea on the land.

**4:2** And He was teaching them many things in parables, and He was saying to them in His teaching:

**4:3** "Listen! Look, the sower went out to sow.

**4:4** And it happened as he was sowing that some fell beside the path, and the birds came and devoured it.

**4:5** And other seed fell on the rocky ground where it did not have much soil, and immediately it sprang up because it had no depth of soil;

**4:6** and when the sun rose, it was scorched, and because it had no root, it withered away.

**4:7** And other seed fell among the thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked it, and it yielded no fruit.

**4:8** And other seeds fell into the good soil, and they yielded fruit, growing up and increasing, and bore thirtyfold, and sixtyfold, and a hundredfold."

**4:9** And He was saying, "The one who has ears to hear, let him hear."

**4:10** And when He was alone, those around Him, together with the Twelve, were asking Him about the parables.

**4:11** And He was saying to them, "To you the mystery of the kingdom of God has been given, but to those outside, all things come in parables,

**4:12** so that seeing they may see and not perceive, and hearing they may hear and not understand, otherwise they might turn back and be forgiven."

**4:13** And He said to them, "Do you not understand this parable? And how will you comprehend all the parables?

**4:14** The sower sows the word.

**4:15** Now these are the ones beside the path where the word is sown; and when they hear, Satan immediately comes and takes away the word that has been sown in them.

**4:16** And in the same way, these are the ones sown on the rocky places, who, when they hear the word, immediately receive it with joy,

**4:17** and they have no root in themselves, but are only temporary; then, when affliction or persecution arises because of the word, immediately they stumble.

**4:18** And others are the ones sown among the thorns; these are the ones who heard the word,

**4:19** and the anxieties of the age, and the deceitfulness of wealth, and the desires for other things enter in and choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful.

**4:20** And those are the ones sown on the good soil, who hear the word and welcome it and bear fruit—thirtyfold, and sixtyfold, and a hundredfold."

**4:21** And He was saying to them, "A lamp is not brought in to be placed under a measuring basket or under a bed, is it? Is it not to be placed on a lampstand?

**4:22** For nothing is hidden except so that it may be revealed, nor has anything become secret except so that it may come into the open.

**4:23** If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear."

**4:24** And He was saying to them, "Watch closely what you hear. By the measure with which you measure, it will be measured to you, and more will be added to you.

**4:25** For whoever has, to him it will be given; and whoever does not have, even what he has will be taken away from him."

**4:26** And He was saying, "The kingdom of God is like this: as if a man scatters seed on the soil,

**4:27** and sleeps and gets up night and day, and the seed sprouts and grows—how, he himself does not know.

**4:28** All by itself the soil bears fruit: first the blade, then the head, then the full grain in the head.

**4:29** And when the fruit is ripe, immediately he sends forth the sickle, because the harvest has arrived."

**4:30** And He was saying, "How shall we compare the kingdom of God, or in what parable shall we present it?

**4:31** It is like a mustard seed, which, when it is sown upon the soil, though smaller than all the seeds on the earth—

**4:32** yet when it is sown, it grows up and becomes larger than all the garden plants and puts out large branches, so that the birds of the sky are able to nest under its shade."

**4:33** And with many such parables He was speaking the word to them, as they were able to hear it;

**4:34** and without a parable He was not speaking to them, yet in private He was explaining everything to His own disciples.

**4:35** And on that day, when evening had come, He said to them, "Let us cross over to the other side."

**4:36** And leaving the crowd behind, they took Him along, just as He was, in the boat; and other boats were with Him.

**4:37** And a fierce squall of wind arose, and the waves were breaking into the boat, so that the boat was already filling up.

**4:38** And He Himself was in the stern, sleeping on the cushion; and they woke Him and said to Him, "Teacher, do You not care that we are perishing?"

**4:39** And waking up, He rebuked the wind and said to the sea, "Be silent! Be still!" And the wind died down, and there was a great calm.

**4:40** And He said to them, "Why are you fearful? Do you not yet have faith?"

**4:41** And they were filled with great fear, and they were saying to one another, "Who indeed is this, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?"

## Translator Notes

- **4:8** — Greek εἰς τριάκοντα καὶ ἓν ἑξήκοντα καὶ ἓν ἑκατόν (literally "to thirty and one-sixty and one-hundred") reflects a Semitic multiplicative idiom meaning thirtyfold, sixtyfold, and a hundredfold; the same ἓν idiom recurs in 4:20.
- **4:12** — The conjunction ἵνα ("so that") can express either purpose or result, echoing Isaiah 6:9-10; the paired participles and finite verbs ("seeing they may see", "hearing they may hear") preserve a Hebraic emphatic idiom.
- **4:29** — Greek παραδοῖ literally means "yields itself" or "permits," an intransitive agricultural idiom for a crop becoming ripe, while "sends forth the sickle" echoes Joel 3:13 (LXX 4:13).
- **4:39** — The command πεφίμωσο ("Be still!") is a perfect passive imperative literally meaning "Be muzzled!" or "Stay silenced!", the same verb used to silence an unclean spirit in Mark 1:25.
