## Text

**7:1** And the Pharisees and some of the scribes who had come from Jerusalem gathered to Him,

**7:2** and saw that some of His disciples were eating bread with defiled hands, that is, unwashed—

**7:3** for the Pharisees and all the Jews do not eat unless they wash their hands with the fist, holding fast to the tradition of the elders,

**7:4** and when coming from the marketplace, they do not eat unless they wash themselves; and there are many other things that they received to hold fast to, washings of cups and pitchers and copper vessels—

**7:5** and the Pharisees and the scribes asked Him, "Why do Your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with defiled hands?"

**7:6** And He said to them, "Isaiah prophesied rightly about you hypocrites, as it is written, 'This people honors Me with their lips, but their heart is far away from Me;

**7:7** and in vain they worship Me, teaching as doctrines the precepts of men.'

**7:8** Abandoning the commandment of God, you hold fast to the tradition of men."

**7:9** And He was saying to them, "How well you set aside the commandment of God so that you may keep your tradition!

**7:10** For Moses said, 'Honor your father and your mother,' and, 'The one who speaks evil of father or mother must surely die.'

**7:11** But you say that if a man says to his father or his mother, 'Whatever you might profit from me is Corban'—that is, a gift—

**7:12** you no longer permit him to do anything for his father or his mother,

**7:13** nullifying the word of God by your tradition that you have handed down; and you do many similar things like this."

**7:14** And calling the crowd to Himself again, He was saying to them, "Listen to Me, all of you, and understand.

**7:15** There is nothing outside a person that, going into him, can defile him; rather, the things coming out of a person are the things that defile the person."

**7:17** And when He entered a house away from the crowd, His disciples were asking Him about the parable.

**7:18** And He said to them, "Are you also so lacking in understanding? Do you not perceive that whatever goes into a person from outside cannot defile him,

**7:19** because it does not go into his heart, but into his stomach, and goes out into the latrine?"—declaring all foods clean.

**7:20** And He was saying, "What comes out of a person, that is what defiles the person.

**7:21** For from within, out of the heart of people, evil thoughts come out: sexual immoralities, thefts, murders, adulteries,

**7:22** acts of greed, acts of wickedness, deceit, licentiousness, an evil eye, slander, arrogance, foolishness.

**7:23** All these evil things come out from within and defile the person."

**7:24** And getting up from there, He went away into the region of Tyre and Sidon. And entering a house, He wanted no one to know, yet He could not escape notice.

**7:25** Instead, immediately a woman whose little daughter had an unclean spirit heard about Him, and she came and fell down at His feet.

**7:26** Now the woman was Greek, a Syrophoenician by birth; and she kept asking Him to cast the demon out of her daughter.

**7:27** And He was saying to her, "Let the children be filled first, for it is not good to take the bread of the children and throw it to the little dogs."

**7:28** But she answered and said to Him, "Yes, Lord; even the little dogs under the table eat from the crumbs of the children."

**7:29** And He said to her, "Because of this word, go; the demon has gone out of your daughter."

**7:30** And going away to her house, she found the child lying on the bed, and the demon gone out.

**7:31** And going out again from the region of Tyre, He came through Sidon to the Sea of Galilee, through the middle of the region of Decapolis.

**7:32** And they brought to Him a man who was deaf and spoke with difficulty, and they implored Him to lay His hand on him.

**7:33** And taking him aside from the crowd privately, He put His fingers into his ears, and after spitting, He touched his tongue;

**7:34** and looking up to heaven, He sighed and said to him, "Ephphatha," that is, "Be opened!"

**7:35** And his ears were opened, and the bond of his tongue was loosed, and he began speaking plainly.

**7:36** And He ordered them to tell no one; yet the more He ordered them, all the more abundantly they kept proclaiming it.

**7:37** And they were astonished beyond all measure, saying, "He has done all things well; He makes both the deaf hear and the mute speak."

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — Greek pygme literally means 'with the fist,' a debated idiom variously understood as washing with a cupped hand, up to the wrist, or thoroughly; some manuscripts read pykna ('often').
- **7:15** — Verse 16 ('If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear') is absent from the earliest and best manuscripts and is omitted in this critical text.
- **7:19** — The masculine participle katharizon ('declaring clean,' literally 'cleansing') refers back to Jesus ('He said' in v. 18), marking Mark's editorial comment on the implication of the saying.
- **7:22** — 'An evil eye' (ophthalmos poneros) is a literal idiom denoting envy, stinginess, or grudging malice.
