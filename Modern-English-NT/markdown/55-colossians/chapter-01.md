## Text

**1:1** Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, and Timothy the brother,

**1:2** to the saints and faithful brothers in Christ in Colossae: Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:3** We give thanks to God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, always praying for you,

**1:4** having heard of your faith in Christ Jesus and the love that you have toward all the saints

**1:5** because of the hope stored up for you in the heavens, of which you heard beforehand in the word of the truth of the gospel

**1:6** which has come to you, just as in all the world also it is bearing fruit and growing, just as it also is among you, from the day you heard and came to know the grace of God in truth,

**1:7** just as you learned from Epaphras, our beloved fellow slave, who is a faithful servant of Christ on our behalf,

**1:8** who also made known to us your love in the Spirit.

**1:9** For this reason we also, from the day we heard, do not cease praying for you and asking that you may be filled with the full knowledge of His will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding,

**1:10** so as to walk in a manner worthy of the Lord, to please Him in every way, bearing fruit in every good work and growing in the full knowledge of God,

**1:11** being empowered with all power according to the might of His glory, for all endurance and patience with joy,

**1:12** giving thanks to the Father, who qualified you for the share of the inheritance of the saints in the light,

**1:13** who rescued us from the authority of darkness and transferred us into the kingdom of the Son of His love,

**1:14** in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins.

**1:15** He is the image of the invisible God, firstborn of all creation,

**1:16** because in Him all things were created, in the heavens and on the earth, the visible and the invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities; all things have been created through Him and for Him.

**1:17** And He Himself is before all things, and in Him all things hold together,

**1:18** and He Himself is the head of the body, the church. He is the beginning, firstborn from the dead, so that in all things He Himself might hold first place,

**1:19** because God was pleased for all the fullness to dwell in Him,

**1:20** and through Him to reconcile all things to Himself, having made peace through the blood of His cross—through Him, whether the things on the earth or the things in the heavens.

**1:21** And you, who were once alienated and enemies in your mind in evil deeds—

**1:22** yet now you have been reconciled in the body of His flesh through death—to present you holy and blameless and beyond reproach before Him,

**1:23** if indeed you continue in the faith, grounded and steadfast, and not shifting away from the hope of the gospel that you heard, which was proclaimed in all creation under heaven, of which I, Paul, became a servant.

**1:24** Now I rejoice in my sufferings on your behalf, and in my flesh I fill up what is lacking in the afflictions of Christ on behalf of His body, which is the church,

**1:25** of which I became a servant according to the stewardship of God that was given to me for you, to fulfill the word of God,

**1:26** the mystery that has been hidden from the ages and from the generations—but now it has been revealed to His saints,

**1:27** to whom God willed to make known what the wealth of the glory of this mystery is among the Gentiles, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.

**1:28** Him we proclaim, admonishing every person and teaching every person in all wisdom, so that we may present every person mature in Christ.

**1:29** For this I also labor, striving according to His working that works in me with power.

## Translator Notes

- **1:7** — Some Greek manuscripts read "on your behalf" (hyper hymon) instead of "on our behalf" (hyper hemon).
- **1:15** — "Firstborn of all creation" denotes preeminence and supreme rank over all creation rather than being the first created being, as v. 16 immediately explains.
- **1:19** — "God" is supplied as the implied subject of "was pleased," confirmed by the masculine participle "having made peace" in v. 20; alternatively, "all the fullness" may be taken as the grammatical subject ("in Him all the fullness was pleased to dwell").
- **1:22** — The Saints Greek Critical Text reads the passive "you have been reconciled" (apokatellagete), forming a parenthetical break in syntax; other manuscripts read the active "He has reconciled" (apokatellaxen).
- **1:24** — "What is lacking in the afflictions of Christ" does not imply a deficiency in Christ's atoning work, but refers to ongoing missionary sufferings endured in union with Him for the sake of His church.
