## Text

**4:1** Masters, provide what is just and fair to your slaves, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven.

**4:2** Persevere in prayer, keeping watch in it with thanksgiving,

**4:3** praying at the same time for us as well, that God may open for us a door for the word, to speak the mystery of Christ, on account of which I have also been bound,

**4:4** so that I may make it manifest as I ought to speak.

**4:5** Walk in wisdom toward those outside, redeeming the opportunity.

**4:6** Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt, so that you know how you ought to answer each individual.

**4:7** Tychicus, the beloved brother and faithful servant and fellow slave in the Lord, will make known to you all the things concerning me.

**4:8** I have sent him to you for this very purpose, that you may know how things are with us and that he may encourage your hearts,

**4:9** together with Onesimus, the faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you. They will make known to you all the things here.

**4:10** Aristarchus, my fellow prisoner, greets you, and Mark, the cousin of Barnabas (concerning whom you received instructions: if he comes to you, welcome him),

**4:11** and Jesus who is called Justus. Of those who are of the circumcision, these alone are fellow workers for the kingdom of God, and they have become a comfort to me.

**4:12** Epaphras, who is one of you, a slave of Christ Jesus, greets you, always striving on your behalf in his prayers, so that you may stand complete and fully assured in all the will of God.

**4:13** For I bear witness for him that he has much toil on your behalf and for those in Laodicea and those in Hierapolis.

**4:14** Luke, the beloved physician, greets you, as well as Demas.

**4:15** Greet the brothers in Laodicea, and Nympha and the church in her house.

**4:16** And when this letter has been read among you, see to it that it is also read in the church of the Laodiceans, and that you also read the one from Laodicea.

**4:17** And say to Archippus, "See to the ministry that you received in the Lord, so that you fulfill it."

**4:18** The greeting is by my own hand—Paul. Remember my chains. Grace be with you.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Greek uses the same word (kyrios) for both earthly "masters" and the heavenly "Master" (Lord).
- **4:8** — Some manuscripts read "that he may know how things are with you," while the critical text reads "that you may know how things are with us."
- **4:15** — Following the feminine pronoun "her" in the critical text, the name is feminine Nympha; other manuscripts read masculine "his" (Nymphas) or plural "their."
- **4:16** — The letter from Laodicea is not preserved under that name; some scholars identify it with Ephesians (as a circular letter) or with a lost Pauline letter.
