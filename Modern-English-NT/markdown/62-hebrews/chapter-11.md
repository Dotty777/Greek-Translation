## Text

**11:1** Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the proof of realities not seen.

**11:2** For by it the elders were attested.

**11:3** By faith we understand that the ages have been framed by the word of God, so that what is seen has not come into being from things that appear.

**11:4** By faith Abel offered to God a greater sacrifice than Cain, through which he was attested to be righteous, with God testifying concerning his gifts; and through it, though he died, he still speaks.

**11:5** By faith Enoch was transferred so as not to see death, and he was not found because God transferred him; for before his transfer he had been attested to have pleased God.

**11:6** And without faith it is impossible to please Him, for the one approaching God must believe that He exists and that He becomes a rewarder to those who earnestly seek Him.

**11:7** By faith Noah, having been divinely warned about things not yet seen, in reverence constructed an ark for the salvation of his household, through which he condemned the world and became an heir of the righteousness that is according to faith.

**11:8** By faith Abraham, when called, obeyed to go out to a place that he was about to receive as an inheritance; and he went out, not knowing where he was going.

**11:9** By faith he resided as a foreigner in the land of the promise, as in a land not his own, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, the co-heirs of the same promise;

**11:10** for he was waiting for the city that has the foundations, whose designer and maker is God.

**11:11** By faith, together with Sarah herself, he received power for the depositing of seed even past the proper time of age, since he considered the One who promised to be faithful.

**11:12** Therefore also from one man, and him as good as dead, there were born as many as the stars of heaven in multitude, and as the innumerable sand along the shore of the sea.

**11:13** All these died according to faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them from afar and greeted them, and having confessed that they were strangers and temporary residents on the earth.

**11:14** For those who say such things make it plain that they are seeking a homeland.

**11:15** And indeed, if they were keeping in mind that land from which they went out, they would have had an opportunity to return;

**11:16** but as it is, they long for a better one, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed of them, to be called their God, for He prepared a city for them.

**11:17** By faith Abraham, when being tested, offered up Isaac; and the one who had received the promises was offering up his one and only son,

**11:18** to whom it was spoken, "In Isaac seed will be named for you,"

**11:19** having reasoned that God is able even to raise from the dead; from which also he received him back in a figure.

**11:20** By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau, even concerning things to come.

**11:21** By faith Jacob, while dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and worshiped upon the top of his staff.

**11:22** By faith Joseph, nearing his end, made mention of the exodus of the sons of Israel and gave instructions concerning his bones.

**11:23** By faith Moses, after being born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that the child was beautiful, and they did not fear the decree of the king.

**11:24** By faith Moses, having grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter,

**11:25** choosing rather to be mistreated together with the people of God than to have the temporary enjoyment of sin,

**11:26** considering the reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt; for he was looking toward the reward.

**11:27** By faith he left Egypt, not fearing the wrath of the king; for he persevered as seeing the invisible One.

**11:28** By faith he observed the Passover and the pouring out of the blood, so that the one destroying the firstborn would not touch them.

**11:29** By faith they crossed through the Red Sea as through dry land; when the Egyptians made an attempt on it, they were swallowed up.

**11:30** By faith the walls of Jericho fell down after having been encircled for seven days.

**11:31** By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish together with those who were disobedient, having welcomed the spies with peace.

**11:32** And what more should I say? For time will fail me if I tell about Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David and Samuel, and the prophets,

**11:33** who through faith conquered kingdoms, practiced righteousness, obtained promises, shut the mouths of lions,

**11:34** quenched the power of fire, escaped the edges of the sword, were made strong out of weakness, became mighty in war, put foreign armies to flight.

**11:35** Women received back their dead by resurrection; and others were tortured, not accepting release, so that they might obtain a better resurrection.

**11:36** And still others experienced mockings and scourgings, and furthermore chains and imprisonment.

**11:37** They were stoned, they were sawn in two, they were tested, they died by slaughter of the sword; they went about in sheepskins, in goatskins, being destitute, afflicted, mistreated

**11:38** —of whom the world was not worthy—wandering in deserts and mountains and caves and the holes of the earth.

**11:39** And all these, though having been attested through faith, did not receive the promise,

**11:40** since God had provided something better concerning us, so that apart from us they would not be made perfect.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — Greek hypostasis can mean "assurance," "reality," or "foundation," while elenchos denotes "proof" or "conviction."
- **11:11** — The Greek text reads the dative aute Sarra ("with Sarah herself"), making Abraham the subject of "received power" and katabolen spermatos ("depositing of seed") a reference to fathering offspring; other manuscripts read the nominative, making Sarah the subject.
- **11:21** — Follows the Septuagint of Gen 47:31, which vocalized the Hebrew consonantal text as "staff" (matteh) rather than "bed" (mittah).
- **11:37** — The Greek text includes epeirasthesan ("they were tested") after "they were sawn in two," a word omitted in several early manuscripts.
