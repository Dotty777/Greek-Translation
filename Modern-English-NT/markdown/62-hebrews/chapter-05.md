## Text

**5:1** For every high priest taken from among people is appointed on behalf of people in matters pertaining to God, so that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins,

**5:2** being able to deal gently with those who are ignorant and going astray, since he himself is also surrounded by weakness,

**5:3** and because of it he is obligated, just as for the people, so also for himself, to make offerings for sins.

**5:4** And no one takes the honor for himself, but only when called by God, just as Aaron also was.

**5:5** So also Christ did not glorify Himself to become a high priest, but He who spoke to Him did: "You are My Son; today I have fathered You,"

**5:6** just as He also says in another passage, "You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek."

**5:7** In the days of His flesh, having offered up both prayers and supplications with a loud cry and tears to the One able to save Him from death, and having been heard because of His reverence,

**5:8** although He was a Son, He learned obedience from the things He suffered;

**5:9** and having been made perfect, He became the source of eternal salvation to all who obey Him,

**5:10** having been designated by God a high priest according to the order of Melchizedek.

**5:11** Concerning this we have much to say, and it is hard to explain, since you have become sluggish in hearing.

**5:12** For though because of the time you ought to be teachers, you again need someone to teach you the basic elements of the beginning of the oracles of God, and you have come to need milk, not solid food.

**5:13** For everyone who partakes of milk is inexperienced in the word of righteousness, for he is an infant.

**5:14** But solid food is for the mature, for those who through practice have their faculties trained to distinguish both good and evil.

## Translator Notes

- **5:7** — The phrase apo tes eulabeias is debated: it most likely means "because of His reverence (or godly submission)," though some understand it as "saved from fear (or dread)."
- **5:8** — The Greek features a well-known wordplay between emathen ("learned") and epathen ("suffered").
- **5:11** — Greek Peri hou may be taken as neuter ("Concerning this," referring to the subject of Christ's priesthood) or masculine ("Concerning him," referring to Melchizedek).
