## Text

**2:1** Because of this, we must pay all the more attention to the things we have heard, so that we do not drift away.

**2:2** For if the word spoken through angels proved firm, and every transgression and disobedience received a just recompense,

**2:3** how will we escape if we neglect so great a salvation, which, having first begun to be spoken through the Lord, was confirmed to us by those who heard,

**2:4** while God also testified alongside them both by signs and wonders and by various works of power and distributions of the Holy Spirit according to His will?

**2:5** For He did not subject to angels the inhabited world to come, about which we are speaking.

**2:6** But someone has testified somewhere, saying, "What is man, that You remember him, or a son of man, that You care for him?

**2:7** You made him a little lower than the angels; You crowned him with glory and honor,

**2:8** You subjected all things beneath his feet." For in subjecting all things to him, He left nothing unsubjected to him. Yet at present we do not yet see all things subjected to him;

**2:9** but we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels, crowned with glory and honor on account of the suffering of death, so that by God's grace He might taste death on behalf of everyone.

**2:10** For it was fitting for Him, for whom are all things and through whom are all things, in bringing many sons to glory, to perfect the pioneer of their salvation through sufferings.

**2:11** For both He who sanctifies and those who are being sanctified are all from one; for this reason He is not ashamed to call them brothers,

**2:12** saying, "I will proclaim Your name to My brothers; in the midst of the assembly I will sing praise to You";

**2:13** and again, "I will trust in Him"; and again, "Here I am, and the children whom God has given Me."

**2:14** Since then the children share in blood and flesh, He Himself likewise partook of the same things, so that through death He might bring to nothing the one who holds the power of death, that is, the devil,

**2:15** and release all those who through fear of death were subject to slavery throughout their entire life.

**2:16** For surely He does not help angels, but He helps the offspring of Abraham.

**2:17** Therefore He had to be made like His brothers in every way, so that He might become a merciful and faithful high priest in matters pertaining to God, in order to make propitiation for the sins of the people.

**2:18** For because He Himself has suffered when tested, He is able to come to the aid of those who are being tested.

## Translator Notes

- **2:7** — Greek βραχύ τι ("a little") can express either degree ("a little lower," as in Psalm 8:5) or duration ("for a little while," highlighting Christ's temporary humiliation in 2:9).
- **2:10** — Greek ἀρχηγόν ("pioneer") denotes a founder, originator, or trailblazing leader who opens the way for others.
- **2:11** — Greek ἐξ ἑνός ("from one") is deliberately concise and may refer to one Father (God) or one common origin.
- **2:16** — Greek ἐπιλαμβάνεται literally means "takes hold of," used here in the sense of coming to someone's aid; σπέρματος literally means "seed" or "offspring."
