## Text

**1:1** In many parts and in many ways, God, having spoken long ago to the fathers in the prophets,

**1:2** at the end of these days spoke to us in a Son, whom He appointed heir of all things, through whom He also made the ages;

**1:3** who, being the radiance of the glory and the exact impress of His essence, and sustaining all things by the word of His power, having made purification for sins, sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high,

**1:4** having become so much superior to the angels, to the extent that He has inherited a more distinguished name than they.

**1:5** For to which of the angels has He ever said, "You are My Son; today I have fathered You," and again, "I will be to Him a Father, and He will be to Me a Son"?

**1:6** And when He again brings the firstborn into the inhabited world, He says, "And let all the angels of God worship Him."

**1:7** And regarding the angels He says, "He who makes His angels winds, and His ministers a flame of fire,"

**1:8** but regarding the Son, "Your throne, O God, is forever and ever, and the scepter of uprightness is the scepter of His kingdom.

**1:9** You loved righteousness and hated lawlessness; therefore God, Your God, anointed You with the oil of gladness beyond Your companions."

**1:10** And, "You, in the beginning, Lord, laid the foundation of the earth, and the heavens are the works of Your hands;

**1:11** they will perish, but You remain; and they will all grow old like a garment,

**1:12** and like a cloak You will roll them up, like a garment they will also be changed; but You are the same, and Your years will not fail."

**1:13** But to which of the angels has He ever said, "Sit at My right hand until I place Your enemies as a footstool for Your feet"?

**1:14** Are they not all ministering spirits, sent out for service for the sake of those who are going to inherit salvation?

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — Greek τοὺς αἰῶνας literally means "the ages," used here (as in 11:3) for the created order of time and space.
- **1:6** — Greek πάλιν ("again") stands inside the temporal clause ("when He again brings"), though some take it with the main verb ("And again, when He brings").
- **1:7** — Greek πνεύματα can mean either "winds" (matching the Hebrew of Psalm 104:4) or "spirits" (anticipating "ministering spirits" in 1:14).
- **1:8** — Greek ὁ θεός is translated as a vocative ("O God") addressing the Son, though grammatically it can also be read as a predicate nominative ("God is Your throne"). The critical text reads αὐτοῦ ("His kingdom") rather than σου ("Your kingdom").
