## Text

**12:1** Therefore, since we also have so great a cloud of witnesses surrounding us, laying aside every weight and the sin that closely clings, let us run with endurance the race set before us,

**12:2** looking intently to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of faith, who for the joy set before Him endured a cross, despising shame, and has sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.

**12:3** For consider carefully Him who has endured such hostility from sinners against themselves, so that you may not grow weary in your souls, losing heart.

**12:4** You have not yet resisted to the point of blood, struggling against sin,

**12:5** and you have forgotten the exhortation that addresses you as sons: "My son, do not regard lightly the discipline of the Lord, nor lose heart when reproved by Him;

**12:6** for the one whom the Lord loves He disciplines, and He scourges every son whom He receives."

**12:7** Endure for the sake of discipline; God deals with you as with sons. For what son is there whom a father does not discipline?

**12:8** But if you are without discipline, of which all have become sharers, then you are illegitimate children and not sons.

**12:9** Furthermore, we had the fathers of our flesh as those who disciplined us, and we respected them; will we not much rather be subject to the Father of spirits and live?

**12:10** For they disciplined us for a few days according to what seemed best to them, but He does so for our benefit, so that we may share in His holiness.

**12:11** Now all discipline for the present does not seem to be a matter of joy, but of sorrow; yet afterward it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.

**12:12** Therefore straighten up the drooping hands and the weakened knees,

**12:13** and make straight paths for your feet, so that what is lame may not be dislocated, but rather be healed.

**12:14** Pursue peace with everyone, and the sanctification without which no one will see the Lord,

**12:15** watching carefully so that no one falls short of the grace of God, so that no root of bitterness springing up causes trouble and through it many become defiled,

**12:16** so that there be no sexually immoral or godless person like Esau, who in exchange for a single meal sold his own birthright.

**12:17** For you know that afterward, even when he wanted to inherit the blessing, he was rejected, for he found no place for repentance, though he earnestly sought it with tears.

**12:18** For you have not come to what can be touched, and to a blazing fire, and darkness, and gloom, and a tempest,

**12:19** and to the blast of a trumpet and a voice of words, whose hearers begged that no further word be added to them;

**12:20** for they could not bear what was being commanded: "Even if a wild beast touches the mountain, it will be stoned."

**12:21** And—so terrifying was the sight—Moses said, "I am terrified and trembling."

**12:22** But you have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to myriads of angels, in festal gathering,

**12:23** and to the assembly of the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven, and to God, the Judge of all, and to the spirits of the righteous made perfect,

**12:24** and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling that speaks better than Abel.

**12:25** See that you do not refuse the One who is speaking. For if those did not escape when they refused the One who warned them on earth, much less will we escape if we turn away from the One from heaven,

**12:26** whose voice shook the earth then, but now He has promised, saying, "Yet once more I will shake not only the earth, but also the heaven."

**12:27** Now the phrase "Yet once more" indicates the removal of the things being shaken, as of things that have been made, so that the things not being shaken may remain.

**12:28** Therefore, receiving an unshakable kingdom, let us show gratitude, through which we may worship God acceptably with reverence and awe;

**12:29** for indeed our God is a consuming fire.

## Translator Notes

- **12:1** — Greek euperistatos appears only here in the New Testament and may mean "closely clinging," "easily entangling," or "readily surrounding."
- **12:2** — Greek anti here is most commonly rendered "for the sake of" the joy awaiting Him, though some take it as "instead of" the joy He relinquished.
- **12:3** — The Greek text reads the plural reflexive eis heautous ("against themselves," indicating hostility that harms the sinners themselves); other manuscripts read the singular "against Himself."
- **12:22** — Greek panegyrei ("festal gathering") may stand in apposition to "myriads of angels" here or be grouped with "assembly of the firstborn" in v. 23.
- **12:28** — The Greek idiom echomen charin normally means "let us show gratitude," though some understand it as "let us hold fast to grace."
