## Text

**6:1** Therefore, leaving behind the elementary teaching about Christ, let us move forward to maturity, not laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,

**6:2** instruction about washings and the laying on of hands, and about the resurrection of the dead and eternal judgment.

**6:3** And this we will do, if indeed God permits.

**6:4** For it is impossible, in the case of those who have once been enlightened, and have tasted the heavenly gift, and have become partakers of the Holy Spirit,

**6:5** and have tasted the good word of God and the powers of the coming age,

**6:6** and have fallen away, to renew them again to repentance, since they crucify the Son of God again for themselves and expose Him to public disgrace.

**6:7** For ground that drinks the rain which often falls on it, and produces vegetation useful to those for whose sake it is also cultivated, receives a blessing from God;

**6:8** but if it bears thorns and thistles, it is rejected and near to a curse, and its end is burning.

**6:9** Yet concerning you, beloved, we are convinced of better things, things that accompany salvation, even though we speak in this way.

**6:10** For God is not unjust so as to forget your work and the love that you have shown toward His name, having served the saints and continuing to serve them.

**6:11** And we desire that each one of you show the same diligence toward the full assurance of hope until the end,

**6:12** so that you may not become sluggish, but imitators of those who through faith and patience inherit the promises.

**6:13** For when God made a promise to Abraham, since He had no one greater by whom to swear, He swore by Himself,

**6:14** saying, "Surely, blessing I will bless you and multiplying I will multiply you."

**6:15** And thus, having waited patiently, he obtained the promise.

**6:16** For people swear by one greater than themselves, and for them an oath serves as confirmation, putting an end to every dispute.

**6:17** Accordingly, God, desiring all the more to demonstrate to the heirs of the promise the unchangeableness of His purpose, intervened with an oath,

**6:18** so that through two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we who have fled for refuge may have strong encouragement to take hold of the hope set before us.

**6:19** This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both secure and steadfast, and entering into the inner place behind the curtain,

**6:20** where Jesus entered as a forerunner on our behalf, having become a high priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — The Greek text reads the accusative didachen ("instruction") in apposition to "foundation" in 6:1, while some manuscripts read the genitive didaches. Plural baptismon typically denotes ceremonial washings rather than exclusively Christian baptism.
- **6:6** — Greek anastaurountas can mean either "crucifying again" or simply "crucifying," and its force may be causal ("since") or temporal ("while").
- **6:14** — Greek Ei men ("Surely") with the cognate participles ("blessing I will bless... multiplying I will multiply") preserves the Septuagint rendering of the emphatic Hebrew infinitive absolute in Genesis 22:17.
- **6:17** — Greek emesiteusen literally means "intervened" or "acted as mediator," expressing how God guaranteed His promise by interposing an oath.
