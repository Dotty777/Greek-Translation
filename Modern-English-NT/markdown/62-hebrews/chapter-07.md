## Text

**7:1** For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of God Most High, who met Abraham as he returned from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him,

**7:2** to whom Abraham also apportioned a tenth of everything, first translated as king of righteousness, and then also king of Salem, which is king of peace,

**7:3** without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, but made like the Son of God, remains a priest continually.

**7:4** Now consider how great this man was, to whom Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth from the choicest spoils.

**7:5** And those of the sons of Levi who receive the priestly office have a commandment to collect a tenth from the people according to the law, that is, from their brothers, even though they have come from the loins of Abraham.

**7:6** Yet the one whose genealogy is not from them received a tenth from Abraham and blessed the one who held the promises.

**7:7** Now beyond any dispute, the lesser is blessed by the greater.

**7:8** And here men who die receive tenths, but there one about whom it is testified that he lives.

**7:9** And, so to speak, through Abraham even Levi, who receives tenths, paid a tenth,

**7:10** for he was still in the loins of his father when Melchizedek met him.

**7:11** Now if perfection were through the Levitical priesthood (for on the basis of it the people received the law), what further need would there be for another priest to arise according to the order of Melchizedek, and not be designated according to the order of Aaron?

**7:12** For when the priesthood is changed, of necessity a change of law also takes place.

**7:13** For He concerning whom these things are spoken belongs to another tribe, from which no one has served at the altar.

**7:14** For it is clear that our Lord has sprung from Judah, a tribe with reference to which Moses spoke nothing concerning priests.

**7:15** And it is even more abundantly clear if another priest arises according to the likeness of Melchizedek,

**7:16** who has become one not according to a law of a fleshly commandment, but according to the power of an indestructible life,

**7:17** for it is testified, "You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek."

**7:18** For on the one hand, a setting aside of a previous commandment takes place because of its weakness and uselessness

**7:19** (for the law made nothing perfect), and on the other hand, the bringing in of a better hope, through which we draw near to God.

**7:20** And inasmuch as it was not without an oath (for they indeed became priests without an oath,

**7:21** but He with an oath through the One who says to Him, "The Lord has sworn and will not change His mind, 'You are a priest forever'"),

**7:22** by so much also Jesus has become the guarantor of a better covenant.

**7:23** And those priests became many in number because they were prevented by death from continuing,

**7:24** but He, because He remains forever, holds His priesthood permanently.

**7:25** Therefore He is also able to save completely those who approach God through Him, since He always lives to intercede for them.

**7:26** For such a high priest was indeed fitting for us: holy, innocent, undefiled, separated from sinners, and raised higher than the heavens,

**7:27** who does not have need daily, like those high priests, to offer up sacrifices first for His own sins and then for those of the people, for He did this once for all when He offered up Himself.

**7:28** For the law appoints men who have weakness as high priests, but the word of the oath, which came after the law, appoints a Son who has been made perfect forever.

## Translator Notes

- **7:21** — The Greek text here follows the shorter reading, omitting "according to the order of Melchizedek" found in later manuscripts.
- **7:24** — The Greek adjective ἀπαράβατος ("permanently") can mean either "unchanging" or "non-transferable," indicating a priesthood that does not pass to a successor.
- **7:25** — The phrase εἰς τὸ παντελές ("completely") can express both completeness ("utterly") and duration ("for all time").
