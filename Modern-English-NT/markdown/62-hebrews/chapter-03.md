## Text

**3:1** Therefore, holy brothers, sharers in a heavenly calling, consider the apostle and high priest of our confession, Jesus,

**3:2** who was faithful to the One who appointed Him, just as Moses also was in His house.

**3:3** For He has been counted worthy of greater glory than Moses, by as much as the one who built the house has greater honor than the house itself.

**3:4** For every house is built by someone, but the One who built all things is God.

**3:5** Now Moses was faithful in all His house as a servant, for a testimony to the things that were to be spoken,

**3:6** but Christ was faithful as a Son over His house; whose house we are, if we hold fast our boldness and the boast of our hope.

**3:7** Therefore, just as the Holy Spirit says, "Today, if you hear His voice,

**3:8** do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion, on the day of testing in the wilderness,

**3:9** where your fathers put Me to the test in trial and saw My works

**3:10** for forty years. Therefore I was indignant with this generation and said, 'They always go astray in their heart, and they themselves have not known My ways,'

**3:11** as I swore in My wrath, 'They will never enter My rest.'"

**3:12** Watch out, brothers, so that there will never be in any one of you an evil heart of unbelief in falling away from the living God;

**3:13** instead, exhort one another every day, as long as it is called "Today," so that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.

**3:14** For we have become sharers in Christ, if indeed we hold fast the beginning of our confidence firm until the end,

**3:15** while it is said, "Today, if you hear His voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion."

**3:16** For who were those who heard and yet rebelled? Was it not indeed all those who came out of Egypt through Moses?

**3:17** And with whom was He indignant for forty years? Was it not with those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness?

**3:18** And to whom did He swear that they would not enter His rest, except to those who disobeyed?

**3:19** And so we see that they were unable to enter because of unbelief.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — Greek τῷ ποιήσαντι αὐτόν literally means "the One who made Him," an idiom for appointing someone to an office (cf. 1 Samuel 12:6 LXX; Mark 3:14).
- **3:9** — The critical text reads ἐπείρασαν ... ἐν δοκιμασίᾳ ("put [Me] to the test in trial"), without an explicit pronoun "Me," rather than the later variant "tested Me and proved Me."
- **3:11** — Greek Εἰ εἰσελεύσονται literally reads "If they will enter," reflecting a Hebrew oath formula that expresses an emphatic negation ("They will never enter").
- **3:14** — Greek ὑπόστασις here denotes firm "confidence" or "assurance," contrasting with its sense of "essence" or "essential being" in 1:3.
