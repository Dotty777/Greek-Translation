## Text

**4:1** Yet the Spirit expressly says that in later times some will depart from the faith, paying heed to misleading spirits and teachings of demons,

**4:2** through the hypocrisy of liars, who have been seared in their own conscience,

**4:3** who forbid marriage and require abstinence from foods that God created to be partaken of with thanksgiving by those who believe and have come to know the truth.

**4:4** For every created thing of God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving,

**4:5** for it is sanctified through God's word and prayer.

**4:6** By setting these things before the brothers, you will be a good servant of Christ Jesus, being nourished on the words of the faith and of the good teaching that you have closely followed.

**4:7** But refuse profane and old wives' myths. Instead, train yourself for godliness;

**4:8** for bodily training is beneficial for a little, while godliness is beneficial for all things, holding the promise of the present life and of the life to come.

**4:9** Trustworthy is the saying and worthy of all acceptance.

**4:10** For toward this goal we labor and strive, because we have fixed our hope on the living God, who is the Savior of all people, especially of believers.

**4:11** Command and teach these things.

**4:12** Let no one think lightly of your youth, but become an example to the believers in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity.

**4:13** Until I come, give attention to the reading, to exhortation, to teaching.

**4:14** Do not neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you through prophecy with the laying on of the hands of the council of elders.

**4:15** Practice these things; be absorbed in them, so that your progress may be evident to all.

**4:16** Pay close attention to yourself and to the teaching; persevere in these things, for by doing this you will save both yourself and those who listen to you.

## Translator Notes

- **4:2** — "Seared" renders a Greek verb literally meaning "branded with a hot iron," denoting a conscience either cauterized into insensitivity or marked with a brand of ownership.
- **4:3** — Greek employs a single participle ("forbidding") over both "to marry" and "to abstain from foods," implying a contrasting verb such as "requiring" for the second clause.
- **4:10** — The critical text reads "we strive" (ἀγωνιζόμεθα), whereas some later manuscripts read "we suffer reproach" (ὀνειδιζόμεθα).
- **4:13** — "The reading" (τῇ ἀναγνώσει) most likely refers to the public reading of Scripture in the assembled church.
