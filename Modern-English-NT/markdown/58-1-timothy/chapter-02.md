## Text

**2:1** Therefore I urge, first of all, that petitions, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings be offered on behalf of all people,

**2:2** for kings and for all those who are in high authority, so that we may lead a tranquil and quiet life in all godliness and dignity.

**2:3** This is good and acceptable in the sight of God our Savior,

**2:4** who desires all people to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.

**2:5** For there is one God, and one mediator as well between God and human beings, the man Christ Jesus,

**2:6** who gave Himself as a ransom on behalf of all, the testimony at its proper times.

**2:7** For this I was appointed a herald and an apostle—I am speaking the truth in Christ, I am not lying—a teacher of the Gentiles in faith and truth.

**2:8** Therefore I want the men in every place to pray, lifting up holy hands without anger and disputing.

**2:9** Likewise, that women adorn themselves in respectable attire, with modesty and self-control, not with braided hair and gold, or pearls, or costly clothing,

**2:10** but with what is proper for women professing reverence for God, through good works.

**2:11** Let a woman learn in quietness, in full submission.

**2:12** And I do not permit a woman to teach or to exercise authority over a man, but to be in quietness.

**2:13** For Adam was formed first, then Eve;

**2:14** and Adam was not deceived, but the woman, having been thoroughly deceived, fell into transgression.

**2:15** Yet she will be saved through childbearing, if they continue in faith and love and sanctification with self-control.

## Translator Notes

- **2:7** — Textual note: the Saints Greek Critical Text includes "in Christ" (ἐν Χριστῷ), which is omitted in several early manuscripts.
- **2:12** — The verb αὐθεντεῖν occurs only here in the New Testament; its exact nuance is debated, ranging from "exercise authority over" to "domineer over."
- **2:15** — "Saved through childbearing" (διὰ τῆς τεκνογονίας) is variously understood as spiritual perseverance in family faithfulness, physical preservation in childbirth, or salvation through the birth of the Messiah; note also the Greek shift from singular ("she") to plural ("they").
