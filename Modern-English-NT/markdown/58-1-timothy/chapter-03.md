## Text

**3:1** Trustworthy is this saying: if anyone aspires to oversight, he desires a good work.

**3:2** Therefore the overseer must be above reproach, a husband of one wife, sober-minded, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach,

**3:3** not given to wine, not violent, but gentle, not quarrelsome, free from the love of money,

**3:4** managing his own household well, having children in submission with all dignity;

**3:5** (but if someone does not know how to manage his own household, how will he care for the church of God?)

**3:6** not a new convert, so that he may not become puffed up with pride and fall into the condemnation of the devil.

**3:7** Moreover, he must also have a good testimony from those outside, so that he may not fall into disgrace and the snare of the devil.

**3:8** Deacons likewise must be dignified, not double-tongued, not devoted to much wine, not greedy for dishonest gain,

**3:9** holding the mystery of the faith with a pure conscience.

**3:10** And let these also be tested first; then let them serve as deacons, being blameless.

**3:11** Women likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, sober-minded, faithful in all things.

**3:12** Let deacons be husbands of one wife, managing their children and their own households well.

**3:13** For those who have served well as deacons gain for themselves a good standing and great boldness in the faith that is in Christ Jesus.

**3:14** I am writing these things to you, hoping to come to you soon;

**3:15** but if I am delayed, so that you may know how one ought to conduct oneself in the household of God, which is the church of the living God, a pillar and foundation of the truth.

**3:16** And undeniably great is the mystery of godliness: He who was revealed in the flesh, was vindicated in the Spirit, appeared to angels, was proclaimed among the nations, was believed on in the world, was taken up in glory.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — Literally "a man of one woman" (μιᾶς γυναικὸς ἄνδρα), commonly understood as requiring marital fidelity; the same phrase appears for deacons in v. 12.
- **3:11** — The Greek noun γυναῖκας ("women") lacks a possessive pronoun here and is debated as referring either to female deacons (deaconesses) or to the wives of deacons.
- **3:16** — Textual note: the Saints Greek Critical Text reads the relative pronoun "He who" (Ὃς), whereas later manuscripts read "God" (θεός). The six parallel clauses are widely recognized as an early Christian hymn or confession. The anarthrous phrase ἐν πνεύματι ("in the Spirit") may denote agency ("by the Spirit") or the spiritual realm in contrast to "in the flesh" ("in spirit," cf. 1 Pet 3:18).
