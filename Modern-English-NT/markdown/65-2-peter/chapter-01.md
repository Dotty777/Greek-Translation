## Text

**1:1** Simeon Peter, a slave and apostle of Jesus Christ, to those who have received a faith of equal worth with ours by the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ:

**1:2** May grace and peace be multiplied to you in the full knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord.

**1:3** Seeing that His divine power has granted to us all things pertaining to life and godliness, through the full knowledge of the One who called us by His own glory and virtue,

**1:4** through which He has granted to us the precious and very great promises, so that through these you may become sharers in the divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world through desire.

**1:5** Now for this very reason also, applying all diligence, supply virtue in your faith, and in your virtue, knowledge,

**1:6** and in your knowledge, self-control, and in your self-control, endurance, and in your endurance, godliness,

**1:7** and in your godliness, brotherly affection, and in your brotherly affection, love.

**1:8** For if these qualities belong to you and increase, they render you neither idle nor unfruitful toward the full knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:9** For the one who lacks these things is blind, being shortsighted, having forgotten the cleansing of his former sins.

**1:10** Therefore, brothers, be all the more diligent to make your calling and election firm; for by doing these things you will never stumble.

**1:11** For in this way the entrance into the eternal kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ will be richly supplied to you.

**1:12** Therefore I intend always to remind you about these things, even though you know them and are established in the truth present with you.

**1:13** And I consider it right, as long as I am in this tent, to stir you up by way of reminder,

**1:14** knowing that the laying aside of my tent is imminent, just as our Lord Jesus Christ also made clear to me.

**1:15** And I will also be diligent so that at every time after my departure you may be able to call these things to remembrance.

**1:16** For we did not follow skillfully crafted myths when we made known to you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ; rather, we were eyewitnesses of His majesty.

**1:17** For He received honor and glory from God the Father when such a voice was brought to Him by the Majestic Glory: "This is My Son, My beloved, with whom I am well pleased."

**1:18** And we ourselves heard this voice brought from heaven when we were with Him on the holy mountain.

**1:19** And we have the prophetic word made more certain, to which you do well to pay attention as to a lamp shining in a murky place, until day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts;

**1:20** knowing this first of all, that no prophecy of Scripture comes from one's own interpretation;

**1:21** for no prophecy was ever brought by human will, but men spoke from God as they were borne along by the Holy Spirit.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Greek "tou theou hemon kai soteros Iesou Christou" places a single article before both nouns, identifying Jesus Christ as both "our God and Savior," though a few interpreters take it as referring to God and the Savior Jesus Christ separately.
- **1:19** — Greek "bebaioteron" can be read predicatively ("made more certain" or confirmed by the Transfiguration) or attributively ("we have an even more reliable prophetic word").
- **1:20** — Greek "idias epilyseos" ("one's own interpretation") is debated: in light of v. 21, many take it to mean a prophet's own origination or explanation of a revelation, while others understand it as an individual's private interpretation.
