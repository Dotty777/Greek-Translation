## Text

**2:1** Yet false prophets also arose among the people, just as false teachers will be among you as well, who will covertly introduce destructive heresies, even denying the Master who purchased them, bringing swift ruin upon themselves.

**2:2** And many will follow their sensual ways, and because of them the way of truth will be reviled.

**2:3** And in greed they will exploit you with fabricated words; their judgment from long ago is not idle, and their destruction is not asleep.

**2:4** For if God did not spare angels when they sinned, but cast them into Tartarus and handed them over to pits of gloomy darkness to be kept for judgment;

**2:5** and did not spare the ancient world, but protected Noah, a herald of righteousness, along with seven others, when He brought a flood upon the world of ungodly people;

**2:6** and by reducing the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah to ashes condemned them to ruin, having set them as an example for those who are going to live ungodly lives;

**2:7** and rescued righteous Lot, who was distressed by the sensual conduct of lawless people—

**2:8** for by sight and hearing, that righteous man, while dwelling among them day after day, tormented his righteous soul over their lawless deeds—

**2:9** then the Lord knows how to rescue the godly from trial, and to keep the unrighteous under punishment for the day of judgment,

**2:10** and especially those who walk after the flesh in the desire of defilement and despise authority. Bold and self-willed, they do not tremble as they revile glorious ones,

**2:11** whereas angels, though greater in strength and power, do not bring a reviling judgment against them before the Lord.

**2:12** But these people, like unreasoning animals, born by nature for capture and destruction, reviling things of which they are ignorant, will also be destroyed in their own destruction,

**2:13** suffering wrong as the wages of wrongdoing. They consider it pleasure to revel in the daytime; they are stains and blemishes, reveling in their deceptions while feasting with you,

**2:14** having eyes full of adultery and never ceasing from sin, enticing unstable souls, having a heart trained in greed—children of a curse.

**2:15** Abandoning the straight way, they have gone astray, having followed the way of Balaam, son of Bosor, who loved the wages of wrongdoing,

**2:16** but he received a rebuke for his own transgression: a speechless donkey, speaking with a human voice, restrained the madness of the prophet.

**2:17** These people are waterless springs and mists driven by a storm, for whom the gloom of darkness has been reserved.

**2:18** For speaking arrogant words of emptiness, they entice through the desires of the flesh, by sensual ways, those who are barely escaping from the ones who live in error,

**2:19** promising them freedom while they themselves are slaves of corruption; for by whatever a person is overcome, to this he has been enslaved.

**2:20** For if, after having escaped the defilements of the world through the full knowledge of the Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, they are again entangled in these things and overcome, the last state has become worse for them than the first.

**2:21** For it would have been better for them not to have known the way of righteousness than, having known it, to turn back from the holy commandment handed down to them.

**2:22** What the true proverb says has happened to them: "A dog returns to its own vomit," and, "A washed sow to wallowing in the mire."

## Translator Notes

- **2:4** — The verb "tartarosas" ("cast into Tartarus") occurs only here in the New Testament, referring to the deepest realm of punishment. The Greek text reads "seirois" ("pits" or "caverns"), whereas many later manuscripts read "seirais" ("chains").
- **2:10** — Greek "doxas" (literally "glories") refers to exalted spiritual beings or angelic authorities, as reflected in the contrast with angels in v. 11.
- **2:13** — The Greek text reads "adikoumenoi" ("suffering wrong" or "harm"), forming a wordplay with "adikias" ("wrongdoing"), while some manuscripts read "komioumenoi" ("receiving"). It also reads "apatais" ("deceptions") rather than the variant "agapais" ("love feasts").
- **2:15** — Greek "Bosor" corresponds to Hebrew "Beor," the father of Balaam in Numbers 22:5.
