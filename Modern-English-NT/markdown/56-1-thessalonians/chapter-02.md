## Text

**2:1** For you yourselves know, brothers, that our entrance to you has not been in vain,

**2:2** but having previously suffered and been abusively treated, as you know, in Philippi, we became bold in our God to speak to you the gospel of God in much struggle.

**2:3** For our exhortation is not from error, nor from impurity, nor in deceit;

**2:4** but just as we have been approved by God to be entrusted with the gospel, so we speak, not as pleasing people, but God, who examines our hearts.

**2:5** For we never came with a word of flattery, as you know, nor with a pretext for greed—God is witness—

**2:6** nor seeking glory from people, neither from you nor from others,

**2:7** even though we could have been a burden as apostles of Christ. Yet we became infants among you. Just as a nursing mother cherishes her own children,

**2:8** so, longing deeply for you, we were pleased to share with you not only the gospel of God, but also our own lives, because you had become dear to us.

**2:9** For you remember, brothers, our labor and hardship: working night and day so as not to burden any of you, we proclaimed to you the gospel of God.

**2:10** You are witnesses, and so is God, how devoutly and righteously and blamelessly we conducted ourselves toward you who believe,

**2:11** just as you know how we dealt with each one of you, as a father with his own children,

**2:12** exhorting, encouraging, and charging you to walk worthily of God, who calls you into His own kingdom and glory.

**2:13** And for this reason we also give thanks to God constantly, that when you received the word of God that you heard from us, you welcomed it not as the word of people, but as what it truly is, the word of God, which is also at work in you who believe.

**2:14** For you, brothers, became imitators of the churches of God that are in Judea in Christ Jesus, because you also suffered the same things from your own countrymen just as they did from the Jews,

**2:15** who killed both the Lord Jesus and the prophets, and drove us out, and do not please God, and are opposed to all people,

**2:16** hindering us from speaking to the Gentiles so that they may be saved, so as always to fill up their sins. But the wrath has come upon them completely.

**2:17** Now we, brothers, having been bereaved of you for a brief time, in presence, not in heart, were all the more eager to see your face with great desire.

**2:18** For we wanted to come to you—I, Paul, both once and twice—yet Satan blocked us.

**2:19** For what is our hope or joy or crown of boasting—is it not indeed you—before our Lord Jesus at His coming?

**2:20** For you are our glory and joy.

## Translator Notes

- **2:7** — The phrase "been a burden" (ἐν βάρει εἶναι) can mean either imposing a financial burden (cf. 2:9) or asserting apostolic weight and authority. The earliest manuscripts read "infants" (νήπιοι), whereas other manuscripts read "gentle" (ἤπιοι), a difference of one Greek letter.
- **2:13** — The relative pronoun "which" (ὅς) agrees grammatically with "the word" (λόγον), though some understand it as referring to "God" ("who is also at work").
- **2:16** — The phrase "completely" (εἰς τέλος) is variously understood as "completely / to the utmost," "at last," or "forever."
- **2:19** — "Coming" translates παρουσία, a term used in the ancient world for the official arrival or visit of a ruler and applied in the New Testament to Christ's return.
