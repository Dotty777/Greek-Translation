## Text

**1:1** Paul, a slave of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, according to the faith of God's chosen ones and the knowledge of the truth that is in accord with godliness,

**1:2** in hope of eternal life, which God, who never lies, promised before eternal ages,

**1:3** and at the proper times manifested His word through the proclamation with which I was entrusted according to the command of God our Savior,

**1:4** to Titus, a genuine child in a shared faith: Grace and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior.

**1:5** For this reason I left you behind in Crete, so that you would set in order the things remaining and appoint elders in every city, just as I directed you:

**1:6** if anyone is blameless, the husband of one wife, having faithful children, not under an accusation of debauchery or insubordinate.

**1:7** For the overseer must be blameless as God's steward: not self-willed, not quick-tempered, not given to wine, not violent, not greedy for shameful gain,

**1:8** but hospitable, a lover of what is good, prudent, righteous, devout, self-controlled,

**1:9** holding firmly to the trustworthy word according to the instruction, so that he may be able both to exhort in sound teaching and to reprove those who contradict.

**1:10** For there are many who are insubordinate, empty talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision,

**1:11** who must be silenced, since they are overturning entire households by teaching things they ought not for the sake of shameful gain.

**1:12** One of them, a prophet of their own, said, "Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons."

**1:13** This testimony is true. For this reason reprove them sharply, so that they may be sound in the faith,

**1:14** not paying attention to Jewish myths and the commands of people who turn away from the truth.

**1:15** All things are pure to the pure, but to those who are defiled and unbelieving nothing is pure; instead, both their mind and their conscience have been defiled.

**1:16** They profess to know God, but by their deeds they deny Him, being detestable and disobedient and disqualified for every good work.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Greek kata ('according to') may here denote either standard ('in accordance with') or goal ('for the faith of God's chosen ones').
- **1:6** — Greek tekna ... pista ('faithful children') is debated and can also be translated 'believing children.'
- **1:12** — The saying is attributed in antiquity to the Cretan poet Epimenides (6th century BC); 'lazy gluttons' is literally 'idle bellies.'
