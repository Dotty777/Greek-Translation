## Text

**3:1** Remind them to submit to rulers, to authorities, to be obedient, to be ready for every good work,

**3:2** to malign no one, to be peaceable and considerate, showing all gentleness toward all people.

**3:3** For we ourselves were also once foolish, disobedient, led astray, enslaved to various desires and pleasures, spending our lives in malice and envy, hateful, hating one another.

**3:4** But when the kindness and love for humanity of God our Savior appeared,

**3:5** not by works in righteousness that we ourselves did, but according to His mercy He saved us, through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit,

**3:6** whom He poured out abundantly on us through Jesus Christ our Savior,

**3:7** so that, having been justified by His grace, we might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life.

**3:8** The saying is trustworthy, and concerning these things I want you to insist confidently, so that those who have believed in God may be careful to devote themselves to good deeds. These things are good and profitable for people.

**3:9** But avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, strifes, and quarrels about the law, for they are unprofitable and futile.

**3:10** Reject a factious person after a first and second warning,

**3:11** knowing that such a person is warped and is sinning, being self-condemned.

**3:12** When I send Artemas to you, or Tychicus, make every effort to come to me at Nicopolis, for I have decided to spend the winter there.

**3:13** Diligently help Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their way, so that nothing may be lacking for them.

**3:14** And let our own people also learn to devote themselves to good deeds for pressing needs, so that they may not be unfruitful.

**3:15** All who are with me greet you. Greet those who love us in faith. Grace be with all of you.

## Translator Notes

- **3:5** — The phrasing may be construed either as two parallel means ('through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit') or with both genitives dependent on 'washing.'
- **3:8** — The Greek idiom kalon ergon proistasthai ('devote themselves to good deeds'; also in 3:14) can refer both to practicing good deeds and, in some contexts, to engaging in honorable occupations.
- **3:10** — Greek hairetikon ('factious' or 'divisive') denotes one who causes divisions or factions rather than the later technical sense of 'heretical.'
