## Text

**2:1** But as for you, speak the things that are fitting for sound teaching.

**2:2** Older men are to be temperate, dignified, prudent, sound in faith, in love, in endurance.

**2:3** Older women likewise are to be reverent in demeanor, not slanderers nor enslaved to much wine, teachers of what is good,

**2:4** so that they may train the young women to love their husbands and to love their children,

**2:5** to be prudent, pure, workers at home, good, submitting to their own husbands, so that the word of God may not be maligned.

**2:6** Likewise, exhort the younger men to be prudent;

**2:7** in all things showing yourself to be a pattern of good deeds, in teaching displaying integrity, dignity,

**2:8** and sound speech that cannot be condemned, so that the opponent may be put to shame, having nothing bad to say about us.

**2:9** Slaves are to submit to their own masters in everything, to be well-pleasing, not talking back,

**2:10** not pilfering, but showing all good faithfulness, so that they may adorn the teaching of God our Savior in everything.

**2:11** For God's grace has appeared, bringing salvation to all people,

**2:12** training us so that, renouncing ungodliness and worldly desires, we may live prudently, righteously, and godly in the present age,

**2:13** awaiting the blessed hope and appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ,

**2:14** who gave Himself on our behalf so that He might redeem us from all lawlessness and purify for Himself a people of His own possession, zealous for good deeds.

**2:15** Speak these things, and exhort and reprove with all authority. Let no one disregard you.

## Translator Notes

- **2:5** — Greek oikourgous ('workers at home') is the reading of the earliest manuscripts, whereas later manuscripts read oikourous ('keepers at home').
- **2:11** — The phrase 'to all people' may modify either the adjective 'bringing salvation' or the verb 'has appeared' ('has appeared to all people, bringing salvation').
- **2:13** — Because a single Greek article governs both 'great God' and 'Savior,' most grammarians understand both titles as referring to Jesus Christ; some alternatively read 'of the great God and of our Savior Jesus Christ.'
