## Text

**20:1** "For the kingdom of heaven is like a man, a master of a house, who went out at dawn to hire laborers for his vineyard.

**20:2** And having agreed with the laborers for a denarius for the day, he sent them into his vineyard.

**20:3** And going out about the third hour, he saw others standing idle in the marketplace;

**20:4** and to those he said, 'You also go into the vineyard, and whatever is right I will give you.'

**20:5** And they went. Going out again about the sixth and the ninth hour, he did likewise.

**20:6** And about the eleventh hour he went out and found others standing, and he said to them, 'Why have you been standing here all day idle?'

**20:7** They said to him, 'Because no one hired us.' He said to them, 'You also go into the vineyard.'

**20:8** When evening came, the owner of the vineyard said to his foreman, 'Call the laborers and pay them their wages, beginning from the last to the first.'

**20:9** And those from about the eleventh hour came and received a denarius each.

**20:10** And when the first came, they supposed that they would receive more; yet they themselves also received a denarius each.

**20:11** And when they received it, they were grumbling against the master of the house,

**20:12** saying, 'These last ones worked one hour, and you have made them equal to us who have borne the burden of the day and the scorching heat.'

**20:13** But he answered one of them and said, 'Friend, I am doing you no wrong. Did you not agree with me for a denarius?

**20:14** Take what is yours and go; yet I wish to give to this last man just as also to you.

**20:15** Or is it not lawful for me to do what I wish with what is mine? Or is your eye evil because I am good?'

**20:16** So the last will be first, and the first, last."

**20:17** And as Jesus was going up to Jerusalem, He took the twelve disciples aside privately, and on the way He said to them,

**20:18** "Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and the Son of Man will be handed over to the chief priests and scribes, and they will condemn Him to death,

**20:19** and will hand Him over to the Gentiles to mock and flog and crucify Him, and on the third day He will rise."

**20:20** Then the mother of the sons of Zebedee came up to Him with her sons, bowing down and asking something from Him.

**20:21** And He said to her, "What do you wish?" She said to Him, "Command that these two sons of mine may sit, one on Your right and one on Your left, in Your kingdom."

**20:22** But Jesus answered and said, "You do not know what you are asking. Are you able to drink the cup that I am about to drink?" They said to Him, "We are able."

**20:23** He said to them, "My cup you will indeed drink, yet to sit on My right and on My left is not Mine to give, but it is for those for whom it has been prepared by My Father."

**20:24** And when the ten heard this, they became indignant about the two brothers.

**20:25** But Jesus called them to Himself and said, "You know that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and the great ones exercise authority over them.

**20:26** It shall not be this way among you; rather, whoever wishes to become great among you shall be your servant,

**20:27** and whoever wishes to be first among you shall be your slave;

**20:28** just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life as a ransom in place of many."

**20:29** And as they were going out from Jericho, a large crowd followed Him.

**20:30** And look, two blind men sitting beside the road, having heard that Jesus was passing by, cried out, saying, "Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!"

**20:31** And the crowd rebuked them so that they would be silent; yet they cried out all the louder, saying, "Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!"

**20:32** And stopping, Jesus called them and said, "What do you want Me to do for you?"

**20:33** They said to Him, "Lord, that our eyes may be opened."

**20:34** And moved with compassion, Jesus touched their eyes, and immediately they regained their sight and followed Him.

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — A denarius was a Roman silver coin that served as the standard daily wage for a common laborer.
- **20:3** — Daytime hours were counted from sunrise (around 6:00 a.m.), placing the third, sixth, ninth, and eleventh hours at roughly 9:00 a.m., noon, 3:00 p.m., and 5:00 p.m.
- **20:15** — "Is your eye evil" is a Semitic idiom denoting envy or stinginess, contrasted here with the owner's "good" (generous) character.
- **20:28** — Greek lytron denotes a ransom price paid for release, and the preposition anti ("in place of" or "for") expresses substitution.
