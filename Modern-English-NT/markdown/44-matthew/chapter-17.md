## Text

**17:1** And after six days, Jesus took along Peter and James and his brother John, and led them up onto a high mountain by themselves.

**17:2** And He was transfigured in front of them, and His face shone like the sun, while His garments became white as the light.

**17:3** And look, Moses and Elijah appeared to them, conversing with Him.

**17:4** Then Peter answered and said to Jesus, "Lord, it is good for us to be here; if You wish, I will make three tents here—one for You, and one for Moses, and one for Elijah."

**17:5** While he was still speaking, look, a bright cloud overshadowed them, and look, a voice came out of the cloud, saying, "This is My Son, the Beloved, in whom I delight; listen to Him."

**17:6** When the disciples heard it, they fell down on their faces and were greatly afraid.

**17:7** And Jesus approached, touched them, and said, "Rise, and do not be afraid."

**17:8** And lifting up their eyes, they saw no one except Jesus Himself alone.

**17:9** And as they were coming down from the mountain, Jesus commanded them, saying, "Tell no one the vision until the Son of Man rises from the dead."

**17:10** And His disciples questioned Him, saying, "Why then do the scribes say that Elijah must come first?"

**17:11** And He answered and said, "Elijah indeed is coming and will restore all things;

**17:12** but I tell you that Elijah has already come, and they did not recognize him, but did to him whatever they wished. In the same way, the Son of Man is also about to suffer from them."

**17:13** Then the disciples understood that He had spoken to them concerning John the Baptist.

**17:14** And when they came to the crowd, a man approached Him, kneeling before Him

**17:15** and saying, "Lord, have mercy on my son, because he has seizures and is ill; for often he falls into the fire and often into the water.

**17:16** And I brought him to Your disciples, and they were not able to heal him."

**17:17** And Jesus answered and said, "O unbelieving and twisted generation, how long shall I be with you? How long shall I bear with you? Bring him here to Me."

**17:18** And Jesus rebuked it, and the demon came out of him; and the boy was healed from that hour.

**17:19** Then the disciples approached Jesus privately and said, "Why were we not able to cast it out?"

**17:20** And He told them, "Because of your little faith. For truly I say to you, if you have faith like a mustard seed, you will say to this mountain, 'Move from here to over there,' and it will move, and nothing will be impossible for you."

**17:22** Now while they were gathering together in Galilee, Jesus said to them, "The Son of Man is about to be handed over into the hands of men,

**17:23** and they will kill Him, and on the third day He will be raised." And they were greatly grieved.

**17:24** When they came to Capernaum, those who collected the two-drachma tax approached Peter and said, "Does your teacher not pay the two-drachma tax?"

**17:25** He said, "Yes." And when he came into the house, Jesus spoke to him first, saying, "What do you think, Simon? From whom do the kings of the earth collect customs or poll tax? From their own sons, or from strangers?"

**17:26** And when he said, "From strangers," Jesus said to him, "Then indeed the sons are free.

**17:27** Yet so that we do not offend them, go to the sea, cast a hook, and take the first fish that comes up; and when you open its mouth, you will find a stater. Take that and give it to them for Me and for you."

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**17:21** *(after `17:20`)* ⟦Yet this kind does not go out except by prayer and fasting.⟧

## Translator Notes

- **17:15** — Greek seleniazetai literally means "is moonstruck," the ancient term for epilepsy or recurring seizures.
- **17:20** — Greek oligopistian means "little faith" rather than "unbelief." Verse 21 ("But this kind does not go out except by prayer and fasting") is absent from the earliest manuscripts; the Saints Greek Critical Text prints it within double brackets ⟦ ⟧ (shown as an additional verse).
- **17:24** — Greek ta didrachma ("the two-drachma") refers to the annual half-shekel temple tax paid by adult Jewish males (Exod 30:13).
- **17:27** — A stater was a silver coin worth four drachmas, the exact sum required to pay the two-drachma temple tax for both Jesus and Peter.
- **17:21 *(additional)*** — Text-critical double brackets ⟦ ⟧ are preserved from the Saints Greek Critical Text; this verse is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
