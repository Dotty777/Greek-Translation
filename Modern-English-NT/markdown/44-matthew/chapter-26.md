## Text

**26:1** And it happened when Jesus finished all these words, He said to His disciples,

**26:2** "You know that after two days the Passover takes place, and the Son of Man is handed over to be crucified."

**26:3** Then the chief priests and the elders of the people gathered together in the courtyard of the high priest who was called Caiaphas,

**26:4** and they took counsel together so that they might seize Jesus by deceit and kill Him.

**26:5** But they were saying, "Not during the festival, so that an uproar may not occur among the people."

**26:6** Now when Jesus was in Bethany, in the house of Simon the leper,

**26:7** a woman came up to Him having an alabaster flask of very costly perfume, and she poured it out upon His head as He reclined at table.

**26:8** But when the disciples saw this, they became indignant, saying, "Why this waste?

**26:9** For this could have been sold for a high price and given to the poor."

**26:10** But Jesus, knowing this, said to them, "Why do you cause trouble for the woman? For she has done a good work for Me.

**26:11** For you always have the poor with you, but you do not always have Me.

**26:12** For by pouring this perfume on My body, she did it to prepare Me for burial.

**26:13** Truly I say to you, wherever this gospel is proclaimed in the whole world, what this woman has done will also be spoken of in memory of her."

**26:14** Then one of the twelve, the one called Judas Iscariot, went to the chief priests

**26:15** and said, "What are you willing to give me, and I will hand Him over to you?" And they weighed out to him thirty pieces of silver.

**26:16** And from that time he was seeking a good opportunity to hand Him over.

**26:17** Now on the first day of Unleavened Bread, the disciples came up to Jesus, saying, "Where do You want us to prepare for You to eat the Passover?"

**26:18** And He said, "Go into the city to a certain man and say to him, 'The Teacher says: My time is near; I am keeping the Passover at your house with My disciples.'"

**26:19** And the disciples did as Jesus had directed them, and they prepared the Passover.

**26:20** Now when evening came, He was reclining at table with the twelve,

**26:21** and as they were eating, He said, "Truly I say to you that one of you will hand Me over."

**26:22** And being deeply grieved, each one began to say to Him, "Surely it is not I, Lord?"

**26:23** And He answered and said, "The one who dipped his hand with Me in the dish, this one will hand Me over.

**26:24** The Son of Man indeed goes just as it is written about Him, but woe to that man through whom the Son of Man is handed over! It would have been good for him if that man had not been born."

**26:25** Then Judas, the one handing Him over, answered and said, "Surely it is not I, Rabbi?" He said to him, "You have said it."

**26:26** Now while they were eating, Jesus took bread, and having said a blessing, He broke it, and giving it to the disciples, He said, "Take, eat; this is My body."

**26:27** And taking a cup and having given thanks, He gave it to them, saying, "Drink from it, all of you,

**26:28** for this is My blood of the covenant, which is poured out on behalf of many for the forgiveness of sins.

**26:29** But I say to you, from now on I will certainly not drink of this fruit of the vine until that day when I drink it new with you in the kingdom of My Father."

**26:30** And having sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives.

**26:31** Then Jesus said to them, "All of you will stumble because of Me on this night, for it is written, 'I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock will be scattered.'

**26:32** But after I have been raised, I will go ahead of you into Galilee."

**26:33** And Peter answered and said to Him, "Though all will stumble because of You, I will never stumble."

**26:34** Jesus said to him, "Truly I say to you that on this night, before a rooster crows, you will deny Me three times."

**26:35** Peter said to Him, "Even if I must die with You, I will certainly not deny You." All the disciples also spoke in the same way.

**26:36** Then Jesus came with them to a place called Gethsemane, and He said to the disciples, "Sit here while I go over there and pray."

**26:37** And taking along Peter and the two sons of Zebedee, He began to be grieved and in anguish.

**26:38** Then He said to them, "My soul is deeply grieved, to the point of death; remain here and keep watch with Me."

**26:39** And going a little farther, He fell on His face, praying and saying, "My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass away from Me; yet not as I will, but as You do."

**26:40** And He came to the disciples and found them sleeping, and He said to Peter, "So, were you unable to keep watch with Me for one hour?

**26:41** Keep watch and pray so that you may not enter into temptation; the spirit indeed is eager, but the flesh is weak."

**26:42** Again, a second time, He went away and prayed, saying, "My Father, if this cannot pass away unless I drink it, let Your will be done."

**26:43** And coming again, He found them sleeping, for their eyes were weighed down.

**26:44** And leaving them again, He went away and prayed a third time, saying the same words once again.

**26:45** Then He came to the disciples and said to them, "Sleep on now and take your rest. Look, the hour has drawn near, and the Son of Man is handed over into the hands of sinners.

**26:46** Get up, let us go! Look, the one handing Me over has drawn near."

**26:47** And while He was still speaking, look, Judas, one of the twelve, arrived, and with him a large crowd with swords and clubs from the chief priests and elders of the people.

**26:48** Now the one handing Him over had given them a sign, saying, "The one whom I kiss, He is the one; seize Him."

**26:49** And immediately coming up to Jesus, he said, "Greetings, Rabbi!" and kissed Him.

**26:50** And Jesus said to him, "Friend, do what you have come for." Then they came up, laid hands on Jesus, and seized Him.

**26:51** And look, one of those with Jesus stretched out his hand, drew his sword, and striking the slave of the high priest, cut off his ear.

**26:52** Then Jesus said to him, "Return your sword to its place, for all who take up a sword will perish by a sword.

**26:53** Or do you suppose that I cannot appeal to My Father, and He will immediately place at My disposal more than twelve legions of angels?

**26:54** How then would the Scriptures be fulfilled, that it must happen in this way?"

**26:55** In that hour Jesus said to the crowds, "Have you come out as against a robber, with swords and clubs, to arrest Me? Day after day I sat in the temple teaching, and you did not seize Me.

**26:56** But all this has happened so that the Scriptures of the prophets may be fulfilled." Then all the disciples left Him and fled.

**26:57** Now those who had seized Jesus led Him away to Caiaphas the high priest, where the scribes and the elders had gathered together.

**26:58** But Peter was following Him at a distance as far as the courtyard of the high priest, and going inside, he sat with the officers to see the outcome.

**26:59** Now the chief priests and the whole Sanhedrin were seeking false testimony against Jesus so that they might put Him to death,

**26:60** and they did not find any, though many false witnesses came forward. But later two came forward

**26:61** and said, "This man stated, 'I am able to destroy the temple of God and build it within three days.'"

**26:62** And standing up, the high priest said to Him, "Do You answer nothing? What is it that these men are testifying against You?"

**26:63** But Jesus kept silent. And the high priest said to Him, "I put You under oath by the living God that You tell us whether You are the Christ, the Son of God."

**26:64** Jesus said to him, "You have said it. Yet I say to you, from now on you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming on the clouds of heaven."

**26:65** Then the high priest tore his garments, saying, "He has blasphemed! What further need do we have of witnesses? Look, now you have heard the blasphemy!

**26:66** What do you think?" And they answered and said, "He is deserving of death."

**26:67** Then they spat in His face and beat Him with their fists, and others slapped Him,

**26:68** saying, "Prophesy to us, Christ: who is the one who struck You?"

**26:69** Now Peter was sitting outside in the courtyard. And a servant girl came up to him, saying, "You also were with Jesus the Galilean."

**26:70** But he denied it before everyone, saying, "I do not know what you are saying."

**26:71** And as he went out to the gateway, another woman saw him and said to those who were there, "This man was with Jesus the Nazarene."

**26:72** And again he denied it with an oath, "I do not know the man."

**26:73** And after a little while, those who were standing there came up and said to Peter, "Truly you also are one of them, for even your way of speaking gives you away."

**26:74** Then he began to curse and to swear, "I do not know the man." And immediately a rooster crowed.

**26:75** And Peter remembered the word Jesus had spoken, "Before a rooster crows, you will deny Me three times." And going outside, he wept bitterly.

## Translator Notes

- **26:2** — Greek paradidōmi literally means "hand over" or "deliver over" and is translated consistently throughout this chapter (vv. 2, 15, 16, 21, 23-25), where it carries the sense of betrayal in Judas's actions.
- **26:15** — Greek estēsan literally means "weighed out," echoing the Septuagint wording of Zechariah 11:12 regarding the thirty pieces of silver.
- **26:18** — Greek ton deina is an indefinite expression meaning "so-and-so" or "a certain man," leaving the host unnamed.
- **26:28** — The earliest manuscripts read "My blood of the covenant" without the word "new," which was added in later manuscripts under the influence of Luke 22:20 and 1 Corinthians 11:25.
- **26:45** — Greek katheudete to loipon kai anapauesthe can be read either as an ironic command ("Sleep on now and take your rest") or as a question ("Are you still sleeping and resting?").
- **26:50** — Greek eph' ho parei is elliptical, literally "for which you are here," and is understood either as a command ("do what you have come for") or as a question ("why are you here?").
- **26:64** — "Power" (Greek tēs dynameōs) is a reverent Jewish circumlocution for God, combining allusions to Psalm 110:1 and Daniel 7:13.
