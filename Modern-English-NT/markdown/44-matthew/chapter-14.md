## Text

**14:1** At that time Herod the tetrarch heard the report about Jesus,

**14:2** and he said to his servants, "This is John the Baptist; he himself has been raised from the dead, and for this reason the miraculous powers are working in him."

**14:3** For Herod had seized John, bound him, and put him away in prison on account of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip,

**14:4** for John kept saying to him, "It is not lawful for you to have her."

**14:5** And although he wanted to kill him, he feared the crowd, because they regarded him as a prophet.

**14:6** But when Herod's birthday arrived, the daughter of Herodias danced in their midst and pleased Herod,

**14:7** so he promised with an oath to give her whatever she might ask.

**14:8** Prompted beforehand by her mother, she said, "Give me here on a platter the head of John the Baptist."

**14:9** And the king was grieved; yet because of his oaths and those reclining with him, he commanded that it be given,

**14:10** and he sent and had John beheaded in the prison.

**14:11** His head was brought on a platter and given to the girl, and she carried it to her mother.

**14:12** Then his disciples came, took away the body, and buried him; and they went and reported it to Jesus.

**14:13** Now when Jesus heard this, He withdrew from there in a boat to a desolate place by Himself; and when the crowds heard of it, they followed Him on foot from the cities.

**14:14** And when He came ashore, He saw a large crowd, and He was moved with compassion for them and healed their sick.

**14:15** When evening came, the disciples approached Him, saying, "The place is desolate and the hour has already passed; dismiss the crowds, so that they may go into the villages and buy food for themselves."

**14:16** But Jesus said to them, "They have no need to go away; you give them something to eat."

**14:17** And they said to Him, "We have nothing here except five loaves and two fish."

**14:18** He said, "Bring them to Me here."

**14:19** And commanding the crowds to recline on the grass, He took the five loaves and the two fish, and looking up toward heaven, He spoke a blessing; then breaking the loaves, He gave them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowds.

**14:20** And all of them ate and were satisfied, and they picked up what remained of the broken pieces, twelve full baskets.

**14:21** Now those who ate were about five thousand men, apart from women and children.

**14:22** And immediately He made the disciples get into the boat and go ahead of Him to the other side, while He dismissed the crowds.

**14:23** And after dismissing the crowds, He went up on the mountain by Himself to pray. When evening came, He was there alone.

**14:24** Now the boat was already in the middle of the sea, being battered by the waves, for the wind was contrary.

**14:25** During the fourth watch of the night, He came to them, walking on the sea.

**14:26** When the disciples saw Him walking on the sea, they were terrified, saying, "It is a ghost!" And they cried out in fear.

**14:27** At once Jesus spoke to them, saying, "Have courage, it is I; do not fear."

**14:28** Peter answered Him and said, "Lord, if it is You, command me to come to You on the waters."

**14:29** And He said, "Come." And stepping down from the boat, Peter walked on the waters and came toward Jesus.

**14:30** But seeing the strong wind, he became afraid, and beginning to sink, he cried out, saying, "Lord, save me!"

**14:31** And immediately Jesus reached out His hand, grasped him, and said to him, "You of little faith, why did you doubt?"

**14:32** And when they climbed into the boat, the wind ceased.

**14:33** Then those in the boat worshiped Him, saying, "Truly You are God's Son."

**14:34** And after crossing over, they came to land at Gennesaret.

**14:35** When the men of that place recognized Him, they sent word throughout all that surrounding region and brought to Him all who were ill,

**14:36** and they were pleading with Him that they might only touch the fringe of His garment; and as many as touched it were made completely well.

## Translator Notes

- **14:9** — The Greek text uses two finite verbs (was grieved ... commanded) without a conjunction linking them, rather than the participial construction found in some manuscripts. The plural 'oaths' likely emphasizes the solemnity or repeated nature of Herod's pledge.
- **14:24** — The Greek text reads 'was already in the middle of the sea,' whereas several ancient manuscripts read 'was already many stadia away from the land.'
- **14:27** — The phrase 'it is I' literally translates Greek 'ego eimi' ('I am'), which serves both as a normal self-identification and as an echo of God's self-revelation in the Greek Old Testament (e.g., Exodus 3:14; Isaiah 43:10).
- **14:29** — The Greek text reads the finite verb 'and came,' whereas some early manuscripts read the infinitive 'to come.'
- **14:36** — 'Fringe' (Greek 'kraspedon') refers to the tassel attached to the corner of a Jewish outer garment in accordance with the Mosaic Law (Numbers 15:38-39; Deuteronomy 22:12).
