## Text

**11:1** I say then, has God cast off His people? May it never happen! For I too am an Israelite, from the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.

**11:2** God has not cast off His people whom He foreknew. Or do you not know what the Scripture says in the account of Elijah, how he appeals to God against Israel?

**11:3** "Lord, they have killed Your prophets, they have torn down Your altars, and I alone am left, and they are seeking my life."

**11:4** But what does the divine answer say to him? "I have left for Myself seven thousand men who have not bent the knee to Baal."

**11:5** So then, at the present time also, there has come to be a remnant according to an election of grace.

**11:6** But if it is by grace, it is no longer from works; otherwise grace is no longer grace.

**11:7** What then? What Israel is seeking, this it did not obtain, but the elect obtained it; and the rest were hardened,

**11:8** just as it is written: "God gave them a spirit of stupor, eyes not to see and ears not to hear, until this very day."

**11:9** And David says: "Let their table become a snare and a trap and a stumbling block and a repayment to them;

**11:10** let their eyes be darkened so as not to see, and bend their backs continually."

**11:11** I say then, did they stumble so that they would fall? May it never happen! Rather, by their trespass salvation has come to the Gentiles, in order to make them jealous.

**11:12** Now if their trespass is riches for the world, and their loss is riches for the Gentiles, how much more will their fullness be!

**11:13** But I am speaking to you who are Gentiles. Inasmuch then as I am an apostle of the Gentiles, I honor my ministry,

**11:14** if somehow I might make my own flesh jealous and save some of them.

**11:15** For if their rejection is the reconciliation of the world, what will their acceptance be but life from the dead?

**11:16** And if the firstfruits are holy, the lump is also; and if the root is holy, the branches are also.

**11:17** But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, being a wild olive shoot, were grafted in among them and became a fellow partaker of the root of the richness of the olive tree,

**11:18** do not boast over the branches; yet if you boast, it is not you who sustains the root, but the root sustains you.

**11:19** You will say then, "Branches were broken off so that I might be grafted in."

**11:20** True enough! By unbelief they were broken off, but you stand by faith. Do not be haughty, but fear;

**11:21** for if God did not spare the natural branches, He will not spare you either.

**11:22** See then the kindness and severity of God: toward those who fell, severity; but toward you, God's kindness, if you remain in that kindness; otherwise you also will be cut off.

**11:23** And those others also, if they do not remain in unbelief, will be grafted in; for God is able to graft them in again.

**11:24** For if you were cut out of what is by nature a wild olive tree, and contrary to nature were grafted into a cultivated olive tree, how much more will these natural branches be grafted into their own olive tree!

**11:25** For I do not want you to be unaware, brothers, of this mystery, so that you will not be wise in your own estimation: that a hardening in part has come upon Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in,

**11:26** and in this way all Israel will be saved, just as it is written: "The Deliverer will come from Zion; He will turn away ungodliness from Jacob.

**11:27** And this is the covenant from Me to them, when I take away their sins."

**11:28** With respect to the gospel, they are enemies for your sake; but with respect to election, they are beloved for the sake of the fathers.

**11:29** For the gracious gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable.

**11:30** For just as you once disobeyed God, yet have now received mercy through their disobedience,

**11:31** so these also have now disobeyed because of the mercy shown to you, so that they themselves may also now receive mercy.

**11:32** For God has confined all in disobedience, so that He may show mercy to all.

**11:33** Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are His judgments and untraceable His ways!

**11:34** For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has become His counselor?

**11:35** Or who has first given to Him, and it will be repaid to him?

**11:36** For all things are from Him and through Him and to Him; to Him be the glory forever. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **11:4** — Greek uses the feminine article with Baal ("the Baal"), reflecting the Septuagint convention of substituting the feminine noun aischyne ("shame") when reading Baal's name aloud.
- **11:6** — Later manuscripts add a symmetrical clause ("and if from works, it is no longer grace; otherwise work is no longer work"), which is absent from the earliest manuscripts.
- **11:17** — The Greek text reads three successive genitives ("of the root of the richness of the olive tree") without "and," whereas some manuscripts insert "and" ("of the root and of the richness").
- **11:25** — "In part" (apo merous) may refer to a partial hardening in extent (affecting part of Israel) or in duration, with the following "until" clause underscoring its temporary scope.
- **11:31** — The second "now" (nyn) is strongly attested in early manuscripts though omitted by some witnesses; the dative "because of the mercy shown to you" may alternatively modify the following clause ("so that by the mercy shown to you they also may now receive mercy").
