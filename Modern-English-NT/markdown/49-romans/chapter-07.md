## Text

**7:1** Or are you unaware, brothers—for I am speaking to those who know the law—that the law has dominion over a person for as long as he lives?

**7:2** For a married woman is bound by law to her living husband; but if her husband dies, she is released from the law concerning her husband.

**7:3** So then, while her husband is living, she will be called an adulteress if she belongs to another man; yet if her husband dies, she is free from that law, so that she is not an adulteress if she belongs to another man.

**7:4** Therefore, my brothers, you also were put to death to the law through the body of Christ, so that you might belong to another—to Him who was raised from the dead—in order that we might bear fruit for God.

**7:5** For when we were in the flesh, the sinful passions that were through the law were at work in our members so as to bear fruit for death.

**7:6** But now we have been released from the law, having died to that in which we were held captive, so that we serve in newness of the Spirit and not in oldness of the letter.

**7:7** What then shall we say? Is the law sin? May it never happen! On the contrary, I would not have come to know sin except through the law; for I would not have known coveting either if the law had not said, "You shall not covet."

**7:8** But sin, seizing an opportunity through the commandment, produced in me every kind of coveting; for apart from law, sin is dead.

**7:9** Now I was once alive apart from law; yet when the commandment came, sin came back to life,

**7:10** and I died; and the very commandment that was for life was found to bring death to me.

**7:11** For sin, seizing an opportunity through the commandment, deceived me and through it killed me.

**7:12** So then, the law is holy, and the commandment is holy and righteous and good.

**7:13** Did what is good, then, become death to me? May it never happen! Rather, it was sin—so that it might be shown to be sin by producing death in me through what is good—so that through the commandment sin might become sinful beyond measure.

**7:14** For we know that the law is spiritual, whereas I am of flesh, sold under sin.

**7:15** For what I am carrying out, I do not understand; for I do not practice what I want to do, but what I hate, that I do.

**7:16** Now if I do the very thing I do not want, I agree with the law, that it is good.

**7:17** So now it is no longer I who carry it out, but sin that dwells in me.

**7:18** For I know that nothing good dwells in me, that is, in my flesh; for the willing is present with me, yet the carrying out of what is good is not.

**7:19** For the good that I want, I do not do; instead, the evil that I do not want, this I practice.

**7:20** Now if I do the very thing I do not want, it is no longer I who carry it out, but sin that dwells in me.

**7:21** I find, therefore, this law: when I want to do what is good, evil is present with me.

**7:22** For I delight in the law of God in my inner self,

**7:23** yet I see a different law in my members, waging war against the law of my mind and making me a captive to the law of sin that is in my members.

**7:24** What a wretched person I am! Who will rescue me from this body of death?

**7:25** Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord! So then, I myself with my mind serve the law of God, yet with my flesh, the law of sin.

## Translator Notes

- **7:6** — The text reads the plural participle apothanontes ("having died"), referring to believers rather than to the law; "Spirit" (pneumatos) contrasts the life-giving Spirit with the written letter.
- **7:7** — Greek epithymia ("coveting") and epithymeo ("covet") share the same root and carry the broader sense of desire (as in 6:12), specifically echoing the tenth commandment (Exod 20:17).
- **7:14** — Interpreters debate whether the first-person "I" of 7:14-25 depicts unredeemed humanity under the law, Israel under the Torah, or the believer's ongoing conflict with indwelling sin.
- **7:21** — The syntax of ton nomon is unusually compressed; commentators debate whether it means "this principle/rule" or refers to the Mosaic law itself.
