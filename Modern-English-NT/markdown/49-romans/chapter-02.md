## Text

**2:1** Therefore you are without excuse, O man, everyone who judges; for in that which you judge the other person, you condemn yourself, because you who judge practice the very same things.

**2:2** Now we know that the judgment of God is according to truth upon those who practice such things.

**2:3** And do you suppose this, O man, you who judge those who practice such things and yet do them yourself, that you will escape the judgment of God?

**2:4** Or do you despise the wealth of His kindness and forbearance and patience, not knowing that the kindness of God is leading you to repentance?

**2:5** But according to your hardness and unrepentant heart, you are storing up wrath for yourself in the day of wrath and revelation of the righteous judgment of God,

**2:6** who will repay each person according to his deeds:

**2:7** to those who by endurance in good work seek glory and honor and imperishability, eternal life;

**2:8** but to those who are driven by selfish ambition and disobey the truth, yet obey unrighteousness, wrath and fury,

**2:9** affliction and distress upon every human soul who carries out what is evil, both of the Jew first and of the Greek;

**2:10** but glory and honor and peace to everyone who works what is good, both to the Jew first and to the Greek.

**2:11** For there is no favoritism with God.

**2:12** For as many as sinned without law will also perish without law, and as many as sinned under law will be judged through law;

**2:13** for the hearers of the law are not righteous before God, but the doers of the law will be justified.

**2:14** For whenever Gentiles, who do not have the law, do by nature the things of the law, these, though not having the law, are a law to themselves,

**2:15** since they show the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience bearing witness along with it, and between one another their thoughts accusing or even defending them,

**2:16** on the day when God judges the hidden things of people, according to my gospel, through Christ Jesus.

**2:17** Now if you bear the name Jew, and rely on the law, and boast in God,

**2:18** and know His will, and approve the things that are superior, being instructed from the law,

**2:19** and are confident that you yourself are a guide of the blind, a light to those in darkness,

**2:20** an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of infants, having the embodiment of knowledge and of the truth in the law—

**2:21** you, therefore, who teach another, do you not teach yourself? You who preach not to steal, do you steal?

**2:22** You who say not to commit adultery, do you commit adultery? You who abhor idols, do you rob temples?

**2:23** You who boast in the law, through the transgression of the law do you dishonor God?

**2:24** For "the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you," just as it is written.

**2:25** For circumcision indeed benefits if you practice the law; but if you are a transgressor of the law, your circumcision has become uncircumcision.

**2:26** If, therefore, the uncircumcised person keeps the righteous decrees of the law, will not his uncircumcision be counted as circumcision?

**2:27** And the uncircumcised by nature who fulfills the law will judge you who, with the written letter and circumcision, are a transgressor of the law.

**2:28** For a Jew is not the one who is so outwardly, nor is circumcision what is outward in the flesh;

**2:29** rather, a Jew is the one who is so inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the letter; his praise is not from people, but from God.

## Translator Notes

- **2:14** — The adverb physei ('by nature') sits between clauses and may modify either 'who do not have the law' or 'do the things of the law.'
- **2:16** — The Greek verb krinei is accented as a present tense ('judges') in the critical text, though the same consonantal spelling can be accented as a future ('will judge').
- **2:29** — Greek en pneumati may refer either to the Holy Spirit ('by the Spirit') or to the inner human spirit ('in spirit'). Paul also alludes to the Hebrew name Judah (source of 'Jew'), which is linked to 'praise' (Genesis 29:35; 49:8).
