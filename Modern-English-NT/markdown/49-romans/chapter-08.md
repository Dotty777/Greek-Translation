## Text

**8:1** So then, there is now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus.

**8:2** For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has freed you from the law of sin and of death.

**8:3** For what was impossible for the law, in that it was weak through the flesh, God—having sent His own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh and concerning sin—condemned sin in the flesh,

**8:4** so that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.

**8:5** For those who exist according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, whereas those who exist according to the Spirit set their minds on the things of the Spirit.

**8:6** For the mindset of the flesh is death, whereas the mindset of the Spirit is life and peace;

**8:7** because the mindset of the flesh is hostility toward God, for it does not submit itself to the law of God, nor indeed is it able to;

**8:8** and those who are in the flesh are not able to please God.

**8:9** You, however, are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if indeed the Spirit of God dwells in you. Yet if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, this person does not belong to Him.

**8:10** And if Christ is in you, though the body is dead because of sin, the spirit is life because of righteousness.

**8:11** Yet if the Spirit of Him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, He who raised Christ Jesus from the dead will also make your mortal bodies alive through His Spirit who dwells in you.

**8:12** So then, brothers, we are debtors, not to the flesh, to live according to the flesh;

**8:13** for if you live according to the flesh, you are going to die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the practices of the body, you will live.

**8:14** For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.

**8:15** For you did not receive a spirit of slavery again leading to fear, but you received a Spirit of adoption, by whom we cry out, "Abba! Father!"

**8:16** The Spirit Himself testifies together with our spirit that we are children of God;

**8:17** and if children, then also heirs: heirs of God, and fellow heirs with Christ, if indeed we suffer together with Him so that we may also be glorified together with Him.

**8:18** For I reckon that the sufferings of the present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory that is about to be revealed to us.

**8:19** For the eager expectation of creation awaits the revealing of the sons of God.

**8:20** For creation was subjected to futility, not willingly, but because of the One who subjected it, in hope

**8:21** that creation itself will also be freed from its slavery to decay into the freedom of the glory of the children of God.

**8:22** For we know that the entire creation groans together and suffers birth pains together until now.

**8:23** And not only that, but we ourselves also, who have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, eagerly awaiting adoption, the redemption of our body.

**8:24** For in this hope we were saved; yet hope that is seen is not hope, for who hopes for what he sees?

**8:25** But if we hope for what we do not see, we eagerly await it through endurance.

**8:26** And in the same way the Spirit also comes alongside to help our weakness; for we do not know what we should pray for as we ought, but the Spirit Himself intercedes with unspoken groanings;

**8:27** and He who searches the hearts knows what the mindset of the Spirit is, because He intercedes for the saints according to God.

**8:28** Now we know that all things work together toward good for those who love God, for those who are called according to His purpose.

**8:29** Because those whom He foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son, so that He would be the firstborn among many brothers;

**8:30** and those whom He predestined, these He also called; and those whom He called, these He also justified; and those whom He justified, these He also glorified.

**8:31** What then shall we say to these things? If God is for us, who is against us?

**8:32** He who indeed did not spare His own Son, but handed Him over on behalf of us all, how will He not also with Him graciously give us all things?

**8:33** Who will bring an accusation against God's chosen ones? God is the One who justifies.

**8:34** Who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is the One who died—even more, who was raised—who is also at the right hand of God, who also intercedes on our behalf.

**8:35** Who will separate us from the love of Christ? Affliction, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or danger, or sword?

**8:36** Just as it is written, "For Your sake we are being put to death all day long; we were reckoned as sheep for slaughter."

**8:37** Yet in all these things we triumph completely through Him who loved us.

**8:38** For I am convinced that neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor things present nor things to come, nor powers,

**8:39** nor height nor depth, nor any other creature will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — The Saints Greek Critical Text has the shorter reading ending at ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ ("in Christ Jesus"), without the later harmonizing addition from 8:4 ("who do not walk according to the flesh").
- **8:2** — The Saints Greek Critical Text reads the second-person singular pronoun σε ("you") rather than με ("me"), rendered literally as "freed you."
- **8:3** — περὶ ἁμαρτίας literally means "concerning sin" and is also the Septuagint idiom for a sin offering (as in Isa 53:10); keeping "concerning sin" leaves both senses open.
- **8:10** — πνεῦμα here could refer either to the believer's human spirit (rendered lowercase "spirit" to balance "the body") or to the Holy Spirit.
- **8:11** — The Saints Greek Critical Text uses the genitive διὰ τοῦ ἐνοικοῦντος αὐτοῦ Πνεύματος ("through His Spirit who dwells"), indicating agency, rather than the accusative variant ("because of His Spirit").
- **8:15** — Abba is the transliterated Aramaic term of intimate filial address, followed by the Greek articular vocative ὁ πατήρ ("Father"). υἱοθεσία literally means "placement as a son," rendered "adoption." Greek uses anarthrous πνεῦμα in both clauses and the neuter relative ἐν ᾧ ("by whom" or "in which"), leaving open whether the second phrase refers directly to the Holy Spirit or to a spirit of adoption.
- **8:26** — ἀλαλήτοις literally means "wordless" or "unspoken" (groanings too deep for words). The Saints Greek Critical Text has the singular τῇ ἀσθενείᾳ ("our weakness") and no explicit ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν after ὑπερεντυγχάνει.
- **8:28** — In the Saints Greek Critical Text, ὁ θεός is not repeated as an explicit subject of συνεργεῖ. Taking πάντα as the neuter plural subject with a singular verb gives "all things work together toward good" (the alternative takes God as the implied subject: "He works all things together for good"). "His" before "purpose" is supplied from the context for κατὰ πρόθεσιν.
- **8:34** — The Saints Greek Critical Text reads Χριστὸς Ἰησοῦς ("Christ Jesus") and ἐγερθείς ("who was raised") without the later addition "from the dead."
