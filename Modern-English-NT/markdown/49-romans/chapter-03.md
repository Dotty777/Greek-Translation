## Text

**3:1** What then is the advantage of the Jew, or what is the benefit of circumcision?

**3:2** Much in every way. For first of all, they were entrusted with the oracles of God.

**3:3** What then? If some proved unfaithful, will their unfaithfulness nullify the faithfulness of God?

**3:4** May that never happen! Rather, let God prove true, and every person a liar, just as it is written: "So that You may be justified in Your words and will prevail when You are judged."

**3:5** But if our unrighteousness establishes God's righteousness, what shall we say? God, who brings wrath, is not unrighteous, is He? I am speaking in human terms.

**3:6** May that never happen! For otherwise, how will God judge the world?

**3:7** But if through my falsehood the truth of God abounded to His glory, why am I too still being judged as a sinner,

**3:8** and why not—just as we are slandered and just as some claim we say—"Let us do evil so that good may come"? Their judgment is deserved.

**3:9** What then? Are we at an advantage? Not at all, for we have already charged that both Jews and Greeks are all under sin,

**3:10** just as it is written: "There is no righteous person, not even one;

**3:11** there is no one who understands, there is no one who seeks out God;

**3:12** all have turned away; together they have become worthless; there is no one doing good, not even a single one.

**3:13** Their throat is an open tomb; with their tongues they kept deceiving; the venom of asps is under their lips;

**3:14** whose mouth is full of cursing and bitterness;

**3:15** their feet are quick to shed blood;

**3:16** ruin and misery lie in their paths,

**3:17** and they have not known the path of peace.

**3:18** No fear of God is before their eyes."

**3:19** Now we know that whatever the law says, it speaks to those in the law, so that every mouth may be silenced and the entire world may become accountable to God;

**3:20** because by works of the law no flesh will be justified before Him, for through the law comes the recognition of sin.

**3:21** But now, apart from the law, a righteousness of God has been revealed, attested by the Law and the Prophets,

**3:22** namely, the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe. For there is no distinction;

**3:23** for all sinned and fall short of the glory of God,

**3:24** being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus,

**3:25** whom God presented publicly as a propitiation through faith, by His blood, as a demonstration of His righteousness, because of the passing over of the sins previously committed

**3:26** in the forbearance of God, for the demonstration of His righteousness at the present time, so that He might be righteous and the One who justifies the one who has faith in Jesus.

**3:27** Where then is boasting? It has been shut out. Through what kind of law? That of works? No, rather through a law of faith.

**3:28** For we conclude that a person is justified by faith apart from works of the law.

**3:29** Or is God the God of Jews only? Is He not of Gentiles also? Yes, of Gentiles also,

**3:30** since indeed there is one God, who will justify the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith.

**3:31** Do we then nullify the law through faith? May that never happen! On the contrary, we establish the law.

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — Paul plays on the cognates for faith and unfaithfulness here: the Greek terms rendered "proved unfaithful" and "unfaithfulness" can also mean "did not believe" and "unbelief," contrasting with the "faithfulness" of God.
- **3:9** — The Greek verb προεχόμεθα is debated: taken as a middle it asks "Are we at an advantage?" (or "Do we make excuses?"), whereas as a passive it asks "Are we at a disadvantage?" Similarly, οὐ πάντως can mean "Not at all" or "Not entirely."
- **3:22** — The Greek genitive phrase πίστεως Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ can be taken either objectively as "faith in Jesus Christ" or subjectively as "the faithfulness of Jesus Christ"; the same ambiguity recurs in 3:26.
- **3:25** — The Greek term ἱλαστήριον denotes a means of propitiation or atoning sacrifice, and in the Septuagint it specifically designates the mercy seat (atonement cover) on the ark of the covenant (Exod 25:17).
