## Text

**14:1** Now welcome the one who is weak in faith, not for disputes over opinions.

**14:2** One person has faith to eat all things, while the one who is weak eats vegetables.

**14:3** The one who eats must not despise the one who does not eat, and the one who does not eat must not judge the one who eats, for God has welcomed him.

**14:4** Who are you, judging another's household servant? To his own master he stands or falls; yet he will be made to stand, for the Lord is able to make him stand.

**14:5** One person judges one day above another, while another judges every day alike; let each one be fully convinced in his own mind.

**14:6** The one who observes the day observes it for the Lord. And the one who eats, eats for the Lord, for he gives thanks to God; and the one who does not eat, for the Lord he does not eat, and gives thanks to God.

**14:7** For none of us lives for himself, and no one dies for himself;

**14:8** for whether we live, we live for the Lord, or whether we die, we die for the Lord. Therefore, whether we live or die, we belong to the Lord.

**14:9** For to this end Christ died and came to life again, so that He might be Lord of both the dead and the living.

**14:10** But you, why do you judge your brother? Or you too, why do you despise your brother? For we will all stand before the judgment seat of God,

**14:11** for it is written, "As I live," says the Lord, "every knee will bow to Me, and every tongue will confess to God."

**14:12** So then, each one of us will give an account of himself to God.

**14:13** Therefore let us no longer judge one another, but rather decide this: not to place a stumbling block or a snare before a brother.

**14:14** I know and am convinced in the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean in itself; yet to the one who considers something to be unclean, to that person it is unclean.

**14:15** For if because of food your brother is grieved, you are no longer walking according to love. Do not by your food destroy that one for whom Christ died.

**14:16** Therefore do not let your good be spoken of as evil.

**14:17** For the kingdom of God does not consist in eating and drinking, but in righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit;

**14:18** for the one who serves Christ in this way is well-pleasing to God and approved by people.

**14:19** So then, let us pursue the things of peace and the things for building up one another.

**14:20** Do not tear down the work of God for the sake of food. All things indeed are clean, but it is wrong for the person who eats in a way that causes stumbling.

**14:21** It is good not to eat meat or drink wine or do anything by which your brother stumbles, or is tripped up, or is made weak.

**14:22** As for you, keep the faith that you have to yourself before God. Blessed is the one who does not judge himself in what he approves;

**14:23** but the one who doubts stands condemned if he eats, because it is not from faith; and everything that is not from faith is sin.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — The phrase "not for disputes over opinions" (Greek me eis diakriseis dialogismon) literally means "not for passing judgment on thoughts/opinions" and can refer either to avoiding quarrels over debatable matters or to refraining from judging the weak believer's scruples.
- **14:13** — Paul plays on two senses of the Greek verb krino: "let us no longer judge (krinomen) one another, but rather decide (krinate) this."
- **14:14** — "Unclean" translates Greek koinos (literally "common"), used in Jewish Greek to denote what is ritually defiled or profane.
- **14:21** — The Greek text here preserves the fuller wording "or is tripped up, or is made weak" (e skandalizetai e asthenei), which is omitted in several early manuscripts.
