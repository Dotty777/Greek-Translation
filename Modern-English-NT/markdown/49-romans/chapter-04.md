## Text

**4:1** What then shall we say that Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh, has found?

**4:2** For if Abraham was justified by works, he has ground for boasting, yet not before God.

**4:3** For what does the Scripture say? "And Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him for righteousness."

**4:4** Now to the one who works, the wage is not credited according to grace, but according to obligation;

**4:5** but to the one who does not work, yet believes in the One who justifies the ungodly, his faith is credited for righteousness,

**4:6** just as David also speaks of the blessedness of the person to whom God credits righteousness apart from works:

**4:7** "Blessed are those whose lawless acts have been forgiven, and whose sins have been covered;

**4:8** blessed is the man to whom the Lord will certainly not credit sin."

**4:9** Is this blessedness then upon the circumcised, or also upon the uncircumcised? For we say: "Faith was credited to Abraham for righteousness."

**4:10** How then was it credited? When he was circumcised, or when uncircumcised? Not when circumcised, but when uncircumcised;

**4:11** and he received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness of the faith he had while uncircumcised, so that he would be the father of all who believe while uncircumcised, in order that righteousness would be credited to them,

**4:12** and the father of the circumcised, for those who are not merely of the circumcision, but who also walk in the footsteps of the faith that our father Abraham had while uncircumcised.

**4:13** For the promise to Abraham or to his offspring, that he would be heir of the world, was not through the law, but through the righteousness of faith.

**4:14** For if those who are of the law are heirs, faith has been made empty and the promise has been nullified;

**4:15** for the law produces wrath; yet where there is no law, neither is there transgression.

**4:16** For this reason it is by faith, so that it may be according to grace, in order that the promise may be certain to all the offspring, not only to those who are of the law, but also to those who are of the faith of Abraham (who is the father of us all,

**4:17** just as it is written: "I have appointed you the father of many nations"), in the presence of the God in whom he believed, who gives life to the dead and calls the things that do not exist as though they exist.

**4:18** Contrary to hope, in hope he believed, so that he would become the father of many nations, according to what had been spoken: "So will your offspring be."

**4:19** And without growing weak in faith, he considered his own body, already deadened—being about a hundred years old—and the deadness of Sarah's womb;

**4:20** yet regarding the promise of God he did not waver in unbelief, but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God

**4:21** and being fully convinced that what He has promised, He is also able to perform.

**4:22** Therefore it was also credited to him for righteousness.

**4:23** Now it was not written for his sake alone that it was credited to him,

**4:24** but also for our sake, to whom it will be credited—those who believe in the One who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,

**4:25** who was handed over because of our trespasses and was raised for our justification.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — The Greek phrase κατὰ σάρκα ("according to the flesh") can modify either "our forefather" or the verb "has found."
- **4:19** — While many later manuscripts read "he did not consider" (adding οὐ), the earliest witnesses omit the negative, indicating that Abraham realistically faced his and Sarah's condition without weakening in faith.
- **4:25** — Both clauses use the same Greek preposition διά with the accusative, expressing retrospective ground in the first ("because of our trespasses") and prospective purpose or result in the second ("for our justification").
