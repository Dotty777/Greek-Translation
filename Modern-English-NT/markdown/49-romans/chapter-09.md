## Text

**9:1** I speak the truth in Christ; I am not lying, as my conscience bears witness with me in the Holy Spirit,

**9:2** that I have great grief and continual anguish in my heart.

**9:3** For I could wish that I myself were accursed, cut off from Christ, for the sake of my brothers, my relatives according to the flesh,

**9:4** who are Israelites, to whom belong the adoption, the glory, the covenants, the giving of the law, the sacred service, and the promises;

**9:5** to whom belong the fathers, and from whom comes the Christ according to the flesh, who is over all, God blessed forever. Amen.

**9:6** Yet it is not as though the word of God has failed. For not all who are from Israel are Israel;

**9:7** nor are all children because they are Abraham's offspring; rather, "In Isaac your offspring will be named."

**9:8** That is, the children of the flesh are not the ones who are children of God, but the children of the promise are counted as offspring.

**9:9** For this is a word of promise: "At this time I will come, and Sarah will have a son."

**9:10** And not only this, but also Rebekah, having conceived by one man, our father Isaac—

**9:11** for when they were not yet born and had done nothing good or bad, so that God's purpose according to election might remain,

**9:12** not from works but from Him who calls—it was said to her, "The older will serve the younger,"

**9:13** just as it is written: "Jacob I loved, yet Esau I hated."

**9:14** What then shall we say? Is there unrighteousness with God? May it never happen!

**9:15** For He says to Moses, "I will have mercy on whomever I have mercy, and I will show compassion to whomever I show compassion."

**9:16** So then, it is not of the one who wills nor of the one who runs, but of God who shows mercy.

**9:17** For the Scripture says to Pharaoh, "For this very purpose I raised you up, so that I might display My power in you, and so that My name might be declared throughout all the earth."

**9:18** So then, He shows mercy to whom He wills, and He hardens whom He wills.

**9:19** You will say to me then, "Why then does He still find fault? For who has resisted His will?"

**9:20** Indeed, who are you, O human, who talks back to God? Will what is molded say to the one who molded it, "Why did you make me like this?"

**9:21** Or does the potter not have authority over the clay, to make from the same lump one vessel for honor and another for dishonor?

**9:22** And what if God, desiring to display wrath and to make His power known, bore with much patience vessels of wrath fitted for destruction,

**9:23** and in order to make known the riches of His glory upon vessels of mercy, which He prepared beforehand for glory—

**9:24** us whom He also called, not only from the Jews, but also from the Gentiles?

**9:25** As He also says in Hosea, "I will call those who were not My people, 'My people,' and her who was not loved, 'Beloved';

**9:26** and it will be that in the place where it was said to them, 'You are not My people,' there they will be called sons of the living God."

**9:27** And Isaiah cries out concerning Israel: "Though the number of the sons of Israel be like the sand of the sea, the remnant will be saved;

**9:28** for the Lord will execute His word upon the earth, completing and cutting it short."

**9:29** And just as Isaiah said beforehand: "If the Lord of Sabaoth had not left us offspring, we would have become like Sodom, and we would have been made like Gomorrah."

**9:30** What then shall we say? That Gentiles who were not pursuing righteousness attained righteousness, namely, the righteousness that is from faith;

**9:31** but Israel, pursuing a law of righteousness, did not arrive at the law.

**9:32** Why? Because it was not from faith, but as if from works. They stumbled against the stone of stumbling,

**9:33** just as it is written: "Look, I am placing in Zion a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense, and the one who believes in Him will not be put to shame."

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — The punctuation of the final clause is debated; following the Greek punctuation here, "who is over all, God blessed forever" refers to Christ, though some punctuate it as an independent doxology to God the Father ("May God, who is over all, be blessed forever").
- **9:7** — Greek σπέρμα ("seed," rendered "offspring" here) echoes Genesis 21:12 (compare Hebrews 11:18 and Romans 1:3, 11:1).
- **9:22** — Greek katertismena ("fitted" or "prepared") can be read as passive ("prepared") or middle/adjectival ("fit/ready for destruction"), contrasting with the active verb proetoimasen ("He prepared beforehand") in 9:23.
- **9:28** — Some later Greek manuscripts expand the quotation from Isaiah to read "in righteousness, because the Lord will carry out a shortened word upon the earth."
- **9:29** — "Sabaoth" is a Greek transliteration of the Hebrew title meaning "hosts" or "armies."
