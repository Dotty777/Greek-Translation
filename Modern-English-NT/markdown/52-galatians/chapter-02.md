## Text

**2:1** Then after fourteen years I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus along as well.

**2:2** I went up in accordance with a revelation, and I laid before them the gospel that I preach among the Gentiles—privately, however, to those of repute—in case I might somehow be running, or had run, in vain.

**2:3** Yet not even Titus, who was with me, though he was a Greek, was compelled to be circumcised.

**2:4** But because of the false brothers secretly brought in, who slipped in to spy out our freedom that we have in Christ Jesus, so that they might enslave us—

**2:5** to whom we did not yield in submission even for an hour, so that the truth of the gospel might remain with you.

**2:6** Now from those who were reputed to be something—whatever they once were makes no difference to me; God does not judge by a person's outward appearance—for those of repute added nothing to me,

**2:7** but on the contrary, seeing that I had been entrusted with the gospel to the uncircumcised, just as Peter had been to the circumcised—

**2:8** for the One who worked effectively in Peter for an apostleship to the circumcised also worked effectively in me for the Gentiles—

**2:9** and recognizing the grace that had been given to me, James and Cephas and John, who were reputed to be pillars, gave to me and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship, so that we should go to the Gentiles, and they to the circumcised;

**2:10** only that we should remember the poor, the very thing I was also eager to do.

**2:11** But when Cephas came to Antioch, I opposed him to his face, because he stood condemned.

**2:12** For before certain people came from James, he used to eat with the Gentiles; but when they arrived, he began to draw back and separate himself, fearing those of the circumcision.

**2:13** And the rest of the Jews also joined him in hypocrisy, so that even Barnabas was carried away by their hypocrisy.

**2:14** But when I saw that they were not walking straight in line with the truth of the gospel, I said to Cephas in front of everyone, "If you, being a Jew, live like a Gentile and not like a Jew, how is it that you compel the Gentiles to live like Jews?"

**2:15** We are Jews by nature and not sinners from among the Gentiles,

**2:16** yet knowing that a person is not justified by works of the law except through faith in Jesus Christ, we too believed in Christ Jesus, so that we might be justified by faith in Christ and not by works of the law, because by works of the law no flesh will be justified.

**2:17** But if, while seeking to be justified in Christ, we ourselves were also found to be sinners, is Christ then a servant of sin? May it never be!

**2:18** For if I rebuild the very things that I tore down, I demonstrate myself to be a transgressor.

**2:19** For through the law I died to the law, so that I might live to God. I have been crucified with Christ;

**2:20** yet I no longer live, but Christ lives in me; and the life I now live in the flesh, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself over for me.

**2:21** I do not set aside the grace of God; for if righteousness comes through the law, then Christ died for nothing.

## Translator Notes

- **2:4** — Paul's sentence breaks off grammatically across verses 4-5, reflecting his forceful refusal to concede to the false brothers regarding Titus's circumcision.
- **2:14** — Because the Greek text lacks quotation marks, interpreters debate whether Paul's spoken rebuke of Cephas concludes at verse 14 or extends through verse 21.
- **2:16** — The Greek phrases rendered 'faith in Jesus Christ' and 'faith in Christ' (pistis Christou) are debated and may mean either faith directed toward Christ (objective genitive) or the faithfulness of Christ (subjective genitive).
- **2:19** — Following the critical Greek versification, 'I have been crucified with Christ' closes verse 19 rather than beginning verse 20 as in some traditional English translations.
