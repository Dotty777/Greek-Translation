## Text

**3:1** O senseless Galatians, who bewitched you, before whose eyes Jesus Christ was publicly displayed as crucified?

**3:2** This alone I want to learn from you: Did you receive the Spirit from works of the law or from the hearing of faith?

**3:3** Are you so senseless? Having started by the Spirit, are you now being brought to completion by the flesh?

**3:4** Did you experience so many things in vain—if indeed it truly was in vain?

**3:5** Therefore, the One who supplies the Spirit to you and works miracles among you—does He do so from works of the law or from the hearing of faith?

**3:6** Just as Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness.

**3:7** Know then that those who are of faith, these are sons of Abraham.

**3:8** And the Scripture, foreseeing that God justifies the nations by faith, announced the good news beforehand to Abraham: "In you all the nations will be blessed."

**3:9** Consequently, those who are of faith are blessed together with believing Abraham.

**3:10** For as many as are from works of the law are under a curse, for it is written, "Cursed is everyone who does not continue in all the things written in the book of the law, to do them."

**3:11** Now that no one is justified before God by the law is evident, for "The righteous one will live by faith."

**3:12** Yet the law is not of faith; rather, "The one who does them will live in them."

**3:13** Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law, becoming a curse on our behalf, because it is written, "Cursed is everyone hanging on a tree,"

**3:14** so that the blessing of Abraham might come to the nations in Christ Jesus, so that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.

**3:15** Brothers, I am speaking in human terms: even a ratified human covenant no one annuls or adds provisions to.

**3:16** Now the promises were spoken to Abraham and to his seed. It does not say, "And to seeds," as of many, but as of one, "And to your seed," who is Christ.

**3:17** Now this is what I say: the law, which came four hundred and thirty years later, does not invalidate a covenant previously ratified by God, so as to nullify the promise.

**3:18** For if the inheritance is from the law, it is no longer from a promise; yet God has graciously granted it to Abraham through a promise.

**3:19** Why then the law? It was added on account of transgressions, until the seed should come to whom it had been promised, having been ordained through angels by the hand of a mediator.

**3:20** Now a mediator is not of one, yet God is one.

**3:21** Is the law then against the promises of God? May it never happen! For if a law had been given that was able to impart life, then righteousness would indeed have been from the law.

**3:22** Instead, the Scripture confined everything under sin, so that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe.

**3:23** Now before faith came, we were guarded under the law, being confined for the faith that was about to be revealed.

**3:24** So then, the law has become our custodian until Christ, so that we might be justified by faith.

**3:25** Now since faith has come, we are no longer under a custodian.

**3:26** For all of you are sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus.

**3:27** For as many as were baptized into Christ, you have clothed yourselves with Christ.

**3:28** There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free person, there is not male and female; for all of you are one in Christ Jesus.

**3:29** And if you belong to Christ, then you are the seed of Abraham, heirs according to promise.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — The Greek phrase rendered 'the hearing of faith' (akoes pisteos) can mean either 'hearing with faith' or 'the message that produces faith.'
- **3:15** — The Greek word diatheke ('covenant') can also denote a human last will or legal disposition, which Paul uses here as an analogy for God's unalterable covenant with Abraham.
- **3:20** — This compressed statement is widely debated; it is commonly read as contrasting the law, which involved a mediator representing multiple parties, with God's direct, unilateral promise.
- **3:22** — The Greek genitive pisteos Iesou Christou can be taken either objectively as 'faith in Jesus Christ' or subjectively as 'the faithfulness of Jesus Christ.'
- **3:24** — A paidagogos ('custodian') was a household slave who escorted and supervised a child until maturity; 'until Christ' (eis Christon) can also carry the directional sense 'leading to Christ.'
