## Text

**15:1** And I saw another sign in heaven, great and marvelous: seven angels having seven plagues, the last ones, because in them the fury of God is completed.

**15:2** And I saw something like a sea of glass mixed with fire, and those who were victorious over the beast and over its image and over the number of its name, standing on the sea of glass, holding harps of God.

**15:3** And they sing the song of Moses, the slave of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying, "Great and marvelous are Your works, Lord God, the Almighty; righteous and true are Your ways, King of the ages.

**15:4** Who will not fear, Lord, and glorify Your name, since You alone are holy? For all the nations will come and worship before You, because Your righteous deeds have been revealed."

**15:5** And after these things I looked, and the temple of the tabernacle of testimony in heaven was opened,

**15:6** and the seven angels having the seven plagues came out of the temple, clothed in pure, bright linen and belted around their chests with golden sashes.

**15:7** And one of the four living creatures gave the seven angels seven golden bowls filled with the fury of God, the One who lives forever and ever.

**15:8** And the temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God and from His power, and no one was able to enter the temple until the seven plagues of the seven angels were completed.

## Translator Notes

- **15:1** — Greek ἐτελέσθη is literally past tense ("was completed"), a proleptic aorist depicting the completion of God's wrath through these final plagues as certain.
- **15:2** — Greek νικῶντας ἐκ ("conquering out of/from") is a compressed idiom conveying both victory over and deliverance from the beast; ἐπὶ τὴν θάλασσαν may mean either "on" or "beside" the sea (echoing Israel at the Red Sea).
- **15:3** — Manuscripts vary between "King of the ages" (τῶν αἰώνων), "King of the nations" (τῶν ἐθνῶν), and later "King of the saints" (τῶν ἁγίων); the critical text reads "of the ages."
- **15:4** — The critical text has no explicit object ("You") after "fear" (φοβηθῇ), though some manuscripts add σε.
- **15:6** — While an early textual variant reads λίθον ("stone," as in a precious stone), the critical text reads λίνον ("linen").
