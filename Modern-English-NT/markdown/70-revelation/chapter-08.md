## Text

**8:1** And when He opened the seventh seal, there came silence in heaven for about half an hour.

**8:2** And I saw the seven angels who stand before God, and to them were given seven trumpets.

**8:3** And another angel came and stood at the altar, holding a golden censer; and much incense was given to him so that he would offer it with the prayers of all the saints upon the golden altar that is before the throne.

**8:4** And the smoke of the incense went up with the prayers of the saints from the hand of the angel before God.

**8:5** And the angel took the censer and filled it from the fire of the altar and threw it to the earth; and there came peals of thunder and sounds and flashes of lightning and an earthquake.

**8:6** And the seven angels who had the seven trumpets made themselves ready to sound them.

**8:7** And the first sounded his trumpet; and there came hail and fire, mixed with blood, and it was thrown to the earth; and a third of the earth was burned up, and a third of the trees were burned up, and all green grass was burned up.

**8:8** And the second angel sounded his trumpet; and something like a great mountain burning with fire was thrown into the sea, and a third of the sea became blood,

**8:9** and a third of the creatures in the sea, those having life, died, and a third of the ships were destroyed.

**8:10** And the third angel sounded his trumpet; and a great star fell from heaven, burning like a torch, and it fell upon a third of the rivers and upon the springs of the waters.

**8:11** And the name of the star is called Wormwood. And a third of the waters turned into wormwood, and many of the people died from the waters, because they had been made bitter.

**8:12** And the fourth angel sounded his trumpet; and a third of the sun was struck, and a third of the moon, and a third of the stars, so that a third of them would be darkened, and the day would not shine for a third of it, and the night likewise.

**8:13** And I looked, and I heard an eagle flying in midheaven, saying with a loud voice, "Woe, woe, woe to those who dwell on the earth, because of the remaining sounds of the trumpet of the three angels who are about to sound their trumpets!"

## Translator Notes

- **8:3** — Greek libanoton ("censer") elsewhere denotes frankincense, but here and in 8:5 refers to a golden incense pan or censer. The clause "that he would offer it with the prayers" is literally "that he will give to the prayers," indicating either adding the incense to the prayers or offering it alongside them.
- **8:7** — The clause "and a third of the earth was burned up" is strongly supported by the earliest manuscripts, though omitted in some later Byzantine witnesses.
- **8:9** — Greek ta echonta psychas ("those having life," lit., "those having souls/lives") stands in the nominative or accusative in apposition to the genitive "of the creatures," a characteristic grammatical irregularity in Revelation.
- **8:11** — Greek Apsinthos ("Wormwood") refers to an intensely bitter plant (Artemisia absinthium), associated in the Hebrew Scriptures with bitter sorrow and divine judgment (e.g., Jer 9:15; 23:15).
- **8:13** — The earliest and best manuscripts read "eagle" (aetou, which can also denote a vulture), whereas some later manuscripts substitute "angel" (angelou). Greek henos ("one") functions here as an indefinite article ("an eagle" or "a single eagle").
