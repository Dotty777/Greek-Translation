## Text

**10:1** And I saw another strong angel descending from heaven, wrapped in a cloud, and the rainbow was on his head, and his face was like the sun, and his feet were like pillars of fire,

**10:2** and he had in his hand an open little scroll. And he set his right foot on the sea, and his left on the earth,

**10:3** and he cried out with a loud voice, just as a lion roars. And when he cried out, the seven thunders spoke with their own voices.

**10:4** And when the seven thunders spoke, I was about to write; and I heard a voice from heaven saying, "Seal up the things that the seven thunders spoke, and do not write them."

**10:5** And the angel whom I saw standing on the sea and on the earth raised his right hand toward heaven,

**10:6** and swore by Him who lives forever and ever, who created heaven and what is in it, and the earth and what is in it, and the sea and what is in it, that there will no longer be delay,

**10:7** but in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he is about to blow the trumpet, then the mystery of God will be completed, just as He announced the good news to His own servants the prophets.

**10:8** And the voice that I heard from heaven was speaking with me again and saying, "Go, take the scroll that is open in the hand of the angel who is standing on the sea and on the earth."

**10:9** And I went to the angel, telling him to give me the little scroll. And he said to me, "Take it and eat it up, and it will make your stomach bitter, but in your mouth it will be sweet as honey."

**10:10** And I took the little scroll from the hand of the angel and ate it up, and in my mouth it was sweet as honey; and when I had eaten it, my stomach became bitter.

**10:11** And they said to me, "You must prophesy again concerning peoples and nations and languages and many kings."

## Translator Notes

- **10:2** — The Greek uses the diminutive biblaridion ("little scroll") here and in vv. 9-10, whereas v. 8 uses the standard noun biblion ("scroll"). The participle echon ("having/holding") stands in the nominative despite referring back to the accusative "angel" in v. 1, a grammatical irregularity characteristic of Revelation.
- **10:6** — Literally "time will no longer be" (chronos ouketi estai), an idiom meaning no further delay or period of waiting before the fulfillment described in v. 7.
- **10:7** — Literally "and the mystery of God was finished" (kai etelesthe), a Semitic construction combining an apodotic "and" ("then") with a prophetic aorist tense to express the certainty of future completion.
- **10:8** — The Greek shifts from the nominative "the voice" (he phone) to accusative participles ("speaking ... and saying"), an abrupt construction smoothed here into English; it also uses biblion ("scroll") rather than the diminutive biblaridion ("little scroll") found in vv. 2, 9, and 10.
- **10:11** — The plural "they said" (legousin) may refer jointly to the heavenly voice and the angel or function as an indefinite plural ("I was told"). The preposition epi with the dative can mean "concerning" or "against," and the final adjective "many" (pollois) may modify "kings" alone or the entire fourfold list.
