## Text

**12:1** And a great sign appeared in heaven: a woman clothed in the sun, and the moon beneath her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars;

**12:2** and she was pregnant, and she was crying out in labor pains and in agony to give birth.

**12:3** And another sign appeared in heaven, and look, a great fiery-red dragon having seven heads and ten horns, and upon his heads seven diadems;

**12:4** and his tail dragged away a third of the stars of heaven and cast them to the earth. And the dragon stood in front of the woman who was about to give birth, so that when she gave birth he might devour her child.

**12:5** And she gave birth to a son, a male, who is to shepherd all the nations with an iron rod; and her child was snatched up to God and to His throne.

**12:6** And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she has a place prepared by God, so that there they might nourish her for one thousand two hundred sixty days.

**12:7** And there was war in heaven: Michael and his angels fought against the dragon, and the dragon and his angels fought,

**12:8** and they did not prevail, nor was a place found for them any longer in heaven.

**12:9** And the great dragon was cast down, the ancient serpent, who is called the Devil and Satan, the one deceiving the whole inhabited world; he was cast to the earth, and his angels were cast down with him.

**12:10** And I heard a loud voice in heaven saying, "Now salvation and power and the kingdom of our God and the authority of His Christ have arrived, because the accuser of our brothers has been cast down, the one accusing them before our God day and night.

**12:11** And they conquered him because of the blood of the Lamb and because of the word of their testimony, and they did not love their life even to the point of death.

**12:12** Because of this, rejoice, heavens and those dwelling in them! Woe to the earth and the sea, because the devil has come down to you, having great fury, knowing that he has a short time."

**12:13** And when the dragon saw that he had been cast to the earth, he pursued the woman who gave birth to the male child.

**12:14** And two wings of the great eagle were given to the woman, so that she might fly into the wilderness to her place, where she is nourished for a time and times and half a time, away from the presence of the serpent.

**12:15** And the serpent cast water like a river out of his mouth after the woman, so that he might cause her to be swept away by the river.

**12:16** And the earth helped the woman, and the earth opened its mouth and swallowed the river that the dragon had cast out of his mouth.

**12:17** And the dragon became enraged at the woman, and went off to wage war against the rest of her offspring, those who keep the commandments of God and hold the testimony of Jesus.

**12:18** And he stood upon the sand of the sea.

## Translator Notes

- **12:5** — The phrase "a son, a male" (υἱόν, ἄρρενα) reflects a Semitic idiom emphasizing the child's identity, while "shepherd" (ποιμαίνειν) directly echoes the Septuagint of Psalm 2:9.
- **12:7** — The Greek combines a nominative subject with a genitive articular infinitive (ὁ Μιχαὴλ καὶ οἱ ἄγγελοι αὐτοῦ τοῦ πολεμῆσαι), a Semitic construction conveying that Michael and his angels fought against the dragon.
- **12:11** — The preposition διά with the accusative strictly expresses cause ("because of").
- **12:14** — "A time and times and half a time" echoes Daniel 7:25 and 12:7 (three and a half years), corresponding to the 1,260 days mentioned in 12:6.
- **12:17** — Greek τοῦ σπέρματος αὐτῆς ("her seed," rendered "her offspring" here) echoes the enmity between the serpent and the woman's seed in Genesis 3:15.
- **12:18** — The Saints Greek Critical Text reads third-person ἐστάθη ("he stood," referring to the dragon), whereas some later manuscripts read first-person ἐστάθην ("I stood," referring to John) and attach the clause to 13:1.
