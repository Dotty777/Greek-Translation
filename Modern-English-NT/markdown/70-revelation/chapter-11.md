## Text

**11:1** And a reed like a staff was given to me, and someone said, "Rise and measure the temple of God and the altar and those who worship in it.

**11:2** But leave out the court that is outside the temple and do not measure it, because it was given to the nations, and they will trample the holy city for forty-two months.

**11:3** And I will grant it to My two witnesses, and they will prophesy for one thousand two hundred sixty days, clothed in sackcloth."

**11:4** These are the two olive trees and the two lampstands that stand before the Lord of the earth.

**11:5** And if anyone desires to harm them, fire comes out from their mouth and devours their enemies; and if anyone should desire to harm them, in this way he must be killed.

**11:6** These have the authority to shut the sky so that rain will not fall during the days of their prophecy, and they have authority over the waters to turn them into blood and to strike the earth with every plague as often as they desire.

**11:7** And when they have finished their testimony, the beast that comes up out of the abyss will make war against them and conquer them and kill them.

**11:8** And their corpse will lie on the street of the great city, which is spiritually called Sodom and Egypt, where their Lord also was crucified.

**11:9** And some from the peoples and tribes and languages and nations look at their corpse for three and a half days, and they do not permit their corpses to be placed in a tomb.

**11:10** And those who dwell on the earth rejoice over them and celebrate, and they will send gifts to one another, because these two prophets tormented those who dwell on the earth.

**11:11** And after the three and a half days, a breath of life from God entered into them, and they stood on their feet, and great fear fell upon those who were watching them.

**11:12** And they heard a loud voice from heaven saying to them, "Come up here," and they went up into heaven in the cloud, and their enemies watched them.

**11:13** And in that hour a great earthquake occurred, and a tenth of the city fell, and seven thousand people were killed in the earthquake; and the rest became terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven.

**11:14** The second woe has passed; look, the third woe is coming quickly.

**11:15** And the seventh angel sounded his trumpet, and there were loud voices in heaven, saying, "The kingdom of the world has become our Lord's and His Christ's, and He will reign forever and ever."

**11:16** And the twenty-four elders, who sit on their thrones before God, fell on their faces and worshiped God,

**11:17** saying, "We give thanks to You, Lord God, the Almighty, the One who is and who was, and because You have taken Your great power and begun to reign.

**11:18** And the nations were enraged, and Your wrath came, and the time for the dead to be judged, and to give the reward to Your slaves the prophets and to the saints and to those who fear Your name, the small and the great, and to destroy those who destroy the earth."

**11:19** And the temple of God that is in heaven was opened, and the ark of His covenant was seen in His temple; and there were flashes of lightning and sounds and peals of thunder and an earthquake and heavy hail.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — The Greek participle rendered "and someone said" (legon, lit. "saying") is masculine singular without an expressed subject; some later manuscripts add "and the angel stood" to supply one.
- **11:3** — Literally "I will give to My two witnesses, and they will prophesy," a Semitic paratactic idiom expressing the granting of a commission or authority to prophesy.
- **11:8** — The Greek uses the collective singular "corpse" (ptoma) here and in the first half of 11:9 before switching to the plural "corpses" (ptomata) in the second half of 11:9. "Spiritually" (pneumatikos) denotes a spiritual or symbolic sense.
- **11:13** — "Seven thousand people" renders the literal Greek "names of people, seven thousand" (onomata anthropon chiliades hepta), an idiom for counting individual persons.
- **11:17** — Unlike 1:4, 1:8, and 4:8, the title here has no "and who is coming" after "who is and who was" (some later manuscripts add it); the aorist ebasileusas ("begun to reign") is ingressive.
