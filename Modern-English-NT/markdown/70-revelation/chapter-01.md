## Text

**1:1** The revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave to Him to show His slaves the things that must happen soon; and He signified it by sending it through His angel to His slave John,

**1:2** who testified to the word of God and the testimony of Jesus Christ—everything he saw.

**1:3** Blessed is the one who reads and those who hear the words of the prophecy and keep the things written in it, because the time is near.

**1:4** John, to the seven churches that are in Asia: Grace to you and peace from the One who is and who was and who is coming, and from the seven spirits that are before His throne,

**1:5** and from Jesus Christ, the faithful witness, the firstborn of the dead, and the ruler of the kings of the earth. To the One who loves us and freed us from our sins by His blood—

**1:6** and He made us a kingdom, priests to His God and Father—to Him be the glory and the might forever and ever. Amen.

**1:7** Look, He is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see Him, including those who pierced Him, and all the tribes of the earth will wail over Him. Yes, amen.

**1:8** "I am the Alpha and the Omega," says the Lord God, the One who is and who was and who is coming, the Almighty.

**1:9** I, John, your brother and fellow sharer in the affliction and kingdom and endurance in Jesus, was on the island called Patmos on account of the word of God and the testimony of Jesus.

**1:10** I was in the Spirit on the Lord's day, and I heard a loud voice behind me, like a trumpet,

**1:11** saying, "Write in a scroll what you see and send it to the seven churches: to Ephesus, and to Smyrna, and to Pergamum, and to Thyatira, and to Sardis, and to Philadelphia, and to Laodicea."

**1:12** And I turned to see the voice that was speaking with me, and when I turned I saw seven golden lampstands,

**1:13** and in the middle of the lampstands, one like a son of man, dressed in a robe reaching to the feet and belted around His chest with a golden sash.

**1:14** His head and hair were white as white wool, as snow, and His eyes were like a flame of fire,

**1:15** and His feet were like fine bronze, as fired in a furnace, and His voice was like the sound of many waters,

**1:16** and in His right hand He was holding seven stars, and from His mouth a sharp double-edged sword was coming out, and His face was like the sun shining in its power.

**1:17** And when I saw Him, I fell at His feet like a dead man; and He laid His right hand on me, saying, "Do not be afraid. I am the first and the last,

**1:18** and the living One—and I died, and look, I am alive forever and ever—and I have the keys of death and of Hades.

**1:19** Write therefore the things you saw, and the things that are, and the things that are about to happen after these things.

**1:20** Regarding the mystery of the seven stars that you saw on My right hand, and the seven golden lampstands: the seven stars are the angels of the seven churches, and the seven lampstands are the seven churches."

## Translator Notes

- **1:4** — The Greek places the title "the One who is and who was and who is coming" in the nominative case after the preposition "from" (which normally takes the genitive), treating it as indeclinable. "The seven spirits" is variously understood as the sevenfold fullness of the Holy Spirit (cf. Isaiah 11:2) or seven heavenly spirits before God's throne.
- **1:5** — The Greek text reads lysanti ("freed" or "released"), attested by the earliest manuscripts, whereas later manuscripts read lousanti ("washed"), a variant arising from their identical pronunciation in Koine Greek.
- **1:10** — "The Lord's day" (he kyriake hemera) uses the adjective kyriakos ("belonging to the Lord"); the phrase occurs only here in the New Testament. Greek ἐν πνεύματι lacks the article and may be rendered either "in the Spirit" (moved by the Holy Spirit) or "in spirit" (in a visionary spiritual state; cf. 4:2, 17:3, 21:10).
- **1:15** — The rare Greek term chalkolibanon ("fine bronze"), found only here and in 2:18, refers to a gleaming copper or brass alloy whose exact composition is uncertain. The following participle pepyromenes ("fired") appears in the feminine genitive singular, creating a notable grammatical mismatch with the masculine plural "feet" and neuter dative "bronze."
- **1:20** — The Greek word angeloi can mean either "angels" or "messengers." Interpreters debate whether the angels of the seven churches are heavenly beings representing each congregation, human leaders or envoys, or symbolic personifications of the churches themselves.
