## Text

**10:1** And I saw another mighty angel descending from heaven, clothed with a cloud, and a rainbow was upon his head; his face was like the sun, and his feet were like pillars of fire,

**10:2** and he held in his hand a little scroll lying open. And he set his right foot upon the sea and his left foot upon the earth,

**10:3** and he cried out with a loud voice, as when a lion roars. And when he cried out, the seven thunders uttered their own voices.

**10:4** Now when the seven thunders had spoken, I was about to write; and I heard a voice from heaven saying, "Seal up the things which the seven thunders have spoken, and do not write them."

**10:5** Then the angel whom I saw standing upon the sea and upon the earth lifted up his right hand toward heaven,

**10:6** and swore by Him who lives forever and ever, who created heaven and the things that are in it, and the earth and the things that are in it, and the sea and the things that are in it, that there shall be delay no longer;

**10:7** but in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he is about to sound his trumpet, the mystery of God shall be fulfilled, just as He declared the good tidings to His own servants the prophets.

**10:8** Then the voice which I had heard from heaven spoke with me again, saying, "Go, take the scroll that lies open in the hand of the angel who stands upon the sea and upon the earth."

**10:9** So I went over to the angel, asking him to give me the little scroll. And he said to me, "Take it and eat it up; it will make your stomach bitter, yet in your mouth it will be sweet as honey."

**10:10** And I took the little scroll from the hand of the angel and ate it up; and in my mouth it was sweet as honey, yet when I had eaten it, my stomach was made bitter.

**10:11** And they said to me, "You must prophesy again concerning peoples, nations, tongues, and many kings."

## Translator Notes

- **10:2** — Greek biblaridion ("little scroll") is a diminutive of biblion (used in 10:8) and denotes a small rolled document rather than a bound book.
- **10:6** — Greek chronos ouketi estai can mean either "there shall be delay no longer" (fitting the context of 6:10-11 and 10:7) or literally "time shall be no more."
- **10:7** — The Greek verb etelesthe is an aorist passive expressing prophetic certainty ("shall be fulfilled" or "is accomplished").
- **10:11** — The critical text reads the plural "they said to me" (legousin moi), either an indefinite plural or referring to the heavenly voice and the mighty angel; later manuscripts read the singular "he said."
