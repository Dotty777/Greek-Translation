## Text

**16:1** Now He was also saying to the disciples, "There was a certain rich man who had a steward, and this man was accused to him of squandering his possessions.

**16:2** And calling him, he said to him, 'What is this I hear concerning you? Render the account of your stewardship, for you can no longer be steward.'

**16:3** Then the steward said within himself, 'What shall I do, since my master is taking the stewardship away from me? I do not have the strength to dig; I am ashamed to beg.

**16:4** I have decided what I will do, so that when I am removed from the stewardship, they may welcome me into their houses.'

**16:5** So summoning each one of his master's debtors, he said to the first, 'How much do you owe my master?'

**16:6** And he said, 'One hundred baths of oil.' Then he said to him, 'Take your bill, sit down quickly, and write fifty.'

**16:7** Next he said to another, 'And how much do you owe?' And he said, 'One hundred kors of wheat.' He said to him, 'Take your bill and write eighty.'

**16:8** And the master commended the unrighteous steward because he had acted shrewdly; for the sons of this age are more shrewd toward their own generation than the sons of light.

**16:9** And I say to you, make friends for yourselves by means of unrighteous mammon, so that when it fails, they may welcome you into the eternal dwellings.

**16:10** Whoever is faithful in a very small matter is faithful also in much, and whoever is unrighteous in a very small matter is unrighteous also in much.

**16:11** Therefore, if you have not proven faithful in unrighteous mammon, who will entrust to you that which is true?

**16:12** And if you have not proven faithful in what belongs to another, who will give to you what is your own?

**16:13** No household servant is able to serve two masters; for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will be devoted to one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon."

**16:14** Now the Pharisees, who were lovers of money, were listening to all these things, and they were scoffing at Him.

**16:15** And He said to them, "You are the ones who justify yourselves in the sight of men, but God knows your hearts; for that which is exalted among men is an abomination before God.

**16:16** The Law and the Prophets were until John; from that time the kingdom of God is proclaimed as good news, and everyone presses forcefully into it.

**16:17** Yet it is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one stroke of a letter of the Law to fall.

**16:18** Everyone who divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery, and he who marries one divorced from a husband commits adultery.

**16:19** Now there was a certain rich man, and he dressed in purple and fine linen, feasting sumptuously every day.

**16:20** And a certain poor man named Lazarus, covered with sores, had been laid at his gate,

**16:21** and longing to be filled from what fell from the rich man's table; moreover, even the dogs came and licked his sores.

**16:22** Now it came to pass that the poor man died and was carried away by the angels into Abraham's bosom. The rich man also died and was buried.

**16:23** And in Hades, lifting up his eyes while being in torments, he saw Abraham from afar and Lazarus in his bosom.

**16:24** And crying out, he said, 'Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus so that he may dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue; for I am in agony in this flame.'

**16:25** But Abraham said, 'Child, remember that in your lifetime you received your good things, and Lazarus likewise bad things; yet now he is comforted here, while you are in agony.

**16:26** And besides all this, between us and you a great chasm has been firmly fixed, so that those who wish to cross over from here to you are not able, nor can any pass from there to us.'

**16:27** Then he said, 'Therefore I beg you, father, that you send him to my father's house,

**16:28** for I have five brothers, so that he may solemnly warn them, lest they also come into this place of torment.'

**16:29** But Abraham said, 'They have Moses and the Prophets; let them listen to them.'

**16:30** And he said, 'No, father Abraham; rather, if someone goes to them from the dead, they will repent.'

**16:31** But he said to him, 'If they do not listen to Moses and the Prophets, neither will they be persuaded even if someone rises from the dead.'"

## Translator Notes

- **16:6** — Greek "batous" (Hebrew "baths") and "korous" ("kors" in verse 7) are large Hebrew measures: one hundred baths of olive oil equaled roughly eight to nine hundred gallons, and one hundred kors of wheat equaled roughly one thousand bushels.
- **16:8** — Greek "ho kyrios" here refers to the rich master within the parable rather than to Jesus, though a minority of interpreters take it as the Lord's own commentary.
- **16:9** — "Mammon" is an Aramaic term for wealth or worldly possessions; the critical Greek text reads the singular "when it fails" (referring to wealth), whereas some later manuscripts read the plural "when you fail."
- **16:16** — The Greek verb "biazetai" is debated: taken as middle voice it means "presses forcefully into it," while taken as passive it can mean "is strongly urged into it."
- **16:21** — The critical Greek text reads "what fell from the rich man's table," without the word "crumbs" supplied in later manuscripts.
