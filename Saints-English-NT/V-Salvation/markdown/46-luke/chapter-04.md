## Text

**4:1** Now Jesus, full of the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan and was led by the Spirit in the wilderness

**4:2** for forty days, being tempted by the devil. And He ate nothing during those days, and when they had come to an end, He became hungry.

**4:3** Then the devil said to Him, "If You are the Son of God, speak to this stone that it may become bread."

**4:4** And Jesus replied to him, "It is written, 'Man shall not live on bread alone.'"

**4:5** And leading Him up, he showed Him all the kingdoms of the inhabited world in an instant of time.

**4:6** And the devil said to Him, "To You I will give all this authority and their glory, for it has been delivered to me, and to whomever I desire I give it.

**4:7** If You, therefore, worship before me, it shall all be Yours."

**4:8** And Jesus answered and said to him, "It is written, 'The Lord your God you shall worship, and Him alone you shall serve.'"

**4:9** Then he led Him to Jerusalem and set Him upon the pinnacle of the temple, and said to Him, "If You are the Son of God, cast Yourself down from here;

**4:10** for it is written, 'He shall command His angels concerning You, to guard You carefully,'

**4:11** and, 'On their hands they shall lift You up, lest You strike Your foot against a stone.'"

**4:12** And answering, Jesus said to him, "It has been said, 'You shall not put the Lord your God to the test.'"

**4:13** And when the devil had finished every temptation, he withdrew from Him until an opportune season.

**4:14** And Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit into Galilee, and a report concerning Him went forth through all the surrounding region.

**4:15** And He Himself was teaching in their synagogues, being glorified by all.

**4:16** And He came to Nazareth, where He had been raised; and according to His custom, He entered into the synagogue on the Sabbath day and stood up to read.

**4:17** And the scroll of the prophet Isaiah was handed to Him; and unrolling the scroll, He found the place where it was written:

**4:18** "The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me, because He has anointed Me to bring good tidings to the poor; He has sent Me to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to send forth the oppressed in liberty,

**4:19** to herald the acceptable year of the Lord."

**4:20** And rolling up the scroll, He returned it to the attendant and sat down. And the eyes of all in the synagogue were fixed intently upon Him.

**4:21** Then He began to speak to them, "Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing."

**4:22** And all bore witness to Him and marveled at the words of grace that proceeded from His mouth, and they said, "Is this not the son of Joseph?"

**4:23** And He said to them, "No doubt you will say this proverb to Me: 'Physician, heal yourself! Whatever we heard took place in Capernaum, do here also in Your own homeland.'"

**4:24** And He said, "Assuredly, I say to you, no prophet is acceptable in his own homeland.

**4:25** And in truth I tell you, there were many widows in Israel in the days of Elijah, when heaven was shut up for three years and six months, when a great famine came upon all the land;

**4:26** yet Elijah was sent to none of them, but only to Zarephath in the territory of Sidon, to a woman who was a widow.

**4:27** And many lepers were in Israel during the time of Elisha the prophet, yet not one of them was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian."

**4:28** And all in the synagogue were filled with wrath as they heard these things,

**4:29** and rising up, they drove Him out of the city and led Him to the brow of the hill on which their city had been built, in order to hurl Him down the cliff.

**4:30** But He, passing through the midst of them, went on His way.

**4:31** And He went down to Capernaum, a city of Galilee; and He was teaching them on the Sabbath days.

**4:32** And they were astounded at His teaching, because His word was with authority.

**4:33** And in the synagogue there was a man having a spirit of an unclean demon, and he cried out with a loud voice,

**4:34** "Ah! What have we to do with You, Jesus of Nazareth? Have You come to destroy us? I know You, who You are—the Holy One of God!"

**4:35** And Jesus rebuked it, saying, "Be silent, and come out of him!" And throwing him down in their midst, the demon came out of him, doing him no harm.

**4:36** And amazement came upon all, and they spoke together with one another, saying, "What is this word, that with authority and power He commands the unclean spirits, and they come out?"

**4:37** And news concerning Him went forth into every place of the surrounding region.

**4:38** Now arising from the synagogue, He entered into the house of Simon. And Simon's mother-in-law was afflicted with a high fever, and they appealed to Him on her behalf.

**4:39** And standing over her, He rebuked the fever, and it left her; and immediately she arose and ministered to them.

**4:40** Now as the sun was setting, all who had any suffering from various diseases brought them to Him; and laying His hands upon each one of them, He healed them.

**4:41** And demons also were coming out of many, crying out and saying, "You are the Son of God!" Yet rebuking them, He did not permit them to speak, because they knew Him to be the Christ.

**4:42** Now when day came, He departed and went to a desolate place; and the multitudes searched for Him and came to Him, and tried to detain Him so that He would not depart from them.

**4:43** But He said to them, "I must bring the good tidings of the kingdom of God to the other cities also, because for this purpose I was sent."

**4:44** And He continued preaching in the synagogues of Judea.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — The earliest Greek manuscripts end the quotation of Deuteronomy 8:3 here without the longer clause "but by every word of God," which entered later manuscripts from Matthew 4:4.
- **4:8** — The critical Greek text omits "Get behind Me, Satan!" (added in later manuscripts in assimilation to Matthew 4:10) and opens Jesus' reply directly with "It is written."
- **4:18** — Luke quotes Isaiah 61:1-2 joined with Isaiah 58:6 from the Septuagint; the earliest manuscripts omit the phrase "to heal the brokenhearted" found in later textual traditions.
- **4:44** — The earliest Greek manuscripts read "Judea" rather than "Galilee," using "Judea" in Luke's broader sense for the entire Jewish homeland (compare Luke 1:5; 23:5).
