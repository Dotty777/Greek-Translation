## Text

**18:1** Now He spoke a parable to them that they ought to pray at all times and not grow faint,

**18:2** saying, "In a certain city there was a judge who did not fear God and had no regard for man.

**18:3** And in that city there was a widow, and she kept coming to him, saying, 'Vindicate me against my adversary.'

**18:4** And for a time he was unwilling; yet afterward he said within himself, 'Even though I do not fear God nor have regard for man,

**18:5** yet because this widow causes me trouble, I will vindicate her, lest by coming continually she wear me down.'"

**18:6** Then the Lord said, "Hear what the unjust judge says.

**18:7** And shall God not bring about vindication for His elect who cry out to Him day and night, even though He bears long over them?

**18:8** I say to you that He will bring about their vindication swiftly. Nevertheless, when the Son of Man comes, will He indeed find faith upon the earth?"

**18:9** And He also spoke this parable to certain ones who trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and held the rest in contempt:

**18:10** "Two men went up to the temple to pray, the one a Pharisee and the other a tax collector.

**18:11** The Pharisee stood and prayed these things to himself: 'O God, I give thanks to You that I am not like the rest of men—extortioners, unrighteous, adulterers—or even like this tax collector.

**18:12** I fast twice a week; I pay tithes on all that I acquire.'

**18:13** But the tax collector, standing at a distance, was unwilling even to lift up his eyes to heaven, but kept beating his breast, saying, 'O God, be merciful to me, the sinner!'

**18:14** I say to you, this man went down to his house justified rather than that one; for everyone who exalts himself shall be humbled, and the one who humbles himself shall be exalted."

**18:15** Now they were bringing even infants to Him that He might touch them; and when the disciples saw this, they rebuked them.

**18:16** But Jesus called them to Himself, saying, "Let the little children come to Me, and do not hinder them; for to such as these belongs the kingdom of God.

**18:17** Assuredly, I say to you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a little child shall never enter into it."

**18:18** And a certain ruler questioned Him, saying, "Good Teacher, what must I do that I may inherit eternal life?"

**18:19** Then Jesus said to him, "Why do you call Me good? None is good except One alone—God.

**18:20** You know the commandments: 'Do not commit adultery; Do not murder; Do not steal; Do not bear false witness; Honor your father and mother.'"

**18:21** And he replied, "I have kept all these things from my youth."

**18:22** When Jesus heard this, He said to him, "One thing you still lack: sell everything you possess and distribute to the poor, and you shall have treasure in the heavens; and come, follow Me."

**18:23** But when he heard these things, he became deeply sorrowful, for he was exceedingly wealthy.

**18:24** And looking at him, Jesus said, "How difficult it is for those who possess wealth to enter into the kingdom of God!

**18:25** For it is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God."

**18:26** And those who heard said, "Who then is able to be saved?"

**18:27** But He said, "The things impossible with men are possible with God."

**18:28** Then Peter said, "Behold, we have left all things and have followed You."

**18:29** And He said to them, "Assuredly, I say to you that there is no one who has left house or wife or brothers or parents or children on account of the kingdom of God,

**18:30** who shall not receive manifold more in this present time, and in the coming age, eternal life."

**18:31** Then taking the twelve aside, He said to them, "Behold, we are going up to Jerusalem, and all the things written through the prophets concerning the Son of Man shall be accomplished.

**18:32** For He shall be handed over to the Gentiles, and shall be mocked and shamefully treated and spat upon;

**18:33** and after scourging Him, they shall put Him to death; and on the third day He shall rise again."

**18:34** Yet they understood none of these things; and this saying was concealed from them, and they did not grasp what was being spoken.

**18:35** Now it came to pass, as He drew near to Jericho, that a certain blind man was sitting beside the road begging.

**18:36** And hearing a multitude passing by, he inquired what this might be.

**18:37** And they reported to him that Jesus the Nazarene was passing by.

**18:38** And he cried aloud, saying, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!"

**18:39** And those who were going ahead rebuked him that he should keep silent; yet he cried out all the more, "Son of David, have mercy on me!"

**18:40** Then Jesus stopped and commanded that he be brought to Him; and when he had drawn near, He questioned him,

**18:41** "What do you want Me to do for you?" And he said, "Lord, that I may regain my sight."

**18:42** And Jesus said to him, "Regain your sight; your faith has saved you."

**18:43** And immediately he regained his sight and began following Him, glorifying God; and when all the people saw it, they gave praise to God.

## Translator Notes

- **18:5** — The Greek verb hypopiazō literally means "to strike under the eye" and is used idiomatically here for wearing someone down through relentless persistence.
- **18:7** — The final clause (kai makrothumei ep' autois) is syntactically debated and may be read either concessively ("even though He bears long over them?") or as a second rhetorical question ("and will He delay long over them?").
- **18:13** — The Greek verb hilasthēti carries the liturgical sense of "be propitious" or "show atoning mercy," and the definite article in tō hamartōlō ("the sinner") underscores the tax collector's deep sense of personal guilt.
- **18:24** — Following the Saints Greek Critical Text, this verse reads simply "looking at him, Jesus said," without the additional phrase "seeing that he had become very sorrowful" present in later manuscripts.
- **18:42** — The Greek verb sōzō ("has saved you") holds a purposeful dual sense in Luke, signifying both physical healing ("has made you well") and spiritual salvation.
