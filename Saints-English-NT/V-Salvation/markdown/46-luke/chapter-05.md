## Text

**5:1** Now it came to pass, while the multitude pressed upon Him and heard the word of God, that He was standing beside the Lake of Gennesaret,

**5:2** and He saw two boats standing by the lake; but the fishermen had stepped out of them and were washing their nets.

**5:3** Then He stepped into one of the boats, which was Simon's, and asked him to put out a little from the land. And sitting down, He taught the multitudes from the boat.

**5:4** When He had ceased speaking, He said to Simon, "Put out into the deep water, and let down your nets for a catch."

**5:5** And Simon answered and said, "Master, we have labored through the whole night and caught nothing; yet at Your word I will let down the nets."

**5:6** And when they had done this, they enclosed a great multitude of fish, and their nets were beginning to tear.

**5:7** So they signaled to their partners in the other boat to come and help them. And they came and filled both boats, so that they began to sink.

**5:8** When Simon Peter saw this, he fell down at the knees of Jesus, saying, "Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, O Lord!"

**5:9** For astonishment had seized him and all those with him at the catch of fish which they had taken,

**5:10** and likewise also James and John, the sons of Zebedee, who were partners with Simon. And Jesus said to Simon, "Do not be afraid; from now on you shall be catching men alive."

**5:11** And when they had brought the boats to land, they forsook all and followed Him.

**5:12** And it came to pass, while He was in one of the cities, that behold, a man full of leprosy was there. Seeing Jesus, he fell upon his face and implored Him, saying, "Lord, if You are willing, You are able to cleanse me."

**5:13** Then He stretched out His hand and touched him, saying, "I am willing; be cleansed." And immediately the leprosy departed from him.

**5:14** And He charged him to tell no one, "but go and show yourself to the priest, and make an offering for your cleansing, just as Moses commanded, as a testimony to them."

**5:15** Yet the report concerning Him spread all the more, and great multitudes gathered together to hear and to be healed of their infirmities.

**5:16** But He Himself would withdraw into the desolate places and pray.

**5:17** Now it came to pass on one of those days, while He was teaching, that Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there, who had come from every village of Galilee and Judea and from Jerusalem. And the power of the Lord was present for Him to heal.

**5:18** And behold, men were carrying on a bed a man who was paralyzed, and they sought to bring him in and set him down before Him.

**5:19** And not finding any way to bring him in because of the multitude, they went up onto the housetop and let him down through the tiles with his couch into the midst before Jesus.

**5:20** And seeing their faith, He said, "Man, your sins have been forgiven you."

**5:21** And the scribes and the Pharisees began to reason, saying, "Who is this who speaks blasphemies? Who is able to forgive sins except God alone?"

**5:22** But Jesus, perceiving their reasonings, answered and said to them, "Why do you reason in your hearts?

**5:23** Which is easier, to say, 'Your sins have been forgiven you,' or to say, 'Arise and walk'?

**5:24** But that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins"—He said to the paralyzed man, "I say to you, arise, take up your couch, and go to your house."

**5:25** And at once he rose up before them, took up that on which he had been lying, and departed to his house, glorifying God.

**5:26** And amazement seized them all, and they glorified God and were filled with fear, saying, "We have seen extraordinary things today!"

**5:27** And after these things He went forth and beheld a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax office. And He said to him, "Follow Me."

**5:28** And leaving all behind, he rose up and followed Him.

**5:29** Then Levi held a great banquet for Him in his house; and there was a great multitude of tax collectors and others who were reclining at table with them.

**5:30** And the Pharisees and their scribes murmured against His disciples, saying, "Why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?"

**5:31** And Jesus answered and said to them, "Those who are healthy have no need of a physician, but those who are sick.

**5:32** I have come not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance."

**5:33** Then they said to Him, "The disciples of John fast often and offer supplications, and likewise those of the Pharisees, but Yours eat and drink."

**5:34** And Jesus said to them, "Can you make the sons of the bridal chamber fast while the bridegroom is with them?

**5:35** Yet the days will come, and when the bridegroom is taken away from them, then they shall fast in those days."

**5:36** Then He also spoke a parable to them: "No one tears a patch from a new garment and puts it on an old garment; otherwise he will both tear the new, and the patch from the new will not match the old.

**5:37** And no one puts new wine into old wineskins; otherwise the new wine will burst the wineskins, and it will be spilled, and the wineskins will be ruined.

**5:38** Rather, new wine must be put into fresh wineskins.

**5:39** And no one, having drunk old wine, desires new; for he says, 'The old is good.'"

## Translator Notes

- **5:10** — The Greek verb zogron literally means "catching alive," contrasting the apostles' new calling to bring people into life with their former work of catching fish.
- **5:17** — The Greek text reads "for Him to heal" (auton), referring to the Lord's power working through Jesus, rather than the later manuscript reading "to heal them."
- **5:26** — The Greek word paradoxa ("extraordinary things") occurs only here in the New Testament and denotes events contrary to or beyond human expectation.
- **5:38** — The Greek text concludes at "fresh wineskins," without the harmonized clause "and both are preserved" found in Matthew 9:17 and later manuscripts.
- **5:39** — The Greek text reads the positive adjective chrestos ("good") rather than the comparative reading "better" found in later manuscripts.
