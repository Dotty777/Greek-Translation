## Text

**10:1** Now after these things the Lord appointed seventy-two others as well, and sent them two by two before His face into every city and place where He Himself was about to go.

**10:2** And He said to them, "The harvest indeed is abundant, yet the laborers are few; therefore pray to the Lord of the harvest that He may send forth laborers into His harvest.

**10:3** Go your way; behold, I am sending you forth as lambs in the midst of wolves.

**10:4** Carry no moneybag, no knapsack, and no sandals; and greet no one along the road.

**10:5** And into whatever house you enter, first say, 'Peace be to this house.'

**10:6** And if a son of peace is there, your peace shall rest upon him; but if not, it shall return to you.

**10:7** And remain in that same house, eating and drinking what is provided by them, for the laborer is worthy of his wages. Do not move from house to house.

**10:8** And into whatever city you enter and they receive you, eat the things set before you,

**10:9** and heal the sick who are in it, and say to them, 'The kingdom of God has drawn near to you.'

**10:10** Yet into whatever city you enter and they do not receive you, go out into its streets and say,

**10:11** 'Even the dust from your city that has clung to our feet we wipe off against you; nevertheless know this, that the kingdom of God has drawn near.'

**10:12** I say to you that on that day it shall be more bearable for Sodom than for that city.

**10:13** Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the mighty works done among you had been performed in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago, sitting in sackcloth and ashes.

**10:14** Yet it shall be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon in the judgment than for you.

**10:15** And you, Capernaum, will you be exalted to heaven? You shall descend to Hades!

**10:16** The one who hears you hears Me, and the one who rejects you rejects Me; and the one who rejects Me rejects Him who sent Me."

**10:17** Then the seventy-two returned with joy, saying, "Lord, even the demons are subject to us in Your name!"

**10:18** And He said to them, "I was watching Satan fall like lightning from heaven.

**10:19** Behold, I have given you authority to tread upon serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall by any means harm you.

**10:20** Nevertheless do not rejoice in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but rejoice that your names have been written in the heavens."

**10:21** In that very hour He rejoiced greatly in the Holy Spirit and said, "I praise You, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because You have hidden these things from the wise and understanding and revealed them to little children; yes, Father, for thus it was well-pleasing in Your sight.

**10:22** All things have been delivered to Me by My Father, and no one knows who the Son is except the Father, and who the Father is except the Son and the one to whom the Son wills to reveal Him."

**10:23** And turning to the disciples privately, He said, "Blessed are the eyes that see the things you see!

**10:24** For I say to you that many prophets and kings desired to see what you are seeing and did not see it, and to hear what you are hearing and did not hear it."

**10:25** And behold, a certain lawyer stood up, testing Him and saying, "Teacher, what shall I do to inherit eternal life?"

**10:26** And He said to him, "What is written in the law? How do you read it?"

**10:27** And answering, he said, "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your strength, and with all your mind, and your neighbor as yourself."

**10:28** And He said to him, "You have answered rightly; do this, and you shall live."

**10:29** Yet he, desiring to justify himself, said to Jesus, "And who is my neighbor?"

**10:30** Jesus replied and said, "A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho and fell among robbers, who both stripped him and beat him, and departed, leaving him half dead.

**10:31** Now by chance a certain priest was going down along that road, and when he saw him, he passed by on the opposite side.

**10:32** Likewise a Levite also, coming to that place and seeing him, passed by on the opposite side.

**10:33** But a certain Samaritan, as he journeyed, came upon him; and seeing him, he was moved with compassion,

**10:34** and coming near, he bound up his wounds, pouring on oil and wine; and setting him upon his own animal, he brought him to an inn and cared for him.

**10:35** And on the next day, taking out two denarii, he gave them to the innkeeper and said, 'Care for him, and whatever more you spend, when I return I will repay you.'

**10:36** Which of these three, do you think, proved to be a neighbor to him who fell among the robbers?"

**10:37** And he said, "The one who showed mercy to him." Then Jesus said to him, "Go, and you also do likewise."

**10:38** Now as they went on their way, He entered a certain village; and a certain woman named Martha welcomed Him.

**10:39** And she had a sister called Mary, who also sat at the feet of Jesus and was listening to His word.

**10:40** But Martha was distracted with much serving; and she came up and said, "Lord, do You not care that my sister has left me alone to serve? Therefore tell her to help me."

**10:41** And the Lord answered and said to her, "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things,

**10:42** yet few things are needed, or indeed one; for Mary has chosen the good portion, which shall not be taken away from her."

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The Greek critical text reads 'seventy-two' (hebdomekonta dyo, also in 10:17), whereas many later manuscripts read 'seventy.'
- **10:15** — The Greek question with me expects a negative answer; the critical text also reads the active/middle 'you shall descend' (katabese) rather than the passive 'you shall be brought down' found in later manuscripts.
- **10:21** — The Greek critical text specifies 'in the Holy Spirit' (to pneumati to hagio), whereas later manuscripts read simply 'in spirit.'
- **10:42** — The Greek critical text preserves the fuller reading 'yet few things are needed, or indeed one' (oligon de estin chreia e henos), which many manuscripts shorten to 'but one thing is needed.'
