## Text

**13:1** Now at that very time some were present who brought Him word concerning the Galileans whose blood Pilate had mingled with their sacrifices.

**13:2** And in answer He said to them, "Do you suppose that these Galileans were greater sinners than all other Galileans because they have suffered these things?

**13:3** No, I say to you; rather, unless you repent, you shall all likewise perish.

**13:4** Or those eighteen upon whom the tower in Siloam fell and killed them—do you suppose that they were greater offenders than all the people who dwell in Jerusalem?

**13:5** No, I say to you; rather, unless you repent, you shall all perish in the same manner."

**13:6** Then He spoke this parable: "A certain man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard, and he came seeking fruit upon it and found none.

**13:7** And he said to the vinedresser, 'Behold, for three years I have been coming seeking fruit on this fig tree, and I find none. Cut it down! Why does it even exhaust the ground?'

**13:8** But in answer he said to him, 'Sir, leave it alone this year also, until I dig around it and put manure on it;

**13:9** and if it bears fruit in the time to come—but if not, you shall cut it down.'"

**13:10** Now He was teaching in one of the synagogues on the Sabbath.

**13:11** And behold, there was a woman who had a spirit of infirmity for eighteen years; she was bent over and altogether unable to straighten herself up.

**13:12** When Jesus saw her, He called her forward and said to her, "Woman, you are set free from your infirmity."

**13:13** And He laid His hands upon her, and immediately she was made straight and began glorifying God.

**13:14** But the ruler of the synagogue, indignant because Jesus had healed on the Sabbath, answered and said to the multitude, "There are six days in which work ought to be done; therefore come and be healed on those days, and not on the day of the Sabbath."

**13:15** Then the Lord answered him and said, "Hypocrites! Does not each of you on the Sabbath loose his ox or donkey from the manger and lead it away to water it?

**13:16** And ought not this woman, being a daughter of Abraham whom Satan has bound—behold, for eighteen years—to be loosed from this bond on the Sabbath day?"

**13:17** And as He was saying these things, all His adversaries were put to shame; and the entire multitude rejoiced over all the glorious deeds that were being done by Him.

**13:18** Therefore He said, "What is the kingdom of God like, and to what shall I compare it?

**13:19** It is like a grain of mustard seed, which a man took and cast into his own garden; and it grew and became a tree, and the birds of the air nested in its branches."

**13:20** And again He said, "To what shall I compare the kingdom of God?

**13:21** It is like leaven, which a woman took and hid in three measures of flour until the whole of it was leavened."

**13:22** And He went on His way through cities and villages, teaching and making His journey toward Jerusalem.

**13:23** Then someone said to Him, "Lord, are there only a few who are being saved?" And He said to them,

**13:24** "Strive to enter through the narrow door, for many, I tell you, will seek to enter and will not be able.

**13:25** Once the Master of the house has risen and shut the door, and you begin to stand outside and knock at the door, saying, 'Lord, open to us!' He will answer and say to you, 'I do not know you, where you are from.'

**13:26** Then you will begin to say, 'We ate and drank in Your presence, and You taught in our streets.'

**13:27** And He will speak, saying to you, 'I do not know where you are from. Depart from Me, all you workers of iniquity!'

**13:28** There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth when you see Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and all the prophets in the kingdom of God, and yourselves cast outside.

**13:29** And people shall come from east and west, and from north and south, and shall recline at table in the kingdom of God.

**13:30** And behold, there are last who shall be first, and there are first who shall be last."

**13:31** At that very hour some Pharisees came up, saying to Him, "Depart and go away from here, for Herod desires to kill You."

**13:32** And He said to them, "Go and tell that fox, 'Behold, I cast out demons and perform healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I reach completion.'

**13:33** Nevertheless, I must journey on today and tomorrow and the day following, for it cannot be that a prophet should perish outside of Jerusalem.

**13:34** Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets and stones those who have been sent to her—how often I desired to gather your children together, just as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, and you were not willing!

**13:35** Behold, your house is left to you. And I say to you, you shall by no means see Me until the time comes when you say, 'Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord!'"

## Translator Notes

- **13:9** — The Greek employs an intentional break (aposiopesis) after the first condition, leaving the implied positive outcome ("well and good") unspoken.
- **13:15** — The critical Greek text reads the plural "Hypocrites," indicating that Jesus addresses both the synagogue ruler and all who shared his objection.
- **13:32** — The Greek verb translated "reach completion" (teleioumai) conveys both finishing an appointed course and being brought to perfection in His redemptive work.
- **13:35** — Following the critical Greek text, "your house is left to you" omits the word "desolate" added in some later manuscripts.
