## Text

**9:1** Then having summoned the Twelve, He gave them power and authority over all the demons, and to cure diseases.

**9:2** And He sent them forth to proclaim the kingdom of God and to heal the sick.

**9:3** And He said to them, "Take nothing for the journey—neither staff, nor traveler's bag, nor bread, nor silver—nor are you to have two tunics.

**9:4** And into whatever house you enter, abide there, and depart from there.

**9:5** And as many as do not receive you, when you depart from that city, shake off the dust from your feet as a testimony against them."

**9:6** So departing, they passed through the villages, proclaiming the gospel and healing everywhere.

**9:7** Now Herod the tetrarch heard of all the things that were taking place, and he was deeply perplexed, because it was being said by some that John had been raised from the dead,

**9:8** and by some that Elijah had appeared, and by others that one of the ancient prophets had risen.

**9:9** And Herod said, "I myself beheaded John; yet who is this about whom I hear such things?" And he sought to see Him.

**9:10** And upon returning, the apostles recounted to Him all that they had done. And taking them with Him, He withdrew privately to a city called Bethsaida.

**9:11** But when the multitudes learned of it, they followed Him; and welcoming them, He spoke to them concerning the kingdom of God, and cured those who stood in need of healing.

**9:12** Now the day began to decline; and the Twelve approached and said to Him, "Dismiss the multitude, that they may go into the surrounding villages and countryside to lodge and find provisions, for here we are in a desolate place."

**9:13** But He said to them, "You yourselves give them something to eat." And they said, "We have no more than five loaves and two fish, unless we ourselves go and buy food for all this people."

**9:14** For there were about five thousand men. And He said to His disciples, "Have them recline in companies of about fifty each."

**9:15** And they did so, and had them all recline.

**9:16** Then taking the five loaves and the two fish, and looking up toward heaven, He blessed them and broke them, and gave them to the disciples to set before the multitude.

**9:17** And all ate and were filled, and what remained over to them was taken up—twelve baskets of broken pieces.

**9:18** And it came to pass, as He was praying alone, that the disciples were with Him; and He questioned them, saying, "Who do the multitudes say that I am?"

**9:19** And they answered and said, "John the Baptist; yet others say Elijah, and others that one of the ancient prophets has risen."

**9:20** Then He said to them, "But who do you yourselves say that I am?" And Peter answered and said, "The Christ of God."

**9:21** And strictly warning them, He commanded them to tell this to no one,

**9:22** saying, "The Son of Man must suffer many things, and be rejected by the elders and chief priests and scribes, and be slain, and on the third day be raised."

**9:23** And He was saying to all, "If anyone wishes to come after Me, let him deny himself and take up his cross day by day, and follow Me.

**9:24** For whoever wishes to save his life shall lose it; yet whoever loses his life on account of Me, that one shall save it.

**9:25** For how is a man profited if he gains the entire world, yet loses or forfeits his own self?

**9:26** For whoever is ashamed of Me and of My words, of that one the Son of Man shall be ashamed when He comes in His glory, and the glory of the Father and of the holy angels.

**9:27** Yet truly I say to you, there are some among those standing here who shall by no means taste death until they see the kingdom of God."

**9:28** Now it came to pass, about eight days after these sayings, that taking Peter and John and James with Him, He ascended the mountain to pray.

**9:29** And as He was praying, the appearance of His countenance became altered, and His apparel became dazzling white.

**9:30** And behold, two men were conversing with Him, who were Moses and Elijah,

**9:31** who appeared in glory and spoke of His departure, which He was about to fulfill in Jerusalem.

**9:32** Now Peter and those with him were weighed down with sleep; yet becoming fully awake, they beheld His glory and the two men who stood with Him.

**9:33** And it came to pass, as they were parting from Him, that Peter said to Jesus, "Master, it is good for us to be here; and let us make three tabernacles—one for You, and one for Moses, and one for Elijah"—not knowing what he was saying.

**9:34** While he was speaking these things, a cloud came and overshadowed them; and they became fearful as they entered into the cloud.

**9:35** And a voice came forth from the cloud, saying, "This is My Son, the Chosen One; hear Him!"

**9:36** And when the voice had spoken, Jesus was found alone. And they kept silent, and in those days reported to no one anything of the things they had seen.

**9:37** Now it came to pass on the following day, when they had descended from the mountain, that a great multitude met Him.

**9:38** And behold, a man from the multitude cried out, saying, "Teacher, I implore You, look upon my son, for he is my only child;

**9:39** and behold, a spirit seizes him, and suddenly he cries out; and it convulses him with foaming, and scarcely departs from him, bruising him;

**9:40** and I implored Your disciples to cast it out, and they were not able."

**9:41** And answering, Jesus said, "O unbelieving and perverse generation, how long shall I remain with you and endure you? Bring your son here."

**9:42** And while he was still approaching, the demon dashed him down and violently convulsed him; but Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, and healed the boy, and restored him to his father.

**9:43** And all were astounded at the majesty of God. Yet while everyone was marveling at all the things which He was doing, He said to His disciples,

**9:44** "As for you, set these words in your ears, for the Son of Man is about to be delivered into the hands of men."

**9:45** But they did not comprehend this saying, and it was veiled from them so that they might not perceive it; and they were afraid to question Him concerning this saying.

**9:46** Then a dispute arose among them as to which of them might be the greatest.

**9:47** But Jesus, knowing the reasoning of their heart, took hold of a little child and set him beside Himself,

**9:48** and said to them, "Whoever welcomes this little child in My name welcomes Me, and whoever welcomes Me welcomes Him who sent Me; for he who is least among you all—that one is great."

**9:49** Now John answered and said, "Master, we saw someone casting out demons in Your name, and we tried to hinder him because he does not follow with us."

**9:50** But Jesus said to him, "Do not hinder him, for whoever is not against you is for you."

**9:51** Now it came to pass, as the days for His being taken up were drawing toward fulfillment, that He resolutely set His face to journey to Jerusalem,

**9:52** and sent messengers before His face. And going on, they entered a village of the Samaritans to make ready for Him;

**9:53** yet they did not welcome Him, because His face was set toward Jerusalem.

**9:54** And when the disciples James and John saw this, they said, "Lord, do You wish that we command fire to descend from heaven and consume them?"

**9:55** But turning, He rebuked them.

**9:56** And they went on to another village.

**9:57** And as they were journeying along the way, someone said to Him, "I will follow You wherever You go."

**9:58** And Jesus said to him, "The foxes have dens and the birds of the sky have nests, yet the Son of Man has nowhere to rest His head."

**9:59** And He said to another, "Follow Me." But he said, "Lord, permit me first to go away and bury my father."

**9:60** But He said to him, "Leave the dead to bury their own dead; yet you, go forth and declare abroad the kingdom of God."

**9:61** And another also said, "I will follow You, Lord; yet first permit me to bid farewell to those at my house."

**9:62** But Jesus said, "No one who has laid his hand upon a plow and keeps looking back is fit for the kingdom of God."

## Translator Notes

- **9:10** — Many later manuscripts read "to a deserted place belonging to a city called Bethsaida," whereas the critical text reads simply "to a city called Bethsaida."
- **9:24** — The Greek word psyche can mean either "life" or "soul," holding both earthly existence and eternal life in view.
- **9:29** — The Greek participle rendered "dazzling" (exastrapton) literally means "flashing like lightning," depicting an intense, radiant brilliance.
- **9:31** — The Greek noun rendered "departure" is exodos ("exodus"), evoking Israel's deliverance and pointing to the redemptive fulfillment of Christ's death, resurrection, and ascension in Jerusalem.
- **9:35** — The critical text reads "the Chosen One" (eklelegmenos), echoing Isaiah 42:1, whereas many later manuscripts harmonize with Mark 9:7 and Matthew 17:5 by reading "the Beloved."
- **9:51** — The Greek noun rendered "being taken up" (analempsis) appears only here in the New Testament and looks ahead to Jesus' ascension as the culmination of His journey to Jerusalem.
- **9:55** — Some later manuscripts expand verses 54 through 56 with "just as Elijah did" and Jesus' rebuke, "You do not know what manner of spirit you are of; for the Son of Man did not come to destroy men's lives, but to save them," which are absent from the earliest witnesses.
- **9:60** — The saying "Leave the dead to bury their own dead" uses the first "dead" figuratively for those who remain spiritually outside the kingdom of God, underscoring the supreme urgency of proclaiming the gospel.
