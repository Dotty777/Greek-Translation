## Text

**17:1** And after six days Jesus took Peter, James, and his brother John, and brought them up to a high mountain apart.

**17:2** And He was transfigured before them; His countenance shone like the sun, and His garments became white as the light.

**17:3** And behold, Moses and Elijah appeared to them, conversing with Him.

**17:4** Then Peter answered Jesus and said, "Lord, it is good for us to be here; if You desire, I will make three tabernacles here—one for You, one for Moses, and one for Elijah."

**17:5** While he was still speaking, behold, a radiant cloud overshadowed them; and behold, a voice came from the cloud, saying, "This is My Son, the Beloved, in whom I am well pleased; listen to Him."

**17:6** And when the disciples heard this, they fell upon their faces and were exceedingly afraid.

**17:7** And Jesus drew near and touched them, saying, "Rise up, and do not be afraid."

**17:8** And when they lifted up their eyes, they beheld no one except Jesus Himself alone.

**17:9** And as they were descending from the mountain, Jesus charged them, saying, "Tell no one the vision until the Son of Man has risen from the dead."

**17:10** And His disciples questioned Him, saying, "Why then do the scribes declare that Elijah must come first?"

**17:11** And He answered and said, "Elijah indeed is coming, and he will restore all things.

**17:12** Yet I say to you that Elijah has already come, and they did not recognize him, but did to him whatever they desired. In the same way, the Son of Man is also about to suffer at their hands."

**17:13** Then the disciples perceived that He had spoken to them concerning John the Baptist.

**17:14** And when they came to the multitude, a man drew near to Him, kneeling before Him

**17:15** and saying, "Lord, have mercy on my son, for he is afflicted with seizures and suffers terribly; for often he falls into the fire, and often into the water.

**17:16** And I brought him to Your disciples, yet they were not able to heal him."

**17:17** And Jesus answered and said, "O unbelieving and perverse generation, how long shall I remain with you? How long shall I endure you? Bring him to Me here."

**17:18** And Jesus rebuked it, and the demon departed from him; and the boy was healed from that hour.

**17:19** Then the disciples came to Jesus privately and said, "Why were we not able to cast it out?"

**17:20** And He said to them, "Because of your little faith; for assuredly, I say to you, if you have faith like a mustard seed, you shall say to this mountain, 'Move from this place to that,' and it shall move; and nothing shall be impossible to you."

**17:22** Now as they were gathering together in Galilee, Jesus said to them, "The Son of Man is about to be delivered into the hands of men,

**17:23** and they will slay Him, and on the third day He shall be raised up." And they were deeply grieved.

**17:24** Now when they arrived in Capernaum, those who collected the two-drachma tax approached Peter and said, "Does your Teacher not pay the two-drachma tax?"

**17:25** He said, "Yes." And when he came into the house, Jesus spoke to him first, saying, "What do you think, Simon? From whom do the kings of the earth collect custom or tribute—from their own sons, or from strangers?"

**17:26** And when he said, "From strangers," Jesus said to him, "Then indeed the sons are free.

**17:27** Yet lest we give offense to them, go to the sea, cast in a hook, and take the first fish that comes up; and when you have opened its mouth, you will find a stater. Take that and give it to them for Me and for you."

## Translator Notes

- **17:15** — Greek seleniazetai (literally "is moonstruck") was the standard ancient term for epilepsy or convulsive seizures.
- **17:20** — The earliest Greek manuscripts read "little faith" (oligopistian) rather than "unbelief" and omit verse 21, which later scribes appear to have assimilated from Mark 9:29.
- **17:22** — The critical Greek text reads systrephomenon ("gathering together"), whereas later manuscripts read anastrephomenon ("staying" or "traveling about").
- **17:24** — The two-drachma tax (didrachma) was the annual half-shekel temple tax (Exod 30:13); the "stater" in verse 27 was a four-drachma silver coin, exact payment for two men.
- **17:21 *(additional)*** — Text-critical double brackets ⟦ ⟧ are preserved verbatim from the Saints Greek Critical Text; this verse is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
