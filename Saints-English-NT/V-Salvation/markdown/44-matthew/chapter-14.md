## Text

**14:1** At that time Herod the tetrarch heard the report concerning Jesus,

**14:2** and said to his servants, "This is John the Baptist; he has been raised from the dead, and for this reason these powers are active in him."

**14:3** For Herod had arrested John, bound him, and put him away in prison on account of Herodias, the wife of Philip his brother,

**14:4** for John was saying to him, "It is not lawful for you to have her."

**14:5** And though he wished to slay him, he feared the multitude, because they held him to be a prophet.

**14:6** Now when Herod's birthday came, the daughter of Herodias danced in the midst and pleased Herod,

**14:7** whereupon he promised with an oath to give her whatever she should ask.

**14:8** And being instructed beforehand by her mother, she said, "Give me here on a platter the head of John the Baptist."

**14:9** And the king was grieved; yet on account of his oaths and those reclining with him at table, he commanded it to be given,

**14:10** and sent and had John beheaded in the prison.

**14:11** And his head was brought upon a platter and given to the maiden, and she carried it to her mother.

**14:12** And his disciples came near, took up the corpse, and buried him; then they went and brought word to Jesus.

**14:13** Now when Jesus heard this, He withdrew from there in a boat to a desolate place apart; and when the multitudes heard it, they followed Him on foot from the cities.

**14:14** And stepping out, He saw a great multitude, and He had compassion upon them and healed their sick.

**14:15** And when evening came, the disciples drew near to Him, saying, "The place is desolate, and the hour has already passed; dismiss the multitudes, so that they may go into the villages and buy provisions for themselves."

**14:16** But Jesus said to them, "They have no need to depart; you yourselves give them something to eat."

**14:17** Then they said to Him, "We have nothing here except five loaves and two fish."

**14:18** And He said, "Bring them to Me here."

**14:19** And having commanded the multitudes to recline upon the grass, He took the five loaves and the two fish, and looking up toward heaven, He blessed and broke them, and gave the loaves to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the multitudes.

**14:20** And all of them ate and were satisfied, and they gathered up what was left over of the broken pieces—twelve baskets full.

**14:21** And those who ate were about five thousand men, apart from women and children.

**14:22** And immediately He compelled the disciples to get into the boat and go ahead of Him to the other side, until He should dismiss the multitudes.

**14:23** And having dismissed the multitudes, He ascended the mountain by Himself to pray. And when evening had come, He was there alone.

**14:24** But the boat was already in the midst of the sea, battered by the waves, for the wind was contrary.

**14:25** And in the fourth watch of the night He came toward them, walking upon the sea.

**14:26** But when the disciples saw Him walking upon the sea, they were terrified, saying, "It is a phantom!" and out of fear they cried aloud.

**14:27** And immediately Jesus spoke to them, saying, "Take courage, it is I; do not fear."

**14:28** And in reply Peter said to Him, "Lord, if it is You, command me to come to You upon the waters."

**14:29** And He said, "Come." And stepping down from the boat, Peter walked upon the waters and came toward Jesus.

**14:30** Yet seeing how strong the wind was, he grew afraid, and as he began to sink, he cried aloud, saying, "Lord, save me!"

**14:31** And at once Jesus stretched forth His hand and grasped him, saying to him, "You of little faith, why did you doubt?"

**14:32** And when they climbed into the boat, the wind abated.

**14:33** And those in the boat worshiped Him, saying, "In truth You are the Son of God."

**14:34** And after crossing over, they reached the land at Gennesaret.

**14:35** And when the men of that place recognized Him, they sent word into all that surrounding country, and brought to Him all those who were ill,

**14:36** and they implored Him that they might only touch the fringe of His garment; and as many as touched it were completely healed.

## Translator Notes

- **14:9** — This Greek text omits a contrastive conjunction between the king's grief and his oaths, requiring a strong punctuation break and 'yet' in English to convey the contrast.
- **14:12** — The Greek shifts from the neuter noun for 'corpse' to the masculine pronoun 'him,' underscoring the personal dignity of John's burial.
- **14:24** — This Greek text reads 'already in the midst of the sea,' whereas some manuscripts read 'many stadia distant from the land.'
- **14:27** — The Greek phrase rendered 'it is I' serves both as ordinary self-identification and as an echo of the Old Testament divine self-revelation 'I am.'
- **14:29** — This Greek text reads 'and came toward Jesus,' indicating that Peter actually walked toward Him before sinking, rather than the variant reading 'to go to Jesus.'
