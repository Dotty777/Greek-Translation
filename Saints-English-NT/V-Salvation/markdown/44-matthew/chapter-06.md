## Text

**6:1** Take heed not to practice your righteousness before men in order to be beheld by them; otherwise you have no reward with your Father who is in heaven.

**6:2** Therefore, whenever you give alms, do not sound a trumpet before yourself, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, so that they may be glorified by men. Assuredly, I say to you, they have received their reward in full.

**6:3** But when you give alms, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing,

**6:4** so that your almsgiving may be in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will repay you.

**6:5** And when you pray, you shall not be like the hypocrites; for they love to stand and pray in the synagogues and at the corners of the broad streets, so that they may be seen by men. Assuredly, I say to you, they have received their reward in full.

**6:6** But you, when you pray, enter into your inner chamber, and having shut your door, pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will repay you.

**6:7** And while praying, do not babble with idle words as the Gentiles do, for they suppose that they will be heard because of their many words.

**6:8** Therefore do not become like them; for your Father knows the things of which you have need before you ask Him.

**6:9** Therefore pray in this manner: "Our Father who is in the heavens, hallowed be Your name;

**6:10** let Your kingdom come; let Your will be done, as in heaven, so also upon earth.

**6:11** Give to us this day our daily bread;

**6:12** and forgive us our debts, even as we also have forgiven our debtors;

**6:13** and do not bring us into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one."

**6:14** For if you forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will forgive you also;

**6:15** but if you do not forgive men, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses.

**6:16** Moreover, when you fast, do not become gloomy like the hypocrites; for they disfigure their faces so that they may appear to men to be fasting. Assuredly, I say to you, they have received their reward in full.

**6:17** Yet you, while fasting, anoint your head and wash your face,

**6:18** so that you may not appear to men to be fasting, but to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will repay you.

**6:19** Do not store up for yourselves treasures upon the earth, where moth and rust destroy, and where thieves break through and steal;

**6:20** but store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust destroys, and where thieves do not break through nor steal;

**6:21** for where your treasure is, there also your heart will be.

**6:22** The lamp of the body is the eye. Therefore, if your eye is sound, your whole body shall be full of light;

**6:23** but if your eye is evil, your whole body shall be full of darkness. If therefore the light that is in you is darkness, how great is that darkness!

**6:24** No one is able to serve two masters; for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will hold fast to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon.

**6:25** For this reason I say to you, do not be anxious about your life, what you shall eat or what you shall drink, nor about your body, what you shall put on. Is not life more than food, and the body more than clothing?

**6:26** Behold the birds of the air, for they neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns, and yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not of far greater worth than they?

**6:27** And which of you by being anxious is able to add one cubit to his stature?

**6:28** And why are you anxious about clothing? Consider well the lilies of the field, how they grow: they neither toil nor spin;

**6:29** yet I say to you that not even Solomon in all his glory was arrayed like one of these.

**6:30** Now if God so clothes the grass of the field, which exists today and tomorrow is cast into the oven, will He not much more clothe you, O you of little faith?

**6:31** Therefore do not be anxious, saying, "What shall we eat?" or, "What shall we drink?" or, "What shall we wear?"

**6:32** For all these things the Gentiles eagerly seek; for your heavenly Father knows that you have need of all these things.

**6:33** Rather, seek first the kingdom and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you.

**6:34** Therefore do not be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow shall be anxious for itself; enough for the day is its own trouble.

## Translator Notes

- **6:11** — The Greek adjective epiousios is exceedingly rare in ancient Greek and appears in the New Testament only here and in Luke 11:3; it is traditionally rendered "daily," though it may also mean "for the coming day" or "necessary for subsistence."
- **6:13** — The Greek phrase tou ponerou may be understood as either "the evil one" (masculine) or "evil" (neuter). Following the earliest Greek manuscripts, the later liturgical doxology ("For Yours is the kingdom...") is absent from the critical text.
- **6:22** — In Jewish idiom, a "sound" (Greek haplous, also "single" or "clear") eye signifies generosity and undivided devotion, whereas an "evil" (Greek poneros) eye in verse 23 denotes envy or stinginess.
- **6:27** — The Greek noun helikia can refer either to physical "stature" or to "span of life"; retaining the literal measure "one cubit" (roughly eighteen inches) preserves the Greek wording.
