## Text

**13:1** On that day Jesus went out of the house and sat beside the sea.

**13:2** And great multitudes were gathered together to Him, so that He entered a boat and sat down; and the entire multitude stood on the shore.

**13:3** And He spoke many things to them in parables, saying, "Behold, a sower went forth to sow.

**13:4** And as he sowed, some fell along the pathway, and the birds came and devoured them.

**13:5** Others fell on rocky places, where they did not have much earth; and immediately they sprang up, because they had no depth of soil.

**13:6** But when the sun had risen, they were scorched; and because they had no root, they withered away.

**13:7** And others fell among the thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked them.

**13:8** But others fell on the good ground and yielded fruit: some a hundred, some sixty, and some thirty.

**13:9** He who has ears, let him hear."

**13:10** And the disciples came near and said to Him, "Why do You speak to them in parables?"

**13:11** And He answered and said to them, "Because to you it has been given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but to them it has not been given.

**13:12** For whoever has, to him it shall be given, and he shall have an abundance; yet whoever does not have, even what he has shall be taken from him.

**13:13** For this reason I speak to them in parables: because seeing they do not see, and hearing they do not hear, nor do they understand.

**13:14** And in them the prophecy of Isaiah is fulfilled, which says, 'With hearing you shall hear and shall by no means understand, and seeing you shall see and shall by no means perceive;

**13:15** for the heart of this people has grown calloused, and with their ears they have scarcely heard, and their eyes they have shut; lest they see with their eyes and hear with their ears and understand with their heart and turn back, and I shall heal them.'

**13:16** Yet blessed are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear.

**13:17** For assuredly, I say to you that many prophets and righteous men longed to see what you see and did not see it, and to hear what you hear and did not hear it.

**13:18** You, therefore, hear the parable of the sower:

**13:19** When anyone hears the word of the kingdom and does not understand it, the evil one comes and snatches away what has been sown in his heart. This is the one who was sown along the pathway.

**13:20** And the one who was sown on the rocky places, this is he who hears the word and immediately receives it with joy;

**13:21** yet he has no root in himself, but lasts only for a season; and when affliction or persecution arises because of the word, immediately he stumbles.

**13:22** And the one who was sown among the thorns, this is he who hears the word, and the anxiety of the age and the deceitfulness of wealth choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful.

**13:23** But the one who was sown on the good ground, this is he who hears the word and understands it, who indeed bears fruit and produces: some a hundred, some sixty, and some thirty."

**13:24** Another parable He set before them, saying, "The kingdom of heaven may be likened to a man who sowed good seed in his field.

**13:25** But while men were sleeping, his enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat and went away.

**13:26** And when the blade sprouted and brought forth fruit, then the tares appeared also.

**13:27** So the servants of the householder came near and said to him, 'Sir, did you not sow good seed in your field? From where then does it have tares?'

**13:28** And he said to them, 'An enemy has done this.' The servants said to him, 'Do you desire then that we go and gather them up?'

**13:29** But he said, 'No, lest while gathering the tares you uproot the wheat together with them.

**13:30** Let both grow together until the harvest; and in the season of harvest I will say to the reapers, "First gather the tares and bind them into bundles to burn them, but gather the wheat into my barn."'"

**13:31** Another parable He set before them, saying, "The kingdom of heaven is like a grain of mustard seed, which a man took and sowed in his field;

**13:32** which indeed is the smallest of all seeds, yet when it has grown, it is greater than the garden herbs and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the sky come and lodge in its branches."

**13:33** Another parable He spoke to them: "The kingdom of heaven is like leaven, which a woman took and hid in three measures of meal until the whole was leavened."

**13:34** All these things Jesus spoke to the multitudes in parables, and without a parable He spoke nothing to them,

**13:35** that what was spoken through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying, "I will open My mouth in parables; I will declare things kept hidden from the foundation of the world."

**13:36** Then, leaving the multitudes, He came into the house. And His disciples approached Him, saying, "Explain to us the parable of the tares of the field."

**13:37** And He answered and said, "The One who sows the good seed is the Son of Man;

**13:38** and the field is the world; as for the good seed, these are the sons of the kingdom, while the tares are the sons of the evil one;

**13:39** and the enemy who sowed them is the devil; the harvest is the consummation of the age, and the reapers are angels.

**13:40** Therefore, just as the tares are gathered and burned in fire, so it shall be at the consummation of the age.

**13:41** The Son of Man shall send forth His angels, and they shall gather out of His kingdom all things that cause stumbling and those who practice lawlessness,

**13:42** and they shall cast them into the furnace of fire; there shall be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth.

**13:43** Then the righteous shall shine forth like the sun in the kingdom of their Father. He who has ears, let him hear.

**13:44** The kingdom of heaven is like treasure hidden in a field, which a man found and concealed; and in his joy he goes and sells all that he possesses and buys that field.

**13:45** Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a merchant in search of fine pearls;

**13:46** and upon finding one pearl of great value, he went and sold all that he possessed and bought it.

**13:47** Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a dragnet cast into the sea, gathering of every kind;

**13:48** which, when it was full, they drew up onto the shore; and sitting down, they gathered the good into vessels, but the bad they cast out.

**13:49** So it shall be at the consummation of the age: the angels shall come forth and separate the wicked from the midst of the righteous,

**13:50** and they shall cast them into the furnace of fire; there shall be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth.

**13:51** Have you understood all these things?" They said to Him, "Yes."

**13:52** And He said to them, "Therefore every scribe who has been discipled for the kingdom of heaven is like a householder who brings forth out of his treasure things new and old."

**13:53** And it came to pass, when Jesus had finished these parables, that He departed from there.

**13:54** And having come to His own homeland, He taught them in their synagogue, so that they were astonished and said, "From where did this Man obtain this wisdom and these mighty works?

**13:55** Is this not the carpenter's son? Is His mother not called Mary, and His brothers James, Joseph, Simon, and Judas?

**13:56** And His sisters, are they not all with us? From where then did this Man obtain all these things?"

**13:57** And they took offense at Him. But Jesus said to them, "A prophet is not without honor except in his homeland and in his own house."

**13:58** And He did not perform many mighty works there because of their unbelief.

## Translator Notes

- **13:25** — Greek zizania ("tares") refers specifically to darnel (Lolium temulentum), a noxious weed whose early blades closely resemble wheat until the grain heads appear.
- **13:33** — Greek sata tria ("three measures") denotes three seahs of flour, roughly half a bushel (about thirty-six to forty liters), sufficient to bake bread for a large gathering.
- **13:35** — Matthew cites Psalm 78:2 (LXX 77:2), attributed to Asaph, whom 2 Chronicles 29:30 calls a seer; the critical text retains "of the world" at the close of the verse.
- **13:51** — Following the critical text, this verse omits "Jesus said to them" at the opening and "Lord" after "Yes," continuing Jesus's direct speech without interruption from verse 50.
