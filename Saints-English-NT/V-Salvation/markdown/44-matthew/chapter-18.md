## Text

**18:1** In that hour the disciples approached Jesus, saying, "Who then is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven?"

**18:2** And calling a little child to Himself, He placed him in their midst

**18:3** and said, "Assuredly, I say to you, unless you turn and become like little children, you shall by no means enter into the kingdom of heaven.

**18:4** Therefore, whoever humbles himself like this little child, he is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven.

**18:5** And whoever welcomes one such little child in My name welcomes Me.

**18:6** Yet whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in Me to stumble, it is better for him that a great millstone be hung around his neck, and that he be drowned in the depths of the open sea.

**18:7** Woe to the world because of stumbling blocks! For it is necessary that stumbling blocks come, yet woe to the man through whom the stumbling block comes!

**18:8** Now if your hand or your foot causes you to stumble, cut it off and cast it away from you; it is better for you to enter into life maimed or lame, than having two hands or two feet to be cast into the eternal fire.

**18:9** And if your eye causes you to stumble, tear it out and cast it away from you; it is better for you to enter into life with one eye, than having two eyes to be cast into the fiery hell.

**18:10** See to it that you do not despise one of these little ones; for I say to you that their angels in heaven continually behold the face of My Father who is in heaven.

**18:12** What do you think? If a certain man has a hundred sheep, and one of them wanders astray, will he not leave the ninety-nine upon the mountains, and go and seek the one that is wandering?

**18:13** And if it comes to pass that he finds it, assuredly, I say to you, he rejoices over it more than over the ninety-nine that have not wandered astray.

**18:14** Likewise, it is not the will of your Father who is in heaven that one of these little ones should perish.

**18:15** Now if your brother sins, go and reprove him between you and him alone. If he heeds you, you have won your brother.

**18:16** Yet if he does not heed you, take with you one or two others, that by the mouth of two or three witnesses every matter may be established.

**18:17** And if he refuses to listen to them, tell it to the church; and if he refuses to listen even to the church, let him be to you as a Gentile and a tax collector.

**18:18** Assuredly, I say to you, whatever things you bind upon the earth shall be bound in heaven, and whatever things you loose upon the earth shall be loosed in heaven.

**18:19** Again, assuredly I say to you that if two among you agree upon the earth concerning any matter that they ask, it shall be done for them by My Father who is in heaven.

**18:20** For where two or three are assembled in My name, there I am in their midst."

**18:21** Then Peter approached and said to Him, "Lord, how many times shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? As many as seven times?"

**18:22** Jesus said to him, "I do not say to you as many as seven times, but as many as seventy-seven times.

**18:23** For this reason the kingdom of heaven may be compared to a king who desired to settle accounts with his servants.

**18:24** And when he began to settle them, one debtor was brought to him who owed ten thousand talents.

**18:25** Yet since he did not have the means to repay, the master commanded him to be sold, together with his wife and children and all that he possessed, and payment to be made.

**18:26** Therefore the servant fell down and paid homage to him, saying, 'Master, have patience with me, and I will repay you everything.'

**18:27** And the master of that servant, being moved with compassion, set him free and forgave him the loan.

**18:28** Yet when that servant went out, he found one of his fellow servants who owed him one hundred denarii; and seizing him, he began to choke him, saying, 'Pay back whatever you owe!'

**18:29** So his fellow servant fell down and pleaded with him, saying, 'Have patience with me, and I will repay you.'

**18:30** Yet he was unwilling, and went away and cast him into prison until he should repay what was owed.

**18:31** Therefore, when his fellow servants saw what had taken place, they were deeply distressed, and came and reported to their master all that had taken place.

**18:32** Then his master summoned him and said to him, 'Wicked servant, I forgave you all that debt because you pleaded with me.

**18:33** Should you not also have shown mercy to your fellow servant, even as I showed mercy to you?'

**18:34** And his master, being moved with wrath, delivered him to the tormentors until he should repay all that was owed to him.

**18:35** So also shall My heavenly Father do to you, if each one of you does not forgive his brother from your hearts."

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**18:11** *(after `18:10`)* ⟦For the Son of Man has come to save that which was lost.⟧

## Translator Notes

- **18:6** — Greek mylos onikos literally denotes a heavy millstone turned by a donkey, far larger than a household hand mill.
- **18:10** — The earliest and best Greek manuscripts omit verse 11 ("For the Son of Man has come to save that which was lost"), which appears to be a later harmonization from Luke 19:10.
- **18:15** — The earliest Greek witnesses omit "against you" (eis se), broadening the counsel to address any sin committed by a fellow believer.
- **18:22** — Greek hebdomēkontakis hepta can mean either "seventy-seven times" (echoing the Septuagint of Gen 4:24 and reversing Lamech's vengeance) or "seventy times seven."
- **18:11 *(additional)*** — Text-critical double brackets ⟦ ⟧ are preserved verbatim from the Saints Greek Critical Text; this verse is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
