## Text

**7:1** Do not judge, lest you be judged;

**7:2** for with the judgment by which you judge, you shall be judged; and with the measure by which you measure, it shall be measured to you.

**7:3** And why do you look upon the speck that is in your brother's eye, yet do not consider the beam that is in your own eye?

**7:4** Or how will you say to your brother, "Let me cast out the speck from your eye," and behold, the beam is in your own eye?

**7:5** Hypocrite! First cast out the beam from your own eye, and then you will see clearly to cast out the speck from your brother's eye.

**7:6** Do not give what is holy to the dogs, nor cast your pearls before swine, lest they trample them beneath their feet, and turning, tear you to pieces.

**7:7** Ask, and it shall be given to you; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened to you.

**7:8** For everyone who asks receives, and the one who seeks finds, and to the one who knocks it shall be opened.

**7:9** Or what man is there among you who, if his son asks him for bread—will he give him a stone?

**7:10** Or if he also asks for a fish—will he give him a serpent?

**7:11** If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more shall your Father who is in the heavens give good things to those who ask Him!

**7:12** In all things, therefore, whatever you desire that men should do to you, even so do to them also; for this is the Law and the Prophets.

**7:13** Enter through the narrow gate; for wide is the gate and broad is the way that leads to destruction, and many are those who enter through it;

**7:14** for narrow is the gate and hard is the way that leads to life, and few are those who find it.

**7:15** Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing, yet inwardly they are ravenous wolves.

**7:16** By their fruits you shall recognize them. Do men gather grapes from thorns, or figs from thistles?

**7:17** Even so, every good tree produces fine fruit, but the corrupt tree produces evil fruit;

**7:18** a good tree is not able to bear evil fruit, nor is a corrupt tree able to produce fine fruit.

**7:19** Every tree that does not produce fine fruit is cut down and cast into the fire.

**7:20** Therefore, by their fruits you shall recognize them.

**7:21** Not everyone who says to Me, "Lord, Lord," shall enter the kingdom of heaven, but the one who does the will of My Father who is in heaven.

**7:22** Many will say to Me in that day, "Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in Your name, and in Your name cast out demons, and in Your name perform many mighty works?"

**7:23** And then I will declare to them, "I never knew you; depart from Me, you who work lawlessness."

**7:24** Therefore, everyone who hears these words of Mine and practices them shall be likened to a wise man who built his house upon the rock.

**7:25** And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew and beat against that house; yet it did not fall, for it had been founded upon the rock.

**7:26** And everyone who hears these words of Mine and does not practice them shall be likened to a foolish man who built his house upon the sand.

**7:27** And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew and struck against that house; and it fell, and its fall was great.

**7:28** And it came to pass, when Jesus had finished these words, that the multitudes were astonished at His teaching;

**7:29** for He was teaching them as one possessing authority, and not as their scribes.

## Translator Notes

- **7:6** — "What is holy" recalls consecrated food from the altar and figuratively denotes sacred truths, warning disciples not to expose precious spiritual realities to hostile scorn.
- **7:14** — The critical Greek text reads hoti ("for" or "because"), continuing the explanation from verse 13, whereas some manuscripts read the exclamatory ti ("How narrow is the gate!").
- **7:29** — Following the critical Greek text, "their scribes" (rather than "the scribes") sharpens the contrast between Jesus' own authority and the derivative instruction of the people's religious teachers.
