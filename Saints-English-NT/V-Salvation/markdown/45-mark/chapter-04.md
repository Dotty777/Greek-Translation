## Text

**4:1** And again He began to teach beside the sea. And a very great multitude gathered to Him, so that He stepped into a boat and sat upon the sea, and all the multitude was on the land beside the sea.

**4:2** And He taught them many things in parables, and spoke to them in His teaching:

**4:3** "Listen! Behold, the sower went forth to sow.

**4:4** And it came to pass, as he sowed, that some fell along the pathway, and the birds came and devoured it.

**4:5** And other seed fell upon rocky ground, where it did not have much soil; and immediately it sprang up, because it had no depth of soil.

**4:6** And when the sun rose, it was scorched; and because it had no root, it withered away.

**4:7** And other seed fell among the thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked it, and it yielded no fruit.

**4:8** And other seeds fell upon the good soil and yielded fruit, springing up and increasing, and brought forth thirtyfold, and sixtyfold, and a hundredfold."

**4:9** And He said, "Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear."

**4:10** And when He was alone, those who were around Him, together with the twelve, asked Him concerning the parables.

**4:11** And He said to them, "To you the mystery of the kingdom of God has been given; but to those who are outside, all things come in parables,

**4:12** so that seeing they may see and yet not perceive, and hearing they may hear and yet not understand, lest they should turn back and it be forgiven them."

**4:13** And He said to them, "Do you not know this parable? And how will you understand all the parables?

**4:14** He who sows, sows the word.

**4:15** Now these are the ones along the pathway where the word is sown: when they have heard, Satan immediately comes and takes away the word that was sown in them.

**4:16** And these likewise are the ones sown upon rocky places, who, when they hear the word, immediately receive it with joy;

**4:17** and they have no root in themselves, but last only for a season; afterward, when tribulation or persecution arises on account of the word, immediately they stumble.

**4:18** And others are the ones sown among the thorns; these are they who have heard the word,

**4:19** and the cares of the age, and the deceitfulness of wealth, and the desires for other things entering in choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful.

**4:20** And those are the ones who were sown upon the good soil, such as hear the word and welcome it, and bear fruit: thirtyfold, and sixtyfold, and a hundredfold."

**4:21** And He said to them, "Is a lamp brought in to be placed beneath a grain measure or beneath a bed? Is it not to be set upon the lampstand?

**4:22** For nothing is hidden except that it may be made manifest, nor has anything been kept secret except that it should come into the open.

**4:23** If any man has ears to hear, let him hear."

**4:24** And He said to them, "Take heed to what you hear. By the measure with which you measure, it shall be measured to you, and still more shall be added to you.

**4:25** For whoever has, to him it shall be given; and whoever does not have, even what he has shall be taken away from him."

**4:26** And He said, "Thus is the kingdom of God, as if a man should cast seed upon the earth,

**4:27** and should sleep and rise night and day, and the seed should sprout and grow tall, though he himself does not know how.

**4:28** Of its own accord the earth brings forth fruit: first the blade, then the ear, then the full grain within the ear.

**4:29** And when the fruit is ripe, immediately he sends forth the sickle, because the harvest is at hand."

**4:30** And He said, "How shall we liken the kingdom of God, or with what parable shall we set it forth?

**4:31** It is like a grain of mustard seed, which, when it is sown upon the earth, is smaller than all the seeds that are upon the earth—

**4:32** yet when it is sown, it rises up and becomes greater than all the garden herbs, and puts forth great branches, so that the birds of heaven are able to dwell beneath its shade."

**4:33** And by many such parables He spoke the word to them, according as they were able to hear.

**4:34** Yet without a parable He did not speak to them; and privately to His own disciples He explained all things.

**4:35** And He said to them on that day, when evening had come, "Let us pass over to the other side."

**4:36** And leaving the multitude, they took Him along, just as He was, in the boat; and other boats were with Him.

**4:37** And a great tempest of wind arose, and the waves were breaking into the boat, so that the boat was already filling.

**4:38** And He Himself was in the stern, sleeping upon the cushion; and they awakened Him and said to Him, "Teacher, do You not care that we are perishing?"

**4:39** And having awakened, He rebuked the wind and said to the sea, "Be silent! Be stilled!" And the wind subsided, and there came a great calm.

**4:40** And He said to them, "Why are you fearful? Do you still have no faith?"

**4:41** And they feared with a great fear, and said to one another, "Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?"

## Translator Notes

- **4:8** — The Greek uses a Semitic-influenced distributive idiom (literally "up to thirty, and in sixty, and in a hundred"), conveying multiplicative yields of thirtyfold, sixtyfold, and a hundredfold (compare 4:20).
- **4:11** — The critical Greek text reads simply "the mystery of the kingdom of God has been given," without the verb "to know" found in later manuscripts and in the parallels at Matthew 13:11 and Luke 8:10.
- **4:28** — The Greek preserves an unusual colloquial construction in which the adjective "full" stands in the nominative form directly before the accusative noun "grain."
- **4:39** — Literally "Be silent! Be muzzled!" The second command uses the same forceful verb with which Jesus silenced an unclean spirit in Mark 1:25.
