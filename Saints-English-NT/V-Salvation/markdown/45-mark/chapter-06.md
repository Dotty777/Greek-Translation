## Text

**6:1** And He departed from there and came into His native country, and His disciples followed Him.

**6:2** And when the Sabbath had come, He began to teach in the synagogue. And many who heard Him were astonished, saying, "From where does this Man have these things? And what wisdom is this that has been given to Him, that such mighty works are performed through His hands?

**6:3** Is this not the carpenter, the son of Mary, and brother of James, Joses, Judas, and Simon? And are His sisters not here among us?" And they took offense at Him.

**6:4** And Jesus said to them, "A prophet is not without honor, except in his native country, among his own kinsmen, and in his own household."

**6:5** And He was unable to perform any mighty work there, except that He laid His hands upon a few who were sick and healed them.

**6:6** And He marveled on account of their unbelief. Then He went about through the surrounding villages, teaching.

**6:7** And He summoned the Twelve to Himself, and began to send them forth two by two, and gave them authority over the unclean spirits.

**6:8** And He charged them to take nothing for the journey except a staff alone—no bread, no traveler's bag, no copper money in their belt—

**6:9** but to be shod with sandals, and not to put on two tunics.

**6:10** And He said to them, "Wherever you enter a house, abide there until you depart from that place.

**6:11** And if any place will not receive you, nor listen to you, as you depart from there, shake off the dust beneath your feet as a testimony against them."

**6:12** And going forth, they proclaimed that people should repent;

**6:13** and they cast out many demons, and anointed with oil many who were sick, and healed them.

**6:14** And King Herod heard of it, for His name had become well known. And some were saying, "John the Baptizer has been raised from the dead, and for this reason these powers are at work in Him."

**6:15** Others were saying, "He is Elijah." And others were saying, "He is a prophet, like one of the prophets."

**6:16** But when Herod heard of it, he said, "John, whom I beheaded—he has been raised!"

**6:17** For Herod himself had sent and seized John, and bound him in prison on account of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip, because he had married her.

**6:18** For John had been saying to Herod, "It is not lawful for you to have the wife of your brother."

**6:19** Therefore Herodias held a grudge against him and desired to put him to death, yet she could not;

**6:20** for Herod feared John, knowing him to be a righteous and holy man, and kept him safe. And when he heard him, he was greatly perplexed, yet he listened to him gladly.

**6:21** Then an opportune day arrived when Herod, on his birthday, made a banquet for his nobles, his military commanders, and the foremost men of Galilee;

**6:22** and when his daughter Herodias came in and danced, she pleased Herod and those who reclined at table with him. And the king said to the girl, "Ask of me whatever you desire, and I will give it to you."

**6:23** And he swore to her, "Whatever you ask of me, I will give to you, up to half of my kingdom."

**6:24** And going out, she said to her mother, "What shall I ask?" And she said, "The head of John the Baptizer."

**6:25** And immediately she came in with haste to the king and made her request, saying, "I want you to give me at once, upon a platter, the head of John the Baptist."

**6:26** And the king was deeply grieved; yet because of his oaths and because of those who reclined with him, he was unwilling to refuse her.

**6:27** And immediately the king sent an executioner and commanded that his head be brought. So the executioner went and beheaded him in the prison,

**6:28** and brought his head upon a platter and gave it to the girl; and the girl gave it to her mother.

**6:29** And when his disciples heard of it, they came and took up his body and laid it in a tomb.

**6:30** And the apostles gathered together to Jesus and reported to Him everything that they had done and everything that they had taught.

**6:31** And He said to them, "Come away by yourselves to a desolate place and rest a little while." For there were many coming and going, and they did not even have leisure to eat.

**6:32** So they departed in the boat to a desolate place by themselves.

**6:33** Yet many saw them departing and recognized them, and they ran there on foot from all the cities and arrived before them.

**6:34** And when He went ashore, He saw a great multitude and was moved with compassion for them, because they were like sheep without a shepherd; and He began to teach them many things.

**6:35** And when the day was already far spent, His disciples came to Him and said, "This is a desolate place, and the hour is already late.

**6:36** Send them away, that they may go into the surrounding countryside and villages and buy themselves something to eat."

**6:37** But He answered them, "You yourselves give them something to eat." And they said to Him, "Shall we go and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread and give it to them to eat?"

**6:38** Then He said to them, "How many loaves do you have? Go and see." And when they had found out, they said, "Five, and two fish."

**6:39** Then He commanded them to have all the people sit down in companies upon the green grass.

**6:40** So they sat down in orderly ranks, by hundreds and by fifties.

**6:41** And taking the five loaves and the two fish, He looked up to heaven, blessed and broke the loaves, and gave them to His disciples to set before the people; and the two fish He divided among them all.

**6:42** And they all ate and were satisfied.

**6:43** And they took up twelve basketfuls of the broken pieces, and also of the fish.

**6:44** And those who ate the loaves were five thousand men.

**6:45** And immediately He compelled His disciples to get into the boat and go ahead to the other side, toward Bethsaida, while He Himself dismissed the multitude.

**6:46** And having taken leave of them, He departed to the mountain to pray.

**6:47** Now when evening had come, the boat was in the midst of the sea, and He was alone upon the land.

**6:48** And seeing them hard-pressed in rowing, for the wind was contrary to them, about the fourth watch of the night He came to them, walking upon the sea, and He intended to pass by them.

**6:49** But when they saw Him walking upon the sea, they supposed it was a phantom and cried out;

**6:50** for they all saw Him and were deeply troubled. Yet immediately He spoke with them and said to them, "Take courage! It is I; do not be afraid."

**6:51** And He went up into the boat with them, and the wind ceased. And they were utterly astonished within themselves,

**6:52** for they had not understood concerning the loaves, but their heart was hardened.

**6:53** And when they had crossed over to the land, they came to Gennesaret and moored at the shore.

**6:54** And as they came out of the boat, immediately the people recognized Him,

**6:55** and ran throughout that entire region, and began to carry about on pallets those who were ill, to wherever they heard that He was.

**6:56** And wherever He entered—into villages, or cities, or the countryside—they laid the sick in the marketplaces and implored Him that they might touch even the fringe of His garment; and as many as touched it were made well.

## Translator Notes

- **6:11** — The Greek text ends with "as a testimony against them," omitting the later scribal addition comparing the city to Sodom and Gomorrah (harmonized from Matthew 10:15).
- **6:20** — Translates the early Greek reading "was greatly perplexed" (polla eporei), whereas later Byzantine manuscripts read "did many things" (polla epoiei).
- **6:22** — The Greek text reads "his daughter Herodias" (tes thygatros autou Herodiados), an early and difficult reading that many later manuscripts smoothed to "the daughter of Herodias herself."
- **6:48** — The clause "He intended to pass by them" reflects Old Testament theophany language in which God "passes by" to reveal His presence and glory (cf. Exodus 33:19, 22; 1 Kings 19:11).
