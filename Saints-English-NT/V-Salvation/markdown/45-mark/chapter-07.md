## Text

**7:1** And the Pharisees and some of the scribes, having come from Jerusalem, gathered together to Him,

**7:2** and seeing that certain of His disciples were eating their bread with defiled, that is, unwashed hands—

**7:3** for the Pharisees and all the Jews do not eat unless they wash their hands thoroughly, holding fast to the tradition of the elders;

**7:4** and coming from the marketplace, they do not eat unless they wash themselves; and there are many other things which they have received to hold, such as the washings of cups, pitchers, and bronze vessels—

**7:5** then the Pharisees and the scribes asked Him, "Why do Your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with defiled hands?"

**7:6** And He said to them, "Isaiah prophesied rightly concerning you hypocrites, as it is written: 'This people honors Me with their lips, yet their heart is far removed from Me;

**7:7** and in vain do they worship Me, teaching the ordinances of men as doctrines.'

**7:8** Forsaking the commandment of God, you hold fast to the tradition of men."

**7:9** And He said to them, "How finely you set aside the commandment of God, in order that you may keep your own tradition!

**7:10** For Moses said, 'Honor your father and your mother,' and, 'He who reviles father or mother, let him surely die.'

**7:11** Yet you say, If a man says to his father or to his mother, 'Whatever benefit you might receive from me is Corban'—that is, a gift—

**7:12** you no longer permit him to do anything for his father or his mother,

**7:13** making void the word of God through your tradition which you have handed down; and you do many similar things like these."

**7:14** And having summoned the multitude again, He said to them, "Hear Me, all of you, and understand:

**7:15** there is nothing outside a man entering into him that can defile him; rather, the things which proceed out of a man are the things that defile a man."

**7:17** And when He had entered a house apart from the multitude, His disciples questioned Him about the parable.

**7:18** And He said to them, "Are you also thus without understanding? Do you not perceive that whatever enters into a man from outside cannot defile him,

**7:19** because it does not enter into his heart, but into his stomach, and passes out into the latrine?"—thus declaring all foods clean.

**7:20** And He went on to say, "That which proceeds out of a man, that is what defiles a man.

**7:21** For from within, out of the heart of men, proceed evil thoughts: fornications, thefts, murders, adulteries,

**7:22** covetousness, wickedness, deceit, licentiousness, an evil eye, blasphemy, arrogance, folly;

**7:23** all these evil things proceed from within and defile a man."

**7:24** And arising from there, He departed into the region of Tyre and Sidon. And having entered a house, He desired that no one should know it, yet He could not remain hidden;

**7:25** but immediately a woman whose little daughter had an unclean spirit heard about Him, and coming, she fell down at His feet.

**7:26** Now the woman was a Greek, a Syrophoenician by birth; and she pleaded with Him to cast the demon out of her daughter.

**7:27** And He said to her, "Let the children be filled first; for it is not fitting to take the bread of the children and cast it to the little dogs."

**7:28** And she answered and said to Him, "Yes, Lord; yet even the little dogs under the table eat from the crumbs of the children."

**7:29** And He said to her, "Because of this saying, go; the demon has departed from your daughter."

**7:30** And when she went to her house, she found the child lying upon the bed, and the demon gone out.

**7:31** And again departing from the region of Tyre, He came by way of Sidon to the Sea of Galilee, through the midst of the region of Decapolis.

**7:32** And they brought to Him a man who was deaf and spoke with difficulty, and they pleaded with Him to lay His hand upon him.

**7:33** And taking him aside from the multitude privately, He put His fingers into his ears, and having spat, He touched his tongue;

**7:34** and looking up to heaven, He sighed and said to him, "Ephphatha," that is, "Be opened."

**7:35** And his ears were opened, and the bond of his tongue was loosed, and he began to speak clearly.

**7:36** And He charged them that they should tell no one; yet the more He charged them, the more abundantly they proclaimed it.

**7:37** And they were overwhelmingly astonished, saying, "He has done everything well: He causes both the deaf to hear and the mute to speak."

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — The Greek adverb pygme literally means "with the fist," a rare expression denoting a prescribed ritual manner of washing the hands thoroughly.
- **7:11** — Corban is a transliteration of a Hebrew/Aramaic term denoting a gift or offering dedicated to God, which could be vowed so as to withhold material support from one's parents.
- **7:15** — Verse 16 ("If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear") is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts and is therefore omitted in the Saints Greek Critical Text.
- **7:19** — The masculine participle katharizon ("purifying" or "cleansing") refers back grammatically to Jesus in verse 18, forming Mark's narrative aside that Jesus thereby declared all foods clean.
- **7:16 *(additional)*** — Text-critical double brackets ⟦ ⟧ are preserved verbatim from the Saints Greek Critical Text; this verse is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
