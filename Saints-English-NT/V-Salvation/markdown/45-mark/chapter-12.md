## Text

**12:1** And He began speaking to them in parables: "A man planted a vineyard, put a hedge around it, dug a winepress vat, and built a tower; and he leased it to vinedressers and went abroad.

**12:2** And at the proper season he sent a servant to the vinedressers, that he might receive from the vinedressers some of the fruits of the vineyard.

**12:3** And seizing him, they beat him and sent him away empty-handed.

**12:4** And again he sent another servant to them; and that one they wounded in the head and treated shamefully.

**12:5** And he sent another, and that one they slew; and many others, beating some and slaying others.

**12:6** He still had one, a beloved son; last of all he sent him to them, saying, 'They will respect my son.'

**12:7** But those vinedressers said among themselves, 'This is the heir. Come, let us slay him, and the inheritance shall be ours.'

**12:8** And taking hold of him, they slew him and cast him out of the vineyard.

**12:9** What then will the lord of the vineyard do? He will come and destroy the vinedressers, and give the vineyard to others.

**12:10** Have you not even read this Scripture: 'The stone which the builders rejected, this one has become the head of the corner;

**12:11** from the Lord this came to pass, and it is marvelous in our eyes'?"

**12:12** And they sought to lay hold of Him, yet they feared the multitude, for they perceived that He spoke the parable against them. And leaving Him, they went away.

**12:13** And they sent to Him some of the Pharisees and of the Herodians, that they might ensnare Him in speech.

**12:14** And coming, they said to Him, "Teacher, we know that You are truthful and defer to no one, for You do not look to the outward appearance of men, but in truth You teach the way of God. Is it lawful to pay tribute to Caesar, or not? Shall we pay, or shall we not pay?"

**12:15** But He, knowing their hypocrisy, said to them, "Why do you test Me? Bring Me a denarius, that I may look at it."

**12:16** And they brought one. Then He said to them, "Whose image and inscription is this?" And they said to Him, "Caesar's."

**12:17** And Jesus said to them, "Render to Caesar the things that belong to Caesar, and to God the things that belong to God." And they marveled greatly at Him.

**12:18** Then Sadducees came to Him, who say that there is no resurrection; and they questioned Him, saying,

**12:19** "Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if a man's brother dies and leaves behind a wife, and leaves no child, his brother should take the wife and raise up offspring for his brother.

**12:20** Now there were seven brothers. The first took a wife, and dying, he left no offspring.

**12:21** And the second took her, and died without leaving offspring; and the third likewise.

**12:22** So the seven left no offspring. Last of all the woman also died.

**12:23** In the resurrection, when they rise, whose wife shall she be among them? For the seven had her as a wife."

**12:24** Jesus said to them, "Are you not in error for this very reason, because you do not know the Scriptures nor the power of God?

**12:25** For when they rise from the dead, they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like angels in the heavens.

**12:26** But concerning the dead, that they are raised, have you not read in the book of Moses, in the passage about the bush, how God spoke to him, saying, 'I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob'?

**12:27** He is not the God of the dead, but of the living. You are greatly in error."

**12:28** Then one of the scribes approached, having heard them disputing together, and seeing that He had answered them well, he asked Him, "Which commandment is foremost of all?"

**12:29** Jesus answered, "The foremost is: 'Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one,

**12:30** and you shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind, and with your whole strength.'

**12:31** The second is this: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself.' There is no other commandment greater than these."

**12:32** And the scribe said to Him, "Well said, Teacher; You have spoken in truth, that He is one, and there is no other besides Him;

**12:33** and to love Him with the whole heart, and with the whole understanding, and with the whole strength, and to love one's neighbor as oneself, is far more than all the burnt offerings and sacrifices."

**12:34** And when Jesus saw that he had answered with discernment, He said to him, "You are not far from the kingdom of God." And after that no one dared to question Him any longer.

**12:35** Then Jesus answered and said, as He taught in the temple, "How is it that the scribes say that the Christ is the Son of David?

**12:36** David himself declared in the Holy Spirit, 'The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at My right hand, until I place Your enemies beneath Your feet.'

**12:37** David himself calls Him Lord; how then is He his son?" And the great multitude heard Him gladly.

**12:38** And in His teaching He said, "Beware of the scribes, who desire to walk about in long robes, and to receive greetings in the marketplaces,

**12:39** and the foremost seats in the synagogues, and the places of honor at banquets;

**12:40** who devour the houses of widows, and for a pretense offer long prayers. These shall receive greater condemnation."

**12:41** And sitting down opposite the treasury, He watched how the multitude was casting money into the treasury; and many who were rich cast in much.

**12:42** And one poor widow came and cast in two lepta, which make a quadrans.

**12:43** Then He called His disciples to Himself and said to them, "Assuredly, I say to you that this poor widow has cast in more than all those who have been casting into the treasury;

**12:44** for they all cast in out of their abundance, whereas she, out of her poverty, cast in all that she possessed—her entire livelihood."

## Translator Notes

- **12:4** — "Wounded in the head" translates the rare Greek verb ekephaliosan, found only here in the New Testament; the critical Greek text does not include the later expansion "threw stones at him."
- **12:26** — "In the passage about the bush" (literally "at the bush") reflects the ancient Jewish way of referencing a section of Scripture (Exodus 3) by its main subject prior to chapter and verse numbering.
- **12:36** — "Beneath Your feet" follows the critical text reading hypokato ("beneath/underneath"), whereas Psalm 110:1 (LXX 109:1) and some later manuscripts read hypopodion ("a footstool").
- **12:42** — A lepton was the smallest Jewish bronze coin in circulation; two lepta equaled one Roman quadrans, the smallest Roman bronze coin (one sixty-fourth of a denarius).
