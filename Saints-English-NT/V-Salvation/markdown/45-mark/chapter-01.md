## Text

**1:1** The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, Son of God.

**1:2** Just as it is written in Isaiah the prophet: "Behold, I am sending My messenger before Your face, who shall prepare Your way;

**1:3** a voice of one crying out in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way of the Lord; make straight His paths,'"

**1:4** John came forth, baptizing in the wilderness and preaching a baptism of repentance for the remission of sins.

**1:5** And all the countryside of Judea and all the inhabitants of Jerusalem were going out to him, and they were being baptized by him in the Jordan River, confessing their sins.

**1:6** Now John was arrayed in camel's hair, with a leather belt around his waist, and he ate locusts and wild honey.

**1:7** And he was preaching, saying, "One mightier than I comes after me, the strap of whose sandals I am not worthy to stoop down and loose;

**1:8** I baptized you with water, but He shall baptize you with the Holy Spirit."

**1:9** And it came to pass in those days that Jesus came from Nazareth of Galilee, and He was baptized in the Jordan by John.

**1:10** And immediately, coming up out of the water, He saw the heavens torn open and the Spirit, like a dove, descending upon Him;

**1:11** and a voice came from the heavens: "You are My Son, the Beloved; in You I am well pleased."

**1:12** And immediately the Spirit drove Him forth into the wilderness.

**1:13** And He was in the wilderness for forty days, being tempted by Satan; and He was with the wild beasts, and the angels ministered to Him.

**1:14** And after John had been delivered up, Jesus came into Galilee, preaching the gospel of God,

**1:15** and saying, "The appointed time has been fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has drawn near; repent, and believe in the gospel."

**1:16** And as He passed along by the Sea of Galilee, He saw Simon and Andrew the brother of Simon casting a net in the sea, for they were fishermen.

**1:17** And Jesus said to them, "Come after Me, and I will make you become fishers of men."

**1:18** And immediately, leaving the nets, they followed Him.

**1:19** And going forward a little, He saw James the son of Zebedee and John his brother, who were also in the boat mending the nets,

**1:20** and immediately He called them. And leaving their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired servants, they went away after Him.

**1:21** And they entered Capernaum, and immediately on the Sabbath He went into the synagogue and began teaching.

**1:22** And they were astounded at His teaching, for He was teaching them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.

**1:23** And immediately there was a man in their synagogue with an unclean spirit, and he cried out,

**1:24** saying, "What have we to do with You, Jesus the Nazarene? Have You come to destroy us? I know who You are—the Holy One of God!"

**1:25** And Jesus rebuked it, saying, "Be silent, and come out of him!"

**1:26** And having convulsed him and cried out with a loud voice, the unclean spirit came out of him.

**1:27** And they were all amazed, so that they questioned among themselves, saying, "What is this? A new teaching! With authority He commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey Him."

**1:28** And immediately the report about Him went out everywhere into the entire surrounding region of Galilee.

**1:29** And immediately, coming out of the synagogue, they went into the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John.

**1:30** Now Simon's mother-in-law was lying ill with a fever, and immediately they spoke to Him concerning her.

**1:31** And coming near, He raised her up, taking her by the hand; and the fever left her, and she ministered to them.

**1:32** Now when evening came, when the sun had set, they were bringing to Him all who were ill and those who were demon-possessed;

**1:33** and the entire city was assembled together at the door.

**1:34** And He healed many who were ill with various diseases, and He cast out many demons; and He did not permit the demons to speak, because they knew Him.

**1:35** And rising early in the morning, while it was still very dark, He went out and departed to a solitary place, and there He was praying.

**1:36** And Simon and those with him searched diligently for Him,

**1:37** and they found Him and said to Him, "Everyone is seeking You."

**1:38** And He said to them, "Let us go elsewhere into the neighboring towns, that I may preach there also; for to this end I came forth."

**1:39** And He went into their synagogues throughout all Galilee, preaching and casting out demons.

**1:40** And a leper came to Him, pleading with Him and kneeling down, saying to Him, "If You are willing, You are able to cleanse me."

**1:41** And moved with compassion, He stretched forth His hand, touched him, and said to him, "I am willing; be cleansed."

**1:42** And immediately the leprosy departed from him, and he was cleansed.

**1:43** And having sternly warned him, He immediately sent him away,

**1:44** and He said to him, "See that you tell no one anything; rather, go, show yourself to the priest, and offer for your cleansing what Moses commanded, as a testimony to them."

**1:45** But having gone out, he began to proclaim it widely and to spread abroad the report, so that He was no longer able to enter a city openly, but remained outside in desolate places; and they were coming to Him from every quarter.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The phrase "Son of God" is present in the Greek text here and in most manuscripts (including Codex Vaticanus), though it is omitted in Codex Sinaiticus and a few other early witnesses.
- **1:2** — The critical text reads "in Isaiah the prophet," introducing a composite quotation drawn from Exodus 23:20 and Malachi 3:1 (v. 2) together with Isaiah 40:3 (v. 3), named after the prominent prophet Isaiah; later manuscripts harmonize this to "in the prophets."
- **1:10** — The Greek participle translated "torn open" (schizomenous) is a forceful verb of rending, the same verb Mark uses for the tearing of the temple curtain in 15:38.
- **1:27** — Following the punctuation of the Greek source text, "with authority" modifies "He commands"; some editors punctuate the clause to read "A new teaching with authority!"
- **1:41** — Most Greek manuscripts read "moved with compassion" (splanchnistheis), followed here, whereas a few Western witnesses read "moved with indignation" (orgistheis).
