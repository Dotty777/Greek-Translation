## Text

**8:1** In those days, when there was again a great multitude and they had nothing to eat, He called His disciples to Himself and said to them,

**8:2** "I am moved with compassion for the multitude, because they have now remained with Me three days and have nothing to eat;

**8:3** and if I send them away fasting to their homes, they will grow faint along the way; and some of them have come from afar."

**8:4** And His disciples answered Him, "Where will anyone be able to satisfy these people with bread here in a desolate place?"

**8:5** And He asked them, "How many loaves do you have?" They replied, "Seven."

**8:6** And He directed the multitude to recline upon the ground. Taking the seven loaves, He gave thanks, broke them, and gave them to His disciples to set before them; and they set them before the multitude.

**8:7** And they had a few small fish; and after blessing them, He directed that these also be set before them.

**8:8** And they ate and were satisfied; and they gathered up seven large baskets of the broken pieces that remained.

**8:9** Now there were about four thousand people. And He sent them away.

**8:10** And immediately He entered the boat with His disciples and came into the district of Dalmanutha.

**8:11** And the Pharisees came forth and began to argue with Him, seeking from Him a sign out of heaven, testing Him.

**8:12** And sighing deeply within His spirit, He said, "Why does this generation seek a sign? Assuredly, I say to you, by no means shall a sign be given to this generation."

**8:13** And leaving them, He embarked again and departed to the other side.

**8:14** Now they had forgotten to take loaves, and except for one loaf they had none with them in the boat.

**8:15** And He solemnly charged them, saying, "Watch, beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and the leaven of Herod."

**8:16** And they reasoned with one another because they had no bread.

**8:17** And knowing this, Jesus said to them, "Why are you reasoning because you have no bread? Do you not yet perceive or understand? Is your heart hardened?

**8:18** Though you have eyes, do you not see? And though you have ears, do you not hear? And do you not remember,

**8:19** when I broke the five loaves for the five thousand, how many baskets full of broken pieces did you gather up?" They said to Him, "Twelve."

**8:20** "And when I broke the seven for the four thousand, how many large basketfuls of broken pieces did you gather up?" And they said, "Seven."

**8:21** And He said to them, "How is it that you do not yet understand?"

**8:22** And they came to Bethsaida; and some brought a blind man to Him and implored Him to touch him.

**8:23** And taking hold of the blind man's hand, He led him outside the village; and after spitting upon his eyes and laying His hands upon him, He asked him, "Do you see anything?"

**8:24** And looking up, he said, "I see men, for I perceive them like trees walking."

**8:25** Then He laid His hands upon his eyes again; and the man looked intently, was restored, and saw all things clearly.

**8:26** And He sent him away to his home, saying, "Do not even enter the village."

**8:27** And Jesus and His disciples went forth into the villages of Caesarea Philippi; and along the way He questioned His disciples, saying to them, "Who do men say that I am?"

**8:28** And they spoke to Him, saying, "John the Baptist; others say Elijah; and still others, one of the prophets."

**8:29** And He asked them, "But you—who do you say that I am?" And Peter answered and said to Him, "You are the Christ."

**8:30** And He sternly warned them to tell no one concerning Him.

**8:31** And He began to teach them that the Son of Man must suffer many things, and be rejected by the elders and the chief priests and the scribes, and be slain, and after three days rise again.

**8:32** And He was speaking this word plainly. And taking Him aside, Peter began to rebuke Him.

**8:33** But turning around and looking upon His disciples, He rebuked Peter and said, "Get behind Me, Satan! For you do not set your mind upon the things of God, but upon the things of men."

**8:34** And calling the multitude together with His disciples, He said to them, "If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow Me.

**8:35** For whoever wishes to save his life shall lose it; but whoever loses his life on account of Me and of the gospel shall save it.

**8:36** For what profit is it to a man to gain the entire world and suffer the loss of his soul?

**8:37** For what could a man give in exchange for his soul?

**8:38** For whoever is ashamed of Me and of My words amid this adulterous and sinful generation, the Son of Man shall also be ashamed of him when He comes in the glory of His Father with the holy angels."

## Translator Notes

- **8:12** — The Greek clause literally reads "if a sign shall be given," an elliptical Semitic oath formula expressing an emphatic negation ("by no means shall a sign be given").
- **8:20** — Mark distinguishes between two Greek words for baskets: kophinos (v. 19), a smaller wicker hand-basket, and spyris (vv. 8, 20), a large woven hamper.
- **8:24** — The Greek syntax literally reads "I see the men, because like trees I perceive them walking," vividly reflecting the man's partial, initial restoration of sight.
- **8:35** — The Greek noun psyche can mean either "life" or "soul," creating a deliberate play across vv. 35-37 between earthly life surrendered for Christ and the enduring soul gained or forfeited.
