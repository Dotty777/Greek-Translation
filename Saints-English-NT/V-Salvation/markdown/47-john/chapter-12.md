## Text

**12:1** Therefore, six days before the Passover, Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was, whom Jesus had raised from the dead.

**12:2** So they prepared a supper for Him there, and Martha served, while Lazarus was one of those who reclined at the table with Him.

**12:3** Then Mary took a pound of very costly ointment of pure spikenard, anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped His feet with her hair; and the house was filled with the fragrance of the ointment.

**12:4** But Judas Iscariot, one of His disciples, who was about to betray Him, said,

**12:5** "Why was this ointment not sold for three hundred denarii and given to the poor?"

**12:6** Now he said this, not because he cared for the poor, but because he was a thief; and having the money box, he used to take what was put into it.

**12:7** Therefore Jesus said, "Leave her alone, that she may keep it for the day of My burial;

**12:8** for the poor you always have with you, but Me you do not always have."

**12:9** Then a great multitude of the Jews learned that He was there; and they came, not because of Jesus alone, but that they might also see Lazarus, whom He had raised from the dead.

**12:10** But the chief priests took counsel that they might put Lazarus to death also,

**12:11** because on account of him many of the Jews were going away and believing in Jesus.

**12:12** On the next day, the great multitude that had come to the feast, when they heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem,

**12:13** took branches of palm trees and went out to meet Him, and cried aloud, "Hosanna! Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord, even the King of Israel!"

**12:14** And Jesus, having found a young donkey, sat upon it, just as it is written:

**12:15** "Do not fear, daughter of Zion; behold, your King is coming, sitting on a donkey's colt."

**12:16** His disciples did not understand these things at first; but when Jesus was glorified, then they remembered that these things were written concerning Him and that they had done these things to Him.

**12:17** Therefore the multitude who had been with Him when He called Lazarus out of the tomb and raised him from the dead bore witness.

**12:18** For this reason also the multitude met Him, because they had heard that He had performed this sign.

**12:19** So the Pharisees said among themselves, "You see that you accomplish nothing! Behold, the world has gone after Him!"

**12:20** Now there were certain Greeks among those who went up to worship at the feast.

**12:21** These, therefore, approached Philip, who was from Bethsaida of Galilee, and asked him, saying, "Sir, we wish to see Jesus."

**12:22** Philip came and told Andrew, and Andrew and Philip came and told Jesus.

**12:23** But Jesus answered them, saying, "The hour has come that the Son of Man should be glorified.

**12:24** Assuredly, assuredly, I say to you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it abides alone; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.

**12:25** He who loves his life loses it, and he who hates his life in this world shall keep it for eternal life.

**12:26** If anyone serves Me, let him follow Me; and where I am, there shall My servant be also. If anyone serves Me, the Father will honor him.

**12:27** Now My soul is troubled, and what shall I say? Father, save Me from this hour! Yet for this purpose I came to this hour.

**12:28** Father, glorify Your name." Then a voice came from heaven: "I have both glorified it, and I will glorify it again."

**12:29** Therefore the multitude who stood by and heard it said that it had thundered; others said, "An angel has spoken to Him."

**12:30** Jesus answered and said, "This voice did not come for My sake, but for yours.

**12:31** Now is the judgment of this world; now the ruler of this world shall be cast out.

**12:32** And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all to Myself."

**12:33** Now He said this, signifying by what manner of death He was about to die.

**12:34** The multitude therefore answered Him, "We have heard from the Law that the Christ abides forever; and how do You say that the Son of Man must be lifted up? Who is this Son of Man?"

**12:35** Then Jesus said to them, "For a little while longer the light is among you. Walk while you have the light, lest darkness overtake you; and he who walks in the darkness does not know where he is going.

**12:36** While you have the light, believe in the light, that you may become sons of light." These things Jesus spoke, and departing, He hid Himself from them.

**12:37** Yet though He had performed so many signs before them, they did not believe in Him,

**12:38** so that the word of Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled, which he spoke: "Lord, who has believed our message, and to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?"

**12:39** For this reason they could not believe, because Isaiah said again:

**12:40** "He has blinded their eyes and hardened their heart, lest they should see with their eyes and understand with their heart and turn, and I would heal them."

**12:41** These things Isaiah said because he saw His glory and spoke concerning Him.

**12:42** Nevertheless, even among the rulers many believed in Him, but because of the Pharisees they did not confess it, lest they should be put out of the synagogue;

**12:43** for they loved the glory of men more than the glory of God.

**12:44** Then Jesus cried aloud and said, "He who believes in Me does not believe in Me, but in Him who sent Me.

**12:45** And he who beholds Me beholds Him who sent Me.

**12:46** I have come into the world as light, that everyone who believes in Me may not abide in the darkness.

**12:47** And if anyone hears My words and does not keep them, I do not judge him; for I did not come to judge the world, but to save the world.

**12:48** He who rejects Me and does not receive My words has one who judges him—the word that I have spoken shall judge him on the last day.

**12:49** For I have not spoken from Myself; rather, the Father who sent Me has Himself given Me a commandment—what I should say and what I should speak.

**12:50** And I know that His commandment is eternal life. Therefore, the things that I speak, just as the Father has spoken to Me, so I speak."

## Translator Notes

- **12:6** — The Greek verb ebastazen literally means "carried," but in the context of Judas being a thief it carries the sense of pilfering or taking from the money box.
- **12:7** — The critical Greek text reads "that she may keep it" (hina ... terese auto) rather than the later variant "she has kept this" (teterken), indicating that Mary was to reserve the ointment for Jesus' burial or that her anointing anticipated it.
- **12:27** — The clause "Father, save Me from this hour" can be read either as an earnest petition immediately yielded to the Father's purpose or as a deliberative question ("Shall I say, 'Father, save Me from this hour'?").
- **12:41** — The critical Greek text reads "because" (hoti) rather than the variant "when" (hote), underscoring that Isaiah spoke these words as a result of beholding Christ's glory (Isaiah 6:1–10).
- **12:47** — The critical Greek text reads "does not keep them" (me phylaxe) rather than the later textual variant "does not believe" (me pisteuse).
