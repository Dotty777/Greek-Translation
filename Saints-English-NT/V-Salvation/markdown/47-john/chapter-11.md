## Text

**11:1** Now there was a certain man who was sick, Lazarus of Bethany, from the village of Mary and Martha her sister.

**11:2** Now it was Mary who anointed the Lord with fragrant ointment and wiped His feet with her hair, whose brother Lazarus lay sick.

**11:3** So the sisters sent word to Him, saying, "Lord, behold, the one You love is sick."

**11:4** And when Jesus heard it, He said, "This sickness is not to end in death, but is for the sake of the glory of God, that through it the Son of God may be glorified."

**11:5** And Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus.

**11:6** Therefore, when He heard that he was sick, He then remained two days in the place where He was.

**11:7** Then after this, He said to the disciples, "Let us go again into Judea."

**11:8** The disciples said to Him, "Rabbi, only just now the Jews were seeking to stone You, and are You going there again?"

**11:9** Jesus replied, "Are there not twelve hours of the day? If anyone walks in the day, he does not stumble, for he sees the light of this world;

**11:10** yet if anyone walks in the night, he stumbles, for the light is not in him."

**11:11** He spoke these words, and afterward He said to them, "Lazarus our friend has fallen asleep; nevertheless, I am going that I may awaken him."

**11:12** So the disciples said to Him, "Lord, if he has fallen asleep, he will be made well."

**11:13** Now Jesus had spoken concerning his death, yet they supposed that He was speaking of the rest of sleep.

**11:14** So then Jesus spoke plainly to them, "Lazarus has died,

**11:15** and for your sake I rejoice that I was not there, so that you may believe. Yet let us go to him."

**11:16** So Thomas, who was called Didymus, spoke to his fellow disciples, "Let us go as well, that we may die with Him."

**11:17** When Jesus came, therefore, He found that he had already been four days in the tomb.

**11:18** Now Bethany was near Jerusalem, about fifteen stadia away.

**11:19** And many from among the Jews had come to Martha and Mary to comfort them over their brother.

**11:20** Therefore Martha, as soon as she heard that Jesus was coming, went to meet Him, while Mary remained sitting in the house.

**11:21** So Martha said to Jesus, "Lord, had You been here, my brother would not have died;

**11:22** and even now I know that whatever You ask of God, God will grant You."

**11:23** Jesus said to her, "Your brother shall rise again."

**11:24** Martha said to Him, "I know that he shall rise in the resurrection on the last day."

**11:25** Jesus said to her, "I am the resurrection and the life. The one who believes in Me, even if he dies, shall live,

**11:26** and everyone who lives and believes in Me shall by no means ever die. Do you believe this?"

**11:27** She said to Him, "Yes, Lord; I have come to believe that You are the Christ, the Son of God, the One coming into the world."

**11:28** When she had spoken this, she departed and privately called her sister Mary, saying, "The Teacher is here, and He calls for you."

**11:29** And when she heard this, she rose up quickly and went toward Him.

**11:30** For Jesus had not yet arrived in the village, but remained still at the place where Martha had met Him.

**11:31** So the Jews who were with her in the house, comforting her, upon seeing that Mary rose swiftly and went out, followed after her, thinking that she was going to the tomb to weep there.

**11:32** Therefore, when Mary came to the place where Jesus was and saw Him, she fell at His feet, saying to Him, "Lord, had You been here, my brother would not have died."

**11:33** When Jesus therefore saw her weeping, and the Jews who had come with her weeping as well, He was deeply stirred in His spirit and troubled Himself,

**11:34** and He asked, "Where have you laid him?" They answered Him, "Lord, come and see."

**11:35** Jesus shed tears.

**11:36** So the Jews were saying, "Behold, how He loved him!"

**11:37** But some among them said, "Could not this Man, who opened the eyes of the blind man, also have kept this one from dying?"

**11:38** Jesus, therefore, deeply stirred once more within Himself, came to the tomb. Now it was a cave, and a stone was laid against it.

**11:39** Jesus said, "Remove the stone." Martha, the sister of the one who had died, said to Him, "Lord, by now there is a stench, for this is the fourth day."

**11:40** Jesus said to her, "Did I not tell you that if you believe, you shall see the glory of God?"

**11:41** So they removed the stone. And Jesus lifted His eyes upward and said, "Father, I thank You that You have heard Me,

**11:42** and I knew that You always hear Me; yet because of the multitude standing around I said this, so that they may believe that You sent Me."

**11:43** And when He had spoken these things, He cried out with a great voice, "Lazarus, come forth!"

**11:44** The one who had died came forth, his feet and hands bound with burial wrappings, and his face wrapped around with a cloth. Jesus said to them, "Loose him and let him depart."

**11:45** Therefore many of the Jews who had come to Mary and beheld the things He did believed in Him;

**11:46** but some among them departed to the Pharisees and told them what Jesus had done.

**11:47** So the chief priests and the Pharisees convened a council and were saying, "What are we to do? For this man is performing many signs.

**11:48** If we permit Him to go on like this, all will believe in Him, and the Romans will come and take away both our place and our nation."

**11:49** But a certain one of them, Caiaphas, being high priest of that year, said to them, "You know nothing whatever,

**11:50** nor do you consider that it is better for you that one man should die on behalf of the people, and that the entire nation should not perish."

**11:51** Now he did not say this of his own accord, but being high priest of that year, he prophesied that Jesus was about to die on behalf of the nation,

**11:52** and not on behalf of the nation alone, but also so that He might gather into one the children of God who are scattered abroad.

**11:53** Therefore, from that day forth, they took counsel that they might put Him to death.

**11:54** So Jesus no longer walked publicly among the Jews, but departed from there into the country near the wilderness, to a city called Ephraim, and there He remained with the disciples.

**11:55** Now the Passover of the Jews was at hand, and many from the country went up to Jerusalem before the Passover to purify themselves.

**11:56** So they were looking for Jesus and speaking among themselves as they stood in the temple, "What do you suppose—that He will by no means come to the feast?"

**11:57** Now the chief priests and the Pharisees had issued commands that if anyone knew where He was, he should disclose it, so that they might seize Him.

## Translator Notes

- **11:16** — Didymus is the Greek equivalent of the Aramaic name Thomas, both meaning Twin.
- **11:18** — Fifteen stadia is approximately two miles (about 2.8 kilometers).
- **11:33** — The Greek verb embrimaomai (used again in 11:38) denotes intense inward agitation, encompassing both deep grief and righteous indignation in the face of death.
- **11:35** — John here shifts from klaio (weep aloud, lament, in 11:31, 33) to dakryo (shed tears), a verb occurring nowhere else in the New Testament.
- **11:41** — The earliest Greek manuscripts read simply So they removed the stone, whereas later manuscripts add from the place where the dead man was lying.
