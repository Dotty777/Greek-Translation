## Text

**19:1** So Pilate then took Jesus and scourged Him.

**19:2** And the soldiers wove a crown of thorns and placed it upon His head, and clothed Him in a purple robe,

**19:3** and they kept coming up to Him and saying, "Hail, King of the Jews!" and kept striking Him with their hands.

**19:4** And Pilate went out again and said to them, "Behold, I bring Him out to you, so that you may know that I find no fault at all in Him."

**19:5** Jesus therefore came outside, wearing the crown of thorns and the purple robe; and he said to them, "Behold, the Man!"

**19:6** When the chief priests and the officers saw Him, therefore, they shouted, saying, "Crucify! Crucify!" Pilate said to them, "Take Him yourselves and crucify Him, for I myself find no fault in Him."

**19:7** The Jews answered him, "We have a law, and according to the law He must die, because He made Himself the Son of God."

**19:8** So when Pilate heard this word, he feared all the more,

**19:9** and he entered into the Praetorium again and said to Jesus, "Where are You from?" Yet Jesus did not give him an answer.

**19:10** Therefore Pilate said to Him, "Do You not speak to me? Do You not know that I have authority to release You, and I have authority to crucify You?"

**19:11** Jesus answered him, "You would have no authority against Me at all unless it had been given to you from above; because of this, the one who delivered Me to you has greater sin."

**19:12** From that moment Pilate was seeking to release Him, but the Jews shouted, saying, "If you release this Man, you are not a friend of Caesar; everyone who makes himself a king speaks against Caesar."

**19:13** Having heard these words, Pilate therefore led Jesus outside and took his seat upon the judgment seat, at a place called the Stone Pavement—but in Hebrew, Gabbatha.

**19:14** Now it was the Preparation of the Passover; it was about the sixth hour. And he said to the Jews, "Behold, your King!"

**19:15** Then they shouted, "Take Him away! Take Him away! Crucify Him!" Pilate said to them, "Shall I crucify your King?" The chief priests answered, "We have no king except Caesar."

**19:16** So then he delivered Him to them that He might be crucified. And they took Jesus;

**19:17** and carrying the cross for Himself, He went out to the place called the Place of a Skull, which in Hebrew is called Golgotha,

**19:18** where they crucified Him, and with Him two others, one on this side and one on that, with Jesus in the midst.

**19:19** Now Pilate also wrote a title and placed it upon the cross, and it was written, "Jesus the Nazarene, the King of the Jews."

**19:20** Therefore many of the Jews read this title, because the place where Jesus was crucified was close to the city; and it was written in Hebrew, in Latin, and in Greek.

**19:21** So the chief priests of the Jews were saying to Pilate, "Do not write, 'The King of the Jews,' but rather that He said, 'I am King of the Jews.'"

**19:22** Pilate replied, "That which I have written, I have written."

**19:23** The soldiers therefore, when they had crucified Jesus, took His garments and made four shares, a share for each soldier, and also the tunic. Now the tunic was seamless, woven from the top throughout.

**19:24** Therefore they said to one another, "Let us not tear it, but let us draw lots for it, whose it shall be," so that the Scripture might be fulfilled which says, "They divided My garments among themselves, and for My vesture they cast a lot." Thus the soldiers did these things.

**19:25** Now beside the cross of Jesus stood His mother and the sister of His mother, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene.

**19:26** Therefore Jesus, seeing His mother and the disciple whom He loved standing nearby, said to His mother, "Woman, behold, your son!"

**19:27** Next He said to the disciple, "Behold, your mother!" And from that hour the disciple received her into his own home.

**19:28** After this, Jesus, knowing that all things had now been accomplished, in order that the Scripture might be fulfilled, said, "I thirst."

**19:29** A vessel full of sour wine was set there; therefore they put a sponge full of the sour wine upon hyssop and brought it to His mouth.

**19:30** When Jesus therefore had received the sour wine, He said, "It is finished!" And bowing His head, He yielded up His spirit.

**19:31** Therefore the Jews, since it was the Preparation, so that the bodies would not remain upon the cross on the Sabbath—for the day of that Sabbath was a high day—asked Pilate that their legs might be broken and that they might be taken away.

**19:32** So the soldiers came and broke the legs of the first, and of the other who had been crucified with Him;

**19:33** but upon coming to Jesus, when they saw that He was already dead, they did not break His legs;

**19:34** rather, one of the soldiers pierced His side with a spear, and immediately blood and water came forth.

**19:35** And he who has seen it has borne witness, and his testimony is true; and he knows that he speaks the truth, so that you also may believe.

**19:36** For these things came to pass so that the Scripture might be fulfilled, "Not a bone of Him shall be broken."

**19:37** And once more another Scripture says, "They shall look upon Him whom they pierced."

**19:38** Now after these things, Joseph of Arimathea—being a disciple of Jesus, though secretly because of fear of the Jews—asked Pilate that he might take away the body of Jesus; and Pilate granted permission. He came therefore and took away His body.

**19:39** And Nicodemus also came—the one who at the first had come to Him by night—bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about one hundred pounds.

**19:40** Therefore they took the body of Jesus and bound it in linen cloths with the spices, as is the custom among the Jews to prepare for burial.

**19:41** Now in the place where He had been crucified was a garden, and in that garden a new tomb, in which no one had as yet been laid.

**19:42** There, therefore, because of the Preparation of the Jews—since the tomb was close at hand—they laid Jesus.

## Translator Notes

- **19:13** — The Greek verb ekathisen can be taken intransitively ("took his seat upon the judgment seat") or transitively ("seated Him upon the judgment seat"), though the intransitive reading is standard. "In Hebrew" (Hebraisti) in John refers to the Aramaic tongue spoken in first-century Judea.
- **19:14** — By Jewish reckoning from sunrise, "about the sixth hour" is roughly noon; some interpreters harmonize this with Mark 15:25 ("the third hour") by understanding John to use Roman timekeeping from midnight (around 6:00 a.m.) or broad three-hour intervals of the day.
- **19:25** — The Greek wording allows either three women (taking "Mary the wife of Clopas" in apposition to "the sister of His mother") or four distinct women. Literally the Greek reads "Mary the [one] of Clopas," traditionally understood as his wife.
- **19:28** — The purpose clause "in order that the Scripture might be fulfilled" can be construed either with what precedes ("all things had now been accomplished") or with what follows (Jesus saying, "I thirst," echoing Psalm 69:21).
- **19:39** — A Roman pound (litra) was about twelve modern ounces (roughly 327 grams), so one hundred pounds equals approximately seventy-five modern pounds (about 34 kilograms), a royal quantity of burial spices.
