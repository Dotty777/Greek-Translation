## Text

**21:1** After these things Jesus manifested Himself again to the disciples at the Sea of Tiberias; and He manifested Himself in this way.

**21:2** There were together Simon Peter, Thomas called Didymus, Nathanael from Cana of Galilee, the sons of Zebedee, and two others from among His disciples.

**21:3** Simon Peter said to them, "I am going fishing." They replied to him, "We also are coming with you." They went forth and entered the boat, and in that night they caught nothing.

**21:4** Now when daybreak had already come, Jesus stood upon the shore; however, the disciples did not know that it was Jesus.

**21:5** Therefore Jesus said to them, "Children, do you have any fish at all?" They replied to Him, "No."

**21:6** And He said to them, "Cast the net upon the right side of the boat, and you shall find some." Therefore they cast it, and they were no longer able to haul it in on account of the multitude of fish.

**21:7** Therefore that disciple whom Jesus loved said to Peter, "It is the Lord!" When Simon Peter heard that it was the Lord, he girded his outer garment around himself, for he was naked, and cast himself into the sea.

**21:8** But the other disciples came in the small boat, for they were not far from the land, only about two hundred cubits away, dragging the net of fish.

**21:9** Therefore, when they had stepped out onto the land, they saw a charcoal fire laid there, and fish lying upon it, and bread.

**21:10** Jesus said to them, "Bring here some of the fish that you have just now caught."

**21:11** So Simon Peter went aboard and hauled the net onto the land, full of large fish, one hundred fifty-three; and even though there were so many, the net was not torn.

**21:12** Jesus said to them, "Come, eat the morning meal." Yet not one of the disciples dared to inquire of Him, "Who are You?" knowing that it was the Lord.

**21:13** Jesus came, took the bread, and gave it to them, and the fish in the same manner.

**21:14** This was now the third time Jesus was manifested to the disciples after He had been raised from the dead.

**21:15** Therefore, when they had finished the morning meal, Jesus said to Simon Peter, "Simon, son of John, do you love Me more than these?" He replied to Him, "Yes, Lord, You know that I love You." He said to him, "Feed My lambs."

**21:16** He spoke to him again, a second time, "Simon, son of John, do you love Me?" He answered Him, "Yes, Lord, You know that I love You." He said to him, "Shepherd My sheep."

**21:17** He said to him the third time, "Simon, son of John, do you love Me?" Peter became grieved because He had said to him the third time, "Do you love Me?" And he replied to Him, "Lord, You know everything; You know that I love You." Jesus said to him, "Feed My sheep.

**21:18** Assuredly, assuredly, I say to you, when you were younger, you girded yourself and walked wherever you desired; yet when you grow old, you shall stretch forth your hands, and another shall gird you and carry you where you do not desire."

**21:19** Now this He said to signify by what manner of death he would glorify God. And having spoken this, He said to him, "Follow Me."

**21:20** Turning around, Peter saw the disciple whom Jesus loved following—the one who at the supper had also leaned back upon His breast and said, "Lord, who is the one betraying You?"

**21:21** Therefore, seeing him, Peter said to Jesus, "Lord, and what of this man?"

**21:22** Jesus said to him, "If I wish that he abide until I come, what is that to you? As for you, follow Me."

**21:23** Therefore this saying went forth among the brethren: that disciple would not die. Yet Jesus did not say to him that he would not die, but rather, "If I wish that he abide until I come, what is that to you?"

**21:24** This is the disciple who bears witness concerning these things, and who has written these things; and we know that his witness is true.

**21:25** Yet there are also many other things that Jesus did; if they were written down one by one, I suppose that not even the world itself could hold the books that would be written.

## Translator Notes

- **21:7** — The Greek word gymnos ('naked') commonly denoted a fisherman or laborer who had laid aside his outer garment and was working lightly clad.
- **21:15** — The critical Greek text reads 'Simon, son of John' here and in verses 16-17 rather than 'son of Jonah,' and 'more than these' may refer to the other disciples, to their devotion, or to Peter's fishing livelihood.
- **21:17** — Across verses 15-17, the Greek alternates between agapao and phileo for 'love,' bosko ('feed') and poimaino ('shepherd'), and terms for 'lambs' and 'sheep' (a diminutive form in verse 17); while some see a deliberate nuance, John frequently uses such pairs as stylistic synonyms.
- **21:25** — The critical Greek text ends here without the closing liturgical 'Amen' added in later manuscripts.
