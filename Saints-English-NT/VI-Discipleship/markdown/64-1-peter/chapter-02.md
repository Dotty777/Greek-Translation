## Text

**2:1** Therefore, putting off all malice and all guile, hypocrisies, envies, and all slanders,

**2:2** as newborn babes, earnestly desire the unadulterated spiritual milk, that by it you may grow up into salvation,

**2:3** if you have tasted that the Lord is good.

**2:4** As you draw near to Him—a living stone, rejected by men indeed, yet chosen and precious in the sight of God—

**2:5** you yourselves also, as living stones, are being built up into a spiritual house for a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.

**2:6** For it is contained in Scripture: "Behold, I lay in Zion a cornerstone, chosen and precious; and he who believes in Him shall never be put to shame."

**2:7** To you therefore who believe belongs this honor; yet to those who disbelieve, "The stone that the builders rejected, that very stone has become the head of the corner,"

**2:8** and, "A stone of stumbling and a rock of offense." They stumble, disobeying the word—to which they were also appointed.

**2:9** But you are a chosen lineage, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for His possession, that you may declare the excellencies of Him who called you out of darkness into His wondrous light;

**2:10** who formerly were not a people, yet now are the people of God; who had not been shown mercy, yet now have been shown mercy.

**2:11** Beloved, I exhort you as aliens and sojourners to abstain from fleshly passions, which wage war against the soul,

**2:12** keeping your conduct noble among the Gentiles, so that in the very matter in which they speak evil of you as wrongdoers, beholding your good works they may glorify God in the day of visitation.

**2:13** Be subject to every human institution for the sake of the Lord, whether to the king as holding supreme authority,

**2:14** or to governors as those sent by him for the punishment of wrongdoers and the commendation of those who do good

**2:15** (for such is the will of God, that by doing good you should silence the ignorance of foolish men);

**2:16** as free people, yet not holding your liberty as a cover for malice, but as bondservants of God.

**2:17** Show honor to everyone; love the brotherhood; fear God; pay honor to the king.

**2:18** Household servants, be subject to your masters in all reverence, not only to those who are good and considerate, but also to those who are perverse.

**2:19** For this is grace, if on account of conscience toward God one bears up under sorrows while suffering unjustly.

**2:20** For what credit is it if you sin and are buffeted and endure it? Yet if, while doing good and suffering, you endure, this is grace before God.

**2:21** For to this very end you were called, because Christ also suffered on your behalf, leaving you a pattern so that you might follow in His footsteps;

**2:22** who did no sin, neither was any guile found in His mouth;

**2:23** who, being reviled, did not revile in return; while suffering, He made no threats, but entrusted Himself to the One who judges justly;

**2:24** He Himself bore our sins in His body upon the tree, so that having died to sins, we might live to righteousness; by His wound you were healed.

**2:25** For you were straying as sheep, yet now you have turned back to the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — Greek logikon carries the senses "spiritual" and "reasonable" (or "of the word"). The critical Greek text includes the concluding phrase "into salvation" (eis soterian), which is absent from the later Byzantine tradition.
- **2:7** — The critical Greek text reads "to those who disbelieve" (apistousin) rather than "to those who are disobedient" (apeithousin).
- **2:19** — Greek charis here denotes "grace" in the sense of divine favor or commendation; syneidesin theou ("conscience toward God") expresses a reverent mindfulness of God.
- **2:21** — The critical Greek text reads "on your behalf, leaving you" (hyper hymon, hymin) rather than "for us, leaving us."
