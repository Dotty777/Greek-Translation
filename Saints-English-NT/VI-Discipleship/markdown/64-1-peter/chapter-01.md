## Text

**1:1** Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to the chosen sojourners of the Dispersion in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia,

**1:2** according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, in the sanctification of the Spirit, for obedience and the sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ: May grace and peace be multiplied to you.

**1:3** Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who according to His great mercy has given us new birth to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead,

**1:4** to an inheritance incorruptible, undefiled, and unfading, preserved in the heavens for you,

**1:5** who are kept under guard by the power of God through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed at the last time.

**1:6** In this you exult, though for a little while now, if it is necessary, you have been grieved by manifold trials,

**1:7** that the proving of your faith—far more precious than gold that perishes, though it is proved by fire—may be found to yield praise, glory, and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ.

**1:8** Though you have not seen Him, you love Him; and though you do not see Him now, yet believing in Him, you exult with a joy beyond telling and filled with glory,

**1:9** obtaining the goal of your faith—the salvation of your souls.

**1:10** Concerning this salvation, the prophets who prophesied of the grace appointed for you searched intently and inquired diligently,

**1:11** searching out what time or what manner of time the Spirit of Christ in them was signifying, when He bore witness beforehand of the sufferings appointed for Christ and the glories that would follow.

**1:12** To them it was revealed that they were ministering these things not for themselves, but for you—things that have now been announced to you through those who preached the gospel to you by the Holy Spirit sent from heaven, things into which angels long to look.

**1:13** Therefore, having girded up the loins of your mind, being wholly sober-minded, set your hope upon the grace that is being brought to you at the revelation of Jesus Christ.

**1:14** As children of obedience, do not be fashioned according to the former lusts in your ignorance,

**1:15** but just as the Holy One who called you is holy, you yourselves also be holy in all your conduct,

**1:16** for it is written, "You shall be holy, because I am holy."

**1:17** And if you call on Him as Father who judges impartially according to the work of each one, conduct yourselves in fear during the time of your sojourning,

**1:18** knowing that it was not with perishable things, silver or gold, that you were redeemed from your vain manner of life handed down from your fathers,

**1:19** but with precious blood, as of an unblemished and spotless lamb—even of Christ,

**1:20** who was foreknown before the foundation of the world, yet was made manifest at the end of the times for your sake,

**1:21** who through Him believe in God, who raised Him from the dead and granted Him glory, so that your faith and hope are set on God.

**1:22** Having purified your souls in obedience to the truth for unfeigned brotherly love, love one another fervently from the heart,

**1:23** having been born again, not of perishable seed, but of imperishable, through the word of God that lives and abides;

**1:24** for, "All flesh is as grass, and all its glory as the blossom of the grass; the grass withers, and the blossom falls away,

**1:25** but the word of the Lord abides forever." And this is the very word that was proclaimed to you as the gospel.

## Translator Notes

- **1:11** — Greek eis tina e poion kairon may be rendered either "what time or what manner of time" (taking both interrogatives with kairon) or "what person or what time."
- **1:12** — The critical Greek text reads "for you" (hymin) rather than "for us" (hemin), emphasizing that the Old Testament prophets ministered for the benefit of the epistle's recipients.
- **1:13** — The adverb teleios ("wholly") is punctuated here with the preceding participle ("being wholly sober-minded"), though many interpreters connect it with the imperative ("set your hope completely").
- **1:22** — The critical Greek text reads simply "from the heart" (ek kardias), without the later manuscript expansions "through the Spirit" and "a pure heart."
