## Text

**3:1** In like manner, wives, be subject to your own husbands, so that even if any do not obey the word, through the manner of life of their wives they shall be won without a word,

**3:2** as they behold your pure conduct in reverent fear.

**3:3** Do not let your adornment be the outward one—the braiding of hair, the wearing of gold ornaments, or the putting on of garments—

**3:4** but rather the hidden person of the heart, in the imperishable grace of a meek and quiet spirit, which is of great value in the sight of God.

**3:5** For thus in times past the holy women who hoped in God also adorned themselves, submitting themselves to their own husbands,

**3:6** just as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him "lord"; whose children you have become, doing good and not fearing any terror.

**3:7** Husbands, in like manner, dwell with them according to knowledge, bestowing honor upon the woman as the weaker vessel, as also to co-heirs of the grace of life, in order that your prayers may not be hindered.

**3:8** Finally, all of you, be harmonious in mind, compassionate, loving the brethren, tenderhearted, and humble-minded,

**3:9** not rendering evil for evil or reviling for reviling, but rather giving a blessing, for to this very purpose you were called, so that you might inherit a blessing.

**3:10** For, "He who wishes to love life and behold good days, let him keep his tongue from evil and his lips from speaking guile;

**3:11** let him turn aside from evil and do what is good; let him search for peace and pursue it.

**3:12** For the eyes of the Lord are upon the righteous, and His ears are attentive to their supplication; yet the face of the Lord is against those who practice evil."

**3:13** And who is the one who will do you harm if you become zealous for that which is good?

**3:14** Yet even if you should suffer on account of righteousness, blessed are you. Do not fear their threats, nor be troubled,

**3:15** but in your hearts sanctify Christ as Lord, always prepared to offer a defense to anyone who asks of you a reason concerning the hope within you,

**3:16** yet with meekness and reverence, maintaining a good conscience, so that in whatever matter you are spoken against, those who malign your good manner of life in Christ may be put to shame.

**3:17** For it is better to suffer while doing good, if the will of God should so desire, than while doing evil.

**3:18** For Christ also died once for sins, the righteous on behalf of the unrighteous, so that He might lead you to God—put to death indeed in the flesh, yet made alive in the spirit;

**3:19** in which also He went and made proclamation to the spirits in prison,

**3:20** who once were disobedient, when the longsuffering of God waited expectantly in the days of Noah, while an ark was being constructed, into which a few—that is, eight souls—were safely delivered through water.

**3:21** Corresponding to this, baptism now saves you also—not the putting off of the filth of the flesh, but the appeal of a good conscience toward God—through the resurrection of Jesus Christ,

**3:22** who is at the right hand of God, having gone into heaven, with angels, authorities, and powers subjected to Him.

## Translator Notes

- **3:15** — The critical Greek text reads "Christ as Lord" (kyrion de ton Christon) rather than "the Lord God" (kyrion de ton theon), applying the language of Isaiah 8:13 directly to Christ.
- **3:18** — The critical Greek text reads "died" (apethanen) and "lead you" (hymas) rather than "suffered" and "us." The dative pneumati ("in the spirit") is debated, referring either to the spiritual realm of Christ's resurrection life or to His being made alive by the Holy Spirit.
- **3:19** — The identity of "the spirits in prison" and the timing of Christ's proclamation are much debated, with interpreters viewing them as rebellious angelic spirits (cf. Gen 6:1-4; 2 Pet 2:4), the disobedient generation of Noah's day, or those to whom Christ preached through Noah in the Spirit.
- **3:21** — Greek eperotema can mean "appeal," "pledge," or "inquiry," contrasting an inward moral appeal or commitment to God with mere outward bodily washing.
