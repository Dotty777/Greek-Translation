## Text

**5:1** Therefore I exhort the elders among you, as a fellow elder and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, and a partaker also of the glory about to be revealed:

**5:2** shepherd the flock of God that is among you, not under compulsion but willingly, according to God; neither for shameful gain, but with eagerness;

**5:3** nor as domineering over those allotted to your charge, but becoming examples to the flock.

**5:4** And when the Chief Shepherd is manifested, you shall receive the unfading crown of glory.

**5:5** Likewise, you younger ones, submit yourselves to the elders. Moreover, all of you, gird yourselves with humility toward one another, because "God resists the proud, yet gives grace to the humble."

**5:6** Humble yourselves, therefore, beneath the mighty hand of God, so that He may exalt you at the appointed time,

**5:7** casting all your anxious care upon Him, because He cares for you.

**5:8** Be sober-minded, keep watch. Your adversary the devil goes about as a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour.

**5:9** Withstand him, steadfast in the faith, knowing that the same sufferings are being fulfilled among your brotherhood in the world.

**5:10** Now the God of all grace, who called you into His eternal glory in Christ, after you have suffered for a little while, shall Himself restore, establish, strengthen, and ground you.

**5:11** To Him belongs the dominion forever and ever. Amen.

**5:12** Through Silvanus, whom I reckon a faithful brother, I have written to you in few words, exhorting and bearing witness that this is the true grace of God; stand fast in it.

**5:13** She who is in Babylon, chosen along with you, greets you, as does Mark, my son.

**5:14** Greet one another with a kiss of love. Peace be with all of you who are in Christ.

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — The critical Greek text omits 'serving as overseers' (episkopountes) and includes 'according to God' (kata theon), both of which vary across ancient manuscripts.
- **5:5** — The rare verb enkomboomai literally means to tie on a garment, evoking a servant girding on an apron of humility; the quotation is drawn from Proverbs 3:34 in the Septuagint.
- **5:12** — The critical Greek text reads the imperative 'stand fast' (stete) rather than the indicative 'you stand' (hestekate) found in later manuscripts.
- **5:13** — 'She who is in Babylon' is widely understood as a figurative reference to the church in Rome, using 'Babylon' as a symbolic designation for the imperial capital.
