## Text

**2:1** Therefore, if there is any encouragement in Christ, if any consolation of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit, if any tender affection and compassion,

**2:2** make my joy full by being of the same mind, keeping the same love, being of one soul, and of one purpose,

**2:3** doing nothing out of selfish ambition or vainglory, but in humility of mind esteeming one another above yourselves,

**2:4** each of you looking not only to his own concerns, but each also to the concerns of others.

**2:5** Keep this mind among yourselves, which was also in Christ Jesus,

**2:6** who, existing in the form of God, did not reckon being equal with God a prize to be grasped,

**2:7** but emptied Himself, taking the form of a bondservant and coming to be in the likeness of men; and being found in appearance as a man,

**2:8** He humbled Himself, becoming obedient even to death—even the death of a cross.

**2:9** Therefore God also exalted Him exceedingly, and graciously bestowed upon Him the name which is above every name,

**2:10** so that at the name of Jesus every knee may bow, of those in heaven, of those upon the earth, and of those beneath the earth,

**2:11** and every tongue may openly confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

**2:12** So then, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed—not only as in my presence, but now far more in my absence—work out your own salvation with fear and trembling,

**2:13** for God is the One working in you both to will and to act for the sake of His good pleasure.

**2:14** Do everything without murmurings and disputes,

**2:15** so that you may prove to be blameless and pure, children of God without blemish in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, among whom you shine as luminaries in the world,

**2:16** holding fast the word of life, so that I may have cause to glory on the day of Christ, that I did not run in vain nor labor in vain.

**2:17** Yet even if I am poured out as a drink offering upon the sacrifice and sacred service of your faith, I rejoice and share my joy with all of you;

**2:18** and in the same manner you also rejoice, and share your joy with me.

**2:19** Now I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, so that I also may be of good cheer upon learning how it goes with you.

**2:20** For I have no one of like spirit who will genuinely care for your concerns,

**2:21** for all seek their own concerns, not the things of Jesus Christ.

**2:22** Yet you know his proven character, that as a child with a father he served alongside me for the gospel.

**2:23** Him, therefore, I hope to send immediately, as soon as I see how matters stand with me;

**2:24** and I am confident in the Lord that I myself shall also come soon.

**2:25** Yet I have deemed it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus, my brother, fellow worker, and fellow soldier, and your messenger and minister to my need,

**2:26** because he was yearning for all of you and was sorely distressed because you heard that he was ill.

**2:27** For indeed he was ill, nearly to the point of death; yet God had mercy on him, and not on him alone, but also on me, lest I should have sorrow upon sorrow.

**2:28** Therefore I have sent him the more eagerly, so that seeing him again you may rejoice, and I may be freer from sorrow.

**2:29** Welcome him, therefore, in the Lord with all joy, and hold such men in honor,

**2:30** because for the work of Christ he came close to death, hazarding his life in order to complete what was lacking in your service toward me.

## Translator Notes

- **2:5** — The Greek omits the verb in the second clause, allowing either an ethical reading ('which was also in Christ Jesus') or a relational reading ('which is also yours in Christ Jesus').
- **2:6** — The rare Greek noun harpagmos ('a prize to be grasped') can denote either something not yet possessed to be seized or an existing privilege to be exploited for self-advantage.
- **2:16** — The Greek participle epechontes can mean either 'holding fast' (clinging firmly to the gospel) or 'holding forth' (offering the word of life to the world).
- **2:30** — The critical Greek text reads paraboleusamenos ('risking' or 'hazarding' one's life), whereas later manuscripts read parabouleusamenos ('disregarding' one's life).
