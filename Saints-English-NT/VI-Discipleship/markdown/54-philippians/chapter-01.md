## Text

**1:1** Paul and Timothy, bondservants of Christ Jesus, to all the saints in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, together with the overseers and deacons:

**1:2** Grace to you and peace from God our Father, and from the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:3** I give thanks to my God upon every remembrance of you,

**1:4** always in every supplication of mine for you all, making my supplication with joy,

**1:5** on account of your fellowship in the gospel from the first day even until now,

**1:6** being persuaded of this very thing, that He who began a good work in you will bring it to perfection until the day of Christ Jesus;

**1:7** even as it is right for me to be thus minded concerning all of you, because I hold you in my heart, seeing that both in my chains and in the defense and confirmation of the gospel, you are all fellow partakers with me of grace.

**1:8** For God is my witness how earnestly I long for all of you with the tender affection of Christ Jesus.

**1:9** And this is my prayer, that your love may abound yet more and more in full knowledge and all discernment,

**1:10** so that you may test and approve the things that are excellent, and so be pure and without offense until the day of Christ,

**1:11** being filled with the fruit of righteousness which is through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God.

**1:12** Now I desire you to know, brethren, that the things concerning me have rather served for the advancement of the gospel,

**1:13** so that my chains have become evident in Christ throughout the entire praetorian guard and to all the rest,

**1:14** and most of the brethren in the Lord, having gained confidence through my chains, are far more bold to speak the word of God without fear.

**1:15** Some indeed preach Christ even out of envy and strife, and others also out of goodwill;

**1:16** the latter preach out of love, knowing that I am appointed for the defense of the gospel;

**1:17** while the former proclaim Christ out of selfish ambition, not sincerely, supposing to stir up affliction in my chains.

**1:18** What then? Only that in every manner, whether in pretense or in truth, Christ is proclaimed; and in this I rejoice. Yes, and I shall rejoice,

**1:19** for I know that through your supplication and the provision of the Spirit of Jesus Christ this will turn out for my deliverance,

**1:20** according to my eager expectation and hope that I shall not be put to shame in anything, but that with all boldness, as always, so now as well Christ shall be magnified in my body, whether by life or by death.

**1:21** For to me, to live means Christ, and to die means gain.

**1:22** Yet if I am to go on living in the flesh, this will mean fruit of labor for me—and which I shall choose I cannot tell.

**1:23** Indeed, I am hard pressed between the two, having the desire to depart and to be with Christ, for that is far better indeed;

**1:24** yet to remain on in the flesh is more necessary on your account.

**1:25** And being persuaded of this, I know that I shall abide and continue with you all for your advancement and joy of the faith,

**1:26** so that in me your ground for glorying in Christ Jesus may abound through my coming to you again.

**1:27** Only conduct yourselves as citizens worthy of the gospel of Christ, so that whether I come and see you or remain absent, I may hear concerning you that you stand firm in one spirit, with one soul contending together for the faith of the gospel,

**1:28** and not frightened in anything by those who oppose you, which to them is a clear sign of destruction, yet of your salvation, and that from God;

**1:29** for to you it has been graciously granted on behalf of Christ, not only to believe in Him, but also to suffer for His sake,

**1:30** engaged in the same conflict that you saw in me and now hear to be in me.

## Translator Notes

- **1:7** — The Greek infinitive clause can be construed either as 'because I hold you in my heart' (the most common reading) or 'because you hold me in your heart.'
- **1:13** — The Greek term praitorion refers either to the elite imperial (praetorian) guard stationed in Rome or to the governor's official headquarters.
- **1:16** — The critical Greek text places the group preaching out of love in verse 16 and the group acting out of selfish ambition in verse 17, reversing the order found in the Majority Text.
- **1:27** — The Greek verb politeuomai literally means 'to conduct oneself as a citizen,' an apt metaphor for believers living in the Roman colony of Philippi.
