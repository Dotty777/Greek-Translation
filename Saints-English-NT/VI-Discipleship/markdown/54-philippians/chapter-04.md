## Text

**4:1** Therefore, my brethren, beloved and longed for, my joy and my crown, stand firm thus in the Lord, beloved.

**4:2** I urge Euodia, and I urge Syntyche, to be of one mind in the Lord.

**4:3** Yes, I ask you also, true yokefellow, assist these women, who contended alongside me in the gospel, together with Clement also and the rest of my fellow laborers, whose names are in the book of life.

**4:4** Rejoice in the Lord always; again I shall say, rejoice.

**4:5** Let your forbearance be known to all men. The Lord is near.

**4:6** Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything, by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving, let your petitions be made known to God;

**4:7** and the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, shall guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.

**4:8** Finally, brethren, whatever things are true, whatever things are honorable, whatever things are righteous, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are of good repute—if there is any virtue and if there is anything worthy of praise, ponder these things.

**4:9** What you have learned and received and heard and seen in me, put these things into practice; and the God of peace shall be with you.

**4:10** Now I rejoiced greatly in the Lord that now at length you have revived your concern for me; indeed, you were already concerned, yet you lacked opportunity.

**4:11** Not that I speak because of want, for I have learned, in whatever state I find myself, to be content.

**4:12** I know how to be brought low, and I know also how to abound. In every situation and in all things I have learned the secret both of being filled and of going hungry, both of abounding and of suffering want.

**4:13** I have strength for all things in Him who empowers me.

**4:14** Nevertheless, you have done well by sharing with me in my affliction.

**4:15** Now you yourselves also know, Philippians, that in the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no church shared with me in the account of giving and receiving except you alone;

**4:16** for even in Thessalonica you sent once and again to supply my need.

**4:17** Not that I seek the gift, rather I seek the fruit that multiplies to your account.

**4:18** Indeed, I have received everything in full and have an abundance; I am well supplied, having received from Epaphroditus the things sent from you, a fragrant aroma, an acceptable sacrifice, well-pleasing to God.

**4:19** And my God shall supply every need of yours according to His riches in glory in Christ Jesus.

**4:20** Now to our God and Father be the glory forever and ever. Amen.

**4:21** Greet every saint in Christ Jesus. The brethren who are with me send greetings to you.

**4:22** All the saints send you greetings, and especially those of Caesar's household.

**4:23** The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Greek phrase gnesie syzyge ("true yokefellow" or "loyal companion") may address an unnamed co-worker, though some take Syzygus as a proper name.
- **4:12** — The verb memyemai ("I have learned the secret") originally referred to initiation into a mystery, here depicting Paul's hard-won spiritual contentment.
- **4:13** — The earliest Greek manuscripts read simply "in Him who empowers me," without explicitly adding "Christ" as in later manuscripts.
- **4:23** — The Greek critical text reads "with your spirit," whereas the later Byzantine manuscript tradition reads "with you all."
