## Text

**5:1** Therefore, become imitators of God, as beloved children,

**5:2** and walk in love, just as Christ also loved you and gave Himself up for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God for a fragrant aroma.

**5:3** But sexual immorality and all impurity or covetousness must not even be named among you, as is fitting for saints,

**5:4** nor obscenity, nor foolish talk, nor coarse jesting, which are not proper, but rather thanksgiving.

**5:5** For this you know with certainty, that no sexually immoral person, nor unclean person, nor covetous man—that is, an idolater—has any inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and God.

**5:6** Let no one deceive you with empty words; for on account of these things the wrath of God comes upon the sons of disobedience.

**5:7** Therefore do not become partakers with them;

**5:8** for you were formerly darkness, yet now you are light in the Lord. Walk as children of light

**5:9** (for the fruit of the light consists in all goodness and righteousness and truth),

**5:10** proving what is well-pleasing to the Lord;

**5:11** and do not share in the unfruitful works of darkness, but instead even expose them;

**5:12** for it is shameful even to speak of the things done by them in secret.

**5:13** Yet all things, when exposed by the light, are made manifest; for everything that is made manifest is light.

**5:14** Therefore it says, "Awake, O sleeper, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall shine upon you."

**5:15** Watch carefully therefore how you walk, not as unwise, but as wise,

**5:16** redeeming the time, for the days are evil.

**5:17** For this reason, do not become foolish, but understand what the will of the Lord is.

**5:18** And do not become drunk with wine, in which is debauchery, but be filled with the Spirit,

**5:19** speaking among yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody with your heart to the Lord,

**5:20** giving thanks at all times for all things, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, to God and the Father,

**5:21** being subject to one another in the fear of Christ.

**5:22** Wives, be in subjection to your own husbands, as to the Lord;

**5:23** for the husband is head of the wife, even as Christ also is head of the church, He Himself being the Savior of the body.

**5:24** Yet as the church is subject to Christ, so also let wives be to their husbands in everything.

**5:25** Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself up for her,

**5:26** so that He might sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of water with the word,

**5:27** so that He Himself might present to Himself the church in splendor, having neither spot nor wrinkle nor any such thing, but that she might be holy and without blemish.

**5:28** In this same way husbands also ought to love their own wives as their own bodies. He who loves his own wife loves himself;

**5:29** for no one ever hated his own flesh, but instead nourishes and cherishes it, even as Christ also does the church,

**5:30** for we are members of His body.

**5:31** For this cause a man shall leave father and mother and be united to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh.

**5:32** This mystery is great, yet I am speaking with reference to Christ and the church.

**5:33** Nevertheless, let each one of you individually love his own wife just as himself, and let the wife see that she reverences her husband.

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — The critical Greek text shifts from "loved you" (humas) to "gave Himself up for us" (hemon), preserving the pronoun variation of the earliest manuscripts rather than harmonizing them.
- **5:9** — The earliest Greek manuscripts read "the fruit of the light" rather than "the fruit of the Spirit," directly continuing the light-and-darkness contrast of verse 8.
- **5:13** — The Greek participle phaneroumenon is debated as either passive ("everything that is made manifest is light") or middle ("everything that makes manifest is light"), and "by the light" may modify either "exposed" or "made manifest."
- **5:22** — The earliest Greek manuscripts have no explicit verb in this verse, carrying over "being subject" from verse 21; the verb "be in subjection" is supplied for English sense.
- **5:30** — The earliest Greek manuscripts end at "members of His body"; later manuscripts expand the verse with "of His flesh and of His bones," echoing Genesis 2:23.
