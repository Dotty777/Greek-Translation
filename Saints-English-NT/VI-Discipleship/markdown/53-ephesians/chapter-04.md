## Text

**4:1** Therefore I, the prisoner in the Lord, exhort you to walk worthy of the calling by which you were called,

**4:2** with all lowliness of mind and meekness, with longsuffering, bearing with one another in love,

**4:3** diligently striving to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

**4:4** There is one body and one Spirit, even as you also were called in one hope of your calling;

**4:5** one Lord, one faith, one baptism;

**4:6** one God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in all.

**4:7** Now to each one of us grace was given according to the measure of the gift of Christ.

**4:8** Therefore it says, "Having ascended on high, He led captivity captive; He gave gifts to men."

**4:9** Now this, "He ascended"—what does it mean, except that He also descended into the lower parts of the earth?

**4:10** He who descended is Himself also the One who ascended far above all the heavens, so that He might fill all things.

**4:11** And He Himself gave some as apostles, some as prophets, some as evangelists, and some as shepherds and teachers,

**4:12** for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, toward the building up of the body of Christ,

**4:13** until we all attain to the unity of the faith and of the full knowledge of the Son of God, to a mature man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;

**4:14** so that we may no longer be infants, tossed by waves and carried about by every wind of doctrine, by the trickery of men, in craftiness toward deceitful scheming;

**4:15** rather, speaking the truth in love, we may grow up in every way into Him who is the head, Christ,

**4:16** from whom the whole body, being framed together and knit together through every joint of supply, according to the working in due measure of each single part, brings about the growth of the body for the building up of itself in love.

**4:17** Therefore this I say and solemnly testify in the Lord, that you must no longer walk just as the Gentiles also walk, in the futility of their mind,

**4:18** being darkened in their understanding, alienated from the life of God because of the ignorance that is in them, because of the hardness of their heart;

**4:19** who, having become callous, gave themselves over to licentiousness for the practice of all uncleanness with greediness.

**4:20** But you did not learn Christ in this way,

**4:21** if indeed you heard Him and were taught in Him, just as truth is in Jesus:

**4:22** that you lay aside, with respect to your former manner of life, the old man who is being corrupted according to the lusts of deceit,

**4:23** and that you be renewed in the spirit of your mind,

**4:24** and that you put on the new man, created according to God in righteousness and holiness of the truth.

**4:25** Therefore, having laid aside falsehood, speak truth, each one with his neighbor, for we are members of one another.

**4:26** Be angry, and do not sin; do not let the sun set upon your wrath,

**4:27** and do not give place to the devil.

**4:28** Let the one who steals steal no more, but rather let him labor, working with his hands what is good, so that he may have something to share with the one who has need.

**4:29** Let no corrupt speech come forth from your mouth, but only what is good for edification according to the need, so that it may give grace to those who hear.

**4:30** And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, in whom you were sealed for the day of redemption.

**4:31** Let all bitterness and fury and wrath and outcry and slander be taken away from you, together with all malice.

**4:32** And become kind toward one another, tenderhearted, forgiving each other, just as God also in Christ forgave you.

## Translator Notes

- **4:8** — Paul cites Psalm 68:18 (LXX 67:19), reading "He gave gifts to men" rather than "you received gifts among men" as in the Hebrew and Septuagint texts. The critical Greek text also omits "and" before "He gave."
- **4:9** — The Greek phrase "the lower parts of the earth" is variously understood as referring to the earth itself in contrast to heaven, to the realm of the dead, or to the depths of the incarnation.
- **4:11** — The Greek term rendered "shepherds" (poimenas) is traditionally translated "pastors" in ecclesiastical usage; the shared syntax of "shepherds and teachers" suggests closely related or overlapping ministries.
- **4:29** — The phrase "for edification according to the need" translates a compressed Greek genitive (pros oikodomen tes chreias, literally "for building up of the need"), indicating speech fitted to build up hearers as the occasion requires.
