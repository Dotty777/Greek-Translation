## Text

**23:1** Then Paul, gazing intently at the council, said, "Men and brethren, I have conducted my life in all good conscience before God until this day."

**23:2** And the high priest Ananias commanded those standing beside him to strike his mouth.

**23:3** Then Paul said to him, "God is about to strike you, you whitewashed wall! And do you sit judging me according to the law, yet contrary to the law command me to be struck?"

**23:4** And those who stood by said, "Do you revile the high priest of God?"

**23:5** Then Paul said, "I did not know, brethren, that he is the high priest; for it is written, 'You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people.'"

**23:6** Now when Paul perceived that the one part were Sadducees and the other Pharisees, he cried out in the council, "Men and brethren, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees; concerning the hope and resurrection of the dead I am being judged!"

**23:7** And as he spoke this, a dissension arose between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the multitude was divided.

**23:8** For Sadducees say that there is no resurrection—neither angel nor spirit—but Pharisees confess both.

**23:9** Then there arose a great outcry, and certain of the scribes of the Pharisees' party stood up and contended sharply, saying, "We find nothing evil in this man; and if a spirit has spoken to him, or an angel—"

**23:10** And when a great dissension arose, the commander, fearing lest Paul be torn in pieces by them, ordered the soldiers to go down and seize him from their midst, and to bring him into the barracks.

**23:11** Now on the following night the Lord stood by him and said, "Be of good courage; for as you have solemnly testified concerning Me in Jerusalem, so you must also bear witness in Rome."

**23:12** And when day came, the Jews formed a conspiracy and bound themselves under a curse, saying that they would neither eat nor drink until they had slain Paul.

**23:13** Now there were more than forty who had made this plot.

**23:14** They came to the chief priests and the elders and said, "We have bound ourselves under a solemn curse to taste nothing until we have slain Paul.

**23:15** Now therefore, you, together with the council, give notice to the commander that he should bring him down to you, as though you were about to examine the matters concerning him more accurately; and we, before he draws near, are ready to put him to death."

**23:16** But when the son of Paul's sister heard of the ambush, he came and entered the barracks and reported it to Paul.

**23:17** Then Paul called one of the centurions to him and said, "Lead this young man to the commander, for he has something to report to him."

**23:18** So he took him and brought him to the commander and said, "Paul the prisoner called me to him and asked me to bring this young man to you, since he has something to tell you."

**23:19** Then the commander took him by the hand, withdrew privately, and inquired, "What is it that you have to report to me?"

**23:20** And he said, "The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down to the council tomorrow, as though you were about to inquire somewhat more accurately concerning him.

**23:21** Do not be persuaded by them, therefore; for more than forty men from among them lie in ambush for him, who have bound themselves under a curse neither to eat nor to drink until they have put him to death; and now they are ready, awaiting the promise from you."

**23:22** So the commander dismissed the young man, charging him, "Tell no one that you have disclosed these things to me."

**23:23** And he called two of the centurions to him and said, "Make ready two hundred soldiers to go as far as Caesarea, along with seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen, at the third hour of the night,"

**23:24** and he told them to provide mounts on which to place Paul, that they might bring him safely to Felix the governor.

**23:25** He also wrote a letter having this form:

**23:26** "Claudius Lysias, to the most excellent governor Felix: Greetings.

**23:27** This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be put to death by them, when I came upon them with the troops and rescued him, having learned that he is a Roman.

**23:28** And desiring to know the charge for which they were accusing him, I brought him down into their council.

**23:29** I found him to be accused concerning questions of their law, yet having no charge worthy of death or of chains.

**23:30** And when it was disclosed to me that there would be a plot against the man, I sent him to you at once, having also commanded his accusers to speak against him before you."

**23:31** So the soldiers, according to what had been commanded them, took Paul and brought him by night to Antipatris.

**23:32** And on the next day, leaving the horsemen to go on with him, they returned to the barracks.

**23:33** When these entered Caesarea and delivered the letter to the governor, they also presented Paul before him.

**23:34** And when he had read it and asked from what province he was, and learned that he was from Cilicia,

**23:35** "I will hear you fully," he said, "when your accusers also have arrived," and he commanded him to be kept under guard in Herod's Praetorium.

## Translator Notes

- **23:9** — The Greek sentence breaks off mid-thought (aposiopesis) amid the uproar; the earliest manuscripts omit the later scribal completion "let us not fight against God."
- **23:23** — The rare Greek term dexiolaboi (literally "right-hand graspers") denotes light-armed spearmen or lancers; the third hour of the night was about nine o'clock in the evening.
- **23:24** — The Greek shifts from direct command in verse 23 to indirect discourse here (an infinitive followed by a third-person verb), reflected by closing the quotation at the end of verse 23.
