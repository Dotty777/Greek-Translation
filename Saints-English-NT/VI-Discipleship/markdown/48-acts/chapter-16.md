## Text

**16:1** Now he arrived also at Derbe and at Lystra. And behold, there was a certain disciple there named Timothy, the son of a believing Jewish woman, yet of a Greek father,

**16:2** who received a good testimony from the brethren in Lystra and Iconium.

**16:3** Paul desired that this man go forth with him; and taking him, he circumcised him because of the Jews who were in those places, for all of them knew that his father was a Greek.

**16:4** And as they journeyed through the cities, they delivered to them the decrees to observe, which had been decided by the apostles and elders who were in Jerusalem.

**16:5** Thus the churches were strengthened in the faith and abounded in number day by day.

**16:6** Now they went through the Phrygian and Galatian region, having been forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word in Asia;

**16:7** and having come up to Mysia, they attempted to journey into Bithynia, yet the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them.

**16:8** Then, passing by Mysia, they descended to Troas.

**16:9** And a vision appeared to Paul during the night: a certain Macedonian man stood appealing to him and saying, "Cross over into Macedonia and help us."

**16:10** And when he had seen the vision, at once we sought to depart for Macedonia, concluding that God had called us to proclaim the gospel to them.

**16:11** Therefore, putting out to sea from Troas, we sailed a straight course to Samothrace, and on the following day to Neapolis,

**16:12** and from there to Philippi, which is a foremost city of that district of Macedonia, a colony. And we remained in this city for some days.

**16:13** And on the Sabbath day we went outside the gate beside a river, where we supposed there was a place of prayer; and sitting down, we spoke to the women who had gathered together.

**16:14** And a certain woman by the name of Lydia, a seller of purple from the city of Thyatira, a worshiper of God, was listening, whose heart the Lord opened to give heed to the things spoken by Paul.

**16:15** And when she had been baptized, along with her household, she appealed to us, saying, "If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, enter into my house and abide." And she prevailed upon us.

**16:16** And it came to pass, as we were going to the place of prayer, that a certain young slave girl with a spirit of divination met us, who brought great profit to her masters by fortune-telling.

**16:17** Following closely after Paul and us, she cried out, saying, "These men are bondservants of the Most High God, who declare to you a way of salvation."

**16:18** And she kept doing this for many days. But Paul, being greatly grieved, turned and said to the spirit, "I charge you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out from her." And it departed that very hour.

**16:19** But when her masters saw that the hope of their profit had departed, seizing Paul and Silas, they dragged them into the marketplace before the rulers,

**16:20** and having brought them before the magistrates, they said, "These men are throwing our city into turmoil, being Jews,

**16:21** and they proclaim customs that are not lawful for us to accept or to practice, since we are Romans."

**16:22** And the multitude rose up together against them; and the magistrates tore off their garments and ordered them to be beaten with rods.

**16:23** And having laid many lashes upon them, they cast them into prison, charging the jailer to guard them securely;

**16:24** who, having received such a charge, thrust them into the inner prison and secured their feet in the stocks.

**16:25** Now about midnight, as they prayed, Paul and Silas were singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening attentively to them.

**16:26** And suddenly a great earthquake took place, so that the foundations of the prison house were shaken; and instantly all the doors were opened, and the bonds of all were unfastened.

**16:27** And the jailer, having awakened from sleep and seeing the doors of the prison standing open, drew his sword and was about to slay himself, supposing that the prisoners had fled.

**16:28** But Paul called out in a loud voice, saying, "Do no harm to yourself, for all of us are here."

**16:29** Then he asked for lights and rushed inside, and trembling, he fell down before Paul and Silas;

**16:30** and having brought them outside, he said, "Sirs, what must I do that I may be saved?"

**16:31** And they said, "Believe upon the Lord Jesus, and you shall be saved—you and your household."

**16:32** And they spoke the word of the Lord to him, together with all those who were in his house.

**16:33** And taking them in that very hour of the night, he washed their wounds; and at once he himself and all his own were baptized.

**16:34** And bringing them up into his house, he set a table before them and rejoiced greatly with his entire household, having believed in God.

**16:35** Now when day broke, the magistrates sent the officers, saying, "Release those men."

**16:36** And the jailer reported these words to Paul: "The magistrates have sent word that you should be released. Now therefore, come forth and go in peace."

**16:37** But Paul said to them, "Having beaten us publicly, uncondemned—men who are Romans—they cast us into prison; and now are they casting us out secretly? Certainly not! Rather, let them come themselves and lead us out."

**16:38** Then the officers reported these words to the magistrates, and they became afraid when they heard that they were Romans;

**16:39** and coming, they apologized to them; and having led them out, they asked them to depart from the city.

**16:40** And coming forth from the prison, they went to Lydia's house; and having seen the brethren, they encouraged them and departed.

## Translator Notes

- **16:7** — The Greek text reads "the Spirit of Jesus" (a rare New Testament title emphasizing the Spirit as sent by the risen Christ); some later manuscripts read simply "the Spirit."
- **16:12** — The Greek phrase rendered "a foremost city of that district of Macedonia" is grammatically debated; it may denote either Philippi's prominence within its Macedonian subdivision or a city of the first district of Macedonia.
- **16:16** — Literally "a Python spirit," referring to the mantic spirit associated in Greek antiquity with the Delphic oracle of Apollo.
- **16:31** — The Greek text reads simply "the Lord Jesus," without "Christ" as found in some later manuscripts.
