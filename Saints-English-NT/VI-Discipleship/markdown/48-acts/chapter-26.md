## Text

**26:1** Then Agrippa said to Paul, "You are permitted to speak for yourself." So Paul stretched forth his hand and made his defense:

**26:2** "Concerning all the things of which I am accused by the Jews, King Agrippa, I count myself fortunate that I am to make my defense before you today,

**26:3** especially because you are well acquainted with all the customs and controversies among the Jews; therefore I implore you to hear me patiently.

**26:4** My manner of life from my youth, which from the beginning was spent among my own nation and in Jerusalem, all the Jews know,

**26:5** having known me from the first, if they are willing to testify, that according to the strictest sect of our religion I lived as a Pharisee.

**26:6** And now I stand on trial for the hope of the promise made by God to our fathers,

**26:7** to which our twelve tribes, earnestly worshiping night and day, hope to attain. Concerning this hope, O king, I am accused by the Jews.

**26:8** Why is it judged incredible among you if God raises the dead?

**26:9** Indeed, I myself once thought that I ought to do many things in opposition to the name of Jesus of Nazareth.

**26:10** This I also did in Jerusalem; and many of the saints I locked up in prisons, having received authority from the chief priests, and when they were being put to death, I cast my vote against them.

**26:11** And punishing them often in every synagogue, I sought to compel them to blaspheme; and being exceedingly enraged against them, I persecuted them even as far as foreign cities.

**26:12** While engaged in these things, as I was journeying to Damascus with authority and a commission from the chief priests,

**26:13** at midday along the road, O king, I saw a light from heaven, surpassing the splendor of the sun, shining round about me and those who were journeying with me.

**26:14** And when we had all fallen to the ground, I heard a voice speaking to me in the Hebrew tongue, 'Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads.'

**26:15** And I said, 'Who are You, Lord?' And the Lord said, 'I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.

**26:16** But rise and stand upon your feet; for to this purpose I have appeared to you, to appoint you as a servant and a witness both of the things in which you have seen Me and of those in which I will yet appear to you,

**26:17** delivering you from the people and from the Gentiles, to whom I am sending you,

**26:18** to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the dominion of Satan to God, that they may receive forgiveness of sins and an inheritance among those who have been sanctified by faith in Me.'

**26:19** Therefore, King Agrippa, I was not disobedient to the heavenly vision,

**26:20** but declared first to those in Damascus and in Jerusalem, and throughout all the region of Judea, and also to the Gentiles, that they should repent and turn to God, performing deeds worthy of repentance.

**26:21** On account of these things the Jews seized me in the temple and attempted to slay me.

**26:22** Having therefore obtained help from God, I stand to this day bearing witness to both small and great, saying nothing beyond what both the prophets and Moses spoke as things that would come to pass:

**26:23** that the Christ must suffer, and that, being the first from the resurrection of the dead, He would proclaim light both to the people and to the Gentiles."

**26:24** Now as he was speaking these things in his defense, Festus cried out with a loud voice, "You are mad, Paul! Your great learning is turning you to madness!"

**26:25** But Paul said, "I am not mad, most excellent Festus, but I utter words of truth and soberness.

**26:26** For the king knows concerning these matters, to whom I also speak with boldness; for I am convinced that none of these things has escaped his notice, since this has not been done in a corner.

**26:27** King Agrippa, do you believe the prophets? I know that you believe."

**26:28** Then Agrippa said to Paul, "In so short a time would you persuade and make me a Christian?"

**26:29** And Paul said, "I would pray to God that, whether in a short time or a long, not only you, but also all who hear me today, might become such as I also am—except for these chains."

**26:30** Then the king arose, and the governor, and Bernice, and those who sat with them;

**26:31** and when they had withdrawn, they spoke with one another, saying, "This man is doing nothing worthy of death or of chains."

**26:32** And Agrippa said to Festus, "This man might have been set at liberty if he had not appealed to Caesar."

## Translator Notes

- **26:16** — The critical Greek text includes the pronoun 'Me' (literally, 'the things in which you have seen Me'), emphasizing Paul's direct encounter with the risen Christ.
- **26:23** — Greek ei ('whether') introduces declarative points of prophetic testimony, and pathetos signifies that the Messiah was appointed to suffer.
- **26:28** — Agrippa's ironic reply is famously compact: en oligo may mean 'in a short time' or 'with little effort,' and the critical text reads poiesai ('to make' or 'to act as') rather than the later variant genesthai ('to become').
