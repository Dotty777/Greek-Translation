## Text

**28:1** Now when we had been brought safely ashore, we then learned that the island was called Malta.

**28:2** And the native people showed us no ordinary kindness, for they kindled a fire and welcomed us all because of the rain that had set in and because of the cold.

**28:3** But when Paul had gathered a bundle of sticks and laid them upon the fire, a viper came out because of the heat and fastened onto his hand.

**28:4** So when the native people saw the creature hanging from his hand, they said to one another, "Without doubt this man is a murderer, whom, though he was delivered from the sea, Justice has not permitted to live."

**28:5** He, however, shook off the creature into the fire and suffered no harm.

**28:6** Yet they were expecting that he would swell up or suddenly fall down dead. But after they had waited a long time and saw nothing amiss happening to him, they changed their minds and began saying that he was a god.

**28:7** Now in the region around that place were estates belonging to the leading man of the island, named Publius, who welcomed us and hospitably lodged us for three days.

**28:8** And it came to pass that the father of Publius lay ill, afflicted with fevers and dysentery. Paul went in to him, and having prayed, laid his hands on him and healed him.

**28:9** When this had taken place, the rest of those on the island who had infirmities also came and were healed.

**28:10** They also honored us with many honors, and when we set sail, they put on board whatever things were needed.

**28:11** After three months we set sail in an Alexandrian ship that had wintered at the island, bearing the figurehead of the Twin Brothers.

**28:12** And putting in at Syracuse, we stayed there three days.

**28:13** From there we weighed anchor and arrived at Rhegium. And after one day a south wind sprang up, and on the second day we came to Puteoli,

**28:14** where we found brethren and were invited to remain with them seven days. And so we came to Rome.

**28:15** And from there the brethren, having heard of us, came out to meet us as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns. When Paul saw them, he gave thanks to God and took courage.

**28:16** Now when we entered Rome, Paul was permitted to dwell by himself with the soldier who guarded him.

**28:17** And it came to pass after three days that he called together the chief men of the Jews. When they had assembled, he said to them, "Men and brethren, though I had done nothing contrary to the people or the customs of our fathers, yet I was delivered as a prisoner from Jerusalem into the hands of the Romans,

**28:18** who, when they had examined me, wished to release me, because there was no ground for death in me.

**28:19** But when the Jews spoke against it, I was compelled to appeal to Caesar—not that I had anything of which to accuse my nation.

**28:20** For this reason therefore I have asked to see you and to speak with you, because for the sake of the hope of Israel I am bound with this chain."

**28:21** Then they said to him, "We have neither received letters from Judea concerning you, nor has any of the brethren who came reported or spoken anything evil about you.

**28:22** Yet we desire to hear from you what your views are; for concerning this sect, it is known to us that it is spoken against everywhere."

**28:23** So when they had appointed him a day, they came to him at his lodging in greater numbers, to whom he expounded, solemnly testifying of the kingdom of God and seeking to persuade them concerning Jesus from both the Law of Moses and the Prophets, from morning until evening.

**28:24** And some were persuaded by the things that were spoken, and others disbelieved.

**28:25** So, being in disagreement among themselves, they began to depart after Paul had spoken one statement: "The Holy Spirit spoke rightly through Isaiah the prophet to your fathers,

**28:26** saying, 'Go to this people and say: Hearing you will hear and shall by no means understand, and seeing you will see and shall by no means perceive;

**28:27** for the heart of this people has grown dull, and with their ears they have scarcely heard, and they have closed their eyes, lest they should see with their eyes and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart and turn, and I should heal them.'

**28:28** Therefore let it be known to you that this salvation of God has been sent to the Gentiles, and they will indeed listen."

**28:30** Now he remained two full years in his own rented dwelling, and welcomed all who came to him,

**28:31** proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching the things concerning the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness, without hindrance.

## Translator Notes

- **28:11** — Greek Dioskouroi ("Twin Brothers") refers to Castor and Pollux, twin deities of Greco-Roman mythology honored as protectors of seafarers.
- **28:13** — The critical Greek text reads perielontes ("casting off" or "weighing anchor"), whereas some later manuscripts read perielthontes ("making a circuit" or "tacking around").
- **28:16** — Later manuscripts add that the centurion delivered the prisoners to the captain of the guard, a clause absent from the earliest Greek witnesses followed here.
- **28:28** — Later manuscripts include a twenty-ninth verse reporting that the Jews departed with much dispute among themselves, which is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
- **28:29 *(additional)*** — Text-critical double brackets ⟦ ⟧ are preserved verbatim from the Saints Greek Critical Text; this verse is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
