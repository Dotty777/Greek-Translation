## Text

**17:1** Now after traveling through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where there was a synagogue of the Jews.

**17:2** And according to his custom, Paul went in among them, and for three Sabbaths he reasoned with them from the Scriptures,

**17:3** opening and setting forth that it was necessary for the Christ to suffer and to rise from the dead, and declaring, "This is the Christ—Jesus, whom I proclaim to you."

**17:4** And some of them were persuaded and joined Paul and Silas, along with a great multitude of the devout Greeks and not a few of the prominent women.

**17:5** But the Jews, becoming filled with jealousy, took along some wicked men from the marketplace, and forming a mob, they threw the city into an uproar; and attacking the house of Jason, they sought to bring them out to the public assembly.

**17:6** Yet when they did not find them, they dragged Jason and certain brethren before the city magistrates, crying out, "These men who have turned the world upside down have arrived here as well,

**17:7** whom Jason has received into his house; and all of these act contrary to the decrees of Caesar, proclaiming that there is another king—Jesus."

**17:8** And they troubled the multitude and the city magistrates when they heard these things.

**17:9** And after taking security from Jason and the rest, they released them.

**17:10** Then the brethren immediately sent Paul and Silas away by night to Berea; and upon arriving there, they went into the synagogue of the Jews.

**17:11** Now these were more noble-minded than those in Thessalonica, for they received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures day by day to see whether these things were so.

**17:12** Therefore many of them believed, as well as not a few of the prominent Greek women and men.

**17:13** But when the Jews from Thessalonica learned that the word of God had been proclaimed by Paul in Berea also, they came there as well, agitating and troubling the multitudes.

**17:14** Then the brethren immediately sent Paul off to go as far as the sea, while both Silas and Timothy remained there.

**17:15** And those who escorted Paul brought him as far as Athens; and having received a command for Silas and Timothy to come to him with all speed, they departed.

**17:16** Now while Paul was waiting for them in Athens, his spirit was greatly provoked within him as he beheld that the city was full of idols.

**17:17** Therefore he reasoned in the synagogue with the Jews and with the devout worshipers, and in the marketplace every day with those who happened to be there.

**17:18** And certain of the Epicurean and Stoic philosophers also engaged him in discussion; and some said, "What might this babbler wish to say?" Others said, "He seems to be a proclaimer of foreign deities," because he was proclaiming the gospel of Jesus and the resurrection.

**17:19** And taking hold of him, they brought him to the Areopagus, saying, "May we know what this new teaching is that is spoken by you?

**17:20** For you are bringing certain strange things to our ears; therefore we wish to know what these things mean."

**17:21** Now all the Athenians and the foreigners who resided there spent their leisure in nothing else but either to speak or to hear something new.

**17:22** Then Paul stood in the midst of the Areopagus and said, "Men of Athens, I perceive that in all respects you are exceedingly religious;

**17:23** for as I passed along and carefully observed the objects of your worship, I even found an altar upon which was inscribed, 'To an Unknown God.' Therefore what you worship in ignorance, this I proclaim to you.

**17:24** The God who made the world and everything that is in it, since He is Lord of heaven and earth, does not dwell in temples made with hands,

**17:25** nor is He served by human hands, as though He needed anything, since He Himself gives to all life, breath, and all things.

**17:26** And from one man He made every nation of mankind to dwell upon all the face of the earth, having determined their appointed seasons and the boundaries of their habitation,

**17:27** that they should seek God, in the hope that they might reach out for Him and find Him, though indeed He is not far from each one of us.

**17:28** For in Him we live and move and exist, as even some of your own poets have spoken, 'For we also are His offspring.'

**17:29** Therefore, being the offspring of God, we ought not to suppose that the Divine Being is like gold or silver or stone, an image formed by the craft and imagination of man.

**17:30** Having overlooked these times of ignorance, God now declares to men that all people everywhere should repent,

**17:31** because He has fixed a day on which He is going to judge the world in righteousness by a Man whom He has appointed, having furnished assurance to all by raising Him from the dead."

**17:32** Now when they heard of the resurrection of the dead, some mocked, while others said, "We will hear you again concerning this matter."

**17:33** So Paul went out from their midst.

**17:34** Yet certain men joined him and believed, among whom were Dionysius the Areopagite, a woman named Damaris, and others with them.

## Translator Notes

- **17:6** — "City magistrates" translates Greek politarchas ("politarchs"), the specific local title attested in inscriptions for the civic rulers of Thessalonica.
- **17:18** — "Babbler" renders Greek spermologos (literally "seed-picker"), a disparaging Athenian slang term for an intellectual scavenger who collects and repeats scraps of borrowed ideas.
- **17:23** — The critical Greek text uses neuter pronouns ("what... this") rather than the masculine pronouns ("whom... Him") found in later manuscripts.
- **17:26** — The critical Greek text reads simply ex henos ("from one [man]"), without the word haimatos ("blood") that appears in later manuscripts.
