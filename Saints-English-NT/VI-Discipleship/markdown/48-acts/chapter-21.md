## Text

**21:1** Now when it came to pass that we had torn ourselves away from them and put out to sea, we ran a straight course and came to Cos, and on the following day to Rhodes, and from there to Patara.

**21:2** And finding a ship crossing over to Phoenicia, we went aboard and set sail.

**21:3** When we had sighted Cyprus and left it on the left, we sailed on to Syria and landed at Tyre, for there the ship was to unload its cargo.

**21:4** Having sought out the disciples, we remained there seven days; and through the Spirit they kept telling Paul not to set foot in Jerusalem.

**21:5** When we had completed those days, we departed and went on our way, while all of them, with wives and children, escorted us until we were outside the city; and kneeling down on the shore, we prayed,

**21:6** and said farewell to one another. Then we went aboard the ship, and they returned to their own homes.

**21:7** And when we had finished the voyage from Tyre, we arrived at Ptolemais; and having greeted the brethren, we stayed with them one day.

**21:8** On the next day we departed and came to Caesarea, and entering the house of Philip the evangelist, who was one of the seven, we stayed with him.

**21:9** Now this man had four virgin daughters who prophesied.

**21:10** And as we stayed there for many days, a certain prophet named Agabus came down from Judea.

**21:11** When he came to us, he took Paul's belt, bound his own feet and hands, and said, "Thus says the Holy Spirit: 'In this manner the Jews in Jerusalem shall bind the man to whom this belt belongs, and deliver him into the hands of the Gentiles.'"

**21:12** Now when we heard these things, both we and those of that place pleaded with him not to go up to Jerusalem.

**21:13** Then Paul answered, "What are you doing, weeping and breaking my heart? For I am ready not only to be bound, but also to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus."

**21:14** And since he would not be persuaded, we fell silent, saying, "Let the will of the Lord be done."

**21:15** After these days we packed our baggage and went up to Jerusalem.

**21:16** Some of the disciples from Caesarea also went with us, bringing us to one Mnason of Cyprus, an early disciple, with whom we were to lodge.

**21:17** And when we arrived in Jerusalem, the brethren received us gladly.

**21:18** On the following day Paul went in with us to James, and all the elders were present.

**21:19** After greeting them, he related one by one the things which God had done among the Gentiles through his ministry.

**21:20** And when they heard it, they glorified God. And they said to him, "You see, brother, how many tens of thousands there are among the Jews who have believed, and they are all zealous for the law;

**21:21** but they have been informed about you that you teach all the Jews who are among the Gentiles to forsake Moses, telling them not to circumcise their children nor to walk according to the customs.

**21:22** What then is to be done? They will certainly hear that you have come.

**21:23** Therefore do this that we tell you: we have four men who have taken a vow upon themselves.

**21:24** Take them and be purified with them, and pay their expenses so that they may shave their heads, and all will know that there is nothing in the things of which they have been informed concerning you, but that you yourself also walk in an orderly manner, keeping the law.

**21:25** But concerning the Gentiles who have believed, we have written, having decided that they should keep themselves from what is offered to idols, from blood, from what is strangled, and from sexual immorality."

**21:26** Then Paul took the men, and on the next day, having been purified with them, he entered the temple, announcing the completion of the days of purification, until the offering was presented for each one of them.

**21:27** Now when the seven days were nearly completed, the Jews from Asia, seeing him in the temple, stirred up the whole crowd and laid hands on him,

**21:28** crying out, "Men of Israel, help! This is the man who teaches everyone everywhere against the people, the law, and this place; and furthermore he has even brought Greeks into the temple and has defiled this holy place."

**21:29** For they had previously seen Trophimus the Ephesian with him in the city, and they supposed that Paul had brought him into the temple.

**21:30** And the whole city was stirred up, and the people ran together; and seizing Paul, they dragged him out of the temple, and immediately the doors were shut.

**21:31** Now as they were seeking to kill him, a report went up to the commander of the cohort that all Jerusalem was in an uproar.

**21:32** At once he took soldiers and centurions and ran down to them; and when they saw the commander and the soldiers, they stopped beating Paul.

**21:33** Then the commander came near and took hold of him, and commanded him to be bound with two chains; and he inquired who he was and what he had done.

**21:34** And some in the crowd were shouting one thing and some another; so when he could not ascertain the truth because of the tumult, he commanded him to be brought into the barracks.

**21:35** When he reached the stairs, he had to be carried by the soldiers because of the violence of the crowd;

**21:36** for the multitude of the people kept following, crying out, "Away with him!"

**21:37** As Paul was about to be led into the barracks, he said to the commander, "May I say something to you?" And he replied, "Do you know Greek?

## Translator Notes

- **21:16** — The Greek dative construction (attracted to the relative pronoun) is usually understood as "bringing us to one Mnason... with whom we were to lodge," though some render it "bringing with them one Mnason."
- **21:22** — The critical Greek text reads simply "What then is to be done? They will certainly hear that you have come," omitting the later Byzantine addition "the multitude must certainly assemble."
- **21:38** — "Assassins" translates Greek sikarioi (a loanword from Latin sicarii, "dagger-men"), referring to a militant faction in first-century Judea who carried concealed daggers.
- **21:40** — "The Hebrew language" (Hebraidi dialekto, here and in 22:2) is widely understood to refer to Aramaic, the Semitic vernacular spoken in Judea, though some take it as Hebrew proper.
