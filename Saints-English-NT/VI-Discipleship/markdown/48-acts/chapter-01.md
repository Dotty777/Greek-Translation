## Text

**1:1** The first account I prepared, O Theophilus, concerning all things that Jesus began both to do and to teach,

**1:2** until the day in which, having given commandments through the Holy Spirit to the apostles whom He had chosen, He was taken up;

**1:3** to whom He also presented Himself alive after His suffering by many unmistakable proofs, appearing to them throughout forty days and speaking of the things concerning the kingdom of God.

**1:4** And while gathered together with them, He charged them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to await the promise of the Father, "which you have heard from Me;

**1:5** for John indeed baptized with water, but you shall be baptized in the Holy Spirit not many days from now."

**1:6** So when they had come together, they were asking Him, saying, "Lord, is it at this time that You are restoring the kingdom to Israel?"

**1:7** And He said to them, "It is not yours to know times or seasons which the Father has set within His own authority,

**1:8** but you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, and you shall be My witnesses both in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the uttermost part of the earth."

**1:9** And having spoken these things, while they were watching, He was lifted up, and a cloud received Him from before their eyes.

**1:10** And as they were gazing intently into heaven while He was going, behold, two men stood beside them in white garments,

**1:11** who also said, "Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? This very Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, shall come in the same manner in which you beheld Him going into heaven."

**1:12** Then they returned to Jerusalem from the mount called Olivet, which lies near Jerusalem, a Sabbath day's journey away.

**1:13** And when they had entered, they went up into the upper room where they were staying: Peter and John and James and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James the son of Alphaeus and Simon the Zealot and Judas the son of James.

**1:14** All these were continuing steadfastly with one accord in prayer, together with the women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with His brothers.

**1:15** And in those days Peter stood up in the midst of the brethren and said (now the company of persons gathered together was about one hundred and twenty):

**1:16** "Men and brethren, it was necessary that the Scripture be fulfilled, which the Holy Spirit spoke beforehand through the mouth of David concerning Judas, who became a guide to those who seized Jesus,

**1:17** for he was numbered among us and obtained the allotted share of this ministry."

**1:18** (Now this man acquired a field from the reward of iniquity, and falling headlong, he burst open in the middle, and all his inward parts poured out.

**1:19** And it became known to all those dwelling in Jerusalem, so that in their own language that field was called Akeldama, that is, Field of Blood.)

**1:20** "For it is written in the Book of Psalms, 'Let his habitation become desolate, and let no one dwell in it,' and, 'Let another take his office.'

**1:21** Therefore, from among the men who accompanied us during all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us,

**1:22** beginning from the baptism of John until the day in which He was taken up from us, one of these must become with us a witness of His resurrection."

**1:23** And they set forward two: Joseph called Barsabbas, who was also named Justus, and Matthias.

**1:24** And praying, they said, "You, O Lord, who know the hearts of all, make known which one of these two You have chosen

**1:25** to receive the place of this ministry and apostleship, from which Judas turned aside to go to his own place."

**1:26** And they cast lots for them, and the lot fell upon Matthias, and he was numbered together with the eleven apostles.

## Translator Notes

- **1:4** — The Greek participle synalizomenos means either "gathered together with them" or "eating together with them" (literally sharing salt at a meal).
- **1:12** — A Sabbath day's journey was the traditional maximum distance permitted for travel on the Sabbath, approximately two thousand cubits (roughly half to three-quarters of a mile).
- **1:18** — The Greek phrase prenes genomenos is traditionally rendered "falling headlong" (face downward), though some interpreters take it in a medical sense meaning "swelling up."
- **1:25** — The Greek critical text reads topon ("place"), creating a deliberate wordplay with Judas departing to "his own place," whereas later manuscripts read kleron ("lot" or "share").
