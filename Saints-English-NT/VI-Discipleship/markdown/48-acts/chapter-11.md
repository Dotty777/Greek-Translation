## Text

**11:1** Now the apostles and the brethren who were throughout Judea heard that the Gentiles also had received the word of God.

**11:2** And when Peter went up to Jerusalem, those of the circumcision disputed with him,

**11:3** saying, "You went in to men who were uncircumcised and ate with them."

**11:4** Then Peter began to set it forth to them in order, saying,

**11:5** "I was in the city of Joppa praying, and in a trance I saw a vision: a certain vessel coming down like a great sheet, lowered from heaven by four corners, and it came even to me.

**11:6** Gazing intently into it, I observed and saw the four-footed creatures of the earth, and the wild beasts, and the creeping things, and the birds of heaven.

**11:7** I also heard a voice saying to me, 'Arise, Peter; slay and eat.'

**11:8** But I replied, 'By no means, Lord! For nothing common or unclean has ever entered my mouth.'

**11:9** Yet a voice answered a second time from heaven, 'What God has made clean, you must not call common.'

**11:10** And this occurred three times, and all things were drawn up again into heaven.

**11:11** And behold, at that very moment three men stood at the house where we were, having been sent to me from Caesarea.

**11:12** And the Spirit told me to go with them, making no distinction. Moreover, these six brethren also accompanied me, and we entered the man's house.

**11:13** And he related to us how he had seen the angel standing in his house and saying, 'Send to Joppa and summon Simon who is called Peter,

**11:14** who will speak words to you by which you shall be saved—you and all your household.'

**11:15** And as I began to speak, the Holy Spirit fell upon them, just as He did upon us at the beginning.

**11:16** Then I recalled the word of the Lord, how He said, 'John indeed baptized with water, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Spirit.'

**11:17** Therefore, if God granted them the same gift as He did to us when we believed on the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I that I should be able to hinder God?"

**11:18** When they heard these things, they fell silent and glorified God, saying, "Then to the Gentiles also God has granted repentance leading to life."

**11:19** Now those who had been scattered because of the tribulation that arose over Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus, and Antioch, speaking the word to no one except Jews alone.

**11:20** Yet some of them were men from Cyprus and Cyrene, who, upon coming to Antioch, spoke to the Hellenists as well, proclaiming the good news of the Lord Jesus.

**11:21** And the hand of the Lord was with them, and a large number who believed turned to the Lord.

**11:22** Now the report concerning them came to the ears of the church that was in Jerusalem, and they sent forth Barnabas as far as Antioch.

**11:23** When he arrived and beheld the grace of God, he rejoiced and exhorted them all with purpose of heart to abide with the Lord,

**11:24** for he was a good man and full of the Holy Spirit and faith. And a considerable multitude was added to the Lord.

**11:25** Then he departed to Tarsus to search for Saul,

**11:26** and when he had found him, he brought him to Antioch. So it came to pass that for a whole year they assembled with the church and taught a considerable multitude; and in Antioch the disciples were first called Christians.

**11:27** Now during those days prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch.

**11:28** And one of them named Agabus stood up and signified through the Spirit that a great famine was about to come upon the whole inhabited world, which indeed occurred under Claudius.

**11:29** Therefore the disciples, each according to his means, resolved to send relief to the brethren who dwelt in Judea;

**11:30** and this they also did, sending it to the elders by the hand of Barnabas and Saul.

## Translator Notes

- **11:12** — The Greek active participle translated "making no distinction" (mēden diakrinanta) emphasizes making no distinction between Jew and Gentile, though it can also carry the sense "without hesitating."
- **11:20** — The Greek text reads Hellēnistas ("Hellenists," Greek-speaking people), whereas a major textual variant reads Hellēnas ("Greeks," i.e., Gentiles).
- **11:28** — Claudius reigned as Roman emperor from AD 41 to 54, during which period historical sources record severe famines, including one affecting Judea around AD 46–48.
