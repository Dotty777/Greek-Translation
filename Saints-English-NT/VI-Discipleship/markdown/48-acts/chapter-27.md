## Text

**27:1** Now when it was decided that we should sail away to Italy, they delivered both Paul and certain other prisoners to a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan Cohort.

**27:2** And boarding a ship of Adramyttium that was about to sail to the places along the coast of Asia, we put out to sea, Aristarchus, a Macedonian of Thessalonica, being with us.

**27:3** And on the next day we put in at Sidon; and Julius, treating Paul kindly, permitted him to go to his friends and receive care.

**27:4** And putting out to sea from there, we sailed under the shelter of Cyprus, because the winds were contrary.

**27:5** And having sailed across the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came down to Myra in Lycia.

**27:6** And there the centurion found an Alexandrian ship sailing to Italy, and put us aboard it.

**27:7** Now sailing slowly for many days, and arriving with difficulty off Cnidus, since the wind did not permit us to proceed further, we sailed under the shelter of Crete off Salmone.

**27:8** And coasting along it with difficulty, we came to a certain place called Fair Havens, near which was the city of Lasea.

**27:9** Now when much time had elapsed, and the voyage was now hazardous because the Fast had already gone by, Paul admonished them,

**27:10** saying to them, "Men, I perceive that the voyage will be with injury and great loss, not only of the cargo and of the ship, but also of our lives."

**27:11** Yet the centurion was persuaded more by the helmsman and the shipowner than by the things spoken by Paul.

**27:12** And since the harbor was unsuitable to winter in, the majority gave counsel to put out to sea from there, if by any means they might reach Phoenix and winter there, a harbor of Crete facing toward the southwest and toward the northwest.

**27:13** Now when a gentle south wind blew, supposing that they had attained their purpose, they weighed anchor and coasted close along Crete.

**27:14** But not long afterward, a tempestuous wind called Euraquilo struck down from the island.

**27:15** And when the ship was caught and could not face into the wind, we gave way and were driven along.

**27:16** And running under the shelter of a small island called Clauda, we were scarcely able to gain control of the ship's boat.

**27:17** When they had hoisted it aboard, they used supports to undergird the ship; and fearing lest they should run aground on the Syrtis, they lowered the gear, and thus were driven along.

**27:18** And because we were violently storm-tossed, on the next day they began casting the cargo overboard,

**27:19** and on the third day with their own hands they cast out the tackle of the ship.

**27:20** Now when neither sun nor stars appeared for many days, and no small tempest pressed upon us, all hope that we would be saved was at last taken away.

**27:21** And after long abstinence from food, Paul stood in the midst of them and said, "Men, you ought indeed to have heeded me and not to have put out to sea from Crete, and thus to have avoided this injury and loss.

**27:22** And now I urge you to take heart, for there shall be no loss of life among you, but only of the ship.

**27:23** For there stood by me this night an angel of the God whose I am and whom I also serve,

**27:24** saying, 'Do not be afraid, Paul; you must stand before Caesar; and behold, God has graciously granted to you all those who sail with you.'

**27:25** Therefore take heart, men, for I believe God that it shall be just as it has been spoken to me.

**27:26** However, we must run aground on a certain island."

**27:27** Now when the fourteenth night had come, as we were driven to and fro in the Adriatic Sea, about midnight the sailors surmised that some land was drawing near to them.

**27:28** And taking soundings, they found twenty fathoms; and when they had gone a little farther and taken soundings again, they found fifteen fathoms.

**27:29** And fearing lest somewhere we should run aground on rocky places, they cast four anchors from the stern and prayed for day to come.

**27:30** Now as the sailors were seeking to flee from the ship, and had lowered the ship's boat into the sea under pretense of intending to lay out anchors from the bow,

**27:31** Paul said to the centurion and to the soldiers, "Unless these men remain in the ship, you cannot be saved."

**27:32** Then the soldiers cut away the ropes of the ship's boat and let it fall away.

**27:33** And as day was about to dawn, Paul urged them all to partake of food, saying, "Today is the fourteenth day you have continued in suspense without food, having taken nothing.

**27:34** Therefore I urge you to partake of food, for this is for your survival; for not a hair from the head of any one of you shall perish."

**27:35** And when he had spoken these things and taken bread, he gave thanks to God in the presence of all, and having broken it, he began to eat.

**27:36** Then they all took heart, and they also partook of food.

**27:37** Now we were in all two hundred and seventy-six souls in the ship.

**27:38** And when they had been satisfied with food, they lightened the ship by casting the wheat out into the sea.

**27:39** Now when day came, they did not recognize the land; yet they observed a certain bay having a beach, onto which they planned, if they were able, to drive the ship.

**27:40** And casting off the anchors, they left them in the sea, at the same time loosening the ropes of the rudders; and hoisting the foresail to the blowing wind, they made for the beach.

**27:41** But striking a place where two seas met, they ran the vessel aground; and the bow stuck fast and remained immovable, while the stern was being broken up by the violence.

**27:42** And the soldiers' plan was to kill the prisoners, lest any of them should swim away and escape.

**27:43** But the centurion, wishing to save Paul, restrained them from their purpose, and commanded those who were able to swim to cast themselves overboard first and get to land,

**27:44** and the rest, some on planks and others on pieces from the ship. And so it came to pass that all were brought safely to land.

## Translator Notes

- **27:9** — The Fast refers to the Day of Atonement in late September or early October, after which autumn and winter sailing on the Mediterranean became perilous.
- **27:12** — Literally looking toward the southwest wind and toward the northwest wind, a nautical expression debated as meaning either facing southwest and northwest or opening toward the northeast and southeast.
- **27:14** — Euraquilo (Greek Eurakylon) is a Greek-Latin compound denoting a violent northeasterly storm wind; later Byzantine manuscripts read Euroklydon.
- **27:37** — Some early witnesses, notably Codex Vaticanus, read about seventy-six, while the majority of manuscripts, including Sinaiticus and Alexandrinus, read two hundred and seventy-six.
- **27:41** — The earliest Greek witnesses read simply by the violence; later manuscripts add of the waves.
