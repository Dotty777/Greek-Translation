## Text

**25:1** Now when Festus had entered upon his province, after three days he went up from Caesarea to Jerusalem.

**25:2** And the chief priests and the foremost men of the Jews brought formal charges before him against Paul, and they urged him,

**25:3** asking a favor against him, that he would send for him to Jerusalem, while preparing an ambush to slay him along the way.

**25:4** Festus, however, answered that Paul was being kept in custody at Caesarea, and that he himself was about to depart shortly.

**25:5** "Therefore," he said, "let those among you who hold authority go down with me, and if there is anything amiss in this man, let them accuse him."

**25:6** And having stayed among them not more than eight or ten days, he went down to Caesarea; and on the next day, sitting upon the judgment seat, he commanded Paul to be brought in.

**25:7** When he had arrived, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood round about him, bringing many and grievous charges which they were unable to prove,

**25:8** while Paul spoke in his own defense: "Neither against the law of the Jews, nor against the temple, nor against Caesar have I sinned in anything."

**25:9** But Festus, wishing to gain favor with the Jews, answered Paul and said, "Are you willing to go up to Jerusalem and there be judged before me concerning these matters?"

**25:10** Then Paul said, "I stand before the judgment seat of Caesar, where I ought to be judged. To the Jews I have done no wrong, as you also know very well.

**25:11** If indeed I am an offender and have committed anything worthy of death, I do not refuse to die; yet if there is nothing in these charges which they bring against me, no one can hand me over to them as a favor. I appeal to Caesar."

**25:12** Then Festus, having conferred with the council, answered, "To Caesar you have appealed; to Caesar you shall go."

**25:13** Now when some days had passed, King Agrippa and Bernice arrived at Caesarea to greet Festus.

**25:14** And as they lingered there many days, Festus laid Paul's case before the king, saying, "There is a certain man left behind as a prisoner by Felix,

**25:15** concerning whom, when I was in Jerusalem, the chief priests and the elders of the Jews brought formal charges, asking for a sentence of condemnation against him.

**25:16** To them I answered that it is not the custom of the Romans to surrender any man as a favor before the accused faces his accusers and receives opportunity to make his defense concerning the charge.

**25:17** Therefore, when they had assembled here, making no delay, on the following day I sat upon the judgment seat and commanded the man to be brought in.

**25:18** When the accusers stood up concerning him, they brought no charge of such evil deeds as I had supposed,

**25:19** but they had certain points of dispute with him concerning their own religion, and concerning a certain Jesus, who was dead, whom Paul asserted to be alive.

**25:20** And because I was at a loss how to inquire into these matters, I asked whether he was willing to go to Jerusalem and there be judged concerning them.

**25:21** But when Paul appealed to be kept in custody for the decision of Augustus, I commanded him to be kept until I might send him up to Caesar."

**25:22** Then Agrippa said to Festus, "I myself would also like to hear the man." "Tomorrow," he said, "you shall hear him."

**25:23** So on the next day, when Agrippa and Bernice had come with great pomp and had entered the audience hall together with the commanders and the prominent men of the city, at Festus's command Paul was brought in.

**25:24** And Festus said, "King Agrippa, and all you men who are present here with us, you behold this man concerning whom the whole multitude of the Jews petitioned me, both in Jerusalem and here, crying out that he ought not to live any longer.

**25:25** Yet I found that he had committed nothing worthy of death, and since he himself appealed to Augustus, I determined to send him.

**25:26** Concerning him I have nothing definite to write to my lord. Therefore I have brought him forth before you, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after the examination has taken place I may have something to write.

**25:27** For it seems unreasonable to me, when sending a prisoner, not also to indicate the charges against him."

## Translator Notes

- **25:6** — The critical Greek text reads 'not more than eight or ten days,' whereas the later Majority Text reads 'more than ten days.'
- **25:19** — The Greek term deisidaimonia can mean either 'religion' (a polite, neutral usage suited to addressing King Agrippa) or 'superstition.'
- **25:21** — Greek Sebastos is the imperial title corresponding to Latin Augustus ('the Venerable One'), referring here to Emperor Nero.
- **25:26** — Here 'my lord' (to kyrio) is an official title referring to the Roman emperor rather than to God.
