## Text

**20:1** Now after the tumult had ceased, Paul summoned the disciples, and having exhorted them, he took leave of them and departed to go into Macedonia.

**20:2** And having passed through those regions and encouraged them with many words, he came into Greece,

**20:3** and spent three months there. And when a plot was laid against him by the Jews as he was about to set sail for Syria, he purposed to return by way of Macedonia.

**20:4** And there accompanied him Sopater the son of Pyrrhus, of Berea; and of the Thessalonians, Aristarchus and Secundus; and Gaius of Derbe, and Timothy; and of Asia, Tychicus and Trophimus.

**20:5** Now these, having arrived, were waiting for us at Troas.

**20:6** But we sailed away from Philippi after the Days of Unleavened Bread, and within five days we came to them at Troas, where we remained seven days.

**20:7** Now on the first day of the week, when we were gathered together to break bread, Paul discoursed with them, intending to depart the next day, and he prolonged his speech until midnight.

**20:8** And there were many lamps in the upper room where we were assembled.

**20:9** And a certain young man named Eutychus was sitting in the window, sinking into a deep sleep as Paul continued speaking at great length; and being overcome by sleep, he fell down from the third story and was lifted up dead.

**20:10** But Paul went down, fell upon him, and embracing him, said, "Do not be alarmed, for his life is in him."

**20:11** Then when he had gone up again, and had broken the bread and eaten, and had conversed a long while, even until daybreak, he so departed.

**20:12** And they brought the lad alive, and they were comforted in no small measure.

**20:13** Now we went ahead to the ship and set sail for Assos, intending to take Paul aboard from there; for so he had arranged, intending himself to travel on foot.

**20:14** And when he met us at Assos, we took him aboard and came to Mitylene.

**20:15** And sailing away from there, on the following day we arrived opposite Chios; and the next day we put in at Samos; and on the day after that we came to Miletus.

**20:16** For Paul had decided to sail past Ephesus, so that he might not have to spend time in Asia; for he was hastening to be in Jerusalem, if it were possible for him, on the Day of Pentecost.

**20:17** Now from Miletus he sent to Ephesus and summoned the elders of the church.

**20:18** And when they had come to him, he said to them, "You yourselves know, from the first day that I set foot in Asia, in what manner I lived among you the whole time,

**20:19** serving the Lord with all humility, and with tears and trials that befell me through the plots of the Jews;

**20:20** how I withheld nothing that was profitable, but proclaimed it to you and taught you publicly and from house to house,

**20:21** solemnly testifying both to Jews and to Greeks of repentance toward God and faith in our Lord Jesus.

**20:22** And now, behold, bound in the spirit, I am going to Jerusalem, not knowing the things that shall meet me there,

**20:23** except that the Holy Spirit solemnly testifies to me in every city, saying that bonds and afflictions await me.

**20:24** Yet I do not count my life of any worth as precious to myself, if only I may complete my course and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify solemnly to the gospel of the grace of God.

**20:25** And now, behold, I know that all of you, among whom I have gone about proclaiming the kingdom, shall see my face no more.

**20:26** Therefore I testify to you on this very day that I am guiltless of the blood of all,

**20:27** for I did not hold back from proclaiming to you the whole counsel of God.

**20:28** Keep watch over yourselves and over all the flock, in which the Holy Spirit has appointed you overseers, to shepherd the church of God, which He acquired through His own blood.

**20:29** I know that after my departure fierce wolves shall come in among you, not sparing the flock;

**20:30** and from among your own selves men shall arise speaking perverse things, to draw away the disciples after themselves.

**20:31** Therefore be watchful, remembering that for three years, night and day, I did not cease to admonish each one with tears.

**20:32** And now I entrust you to God and to the word of His grace, which has power to build you up and to grant the inheritance among all who have been sanctified.

**20:33** I have coveted no one's silver or gold or apparel.

**20:34** You yourselves know that these hands have ministered to my own needs and to those who were with me.

**20:35** In all things I have shown you that by laboring in this manner we must support the weak, and remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how He Himself said, 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.'"

**20:36** And when he had spoken these things, he knelt down and prayed with them all.

**20:37** And there was great weeping among them all, and falling upon Paul's neck, they kissed him again and again,

**20:38** grieving most of all over the word he had spoken, that they would behold his face no more. And they accompanied him to the ship.

## Translator Notes

- **20:5** — The critical Greek text reads proselthontes ("having arrived" or "having come"), whereas many manuscripts read proelthontes ("having gone ahead").
- **20:22** — The Greek phrase dedemenos to pneumati ("bound in the spirit") may refer either to an inward compulsion in Paul's own spirit or to being constrained by the Holy Spirit (cf. v. 23).
- **20:28** — The Greek phrase dia tou haimatos tou idiou may be rendered either "through His own blood" or "through the blood of His Own [Son]." Some Greek manuscripts also read "the church of the Lord" instead of "the church of God."
- **20:35** — This saying of the Lord Jesus is not recorded verbatim in the four Gospels, preserving an early tradition of His teaching.
