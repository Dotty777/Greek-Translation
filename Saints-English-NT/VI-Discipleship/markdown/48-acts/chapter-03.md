## Text

**3:1** Now Peter and John were ascending to the temple at the hour of prayer, the ninth hour.

**3:2** And a certain man who had been lame from his mother's womb was being carried, whom they laid daily at the gate of the temple called Beautiful, to ask alms from those entering into the temple.

**3:3** Seeing Peter and John as they were about to enter the temple, he asked to receive alms.

**3:4** Then Peter, gazing intently upon him along with John, said, "Look at us."

**3:5** So he gave heed to them, expecting to receive something from them.

**3:6** Then Peter said, "Silver and gold I do not possess, yet what I have, this I give to you: In the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene, walk!"

**3:7** And seizing him by the right hand, he raised him up; and at once his feet and ankles were made firm.

**3:8** And leaping up, he stood and walked about, and he entered the temple with them, walking and leaping and praising God.

**3:9** And all the people beheld him walking about and praising God.

**3:10** And they recognized him, that this was the one who sat for alms at the Beautiful Gate of the temple; and they were filled with wonder and astonishment at what had befallen him.

**3:11** Now as he clung to Peter and John, all the people ran together to them in the portico called Solomon's, utterly astonished.

**3:12** When Peter saw this, he answered the people: "Men of Israel, why do you marvel at this, or why do you gaze intently at us, as though by our own power or godliness we had caused him to walk?

**3:13** The God of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, the God of our fathers, has glorified His Servant Jesus, whom you indeed handed over and denied before the face of Pilate, when he had decided to release Him.

**3:14** Yet you denied the Holy and Righteous One, and asked that a murderer be granted to you,

**3:15** and you slew the Author of life, whom God raised from the dead—of which we are witnesses.

**3:16** And upon the basis of faith in His name, His name has made firm this man whom you behold and know; and the faith that is through Him has given him this complete soundness in the presence of you all.

**3:17** And now, brethren, I know that you acted in ignorance, just as your rulers also did.

**3:18** But what God foretold through the mouth of all the prophets, that His Christ should suffer, He has thus fulfilled.

**3:19** Repent therefore and turn again, so that your sins may be blotted out,

**3:20** that seasons of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord, and that He may send the Christ appointed beforehand for you, Jesus,

**3:21** whom heaven indeed must receive until the times of the restoration of all things, about which God spoke through the mouth of His holy prophets from of old.

**3:22** Moses indeed said, 'The Lord our God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from among your brethren; to Him you shall listen in all things, whatever He may speak to you.

**3:23** And it shall come to pass that every soul that does not listen to that Prophet shall be utterly destroyed from among the people.'

**3:24** Yes, and all the prophets from Samuel and those who came after him, as many as spoke, have also proclaimed these days.

**3:25** You are the sons of the prophets and of the covenant that God established with your fathers, saying to Abraham, 'And in your seed all the families of the earth shall be blessed.'

**3:26** To you first, God, having raised up His Servant, sent Him to bless you by turning each one away from your iniquities."

## Translator Notes

- **3:6** — The Greek critical text reads simply "walk" without the longer Byzantine addition "rise up and walk."
- **3:13** — Greek pais means "Servant" (echoing the Septuagint Servant Songs of Isaiah) rather than merely "child," a messianic title recurring in 3:26, 4:27, and 4:30.
- **3:15** — Greek archegos carries the sense of "Author," "Originator," or "Prince," denoting the one who pioneers and imparts life.
- **3:22** — The Greek critical text reads "the Lord our God," whereas many manuscripts harmonize with Deuteronomy 18:15 to read "your God."
