## Text

**9:1** Now Saul, still breathing threats and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord, approached the high priest

**9:2** and requested letters from him to the synagogues in Damascus, that if he should find any who belonged to the Way, both men and women, he might bring them bound to Jerusalem.

**9:3** And as he journeyed, it came to pass that he drew near to Damascus, and suddenly a light from heaven flashed around him.

**9:4** And falling to the ground, he heard a voice saying to him, "Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?"

**9:5** And he said, "Who are You, Lord?" And He said, "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.

**9:6** But arise and enter the city, and it will be told you what you must do."

**9:7** Now the men who were traveling with him stood speechless, hearing indeed the voice, yet seeing no one.

**9:8** And Saul arose from the ground, yet though his eyes were opened, he saw nothing; so leading him by the hand, they brought him into Damascus.

**9:9** And for three days he remained without sight, and neither ate nor drank.

**9:10** Now there was a certain disciple in Damascus named Ananias, and the Lord spoke to him in a vision, "Ananias." And he said, "Behold, here I am, Lord."

**9:11** And the Lord said to him, "Arise and go to the street called Straight, and inquire at the house of Judas for one named Saul of Tarsus; for behold, he is praying,

**9:12** and in a vision he has seen a man named Ananias coming in and laying hands on him, so that he might regain his sight."

**9:13** Then Ananias answered, "Lord, I have heard from many concerning this man, how much evil he has done to Your saints in Jerusalem;

**9:14** and here he holds authority from the chief priests to bind all who call upon Your name."

**9:15** But the Lord said to him, "Go, for this man is a chosen vessel of Mine to bear My name before Gentiles and kings and the sons of Israel;

**9:16** for I will show him how many things he must suffer for the sake of My name."

**9:17** So Ananias departed and entered the house, and laying his hands upon him, he said, "Brother Saul, the Lord has sent me—Jesus, who appeared to you on the road along which you came—that you may regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit."

**9:18** And immediately something like scales fell from his eyes, and he regained his sight; and arising, he was baptized,

**9:19** and taking food, he was strengthened. Now he spent several days with the disciples in Damascus,

**9:20** and immediately in the synagogues he proclaimed Jesus, that He is the Son of God.

**9:21** And all who heard him were astonished and said, "Is this not the one who ravaged in Jerusalem those who call upon this name, and had come here for this very purpose, that he might bring them bound before the chief priests?"

**9:22** But Saul increased all the more in strength, and confounded the Jews who dwelt in Damascus, proving that this One is the Christ.

**9:23** Now when many days had passed, the Jews took counsel together to slay him;

**9:24** but their plot became known to Saul. And they kept watch over the gates both day and night, that they might slay him.

**9:25** Then his disciples took him by night and let him down through the wall, lowering him in a basket.

**9:26** And when he had come to Jerusalem, he sought to join himself to the disciples; yet they were all afraid of him, not believing that he was a disciple.

**9:27** But Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles, and recounted to them how on the road he had seen the Lord, and that He had spoken to him, and how in Damascus he had spoken boldly in the name of Jesus.

**9:28** So he remained with them, going in and going out in Jerusalem, speaking boldly in the name of the Lord,

**9:29** and he spoke and disputed with the Hellenists; but they attempted to slay him.

**9:30** When the brethren learned of it, they brought him down to Caesarea and sent him away to Tarsus.

**9:31** So the church throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and was built up; and walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit, it was multiplied.

**9:32** Now it came to pass, as Peter journeyed through all those parts, that he came down also to the saints who dwelt in Lydda.

**9:33** There he found a certain man named Aeneas, who had lain on a pallet for eight years and was paralyzed.

**9:34** And Peter said to him, "Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you. Arise and make your own bed." And immediately he arose.

**9:35** And all who dwelt in Lydda and Sharon saw him, and they turned to the Lord.

**9:36** Now in Joppa there was a certain disciple named Tabitha, which, translated, means Dorcas. This woman was full of good works and deeds of charity that she did.

**9:37** And it came to pass in those days that she fell ill and died; and having washed her, they laid her in an upper room.

**9:38** Now since Lydda was near Joppa, the disciples, hearing that Peter was there, sent two men to him, urging him, "Do not delay in coming to us."

**9:39** So Peter arose and went with them. When he had arrived, they led him up into the upper room, and all the widows stood beside him weeping and displaying the tunics and garments that Dorcas had made while she was with them.

**9:40** But Peter put them all outside, and kneeling down, he prayed; and turning toward the body, he said, "Tabitha, arise." And she opened her eyes, and when she saw Peter, she sat up.

**9:41** Then giving her his hand, he raised her up; and calling the saints and the widows, he presented her alive.

**9:42** And this became known throughout all Joppa, and many believed in the Lord.

**9:43** And it came to pass that he stayed many days in Joppa with a certain Simon, a tanner.

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — The critical Greek text omits the longer expansion found in the Textus Receptus ("It is hard for you to kick against the goads..." and Saul's reply in verse 6), which entered later manuscripts from the parallel accounts in Acts 26:14 and 22:10.
- **9:20** — The critical Greek text reads "Jesus" here rather than "the Christ," which appears in later Byzantine manuscripts.
- **9:25** — The critical Greek text reads "his disciples," whereas some later manuscripts smooth the phrase to "the disciples."
- **9:31** — The critical Greek text uses the singular "the church" with singular verbs across Judea, Galilee, and Samaria, whereas later Byzantine manuscripts read the plural "the churches."
