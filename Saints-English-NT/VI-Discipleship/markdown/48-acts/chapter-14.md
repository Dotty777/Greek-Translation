## Text

**14:1** Now it came to pass in Iconium that they entered together into the synagogue of the Jews, and spoke in such a manner that a great multitude of both Jews and Greeks believed.

**14:2** But the Jews who refused to believe stirred up and embittered the souls of the Gentiles against the brethren.

**14:3** Therefore they stayed a considerable time, speaking boldly in the Lord, who bore witness to the word of His grace, granting signs and wonders to be done through their hands.

**14:4** But the multitude of the city was divided; some stood with the Jews, and others with the apostles.

**14:5** And when a violent attempt was made by both the Gentiles and the Jews, together with their rulers, to mistreat and stone them,

**14:6** they became aware of it and fled to the cities of Lycaonia—Lystra and Derbe—and to the surrounding region,

**14:7** and there they continued proclaiming the gospel.

**14:8** Now in Lystra a certain man sat who had no strength in his feet, lame from his mother's womb, who had never walked.

**14:9** This man listened to Paul speaking; and Paul, gazing intently at him and perceiving that he had faith to be made well,

**14:10** said with a loud voice, "Stand upright upon your feet!" And he leaped up and began to walk.

**14:11** When the crowds saw what Paul had done, they lifted up their voices, saying in the Lycaonian tongue, "The gods have become like men and have come down to us!"

**14:12** And they called Barnabas Zeus, and Paul Hermes, because he was the chief speaker.

**14:13** Then the priest of Zeus, whose temple stood before the city, brought oxen and garlands to the gates and desired to offer sacrifice along with the crowds.

**14:14** But when the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard of it, they tore their garments and rushed out into the crowd, crying aloud

**14:15** and saying, "Men, why are you doing these things? We also are men of the same nature as you, bringing you good news that you should turn from these vain things to the living God, who made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that is in them,

**14:16** who in bygone generations allowed all the nations to walk in their own ways.

**14:17** Yet He did not leave Himself without witness, for He did good, giving you rains from heaven and fruitful seasons, filling your hearts with food and gladness."

**14:18** Even by saying these words, they scarcely restrained the crowds from offering sacrifice to them.

**14:19** Then Jews arrived from Antioch and Iconium, and having won over the crowds, they stoned Paul and dragged him outside the city, supposing him to be dead.

**14:20** However, when the disciples gathered around him, he rose up and went into the city. And on the next day he departed with Barnabas for Derbe.

**14:21** After they had proclaimed the gospel to that city and made many disciples, they returned to Lystra, to Iconium, and to Antioch,

**14:22** strengthening the souls of the disciples, exhorting them to continue in the faith, and saying that through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God.

**14:23** And when they had appointed elders for them in every church, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord in whom they had believed.

**14:24** Then, having passed through Pisidia, they came to Pamphylia.

**14:25** And when they had spoken the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia.

**14:26** From there they sailed away to Antioch, where they had been commended to the grace of God for the work which they had fulfilled.

**14:27** Now when they had arrived and gathered the church together, they reported all that God had done with them, and how He had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.

**14:28** And they remained no little time with the disciples.

## Translator Notes

- **14:12** — The Greek text names Zeus and Hermes directly, whereas older English versions followed the Latin Vulgate in substituting their Roman equivalents, Jupiter and Mercury.
- **14:17** — The Greek critical text reads the second-person plural "you" and "your," rather than "us" and "our" found in some later manuscripts.
- **14:23** — The Greek verb cheirotoneo originally meant to elect by raising the hand and came in Koine Greek to denote formally appointing leaders.
