## Text

**24:1** Now after five days the high priest Ananias came down with certain elders and a certain orator named Tertullus, and they laid their case before the governor against Paul.

**24:2** And when he was summoned, Tertullus began to accuse him, saying, "Since through you we enjoy great peace, and reforms are being brought about for this nation through your foresight,

**24:3** we accept it in every way and in every place, most excellent Felix, with all thankfulness.

**24:4** Yet lest I detain you any further, I implore you to hear us briefly in your graciousness.

**24:5** For we have found this man to be a plague, one who stirs up dissensions among all the Jews throughout the inhabited world, and a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes,

**24:6** who even attempted to profane the temple, and whom we also seized;

**24:8** from whom, by examining him yourself, you will be able to ascertain concerning all these things of which we accuse him."

**24:9** And the Jews also joined in the charge, affirming that these things were so.

**24:10** Then Paul, after the governor had nodded to him to speak, answered, "Knowing that for many years you have been a judge over this nation, I gladly make my defense concerning myself,

**24:11** since you are able to ascertain that it has been no more than twelve days since I went up to Jerusalem to worship.

**24:12** And neither in the temple did they find me disputing with anyone or stirring up a crowd, nor in the synagogues, nor throughout the city;

**24:13** nor are they able to prove to you the things of which they now accuse me.

**24:14** Yet this I confess to you, that according to the Way, which they call a sect, so I worship the God of our fathers, believing all things that are according to the Law and that are written in the Prophets,

**24:15** having a hope in God, which these men themselves also await, that there is about to be a resurrection both of the righteous and of the unrighteous.

**24:16** In view of this, I myself also strive to keep a blameless conscience toward God and men at all times.

**24:17** Now after many years I came to bring alms to my nation, and offerings,

**24:18** in the midst of which they found me purified in the temple, neither with a crowd nor with tumult.

**24:19** But there were certain Jews from Asia, who ought to have been present before you and to bring accusation if they had anything against me;

**24:20** or else let these men themselves say what wrongdoing they found when I stood before the council,

**24:21** other than this one statement which I cried out while standing among them, 'Concerning the resurrection of the dead I am being judged before you this day.'"

**24:22** Then Felix, having more accurate knowledge of the matters concerning the Way, adjourned them, saying, "When Lysias the commander comes down, I will decide your case."

**24:23** And he gave orders to the centurion that he should be kept under guard, yet have some liberty, and that none of his own people should be hindered from ministering to him.

**24:24** Now after some days, when Felix arrived with his wife Drusilla, who was a Jewess, he sent for Paul and heard him concerning the faith in Christ Jesus.

**24:25** And as he reasoned about righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come, Felix became terrified and answered, "Go your way for now; and when I have an opportune time, I will summon you."

**24:26** At the same time he was also hoping that money would be given him by Paul; therefore he sent for him the more frequently and conversed with him.

**24:27** Now when two years had been fulfilled, Felix received Porcius Festus as his successor; and desiring to do the Jews a favor, Felix left Paul in chains.

## Translator Notes

- **24:6** — The earliest Greek manuscripts omit the clauses traditionally numbered as verse 7 (along with the end of verse 6 and start of verse 8 concerning Lysias), so verse 6 leads directly into verse 8 with "from whom" referring to Paul.
- **24:18** — Verses 18-19 contain a grammatical break (anacoluthon) in Greek, introducing "certain Jews from Asia" without a main verb before moving into the relative clause "who ought to have been present before you."
- **24:24** — The critical Greek text reads "Christ Jesus" rather than simply "Christ."
- **24:7 *(additional)*** — Text-critical double brackets ⟦ ⟧ are preserved verbatim from the Saints Greek Critical Text; this verse is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
