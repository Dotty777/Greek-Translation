## Text

**18:1** After these things he departed from Athens and came to Corinth.

**18:2** And finding a certain Jew named Aquila, a native of Pontus, who had recently arrived from Italy with his wife Priscilla—because Claudius had commanded all the Jews to depart from Rome—he went to them.

**18:3** And because he was of the same trade, he stayed with them and they worked together, for by trade they were tentmakers.

**18:4** And he reasoned in the synagogue every Sabbath, and sought to persuade both Jews and Greeks.

**18:5** Now when both Silas and Timothy had come down from Macedonia, Paul was wholly occupied with the word, solemnly testifying to the Jews that Jesus is the Christ.

**18:6** But when they opposed him and spoke blasphemy, he shook out his garments and said to them, "Your blood be upon your own heads! I am innocent; from now on I shall go to the Gentiles."

**18:7** And departing from there, he entered the house of a certain man named Titius Justus, one who worshiped God, whose house adjoined the synagogue.

**18:8** Then Crispus, the ruler of the synagogue, believed in the Lord together with his entire household; and many of the Corinthians, as they heard, believed and were baptized.

**18:9** And the Lord spoke to Paul by night in a vision, "Do not be afraid, but speak, and do not keep silent,

**18:10** for I am with you, and no one shall attack you to do you harm; for I have many people in this city."

**18:11** And he stayed there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.

**18:12** Now when Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, the Jews rose up with one accord against Paul and brought him before the judgment seat,

**18:13** saying, "This man is persuading people to worship God contrary to the law."

**18:14** But as Paul was about to open his mouth, Gallio said to the Jews, "If indeed it were a matter of some wrongdoing or wicked crime, O Jews, I would rightly bear with you;

**18:15** but since these are questions concerning words and names and your own law, see to it yourselves; I have no desire to be a judge of such matters."

**18:16** And he drove them away from the judgment seat.

**18:17** Then they all seized Sosthenes, the ruler of the synagogue, and beat him in front of the judgment seat; yet Gallio paid no regard to any of these things.

**18:18** Now Paul, after remaining yet many days, took leave of the brethren and set sail for Syria, accompanied by Priscilla and Aquila, having had his hair cut short at Cenchreae, for he had taken a vow.

**18:19** And they arrived at Ephesus, where he left them behind; yet he himself entered the synagogue and reasoned with the Jews.

**18:20** When they asked him to remain for a longer time, he did not consent,

**18:21** but taking leave of them and saying, "I shall return to you again, if God wills," he set sail from Ephesus.

**18:22** And having landed at Caesarea, he went up and greeted the church, and then went down to Antioch.

**18:23** After spending some time there, he departed and passed through the region of Galatia and Phrygia in order, strengthening all the disciples.

**18:24** Now a certain Jew named Apollos, an Alexandrian by birth, an eloquent man, arrived at Ephesus, being mighty in the Scriptures.

**18:25** This man had been instructed in the way of the Lord; and being fervent in spirit, he spoke and taught accurately the things concerning Jesus, though he knew only the baptism of John.

**18:26** And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue; but when Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained to him the way of God more accurately.

**18:27** Now when he wished to cross over to Achaia, the brethren encouraged him and wrote to the disciples to welcome him; and upon arriving, he greatly helped those who had believed through grace,

**18:28** for he vigorously refuted the Jews in public, demonstrating through the Scriptures that Jesus is the Christ.

## Translator Notes

- **18:5** — The critical Greek text reads "occupied with the word" (to logo) rather than "constrained in the spirit" (to pneumati), which appears in later manuscripts.
- **18:17** — The critical Greek text reads simply "all" (pantes), omitting "the Greeks" (hoi Hellenes) found in later manuscripts, leaving open whether the crowd of bystanders or the thwarted accusers beat Sosthenes.
- **18:18** — The singular participle "having had his hair cut short" most naturally refers to Paul as the subject of the sentence, though syntactically some connect it with the immediately preceding name, Aquila.
- **18:21** — The critical Greek text follows the shorter reading, omitting the clause found in later manuscripts in which Paul states that he must keep the coming feast in Jerusalem.
