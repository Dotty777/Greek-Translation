## Text

**3:1** O foolish Galatians, who has bewitched you, before whose eyes Jesus Christ was openly set forth as crucified?

**3:2** This alone I wish to learn from you: Did you receive the Spirit from works of the law, or from the hearing of faith?

**3:3** Are you so foolish? Having begun in the Spirit, are you now being brought to perfection in the flesh?

**3:4** Did you endure so many things in vain—if indeed it really was in vain?

**3:5** Therefore He who supplies the Spirit to you and works mighty deeds among you, does He do so from works of the law, or from the hearing of faith?

**3:6** Just as "Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness."

**3:7** Know therefore that those who are of faith—these are the sons of Abraham.

**3:8** And the Scripture, foreseeing that God justifies the Gentiles by faith, proclaimed the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying, "In you all the nations shall be blessed."

**3:9** Therefore those who are of faith are blessed together with faithful Abraham.

**3:10** For all who are of works of the law are under a curse; for it is written, "Cursed is everyone who does not abide in all the things written in the book of the law, to perform them."

**3:11** And that no one is justified by the law before God is evident, for "The righteous one shall live by faith."

**3:12** However, the law is not of faith; rather, "He who does them shall live in them."

**3:13** Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse on our behalf (for it is written, "Cursed is everyone who hangs upon a tree"),

**3:14** in order that the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles in Christ Jesus, so that through faith we might receive the promise of the Spirit.

**3:15** Brethren, I speak according to human terms: even though it is only a man's covenant, once it has been ratified, no one sets it aside or adds to it.

**3:16** Now the promises were spoken to Abraham and to his seed. It does not say, "And to seeds," as concerning many, but as concerning one, "And to your seed," who is Christ.

**3:17** Now this is what I say: the law, which came four hundred and thirty years afterward, does not invalidate a covenant previously ratified by God, so as to abolish the promise.

**3:18** For if the inheritance comes from the law, it no longer comes from promise; yet God has graciously granted it to Abraham through a promise.

**3:19** Why then the law? It was added on account of transgressions, until the seed should come to whom the promise had been made, having been ordained through angels by the hand of a mediator.

**3:20** Now a mediator is not for one only, yet God is one.

**3:21** Is the law then contrary to the promises of God? By no means! For if a law had been given that was able to impart life, truly righteousness would have come from the law.

**3:22** Instead, the Scripture shut up all things under sin, in order that the promise from faith in Jesus Christ might be granted to those who believe.

**3:23** Now before faith came, we were guarded under the law, shut up until the faith that was to be revealed.

**3:24** Therefore the law has become our custodian until Christ, so that we might be justified by faith.

**3:25** Yet now that faith has come, we are no longer under a custodian.

**3:26** For all of you are sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus.

**3:27** For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ.

**3:28** There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bondservant nor free, there is no male and female; for all of you are one in Christ Jesus.

**3:29** And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham's seed, heirs according to promise.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The critical Greek text reads the shorter form without "that you should not obey the truth" and "among you," which appear in later manuscripts.
- **3:20** — Paul's concise statement contrasts the indirect, multi-party mediation of the law at Sinai with the direct, unilateral promise made by the one God to Abraham.
- **3:22** — The Greek phrase ἐκ πίστεως Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ is debated and may be understood either as "from faith in Jesus Christ" (objective genitive) or "from the faithfulness of Jesus Christ" (subjective genitive).
- **3:24** — Greek παιδαγωγός refers to a household custodian or guardian who supervised a minor child's conduct and escorted him to school, rather than a classroom teacher.
