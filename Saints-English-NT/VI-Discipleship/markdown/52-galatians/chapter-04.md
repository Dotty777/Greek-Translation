## Text

**4:1** Now I say this: for as long as the heir is a young child, he differs in nothing from a bondservant, though he is master of all,

**4:2** but he remains under guardians and stewards until the appointed time set by the father.

**4:3** In the same way we also, when we were young children, were held in bondage under the elemental principles of the world.

**4:4** Yet when the fullness of time arrived, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under the law,

**4:5** that He might redeem those who were under the law, in order that we might receive adoption as sons.

**4:6** And because you are sons, God sent forth the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying out, "Abba, Father!"

**4:7** Therefore you are no longer a bondservant, but a son; and if a son, then an heir also through God.

**4:8** Formerly, indeed, when you did not know God, you were enslaved to those things which by nature are not gods.

**4:9** Yet now, having come to know God—or rather, having been known by God—how is it that you are turning back again to the weak and beggarly elemental principles, to which you wish to be enslaved once more?

**4:10** You closely observe days and months and seasons and years.

**4:11** I fear for you, lest somehow I have labored over you in vain.

**4:12** Become as I am, for I also became as you are, brethren, I implore you. You did me no wrong at all.

**4:13** Indeed, you know that it was on account of a weakness of the flesh that I proclaimed the gospel to you the first time;

**4:14** and what was a trial to you in my flesh you did not despise or loathe, but you welcomed me as an angel of God, even as Christ Jesus.

**4:15** Where then is your sense of blessedness? For I testify to you that, if it had been possible, you would have torn out your own eyes and given them to me.

**4:16** So then, have I become your enemy by speaking the truth to you?

**4:17** They are zealous for you, yet for no honorable end; rather, they desire to shut you out, so that you may be zealous for them.

**4:18** Yet it is always honorable to be zealous in an honorable cause, and not only when I am present with you,

**4:19** my children, for whom I am once more in the travails of childbirth until Christ is formed in you!

**4:20** Indeed, I could wish to be present with you right now and to alter my tone, for I am at a loss concerning you.

**4:21** Tell me, you who wish to be under the law, do you not hear the law?

**4:22** For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by the bondwoman and one by the free woman.

**4:23** Yet the one by the bondwoman was born according to the flesh, whereas the one by the free woman was born through promise.

**4:24** Now these things are spoken allegorically: for these women are two covenants—one from Mount Sinai, bearing children for bondage, which is Hagar.

**4:25** Now Hagar is Mount Sinai in Arabia, and corresponds to the present Jerusalem, for she is in bondage with her children.

**4:26** Yet the Jerusalem above is free, and she is our mother.

**4:27** For it is written: "Rejoice, O barren woman who does not give birth; break forth and cry aloud, you who are not in travail; for more are the children of the desolate woman than of her who has a husband."

**4:28** Now you, brethren, just as Isaac was, are children of promise.

**4:29** Yet just as at that time the one born according to the flesh persecuted the one born according to the Spirit, even so it is now.

**4:30** Yet what does the Scripture say? "Cast out the bondwoman and her son, for the son of the bondwoman shall by no means inherit together with the son of the free woman."

**4:31** Therefore, brethren, we are not children of a bondwoman, but of the free woman.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — Greek τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου ("the elemental principles of the world") is debated and may denote rudimentary religious principles, basic cosmic elements, or spiritual powers governing the present age.
- **4:7** — The critical Greek text reads διὰ θεοῦ ("through God"), whereas later manuscripts expand the phrase to "of God through Christ."
- **4:14** — The critical Greek text reads τὸν πειρασμὸν ὑμῶν ("what was a trial to you"), whereas later manuscripts read "my trial."
- **4:25** — The neuter article in τὸ δὲ Ἁγὰρ treats the name "Hagar" itself as the grammatical subject, linking her allegorically to Mount Sinai in Arabia and the present Jerusalem.
