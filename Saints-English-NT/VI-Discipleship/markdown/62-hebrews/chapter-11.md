## Text

**11:1** Now faith is the firm assurance of things hoped for, the certain proof of things unseen.

**11:2** For by this the elders received a good testimony.

**11:3** By faith we understand that the ages were fashioned by the word of God, so that what is seen did not come into being out of things that are visible.

**11:4** By faith Abel offered to God a better sacrifice than Cain, through which he received testimony that he was righteous, as God bore witness concerning his gifts; and through it, though he has died, he still speaks.

**11:5** By faith Enoch was taken up so that he should not see death, and he was not found, because God had taken him up; for before he was taken up, he had received testimony that he had pleased God.

**11:6** And without faith it is impossible to please Him; for the one who draws near to God must believe that He exists and that He becomes a rewarder of those who earnestly seek Him.

**11:7** By faith Noah, having been divinely warned concerning things not yet seen, moved with reverent fear, constructed an ark for the salvation of his household, by which he condemned the world and became an heir of the righteousness that is according to faith.

**11:8** By faith Abraham, when he was called, obeyed to go out to a place that he was about to receive as an inheritance; and he went out, not knowing where he was going.

**11:9** By faith he sojourned in the land of promise as in a foreign land, dwelling in tents with Isaac and Jacob, the fellow heirs of the same promise;

**11:10** for he was waiting for the city that has foundations, whose designer and builder is God.

**11:11** By faith, even with Sarah herself, he received strength to beget offspring, even beyond the proper time of age, because he considered Him faithful who had promised.

**11:12** Therefore from one man, and him as good as dead, there sprang offspring as many as the stars of heaven in multitude, and like the innumerable sand by the shore of the sea.

**11:13** All these died in faith, not having received the promises, yet having seen them from afar and welcomed them, and having confessed that they were strangers and sojourners upon the earth.

**11:14** For those who say such things make clear that they are seeking a homeland.

**11:15** And indeed, if they had been thinking of that land from which they went out, they would have had opportunity to return.

**11:16** But as it is, they long for a better one, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed of them, to be called their God; for He has prepared a city for them.

**11:17** By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac; and he who had welcomed the promises was offering up his only begotten son,

**11:18** to whom it was spoken, "In Isaac your offspring shall be called,"

**11:19** reckoning that God is able to raise him even from the dead, from which, in a figure, he also received him back.

**11:20** By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau, even concerning things to come.

**11:21** By faith Jacob, as he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and worshiped leaning upon the top of his staff.

**11:22** By faith Joseph, at the end of his life, made mention of the exodus of the sons of Israel and gave charge concerning his bones.

**11:23** By faith Moses, when he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that the child was fair, and they did not fear the edict of the king.

**11:24** By faith Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter,

**11:25** choosing rather to endure mistreatment together with the people of God than to have the fleeting enjoyment of sin,

**11:26** considering the reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, for he was looking ahead to the reward.

**11:27** By faith he left Egypt, not fearing the wrath of the king; for he stood steadfast as seeing Him who is invisible.

**11:28** By faith he observed the Passover and the sprinkling of the blood, lest the destroyer of the firstborn should touch them.

**11:29** By faith they crossed the Red Sea as through dry land; and when the Egyptians attempted the same, they were swallowed up.

**11:30** By faith the walls of Jericho fell, having been encircled for seven days.

**11:31** By faith Rahab the harlot did not perish with those who were disobedient, having welcomed the spies in peace.

**11:32** And what more shall I say? For time will fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, and of David and Samuel and the prophets,

**11:33** who through faith conquered kingdoms, practiced righteousness, obtained promises, shut the mouths of lions,

**11:34** quenched the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, were made strong out of weakness, became mighty in war, put foreign armies to flight.

**11:35** Women received back their dead by resurrection; and others were tortured, refusing to accept release, so that they might obtain a better resurrection.

**11:36** Still others experienced mockings and scourgings, and moreover chains and imprisonment.

**11:37** They were stoned, they were sawn in two, they were tested, they died by the slaughter of the sword; they went about in sheepskins and in goatskins, being destitute, afflicted, mistreated—

**11:38** of whom the world was not worthy—wandering in deserts and mountains, and in caves and dens of the earth.

**11:39** And all these, though they received a good testimony through faith, did not receive the promise,

**11:40** since God had provided beforehand something better for us, so that apart from us they should not be made perfect.

## Translator Notes

- **11:11** — The Greek text reads the dative 'αὐτῇ Σάρρᾳ' ("with Sarah herself"), keeping Abraham as the subject who received strength to beget offspring ('καταβολὴν σπέρματος'); other manuscripts read the nominative, making Sarah the subject who received strength to conceive.
- **11:21** — Follows the Septuagint of Genesis 47:31 ("the top of his staff"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic vocalization reads "the head of his bed."
- **11:37** — The Greek text retains 'ἐπειράσθησαν' ("they were tested"), a word omitted in some early manuscripts.
