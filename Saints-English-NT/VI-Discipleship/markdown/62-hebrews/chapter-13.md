## Text

**13:1** Let brotherly love abide.

**13:2** Do not forget hospitality to strangers, for through it some have entertained angels without knowing it.

**13:3** Remember those who are in bonds as though bound with them, and those who are mistreated, since you yourselves are also in the body.

**13:4** Let marriage be honored among all, and let the marriage bed remain undefiled; for God will judge fornicators and adulterers.

**13:5** Let your conduct be free from the love of money, being content with what you have; for He Himself has said, "I will by no means leave you, nor will I ever forsake you."

**13:6** So we may say with confidence, "The Lord is my helper, and I will not fear; what shall man do to me?"

**13:7** Remember your leaders, who spoke the word of God to you; considering closely the outcome of their manner of life, imitate their faith.

**13:8** Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today, and forevermore.

**13:9** Do not be carried away by diverse and strange teachings, for it is good for the heart to be established by grace, not by foods, by which those who walk in them have not profited.

**13:10** We have an altar from which those who minister at the tabernacle have no authority to eat.

**13:11** For the bodies of those animals whose blood is carried into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin are burned up outside the camp.

**13:12** Therefore Jesus also, in order that He might sanctify the people through His own blood, suffered outside the gate.

**13:13** Let us then go out to Him outside the camp, bearing His reproach.

**13:14** For here we do not have an abiding city, but we earnestly seek the one that is to come.

**13:15** Through Him, therefore, let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that confess His name.

**13:16** And do not forget to do good and to share with others, for God is well pleased with such sacrifices.

**13:17** Obey your leaders and yield to them, for they keep watch over your souls as those who shall render an account, so that they may do this with joy and not with groaning; for that would be unprofitable to you.

**13:18** Pray for us, for we are persuaded that we have a good conscience, desiring to conduct ourselves honorably in all things.

**13:19** And I exhort you all the more earnestly to do this, so that I may be restored to you the more quickly.

**13:20** Now may the God of peace, who brought up from the dead the great Shepherd of the sheep through the blood of the eternal covenant, our Lord Jesus,

**13:21** equip you in every good thing to do His will, working in us what is well-pleasing before Him through Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory forever and ever. Amen.

**13:22** Now I urge you, brethren, bear patiently with the word of exhortation, for indeed I have written to you briefly.

**13:23** Know that our brother Timothy has been released, with whom, if he comes soon, I shall see you.

**13:24** Greet all your leaders and all the saints. Those who are from Italy greet you.

**13:25** May grace be with you all.

## Translator Notes

- **13:4** — The opening Greek clauses contain no explicit verb and may be taken either imperatively ("Let marriage be honored") in keeping with the surrounding exhortations or declaratively ("Marriage is honored"); the text also reads "for" (gar) rather than "but" (de).
- **13:10** — Interpreters debate whether "altar" (thysiasterion) refers primarily to the cross, Christ's self-offering, or the heavenly sanctuary, contrasting the new-covenant sacrifice with the Levitical tabernacle service.
- **13:21** — The Greek text reads "in every good thing" (without "work") and "working in us" (hemin rather than "in you"); the closing doxology renders "to the ages of the ages" as "forever and ever."
- **13:24** — The phrase "Those who are from Italy" (hoi apo tes Italias) can mean either Italian believers currently abroad with the author or believers writing from within Italy.
