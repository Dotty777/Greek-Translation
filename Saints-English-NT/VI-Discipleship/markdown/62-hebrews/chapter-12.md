## Text

**12:1** Therefore we also, having so great a cloud of witnesses surrounding us, laying aside every burden and the sin that so easily entangles us, let us run with perseverance the race that lies before us,

**12:2** fixing our eyes upon Jesus, the founder and perfecter of faith, who for the joy set before Him endured the cross, scorning the shame, and has taken His seat at the right hand of the throne of God.

**12:3** For consider Him who has endured such hostility from sinners against themselves, lest you grow weary and faint in your souls.

**12:4** You have not yet resisted to the point of blood, contending against sin,

**12:5** and you have forgotten the exhortation that addresses you as sons: "My son, do not make light of the discipline of the Lord, nor lose heart when you are reproved by Him;

**12:6** for the Lord disciplines the one whom He loves, and He scourges every son whom He receives."

**12:7** Endure for the sake of discipline; God deals with you as with sons. For what son is there whom a father does not discipline?

**12:8** But if you are without discipline, in which all have become partakers, then you are illegitimate children and not sons.

**12:9** Furthermore, we had fathers of our flesh who disciplined us, and we respected them; shall we not much more be subject to the Father of spirits and live?

**12:10** For they disciplined us for a few days as seemed good to them, but He does so for our benefit, that we may share in His holiness.

**12:11** Now all discipline seems for the present not to be a matter of joy, but of sorrow; yet afterward it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.

**12:12** Therefore lift up the drooping hands and the weakened knees,

**12:13** and make straight paths for your feet, so that what is lame may not be put out of joint, but rather may be healed.

**12:14** Pursue peace with all, and the sanctification without which no one will see the Lord,

**12:15** watching carefully lest anyone fall short of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up cause trouble, and by it many be defiled;

**12:16** lest there be any immoral or profane person like Esau, who for a single meal sold his own birthright.

**12:17** For you know that afterward, when he desired to inherit the blessing, he was rejected; for he found no place for repentance, though he earnestly sought it with tears.

**12:18** For you have not drawn near to what may be touched, and to blazing fire, and to darkness, and gloom, and tempest,

**12:19** and to the blast of a trumpet and a voice of words, whose hearers pleaded that no further message be spoken to them;

**12:20** for they could not bear the command that was given: "Even if a beast touches the mountain, it shall be stoned."

**12:21** And so fearful was the sight that Moses said, "I am terrified and trembling."

**12:22** Rather, you have drawn near to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to myriads of angels in festive gathering,

**12:23** and to the assembly of the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven, and to God, the Judge of all, and to the spirits of the righteous made perfect,

**12:24** and to Jesus, the Mediator of a new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling that speaks better than Abel.

**12:25** See that you do not refuse Him who is speaking. For if those did not escape when they refused Him who warned them on earth, much less shall we escape if we turn away from Him who warns from heaven,

**12:26** whose voice shook the earth at that time; but now He has promised, saying, "Yet once more I will shake not only the earth, but also heaven."

**12:27** Now this phrase, "Yet once more," signifies the removal of the things that are shaken, as of things that have been made, so that the things which cannot be shaken may remain.

**12:28** Therefore, since we are receiving an unshakable kingdom, let us have gratitude, through which we may offer acceptable worship to God with reverence and awe,

**12:29** for indeed our God is a consuming fire.

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — The Greek text reads the plural reflexive 'εἰς ἑαυτούς' ("against themselves"), highlighting that sinners' hostility toward Christ turns against themselves; other manuscripts read the singular, "against Himself."
- **12:7** — The Greek text reads 'εἰς παιδείαν' ("for the sake of discipline" or "it is for discipline that you endure"), whereas later manuscripts read the conditional 'εἰ' ("if").
- **12:18** — The Greek text omits the word "mountain" after "what may be touched," contrasting the tangible terrors of Sinai with Mount Zion in verse 22.
- **12:28** — The Greek idiom 'ἔχωμεν χάριν' means "let us have gratitude" (be thankful), though some understand it as "let us hold fast to grace."
