## Text

**2:1** For this reason we must pay much more earnest attention to the things we have heard, lest at any time we drift away.

**2:2** For if the message spoken through angels stood firm, and every transgression and disobedience received a just recompense,

**2:3** how shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation, which, having at the first been spoken through the Lord, was confirmed to us by those who heard,

**2:4** while God also bore witness with them both by signs and wonders, and by various mighty works, and by distributions of the Holy Spirit according to His own will?

**2:5** For it was not to angels that He subjected the inhabited world to come, of which we are speaking.

**2:6** But one has testified somewhere, saying, "What is man that You remember him, or a son of man that You care for him?

**2:7** You made him for a little while lower than the angels; You crowned him with glory and honor;

**2:8** You subjected all things beneath his feet." For in subjecting all things to him, He left nothing that is not subjected to him. Yet at present we do not yet see all things subjected to him.

**2:9** But we behold Jesus, who for a little while was made lower than the angels, crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death, so that by the grace of God He might taste death for everyone.

**2:10** For it was fitting for Him, for whom are all things and through whom are all things, in bringing many sons to glory, to perfect through sufferings the pioneer of their salvation.

**2:11** For both He who sanctifies and those who are being sanctified are all of one; for which reason He is not ashamed to call them brethren,

**2:12** saying, "I will proclaim Your name to My brethren; in the midst of the congregation I will praise You in song."

**2:13** And again, "I will place My trust in Him." And again, "Behold, I and the children whom God has given Me."

**2:14** Since therefore the children have shared in blood and flesh, He Himself likewise partook of the same, that through death He might render powerless him who holds the dominion of death, that is, the devil,

**2:15** and deliver all those who through fear of death were throughout their entire lifetime subject to bondage.

**2:16** For assuredly He does not come to the aid of angels, but He comes to the aid of the seed of Abraham.

**2:17** Therefore He was obligated in every respect to be made like His brethren, so that He might become a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make propitiation for the sins of the people.

**2:18** For because He Himself has suffered in being tempted, He is able to come to the aid of those who are being tempted.

## Translator Notes

- **2:7** — The phrase brachy ti may mean "for a little while" (temporal, fitting 2:9) or "a little" (in degree); the Greek text here also follows the shorter reading without "and set him over the works of Your hands."
- **2:9** — The Greek text reads chariti theou ("by the grace of God"); a small number of witnesses read choris theou ("apart from God").
- **2:16** — The verb epilambanetai literally means "takes hold of," used here in the sense of laying hold of someone in order to rescue or aid.
