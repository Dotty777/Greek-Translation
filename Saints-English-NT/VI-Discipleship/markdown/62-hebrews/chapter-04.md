## Text

**4:1** Let us therefore fear, lest while a promise remains of entering into His rest, any among you should appear to have fallen short.

**4:2** For indeed we have had the good news proclaimed to us just as they also did; yet the word they heard did not profit them, since they were not united by faith with those who heard.

**4:3** For we who have believed enter into rest, just as He has said, "As I swore in My wrath, 'They shall not enter into My rest,'" although the works were accomplished from the foundation of the world.

**4:4** For He has spoken somewhere concerning the seventh day in this manner: "And God rested on the seventh day from all His works";

**4:5** and in this passage again: "They shall not enter into My rest."

**4:6** Since therefore it remains for some to enter into it, and those to whom the good news was formerly proclaimed did not enter because of disobedience,

**4:7** He again appoints a certain day, "Today," speaking through David after so long a time, just as it has been spoken beforehand: "Today, if you hear His voice, do not harden your hearts."

**4:8** For if Joshua had given them rest, He would not have spoken afterward concerning another day.

**4:9** So then, a Sabbath rest remains for the people of God.

**4:10** For the one who has entered into His rest has himself also rested from his works, just as God did from His own.

**4:11** Let us therefore strive earnestly to enter into that rest, lest anyone fall in the same pattern of disobedience.

**4:12** For the word of God is living and effective, and keener than every two-edged sword, piercing even to the dividing of soul and spirit, of both joints and marrow, and is a judge of the thoughts and purposes of the heart.

**4:13** And no creature is unseen before Him, but all things are naked and laid bare to the eyes of Him to whom we must render an account.

**4:14** Having therefore a great High Priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold firmly to our confession.

**4:15** For we do not have a High Priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but One who has been tempted in all things in like manner, yet without sin.

**4:16** Let us therefore draw near with boldness to the throne of grace, so that we may receive mercy and find grace for timely help.

## Translator Notes

- **4:2** — The critical Greek text reads the accusative plural participle synkekerasmenous ("since they were not united by faith with those who heard"), whereas some later manuscripts read the nominative singular referring to the message ("not being mixed with faith in those who heard").
- **4:3** — The Greek quotation follows the Septuagint's Hebraic oath formula (literally "If they shall enter"), which carries the force of an emphatic negative: "They shall not enter."
- **4:8** — The Greek text reads Iesous, the Septuagint equivalent of the Hebrew name Joshua, referring here to Joshua son of Nun who led Israel into Canaan.
- **4:13** — "Laid bare" translates tetrachelismena, literally "bent back by the neck" so as to be fully exposed; the verse also concludes with a play on logos ("word" in v. 12, "account" here).
