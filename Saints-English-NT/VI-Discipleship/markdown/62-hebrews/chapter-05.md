## Text

**5:1** For every high priest taken from among men is appointed on behalf of men in matters pertaining to God, so that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins,

**5:2** being able to deal gently with those who are ignorant and wandering astray, since he himself is also beset with weakness.

**5:3** And because of it he is obligated, just as for the people, so also for himself, to make offering for sins.

**5:4** And no one takes this honor for himself, but only when called by God, just as Aaron also was.

**5:5** So also Christ did not glorify Himself to become a High Priest, but it was He who spoke to Him: "You are My Son; today I have begotten You";

**5:6** just as He also says in another passage: "You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek."

**5:7** In the days of His flesh, He offered up both petitions and supplications, with loud crying and tears, to Him who was able to save Him from death, and He was heard because of His godly reverence;

**5:8** although He was a Son, He learned obedience from the things which He suffered.

**5:9** And having been made perfect, He became the source of eternal salvation to all who obey Him,

**5:10** designated by God as a High Priest according to the order of Melchizedek.

**5:11** Concerning this we have much to speak, and it is difficult to explain, since you have become sluggish in hearing.

**5:12** For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you again have need for someone to teach you the elementary principles of the beginning of the oracles of God, and you have come to need milk, and not solid food.

**5:13** For everyone who partakes of milk is inexperienced in the word of righteousness, for he is an infant.

**5:14** But solid food belongs to the mature, to those who through practice have their faculties trained for the discernment of both good and evil.

## Translator Notes

- **5:7** — The phrase apo tes eulabeias is best understood as "because of His godly reverence" (referring to His pious submission to the Father), though some interpret it as deliverance "from His fear."
- **5:8** — The Greek preserves a striking paronomasia between emathen ("learned") and epathen ("suffered").
- **5:12** — The Greek text accents tina as the indefinite pronoun ("that someone teach you"); an alternative interrogative accentuation yields "what are the elementary principles."
