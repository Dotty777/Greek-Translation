## Text

**9:1** Now indeed the first covenant had ordinances of worship and an earthly sanctuary.

**9:2** For a tabernacle was prepared: the first section, in which were the lampstand, the table, and the bread of the Presence, which is called the Holy Place;

**9:3** and behind the second veil, the tabernacle called the Holy of Holies,

**9:4** having a golden altar of incense and the ark of the covenant overlaid on every side with gold, in which were a golden jar containing the manna, Aaron's rod that budded, and the tablets of the covenant;

**9:5** and above it were the cherubim of glory overshadowing the mercy seat. Concerning these things we cannot now speak in detail.

**9:6** Now with these things thus prepared, the priests continually enter the first tabernacle, performing their sacred services;

**9:7** but into the second the high priest alone enters once a year, not without blood, which he offers for himself and for the people's sins of ignorance,

**9:8** by this the Holy Spirit signifies that the way into the sanctuary has not yet been made manifest while the first tabernacle still has its standing,

**9:9** which is a symbol for the present time; according to it both gifts and sacrifices are offered that are unable to make the worshiper perfect in conscience,

**9:10** dealing only with foods and drinks and various washings, ordinances of the flesh imposed until a time of reformation.

**9:11** But when Christ appeared as High Priest of the good things that have come, through the greater and more perfect tabernacle not made with hands—that is, not of this creation—

**9:12** and not by means of the blood of goats and calves, but by means of His own blood, He entered once for all into the sanctuary, having secured eternal redemption.

**9:13** For if the blood of goats and bulls, and the ashes of a heifer sprinkling those who have been defiled, sanctify for the purification of the flesh,

**9:14** how much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered Himself without blemish to God, cleanse your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?

**9:15** And for this reason He is the Mediator of a new covenant, so that, since a death has taken place for the redemption of the transgressions under the first covenant, those who have been called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.

**9:16** For where there is a will, the death of the one who made it must of necessity be established;

**9:17** for a will is confirmed only upon death, since it is never in force while the one who made it lives.

**9:18** Therefore not even the first covenant was inaugurated without blood.

**9:19** For when every commandment according to the law had been spoken by Moses to all the people, he took the blood of the calves and the goats, with water, scarlet wool, and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book itself and all the people,

**9:20** saying, "This is the blood of the covenant which God has enjoined upon you."

**9:21** And in the same manner he sprinkled with the blood both the tabernacle and all the vessels of the ministry.

**9:22** And almost all things are cleansed with blood according to the law, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness.

**9:23** Therefore it was necessary for the copies of the things in the heavens to be cleansed with these, but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these.

**9:24** For Christ did not enter a sanctuary made with hands, a counterpart of the true, but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf;

**9:25** nor was it that He should offer Himself repeatedly, as the high priest enters the sanctuary year by year with blood that is not his own,

**9:26** for then He would have had to suffer repeatedly since the foundation of the world; but now, once at the consummation of the ages, He has been manifested to put away sin through the sacrifice of Himself.

**9:27** And just as it is appointed for men to die once, and after this comes judgment,

**9:28** so Christ also, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, shall appear a second time, apart from sin, to those who eagerly await Him, for salvation.

## Translator Notes

- **9:4** — Greek thymiaterion can denote either the "altar of incense" (associated with the inner sanctuary on the Day of Atonement) or a "censer."
- **9:11** — The critical text reads ton genomenon agathon ("the good things that have come"), whereas other manuscripts read mellonton ("the good things to come").
- **9:14** — The critical text reads hymon ("your conscience"), whereas other manuscripts read hemon ("our conscience").
- **9:16** — Greek diatheke means both "covenant" (9:15, 20) and "will" or "testament" (9:16–17), a dual meaning the author uses to link Christ's death with the inauguration of the new covenant.
