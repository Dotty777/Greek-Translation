## Text

**7:1** For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of God Most High, who met Abraham as he returned from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him,

**7:2** to whom Abraham also apportioned a tenth of all, being by interpretation first king of righteousness, and then also king of Salem, which is king of peace,

**7:3** without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, yet made to resemble the Son of God, abides a priest for all time.

**7:4** Now observe how great this man was, to whom Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth from the choicest spoils.

**7:5** And indeed those among the sons of Levi who receive the priestly office have a commandment to collect tithes from the people according to the law, that is, from their brethren, though they have come forth from the loins of Abraham;

**7:6** yet he whose genealogy is not traced from them received a tithe from Abraham and blessed the one who held the promises.

**7:7** And beyond all dispute, the lesser is blessed by the greater.

**7:8** And here men who die receive tithes, but there one of whom it is witnessed that he lives.

**7:9** And, so to speak, through Abraham even Levi, who receives tithes, paid tithes,

**7:10** for he was still in his father's loins when Melchizedek met him.

**7:11** Now if perfection had come through the Levitical priesthood—for on the basis of it the people received the law—what further need would there have been for another priest to arise according to the order of Melchizedek, and not to be designated according to the order of Aaron?

**7:12** For when the priesthood is changed, of necessity there takes place a change of the law as well.

**7:13** For the One of whom these things are spoken belonged to another tribe, from which no one has attended at the altar;

**7:14** for it is clearly evident that our Lord has sprung from Judah, concerning which tribe Moses spoke nothing of priests.

**7:15** And it is even more abundantly clear if another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek,

**7:16** who has become such not according to a law of fleshly commandment, but according to the power of an indestructible life,

**7:17** for it is testified, "You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek."

**7:18** For on the one hand, there is a setting aside of the former commandment because of its weakness and unprofitableness—

**7:19** for the law perfected nothing—and on the other hand, the bringing in of a better hope, through which we draw near to God.

**7:20** And by as much as this was not without an oath—for those others indeed became priests without an oath,

**7:21** but He with an oath through the One who said to Him, "The Lord has sworn and will not relent, 'You are a priest forever'"—

**7:22** by so much Jesus has also become the guarantor of a better covenant.

**7:23** Moreover, those priests have been many in number, because death prevented them from remaining in office;

**7:24** but He, because He abides forever, holds an unchangeable priesthood;

**7:25** Therefore He is also able to save completely those who draw near to God through Him, since He ever lives to intercede on their behalf.

**7:26** For such a High Priest was indeed fitting for us—holy, innocent, undefiled, separated from sinners, and exalted higher than the heavens;

**7:27** who has no need day by day, like the high priests, to offer up sacrifices first for His own sins and then for the sins of the people—for this He did once for all when He offered up Himself.

**7:28** For the law appoints as high priests men who have weakness, but the word of the oath, which came after the law, appoints a Son who has been perfected forever.

## Translator Notes

- **7:17** — The critical text reads passive martyreitai ("it is testified"), whereas some later manuscripts read active martyrei ("He testifies").
- **7:21** — The critical text ends the quotation from Psalm 110:4 at "forever"; later manuscripts add "according to the order of Melchizedek."
- **7:25** — Greek eis to panteles carries both a qualitative sense ("completely" or "to the uttermost") and a temporal sense ("for all time").
