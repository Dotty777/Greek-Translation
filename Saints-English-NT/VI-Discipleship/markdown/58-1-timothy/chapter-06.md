## Text

**6:1** Let all who are bondservants under the yoke regard their own masters as worthy of full honor, so that the name of God and the teaching may not be blasphemed.

**6:2** And let those who have believing masters not despise them because they are brethren; rather, let them serve them all the more, because those who partake of the benefit are believers and beloved. Teach these things and exhort them.

**6:3** If anyone teaches a different doctrine and does not assent to sound words—those of our Lord Jesus Christ—and to the teaching that accords with godliness,

**6:4** he is puffed up with conceit and understands nothing, but has a morbid obsession with controversies and disputes over words, out of which arise envy, strife, slanders, evil suspicions,

**6:5** and constant friction among men who are corrupted in mind and bereft of the truth, supposing that godliness is a means of gain.

**6:6** Yet godliness with contentment is great gain indeed;

**6:7** for we brought nothing into the world, so that neither are we able to carry anything out;

**6:8** and having nourishment and covering, with these we shall be content.

**6:9** But those who desire to become rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many senseless and harmful desires that plunge men into ruin and destruction.

**6:10** For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil; reaching out after it, some have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.

**6:11** But as for you, O man of God, flee from these things; rather, pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, steadfastness, and gentleness.

**6:12** Fight the noble fight of the faith; lay hold of the eternal life to which you were called, and you confessed the good confession in the presence of many witnesses.

**6:13** I charge you in the presence of God, who gives life to all things, and of Christ Jesus, who testified the good confession before Pontius Pilate,

**6:14** that you keep the commandment spotless and above reproach until the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**6:15** which He will reveal in His own appointed times—He who is the blessed and only Sovereign, the King of those who reign and the Lord of those who rule,

**6:16** who alone possesses immortality, dwelling in unapproachable light, whom no man has seen nor can see; to Him be honor and eternal dominion. Amen.

**6:17** Charge those who are rich in the present age not to be haughty, nor to rest their hope on the uncertainty of wealth, but upon God, who richly furnishes us with all things for our enjoyment;

**6:18** to do good, to be rich in noble works, to be generous and willing to share,

**6:19** storing up for themselves a good foundation for the time to come, so that they may lay hold of that which is truly life.

**6:20** O Timothy, guard the deposit entrusted to you, turning away from profane, empty chatter and the opposing assertions of what is falsely called knowledge,

**6:21** by professing which, some have missed the mark regarding the faith. Grace be with you all.

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — "Those who partake of the benefit" is a debated Greek clause, most likely referring to the believing masters who benefit from their bondservants' faithful labor.
- **6:5** — The critical Greek text ends the verse here, omitting the closing clause "from such withdraw yourself" found in later manuscripts.
- **6:7** — The critical Greek text reads simply hoti ("so that") without the word "certain" (delon) added in later manuscripts to smooth the syntax.
- **6:10** — Because "root" (rhiza) lacks the definite article and precedes the verb, it is rendered "a root of all kinds of evil" (literally "a root of all evils").
- **6:21** — "You all" reflects the plural Greek pronoun hymon, indicating that the closing benediction extends beyond Timothy to the whole church; the critical text also omits "Amen."
