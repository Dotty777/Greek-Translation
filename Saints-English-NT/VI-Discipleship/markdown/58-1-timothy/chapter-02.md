## Text

**2:1** First of all, then, I exhort that supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings be offered on behalf of all men,

**2:2** for kings and all who hold high station, that we may lead a tranquil and quiet life in all godliness and dignity.

**2:3** This is noble and acceptable before God our Savior,

**2:4** who wills that all men be saved and come to the full knowledge of the truth.

**2:5** For God is one, and one also is the Mediator between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus,

**2:6** who gave Himself as a ransom for all, the testimony borne at the proper times;

**2:7** for which I was appointed a herald and an apostle—I speak the truth in Christ, I do not lie—a teacher of the Gentiles in faith and truth.

**2:8** I desire, therefore, that in every place the men pray, lifting up holy hands without wrath or disputing;

**2:9** likewise also that women adorn themselves in respectable attire, with modesty and self-control, not with braided hair and gold, or pearls, or costly garments,

**2:10** but with what is fitting for women who profess godliness, through good works.

**2:11** Let a woman learn in quietness, in all submission.

**2:12** Now I do not allow a woman to teach, nor to hold authority over a man, but to be in quietness.

**2:13** For Adam was fashioned first, and afterward Eve;

**2:14** and Adam was not deceived, but the woman, having been thoroughly deceived, came into transgression.

**2:15** Yet she shall be saved through childbearing, if they abide in faith and love and sanctification, with self-control.

## Translator Notes

- **2:7** — The phrase en Christo ("in Christ") is present in the Greek source text, though omitted by several early manuscripts.
- **2:12** — Greek authentein ("to hold authority over") occurs only here in the New Testament and is variously understood as exercising governing authority or acting in a domineering manner.
- **2:15** — Greek dia tes teknogonias ("through childbearing") includes the definite article ("the childbearing"), leading interpreters to view it either as faithful perseverance in the calling of motherhood or as an allusion to the birth of the promised Seed (cf. Genesis 3:15).
