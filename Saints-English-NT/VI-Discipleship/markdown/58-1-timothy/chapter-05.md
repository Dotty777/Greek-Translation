## Text

**5:1** Do not sharply rebuke an older man, but appeal to him as a father, younger men as brothers,

**5:2** older women as mothers, and younger women as sisters, in all purity.

**5:3** Give honor to widows who are widows indeed.

**5:4** But if any widow has children or grandchildren, let them first learn to practice godliness toward their own household and to make repayment to their forebears, for this is acceptable in the sight of God.

**5:5** Now she who is a widow indeed, and left alone, has placed her hope in God and perseveres in supplications and prayers night and day;

**5:6** yet she who lives in self-indulgence is dead even while she lives.

**5:7** And charge them with these things, so that they may be above reproach.

**5:8** But if anyone fails to make provision for his own, and especially for members of his own household, he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever.

**5:9** Let a widow be enrolled only if she is no less than sixty years of age, having been the wife of one husband,

**5:10** well attested for noble deeds: if she has reared children, if she has shown hospitality to strangers, if she has washed the feet of the saints, if she has brought relief to the afflicted, and if she has diligently followed every good work.

**5:11** But refuse younger widows; for when their sensual desires turn them away from Christ, they wish to marry,

**5:12** thus incurring condemnation because they have cast aside their first pledge.

**5:13** And at the same time they also learn to be idle, wandering about from house to house; and not only idle, but gossips and busybodies as well, speaking things that ought not to be spoken.

**5:14** Therefore I desire that younger widows marry, bear children, manage the household, and give the adversary no occasion for reproach;

**5:15** for some have already strayed away after Satan.

**5:16** If any believing woman has widows, let her support them, and let the church not be burdened, so that it may support those who are widows indeed.

**5:17** Let the elders who govern well be counted worthy of double honor, especially those who labor in the word and in teaching.

**5:18** For the Scripture declares, "You shall not muzzle an ox while it threshes," and, "The worker is worthy of his wages."

**5:19** Do not entertain an accusation against an elder except on the testimony of two or three witnesses.

**5:20** As for those who persist in sin, reprove them in the presence of all, so that the rest also may have fear.

**5:21** I solemnly charge you in the presence of God and of Christ Jesus and of the elect angels to observe these things without prejudgment, doing nothing out of partiality.

**5:22** Do not lay hands hastily upon anyone, nor take part in the sins of others; keep yourself pure.

**5:23** No longer drink water exclusively, but take a little wine for the sake of your stomach and your frequent infirmities.

**5:24** The sins of some men are openly evident, going ahead of them to judgment, while the sins of others follow after;

**5:25** likewise good works also are openly evident, and even those that are otherwise cannot remain concealed.

## Translator Notes

- **5:11** — The rare Greek verb katastreniao denotes growing restive with sensual passions that draw one away from devotion to Christ.
- **5:12** — "Their first pledge" (literally "their first faith") likely refers to an earlier vow of dedicated service taken by enrolled widows.
- **5:16** — The critical Greek text specifies "any believing woman" (feminine piste), whereas later manuscripts expand this to "any believing man or woman."
- **5:18** — The first quotation cites Deuteronomy 25:4, and the second corresponds to the saying of Jesus recorded in Luke 10:7.
