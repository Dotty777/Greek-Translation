## Text

**3:1** Faithful is the saying: If anyone aspires to the office of an overseer, he sets his heart on a noble work.

**3:2** An overseer, therefore, must be irreproachable, the husband of one wife, temperate, prudent, orderly, hospitable, apt to teach,

**3:3** not addicted to wine, not violent, but forbearing, peaceable, free from the love of money,

**3:4** governing his own household well, keeping his children in submission with all dignity;

**3:5** (now if someone does not know how to govern his own household, how shall he care for the church of God?)

**3:6** not a recent convert, lest, being puffed up with conceit, he fall into the condemnation of the devil.

**3:7** Moreover, he must also have an honorable testimony from those outside, lest he fall into reproach and the snare of the devil.

**3:8** Deacons likewise must be dignified, not double-tongued, not indulging in much wine, not greedy for dishonest gain,

**3:9** holding fast the mystery of the faith in a pure conscience.

**3:10** And let these also be proved first; afterward let them serve as deacons, being blameless.

**3:11** Women likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, sober, faithful in all things.

**3:12** Let deacons be husbands of one wife, governing their children and their own households well.

**3:13** For those who have served well as deacons gain for themselves an honorable standing and great boldness in the faith that is in Christ Jesus.

**3:14** I am writing these things to you, hoping to come to you soon;

**3:15** yet if I am delayed, that you may know how one must conduct oneself in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and foundation of the truth.

**3:16** And beyond dispute, great is the mystery of godliness: He who was manifested in the flesh, vindicated in the Spirit, seen by angels, heralded among the nations, believed on in the world, received up in glory.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Greek episkopes ("the office of an overseer") is traditionally rendered "bishop" and is closely associated with the office of elder in the New Testament (cf. Titus 1:5-7).
- **3:11** — Greek gynaikas ("Women") can refer either to the wives of deacons or to women serving as deacons (deaconesses).
- **3:16** — The Greek text reads hos ("He who"), introducing an early Christological confession; later manuscripts read theos ("God"). Greek en pneumati may be understood as "in the Spirit" (the Holy Spirit) or "in spirit" in contrast with "in the flesh."
