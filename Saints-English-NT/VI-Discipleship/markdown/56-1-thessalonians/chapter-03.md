## Text

**3:1** Therefore, when we could bear it no longer, we were willing to be left behind in Athens alone,

**3:2** and we sent Timothy, our brother and minister of God in the gospel of Christ, to strengthen and encourage you concerning your faith,

**3:3** so that no one might be unsettled amid these afflictions. For you yourselves know that we are appointed for this.

**3:4** For indeed, when we were with you, we kept telling you beforehand that we were about to suffer affliction, just as it came to pass, and you know.

**3:5** For this reason, when I too could bear it no longer, I sent to learn of your faith, lest somehow the tempter had tempted you, and our labor should prove in vain.

**3:6** But now that Timothy has come to us from you, and has brought us good tidings of your faith and love, and that you always keep a kindly remembrance of us, longing to see us just as we also long to see you,

**3:7** for this reason we were comforted, brethren, over you in all our distress and affliction through your faith,

**3:8** for now we live, if you stand steadfast in the Lord.

**3:9** For what thanksgiving can we render in return to God concerning you, for all the joy with which we rejoice on your account before our God,

**3:10** night and day praying beyond measure that we may see your face and make complete what is lacking in your faith?

**3:11** Now may our God and Father Himself, and our Lord Jesus, direct our path to you.

**3:12** And may the Lord cause you to increase and overflow in love toward one another and toward all, just as we also do toward you,

**3:13** so that He may strengthen your hearts blameless in holiness before our God and Father at the coming of our Lord Jesus with all His holy ones.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — The Greek critical text reads 'minister of God' (διάκονον τοῦ θεοῦ); other manuscripts read 'coworker of God' or combine both titles.
- **3:11** — In Greek, the optative verb 'direct' is singular despite having a compound subject ('our God and Father Himself, and our Lord Jesus'), emphasizing their unity of action.
- **3:13** — The Greek phrase τῶν ἁγίων αὐτοῦ ('His holy ones') may refer to angels (as in Zechariah 14:5), to redeemed believers, or to both together.
