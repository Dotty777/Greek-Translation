## Text

**2:1** For you yourselves know, brethren, that our entrance to you has not proved in vain,

**2:2** but though we had previously suffered and been shamefully mistreated at Philippi, just as you know, we grew bold in our God to speak to you the gospel of God amid great conflict.

**2:3** For our exhortation does not arise from error, nor from impurity, nor in guile,

**2:4** but just as we have been tested and approved by God to be entrusted with the gospel, so we speak—not as pleasing men, but God, who examines our hearts.

**2:5** For at no time did we come with speech of flattery, just as you know, nor with a pretext for covetousness—God is witness—

**2:6** nor seeking glory from men, neither from you nor from others,

**2:7** though we could have made demands as apostles of Christ. Yet we became infants in your midst, as a nursing mother cherishes her own children;

**2:8** thus, yearning affectionately for you, we were delighted to impart to you not only the gospel of God, but also our very own souls, because you had become dearly beloved to us.

**2:9** For you remember, brethren, our toil and hardship: working night and day, so as not to burden any of you, we proclaimed to you the gospel of God.

**2:10** You are witnesses, and so is God, how devoutly, righteously, and blamelessly we conducted ourselves toward you who believe,

**2:11** just as you know how we dealt with each one of you, as a father does his own children,

**2:12** exhorting you, encouraging you, and solemnly charging you to walk worthy of God, who calls you into His own kingdom and glory.

**2:13** And for this reason we also give thanks to God unceasingly, that when you received the word of God which you heard from us, you accepted it not as the word of men, but as it truly is, the word of God, which is also at work in you who believe.

**2:14** For you became imitators, brethren, of the churches of God in Judea that are in Christ Jesus, because you yourselves also suffered the same things from your own countrymen, just as they did from the Jews,

**2:15** who put to death both the Lord Jesus and the prophets, and drove us out; they do not please God, and are opposed to all men,

**2:16** hindering us from speaking to the Gentiles that they might be saved, so as to fill up the measure of their sins at all times. But wrath has overtaken them to the uttermost.

**2:17** But we, brethren, having been torn away from you for a brief season—in presence, not in heart—strove all the more earnestly to see your face with great longing.

**2:18** For we desired to come to you—I, Paul, both once and twice—yet Satan hindered us.

**2:19** For what is our hope, or joy, or crown of boasting—is it not even you—before our Lord Jesus at His coming?

**2:20** For you are our glory and our joy.

## Translator Notes

- **2:7** — The Greek critical text reads 'infants' (νήπιοι), differing by one letter from the manuscript variant 'gentle' (ἤπιοι). The phrase rendered 'made demands' literally means 'to be in weight,' conveying either asserting apostolic authority or imposing a financial burden.
- **2:16** — The Greek phrase εἰς τέλος ('to the uttermost') is variously interpreted as 'completely,' 'at last,' or 'forever.'
- **2:19** — The Greek term παρουσία ('coming') denotes a royal arrival or personal presence.
