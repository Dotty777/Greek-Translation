## Text

**12:1** Now concerning spiritual gifts, brethren, I do not wish you to be ignorant.

**12:2** You know that when you were Gentiles, you were carried off toward mute idols, in whatever way you were led.

**12:3** Therefore I make it known to you that no one speaking by the Spirit of God says, "Accursed is Jesus," nor is anyone able to say, "Jesus is Lord," except by the Holy Spirit.

**12:4** Now there are diversities of gifts, yet the same Spirit;

**12:5** and there are diversities of ministries, and the same Lord;

**12:6** and there are diversities of workings, yet it is the same God who works all things in all.

**12:7** Now to each one the manifestation of the Spirit is given for the common profit.

**12:8** for to one through the Spirit is given a word of wisdom, and to another a word of knowledge according to the same Spirit,

**12:9** to another faith by the same Spirit, and to another gifts of healings by the one Spirit,

**12:10** and to another workings of miracles, to another prophecy, to another discernings of spirits, to another various kinds of tongues, and to another the interpretation of tongues;

**12:11** yet one and the same Spirit works all these things, apportioning to each one individually just as He wills.

**12:12** For just as the body is one and possesses many members, and all the members of the body, though being many, are one body, so also is Christ;

**12:13** for indeed in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body—whether Jews or Greeks, whether slaves or free—and all were made to drink of one Spirit.

**12:14** For truly the body is not one member, but many.

**12:15** If the foot should say, "Because I am not a hand, I am not part of the body," is it therefore not part of the body?

**12:16** And if the ear should say, "Because I am not an eye, I am not part of the body," it is not on that account any less part of the body;

**12:17** If the entire body were an eye, where would the hearing be? If the whole were hearing, where would the sense of smell be?

**12:18** But as it is, God placed the members, every single one of them, in the body just as He willed.

**12:19** And if all were a single member, where would the body be?

**12:20** As it is, however, there are many members, yet one body.

**12:21** And the eye is unable to say to the hand, "I have no need of you," nor again the head to the feet, "I have no need of you";

**12:22** on the contrary, much rather, those members of the body that appear to be weaker are indispensable,

**12:23** and those parts of the body that we consider less honorable, upon these we bestow more abundant honor; and our unseemly parts receive greater modesty,

**12:24** whereas our presentable parts have no such need. Rather, God has blended the body together, giving more abundant honor to the part that lacks it,

**12:25** lest there be any schism in the body, but rather that the members should show equal care for one another.

**12:26** And whether one member suffers, all the members suffer together with it; or a member is honored, all the members rejoice together with it.

**12:27** Now you are Christ's body, and each of you a member in particular.

**12:28** And God has appointed some in the church: first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then miracles, then gifts of healings, deeds of help, administrations, kinds of tongues.

**12:29** Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? Do all work mighty deeds?

**12:30** Do all possess gifts of healings? Do all speak in tongues? Do all interpret?

**12:31** Yet earnestly desire the greater gifts. And still I show you a surpassingly excellent way.

## Translator Notes

- **12:1** — Greek ton pneumatikon may be neuter ("spiritual gifts" or "spiritual matters") or masculine ("spiritual persons").
- **12:2** — The Greek syntax is abrupt (anacoluthon), combining "when you were Gentiles" with the participle "being carried off" to depict the compelling pull of pagan idolatry.
- **12:13** — The critical Greek text reads "made to drink of one Spirit" without the preposition "into" found in later manuscripts.
- **12:31** — The critical Greek text reads ta meizona ("the greater" gifts) rather than the later variant ta kreittona ("the best" gifts), and zeloute may be taken either as an imperative ("earnestly desire") or an indicative ("you are earnestly desiring").
