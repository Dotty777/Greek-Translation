## Text

**13:1** If I speak in the tongues of men and of angels, yet do not have love, I have become resounding bronze or a clashing cymbal.

**13:2** And if I have prophecy and know all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith so as to move mountains, yet do not have love, I am nothing.

**13:3** And if I distribute all my possessions, and if I deliver up my body that I may boast, yet do not have love, I profit nothing.

**13:4** Love is longsuffering, love is kind; love is not envious, does not boast, is not puffed up,

**13:5** it does not behave improperly, does not seek what is its own, is not provoked to anger, does not keep account of wrong;

**13:6** it does not rejoice over unrighteousness, but rejoices together with the truth;

**13:7** it bears all things, trusts all things, hopes for all things, steadfastly endures all things.

**13:8** Love shall never fail. Yet whether there are prophecies, they shall be brought to an end; whether there are tongues, they shall cease; whether there is knowledge, it shall be brought to an end.

**13:9** For in part we know, and in part we prophesy;

**13:10** but when that which is perfect comes, that which is in part shall be brought to an end.

**13:11** When I was a child, I spoke as a child, I thought as a child, I reasoned as a child; when I became a man, I put away the ways of a child.

**13:12** For now we see through a mirror obscurely, yet then face to face; now I know in part, yet then I shall know fully, even as I also have been fully known.

**13:13** Yet now faith, hope, and love abide—these three; and the greatest of these is love.

## Translator Notes

- **13:3** — The critical Greek text reads 'that I may boast' (kauchesomai) rather than the later textual variant 'to be burned' (kauthesomai).
- **13:4** — The Greek text repeats 'love' three times at the opening of this verse ('Love is longsuffering, love is kind; love is not envious'), underscoring its centrality.
- **13:12** — Greek 'en ainigmati' literally means 'in a riddle' or 'in an obscure reflection,' alluding to the dim image cast by ancient polished metal mirrors.
