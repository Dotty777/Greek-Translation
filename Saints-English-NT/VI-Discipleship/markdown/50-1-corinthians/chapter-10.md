## Text

**10:1** For I do not wish you to be ignorant, brethren, that all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea,

**10:2** and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea,

**10:3** and all ate the same spiritual food,

**10:4** and all drank the same spiritual drink; for they were drinking from a spiritual Rock that followed them, and the Rock was Christ.

**10:5** Yet with most of them God was not well pleased, for they were laid low in the wilderness.

**10:6** Now these things took place as examples for us, so that we should not lust after evil things, even as they also lusted.

**10:7** And do not become idolaters, as some of them did; even as it is written, "The people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play."

**10:8** Nor let us engage in sexual immorality, as some of them did, and twenty-three thousand fell in a single day;

**10:9** nor let us put the Lord to the test, as some of them tested Him, and were destroyed by the serpents;

**10:10** nor murmur, even as some of them murmured, and were destroyed by the destroyer.

**10:11** Now these things happened to them by way of example, and were written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the ages have arrived.

**10:12** Therefore let him who supposes he stands watch carefully, lest he fall.

**10:13** No temptation has seized you except what is common to mankind; yet God is faithful, who will not permit you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but together with the temptation He will also make the way out, so that you may be able to endure it.

**10:14** For this very reason, my beloved, flee from idolatry.

**10:15** I speak as to discerning people; judge for yourselves what I say.

**10:16** The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not fellowship in the blood of Christ? The bread that we break, is it not fellowship in the body of Christ?

**10:17** Since there is one loaf, we, the many, are one body; for all of us partake from the one loaf.

**10:18** Consider Israel according to the flesh: are those who eat the sacrifices not partners with the altar?

**10:19** What then do I say? That what is sacrificed to idols is anything, or that an idol is anything?

**10:20** Rather, that what the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons and not to God; and I do not wish you to become partners with demons.

**10:21** You are not able to drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons; you are not able to share in the table of the Lord and the table of demons.

**10:22** Or are we provoking the Lord to jealousy? Surely we are not stronger than He?

**10:23** All things are lawful, yet not all things are beneficial; all things are lawful, yet not all things edify.

**10:24** Let no one seek his own benefit, but that of the other.

**10:25** Eat whatever is sold in the meat market, making no inquiry on account of conscience;

**10:26** for to the Lord belongs the earth, and all its fullness.

**10:27** If one of the unbelievers invites you and you wish to go, eat whatever is set before you, making no inquiry on account of conscience.

**10:28** Yet if someone says to you, "This has been offered in sacrifice," do not eat it, on account of the one who informed you and for the sake of conscience;

**10:29** conscience, I mean, not your own, but that of the other. For why should my liberty be judged by another's conscience?

**10:30** If I partake with thankfulness, why am I reviled over that for which I give thanks?

**10:31** Therefore, whether you eat or whether you drink, or whatever you do, do all things to the glory of God.

**10:32** Give no cause for stumbling either to Jews or to Greeks or to the church of God,

**10:33** even as I also please everyone in all things, not seeking my own benefit, but that of the many, that they may be saved.

## Translator Notes

- **10:8** — Paul specifies twenty-three thousand falling in a single day, whereas Numbers 25:9 records twenty-four thousand as the total toll of the plague.
- **10:9** — This Greek text reads "the Lord" (ton Kyrion), whereas other early manuscripts read "Christ" (ton Christon).
- **10:20** — This Greek text explicitly includes "the Gentiles" as the subject of the sacrifices offered to demons.
- **10:28** — The Greek uses hierothyton ("offered in sacrifice," the neutral term a pagan host would use) rather than eidolothyton ("offered to idols"), and omits the repetition of Psalm 24:1 found at the end of the verse in later manuscripts.
