## Text

**5:1** In fact, sexual immorality is reported among you, and of such a kind as is not even found among the Gentiles—that a man has his father's wife.

**5:2** And are you puffed up, and have you not rather mourned, so that he who has committed this deed might be removed from your midst?

**5:3** For I indeed, absent in body yet present in spirit, have already judged, as though present, him who has wrought this deed in such a way,

**5:4** in the name of our Lord Jesus, when you and my spirit are gathered together, with the power of our Lord Jesus,

**5:5** to hand such a one over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that the spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord.

**5:6** Your boasting is not fitting. Do you not know that a little leaven leavens the entire lump of dough?

**5:7** Cleanse out the old leaven, that you may be a fresh lump, just as you are unleavened. For Christ, our Passover, has indeed been sacrificed.

**5:8** Therefore let us celebrate the festival, not with old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and evil, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

**5:9** I wrote to you in the letter not to associate with sexually immoral persons,

**5:10** not at all meaning the sexually immoral of this world, or the covetous and extortioners, or idolaters, since then you would be obliged to go out of the world.

**5:11** But now I write to you not to associate with anyone who bears the name of brother if he is sexually immoral, or covetous, or an idolater, or a reviler, or a drunkard, or an extortioner—not even to eat with such a man.

**5:12** For what is it to me to judge those who are outside? Do you not judge those who are inside,

**5:13** while God judges those who are outside? Expel the wicked man from among yourselves.

## Translator Notes

- **5:4** — The Saints Greek text reads "our Lord Jesus" in both clauses (without "Christ"), and in 5:5 reads "the day of the Lord" (without "Jesus").
- **5:7** — The Saints Greek text reads simply "has been sacrificed," without the explanatory phrase "for us" found in later manuscripts.
- **5:13** — The closing command echoes the recurring formula of Deuteronomy (for example, Deut 17:7 and 21:21 LXX) instructing Israel to remove evil from its midst.
