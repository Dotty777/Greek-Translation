## Text

**4:1** In this manner let a man regard us: as servants of Christ and stewards of the mysteries of God.

**4:2** In this matter, moreover, it is required among stewards that one be found faithful.

**4:3** Yet to me it is a very small matter that I should be examined by you, or by any human tribunal; indeed, I do not even examine myself.

**4:4** For I am conscious of nothing against myself, yet I am not by this justified; rather, He who examines me is the Lord.

**4:5** Therefore do not judge anything before the time, until the Lord comes, who will both illuminate the secret things of darkness and make manifest the counsels of the hearts; and then praise shall come to each one from God.

**4:6** Now these things, brethren, I have applied figuratively to myself and Apollos for your sakes, that in us you may learn the rule, "Not beyond what is written," lest any one of you be puffed up on behalf of the one against the other.

**4:7** For who sets you apart from another? And what do you possess that you did not receive? Yet if indeed you received it, why do you boast as though you had not received it?

**4:8** Already you are filled! Already you have grown rich! Apart from us you have come to reign! And I wish indeed that you did reign, so that we also might reign together with you!

**4:9** For it seems to me that God has exhibited us, the apostles, last of all, like men sentenced to death; because we have become a spectacle to the world, both to angels and to men.

**4:10** We are fools for the sake of Christ, yet you are prudent in Christ; we are weak, yet you are strong; you are held in honor, yet we are in dishonor.

**4:11** Even to this present hour we both hunger and thirst, we are poorly clad, we are struck with blows, and we have no settled dwelling,

**4:12** and we toil, laboring with our own hands. When reviled, we bless; when persecuted, we endure;

**4:13** when slandered, we speak gently. We have become like the refuse of the world, the offscouring of all things, even until now.

**4:14** I am not writing these things to put you to shame, but to admonish you as my beloved children.

**4:15** For though you should have ten thousand guardians in Christ, yet you do not have many fathers; for in Christ Jesus I became your father through the gospel.

**4:16** I exhort you, therefore, become imitators of me.

**4:17** For this very reason I sent Timothy to you, who is my beloved and faithful child in the Lord; he will put you in remembrance of my ways in Christ Jesus, just as I teach everywhere in every church.

**4:18** Now certain ones have become puffed up, as though I were not coming to you.

**4:19** Yet I will come to you quickly, if the Lord wills, and I shall discover not the speech of those who are puffed up, but the power;

**4:20** for the kingdom of God does not consist in speech, but in power.

**4:21** What do you desire? Shall I come to you with a rod, or in love and a spirit of meekness?

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Greek phrase rendered "human tribunal" is literally "human day," referring to a day appointed for human court proceedings in contrast to the coming day of the Lord.
- **4:6** — The Saints Greek text preserves the elliptical maxim "Not beyond what is written" without the explanatory verb "to think" added in later manuscripts.
- **4:15** — The Greek word paidagogos ("guardian") denoted a household attendant responsible for supervising a child's conduct, contrasted here with the unique bond of a spiritual father.
