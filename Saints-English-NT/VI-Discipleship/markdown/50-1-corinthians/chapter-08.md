## Text

**8:1** Now concerning things sacrificed to idols, we know that we all have knowledge. Knowledge puffs up, yet love edifies.

**8:2** If anyone supposes that he has come to know anything, he has not yet known as he ought to know;

**8:3** yet if anyone loves God, this one has been known by Him.

**8:4** Concerning the eating therefore of things sacrificed to idols, we know that an idol is nothing in the world, and that there is no God except one.

**8:5** For even if indeed there are so-called gods, whether in heaven or on earth, just as there are many gods and many lords,

**8:6** yet to us there is one God, the Father, from Whom are all things and we are for Him; and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through Whom are all things and we are through Him.

**8:7** Yet this knowledge is not in all; for some, through being accustomed until now to the idol, eat of it as something sacrificed to an idol, and their conscience, being weak, is defiled.

**8:8** Now food will not commend us to God; neither if we eat do we abound, nor if we do not eat do we fall short.

**8:9** Yet take heed lest somehow this right of yours become a stumbling block to the weak.

**8:10** For if anyone sees you who have knowledge reclining at table in an idol's temple, will not his conscience, since he is weak, be emboldened to eat the things sacrificed to idols?

**8:11** For the weak one is perishing by your knowledge, the brother for whom Christ died.

**8:12** And thus, by sinning against the brethren and striking their weak conscience, you sin against Christ.

**8:13** Therefore, if food causes my brother to stumble, I will by no means ever eat meat again, lest I cause my brother to stumble.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — The claim 'we all have knowledge' (and similarly the monotheistic statements in v. 4) may echo a Corinthian slogan that Paul qualifies by setting love above mere knowledge.
- **8:7** — The critical Greek text reads 'through being accustomed to' (synetheia) rather than 'with consciousness of' (syneidesei), pointing to the lingering pull of former idolatrous habit.
- **8:10** — The verb 'be emboldened' translates Greek oikodomethesetai (literally 'be built up'), echoing 'love edifies' in verse 1 with sharp irony.
