## Text

**14:1** Pursue love, and earnestly desire the spiritual gifts, yet especially that you may prophesy.

**14:2** For the one who speaks in a tongue does not speak to men, but to God, for no one understands; rather, in spirit he utters mysteries.

**14:3** Yet the one who prophesies speaks to men edification, exhortation, and consolation.

**14:4** The one who speaks in a tongue edifies himself, whereas the one who prophesies edifies the church.

**14:5** Now I desire that you all speak in tongues, yet even more that you prophesy; and greater is the one who prophesies than the one who speaks in tongues, unless indeed he interprets, that the church may receive edification.

**14:6** Now then, brethren, if I come to you speaking in tongues, what benefit shall I bring you unless I speak to you either by revelation, or by knowledge, or by prophecy, or by instruction?

**14:7** Even so, lifeless things that produce a sound, whether flute or harp—if they do not make a distinction in the tones, how shall it be known what is played on the flute or on the harp?

**14:8** For indeed, if a trumpet gives an indistinct call, who shall prepare himself for battle?

**14:9** So likewise you—if with the tongue you do not utter intelligible speech, how will what is spoken be known? For you will be speaking into the air.

**14:10** There are, it may be, so many kinds of languages in the world, and not one is without meaning;

**14:11** therefore, if I do not know the meaning of the language, I shall be a foreigner to the one who speaks, and the one who speaks shall be a foreigner to me.

**14:12** So also with you: since you are zealous for spiritual gifts, seek to abound in them for the edification of the church.

**14:13** Therefore, let whoever speaks in a tongue pray that he may interpret.

**14:14** For if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prays, yet my mind is unfruitful.

**14:15** What then shall I do? I will pray with the spirit, and I will also pray with the mind; I will sing praise with the spirit, and I will also sing praise with the mind.

**14:16** Otherwise, if you bless in spirit, how will the one who occupies the place of the uninstructed say the "Amen" at your thanksgiving, since he does not know what you are saying?

**14:17** For you indeed give thanks well, yet the other person is not edified.

**14:18** I give thanks to God that I speak in tongues more than you all;

**14:19** yet in the church I would rather speak five words with my mind, in order that I may also instruct others, than ten thousand words in a tongue.

**14:20** Brethren, do not become children in your thinking; rather, in malice be infants, yet in your thinking be mature.

**14:21** In the Law it is written: "By people of foreign tongues and by the lips of others I will speak to this people, and even so they will not listen to Me," says the Lord.

**14:22** So then, tongues serve as a sign, not for believers, but for unbelievers; whereas prophecy is not for unbelievers, but for believers.

**14:23** If therefore the whole church gathers together in one place and all speak in tongues, and uninstructed persons or unbelievers enter, will they not say that you are out of your minds?

**14:24** Yet if all prophesy, and an unbeliever or an uninstructed person enters, he is convicted by all, he is called to account by all;

**14:25** the secrets of his heart are made manifest; and so, falling upon his face, he will worship God, declaring, "Truly God is among you."

**14:26** What then is to be done, brethren? Whenever you assemble together, each one has a psalm, has a teaching, has a revelation, has a tongue, has an interpretation. Let all things be done for edification.

**14:27** If anyone speaks in a tongue, let it be by two, or at the most three, and each in turn, and let one interpret;

**14:28** but if there is no interpreter, let him keep silent in the church, and let him speak to himself and to God.

**14:29** And let two or three prophets speak, and let the others weigh what is spoken;

**14:30** yet if something is revealed to another who is seated, let the first keep silent.

**14:31** For you can all prophesy one by one, that all may learn and all may be encouraged

**14:32** (and the spirits of prophets are subject to prophets,

**14:33** for God is not a God of disorder, but of peace), as in all the churches of the saints.

**14:34** Let the women keep silent in the churches, for they are not permitted to speak; rather, let them be in subjection, even as the Law also says.

**14:35** And if they desire to learn anything, let them inquire of their own husbands at home; for it is shameful for a woman to speak in the church.

**14:36** Or did the word of God go forth from you, or has it reached you alone?

**14:37** If anyone considers himself to be a prophet or spiritual, let him acknowledge that the things I write to you are of the Lord—a commandment.

**14:38** Yet if anyone is ignorant, let him remain ignorant.

**14:39** Therefore, my brethren, earnestly desire to prophesy, and do not forbid speaking in tongues;

**14:40** yet let all things be done fittingly and in good order.

## Translator Notes

- **14:2** — Greek 'pneumati' ('in spirit') appears without the article and may refer either to the speaker's own spirit (compare verses 14-15) or to the Holy Spirit.
- **14:12** — Literally 'zealous for spirits,' referring contextually to manifestations or gifts of the Spirit.
- **14:33** — Following the punctuation of the Greek text, 'as in all the churches of the saints' concludes verses 31-33; some editions attach this clause to the beginning of verse 34.
- **14:38** — The Greek text reads the active imperative 'let him remain ignorant' (agnoeito); an ancient textual variant reads the passive 'he is not recognized' (agnoeitai).
