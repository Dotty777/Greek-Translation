## Text

**9:1** Am I not free? Am I not an apostle? Have I not beheld Jesus our Lord? Are you not my work in the Lord?

**9:2** If to others I am not an apostle, yet surely I am to you; for you yourselves are the seal of my apostleship in the Lord.

**9:3** My defense to those who scrutinize me is this:

**9:4** Do we not have the right to eat and to drink?

**9:5** Do we not have the right to take along a sister as a wife, even as the rest of the apostles, the brothers of the Lord, and Cephas do?

**9:6** Or do only I and Barnabas lack the right to refrain from working?

**9:7** Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat its fruit? Or who shepherds a flock and does not partake of the milk of the flock?

**9:8** Do I speak these things merely as a man, or does the law not also say these very things?

**9:9** For in the law of Moses it is written, "You shall not muzzle an ox while it is threshing." Is it for oxen that God is concerned,

**9:10** or does He speak altogether for our sake? For on our account it was written, that he who plows ought to plow in hope, and he who threshes, in hope of partaking.

**9:11** If we sowed spiritual things among you, is it a great matter if we reap your material things?

**9:12** If others share in this right over you, do we not even more? Yet we did not make use of this right, but we bear all things, lest we place any obstacle before the gospel of Christ.

**9:13** Do you not know that those who perform the sacred duties eat from the temple, and those who attend at the altar share with the altar?

**9:14** In the same way also the Lord ordained that those who proclaim the gospel should live by the gospel.

**9:15** But I have made use of none of these things, nor have I written these things that it should be done thus in my case; for it would be better for me to die than—no one shall make my boast void!

**9:16** For if I proclaim the gospel, it is no ground for boasting to me, for necessity rests upon me; for woe to me if I do not proclaim the gospel!

**9:17** For if I do this of my own will, I have a reward; but if against my will, a stewardship has been entrusted to me.

**9:18** What then is my reward? That in proclaiming the gospel I may offer the gospel free of charge, so as not to make full use of my right in the gospel.

**9:19** For though I am free from all, I have made myself a bondservant to all, that I might win the more;

**9:20** and to the Jews I became as a Jew, that I might win Jews; to those under the law, as one under the law—though not myself under the law—that I might win those under the law;

**9:21** to those outside the law, as one outside the law—not being outside the law of God, but within the law of Christ—that I might win those outside the law;

**9:22** to the weak I became weak, that I might win the weak; to all I have become all things, that by every means I might save some.

**9:23** And I do all things for the sake of the gospel, that I may become a joint partaker of it.

**9:24** Do you not know that those who run in a race all run, yet one receives the prize? Run in such a manner that you may lay hold of it.

**9:25** And everyone who contends in the games exercises self-control in all things; they indeed do so to receive a perishable crown, but we an imperishable one.

**9:26** Therefore I run in this way, not aimlessly; I box in this way, not as one beating the air;

**9:27** but I sternly discipline my body and bring it into subjection, lest perhaps, after preaching to others, I myself should become disqualified.

## Translator Notes

- **9:1** — The Greek critical text places "Am I not free?" before "Am I not an apostle?", reversing the order found in later Byzantine manuscripts.
- **9:5** — Literally "a sister, a wife," referring to a believing Christian wife.
- **9:15** — The Greek sentence breaks off abruptly (anacoluthon) after "than," reflecting Paul's strong emotion as he refuses even to state the alternative to death.
- **9:20** — The Greek critical text includes the clarifying parenthetical clause "though not myself under the law," which is absent from later Byzantine manuscripts.
