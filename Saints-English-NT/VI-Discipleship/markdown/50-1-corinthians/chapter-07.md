## Text

**7:1** Now concerning the matters about which you wrote: it is good for a man not to touch a woman.

**7:2** Yet because of cases of sexual immorality, let each man have his own wife, and let each woman have her own husband.

**7:3** Let the husband render to his wife what is due her, and likewise also the wife to her husband.

**7:4** The wife does not exercise authority over her own body, but the husband does; and likewise also the husband does not exercise authority over his own body, but the wife does.

**7:5** Do not deprive one another, except perhaps by mutual agreement for a season, that you may devote yourselves to prayer and be together again, lest Satan tempt you because of your lack of self-control.

**7:6** Now this I say by way of concession, not by way of command.

**7:7** Yet I wish that all men were even as I myself am; nevertheless, each one has his own gift from God, one in this manner and another in that.

**7:8** Now I say to the unmarried and to the widows, it is good for them if they abide even as I am;

**7:9** but if they do not exercise self-control, let them marry; for it is better to marry than to burn.

**7:10** Now to the married I give charge, not I but the Lord, that a wife is not to separate from her husband—

**7:11** yet if indeed she separates, let her remain unmarried or be reconciled to her husband—and that a husband is not to divorce his wife.

**7:12** Now to the rest I say, not the Lord: if any brother has an unbelieving wife, and she consents to dwell with him, let him not divorce her.

**7:13** And a woman who has an unbelieving husband, and he consents to dwell with her, let her not divorce her husband.

**7:14** For the unbelieving husband has been sanctified in his wife, and the unbelieving wife has been sanctified in the brother; otherwise your children would be unclean, whereas now they are holy.

**7:15** Yet if the unbeliever separates, let him separate; the brother or the sister is not held in bondage in such cases, but God has called us in peace.

**7:16** For how do you know, wife, whether you shall save your husband? Or how do you know, husband, whether you shall save your wife?

**7:17** Only, as the Lord has apportioned to each one, as God has called each one, so let him walk; and thus I ordain in all the churches.

**7:18** Was anyone called while circumcised? Let him not seek to undo his circumcision. Has anyone been called in uncircumcision? Let him not be circumcised.

**7:19** Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of the commandments of God.

**7:20** Each one, in the calling in which he was called, in that let him abide.

**7:21** Were you called as a bondservant? Do not let it trouble you; yet even if you are able to become free, rather make use of it.

**7:22** For the one who was called in the Lord as a bondservant is a freedman of the Lord; likewise the one who was called as a free man is a bondservant of Christ.

**7:23** You were bought with a price; do not become bondservants of men.

**7:24** Brethren, let each one abide with God in that condition in which he was called.

**7:25** Now concerning virgins, I have no command from the Lord, yet I offer my judgment as one shown mercy by the Lord to be trustworthy.

**7:26** I judge therefore that this is good because of the present distress—that it is good for a man to remain thus.

**7:27** Are you bound to a wife? Do not seek release. Are you freed from a wife? Do not seek a wife.

**7:28** Yet even if you marry, you have not sinned; and if a virgin marries, she has not sinned. Nevertheless, such ones shall have affliction in the flesh, and I would spare you.

**7:29** Now this I declare, brethren: the time has been shortened, so that from now on even those who have wives should be as though they had none,

**7:30** and those who weep as though not weeping, and those who rejoice as though not rejoicing, and those who buy as though not possessing,

**7:31** and those who use the world as though not making full use of it; for the outward form of this world is passing away.

**7:32** Now I desire you to be free from care. The unmarried man cares for the things of the Lord, how he may please the Lord;

**7:33** whereas the married man cares for the things of the world, how he may please his wife, and he is divided. And the unmarried woman, as well as the virgin

**7:34** who is unmarried, cares for the things of the Lord, that she may be holy both in body and in spirit; whereas the married woman cares for the things of the world, how she may please her husband.

**7:35** Now this I say for your own benefit, not that I may cast a snare upon you, but for what is seemly and for devoted attendance upon the Lord without distraction.

**7:36** Yet if anyone judges that he is behaving improperly toward his virgin if she is past her prime, and thus it ought to be, let him do what he wills; he does not sin; let them marry.

**7:37** Yet he who stands steadfast in his heart, having no necessity, and has authority over his own will, and has determined this in his own heart, to keep his own virgin, shall do well.

**7:38** So then he who gives his own virgin in marriage does well, and he who does not give her in marriage shall do better.

**7:39** A wife is bound for as long a time as her husband lives; yet if her husband falls asleep, she is free to be married to whom she wishes, only in the Lord.

**7:40** Yet she is more blessed if she abides thus, according to my judgment; and I think that I also have the Spirit of God.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The statement 'it is good for a man not to touch a woman' is widely taken as a slogan quoted from the Corinthians' letter rather than Paul's own maxim, though the Greek text has no quotation marks.
- **7:21** — The terse imperative 'rather make use of it' (Greek mallon chresai) supplies no explicit object and is debated: most interpreters understand it as availing oneself of the opportunity for freedom, while some take it as making use of one's present station in Christ.
- **7:33** — Following the critical Greek text, 'and he is divided' concludes verse 33, while 'who is unmarried' at the opening of verse 34 modifies 'the virgin' at the close of verse 33.
- **7:36** — The expression 'his virgin' in verses 36-38 is interpreted either as a father's virgin daughter (especially with 'gives in marriage' in v. 38) or as a betrothed man's fiancée; the literal rendering retains the Greek ambiguity.
