## Text

**2:1** Therefore you are without excuse, O man, everyone who judges; for in judging another you condemn yourself, because you who judge practice the very same things.

**2:2** Now we know that God's judgment is in accordance with truth upon those who practice such things.

**2:3** And do you reckon this, O man—you who judge those who practice such things and yet do them—that you yourself will escape the judgment of God?

**2:4** Or do you despise the riches of His kindness and His forbearance and His longsuffering, not recognizing that God's kindness leads you to repentance?

**2:5** But according to your stubbornness and impenitent heart, you are storing up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of God's righteous judgment,

**2:6** who will render to each according to his works:

**2:7** to those who by perseverance in good work seek glory, honor, and incorruption, eternal life;

**2:8** but to those who act from selfish ambition and disobey the truth, yet obey unrighteousness—wrath and indignation,

**2:9** tribulation and distress upon every soul of man who works evil, of the Jew first and also of the Greek;

**2:10** but glory and honor and peace to everyone who works what is good, to the Jew first and also to the Greek.

**2:11** For with God there is no partiality.

**2:12** For all who have sinned apart from the law shall also perish apart from the law, and all who have sinned within the law shall be judged by the law.

**2:13** For the hearers of the law are not righteous before God, but the doers of the law will be justified.

**2:14** For whenever Gentiles, who do not possess the law, do by nature the things of the law, these, though not having the law, are a law to themselves;

**2:15** for they demonstrate the work of the law written on their hearts, their conscience bearing witness alongside, and their thoughts among themselves accusing or else defending them,

**2:16** on the day when God judges the hidden things of men, according to my gospel, through Christ Jesus.

**2:17** But if you bear the name of Jew, and rest upon the law, and boast in God,

**2:18** and know His will, and approve what is superior, being instructed from the law,

**2:19** and are persuaded that you yourself are a guide of the blind, a light to those in darkness,

**2:20** a corrector of the foolish, a teacher of babes, possessing the form of knowledge and of the truth in the law—

**2:21** you, then, who teach another, do you not teach yourself? You who proclaim not to steal, do you steal?

**2:22** You who say not to commit adultery, do you commit adultery? You who detest idols, do you plunder temples?

**2:23** You who boast in the law, do you dishonor God by the transgression of the law?

**2:24** For "because of you the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles," even as it is written.

**2:25** For circumcision indeed is of benefit if you practice the law; but if you are a transgressor of the law, your circumcision has become uncircumcision.

**2:26** Therefore, if the uncircumcised man keeps the righteous ordinances of the law, will not his uncircumcision be reckoned as circumcision?

**2:27** And he who is uncircumcised by nature, fulfilling the law, shall judge you who, with the letter and circumcision, are a transgressor of the law.

**2:28** For he is not a Jew who is so outwardly, nor is that circumcision which is outward in the flesh;

**2:29** but he is a Jew who is so inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the letter; whose praise is not from men, but from God.

## Translator Notes

- **2:14** — The Greek adverb 'by nature' (physei) can be construed either with the preceding clause ('who by nature do not possess the law') or with the following verb ('do by nature the things of the law').
- **2:17** — The Greek critical text reads 'But if' (Ei de), introducing a conditional clause that extends through verse 20, rather than 'Behold' or 'Indeed' (Ide) found in later manuscripts.
- **2:27** — Following the punctuation of the critical text, this verse stands as a declarative statement rather than a question; 'with the letter and circumcision' renders the Greek dia of attendant circumstance, denoting possession of the written law and circumcision.
- **2:29** — The Greek phrase 'en pneumati' can refer either to the Holy Spirit ('by the Spirit') or to the inner spiritual realm ('in spirit'). The mention of 'praise' also plays on the Hebrew meaning of the name Judah (Genesis 29:35).
