## Text

**7:1** Or are you unaware, brethren—for I speak to those who know law—that the law rules over a man for as long as he lives?

**7:2** For a married woman is bound by law to her living husband; yet if the husband dies, she is released from the law concerning the husband.

**7:3** Therefore, while her husband is living, she shall be called an adulteress if she is joined to another man; yet if her husband dies, she is free from that law, so that she is not an adulteress when she is joined to another man.

**7:4** So then, my brethren, you also were put to death to the law through the body of Christ, so that you might be joined to another—to Him who was raised from the dead—in order that we might bear fruit to God.

**7:5** For when we were in the flesh, the passions of sins, brought about through the law, were working in our members to bear fruit to death.

**7:6** Yet now we have been released from the law, having died to that in which we were held captive, so that we serve in newness of the Spirit and not in oldness of the letter.

**7:7** What shall we say then? Is the law sin? Far be it! Rather, I would not have come to know sin except through the law; indeed, I would not have known coveting had the law not said, "You shall not covet."

**7:8** Yet sin, taking opportunity through the commandment, produced in me every covetous desire; for apart from law, sin is dead.

**7:9** Once I was alive apart from law; yet when the commandment came, sin sprang to life,

**7:10** and I died; and the commandment which was for life was found in my case to result in death.

**7:11** For sin, seizing opportunity through the commandment, deceived me, and through it slew me.

**7:12** Thus the law indeed is holy, and the commandment is holy, righteous, and good.

**7:13** Did that which is good, then, become death to me? Far be it! Rather, it was sin, that it might be shown to be sin, working death in me through what is good, so that through the commandment sin might become sinful beyond measure.

**7:14** For we know that the law is spiritual, yet I am of flesh, sold under sin.

**7:15** For what I perform, I do not understand; for it is not what I desire that I practice, but what I hate, that I do.

**7:16** Now if I do what I do not desire, I concur with the law that it is good.

**7:17** As it is, then, it is no longer I who perform it, but sin dwelling within me.

**7:18** For I know that in me—that is, in my flesh—no good thing dwells; for the will to do it is present with me, yet the performing of what is good is not.

**7:19** For I do not do the good that I desire, rather the evil that I do not desire, this I practice.

**7:20** Now if I do that which I do not desire, it is no longer I who perform it, but sin dwelling within me.

**7:21** I find therefore this law, that when I desire to do what is good, evil is right at hand with me.

**7:22** For I take delight in the law of God in my inner man,

**7:23** yet I behold another law in my members, waging war against the law of my mind and leading me captive into the law of sin which is in my members.

**7:24** What a wretched man I am! Who shall deliver me from the body of this death?

**7:25** Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord! Accordingly, I myself serve the law of God with the mind, yet with the flesh the law of sin.

## Translator Notes

- **7:6** — The earliest manuscripts read the plural participle apothanontes ("having died" to that which held us), whereas later manuscripts read the singular apothanontos ("that being dead").
- **7:21** — Commentators debate whether ton nomon ("this law") refers to a general principle operating in the speaker or specifically to the Mosaic law.
- **7:25** — The Greek critical text reads charis to theo ("Thanks be to God"), whereas later manuscripts read eucharisto to theo ("I thank God").
