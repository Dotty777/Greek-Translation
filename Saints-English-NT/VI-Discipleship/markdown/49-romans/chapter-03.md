## Text

**3:1** What advantage, then, belongs to the Jew, or what is the benefit of circumcision?

**3:2** Much in every way. First of all, because the oracles of God were entrusted to them.

**3:3** For what if some were unfaithful? Will their unfaithfulness make void the faithfulness of God?

**3:4** May it never be! Rather, let God prove true, and every man a liar, just as it is written: "That You may be justified in Your words, and shall prevail when You are judged."

**3:5** But if our unrighteousness confirms the righteousness of God, what shall we say? Is God unrighteous who inflicts wrath? I speak in human terms.

**3:6** May it never be! For otherwise, how shall God judge the world?

**3:7** Yet if through my falsehood the truth of God has abounded to His glory, why am I also still judged as a sinner?

**3:8** And why not say—as we are slandered and as some assert that we say—"Let us do evil that good may come"? Their judgment is just.

**3:9** What then? Are we at any advantage? Not at all; for we have already charged both Jews and Greeks that all are under sin,

**3:10** just as it is written: "There is none who is righteous, not even one;

**3:11** there is none who understands, there is none who seeks out God.

**3:12** All have turned aside; together they have become worthless; there is none who does goodness, not so much as one.

**3:13** Their throat is an open grave; with their tongues they have dealt deceitfully; the venom of asps is beneath their lips;

**3:14** whose mouth is filled with cursing and bitterness.

**3:15** Their feet are swift to shed blood;

**3:16** ruin and wretchedness are in their paths,

**3:17** and they have not known the way of peace.

**3:18** No fear of God is before their eyes."

**3:19** Now we know that whatever the law says, it speaks to those who are under the law, so that every mouth may be silenced and the whole world may become accountable to God.

**3:20** Because by works of the law no flesh shall be justified before Him; for through the law comes the knowledge of sin.

**3:21** But now, apart from the law, the righteousness of God has been manifested, attested by the Law and the Prophets—

**3:22** even the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ, for all who believe; for there is no distinction.

**3:23** For all sinned and fall short of the glory of God,

**3:24** being justified as a free gift by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus,

**3:25** whom God put forward as a propitiation through faith in His blood, for a demonstration of His righteousness, because of the passing over of the sins committed beforehand

**3:26** in the forbearance of God, for the demonstration of His righteousness at the present time, so that He may be both just and the One who justifies the one who has faith in Jesus.

**3:27** Where, then, is boasting? It has been shut out. By what kind of law? That of works? No, but by a law of faith.

**3:28** For we reckon that a man is justified by faith apart from works of the law.

**3:29** Or is God the God of Jews alone? Is He not of Gentiles also? Yes, of Gentiles also,

**3:30** since indeed God is one, who shall justify the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith.

**3:31** Do we then nullify the law through faith? May it never be! Rather, we uphold the law.

## Translator Notes

- **3:9** — The Greek verb proechometha can be read as middle ("Are we at any advantage?") or passive ("Are we at a disadvantage?"); the theme of Jewish privilege from 3:1 favors the middle sense.
- **3:22** — The Greek phrase dia pisteos Iesou Christou is rendered "through faith in Jesus Christ" (objective genitive), though some understand it as "through the faithfulness of Jesus Christ" (subjective genitive). The critical Greek text reads "for all who believe" without the later expansion "and upon all."
- **3:25** — Greek hilasterion ("propitiation") is the Septuagint term for the mercy seat atop the ark of the covenant where atoning blood was sprinkled, expressing both atonement and the turning away of wrath.
- **3:28** — The critical Greek text reads gar ("For") rather than oun ("Therefore").
