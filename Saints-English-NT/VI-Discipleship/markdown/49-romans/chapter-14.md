## Text

**14:1** Now receive the one who is weak in faith, not for debates over opinions.

**14:2** One has faith to eat all things, while the one who is weak eats vegetables.

**14:3** Do not let the one who eats despise the one who does not eat, and do not let the one who does not eat judge the one who eats, for God has received him.

**14:4** Who are you to judge the household servant of another? Before his own master he stands or falls; yet he shall be made to stand, for the Lord is able to make him stand.

**14:5** One regards one day above another, while another regards every day alike; let each person be fully persuaded in his own mind.

**14:6** The one who observes the day observes it to the Lord. And the one who eats eats to the Lord, for he gives thanks to God; and the one who does not eat, to the Lord he does not eat, and he gives thanks to God.

**14:7** For no one among us lives to himself, and no one dies to himself;

**14:8** for whether we live, we live to the Lord, or whether we die, we die to the Lord. Therefore, whether we live or whether we die, we belong to the Lord.

**14:9** For to this purpose Christ died and lived again, that He might be Lord over both the dead and the living.

**14:10** But you, why do you judge your brother? Or you also, why do you hold your brother in contempt? For we shall all stand before the judgment seat of God.

**14:11** For it is written: "As I live," says the Lord, "to Me every knee shall bow, and every tongue shall confess to God."

**14:12** Therefore, each one of us shall render an account concerning himself to God.

**14:13** Therefore let us no longer judge one another, but rather resolve this: not to place a stumbling block or a snare before a brother.

**14:14** I know and am persuaded in the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean of itself; yet to the one who reckons anything to be unclean, to him it is unclean.

**14:15** For if because of food your brother is grieved, you are no longer walking according to love. Do not by your food destroy that one for whom Christ died.

**14:16** Therefore do not let your good be reviled.

**14:17** For the kingdom of God is not in food and drink, but in righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit;

**14:18** for the one who serves Christ in this way is well-pleasing to God and approved among men.

**14:19** So then let us pursue the things of peace and the things that build up one another.

**14:20** Do not for the sake of food tear down the work of God. All things indeed are clean, yet it is evil for the man who causes stumbling by his eating.

**14:21** It is good not to eat meat, nor to drink wine, nor to do anything by which your brother stumbles, or is ensnared, or becomes weak.

**14:22** The faith that you hold, keep to yourself before God. Blessed is the one who does not judge himself in what he approves;

**14:23** but the one who doubts stands condemned if he eats, because it is not from faith; and everything that is not from faith is sin.

## Translator Notes

- **14:6** — The Greek critical text omits the additional clause "and the one who does not observe the day does not observe it to the Lord," which appears in later manuscripts.
- **14:10** — The Greek critical text reads "the judgment seat of God" rather than "the judgment seat of Christ," a later variant likely harmonized with 2 Corinthians 5:10.
- **14:20** — The Greek phrase rendered "causes stumbling by his eating" (literally "eats through stumbling") is debated and may mean either causing a fellow believer to stumble or eating against one's own troubled conscience.
- **14:21** — The Greek text here retains the fuller threefold sequence "stumbles, or is ensnared, or becomes weak," whereas some manuscripts contain only "stumbles."
- **14:24 *(additional)*** — Text-critical double brackets ⟦ ⟧ are preserved verbatim from the Saints Greek Critical Text; some Greek manuscripts place this doxology at the end of chapter 14 rather than 16:25-27.
