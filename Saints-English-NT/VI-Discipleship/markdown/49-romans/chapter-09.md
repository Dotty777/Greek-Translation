## Text

**9:1** I speak the truth in Christ, I do not lie, my conscience bearing witness with me in the Holy Spirit,

**9:2** that I have great grief and unceasing anguish in my heart.

**9:3** For I could wish myself to be accursed from Christ on behalf of my brethren, my kinsmen according to the flesh,

**9:4** who are Israelites, to whom belong the adoption as sons, and the glory, and the covenants, and the giving of the law, and the sacred service, and the promises;

**9:5** whose are the fathers, and from whom comes the Christ according to the flesh—He who is over all, God blessed forevermore. Amen.

**9:6** Now it is not as though the word of God has failed; for not all those who are of Israel are Israel,

**9:7** neither, because they are the seed of Abraham, are they all children; rather, "In Isaac shall your seed be named."

**9:8** That is, the children of the flesh—these are not the children of God; rather, the children of the promise are reckoned as the seed.

**9:9** For this is a word of promise: "At this season I will come, and Sarah shall have a son."

**9:10** And not only so, but also when Rebekah had conceived by one man, our father Isaac—

**9:11** for while they were not yet born and had done nothing good or evil, so that the purpose of God according to election might abide,

**9:12** not from works, but from Him who calls—it was said to her, "The elder shall serve the younger,"

**9:13** just as it is written, "Jacob I loved, yet Esau I hated."

**9:14** What then shall we say? Is there unrighteousness with God? Far be it!

**9:15** For He says to Moses, "I will show mercy to whomever I show mercy, and I will show compassion to whomever I show compassion."

**9:16** Therefore it does not depend on him who wills, nor on him who runs, but on God who shows mercy.

**9:17** For the Scripture says to Pharaoh, "For this very reason I raised you up, that I might display My power in you, and that My name might be proclaimed in all the earth."

**9:18** Therefore, on whomever He wills He shows mercy, and whomever He wills He hardens.

**9:19** You will say to me then, "Why then does He still find fault? For who has withstood His will?"

**9:20** On the contrary, O man, who are you to answer back to God? Shall that which is molded say to him who molded it, "Why did you make me like this?"

**9:21** Or does the potter not have authority over the clay, from the same lump to fashion one vessel for honor and another for dishonor?

**9:22** What if God, desiring to display His wrath and make His power known, bore with much longsuffering vessels of wrath fitted for destruction,

**9:23** and in order that He might make known the riches of His glory upon vessels of mercy, which He prepared beforehand for glory—

**9:24** even us whom He also called, not from among the Jews only, but also from among the Gentiles?

**9:25** As He also says in Hosea: "I will call those who are not My people 'My people,' and her who was not loved 'Beloved.'"

**9:26** "And it shall be, in the place where it was spoken to them, 'You are not My people,' there they shall be called sons of the living God."

**9:27** And Isaiah cries out concerning Israel: "Though the number of the sons of Israel be as the sand of the sea, it is the remnant that shall be saved;

**9:28** for the Lord will execute His word upon the earth, finishing it and cutting it short."

**9:29** And just as Isaiah foretold: "Had the Lord of Sabaoth not left us a seed, we would have become as Sodom, and we would have been likened to Gomorrah."

**9:30** What then shall we say? That Gentiles, who were not pursuing righteousness, attained righteousness, even the righteousness that is by faith;

**9:31** yet Israel, pursuing a law of righteousness, did not attain to that law.

**9:32** Why? Because it was not by faith, but as if by works. They stumbled over the stone of stumbling,

**9:33** just as it is written: "Behold, I set in Zion a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense, and he who believes on Him shall not be put to shame."

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — The Greek punctuation of the closing clause is debated; it may be read as a direct affirmation of Christ's deity ("who is over all, God blessed forevermore") or as a separate doxology addressed to God the Father ("May God, who is over all, be blessed forevermore").
- **9:22** — The Greek participle rendered "fitted" can be read as passive ("prepared by God") or middle ("having fitted themselves"), contrasting with the active verb "prepared beforehand" in 9:23.
- **9:28** — Following the earliest Greek witnesses, this text presents the concise form of Isaiah 10:22-23; later manuscripts expand the verse to align more closely with the fuller Septuagint wording.
- **9:29** — "Sabaoth" preserves the Greek transliteration of the Hebrew word for "hosts" or "armies," a Septuagint title designating God as ruler of the heavenly hosts.
