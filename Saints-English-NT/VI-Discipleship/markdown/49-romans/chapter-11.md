## Text

**11:1** I say then, has God cast off His people? May it never be! For I myself am an Israelite, from the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.

**11:2** God has not cast off His people whom He foreknew. Or do you not know what the Scripture says concerning Elijah, how he pleads with God against Israel?

**11:3** "Lord, they have slain Your prophets, they have overthrown Your altars, and I alone have been left, and they are seeking my life."

**11:4** But what does the divine reply say to him? "I have kept for Myself seven thousand men who have not bent the knee to Baal."

**11:5** In the same way, then, at this present time also, there has come to be a remnant according to the election of grace.

**11:6** But if by grace, it is no longer by works; otherwise grace ceases to be grace.

**11:7** What then? That which Israel earnestly seeks, it did not obtain; yet the elect obtained it, while the rest were hardened,

**11:8** just as it is written: "God gave them a spirit of stupor, eyes not to see and ears not to hear, down to this present day."

**11:9** And David says: "May their table become a snare and a trap, and a stumbling block and a recompense to them;

**11:10** may their eyes be darkened so as not to see, and bow down their back continually."

**11:11** I say then, did they stumble so that they should fall? May it never be! Rather, by their transgression salvation has come to the Gentiles, to provoke them to jealousy.

**11:12** Now if their transgression is riches for the world, and their loss is riches for the Gentiles, how much more will their fullness be!

**11:13** Now I speak to you who are Gentiles: since, then, I am an apostle of the Gentiles, I magnify my ministry,

**11:14** if somehow I may provoke my own flesh to jealousy and save some from among them.

**11:15** For if their casting away brings reconciliation for the world, what will their reception be but life from the dead?

**11:16** And if the firstfruit is holy, so also is the lump; and if the root is holy, so also are the branches.

**11:17** Yet if some of the branches were broken off, and you, being a wild olive shoot, were grafted in among them and became a partaker of the root, of the richness of the olive tree,

**11:18** do not boast over the branches. But if you do boast—it is not you who bear the root, but the root bears you.

**11:19** Then you will say, "Branches were broken off in order that I might be grafted in."

**11:20** True enough! By unbelief they were broken off, whereas you stand firm by faith. Do not be high-minded, but fear;

**11:21** for if God did not spare the natural branches, He will not spare you either.

**11:22** Behold, then, the kindness and severity of God: toward those who fell, severity; but toward you, the kindness of God, if you abide in that kindness; otherwise you also will be cut off.

**11:23** And they too, if they do not remain in unbelief, will be grafted in; for God has power to graft them in again.

**11:24** For if you were cut from what is by nature a wild olive tree, and were grafted contrary to nature into a cultivated olive tree, how much more shall these, the natural branches, be grafted into their own olive tree!

**11:25** For I do not wish you to be unaware of this mystery, brethren, lest you be wise in your own estimation: a partial hardening has come upon Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has entered in,

**11:26** and thus all Israel shall be saved, even as it is written: "Out of Zion shall come the Deliverer; He shall turn away ungodliness from Jacob.

**11:27** And this is the covenant from Me to them, when I take away their sins."

**11:28** With respect to the gospel, they are enemies for your sake; yet with respect to election, they are beloved because of the fathers.

**11:29** For the gracious gifts and the calling of God are never revoked.

**11:30** For just as you once disobeyed God, yet now have been shown mercy because of their disobedience,

**11:31** so these also have now disobeyed, in order that through the mercy shown to you they themselves may now be shown mercy.

**11:32** For God has shut up all in disobedience, so that He may show mercy to all.

**11:33** O the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable His judgments, and how untraceable His ways!

**11:34** For who has understood the mind of the Lord, or who has served as His counselor?

**11:35** Or who has given beforehand to Him, so that it shall be repaid to him?

**11:36** For from Him and through Him and to Him are all things; to Him be the glory throughout all ages. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **11:4** — The Greek places the feminine article before Baal, reflecting the Septuagint convention of substituting the feminine noun for shame (aischyne) when reading the pagan deity's name aloud.
- **11:6** — The earliest manuscripts end the verse here; later manuscripts add a parallel balancing clause stating that if it is by works, it is no longer grace, otherwise work is no longer work.
- **11:17** — The Greek links two successive genitives without a conjunction (of the root, of the richness of the olive tree), emphasizing that Gentile believers share in the nourishing richness of Israel's covenant root.
- **11:31** — Many early manuscripts include the second "now" before "be shown mercy," highlighting the present-age reality of Israel's reception of mercy; the dative phrase "through the mercy shown to you" is fronted before the purpose clause.
