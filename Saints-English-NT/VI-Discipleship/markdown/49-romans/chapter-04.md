## Text

**4:1** What then shall we say that Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh, has found?

**4:2** For if Abraham was justified by works, he has a ground for boasting, yet not before God.

**4:3** For what does the Scripture say? "Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness."

**4:4** Now to the one who works, the wage is not reckoned according to grace, but as a debt;

**4:5** but to the one who does not work, yet believes in Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned as righteousness,

**4:6** just as David also speaks of the blessedness of the man to whom God reckons righteousness apart from works:

**4:7** "Blessed are they whose iniquities have been forgiven, and whose sins have been covered;

**4:8** blessed is the man to whom the Lord shall by no means reckon sin."

**4:9** Is this blessing then upon the circumcised, or upon the uncircumcised also? For we say, "Faith was reckoned to Abraham as righteousness."

**4:10** How then was it reckoned? While he was in circumcision, or in uncircumcision? Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision.

**4:11** And he received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness of the faith he had while uncircumcised, so that he might be the father of all who believe without being circumcised, that righteousness might be reckoned to them,

**4:12** and the father of the circumcised—for those who are not merely of the circumcision, but who also walk in the footsteps of the faith which our father Abraham had while in uncircumcision.

**4:13** For the promise to Abraham or to his seed, that he should be heir of the world, was not through the law, but through the righteousness of faith.

**4:14** For if those who are of the law are heirs, faith has been rendered empty and the promise has been nullified;

**4:15** for the law produces wrath; yet where there is no law, neither is there transgression.

**4:16** For this reason it is by faith, that it might be according to grace, so that the promise may be guaranteed to all the seed—not only to those who are of the law, but also to those who are of the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all

**4:17** (just as it is written: "I have appointed you a father of many nations") before God, in whom he believed—the One who gives life to the dead and calls the things that are not as though they were;

**4:18** who, beyond hope, believed in hope, that he might become a father of many nations, according to what had been spoken: "So shall your seed be."

**4:19** And without growing weak in faith, he considered his own body, now already dead—being about a hundred years old—as well as the deadness of Sarah's womb;

**4:20** yet toward the promise of God he did not waver through unbelief, but grew strong in faith, giving glory to God,

**4:21** and being fully assured that what He had promised, He was also able to do.

**4:22** Therefore also it was reckoned to him as righteousness.

**4:23** Now it was not written on his account alone that it was reckoned to him,

**4:24** but on our account also, to whom it is to be reckoned—those who believe in Him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,

**4:25** who was delivered over on account of our trespasses and was raised on account of our justification.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — The critical Greek text reads propatora ("forefather") rather than patera ("father"), and "according to the flesh" may modify either "our forefather" or "has found."
- **4:19** — The critical Greek text reads katenoesen ("he considered") without the negative ou ("did not consider") found in later manuscripts, showing that Abraham faced his physical condition squarely without weakening in faith.
- **4:25** — Both halves of the verse employ Greek dia with the accusative ("on account of"), pairing Christ's being delivered over with our trespasses and His resurrection with our justification.
