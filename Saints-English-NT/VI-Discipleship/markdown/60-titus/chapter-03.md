## Text

**3:1** Remind them to be subject to rulers and authorities, to be obedient, to be ready for every good work,

**3:2** to malign no one, to be peaceable and forbearing, displaying all meekness toward all mankind.

**3:3** For we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, and led astray, enslaved to various desires and pleasures, spending our days in malice and envy, detestable and hating one another.

**3:4** But when the kindness and love for mankind of God our Savior appeared,

**3:5** not by works done in righteousness that we ourselves performed, but according to His mercy He saved us, through the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit,

**3:6** whom He poured out bountifully upon us through Jesus Christ our Savior,

**3:7** so that, having been justified by His grace, we may become heirs in keeping with the hope of eternal life.

**3:8** This saying is trustworthy, and concerning these things I desire you to speak with firm conviction, so that those who have believed in God may be careful to devote themselves to good works. These things are excellent and profitable for mankind.

**3:9** But shun foolish controversies, genealogies, dissensions, and quarrels about the law, for they are unprofitable and futile.

**3:10** After a first and second warning, reject a factious person,

**3:11** knowing that such a one has been perverted and goes on sinning, standing self-condemned.

**3:12** When I send Artemas to you, or Tychicus, make every effort to come to me at Nicopolis, for I have decided to winter there.

**3:13** Diligently help Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their journey, so that they may lack nothing.

**3:14** And let our own people also learn to devote themselves to good works to meet urgent needs, so that they may not be unfruitful.

**3:15** All who are with me send you greetings. Greet those who love us in the faith. Grace be with all of you.

## Translator Notes

- **3:5** — The phrase 'washing of regeneration' (loutron palingenesias) may also be rendered 'washing of rebirth,' and the genitive 'of the Holy Spirit' may qualify 'renewal' or both 'washing' and 'renewal.'
- **3:10** — The Greek hairetikon anthropon denotes a 'factious' or 'divisive' person who stirs up schisms within the church rather than the later technical sense of a doctrinal heretic alone.
- **3:15** — The earliest Greek manuscripts conclude the letter with 'Grace be with all of you,' without a final 'Amen.'
