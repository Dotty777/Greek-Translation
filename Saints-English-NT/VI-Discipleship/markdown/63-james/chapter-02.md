## Text

**2:1** My brethren, do you hold the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with acts of partiality?

**2:2** For if a man wearing a gold ring, in splendid apparel, enters your assembly, and a poor man in filthy garments also enters,

**2:3** and you pay special regard to the one wearing the splendid apparel and say, "Sit here in an honorable place," while to the poor man you say, "Stand over there," or, "Sit beneath my footstool,"

**2:4** have you not made distinctions among yourselves and become judges with evil reasonings?

**2:5** Hear, my beloved brethren: did not God choose those who are poor in the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom which He has promised to those who love Him?

**2:6** Yet you have dishonored the poor man. Are not the rich the ones who oppress you, and do they not themselves drag you into the courts?

**2:7** Do they not blaspheme the honorable name that has been invoked over you?

**2:8** If indeed you are fulfilling the royal law according to the Scripture, "You shall love your neighbor as yourself," you do well;

**2:9** yet if you show partiality, you commit sin, being convicted by the law as transgressors.

**2:10** For whoever keeps the entire law, yet stumbles in one point, has become guilty of all.

**2:11** For the One who said, "Do not commit adultery," also said, "Do not murder." So if you do not commit adultery, yet commit murder, you have become a transgressor of the law.

**2:12** Thus speak and thus act, as those who are to be judged by the law of liberty.

**2:13** For judgment shall be merciless to the one who has shown no mercy; mercy exults over judgment.

**2:14** What is the profit, my brethren, if someone claims to have faith, yet has no works? Can that faith save him?

**2:15** If a brother or sister is naked and lacking in daily sustenance,

**2:16** and one of you says to them, "Go in peace, be warmed and be filled," yet you do not give them the things needful for the body, what is the profit?

**2:17** In the same way faith also, if it has no works, is dead in itself.

**2:18** Yet someone will say, "You have faith, and I have works." Show me your faith apart from works, and I will show you faith by my works.

**2:19** You believe that God is one? You do well; even the demons believe, and shudder.

**2:20** Yet do you wish to learn, O empty man, that faith apart from works is barren?

**2:21** Was not our father Abraham justified by works when he offered up his son Isaac upon the altar?

**2:22** You see that faith was working alongside his works, and by works faith was brought to completion,

**2:23** and the Scripture was fulfilled which says, "And Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness," and he was called the friend of God.

**2:24** You see that a man is justified by works, and not by faith alone.

**2:25** And in like manner, was not Rahab the harlot also justified by works, when she welcomed the messengers and sent them out by another way?

**2:26** For just as the body apart from the spirit is dead, so also faith apart from works is dead.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — Rendered as a question following the punctuation of the Greek source text, though the verb form echete may also be read as a prohibition ("Do not hold...").
- **2:18** — The extent of the speaker's quotation is debated; here it concludes after "and I have works," and the critical text reads "apart from works" rather than "by your works."
- **2:20** — Translates the critical text reading arge ("barren" or "idle") rather than the later variant nekra ("dead").
- **2:23** — Cites Genesis 15:6 according to the Septuagint wording.
