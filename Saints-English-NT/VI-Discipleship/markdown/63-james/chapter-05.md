## Text

**5:1** Come now, you who are rich, weep with loud wailing over your miseries that are coming upon you.

**5:2** Your wealth has decayed, and your garments have become moth-eaten;

**5:3** your gold and your silver have corroded, and their corrosion shall stand as a witness against you and shall devour your flesh; as fire you have laid up treasure in the last days.

**5:4** Behold, the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields—kept back by you—cry aloud, and the cries of those who reaped have entered into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth.

**5:5** You have dwelt in luxury upon the earth and indulged in excess; you have fattened your hearts in a day of slaughter.

**5:6** You have condemned, you have murdered the righteous one. Does he not resist you?

**5:7** Be patient, therefore, brethren, until the coming of the Lord. Behold, the farmer awaits the precious fruit of the earth, waiting patiently over it until it receives the early and the late rain.

**5:8** Be patient, you also; make your hearts steadfast, for the coming of the Lord has drawn near.

**5:9** Do not groan against one another, brethren, lest you be judged; behold, the Judge stands before the doors.

**5:10** Take as an example of suffering hardship and of patience, brethren, the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord.

**5:11** Behold, we call blessed those who endured. You have heard of the steadfast endurance of Job, and you have seen the purpose of the Lord—that the Lord is full of tender compassion and merciful.

**5:12** Above all things, however, my brethren, do not swear—neither by heaven, nor by earth, nor by any other oath; rather, let your "Yes" be yes and your "No" be no, lest you fall under judgment.

**5:13** Is anyone among you suffering hardship? Let him pray. Is anyone of good cheer? Let him sing praises.

**5:14** Is anyone among you sick? Let him summon the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord.

**5:15** And the prayer of faith shall save the one who is ailing, and the Lord shall raise him up; and even if he has committed sins, he shall be forgiven.

**5:16** Confess your sins, therefore, to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The supplication of a righteous one avails greatly in its working.

**5:17** Elijah was a man of like nature with us, and he prayed fervently that it might not rain; and it did not rain upon the earth for three years and six months.

**5:18** And again he prayed, and the heaven gave rain, and the earth brought forth its fruit.

**5:19** My brethren, if anyone among you strays from the truth and someone turns him back,

**5:20** let him know that whoever turns a sinner back from the error of his way shall save his soul from death and cover a multitude of sins.

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — Following the punctuation of the Greek source text, the phrase "as fire" opens the final clause ("as fire you have laid up treasure"); other editions instead join it to the preceding clause ("shall devour your flesh like fire").
- **5:4** — "Sabaoth" is the Greek transliteration of the Hebrew word for "Hosts" or "Armies" (Kyriou Sabaoth, "Lord of Hosts").
- **5:6** — The Greek source text punctuates the final clause as a rhetorical question ("Does he not resist you?"); other editions punctuate it as a statement ("He does not resist you").
- **5:20** — Following the Greek critical text, the verse reads "his soul" (referring to the straying sinner's soul), whereas some manuscripts omit the possessive pronoun and read simply "a soul."
