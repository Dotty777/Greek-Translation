## Text

**4:1** From where do wars and from where do fights arise among you? Is it not from this—from your pleasures that wage war in your members?

**4:2** You crave, and you do not have; you murder and burn with envy, yet you are unable to obtain; you fight and wage war. You do not possess because you do not ask.

**4:3** You ask and do not receive, because you ask wickedly, that you may spend it upon your pleasures.

**4:4** You adulteresses! Do you not know that friendship with the world is enmity against God? Whoever, therefore, determines to be a friend of the world becomes an enemy of God.

**4:5** Or do you think that the Scripture speaks in vain, "With jealousy He longs for the spirit that He caused to dwell in us"?

**4:6** Yet He grants greater grace. Therefore it says, "God stands against the proud, but to the humble He gives grace."

**4:7** Be subject, therefore, to God; but withstand the devil, and he will flee from you.

**4:8** Draw near to God, and He will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, O sinners, and purify your hearts, O double-minded.

**4:9** Be afflicted, and mourn, and weep; let your laughter be transformed into mourning, and your joy into dejection.

**4:10** Humble yourselves in the presence of the Lord, and He will exalt you.

**4:11** Do not speak evil against one another, brethren. Whoever speaks evil against a brother or judges his brother speaks evil against the law and judges the law; yet if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law, but a judge.

**4:12** There is one Lawgiver and Judge, the One who is able to save and to destroy; who then are you, who judge your neighbor?

**4:13** Come now, you who say, "Today or tomorrow we will travel to such-and-such a city, spend a year there, engage in trade, and gain a profit,"

**4:14** whereas you do not know what tomorrow holds, or what your life is; for you are a vapor that appears for a short while and afterward vanishes away.

**4:15** Rather, you should say, "If the Lord wills, we shall both live and do this or that."

**4:16** As it is, however, you boast in your arrogant pretensions; all boasting of this kind is evil.

**4:17** Therefore, to the one who knows to do good and does not perform it, to him it is sin.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — The Greek addresses the readers in the feminine plural (moichalides, "adulteresses"), evoking Old Testament imagery of covenant unfaithfulness to God; some later manuscripts expand this to "adulterers and adulteresses."
- **4:5** — The Greek syntax is famously debated and admits two main renderings: either God jealously longs for the human spirit He placed within us, or the Spirit whom He caused to dwell in us yearns with jealousy (following the critical verb katokisen, "caused to dwell").
- **4:14** — Following the Greek critical text, the second clause reads in the second person plural ("for you are a vapor") rather than the third person singular ("for it is a vapor") found in some later manuscripts.
