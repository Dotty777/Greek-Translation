## Text

**3:1** Not many of you should become teachers, my brethren, knowing that we shall receive a weightier judgment.

**3:2** For we all stumble in many ways. If anyone does not stumble in word, this is a complete man, able to bridle the entire body as well.

**3:3** Now if we put bits into the mouths of horses so that they obey us, we also direct their entire body.

**3:4** Behold also the ships: though they are so great and are driven by fierce winds, they are steered by a very small rudder wherever the impulse of the helmsman wills.

**3:5** So also the tongue is a small member, yet it boasts of great things. Behold how small a fire kindles how vast a forest!

**3:6** And the tongue is a fire, the very world of iniquity. The tongue is set among our members, staining the entire body and setting ablaze the course of existence, and is itself set on fire by hell.

**3:7** For every species of wild beast and bird, of creeping thing and creature of the sea, is being tamed and has been tamed by mankind;

**3:8** yet no one among mankind is able to tame the tongue; it is a restless evil, full of deadly venom.

**3:9** With it we bless the Lord and Father, and with it we curse men who have been made in the likeness of God.

**3:10** From the same mouth come forth blessing and cursing; my brethren, these things should not happen thus.

**3:11** Does a spring gush forth fresh water and bitter from the same opening?

**3:12** Can a fig tree, my brethren, produce olives, or a vine produce figs? Neither can salt water yield fresh water.

**3:13** Who among you is wise and discerning? By his noble conduct let him show his works in the meekness of wisdom.

**3:14** Yet if you harbor bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your heart, do not boast and lie against the truth.

**3:15** Such wisdom is not that which comes down from above, but is earthly, unspiritual, demonic;

**3:16** for where jealousy and selfish ambition are, there is disorder and every vile practice.

**3:17** But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, forbearing, readily yielding, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial, and unfeigned.

**3:18** And the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace for those who make peace.

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — Translates the critical text reading ei de ("Now if") rather than the later variant idou ("Behold").
- **3:6** — "The course of existence" renders the rare Greek phrase ton trochon tes geneseos (literally "the wheel of birth/existence"), and "hell" translates geenna (Gehenna).
- **3:8** — Translates the critical text reading akatastaton ("restless" or "unstable") rather than the later variant akatascheton ("unruly").
- **3:18** — The Greek dative tois poiousin eirenen may be understood either as "for those who make peace" (dative of advantage) or "by those who make peace" (dative of agency).
