## Text

**1:1** James, a bondservant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes that are in the dispersion: Greetings.

**1:2** Regard it as all joy, my brethren, whenever you fall into diverse trials,

**1:3** knowing that the proving of your faith brings forth endurance.

**1:4** And let endurance have its complete work, so that you may be complete and whole, lacking in nothing.

**1:5** Now if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask from God, who gives to all generously and without upbraiding, and it shall be given to him.

**1:6** Yet let him ask in faith, doubting nothing; for he who doubts resembles a billow of the sea, driven by the wind and tossed about.

**1:7** For that man must not suppose that he will receive anything from the Lord,

**1:8** being a double-minded man, unsteady in all his ways.

**1:9** Now let the lowly brother boast in his high estate,

**1:10** and the rich man in his humiliation, because like a flower of the grass he shall pass away.

**1:11** For the sun rises with its scorching heat and withers the grass, and its flower falls off, and the beauty of its appearance perishes; so also the rich man shall fade away in his endeavors.

**1:12** Blessed is the man who endures trial; for once he has been proved, he shall receive the crown of life, which He has promised to those who love Him.

**1:13** Let no one who is tempted say, "I am tempted from God"; for God cannot be tempted by evil things, and He Himself tempts no one.

**1:14** Rather, each one is tempted when he is drawn away and lured by his own desire;

**1:15** then desire, having conceived, bears sin; and sin, when it has reached its full measure, brings forth death.

**1:16** Do not be led astray, my beloved brethren.

**1:17** Every good bestowal and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no shifting nor shadow cast by turning.

**1:18** Of His own purpose He brought us forth by a word of truth, so that we might be a kind of firstfruits of His created works.

**1:19** Know this, my beloved brethren. And let every man be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath,

**1:20** for the wrath of man does not accomplish the righteousness of God.

**1:21** Therefore, putting away all filthiness and abundance of wickedness, receive with meekness the implanted word, which has power to save your souls.

**1:22** And become doers of the word, and not hearers alone, deluding yourselves.

**1:23** For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, such a one resembles a man beholding his natural countenance in a mirror;

**1:24** for he beholds himself and departs, and at once forgets what manner of man he was.

**1:25** But he who looks intently into the perfect law, that of liberty, and abides by it, proving not a forgetful hearer but a doer of the work—this man shall be blessed in his doing.

**1:26** If anyone supposes himself to be religious, yet does not bridle his tongue, but deceives his own heart, this man's religion is vain.

**1:27** Religion pure and undefiled before God and the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unstained from the world.

## Translator Notes

- **1:12** — The Greek critical text reads "which He has promised" with an implied divine subject, whereas later manuscripts supply "the Lord."
- **1:17** — The phrasing draws on astronomical imagery ("Father of lights," "shifting," "shadow cast by turning") to contrast the unchanging God with the changing heavenly bodies.
- **1:19** — Translates the critical text reading iste ("Know this") rather than the later variant hoste ("So then").
- **1:25** — The participle parakypsas literally means to stoop down and peer intently, emphasizing sustained attention to the law of liberty.
