## Text

**1:1** Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, and our brother Timothy, to the church of God that is in Corinth, together with all the saints who are throughout all Achaia:

**1:2** Grace be to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:3** Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of compassions and the God of all consolation,

**1:4** who consoles us in all our affliction, so that we may be able to console those who are in any affliction through the consolation with which we ourselves are consoled by God.

**1:5** For just as the sufferings of Christ abound to us, so through Christ our consolation also abounds.

**1:6** Now if we are afflicted, it is for your consolation and salvation; and if we are consoled, it is for your consolation, which works effectively in the patient endurance of the same sufferings that we also suffer;

**1:7** and our hope for you is steadfast, knowing that as you are partakers of the sufferings, so also you are of the consolation.

**1:8** For we do not want you to be unaware, brethren, concerning the affliction that befell us in Asia: we were weighed down beyond measure, beyond our strength, so that we despaired even of life.

**1:9** Indeed, we ourselves had the sentence of death within us, so that we should not rely on ourselves, but on God who raises the dead,

**1:10** who delivered us from so great a death, and will deliver us; in Him we have set our hope that He will deliver us yet again,

**1:11** as you also join in helping us by supplication on our behalf, so that thanks may be given by many on our behalf for the gracious gift granted to us through many persons.

**1:12** For our boasting is this, the testimony of our conscience: that we conducted ourselves in the world in holiness and sincerity from God, not in fleshly wisdom but in the grace of God, and even more abundantly toward you.

**1:13** For we write nothing else to you than what you read or also understand; and I hope that you will understand to the full,

**1:14** just as you have also understood us in part, that we are your boast, even as you also are ours, in the day of our Lord Jesus.

**1:15** And with this confidence I intended to come to you first, so that you might receive a second favor,

**1:16** and to pass by way of you into Macedonia, and from Macedonia to return to you again, and to be sent on my way by you to Judea.

**1:17** Therefore, when I was intending this, did I act with fickleness? Or do I form my plans according to the flesh, so that with me there should be "Yes, yes" and "No, no"?

**1:18** But as God is faithful, our word to you is not "Yes" and "No."

**1:19** For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, who was proclaimed among you by us—by me and Silvanus and Timothy—was not "Yes" and "No," but in Him it has been "Yes."

**1:20** For however many promises of God there are, in Him is the "Yes"; therefore also through Him is the "Amen" to God, for glory through us.

**1:21** Now He who establishes us together with you in Christ, and anointed us, is God,

**1:22** who also set His seal upon us and gave us the pledge of the Spirit in our hearts.

**1:23** Moreover, I call upon God as witness against my own soul that it was to spare you that I did not come again to Corinth.

**1:24** Not that we exercise lordship over your faith, but we are fellow workers for your joy; for by faith you stand firm.

## Translator Notes

- **1:10** — The critical text reads the future "will deliver" twice (Greek rhysetai), whereas later manuscripts read the present "does deliver" in the first clause.
- **1:11** — Greek ek pollon prosopon literally means "from many faces" (or "persons"), referring to the many believers whose intercessions obtain God's gracious deliverance for Paul.
- **1:12** — The earliest manuscripts read "holiness" (hagioteti), whereas later Byzantine manuscripts read "simplicity" (haploteti).
- **1:20** — The critical text reads "therefore also through Him is the 'Amen'" (dio kai di' autou to Amen), emphasizing Christ as the mediator through whom believers affirm God's promises.
