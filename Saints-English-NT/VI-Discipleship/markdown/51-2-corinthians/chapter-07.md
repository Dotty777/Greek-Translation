## Text

**7:1** Therefore, having these promises, beloved, let us purify ourselves from every defilement of flesh and spirit, bringing holiness to perfection in the fear of God.

**7:2** Make room for us; we have wronged no one, we have ruined no one, we have defrauded no one.

**7:3** I do not speak by way of condemnation, for I have already said that you are in our hearts, to die together and to live together.

**7:4** Great is my candor toward you, great is my boasting concerning you; I am filled with comfort; I overflow exceedingly with joy in all our affliction.

**7:5** For indeed, when we came into Macedonia, our flesh had no relief, but we were afflicted in every way—conflicts outside, fears within—

**7:6** yet God, who comforts those who are cast down, comforted us through the arrival of Titus;

**7:7** and not only by his arrival, but also by the comfort with which he was comforted over you, while he reported to us your deep longing, your mourning, and your zeal on my behalf, so that I rejoiced all the more.

**7:8** For even if I caused you sorrow by the letter, I do not regret it; even though I did regret it (I see that that letter caused you sorrow, though only for a while),

**7:9** now I rejoice, not because you were made sorrowful, but because you were made sorrowful to repentance; for you were made sorrowful according to God, so that in nothing you might suffer loss from us.

**7:10** For sorrow according to God works repentance to salvation without regret, whereas the sorrow of the world produces death.

**7:11** For behold this very thing, that you were made sorrowful according to God: what great earnestness it produced in you—indeed, what defense, what indignation, what fear, what longing, what zeal, what avenging of wrong! In every respect you have demonstrated yourselves to be innocent in the matter.

**7:12** Therefore, although I wrote to you, it was not for the sake of the one who did the wrong, nor for the sake of the one who was wronged, but in order that your earnestness on our behalf might be made manifest to you in the sight of God.

**7:13** For this reason we have been comforted. And besides our comfort, we rejoiced all the more exceedingly over the joy of Titus, because his spirit has been refreshed by all of you;

**7:14** for if in anything I have boasted to him about you, I was not put to shame; rather, just as we spoke all things to you in truth, so also our boasting before Titus has proved to be truth.

**7:15** And his tender affection for you abounds all the more as he calls to mind the obedience of all of you, how you welcomed him with fear and trembling.

**7:16** I rejoice because in every respect I have confidence in you.

## Translator Notes

- **7:10** — The Greek adjective ametamelēton ("without regret") may modify either "repentance" or "salvation"; in either case, sorrow according to God yields an enduring turning that brings no remorse.
- **7:12** — The critical Greek text reads "your earnestness on our behalf" (tēn spoudēn hymōn tēn hyper hēmōn), whereas later manuscripts invert the pronouns to "our earnestness for you."
- **7:13** — The critical text concludes the opening sentence at "we have been comforted" and begins the next with "besides our comfort," whereas later manuscripts attach "by your comfort" directly to the preceding clause.
