## Text

**3:1** Are we beginning to commend ourselves again? Or do we need, as some do, letters of commendation to you or from you?

**3:2** You yourselves are our letter, inscribed upon our hearts, known and read by all men,

**3:3** plainly shown to be a letter of Christ, ministered by us, inscribed not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets that are hearts of flesh.

**3:4** Now such is the confidence that we have through Christ toward God.

**3:5** Not that we are sufficient in ourselves to account anything as coming from ourselves; rather, our sufficiency comes from God,

**3:6** who also made us sufficient to be ministers of a new covenant, not of the letter but of the Spirit; for the letter puts to death, while the Spirit makes alive.

**3:7** Now if the ministry of death, engraved in letters upon stones, came with glory, so that the sons of Israel could not gaze intently upon the face of Moses because of the glory of his countenance, a glory that was passing away,

**3:8** how shall the ministry of the Spirit not be even more in glory?

**3:9** For if the ministry of condemnation had glory, much more does the ministry of righteousness abound in glory.

**3:10** For indeed, what had been glorified has come to have no glory in this respect, on account of the surpassing glory.

**3:11** For if that which is passing away came through glory, much more does that which abides remain in glory.

**3:12** Therefore, having such a hope, we exercise great boldness,

**3:13** and are not like Moses, who would place a veil over his face so that the sons of Israel might not gaze steadily at the end of what was passing away.

**3:14** But their minds were hardened; for until this very day the same veil remains unlifted at the reading of the old covenant, because in Christ it is done away with.

**3:15** Indeed, even to this day, whenever Moses is read, a veil lies over their heart;

**3:16** yet whenever one turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away.

**3:17** And the Lord is the Spirit, and wherever the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty.

**3:18** And all of us, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord as in a mirror, are being transformed into the very same image from glory to glory, even as from the Lord, who is the Spirit.

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — The critical text places "hearts" in dative apposition ("tablets that are hearts of flesh"), echoing the new-covenant promises of Jeremiah 31:33 and Ezekiel 11:19; 36:26.
- **3:13** — Greek telos can mean either "termination" (the fading end of the old glory) or "goal" (Christ as the ultimate aim of the Mosaic covenant).
- **3:16** — Echoing Exodus 34:34, the verb "turns" has no expressed subject in Greek, enabling Paul to extend Moses's turning to the Lord to anyone who turns to Him.
- **3:18** — Greek katoptrizomenoi can mean either "beholding as in a mirror" or "reflecting as in a mirror"; the closing phrase apo kyriou pneumatos literally reads "from the Lord, Spirit," linking back to 3:17.
