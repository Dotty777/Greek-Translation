## Text

**13:1** This third time I am coming to you. By the mouth of two or three witnesses every matter shall be established.

**13:2** I have forewarned, and I forewarn—as when present the second time and now while absent—those who have sinned previously and all the rest, that if I come again I will not spare,

**13:3** since you are seeking proof of Christ, who speaks in me. He is not weak toward you, but is powerful in you.

**13:4** For indeed He was crucified in weakness, yet He lives by the power of God. For we also are weak in Him, yet we shall live with Him by the power of God toward you.

**13:5** Test yourselves as to whether you are in the faith; prove yourselves. Or do you not recognize about yourselves that Jesus Christ is in you?—unless indeed you are unapproved.

**13:6** But I hope that you shall know that we ourselves are not unapproved.

**13:7** Now we pray to God that you may do no evil at all, not that we ourselves may appear approved, but that you may do what is noble, even though we may be as unapproved.

**13:8** For we have no power to do anything against the truth, but only for the truth.

**13:9** For we rejoice whenever we are weak, and you are strong; this also we pray for—your restoration.

**13:10** For this reason I write these things while absent, lest when present I should act severely, according to the authority which the Lord gave me, for building up and not for pulling down.

**13:11** Finally, brethren, rejoice, be restored, be encouraged, be of the same mind, be at peace; and the God of love and peace shall be with you.

**13:12** Greet one another with a holy kiss. All the saints send greetings to you.

**13:13** May the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Spirit be with all of you.

## Translator Notes

- **13:4** — The Greek critical text reads simply 'For indeed He was crucified' without the conjunction 'though' found in some later manuscripts. The parallel prepositional phrases contrast the sphere of human weakness in Christ's crucifixion with the life-giving power of God in His resurrection.
- **13:7** — The critical text reads plural 'we pray' rather than singular 'I pray.' Across verses 5-7, Paul plays on a single Greek word family for testing and approval (dokimazo, dokimos, adokimos), rendered here as 'prove,' 'approved,' and 'unapproved.'
- **13:11** — The Greek imperative chairete can mean either 'rejoice' or 'farewell,' and Paul may intentionally evoke both senses in his closing words. The verb 'be restored' echoes 'restoration' in verse 9, signifying the mending and setting in order of the congregation.
- **13:13** — In the critical text, the greeting of all the saints belongs to verse 12, making this trinitarian benediction verse 13 rather than verse 14. The earliest manuscripts also end the letter here without a closing 'Amen.'
