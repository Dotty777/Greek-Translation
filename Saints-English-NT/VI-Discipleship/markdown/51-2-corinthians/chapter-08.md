## Text

**8:1** Now we make known to you, brethren, the grace of God which has been given among the churches of Macedonia,

**8:2** that in a great testing of affliction, the abundance of their joy and their profound poverty abounded into the wealth of their generosity;

**8:3** for I bear witness that they gave according to their ability, yes, and beyond their ability, of their own free will,

**8:4** imploring us with much urgency for the grace and the fellowship of the ministry to the saints—

**8:5** and not as we had hoped, but they gave themselves first to the Lord, and to us through the will of God,

**8:6** so that we urged Titus that, just as he had previously begun, so he would also complete among you this grace as well;

**8:7** yet just as you abound in every respect—in faith and speech and knowledge and all earnestness, and in the love from us that is in you—see that you abound in this grace as well.

**8:8** I do not speak by way of command, yet through the earnestness of others I am testing the genuineness of your love as well;

**8:9** for you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that on your account He became poor, though being rich, in order that through His poverty you might be rich.

**8:10** And in this matter I give my judgment, for this is to your benefit, you who since last year began not only to do it, but also to desire it;

**8:11** but now finish the doing of it as well, so that just as there was eagerness in willing, so also there may be completion from what you possess.

**8:12** For if the eagerness is present, it is well received according to whatever a person has, not according to what he does not have.

**8:13** For it is not that there should be relief for others and affliction for you, but by way of equality:

**8:14** at the present time your abundance is for their want, so that their abundance may also come to meet your want, in order that there may be equality;

**8:15** just as it is written: "He who had much did not have an excess, and he who had little did not fall short."

**8:16** Now thanks be to God, who places the same earnestness for you in the heart of Titus,

**8:17** because he indeed welcomed our appeal, yet being even more earnest, he set out to you of his own free will.

**8:18** And together with him we have sent the brother whose praise in the gospel goes through all the churches—

**8:19** and not only that, but he has also been appointed by the churches as our fellow traveler with this grace that is being administered by us for the glory of the Lord Himself and our own eagerness—

**8:20** guarding against this, lest anyone find fault with us concerning this bountiful offering which is being administered by us,

**8:21** for we take thought for what is honorable not only before the Lord, but also before men.

**8:22** And together with them we have sent our brother, whom we have tested many times in many matters and found earnest, yet now far more earnest because of great confidence in you.

**8:23** As for Titus, he is my partner and fellow laborer on your behalf; as for our brethren, they are envoys of the churches, the glory of Christ.

**8:24** Therefore, manifest to them, before the face of the churches, the proof of your love and of our boasting concerning you.

## Translator Notes

- **8:3** — Paul's Greek in verses 3–4 is elliptical, omitting a finite verb ("they gave") as he rushes toward the Macedonians' self-giving in verse 5; later manuscripts insert "to receive" in verse 4 to smooth the construction.
- **8:7** — The critical text reads "the love from us that is in you" (tē ex hēmōn en hymin agapē), pointing to the love engendered in the Corinthians through Paul's ministry, whereas other manuscripts read "your love for us."
- **8:19** — The critical text reads "our own eagerness" (prothymian hēmōn), referring to Paul and his coworkers' willing readiness in administering the offering, while later manuscripts read "your eagerness."
- **8:23** — "Envoys" translates apostoloi (literally "apostles" or "sent ones"), used here for commissioned representatives of the churches rather than apostles of Christ in the strict office.
