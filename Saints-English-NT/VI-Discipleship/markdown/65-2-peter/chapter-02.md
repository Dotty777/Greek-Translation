## Text

**2:1** Yet false prophets also rose up among the people, even as there shall be false teachers among you, who will covertly bring in ruinous heresies, even denying the Master who purchased them, drawing upon themselves swift destruction.

**2:2** And many will follow their licentious ways, and because of them the way of truth will be blasphemed;

**2:3** and in covetousness they will exploit you with fabricated words; for them the judgment from long ago is not idle, and their destruction does not slumber.

**2:4** For if God did not spare angels when they sinned, but cast them into Tartarus and delivered them to pits of gloom to be kept for judgment,

**2:5** and did not spare the world of old, but guarded Noah, a proclaimer of righteousness, together with seven others, when He brought a deluge upon a world of the ungodly;

**2:6** and turning the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah into ashes, condemned them to utter overthrow, setting them forth as an example to those who would afterward live in ungodliness;

**2:7** and rescued righteous Lot, who was greatly distressed by the licentious conduct of the lawless—

**2:8** for by what he saw and heard, that righteous man, dwelling among them, tormented his righteous soul day after day because of their lawless deeds—

**2:9** then the Lord knows how to rescue the godly out of trial, and to keep the unrighteous under punishment until the day of judgment,

**2:10** and especially those who walk after the flesh in the lust of defilement and despise authority. Bold and self-willed, they do not tremble to revile the glorious ones,

**2:11** whereas angels, though greater in strength and power, do not bring a reviling judgment against them before the Lord.

**2:12** But these men, like unreasoning beasts born by instinct to be captured and destroyed, reviling matters of which they are ignorant, shall also be destroyed in their own destruction,

**2:13** suffering harm as the wages of unrighteousness. They count it pleasure to indulge in revelry in the daytime; they are spots and blemishes, reveling in their own deceptions while they feast with you,

**2:14** having eyes full of adultery and that never cease from sin, enticing unstable souls, having a heart trained in covetousness—children of a curse!

**2:15** Forsaking the straight way, they have gone astray, having followed the way of Balaam, the son of Bosor, who loved the wages of unrighteousness,

**2:16** but he received a rebuke for his own transgression: a speechless donkey, speaking with a human voice, restrained the madness of the prophet.

**2:17** These are waterless springs and mists driven by a tempest, for whom the gloom of darkness has been reserved.

**2:18** For speaking arrogant words of vanity, they entice through the lusts of the flesh, by licentiousness, those who are barely escaping from those who walk in error,

**2:19** promising them liberty, while they themselves are slaves of corruption; for by whatever a man is overcome, to that he is enslaved.

**2:20** For if, after having escaped the defilements of the world through the knowledge of the Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, they are again entangled in them and overcome, their last state has become worse than the first.

**2:21** For it would have been better for them not to have known the way of righteousness, than after knowing it to turn back from the holy commandment delivered to them.

**2:22** What the true proverb says has happened to them: "A dog returns to its own vomit," and, "A sow, after washing, returns to wallowing in the mire."

## Translator Notes

- **2:4** — The Greek verb rendered "cast them into Tartarus" occurs only here in the New Testament, denoting the lowest abyss of the underworld. Early manuscripts read "pits of gloom," whereas later manuscripts read "chains of darkness."
- **2:13** — Early manuscripts read "suffering harm" (creating a wordplay with "unrighteousness") and "deceptions," whereas other manuscripts read "receiving" and "love feasts."
- **2:15** — The Greek text gives the name Bosor, corresponding to Hebrew Beor in Numbers 22:5.
- **2:18** — Early manuscripts read "barely" (or "scarcely"), whereas later manuscripts read "actually" (or "truly").
