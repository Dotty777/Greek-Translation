## Text

**1:1** Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, and Timothy our brother,

**1:2** to the saints and faithful brethren in Christ in Colossae: Grace be to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:3** We give thanks to God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, always as we pray for you,

**1:4** having heard of your faith in Christ Jesus and of the love which you have toward all the saints,

**1:5** because of the hope stored up for you in the heavens, of which you heard beforehand in the word of the truth of the gospel,

**1:6** which has come to you, even as in all the world it is bearing fruit and increasing, as it also does among you from the day you heard and came to know the grace of God in truth;

**1:7** just as you learned from Epaphras, our beloved fellow servant, who is a faithful minister of Christ on our behalf,

**1:8** who also made known to us your love in the Spirit.

**1:9** For this reason we also, from the day we heard it, do not cease praying for you and asking that you may be filled with the full knowledge of His will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding,

**1:10** so that you may walk worthy of the Lord, pleasing Him in every way, bearing fruit in every good work and increasing in the full knowledge of God;

**1:11** being strengthened with all power according to the might of His glory, for all endurance and longsuffering with joy,

**1:12** giving thanks to the Father, who has qualified you for the portion of the inheritance of the saints in the light,

**1:13** who delivered us out of the dominion of darkness and transferred us into the kingdom of the Son of His love,

**1:14** in whom we have our redemption, the forgiveness of sins;

**1:15** who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation,

**1:16** for in Him all things were created in the heavens and upon the earth, things visible and things invisible, whether thrones or dominions or principalities or authorities; all things have been created through Him and for Him.

**1:17** And He Himself is before all things, and in Him all things hold together.

**1:18** And He Himself is the head of the body, the church, who is the beginning, the firstborn from among the dead, that in all things He Himself may be preeminent.

**1:19** For it pleased God that all the fullness should dwell in Him,

**1:20** and through Him to reconcile all things to Himself, having made peace through the blood of His cross—through Him, whether things upon the earth or things in the heavens.

**1:21** And you, who once were alienated and enemies in your mind in wicked works—

**1:22** yet now you have been reconciled in the body of His flesh through death—to present you holy and blameless and above reproach in His sight,

**1:23** if indeed you continue in the faith, firmly founded and steadfast, and not shifting away from the hope of the gospel which you heard, which was proclaimed in all creation under heaven, of which I, Paul, became a minister.

**1:24** Now I rejoice in my sufferings on your behalf, and I fill up what remains lacking in the afflictions of Christ in my flesh for the sake of His body, which is the church,

**1:25** of which I became a minister according to the stewardship of God given to me for your sake, to fulfill the word of God,

**1:26** the mystery hidden from the ages and from the generations—yet now it has been made manifest to His saints,

**1:27** to whom God desired to make known what the riches of the glory of this mystery are among the Gentiles, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.

**1:28** Him we proclaim, admonishing every man and teaching every man in all wisdom, so that we may present every man mature in Christ.

**1:29** For this I also labor, striving according to His working which works in me with power.

## Translator Notes

- **1:7** — The critical Greek text reads "on our behalf" (representing Paul and his coworkers), whereas some later manuscripts read "on your behalf."
- **1:12** — The critical Greek text reads "you" here before shifting to "us" in verse 13; other manuscripts harmonize verse 12 to "us."
- **1:19** — The Greek verb "was pleased" has no explicit noun subject; "God" is supplied as the implied masculine subject required by the masculine participle "having made peace" in verse 20, though some take "all the fullness" as the subject.
- **1:22** — Following the critical Greek passive verb "you have been reconciled," verses 21-22 contain an abrupt grammatical shift marked by dashes; other manuscripts read the active "He has reconciled."
