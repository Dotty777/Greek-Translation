## Translator Notes

- **7:2** — The Greek text explicitly counts them as 'twelve leaders,' where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads only 'heads of their ancestral houses.'
- **7:17** — Greek 'sacrifice of deliverance' (θυσίαν σωτηρίου) is the Septuagint rendering of the peace or fellowship offering. The Greek also specifies female animals—'two heifers' (δαμάλεις) and 'five year-old ewe lambs' (ἀμνάδας)—where the Hebrew text has oxen and male lambs.
- **7:29** — The Greek text omits 'five he-goats' (τράγους πέντε) from the list here and again in 7:41, likely due to an early scribal skip between repeated instances of 'five.'
- **7:30** — The Greek text reads 'Edisur' (Ἐδισούρ) here, a minor manuscript spelling variant of 'Shedeur' (Σεδιούρ, as in 7:35).
- **7:42** — The Greek text reads 'Reuel' (Ῥαγουήλ), matching Numbers 2:14, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'Deuel' here.
- **7:84** — The Greek reads 'from the sons of Israel' (παρὰ τῶν υἱῶν Ἰσραήλ), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'from the leaders of Israel.'
- **7:86** — The Greek omits the Hebrew clause stating ten shekels per pan by the sanctuary shekel, and gives the total in 'gold pieces' (χρυσοῖ).
- **7:87** — The Greek adds 'and their drink offerings' (καὶ αἱ σπονδαὶ αὐτῶν) after 'their sacrifices,' whereas the Hebrew text mentions only their grain offering.
- **7:88** — The Greek places the sixty ewe lambs before the he-goats and adds 'without blemish' (ἄμωμοι). It also adds 'after filling his hands' (the priestly ordination idiom) and reads masculine 'anointing him' (αὐτόν) rather than neuter 'it' for the altar.
- **7:89** — The Greek explicitly reads 'the voice of the Lord' (τὴν φωνήν Κυρίου), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads simply 'the voice.'
