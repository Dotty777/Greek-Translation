## Text

**9:1** And the Lord spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai in the second year after they came out of the land of Egypt, in the first month, saying,

**9:2** "Speak, and let the sons of Israel observe the Passover at its time;

**9:3** on the fourteenth day of the first month toward evening you shall observe it at the appointed times; according to its law and according to its ruling you shall observe it."

**9:4** And Moses spoke to the sons of Israel to observe the Passover, beginning on the fourteenth day of the month in the wilderness of Sinai;

**9:5** just as the Lord directed Moses, so the sons of Israel did.

**9:6** And the men arrived who were unclean because of a human corpse and could not observe the Passover on that day, and they came before Moses and Aaron on that day;

**9:7** and those men said to him, "We are unclean because of a human corpse; should we then fail to present the offering to the Lord at its appointed time in the midst of the sons of Israel?"

**9:8** And Moses said to them, "Stand here, and I will hear what the Lord will command concerning you."

**9:9** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying,

**9:10** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying, 'Any person who becomes unclean because of a human corpse, or is on a journey far away, among you or in your generations, he shall observe the Passover to the Lord; in the second month, on the fourteenth day,

**9:11** toward evening, they shall observe it. With unleavened bread and bitter herbs they shall eat it;

**9:12** they shall not leave any of it until the morning, and they shall not break a bone from it; according to the law of the Passover they shall observe it.

**9:13** And a person who is clean and is not on a distant journey and fails to observe the Passover, that soul shall be utterly destroyed from its people; because he did not present the offering to the Lord at its appointed time, that person shall bear his sin.

**9:14** And if a resident alien comes to you in your land and observes the Passover to the Lord, according to the law of the Passover and according to its regulation he shall observe it; there shall be one law for you, both for the resident alien and for the native of the land.'"

**9:15** And on the day that the tent was set up, the cloud covered the tent, the house of the testimony; and in the evening it was over the tent like an appearance of fire until morning.

**9:16** So it happened continually: the cloud covered it by day and an appearance of fire by night.

**9:17** And whenever the cloud went up from the tent, after that the sons of Israel set out. And in the place where the cloud stood, there the sons of Israel encamped.

**9:18** By the command of the Lord the sons of Israel will encamp; by the command of the Lord they will set out. All the days in which the cloud overshadows the tent, the sons of Israel will encamp.

**9:19** And when the cloud lingers over the tent for many days, the sons of Israel will keep the watch of God and will by no means depart;

**9:20** and it will be, when the cloud covers the tent for a number of days, by the voice of the Lord they will encamp, and by the command of the Lord they will set out.

**9:21** And it will be, when the cloud is present from evening until morning, and the cloud goes up in the morning, they will set out, by day or by night.

**9:22** For a month, for days, while the cloud stays longer, overshadowing it, the sons of Israel will encamp and will by no means set out.

**9:23** For by the command of the Lord they will set out, the watch of the Lord which they kept by the command of the Lord through the hand of Moses.

## Translator Notes

- **9:4** — The Greek compresses the transition into v. 5 with the participle enarchomenou ("beginning") on the fourteenth day, lacking the Hebrew Masoretic Text's explicit narrative clause "and they observed the Passover in the first [month]."
- **9:6** — "A human corpse" renders the Greek idiom epi psyche anthropou (literally "because of a human soul," also in vv. 7, 10), which mirrors Hebrew nephesh adam in the legal sense of a dead person.
- **9:18** — From here through v. 23, the Greek shifts from past narrative verbs to future verbs ("will encamp," "will set out"), rendering Hebrew habitual imperfects prospectively; this verse also places "encamp" before "set out," reversing the Hebrew order.
- **9:22** — The Greek lacks the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "two days" and its closing clause ("when it lifted, they set out"); "For a month, for days" translates menos, hemeras, which could also be read as "for a month, for a day."
- **9:23** — The Greek reads "For by the command of the Lord they will set out" without the Hebrew's "they encamped," followed by the appositional relative clause "the watch of the Lord which they kept."
