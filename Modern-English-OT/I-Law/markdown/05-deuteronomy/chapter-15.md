## Text

**15:1** Every seven years you shall grant a release.

**15:2** And thus is the ordinance of the release: you shall release every personal debt that your neighbor owes you, and you shall not demand payment from your brother, for the release has been proclaimed to the Lord your God.

**15:3** From the foreigner you shall demand whatever is yours with him; to your brother you shall grant a release of your debt,

**15:4** because there will be no needy person among you, for the Lord your God will surely bless you in the land that the Lord your God is giving you as an inheritance for you to possess,

**15:5** if only you diligently listen to the voice of the Lord your God, to keep and to do all these commandments that I command you today.

**15:6** For the Lord your God has blessed you just as He spoke to you, and you will lend to many nations, yet you will not borrow; and you will rule over many nations, yet they will not rule over you.

**15:7** Now if there is a needy person among you from your brothers, in one of your cities in the land that the Lord your God is giving you, you shall not turn your heart away, nor shall you in any way clench your hand from your needy brother;

**15:8** you shall surely open your hands to him; you shall surely lend him a loan for as much as he needs, according to what he lacks.

**15:9** Watch yourself so that a hidden word does not arise in your heart, a lawless thing, saying, "The seventh year is drawing near, the year of release," and your eye becomes evil toward your needy brother, and you do not give to him; and he will cry out against you to the Lord, and there will be a great sin in you.

**15:10** You shall surely give to him, you shall surely lend him a loan for as much as he needs, according to what he lacks; and you shall not be grieved in your heart when you give to him, because on account of this thing the Lord your God will bless you in all the works and in everything to which you put your hand.

**15:11** For a needy person will never cease from the land; therefore I command you to do this thing, saying, "You shall surely open your hands to your brother, to the poor and to the needy one who is in your land."

**15:12** Now if your brother, the Hebrew man and the Hebrew woman, is sold to you, he shall serve you six years, and in the seventh you shall send him away free from you.

**15:13** And when you send him away free from you, you shall not send him away empty;

**15:14** you shall surely supply him with provisions from your sheep and from your grain and from your wine; just as the Lord your God has blessed you, you shall give to him.

**15:15** And you shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and the Lord your God redeemed you from there; therefore I command you to do this thing.

**15:16** Now if he says to you, "I will not go out from you," because he has loved you and your household, because it is well for him with you,

**15:17** then you shall take the awl and pierce his ear against the door, and he shall be your slave forever; and to your female slave you shall do likewise.

**15:18** It shall not be hard before you when they are sent away free from you, because he served you six years for the annual wage of a hired worker; and the Lord your God will bless you in all that you do.

**15:19** Every firstborn that is born among your cattle and among your sheep, the males you shall consecrate to the Lord your God; you shall not work with your firstborn calf, and you shall by no means shear the firstborn of your sheep;

**15:20** before the Lord you shall eat it year by year in the place that the Lord your God chooses, you and your household.

**15:21** And if there is a blemish in it, lame or blind, a bad blemish, you shall not sacrifice it to the Lord your God;

**15:22** in your cities you shall eat it; the unclean person among you and the clean person alike shall eat it, like a gazelle or a deer;

**15:23** only you shall not eat blood; you shall pour it out on the ground like water.

## Translator Notes

- **15:7** — The rare Greek verb apostergo ('turn away affection / cease to love') is used here instead of the Hebrew idiom 'harden your heart'.
- **15:10** — The Greek repeats the lending command from v. 8 ('you shall surely lend him a loan... according to what he lacks'), which does not appear in v. 10 of the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **15:18** — The Greek reads 'the annual wage' (ephetion misthon) rather than 'double the wage' of the Hebrew Masoretic Text, reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew root, and uses a plural passive participle ('when they are sent away free') encompassing both male and female slaves.
