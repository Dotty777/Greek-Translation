## Text

**27:1** And Moses and the council of elders of the Israelites commanded, "Observe all these commandments that I am commanding you today.

**27:2** And it will be, on the day when you cross the Jordan into the land that the Lord your God is giving you, that you shall set up for yourself large stones and coat them with plaster.

**27:3** And you shall write on these stones all the words of this law when you cross the Jordan, at the time you enter the land that the Lord God of your fathers is giving you, a land flowing with milk and honey, just as the Lord God of your fathers spoke to you.

**27:4** And it will be, when you cross the Jordan, that you shall set up these stones, which I am commanding you today, on Mount Ebal, and you shall coat them with plaster.

**27:5** And you shall build an altar there to the Lord your God, an altar of stones; you shall not lay iron on it.

**27:6** With whole stones you shall build an altar to the Lord your God, and you shall offer up on it the burnt offerings to the Lord your God.

**27:7** And you shall sacrifice there a sacrifice of deliverance, and you shall eat and be filled and rejoice before the Lord your God.

**27:8** And you shall write on the stones all this law very clearly."

**27:9** And Moses and the Levite priests spoke to all Israel, saying, "Be silent and listen, Israel. On this day you have become a people for the Lord your God.

**27:10** And you shall obey the voice of the Lord your God, and you shall do all His commandments and His ordinances that I am commanding you today."

**27:11** And Moses commanded the people on that day, saying,

**27:12** "These shall stand to bless the people on Mount Gerizim after crossing the Jordan: Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Joseph, and Benjamin.

**27:13** And these shall stand for the curse on Mount Ebal: Reuben, Gad and Asher, Zebulun, Dan and Naphtali."

**27:14** And the Levites shall answer and say to all Israel with a loud voice:

**27:15** "Cursed is a person who makes a carved and cast image, an abomination to the Lord, the work of the hands of craftsmen, and sets it in a secret place." And the people shall answer and say, "So be it."

**27:16** "Cursed is the one who dishonors his father or his mother." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:17** "Cursed is the one who moves the boundaries of a neighbor." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:18** "Cursed is the one who leads a blind person astray on the road." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:19** "Cursed is whoever perverts the justice of a resident foreigner, an orphan, and a widow." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:20** "Cursed is the one who lies with a wife of his father, because he uncovered his father's covering." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:21** "Cursed is the one who lies with any animal." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:22** "Cursed is the one who lies with a sister of his father or his mother." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:23** "Cursed is the one who lies with his daughter-in-law." And all the people shall say, "So be it." "Cursed is the one who lies with his wife's sister." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:24** "Cursed is the one who strikes his neighbor by treachery." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:25** "Cursed is whoever takes bribes to strike down a life of innocent blood." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

**27:26** "Cursed is every person who does not remain in all the words of this law to do them." And all the people shall say, "So be it."

## Translator Notes

- **27:7** — Greek "θυσίαν σωτηρίου" ("sacrifice of deliverance" or "salvation") is the Septuagint's standard rendering for the Hebrew peace or well-being offering. The Greek also places "there" after "sacrifice" and adds "and be filled" after "eat."
- **27:15** — Throughout vv. 15-26, the Greek translates the people's liturgical response with the optative "Γένοιτο" ("So be it") rather than transliterating Hebrew "Amen." In this verse, the Greek also reads "the people" (without "all") and "craftsmen" (plural).
- **27:22** — The Greek reads "a sister of his father or his mother" (i.e., an aunt), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "his sister, the daughter of his father or the daughter of his mother."
- **27:23** — The Greek reads "νύμφης" ("daughter-in-law") where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "mother-in-law," and then includes an additional curse against lying with one's wife's sister.
- **27:26** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text, the Greek includes "every person" and "all" ("all the words") and renders the verb as "remain in" ("ἐμμένει ἐν") rather than "confirm," wording closely echoed in Galatians 3:10.
