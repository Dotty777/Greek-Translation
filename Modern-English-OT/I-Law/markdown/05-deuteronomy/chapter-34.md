## Text

**34:1** And Moses went up from Araboth of Moab to Mount Nebo, to the summit of Pisgah, which faces Jericho; and the Lord showed him all the land of Gilead as far as Dan,

**34:2** and all the land of Naphtali, and all the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah as far as the farthest sea,

**34:3** and the wilderness, and the surrounding region of Jericho, the city of palm trees, as far as Zoar.

**34:4** And the Lord said to Moses, "This is the land which I swore to Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, saying, 'To your seed I will give it'; and I have shown it to your eyes, and you will not enter there."

**34:5** And Moses, the servant of the Lord, died in the land of Moab by the word of the Lord.

**34:6** And they buried him in Gai near the house of Peor; and no one knows his burial place until this day.

**34:7** Now Moses was one hundred and twenty years old when he died; his eyes were not dimmed, nor did they decay.

**34:8** And the sons of Israel wept for Moses in Araboth of Moab by the Jordan opposite Jericho for thirty days; and the days of mourning of the weeping for Moses were completed.

**34:9** And Joshua son of Nun was filled with a spirit of understanding, for Moses had laid his hands upon him; and the sons of Israel listened to him, and did just as the Lord had commanded Moses.

**34:10** And no prophet has arisen again in Israel like Moses, whom the Lord knew face to face,

**34:11** in all the signs and wonders, he whom the Lord sent to do them in the land of Egypt to Pharaoh and to his attendants and to all his land,

**34:12** the great marvels and the mighty hand, which Moses did before all Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **34:1** — Greek transliterates Hebrew arboth ("plains") directly as "Araboth" (also in 34:8).
- **34:6** — Greek has a plural verb ("they buried him") rather than the Hebrew singular ("He buried him") and transliterates Hebrew "the valley" as the proper name "Gai."
- **34:7** — This Greek text omits "his cheeks" (present in some Septuagint manuscripts), making "his eyes" the subject of "nor did they decay" (Hebrew: "nor had his vigor fled").
- **34:11** — Greek uses a masculine singular relative pronoun referring to Moses ("he whom the Lord sent to do them") rather than a neuter relative referring to the signs and wonders.
