## Text

**10:1** At that time the Lord said to me, "Chisel for yourself two stone tablets like the first ones, and come up to Me on the mountain, and you shall make for yourself a wooden ark,

**10:2** and you shall write on the tablets the words that were on the first tablets which you shattered, and you shall put them into the ark."

**10:3** And I made an ark of imperishable wood, and I chiseled the stone tablets like the first ones, and I went up the mountain, and the two tablets were in my hands.

**10:4** And He wrote on the tablets, according to the first writing, the ten words which the Lord spoke to you on the mountain from the midst of the fire, and the Lord gave them to me.

**10:5** And I turned and went down from the mountain, and I put the tablets into the ark which I had made, and they were there, just as the Lord had commanded me.

**10:6** And the sons of Israel set out from Beeroth of the sons of Jakim Misadai; there Aaron died and was buried there, and Eleazar his son served as priest in his place.

**10:7** From there they set out to Gadgad, and from Gadgad to Taibatha, a land of streams of water.

**10:8** At that time the Lord set apart the tribe of Levi to carry the ark of the covenant of the Lord, to stand before the Lord, to minister and to invoke blessings in His name to this day.

**10:9** Therefore the Levites have no portion or inheritance among their brothers; the Lord Himself is his inheritance, just as He told him.

**10:10** And I stood on the mountain forty days and forty nights, and the Lord heard me at this time also, and the Lord was not willing to utterly destroy you.

**10:11** And the Lord said to me, "Go, set out before this people, and let them go in and inherit the land which I swore to their fathers to give to them."

**10:12** And now, Israel, what does the Lord your God ask of you except to fear the Lord your God, and to walk in all His ways, and to love Him, and to serve the Lord your God with your whole heart and with your whole soul,

**10:13** to keep the commandments of the Lord your God and His statutes, all that I am commanding you today, so that it may go well with you?

**10:14** Look, to the Lord your God belong heaven and the heaven of heaven, the earth and all things that are in it.

**10:15** Yet the Lord selected your fathers, to love them, and He chose their seed after them—you—above all the nations, as on this day.

**10:16** And you shall circumcise the hardness of your heart, and you shall not stiffen your neck.

**10:17** For the Lord your God, He is God of gods and Lord of lords, the God who is great, strong, and awesome, who shows no partiality and will by no means take a bribe,

**10:18** executing justice for the resident alien and the orphan and the widow, and He loves the resident alien, giving him bread and clothing.

**10:19** And you shall love the resident alien, for you were resident aliens in the land of Egypt.

**10:20** The Lord your God you shall fear, and you shall serve Him, and cling to Him, and swear by His name.

**10:21** He is your boast and He is your God, who did among you these great and glorious things which your eyes saw.

**10:22** With seventy souls your fathers went down to Egypt, but now the Lord your God has made you like the stars of heaven in multitude.

## Translator Notes

- **10:2** — The Greek reads the second-person verb "and you shall write" (addressing Moses), contrasting with v. 4 ("And He wrote") and with the Hebrew text and other Greek witnesses that read "and I will write."
- **10:3** — The Greek translates Hebrew "acacia wood" as "imperishable wood" (literally "non-decaying wood"), its standard Septuagint rendering for this timber.
- **10:6** — The Greek preserves the distinct place names "Beeroth of the sons of Jakim" and "Misadai" (Hebrew Beeroth Bene-jaakan and Moserah), and in v. 7 reads "Gadgad" and "Taibatha" (Hebrew Gudgodah and Jotbathah).
- **10:16** — The Greek renders the Hebrew idiom "the foreskin of your heart" directly as "the hardness of your heart."
- **10:18** — The Greek explicitly includes "the resident alien" alongside the orphan and the widow in the first clause, where the Hebrew text mentions only the fatherless and the widow.
