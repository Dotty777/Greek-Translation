## Text

**24:1** Now if someone takes a wife and lives with her, and it happens that she does not find favor before him because he found an indecent thing in her, and he writes her a document of divorce and puts it into her hands and sends her away from his house,

**24:2** and having departed she belongs to another man,

**24:3** and the latter husband hates her and writes her a document of divorce and puts it into her hands and sends her away from his house, and the latter husband who took her as a wife for himself dies,

**24:4** the former husband who sent her away will not be able to return and take her as a wife for himself after she has been defiled, for it is an abomination before the Lord your God; and you must not defile the land that the Lord your God is giving you as an allotment.

**24:5** Now if someone has recently taken a wife, he must not go out to war, and no duty will be imposed on him; he will be exempt in his house for one year; he will bring joy to his wife whom he has taken.

**24:6** You must not take a millstone or an upper millstone as a pledge, for that person takes a life as a pledge.

**24:7** Now if a man is caught stealing a person from his brothers, the sons of Israel, and after overpowering him sells him, that thief must die; and you will remove the evil person from among yourselves.

**24:8** Watch yourself regarding the plague of leprosy; be very careful to act according to all the law that the priests, the Levites, declare to you; in the way I commanded you, be careful to act.

**24:9** Remember all that the Lord your God did to Miriam on the way, as you were coming out of Egypt.

**24:10** If there is a debt against your neighbor, any debt at all, and you go into his house to take his pledge,

**24:11** you must stand outside, and the man who owes your loan will bring the pledge outside to you.

**24:12** And if the man is poor, you must not sleep in the pledge;

**24:13** you must certainly return his pledge by sunset, and he will sleep in his cloak and bless you, and it will be mercy for you before the Lord your God.

**24:14** You must not unjustly withhold the wage of a poor and needy person from among your brothers or from among the resident aliens who are in your cities;

**24:15** on the same day you must pay his wage; the sun must not go down on it, for he is poor and has his hope in it, and he will cry out against you to the Lord, and there will be sin in you.

**24:16** Fathers must not die for children, and sons must not die for fathers; each person will die in his own sin.

**24:17** You must not pervert the justice of a resident alien, an orphan, and a widow,

**24:18** because you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and the Lord your God redeemed you from there; for this reason I command you to do this thing.

**24:19** Now if you reap a harvest in your field and forget a sheaf in your field, you must not turn back to take it; it will be for the resident alien, the orphan, and the widow, so that the Lord your God may bless you in all the works of your hands.

**24:20** And if you gather olives, you must not go back to glean what is behind you; it will be for the resident alien, the orphan, and the widow; and you will remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt; for this reason I command you to do this thing.

**24:21** And if you harvest your vineyard, you must not re-harvest it for what is behind you; it will be for the resident alien, the orphan, and the widow;

**24:22** and you will remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt; for this reason I command you to do this thing.

## Translator Notes

- **24:3** — The Greek text reads "and" (kai) rather than "or if" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and some Greek manuscripts, joining the second divorce and the second husband's death with a conjunctive link.
- **24:10** — The Greek text reads "and you go into his house" (kai eiseleuse) without a negative particle, treating the clause as part of the condition leading into v. 11's command to stand outside; the Hebrew Masoretic Text and other Greek manuscripts prohibit entering ("you shall not go into his house").
- **24:13** — Greek eleemosyne ("mercy" or "charitable deed") renders Hebrew tsedaqah ("righteousness").
- **24:17** — This Greek text ends after "a widow," and v. 18 begins directly with "because," lacking the prohibition against taking a widow's garment in pledge and the opening "and you shall remember" found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and other Greek witnesses.
