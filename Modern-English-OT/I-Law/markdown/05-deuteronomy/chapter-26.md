## Text

**26:1** And it will be that when you enter the land that the Lord your God is giving you to inherit, and take possession of it and live on it,

**26:2** then you must take from the firstfruits of the fruits of your land that the Lord your God is giving you, and put them into a basket, and go to the place that the Lord your God chooses for His name to be called upon there;

**26:3** and you must come to the priest who will be in those days and say to him, "I declare today to the Lord my God that I have entered the land that the Lord swore to our fathers to give us."

**26:4** And the priest will take the basket from your hands and set it in front of the altar of the Lord your God;

**26:5** and answering he will say before the Lord your God, "My father abandoned Syria and went down to Egypt, and lived there as a foreigner with a small number, and became there a great nation and a large multitude.

**26:6** And the Egyptians mistreated us and humbled us and laid harsh labors upon us;

**26:7** and we cried out to the Lord our God, and the Lord heard our voice and saw our humiliation and our oppression;

**26:8** and the Lord brought us out of Egypt, He Himself with His great strength and with a mighty hand and a raised arm, and with great visions and with signs and with wonders;

**26:9** and He brought us into this place and gave us this land, a land flowing with milk and honey.

**26:10** And now, look, I have brought the firstfruits of the produce of the land that You gave me, Lord, a land flowing with milk and honey." And you will bow down before the Lord your God;

**26:11** and you will rejoice in all the good things that the Lord your God has given you, along with your household, the Levite, and the resident alien who is among you.

**26:12** Now when you finish tithing the entire tithe of your produce in the third year, you must give the second tithe to the Levite, the resident alien, the orphan, and the widow, and they will eat in your cities and rejoice.

**26:13** And you will say before the Lord your God, "I have cleared out the holy things from my house and given them to the Levite, the resident alien, the orphan, and the widow, according to all the commandments that You commanded me; I have not transgressed Your commandment, and I have not forgotten;

**26:14** and I have not eaten any of them in my grief, I have not offered any of them for what is unclean, I have not given any of them to the dead; I have obeyed the voice of the Lord our God; I have obeyed just as You commanded me.

**26:15** Look down from Your holy house, from heaven, and bless Your people Israel and the land that You gave them, just as You swore to our fathers to give us a land flowing with milk and honey."

**26:16** On this day the Lord your God has commanded you to do all the statutes and the judgments; and you must keep and do them with all your heart and with all your soul.

**26:17** You have chosen God today to be your God, and to walk in all His ways, and to keep the statutes and the judgments, and to obey His voice;

**26:18** and the Lord has chosen you today for you to become a treasured people for Him, just as He said, to keep His commandments,

**26:19** and for you to be above all the nations, as He made you renowned and a boast and honored, for you to be a holy people to the Lord your God, just as He spoke.

## Translator Notes

- **26:5** — This Greek text reads third-person singular erei ("he will say") rather than second-person "you shall say" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text, and translates the famous Hebrew phrase ("a wandering Aramean was my father") as "My father abandoned Syria" (Syrian apebalen ho pater mou).
- **26:8** — The Greek adds "He Himself with His great strength" and renders the Hebrew "great terror" as "great visions" (horamasin megalois), deriving the Hebrew noun from the root "to see" rather than "to fear."
- **26:12** — Greek to deuteron epidekaton ("the second tithe") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "the year of the tithe," reflecting early Jewish legal terminology for the triennial tithe distribution.
- **26:14** — Greek ekarpousa ("I have offered," a sacrificial term) renders the Hebrew "removed/consumed," and the text repeats epekousa ("I have obeyed") where the Hebrew Masoretic Text and other Greek witnesses read "I have done."
- **26:17** — Greek heilou ("you have chosen") and heilato in v. 18 ("He has chosen") represent the Septuagint's interpretation of the rare and debated Hebrew verbal forms (Hiphil of amar, often rendered "declared" or "avouched").
