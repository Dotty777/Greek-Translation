## Text

**25:1** Now if a dispute arises between people, and they come forward for judgment, and they vindicate what is right and condemn the ungodly person,

**25:2** then it will be that if the one acting ungodly deserves blows, you must seat him before them.

**25:3** And by the number of forty they will flog him; they must not add to it; but if you proceed to flog with more blows beyond these, your brother will be disgraced before you.

**25:4** You must not muzzle a threshing ox.

**25:5** Now if brothers live together, and one of them dies, and he had no seed, the wife of the deceased man must not belong outside to a man who is not a close relative; her husband's brother will go in to her and take her as a wife for himself and live with her.

**25:6** And it will be that the child whom she bears will be established by the name of the deceased, and his name will not be blotted out from Israel.

**25:7** But if the man does not wish to take his brother's wife, then the woman will go up to the gate, to the council of elders, and say, "My husband's brother is not willing to raise up his brother's name in Israel; my husband's brother was not willing."

**25:8** And the council of elders of his city will summon him and speak to him, and if he stands and says, "I do not wish to take her,"

**25:9** then his brother's wife, coming forward before the council of elders, will untie his one sandal from his foot and spit in his face, and answering she will say, "In this way they will do to the man who will not build his brother's house in Israel."

**25:10** And his name will be called in Israel, "House of the One Whose Sandal Was Untied."

**25:11** Now if men fight together, a man with his brother, and the wife of one of them comes near to rescue her husband from the hand of the one striking him, and stretching out her hand she seizes his testicles,

**25:12** you must cut off her hand; your eye must not spare her.

**25:13** You must not have in your bag a weight and a weight, a large or a small;

**25:14** there must not be in your house a measure and a measure, a large or a small;

**25:15** you must have a true and just weight, so that you may live many days on the land that the Lord your God is giving you as an allotment.

**25:16** For everyone who does these things, everyone who does what is unjust, is an abomination to the Lord your God.

**25:17** Remember all that Amalek did to you on the way as you were coming out of the land of Egypt,

**25:18** how he opposed you on the way and cut off your rear guard, those who were weary behind you, while you were hungry and weary, and he did not fear God;

**25:19** and it will be that when the Lord your God gives you rest from all your enemies around you in the land that the Lord your God is giving you to inherit, you must blot out the name of Amalek from under heaven, and you must never forget.

## Translator Notes

- **25:1** — The Greek text reads the neuter singular to dikaion ("what is right") rather than the masculine "the righteous person" found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and some Greek manuscripts.
- **25:2** — Greek kathieis means "you must seat him" (contrasting with the Hebrew "make him lie down"), and this Greek text ends after "before them," omitting the additional clause on beating him in proportion to his offense found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and other Greek witnesses.
- **25:5** — Greek sperma ("offspring") is gender-neutral rather than the Hebrew "son" (ben); similarly, v. 6 reads "the child" (to paidion) rather than "the firstborn."
- **25:18** — Greek epeinas means "you were hungry" rather than "faint" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text, and anteste ("opposed") is stronger than the Hebrew "met."
