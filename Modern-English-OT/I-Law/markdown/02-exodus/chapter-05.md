## Text

**5:1** And after these things, Moses and Aaron went in to Pharaoh and said to him, "Thus says the Lord God of Israel: 'Send My people away, so that they may celebrate a festival to Me in the wilderness.'"

**5:2** And Pharaoh said, "Who is He, that I should listen to His voice so as to send the sons of Israel away? I do not know the Lord, and I will not send Israel away."

**5:3** And they said to him, "The God of the Hebrews has summoned us; therefore we will go a journey of three days into the wilderness, so that we may sacrifice to our God, so that death or slaughter may not meet us."

**5:4** And the king of Egypt said to them, "Why, Moses and Aaron, are you turning my people aside from the labors? Depart, each one of you to his labors."

**5:5** And Pharaoh said, "Look, now the people are very numerous; therefore let us not give them rest from the labors."

**5:6** So Pharaoh commanded the taskmasters of the people and the scribes, saying,

**5:7** "No longer shall straw continue to be given to the people for brickmaking, as yesterday and the third day; let them go themselves and gather straw for themselves.

**5:8** And the quota of brickmaking that they make each day you shall impose on them; you shall not reduce it at all; for they are idle, and therefore they have been crying out, saying, 'Let us arise and sacrifice to our God.'

**5:9** Let the labors of these men be made heavy, and let them be occupied with these things, and let them not be occupied with empty words."

**5:10** And the taskmasters and the scribes pressed them hard and spoke to the people, saying, "Thus says Pharaoh: 'I am no longer giving you straw;

**5:11** go yourselves and gather straw for yourselves wherever you may find it, for nothing at all is being reduced from your quota.'"

**5:12** And the people were scattered throughout all the land of Egypt to gather stubble for straw.

**5:13** And the taskmasters pressed them hard, saying, "Complete the labors required each day, just as when the straw was given to you."

**5:14** And the scribes of the race of the sons of Israel, who had been appointed over them by Pharaoh's overseers, were flogged and were asked, "Why have you not completed your quotas of brickmaking, as yesterday and the third day, and that of today?"

**5:15** Then the scribes of the sons of Israel went in and cried out to Pharaoh, saying, "Why are you dealing this way with your household servants?

**5:16** Straw is not given to your household servants, yet they tell us to make brick; and look, your servants have been flogged. Therefore you will wrong your people."

**5:17** And he said to them, "You are idle, you are idlers; therefore you say, 'Let us go, let us sacrifice to our God.'

**5:18** Now therefore go and work; for the straw will not be given to you, yet you shall deliver the quota of brickmaking."

**5:19** And the scribes of the sons of Israel saw themselves in trouble, saying, "You shall not fall short of the daily requirement of brickmaking."

**5:20** And they met Moses and Aaron coming to meet them, as they were going out from Pharaoh,

**5:21** and they said to them, "May God look upon you and judge, because you have made our odor loathsome before Pharaoh and before his servants, putting a sword into his hands to kill us."

**5:22** Then Moses returned to the Lord and said, "Please, Lord, why have You harmed this people? And why have You sent me?

**5:23** And since I went to Pharaoh to speak in Your name, he has harmed this people, and You have not rescued Your people."

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — The Greek text reads "has summoned us" (προσκέκληται ἡμᾶς) and "death or slaughter" (θάνατος ἢ φόνος), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "has met with us" and "pestilence or sword."
- **5:14** — The Greek participle λέγοντες ("saying") follows the passive "were flogged" as a Semitic impersonal construction introducing the question asked by Pharaoh's overseers.
- **5:16** — The Septuagint concludes with "Therefore you will wrong your people" (ἀδικήσεις οὖν τὸν λαόν σου), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "but the fault is in your own people."
