## Text

**11:1** And the Lord said to Moses, "Yet one more plague I will bring upon Pharaoh and upon Egypt, and after these things he will send you away from here; and when he sends you away, with everything he will surely drive you out.

**11:2** Speak secretly, therefore, in the ears of the people, and let each one ask his neighbor for articles of silver and gold and clothing."

**11:3** And the Lord gave favor to His people in the sight of the Egyptians, and they lent to them; and the man Moses became exceedingly great in the sight of the Egyptians and in the sight of Pharaoh and in the sight of all his attendants.

**11:4** And Moses said, "Thus says the Lord: 'Around midnight I am going into the midst of Egypt,

**11:5** and every firstborn in the land of Egypt will die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits on the throne even to the firstborn of the maidservant who is beside the mill, and even to the firstborn of every animal;

**11:6** and there will be a great outcry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as has never occurred and such as will never occur again.

**11:7** And among all the sons of Israel not a dog will snarl with its tongue, not from man to animal, so that you may see how wondrously the Lord makes a distinction between the Egyptians and Israel.'

**11:8** And all these servants of yours will come down to me and bow down to me, saying, 'Go out, you and all your people whom you lead!' And after these things I will go out." And Moses went out from Pharaoh in anger.

**11:9** And the Lord said to Moses, "Pharaoh will not listen to you, so that I may greatly multiply My signs and wonders in the land of Egypt."

**11:10** Now Moses and Aaron performed all these signs and wonders in the land of Egypt before Pharaoh; and the Lord hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he did not listen so as to send away the sons of Israel from the land of Egypt.

## Translator Notes

- **11:2** — The Septuagint adds "secretly" (κρυφῇ) and "clothing" (ἱματισμόν) here, while omitting the parallel clause about each woman asking her neighbor found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **11:3** — The Septuagint includes the additional clause "and they lent to them" (καὶ ἔχρησαν αὐτοῖς) and explicitly mentions "in the sight of the Egyptians" alongside Pharaoh and his attendants.
- **11:7** — The verb παραδοξάζει ("acts wondrously / makes a wondrous distinction") is a distinctive Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew verb for setting apart miraculously; the Greek also reads "so that you [singular] may see" (ἴδῃς) rather than "that you [plural] may know."
- **11:10** — The Greek concludes with "he did not listen so as to send away" (οὐκ εἰσήκουσεν ἐξαποστεῖλαι), directly echoing the prediction of Pharaoh's refusal to listen in 11:9.
