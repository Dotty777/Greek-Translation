## Text

**2:1** Now there was a certain man from the tribe of Levi who took one of the daughters of Levi.

**2:2** And she conceived and bore a male; and seeing that he was handsome, they sheltered him for three months.

**2:3** But when they could no longer hide him, his mother took a basket for him and coated it with bitumen-pitch, and placed the child in it, and set it in the marsh beside the river.

**2:4** And his sister kept watch from a distance to learn what would happen to him.

**2:5** Now the daughter of Pharaoh went down to bathe at the river, and her female attendants were walking beside the river; and seeing the basket in the marsh, she sent her attendant and took it up.

**2:6** And opening it, she saw a child crying in the basket; and the daughter of Pharaoh spared him and said, "This is one of the children of the Hebrews."

**2:7** Then his sister said to the daughter of Pharaoh, "Do you want me to call for you a nursing woman from the Hebrews, and she will nurse the child for you?"

**2:8** And the daughter of Pharaoh said, "Go." So the young woman went and called the mother of the child.

**2:9** And the daughter of Pharaoh said to her, "Keep this child safe for me and nurse him for me, and I will give you the wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him.

**2:10** When the child had grown up, she brought him in to the daughter of Pharaoh, and he became a son to her; and she named his name Moses, saying, "Out of the water I took him up."

**2:11** Now it happened in those many days that when Moses had grown up, he went out to his brothers, the sons of Israel. And observing their toil, he saw an Egyptian man beating a certain Hebrew of his own brothers, the sons of Israel.

**2:12** And looking around this way and that, he saw no one; and striking down the Egyptian, he hid him in the sand.

**2:13** And going out on the second day, he saw two Hebrew men fighting with one another, and he said to the one doing wrong, "Why are you beating your neighbor?"

**2:14** And he said, "Who appointed you ruler and judge over us? Do you intend to kill me in the way you killed the Egyptian yesterday?" Then Moses became afraid and said, "Has this matter become known like this?"

**2:15** Now Pharaoh heard of this matter, and he sought to kill Moses; so Moses withdrew from the presence of Pharaoh and settled in the land of Midian; and having come into the land of Midian, he sat down by the well.

**2:16** Now the priest of Midian had seven daughters, who were shepherding the sheep of their father Jethro; and coming there, they drew water until they filled the troughs, to water the sheep of their father Jethro.

**2:17** But the shepherds came and were driving them away; and Moses stood up and rescued them, and drew water for them and watered their sheep.

**2:18** Then they came to Reuel their father; and he said to them, "Why have you been so quick to come today?"

**2:19** And they said, "An Egyptian man rescued us from the shepherds, and he drew water for us and watered our sheep."

**2:20** And he said to his daughters, "And where is he? And why have you left the man behind? Call him, therefore, so that he may eat bread."

**2:21** So Moses took up residence with the man; and he gave his daughter Zipporah to Moses as a wife.

**2:22** And the woman conceived and bore a son; and Moses named his name Gershom, saying, "Because I am a sojourner in a foreign land."

**2:23** Now after those many days, the king of Egypt died; and the sons of Israel groaned because of the works and cried out, and their outcry went up to God from the works.

**2:24** And God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob.

**2:25** And God looked upon the sons of Israel, and He was made known to them.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — LXX uses plural verbs here and in v. 3 ("seeing... they sheltered him... they could"), attributing Moses' concealment to both parents (echoed in Heb 11:23), whereas the Hebrew uses singular verbs for the mother.
- **2:16** — LXX explicitly names the priest of Midian "Jethro" twice in this verse (absent from the Masoretic Hebrew here), while calling him "Reuel" in v. 18.
- **2:25** — LXX reads a passive verb, "and He was made known to them," reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew verb where the Masoretic text reads "and God knew."
