## Text

**32:1** And when the people saw that Moses had delayed to come down from the mountain, the people gathered together against Aaron and said to him, "Rise up and make for us gods who will go before us; for this Moses, the man who brought us out of Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him."

**32:2** And Aaron said to them, "Take off the gold earrings that are in the ears of your wives and daughters, and bring them to me."

**32:3** And all the people took off the gold earrings that were in their ears, and brought them to Aaron.

**32:4** And he received them from their hands and formed them with the engraving tool, and made them into a cast calf, and he said, "These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt."

**32:5** And when Aaron saw this, he built an altar in front of it; and Aaron made a proclamation, saying, "Tomorrow is a festival of the Lord."

**32:6** And rising early on the next day, he offered up whole burnt offerings and presented a sacrifice of deliverance; and the people sat down to eat and to drink, and they rose up to play.

**32:7** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying, "Go quickly from here, go down; for your people, whom you brought out from the land of Egypt, have acted lawlessly.

**32:8** They have turned aside quickly from the way that you commanded them; they have made for themselves a calf, and they have worshiped it and said, 'These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt.'

**32:10** And now leave Me alone, and becoming furious with anger against them, I will wipe them out, and I will make you into a great nation."

**32:11** And Moses pleaded before the Lord God and said, "Why, Lord, are You furious with anger against Your people, whom You brought out from the land of Egypt with great strength and with Your uplifted arm?

**32:12** Do not let the Egyptians speak, saying, 'With evil intent He brought them out to kill them in the mountains and to consume them from the earth.' Cease from the anger of Your fury, and be merciful concerning the wickedness of Your people,

**32:13** remembering Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, Your household servants, to whom You swore by Yourself and spoke to them, saying, 'I will greatly multiply your offspring like the stars of heaven in multitude,' and all this land that You spoke of giving to them, and they will possess it forever."

**32:14** And the Lord was appeased so as to preserve His people.

**32:15** And Moses turned and went down from the mountain; and the two tablets of the testimony were in his hands, stone tablets, inscribed on both of their sides; on this side and on that side they were written.

**32:16** And the tablets were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, engraved on the tablets.

**32:17** And when Joshua heard the sound of the people shouting, he said to Moses, "There is a sound of war in the camp."

**32:18** And he said, "It is not the sound of those leading off in strength, nor the sound of those leading off in defeat; rather, it is the sound of those leading off with wine that I hear."

**32:19** And when he drew near to the camp, he saw the calf and the dances; and becoming angry with fury, Moses threw the two tablets from his hands and shattered them beneath the mountain.

**32:20** And taking the calf that they had made, he burned it up in fire, and ground it fine, and scattered it under the water, and made the sons of Israel drink it.

**32:21** And Moses said to Aaron, "What did this people do to you, that you brought a great sin upon them?"

**32:22** And Aaron said to Moses, "Do not be angry, lord; for you know the impulse of this people.

**32:23** For they said to me, 'Make for us gods who will go before us; for this Moses, the man who brought us out of Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him.'

**32:24** And I said to them, 'If anyone has gold jewelry, take it off.' And they gave it to me; and I threw it into the fire, and this calf came out."

**32:25** And when Moses saw that the people had been scattered--for Aaron had scattered them, an object of gloating to their adversaries--

**32:26** Moses stood at the gate of the camp and said, "Who is for the Lord? Let him come to me." So all the sons of Levi came together.

**32:27** And he said to them, "Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel: 'Each of you, put his own sword on his thigh, and pass through and return from gate to gate through the camp, and kill, each one his brother and each one his nearest neighbor.'"

**32:28** And the sons of Levi did just as Moses spoke to them; and there fell from the people on that day about three thousand men.

**32:29** And Moses said to them, "You have filled your hands today for the Lord, each one against his son or his brother, so that a blessing may be given upon you."

**32:30** And it happened after the next day that Moses said to the people, "You have sinned a great sin. And now I will go up to God, so that I may make atonement for your sin."

**32:31** So Moses returned to the Lord and said, "I implore You, Lord; this people has sinned a great sin, and they have made for themselves gods of gold.

**32:32** And now, if You forgive them their sin, forgive it; but if not, blot me out from Your book that You have written."

**32:33** And the Lord said to Moses, "If anyone has sinned, I will blot them out from My book.

**32:34** But you, go, go down, and lead this people into the place that I told you; look, My angel goes before your face; and on whatever day I visit, I will bring their sin upon them."

**32:35** And the Lord struck the people because of the making of the calf that Aaron had made.

## Translator Notes

- **32:4** — The Greek uses the singular verb "he said" (referring to Aaron) rather than the plural "they said" of the Hebrew text; similarly in 32:6, the verbs for rising early and offering sacrifices are singular.
- **32:8** — The Greek reads "that you commanded them" (addressing Moses) rather than "that I commanded them," and verse 32:9 is absent from the Septuagint text.
- **32:14** — Where the Hebrew text speaks of the Lord relenting from the disaster He planned, the Septuagint states that the Lord "was appeased so as to preserve His people."
- **32:18** — The phrase "those leading off with wine" (ἐξαρχόντων οἴνου) reflects a distinctive Septuagint reading where the Hebrew text refers to the sound of singing.
- **32:29** — "You have filled your hands" preserves the literal Greek idiom for consecration, rendered here as a past indicative statement rather than a command.
