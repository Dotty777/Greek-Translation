## Text

**15:1** Then Moses and the sons of Israel sang this song to God, and spoke, saying, "Let us sing to the Lord, for He has been gloriously glorified; horse and rider He hurled into the sea.

**15:2** A helper and protector He became to me for salvation; this is my God, and I will glorify Him, the God of my father, and I will exalt Him.

**15:3** The Lord is one who crushes wars; the Lord is His name.

**15:4** Pharaoh's chariots and his army He hurled into the sea, chosen riders, officers; they were swallowed up in the Red Sea.

**15:5** With the deep sea He covered them; they sank down into the depth like a stone.

**15:6** Your right hand, Lord, has been glorified in strength; Your right hand, Lord, shattered enemies.

**15:7** And by the abundance of Your glory You crushed the adversaries; You sent forth Your wrath, and it devoured them like stubble.

**15:8** And through the breath of Your anger the water parted; the waters congealed like a wall, the waves congealed in the midst of the sea.

**15:9** The enemy said, 'Pursuing, I will overtake; I will divide spoils, I will fill my soul, I will slay with my sword, my hand will rule.'

**15:10** You sent forth Your breath, the sea covered them; they sank like lead in violent water.

**15:11** Who is like You among the gods, Lord? Who is like You? Glorified among the holy ones, marvelous in glories, performing wonders.

**15:12** You stretched out Your right hand; the earth swallowed them.

**15:13** You guided by Your righteousness this people of Yours whom You redeemed; You encouraged them by Your strength to Your holy lodging place.

**15:14** Nations heard and became angry; birth pains seized those inhabiting Philistia.

**15:15** Then the leaders of Edom hastened; and the rulers of the Moabites, trembling seized them; all those inhabiting Canaan melted away.

**15:16** May trembling and fear fall upon them; by the greatness of Your arm let them be turned to stone, until Your people pass by, Lord, until this people of Yours whom You acquired pass by.

**15:17** Bring them in and plant them on the mountain of Your inheritance, in Your prepared dwelling place which You fashioned, Lord, a sanctuary, Lord, which Your hands prepared.

**15:18** The Lord reigns forever and ever and beyond."

**15:19** For Pharaoh's horse went with chariots and riders into the sea, and the Lord brought the water of the sea upon them; but the sons of Israel walked through dry ground in the midst of the sea.

**15:20** And Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took the tambourine in her hand, and all the women went out after her with tambourines and dances.

**15:21** And Miriam led them in song, saying, "Let us sing to the Lord, for He has been gloriously glorified; horse and rider He hurled into the sea."

**15:22** Then Moses led the sons of Israel away from the Red Sea, and brought them into the wilderness of Shur; and they traveled three days in the wilderness and did not find water to drink.

**15:23** And they came to Marah, and they were not able to drink from Marah, for it was bitter; therefore he named the name of that place Bitterness.

**15:24** And the people were grumbling against Moses, saying, "What will we drink?"

**15:25** And Moses cried out to the Lord; and the Lord showed him a piece of wood, and he threw it into the water, and the water was made sweet. There He set for him ordinances and judgments, and there He tested him,

**15:26** and said, "If you carefully listen to the voice of the Lord your God, and do the things pleasing before Him, and give ear to His commandments, and keep all His ordinances, no disease which I brought upon the Egyptians will I bring upon you; for I am the Lord your God who heals you."

**15:27** And they came to Elim, and there were twelve springs of water there and seventy trunks of palm trees; and they encamped there beside the waters.

## Translator Notes

- **15:3** — The Greek renders the title as "one who crushes wars" (syntribōn polemous), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "man of war."
- **15:5** — The Greek uses an instrumental dative ("with the deep sea He covered them"), making the Lord rather than the depths the grammatical subject.
- **15:13** — The Greek has "by Your righteousness" (dikaiosynē) and "encouraged" (parekalesas), distinctive Septuagint renderings where the Hebrew has "steadfast love" and "guided."
- **15:23** — The Greek translates the etiology directly into the place name "Bitterness" (Pikria) rather than repeating the transliterated name Marah (Merra).
