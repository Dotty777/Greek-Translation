## Text

**3:1** Now the serpent was the most shrewd of all the wild animals on the earth which the Lord God had made. And the serpent said to the woman, "Why is it that God said, 'You shall not eat from any tree of the garden'?"

**3:2** And the woman said to the serpent, "From every tree of the garden we may eat;

**3:3** but from the fruit of the tree which is in the middle of the garden, God said, 'You shall not eat from it, nor shall you touch it, so that you do not die.'"

**3:4** And the serpent said to the woman, "You will not die by death;

**3:5** for God knew that in whatever day you eat from it, your eyes will be opened, and you will be like gods, knowing good and evil."

**3:6** And the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasing to the eyes to look at and beautiful to contemplate, and taking some of its fruit she ate; and she gave also to her husband with her, and they ate.

**3:7** And the eyes of the two were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together and made loincloths for themselves.

**3:8** And they heard the sound of the Lord God walking in the garden toward evening; and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the Lord God among the trees of the garden.

**3:9** And the Lord God called Adam and said to him, "Adam, where are you?"

**3:10** And he said to Him, "I heard the sound of You walking in the garden, and I was afraid, because I am naked, and I hid myself."

**3:11** And He said to him, "Who told you that you are naked, unless you have eaten from the tree that I commanded you, this one alone, not to eat from?"

**3:12** And Adam said, "The woman whom You gave to be with me, she gave me some from the tree, and I ate."

**3:13** And the Lord God said to the woman, "Why have you done this?" And the woman said, "The serpent deceived me, and I ate."

**3:14** And the Lord God said to the serpent, "Because you have done this, cursed are you above all the livestock and above all the wild animals of the earth; on your chest and belly you will go, and earth you will eat all the days of your life.

**3:15** And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed; he will watch for your head, and you will watch for his heel."

**3:16** And to the woman He said, "I will greatly multiply your pains and your groaning; in pains you will bear children, and your turning will be toward your husband, and he will rule over you."

**3:17** And to Adam He said, "Because you listened to the voice of your wife and ate from the tree that I commanded you, this one alone, not to eat from, and you ate from it, cursed is the earth in your labors; in pains you will eat from it all the days of your life;

**3:18** thorns and thistles it will raise up for you, and you will eat the plants of the field.

**3:19** By the sweat of your face you will eat your bread until you return to the earth from which you were taken; for you are earth, and into earth you will depart."

**3:20** And Adam called the name of his wife Life, because she is the mother of all the living.

**3:21** And the Lord God made for Adam and his wife tunics of skin, and clothed them.

**3:22** And the Lord God said, "See, Adam has become like one of Us, in knowing good and evil; and now, so that he does not stretch out his hand and take from the tree of life and eat, and live forever—"

**3:23** And the Lord God sent him out of the garden of delight to work the earth from which he was taken.

**3:24** And He drove Adam out and settled him opposite the garden of delight, and He stationed the cherubim and the flaming sword that turns, to guard the way to the tree of life.

## Translator Notes

- **3:5** — ὡς θεοί is plural, "like gods." The Greek does not make it a title for the one God here.
- **3:8** — ἐν μέσῳ τοῦ ξύλου is literally "in the midst of the tree"; the singular is taken as collective, "among the trees." τὸ δειλινόν means late afternoon toward evening.
- **3:14** — ἀπὸ πάντων is rendered "above all," meaning set apart in curse from all the other animals. "Earth you will eat" keeps γῆν; the Greek uses the same word as for the ground in 3:17–19.
- **3:15** — The Greek pronoun αὐτός ("he") is masculine, although its antecedent σπέρμα ("seed") is neuter. Early Christian readers noticed this. τηρέω means "watch, guard, keep watch for"; the Greek uses it in both clauses, so both get "watch for."
- **3:16** — Πληθύνων πληθυνῶ ("multiplying I will multiply") is an emphatic idiom, rendered "I will greatly multiply." ἀποστροφή means a turning or recourse; the Greek does not use a word for "desire."
- **3:19** — The Greek uses γῆ ("earth") in every clause, "you are earth, and into earth you will depart." It is kept so, not changed to "dust."
- **3:20** — The Greek translates the name as Ζωή, "Life." Following the Greek, the name is given as Life instead of Eve, which is the Hebrew-based name.
- **3:23** — παράδεισος τῆς τρυφῆς, "the garden of delight," is the Greek's rendering of the place name Eden.
- **3:24** — In the Greek, God "settled him opposite the garden," which is the Greek's own wording. The Greek uses the neuter article τὰ with χερουβίν, treating "cherubim" as a plural noun.
