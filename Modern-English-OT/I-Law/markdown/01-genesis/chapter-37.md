## Text

**37:1** Now Jacob lived in the land where his father had sojourned, in the land of Canaan. And these are the generations of Jacob.

**37:2** Joseph, seventeen years old, was shepherding the sheep with his brothers, being young, with the sons of Bilhah and with the sons of Zilpah, his father's wives; and they brought an evil accusation against Joseph to Israel their father.

**37:3** Now Jacob loved Joseph more than all his sons, because he was a son of his old age; and he made him a multicolored tunic.

**37:4** And when his brothers saw that his father loved him more than all his sons, they hated him and could not speak anything peaceful to him.

**37:5** Then Joseph dreamed a dream and reported it to his brothers,

**37:6** and he said to them, "Listen to this dream that I have dreamed:

**37:7** I thought you were binding sheaves in the middle of the field, and my sheaf rose up and stood upright; and your sheaves turned around and bowed down to my sheaf."

**37:8** And his brothers said to him, "Will you really reign over us, or will you really rule over us?" So they hated him even more because of his dreams and because of his words.

**37:9** Then he saw another dream, and he recounted it to his father and to his brothers, and said, "Look, I have dreamed another dream: it was as if the sun and the moon and eleven stars were bowing down to me."

**37:10** And his father rebuked him and said, "What is this dream that you have dreamed? Will I and your mother and your brothers really come to bow down to you to the ground?"

**37:11** And his brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the saying in mind.

**37:12** Now his brothers went to Shechem to pasture their father's sheep.

**37:13** And Israel said to Joseph, "Are not your brothers shepherding in Shechem? Come, let me send you to them." And he said to him, "Here I am."

**37:14** Then Israel said to him, "Go and see whether your brothers and the sheep are well, and bring word back to me." So he sent him from the valley of Hebron, and he came to Shechem.

**37:15** And a man found him wandering in the field; and the man asked him, saying, "What are you looking for?"

**37:16** And he said, "I am looking for my brothers; tell me where they are pasturing."

**37:17** And the man said to him, "They have departed from here, for I heard them saying, 'Let us go to Dothan.'" So Joseph went after his brothers and found them in Dothan.

**37:18** And they saw him from a distance before he came near to them, and they were setting out to kill him.

**37:19** And each said to his brother, "Look, that dreamer is coming;

**37:20** now then, come, let us kill him and throw him into one of the pits, and we will say, 'A vicious wild beast has devoured him'; and we will see what his dreams are."

**37:21** But when Reuben heard it, he rescued him from their hands and said, "We will not strike him to take his life."

**37:22** And Reuben said to them, "Do not shed blood; rather, throw him into one of the pits that are in the wilderness, and do not lay a hand on him"—so that he might rescue him from their hands and return him to his father.

**37:23** And it came about, when Joseph came to his brothers, that they stripped Joseph of the multicolored tunic that was on him,

**37:24** and taking him, they threw him into the pit; and that pit had no water.

**37:25** Then they sat down to eat bread; and lifting up their eyes they looked, and look, Ishmaelite travelers were coming from Gilead, and their camels were loaded with incense and resin and myrrh oil; and they were on their way to bring them down to Egypt.

**37:26** And Judah said to his brothers, "What advantage is it if we kill our brother and conceal his blood?

**37:27** Come, let us sell him to these Ishmaelites, and let our hands not be on him, for he is our brother and our flesh." And his brothers listened.

**37:28** And the men, the Midianite merchants, were passing by, and they pulled Joseph out and brought him up from the pit; and they sold Joseph to the Ishmaelites for twenty gold pieces; and they brought Joseph down to Egypt.

**37:29** Then Reuben returned to the pit, and he did not see Joseph in the pit; and he tore his clothes.

**37:30** And he returned to his brothers and said, "The boy is not there; and as for me, where do I go now?"

**37:31** Then taking Joseph's tunic, they slaughtered a young goat and stained the tunic with blood.

**37:32** And they sent the multicolored tunic and brought it to their father, and said, "We found this; identify whether it is your son's tunic or not."

**37:33** And he recognized it and said, "It is my son's tunic! A vicious wild beast has devoured him; a wild beast has snatched Joseph away."

**37:34** Then Jacob tore his clothes and put sackcloth on his waist, and he mourned for his son for several days.

**37:35** And all his sons and daughters gathered together and came to comfort him; but he refused to be comforted, saying, "I will go down to my son mourning to Hades." And his father wept for him.

**37:36** Meanwhile, the Midianites sold Joseph into Egypt to Potiphar, the eunuch of Pharaoh, the chief cook.

## Translator Notes

- **37:2** — The Greek text uses a plural verb ('they brought an evil accusation against Joseph to Israel their father'), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text makes Joseph the subject bringing a bad report about his brothers.
- **37:28** — The Greek text specifies 'twenty gold pieces' (εἴκοσι χρυσῶν), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads twenty pieces of silver.
- **37:36** — The Greek renders Potiphar's titles as 'eunuch' (σπάδοντι) and 'chief cook' (ἀρχιμαγείρῳ), reflecting a literal rendering of the Hebrew root for slaughtering/cooking rather than 'captain of the guard.'
