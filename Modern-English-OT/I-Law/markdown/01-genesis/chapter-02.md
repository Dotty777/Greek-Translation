## Text

**2:1** And heaven and earth were completed, and all their adornment.

**2:2** And God finished on the sixth day His works which He had made, and He rested on the seventh day from all His works which He had made.

**2:3** And God blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, because in it He rested from all His works which God had begun to make.

**2:4** This is the book of the origin of heaven and earth, when it came to be, in the day that the Lord God made heaven and earth,

**2:5** and every green thing of the field before it came to be on the earth, and every plant of the field before it sprang up. For God had not sent rain on the earth, and there was no man to work the earth;

**2:6** but a spring was going up out of the earth and was watering the whole face of the earth.

**2:7** And God formed the man, dust from the earth, and breathed into his face a breath of life, and the man became a living creature.

**2:8** And the Lord God planted a garden in Eden toward the east, and placed there the man whom He had formed.

**2:9** And God further made to spring up out of the earth every tree beautiful to look at and good for food, and the tree of life in the middle of the garden, and the tree for knowing what can be known of good and evil.

**2:10** Now a river goes out from Eden to water the garden; from there it divides into four headwaters.

**2:11** The name of the one is Pishon; this is the one that encircles the whole land of Havilah, where the gold is.

**2:12** And the gold of that land is good; and the carbuncle and the green stone are there.

**2:13** And the name of the second river is Gihon; this is the one that encircles the whole land of Ethiopia.

**2:14** And the third river is the Tigris; this is the one that flows opposite the Assyrians. And the fourth river, this is the Euphrates.

**2:15** And the Lord God took the man whom He had formed and placed him in the garden, to work it and to guard it.

**2:16** And the Lord God commanded Adam, saying, "From every tree that is in the garden you shall eat for food;

**2:17** but from the tree for knowing good and evil, you shall not eat from it; and in whatever day you eat from it, you shall die by death."

**2:18** And the Lord God said, "It is not good for the man to be alone; let Us make for him a helper corresponding to him."

**2:19** And God further formed out of the earth all the wild animals of the field and all the birds of heaven, and He brought them to Adam to see what he would call them; and whatever Adam called it, a living creature, that was its name.

**2:20** And Adam gave names to all the livestock and to all the birds of heaven and to all the wild animals of the field; but for Adam there was not found a helper like him.

**2:21** And God cast a trance upon Adam, and he slept; and He took one of his ribs and filled up flesh in its place.

**2:22** And the Lord God built the rib that He had taken from Adam into a woman, and brought her to Adam.

**2:23** And Adam said, "This now is bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of her man."

**2:24** For this reason a man will leave his father and his mother and will be joined to his wife, and the two will become one flesh.

**2:25** And the two were naked, both Adam and his wife, and they were not ashamed.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — κόσμος here means "order, array, adornment" (the Greek word behind "cosmos" and "cosmetic"); it is rendered "adornment."
- **2:2** — The Greek reads "on the sixth day" God finished His works, so that the seventh day is free of work. The rendering follows the Greek.
- **2:7** — ψυχὴ ζῶσα is the same phrase rendered "living creature" for the animals in 1:20, 1:24 and 2:19. It is kept the same here so that the link stays visible. "Dust from the earth" stands in apposition to "the man" in the Greek.
- **2:8** — παράδεισος (a park or enclosed garden, from which "paradise" comes) is rendered "garden" throughout. The Greek spells the place Ἔδεμ (Edem); the standard English "Eden" is used.
- **2:9** — τὸ ξύλον τοῦ εἰδέναι γνωστὸν καλοῦ καὶ πονηροῦ is literally "the tree of knowing what is knowable of good and evil." In 2:17 the Greek uses the simpler phrase τοῦ γινώσκειν καλὸν καὶ πονηρόν.
- **2:11** — The Greek spells the names Φεισών (Pheison) and Εὑειλάτ (Heuilat); the standard English forms Pishon and Havilah are used.
- **2:13** — The Greek spells the river Γηών (Geon), rendered with the standard English form Gihon. The Greek has "Ethiopia" where the Hebrew has Cush; the rendering follows the Greek.
- **2:16** — βρώσει φάγῃ ("you shall eat with eating") echoes a Hebrew idiom. It is rendered "you shall eat for food," which keeps the dative noun.
- **2:17** — The Greek switches to the plural "you" (φάγεσθε) in this verse. θανάτῳ ἀποθανεῖσθε, "you shall die by death," is a Hebraic emphatic idiom kept literal; it means certain death.
- **2:18** — The Greek has the plural ποιήσωμεν, "let Us make," where the Hebrew has a singular. The rendering follows the Greek.
- **2:23** — The Greek pairs γυνή (woman) with ἀνήρ (man, husband). "Her man" keeps the wordplay; the same noun is rendered "husband" in 3:6 and 3:16.
