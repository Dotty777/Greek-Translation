## Text

**42:1** Now when Jacob saw that there was a sale in Egypt, he said to his sons, "Why are you being idle?

**42:2** Look, I have heard that there is grain in Egypt; go down there and buy a little food for us, so that we may live and not die."

**42:3** So the ten brothers of Joseph went down to buy grain from Egypt.

**42:4** But Benjamin, the brother of Joseph, he did not send with his brothers, for he said, "Perhaps an illness might happen to him."

**42:5** So the sons of Israel came to buy with those who were coming, for the famine was in the land of Canaan.

**42:6** Now Joseph was ruler of the land; he was the one who sold to all the people of the land. And when the brothers of Joseph came, they bowed down to him face down to the ground.

**42:7** And when Joseph saw his brothers, he recognized them, yet he acted like a stranger toward them and spoke harsh things to them, and he said to them, "Where have you come from?" And they said, "From the land of Canaan, to buy food."

**42:8** Now Joseph recognized his brothers, but they did not recognize him.

**42:9** And Joseph remembered the dreams that he himself had seen. And he said to them, "You are spies; you have come to observe the tracks of the country."

**42:10** And they said, "No, lord; we, your servants, have come to buy food.

**42:11** We are all sons of one man; we are peaceable; your servants are not spies."

**42:12** But he said to them, "No, rather you have come to see the tracks of the land."

**42:13** And they said, "We, your servants, are twelve brothers in the land of Canaan; and look, the youngest is with our father today, and the other one is no more."

**42:14** And Joseph said to them, "This is what I have spoken to you, saying that you are spies:

**42:15** by this you will be revealed: by the health of Pharaoh, you will certainly not go out from here unless your youngest brother comes here.

**42:16** Send one from among you, and fetch your brother, but as for you, be led away until your words become evident, whether you are telling the truth or not; otherwise, by the health of Pharaoh, surely you are spies."

**42:17** And he put them in prison for three days.

**42:18** Then on the third day he said to them, "Do this, and you will live, for I fear God:

**42:19** if you are peaceable, let one of your brothers be held in prison, but you yourselves go and carry away the purchase of your grain supply,

**42:20** and bring your youngest brother down to me, and your words will be believed; otherwise, you will die." And they did so.

**42:21** And each one said to his brother, "Yes, for we are in sin concerning our brother, because we disregarded the distress of his soul when he pleaded with us, and we did not listen to him; on account of this, this distress has come upon us."

**42:22** And Reuben answered and said to them, "Did I not speak to you, saying, 'Do not wrong the boy'? Yet you did not listen to me; and look, his blood is being demanded."

**42:23** Now they did not know that Joseph understood, for the interpreter was between them.

**42:24** And turning away from them, Joseph wept. And again he approached them and spoke to them; and he took Simeon from them and bound him before them.

**42:25** Then Joseph commanded to fill their vessels with grain, and to return each one's silver into his sack, and to give them provisions for the journey. And so it was done for them.

**42:26** And having loaded the grain onto their donkeys, they departed from there.

**42:27** Now when one untied his bag to give fodder to his donkeys where they lodged, he saw his bundle of silver, and it was on top of the mouth of the bag.

**42:28** And he said to his brothers, "My silver has been returned to me, and look, here it is in my bag." And their heart was astonished, and they were troubled, saying to one another, "What is this that God has done to us?"

**42:29** And they came to Jacob their father in the land of Canaan, and reported to him all the things that had happened to them, saying,

**42:30** "The man, the lord of the land, spoke harsh things to us, and put us in prison as spying out the land.

**42:31** And we said to him, 'We are peaceable; we are not spies;

**42:32** we are twelve brothers, sons of our father; the one is no more, and the youngest is with our father today in the land of Canaan.'

**42:33** And the man, the lord of the land, said to us, 'By this we will know that you are peaceable: leave one brother here with me, and taking the purchase of your grain supply, depart;

**42:34** and bring your youngest brother to me, and I will know that you are not spies, but that you are peaceable; and I will return your brother to you, and you may trade in the land.'"

**42:35** And it happened as they were emptying their sacks that each one's bundle of silver was in their sack; and they and their father saw their bundles of silver, and they were afraid.

**42:36** And Jacob their father said to them, "You have made me childless: Joseph is no more, Simeon is no more, and you will take Benjamin! All these things have come upon me."

**42:37** And Reuben spoke to his father, saying, "Kill my two sons if I do not bring him to you; give him into my hand, and I will bring him back to you."

**42:38** But he said, "My son will not go down with you, for his brother died, and he alone has been left; and it will happen that he falls ill on the road that you go, and you will bring down my old age with grief to Hades."

## Translator Notes

- **42:4** — The Septuagint reads "illness" (μαλακία, and similarly "falls ill" in 42:38) rather than the Masoretic Text's "harm" or "mischief."
- **42:9** — The Septuagint renders this phrase as "the tracks of the country" (τὰ ἴχνη τῆς χώρας, and "tracks of the land" in 42:12), interpreting the Hebrew idiom for the land's vulnerable spots ("nakedness").
- **42:11** — The Septuagint translates the brothers' self-description as "peaceable" (εἰρηνικοί; also in 42:19, 31, 33, 34), whereas the Masoretic Text reads "honest."
- **42:15** — The Septuagint oath "by the health of Pharaoh" (νὴ τὴν ὑγίαν Φαραώ; also in 42:16) corresponds to "by the life of Pharaoh" in the Masoretic Text.
- **42:38** — The Septuagint renders the Hebrew "Sheol" (the realm of the dead) as "Hades" (ᾅδου).
