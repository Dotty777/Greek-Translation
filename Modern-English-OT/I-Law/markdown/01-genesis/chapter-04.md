## Text

**4:1** Now Adam knew Eve his wife, and she conceived and gave birth to Cain. And she said, "I have acquired a man through God."

**4:2** And again she gave birth to his brother Abel. And Abel became a shepherd of sheep, while Cain was working the ground.

**4:3** And it happened after some days that Cain brought from the fruits of the ground a sacrifice to the Lord.

**4:4** And Abel himself also brought from the firstborn of his sheep and from their fat portions. And God looked upon Abel and upon his gifts,

**4:5** but to Cain and to his sacrifices He paid no attention. And it grieved Cain greatly, and his face fell.

**4:6** And the Lord God said to Cain, "Why have you become deeply grieved, and why has your face fallen?

**4:7** If you offer rightly, yet do not divide rightly, have you not sinned? Be still; toward you is his turning, and you will rule over him."

**4:8** And Cain said to Abel his brother, "Let us go out into the field." And it happened, while they were in the field, that Cain rose up against Abel his brother and killed him.

**4:9** And God said to Cain, "Where is Abel your brother?" And he said, "I do not know; am I my brother's guardian?"

**4:10** And God said, "What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood cries out to Me from the ground.

**4:11** And now cursed are you upon the earth, which opened its mouth wide to receive your brother's blood from your hand.

**4:12** For you will work the ground, and it will no longer give its strength to you; groaning and trembling you will be upon the earth."

**4:13** And Cain said to the Lord, "My guilt is too great for me to be forgiven.

**4:14** If You are driving me out today from the face of the earth, I will also be hidden from Your face, and I will be groaning and trembling upon the earth, and everyone who finds me will kill me."

**4:15** And the Lord God said to him, "Not so; everyone who kills Cain will incur sevenfold vengeance." And the Lord God set a sign for Cain, so that no one who found him would kill him.

**4:16** Then Cain went out from the face of God and settled in the land of Naid, opposite Eden.

**4:17** And Cain knew his wife, and she conceived and gave birth to Enoch. And he was building a city, and he named the city after the name of his son, Enoch.

**4:18** And to Enoch was born Gaidad, and Gaidad fathered Maiel, and Maiel fathered Methuselah, and Methuselah fathered Lamech.

**4:19** And Lamech took for himself two wives; the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the second was Zillah.

**4:20** And Adah gave birth to Jabal; he was the father of livestock-keepers who dwell in tents.

**4:21** And the name of his brother was Jubal; he was the one who introduced the harp and the lyre.

**4:22** And Zillah herself also gave birth to Tubal; and he was a hammerer, a smith of bronze and iron. And the sister of Tubal was Naamah.

**4:23** And Lamech said to his wives, "Adah and Zillah, hear my voice; wives of Lamech, give ear to my words; for I have killed a man for a wound to me, and a young man for a bruise to me;

**4:24** because seven times vengeance has been taken for Cain, but for Lamech seventy times seven."

**4:25** And Adam knew Eve his wife, and she conceived and gave birth to a son. And she called his name Seth, saying, "For God has raised up for me another seed in place of Abel, whom Cain killed."

**4:26** And to Seth was born a son, and he called his name Enosh. He hoped to call upon the name of the Lord God.

## Translator Notes

- **4:7** — The Septuagint reads "if you offer rightly, yet do not divide rightly, have you not sinned?" rather than the Masoretic Text's image of sin crouching at the door. The pronoun "his" (Greek autou) lacks an explicit noun for sin in the Greek and is often taken to refer to Abel, though "its" is also possible.
- **4:16** — Greek Naid is the Septuagint form of the region named Nod in the Hebrew text, and katenanti means "opposite" rather than "east of."
- **4:18** — The Septuagint reads the distinct names Gaidad, Maiel, and Methuselah (matching Methuselah in 5:21), corresponding to Irad, Mehujael, and Methushael in the Masoretic Text.
- **4:22** — The Septuagint has the shorter name Tubal rather than the compound Tubal-cain of the Masoretic Text.
- **4:26** — The Septuagint reads "He hoped to call upon the name of the Lord God," reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew verb than the Masoretic Text's "then people began to call."
