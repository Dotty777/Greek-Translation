## Text

**12:1** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying,

**12:2** "Speak to the sons of Israel, and you shall say to them, 'Any woman who receives seed and gives birth to a male shall be unclean for seven days; as during the days of the separation of her menstruation, she shall be unclean.

**12:3** And on the eighth day she shall circumcise the flesh of his foreskin.

**12:4** And for thirty-three days she shall remain in her unclean blood. She shall not touch any vessel, and she shall not enter the sanctuary until the days of her cleansing are completed.

**12:5** But if she gives birth to a female, then she shall be unclean for twice seven days, as during menstruation; and for sixty-six days she shall remain in her unclean blood.

**12:6** And when the days of her cleansing are fully completed, for a son or for a daughter, she shall bring a year-old lamb without blemish for a burnt offering and a young pigeon or a turtledove for a sin offering to the door of the tent of testimony, to the priest.

**12:7** And he shall offer them before the Lord, and the priest shall make atonement for her, and he shall cleanse her from the flow of her blood. This is the law for the woman who gives birth to a male or a female.

**12:8** But if her hand does not find what is sufficient for a lamb, then she shall take two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one for a burnt offering and one for a sin offering; and the priest shall make atonement for her, and she shall be cleansed.'"

## Translator Notes

- **12:2** — Greek σπερματισθῇ (aorist passive of σπερματίζω) literally means "is seeded" or "receives seed," denoting conception.
- **12:3** — Greek uses the active verb περιτεμεῖ ("shall circumcise") with no new explicit subject, continuing the mother from v. 2 as grammatical subject (or acting impersonally as "one shall circumcise"), whereas the Hebrew text is passive.
- **12:4** — Greek reads ἐν αἵματι ἀκαθάρτῳ αὐτῆς ("in her unclean blood," also in v. 5) where the Hebrew has "blood of purification," and reads ἀγγίου ("vessel") where other Greek witnesses and the Hebrew read ἁγίου ("holy thing").
- **12:7** — Greek καθαριεῖ αὐτήν ("he shall cleanse her") is active, with the priest as its implied subject, whereas the Hebrew text reads "and she shall be clean."
- **12:8** — "Her hand does not find what is sufficient" preserves a literal Greek rendering of a Semitic idiom meaning she cannot afford a lamb.
