## Text

**6:1** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying,

**6:2** "Command Aaron and his sons, saying, 'This is the law of the burnt offering: The burnt offering itself shall be on its burning on the altar all night until the morning, and the fire of the altar shall burn on it; it shall not be extinguished.

**6:3** And the priest shall put on a linen tunic, and he shall put on linen undergarments around his body, and he shall remove the burnt residue that the fire consumes, the burnt offering, from the altar, and he shall place it beside the altar.

**6:4** And he shall take off his robe and put on another robe, and he shall carry the burnt residue outside the camp to a clean place.

**6:5** And fire on the altar shall burn from it and shall not be extinguished; and the priest shall burn wood on it in the morning, and he shall arrange the burnt offering on it, and he shall place the fat of the deliverance offering on it.

**6:6** And fire shall burn continually on the altar; it shall not be extinguished.

**6:7** This is the law of the sacrifice that the sons of Aaron shall present before the Lord in front of the altar:

**6:8** And he shall remove a handful from it, from the fine flour of the sacrifice with its oil and with its frankincense, the things that are on the sacrifice, and he shall offer it up on the altar as an offering, a fragrant aroma, its memorial to the Lord.

**6:9** And what is left over from it Aaron and his sons shall eat; it shall be eaten unleavened in a holy place; in the courtyard of the tent of testimony they shall eat it.

**6:10** It shall not be baked leavened; I have given it to them as a portion from the offerings of the Lord; it is most holy, just like the sin offering and just like the trespass offering.

**6:11** Every male among the priests shall eat it; it is an eternal statute for your generations from the offerings of the Lord. Everyone who touches them shall become holy.'"

**6:12** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying,

**6:13** "This is the gift of Aaron and his sons that they shall offer to the Lord on the day when you anoint him: one-tenth of an ephah of fine flour for a continual sacrifice, half of it in the morning and half of it in the evening.

**6:14** It shall be prepared on a griddle in oil; he shall bring it kneaded, rolled pieces, a sacrifice of broken pieces, a sacrifice, a fragrant aroma to the Lord.

**6:15** The priest who is anointed in his place from among his sons shall prepare it; it is an eternal law; all of it shall be fully performed.

**6:16** And every sacrifice of a priest shall be wholly burned, and it shall not be eaten."

**6:17** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying,

**6:18** "Speak to Aaron and to his sons, saying, 'This is the law of the sin offering: In the place where they slaughter the burnt offering, they shall slaughter the sin offering before the Lord; it is most holy.

**6:19** The priest who offers it up shall eat it; it shall be eaten in a holy place, in the courtyard of the tent of testimony.

**6:20** Everyone who touches its flesh shall become holy; and if on anyone some of its blood is sprinkled onto the garment, whoever is sprinkled on it shall be washed in a holy place.

**6:21** And an earthenware vessel in which it is boiled shall be shattered; but if it is boiled in a bronze vessel, he shall scour it and rinse it out with water.

**6:22** Every male among the priests shall eat it; it is most holy to the Lord.

**6:23** And all the sin offerings, any of whose blood is brought into the tent of testimony to make atonement in the holy place, shall not be eaten; they shall be burned up in fire.'"

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — Greek kausis literally means "burning" (corresponding to the hearth or firebed of the altar in the Hebrew text), and the Septuagint adds the closing clause "it shall not be extinguished," which is absent from the Masoretic Text.
- **6:3** — Greek katakarposis ("burnt residue") denotes the consumed remains of the burnt offering on the altar, where the Hebrew text refers to the fatty ashes.
- **6:7** — Throughout chapters 6-7, Greek thysia ("sacrifice") renders both the Hebrew grain offering (6:7-16; 7:9-10) and the animal sacrifice (7:11-37).
- **6:14** — Greek helikta ("rolled pieces") is a distinctive Septuagint rendering of an obscure Hebrew culinary term often interpreted as baked or folded pieces.
- **6:20** — The Greek clause literally places a personal pronoun ("whoever is sprinkled on it") as the subject of the passive verb "shall be washed" (plyno), a verb normally used for washing garments, reflecting a compressed translation of the Hebrew syntax.
