## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Greek text preserves several distinctive leader names compared to the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Araiah for Seraiah, Marathchai for Mordecai, Baspham for Bilshan, Malsar for Mispar, Batusi for Bigvai, and Balleia for Baanah) and omits Rehum; it also reads 'the men of Israel' without 'the people.'
- **2:4** — The Greek reads 'Asaph' with 472 (MT reads 'Shephatiah' with 372); similarly, 2:3 reads 'Perez' (MT 'Parosh'), 2:5 reads 'Era' (MT 'Arah'), 2:6 reads 'Phalab-moab' and 'Jobab' (MT 'Pahath-moab' and 'Joab'), and 2:7 reads 'Malam' (MT 'Elam').
- **2:12** — The Greek gives 3,222 (MT reads 1,222), and 2:16 reads 92 (MT 98). Distinctive Greek name forms also appear in 2:9-10, 14, 17-18, and 20-21 (Zacchu, Banu, Baogei, Basu, Ura, Taber, and Barthaleem for MT Zaccai, Bani, Bigvai, Bezai, Jorah, Gibbar, and Bethlehem).
- **2:31** — The Greek fuses the Hebrew phrase for 'the other Elam' into a single proper name, 'Elamar,' and reads 2,254 (MT reads 1,254); similarly, 2:26 reads 'Haram' (MT 'Ramah') and 2:28 reads 'Gaithel' (MT 'Bethel').
- **2:33** — The Greek reads 'Haroth' and 625 (MT reads 'Hadid' and 725); in 2:34 the Greek likewise reads 'Jereia' where MT reads 'Jericho.'
- **2:43** — The Greek transliterates Hebrew 'Nethinim' (temple servants; spelled 'Thanieim' in 2:70) and preserves several distinctive names across 2:36-57 (including Jeuda, Soduiah, Akum, Abau, Suthia, Kadesh, Soel, Akaboth, Samaan, Keded, Gael, Reel, Phison, Manoemin, Naphison, Haphika, Basadoe, Mauda, Nasus, Phadura, Gedea, Hateia, Phasrath, and Haseboin), while omitting Sisera in 2:53. In 2:39 and 2:41 the Greek counts 1,007 and 148 (MT reads 1,017 and 128).
- **2:55** — The Greek transliterates the Hebrew title 'servants of Solomon' as proper names: 'Abdesel' here and 'Asedeselma' in 2:58. In 2:58 the Greek numeral gives 372 (MT reads 392).
- **2:62** — 'Methoesim' transliterates the Hebrew participle for 'those enrolled by genealogy,' and 'excluded' renders a Greek verb literally meaning 'be treated as next of kin,' reflecting the Hebrew homonym for 'be defiled/excluded.' Distinctive Greek names also appear in 2:59-61, including the extra clan 'Bua' in 2:60 and 'Zarbelthei' alongside 'Barzillai' in 2:61.
- **2:63** — 'Hathersaa' transliterates the Persian title for 'the governor' (Tirshatha). 'The lights and the perfections' translates the Greek rendering of Hebrew Urim and Thummim.
- **2:65** — The Greek reads 7,334 (MT 7,337) and literally 'singers and songs' (MT 'male and female singers'). Distinctive Greek figures also appear in 2:66 (730 horses; MT 736), 2:67 (720 donkeys; MT 6,720), and 2:69 (61,000 minas of pure gold; MT 61,000 gold darics).
