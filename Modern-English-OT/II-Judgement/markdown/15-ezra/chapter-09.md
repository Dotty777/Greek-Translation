## Text

**9:1** And when these things were completed, the leaders approached me, saying, "The people of Israel and the priests and the Levites have not separated themselves from the peoples of the lands in their abominations: the Canaanite, the Hittite, the Perizzite, the Jebusite, the Ammonite, Moab, the Egyptian, and the Amorite;

**9:2** for they have taken some of their daughters for themselves and for their sons, and the holy seed has been led aside among the peoples of the lands, and the hand of the leaders is in this unfaithfulness."

**9:3** And when I heard this word, I tore my garments and trembled, and I pulled out some of the hair of my head and of my beard, and I sat still.

**9:4** And everyone who pursued the word of the God of Israel gathered to me over the unfaithfulness of the exile; and I was sitting still until the evening sacrifice.

**9:5** And at the evening sacrifice I rose from my humiliation; and as I tore my garments, I trembled, and I bowed down on my knees and spread out my hands to God,

**9:6** and I said, "I am ashamed and humiliated to lift up my face to You, for our iniquities have been multiplied above our head, and our transgressions have grown great up to heaven.

**9:7** From the days of our fathers we have been in great transgression until this day; and in our iniquities we and our kings and our sons have been handed over into the hand of the kings of the nations, to the sword and to captivity, to plunder and to shame of our face, as it is this day.

**9:8** And now our God has made provision for us to leave us for deliverance and to give us a firm hold in the place of His sanctuary, to enlighten our eyes and to give a little reviving in our bondage.

**9:9** For we are slaves, and in our bondage the Lord our God did not forsake us, and He extended mercy to us before the kings of Persia, to give us reviving, that they might raise up the house of our God and restore its ruins, and to give us a wall in Judah and in Jerusalem.

**9:10** What shall we say, our God, after this? For we have forsaken Your commandments,

**9:11** which You gave to us by the hand of Your servants the prophets: 'The land into which you are entering to inherit it is a shifting land in the shifting of the peoples of the nations, in their abominations, with which they have filled it from mouth to mouth in their impurities.

**9:12** And now do not give your daughters to their sons, and do not take any of their daughters for your sons, and you shall not seek their peace and their good forever, so that you may grow strong and eat the good things of the land and leave it as an inheritance to your sons forever.'

**9:13** And after all that has come upon you in your evil deeds and in your great transgression, for there is none like our God, because You have lightened our iniquities and have given us deliverance,

**9:14** that we have turned back to break Your commandments and to intermarry with the peoples; do not be provoked to anger with us to complete destruction, so that there would be no remnant and no survivor.

**9:15** Lord God of Israel, You are righteous, for we have been left as survivors, as it is this day; we are before You in our transgressions, for it is not possible to stand before You because of this."

## Translator Notes

- **9:1** — The rare Greek noun makrymma (literally 'something put far away,' also in 9:11) renders Hebrew 'abominations,' and 'the Egyptian' translates the Greek transliteration Moserei (from Hebrew Mitsri). In 9:2 the Greek reads 'has been led aside' (parechthe) rather than 'has mingled.'
- **9:3** — Here and in 9:5 the Greek reads 'and trembled' (epallomen) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'and my robe.' In 9:4 'everyone who pursued the word' renders Greek diokon logon (versus Hebrew 'trembled at the words').
- **9:8** — The Greek reads 'has made provision for us' (epeskeuasato, likely an early Greek variant for epieikeusato, 'dealt graciously'), differing from the Hebrew text's 'for a brief moment favor has been shown.' In 9:7 the Greek reads 'our sons' rather than 'our priests.'
- **9:11** — 'A shifting land in the shifting of the peoples of the nations' renders Greek metakinoumene ... en metakinesei, connecting Hebrew niddah ('impurity') to the root for moving or shifting. 'From mouth to mouth' literally preserves a Hebrew idiom meaning 'from end to end.'
- **9:13** — The Greek shifts briefly to second-person plural ('you,' 'your') before returning to first-person plural, and reads 'for there is none like our God, because You have lightened our iniquities.' In 9:14 the Greek frames the confession as a statement followed by a plea ('do not be provoked to anger') rather than rhetorical questions.
