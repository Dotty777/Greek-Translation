## Text

**4:1** And those afflicting Judah and Benjamin heard that the sons of the exile were building a house for the Lord God of Israel;

**4:2** and they approached Zerubbabel and the leaders of the fathers' houses and said to them, "We will build with you, for like you we seek our God, and to Him we have been sacrificing from the days of Esarhaddon king of Assyria, who brought us here."

**4:3** And Zerubbabel and Jeshua and the rest of the leaders of the fathers' houses of Israel said to them, "It is not for us and for you to build a house for our God, for we together will build for the Lord our God, as Cyrus king of the Persians commanded us."

**4:4** And the people of the land were weakening the hands of the people of Judah, and they were hindering them from building,

**4:5** and hiring people against them, wanting to frustrate their plan, all the days of Cyrus king of the Persians and until the reign of Darius king of the Persians.

**4:6** And in the reign of Astherus, at the beginning of his reign, he wrote against those inhabiting Judah and Jerusalem.

**4:7** And in the days of Artaxerxes, he wrote in peace to Mithredath, Tabeel, and the rest of the fellow servants; to the king of the Persians the tax collector wrote a document in Aramaic and translated.

**4:8** Raul Badatamen and Shimshai the scribe wrote one letter against Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes.

**4:9** Thus decided Rehum Baal and Shimshai the scribe and the rest of our fellow servants, the Dinaites, the Pharesthachites, the Taraphallites, the Aphrasites, the Archuites, the Babylonians, the Susynachites, who are Elamites,

**4:10** and the rest of the nations whom Osnappar the great and honorable deported and settled in the cities of Samaria, and the rest beyond the River.

**4:11** This is the copy of the letter that they sent to him: "To King Artaxerxes: your servants, the men beyond the River.

**4:12** Let it be known to the king that the Jews who went up from Cyrus to us have come to Jerusalem; they are building the rebellious and wicked city, and they have finished its walls and raised up its foundations.

**4:13** Now therefore let it be known to the king that if that city is built and its walls are finished, there will be no taxes for you, nor will they give any; and this harms kings.

**4:14** And it is not right for us to see the dishonor of the king; for this reason we have sent and made this known to the king,

**4:15** so that he may search in the book of records of your fathers, and you will find and know that that city is a rebellious city, harming kings and provinces, and that places of refuge for slaves have been in its midst from days of old; because of these things this city was laid waste.

**4:16** We make it known to the king that if that city is built and its walls are finished, you have no peace."

**4:17** And the king sent peace and a statement to Rehum Balgam and Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their fellow servants living in Samaria and the rest beyond the River:

**4:18** "The tax collector whom you sent to us was summoned before me.

**4:19** And a decree was issued by me, and we searched and found that that city from days of old rises up against kings, and rebellions and places of refuge arise in it;

**4:20** and mighty kings arise over Jerusalem, ruling over all the west of the River, and full taxes and a portion are given to them.

**4:21** Now issue a decree to stop those men, and that city will not be built anymore because of the decree.

**4:22** Be on guard against showing slackness concerning this, so that destruction may never increase to the harm of kings."

**4:23** Then the tax collector of King Artaxerxes read aloud before Rehum and Shimshai the scribe, their fellow servants; and they went in haste to Jerusalem and in Judah, and stopped them with horses and force.

**4:24** Then the work of the house of God that is in Jerusalem ceased, and it remained at a standstill until the second year of Darius the king of the Persians.

## Translator Notes

- **4:6** — Greek Astherou is a distinct textual form corresponding to Ahasuerus in the Hebrew text; the Greek also reads singular "he wrote" and omits "an accusation."
- **4:7** — The Greek text spells Artaxerxes as Asarthatha here (and Arsartha in vv. 8, 11, 23), translates the Hebrew proper name Bishlam as "in peace," and renders the Persian loanword for "letter" as "the tax collector" (ho phorologos, also in vv. 18, 23; 5:5).
- **4:8** — Greek Raoul Badatamen (and Rehum Baal in v. 9, Rehum Balgam in v. 17) transliterates the Aramaic title for "commander/chancellor" rather than translating it; the Greek also spells Shimshai variously across the chapter and reads "who are Elamites" in v. 9 where the Aramaic lists the Dehavites.
- **4:12** — The Greek text reads "from Cyrus" (apo Kyrou) where the Aramaic has "from you," and in v. 14 omits the clause about eating the salt of the palace.
- **4:15** — Greek phygadeia doulon ("places of refuge for slaves") reflects a different reading of the Aramaic phrase for "stirring up sedition" (similarly in v. 19); in v. 17 Greek phasin is read as the noun "statement" rather than a verb.
