## Text

**1:1** And in the first year of Cyrus the king of the Persians, so that it might be fulfilled from the mouth of Jeremiah, He roused the spirit of Cyrus king of the Persians, and he issued a proclamation in all his kingdom, and also in writing, saying,

**1:2** "Thus said Cyrus king of the Persians: The God of heaven has given me all the kingdoms, and He Himself has charged me to build a house for Him in Jerusalem, which is in Judah.

**1:3** Who among you is from all His people? And his God will be with him, and he will go up to Jerusalem.

**1:4** And as for everyone who remains from all the places where he resides, the men of his place will support him with silver and gold and goods and livestock, along with the freewill offering for the house of the God who is in Jerusalem."

**1:5** And the leaders of the ancestral houses of Judah and Benjamin, and the priests, and the Levites rose up, all those whose spirit God roused to go up to build the house that is in Jerusalem.

**1:6** And all those around them strengthened their hands with vessels of silver, with gold, with goods, and with livestock, and with gifts, besides the freewill offerings.

**1:7** And King Cyrus brought out the vessels of the house, which Nebuchadnezzar had taken from Jerusalem and placed in the house of his god.

**1:8** And Cyrus king of the Persians brought them out into the hand of Mithredath Tasbarenus, and he counted them out to Sabanasar, leader of Judah.

**1:9** And this is their number: for the Lord, thirty of gold and one thousand of silver, twenty-nine altered items,

**1:10** thirty bowls of gold and six of silver, and one thousand other vessels.

**1:11** All the vessels of gold and of silver were five thousand four hundred, all of them going up from the exile out of Babylon to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — This Greek text lacks "the word of the Lord" and "the Lord" here (as well as "the Lord" in vv. 2, 5, and 7, and the closing clause of v. 3), leaving "to be fulfilled" without an explicit noun and "He roused" with an implied divine subject (compare v. 5).
- **1:2** — Greek epeskepsato me ep' eme (literally "visited/charged me upon me") doubles the pronominal object, reflecting the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew idiom for appointing or commissioning; the verse also reads "all the kingdoms" without "of the earth".
- **1:4** — Greek lempsontai auton literally means "will take him [up]," used idiomatically here in the sense of "support" or "assist" a departing exile with provisions (compare v. 6).
- **1:8** — Greek Tasbarenou (a textual variant for Gasbarenou) transliterates the Persian title for "treasurer" (Hebrew haggizbar) as a proper name, and Greek Sabanasar corresponds to Hebrew Sheshbazzar.
- **1:9** — Greek Kyrio ("for the Lord") appears where other Septuagint witnesses read psyktēres ("basins/coolers"), leaving the adjectives "gold" and "silver" without a noun; paregmena (literally "things passed along/altered") renders an obscure Hebrew term often understood as "knives" or "censers".
- **1:10** — Greek kepphoures transliterates the Hebrew vessel term keporim ("bowls" or "covered bowls"); this Greek text also reads "six" silver bowls (rather than "four hundred and ten" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text) and omits Sheshbazzar/Sabanasar in v. 11.
