## Text

**14:1** And Samson went down to Timnah, and he saw a woman in Timnah from the daughters of the foreigners.

**14:2** And he went up and told his father and his mother and said, "I have seen a woman in Timnah from the daughters of the Philistines; and now, take her for me as a wife."

**14:3** And his father and his mother said to him, "Are there not daughters of your brothers, and a woman from all my people, that you are going to take a wife from the uncircumcised foreigners?" And Samson said to his father, "Take this one for me, because she is right in my eyes."

**14:4** And his father and his mother did not know that it was from the Lord, for He Himself was seeking vengeance from the foreigners. And at that time the foreigners were ruling in Israel.

**14:5** And Samson and his father and his mother went down to Timnah; and he came as far as the vineyard of Timnah, and look, a young lion was roaring to meet him.

**14:6** And the Spirit of the Lord leaped upon him, and he crushed it as one crushes a young goat, and there was nothing in his hands. And he did not tell his father and his mother what he had done.

**14:7** And they went down and spoke to the woman, and she was right in the eyes of Samson.

**14:8** And he returned after some days to take her; and he turned aside to see the carcass of the lion, and look, a swarm of bees was in the mouth of the lion, and honey.

**14:9** And he took it out into his hands, and went along, walking and eating; and he went to his father and his mother, and gave to them, and they ate, and he did not tell them that he had taken the honey out from the mouth of the lion.

**14:10** And his father went down to the woman; and Samson made a drinking feast there for seven days, for so the young men do.

**14:11** And it happened when they saw him, that they took thirty invited guests, and they were with him.

**14:12** And Samson said to them, "I propose a riddle to you: if you surely tell it within the seven days of the drinking feast and find it out, I will give you thirty linen garments and thirty robes of clothing;

**14:13** and if you are not able to tell me, you yourselves shall give me thirty linen cloths and thirty changes of robes of clothing." And they said to him, "Propose the riddle, and we will hear it."

**14:14** And he said to them, "What edible thing came out from an eater, and from a strong one, what sweet thing?" And they were not able to tell the riddle for three days.

**14:15** And it happened on the fourth day that they said to Samson's wife, "Now deceive your husband and let him tell you the riddle, so that we do not burn you and your father's house with fire. Or have you invited us to force us out?"

**14:16** And Samson's wife wept before him and said, "You only hate me and have not loved me, because you have not told me the riddle that you proposed to the sons of my people." And Samson said to her, "If I have not told my father and my mother, shall I tell you?"

**14:17** And she wept before him for the seven days during which they had the drinking feast; and it happened on the seventh day that he told her, because she harassed him; and she told the sons of her people.

**14:18** And the men of the city said to him on the seventh day before the sun rose, "What is sweeter than honey, and what is stronger than a lion?" And Samson said to them, "If you had not plowed with my heifer, you would not have known my riddle."

**14:19** And the Spirit of the Lord leaped upon him, and he went down to Ashkelon and struck down thirty men from among them, and took their garments, and gave the robes to those who had told the riddle. And Samson was enraged with fury, and he went up to his father's house.

**14:20** And Samson's wife came to belong to one of his friends whom he had befriended.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — Here and in verses 3-4 the Greek uses "allophyloi" ("foreigners"), whereas verse 2 uses "Phylistieim" ("Philistines").
- **14:4** — The Greek reads "vengeance" (ekdikesis) rather than "an occasion" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **14:8** — Here and in verse 9 the Greek reads "mouth" (stoma) rather than "body" or "carcass" as in the Hebrew.
- **14:14** — Codex Vaticanus frames Samson's riddle as an explicit question beginning with "What" rather than a declarative statement.
- **14:18** — The Greek reads "before the sun rose" (compare also "on the fourth day" in verse 15), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "before the sun went down" (and "on the seventh day" in verse 15).
