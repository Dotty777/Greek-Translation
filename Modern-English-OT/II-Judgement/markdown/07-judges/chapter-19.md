## Text

**19:1** And there was a Levite man residing as an outsider in the flanks of Mount Ephraim, and he took for himself a concubine wife from Bethlehem of Judah.

**19:2** And his concubine went away from him, and she departed from him to her father's house, to Bethlehem of Judah; and she was there for a period of four months.

**19:3** And her husband rose up and went after her to speak to her heart, to bring her back to himself; and his young man was with him, and a pair of donkeys. And she brought him into her father's house; and the father of the young woman saw him and rejoiced to meet him.

**19:4** And his father-in-law, the father of the young woman, detained him, and he stayed with him for three days; and they ate and drank, and spent the night there.

**19:5** And it happened on the fourth day that they rose early in the morning, and he rose up to go; and the father of the young woman said to his son-in-law, "Strengthen your heart with a morsel of bread, and after this you will go."

**19:6** And he sat down, and the two of them ate together and drank; and the father of the young woman said to the man, "Come now, spend the night, and your heart will be made glad."

**19:7** And the man rose up to go; and his father-in-law urged him strongly, and he stayed and spent the night there.

**19:8** And he rose early in the morning on the fifth day to go, and the father of the young woman said, "Please strengthen your heart, and go on campaign until the day declines." And the two of them ate.

**19:9** And the man rose up to go, he and his concubine and his young man, and his father-in-law, the father of the young woman, said to him, "Look now, the day has grown weak toward evening; spend the night here, and your heart will be made glad, and tomorrow you will rise early for your way, and you will go to your tent."

**19:10** And the man was not willing to spend the night, and he rose up and departed, and came as far as opposite Jebus, that is, Jerusalem; and with him was a pair of saddled donkeys, and his concubine was with him.

**19:11** And they came as far as Jebus; and the day had advanced very far, and the young man said to his master, "Come now, and let us turn aside into this city of the Jebusites, and let us spend the night in it."

**19:12** And his master said to him, "We will not turn aside into a foreign city in which there is no one from the sons of Israel here, and we will pass on as far as Gibeah."

**19:13** And he said to his young man, "Come, and let us draw near to one of the places, and we will spend the night in Gibeah or in Ramah."

**19:14** And they passed on and traveled, and the sun set on them near Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin.

**19:15** And they turned aside there to go in to spend the night in Gibeah; and they went in and sat down in the open square of the city, and there was no man taking them into a house to spend the night.

**19:16** And look, an old man was coming from his work out of the field in the evening, and the man was from Mount Ephraim and was residing as an outsider in Gibeah, and the men of the place were sons of Benjamin.

**19:17** And he lifted up his eyes and saw the traveling man in the open square of the city; and the old man said, "Where are you going, and where are you coming from?"

**19:18** And he said to him, "We are passing along from Bethlehem of Judah to the flanks of Mount Ephraim; I am from there, and I went as far as Bethlehem of Judah, and I am going to my house; and there is no man taking me into the house.

**19:19** Moreover, there is straw and fodder for our donkeys, and there is bread and wine for me and for the female servant and for the young man with your servants; there is no lack of anything."

**19:20** And the old man said, "Peace to you; only let all your lack be upon me; only you will by no means spend the night in the open square."

**19:21** And he brought him into his house, and he made a place for the donkeys; and they washed their feet, and ate and drank.

**19:22** Now while they were making their heart glad, look, men of the city, sons of lawless men, surrounded the house, pounding on the door; and they spoke to the man, the master of the house, the old man, saying, "Bring out the man who entered into your house, so that we may know him."

**19:23** And the man, the master of the house, went out to them and said, "No, brothers, please do not act wickedly after this man has entered into my house; do not commit this folly.

**19:24** Look, here are my virgin daughter and his concubine. I will bring them out, and humiliate them, and do to them what is good in your eyes; and to this man you will not do this matter of folly."

**19:25** And the men were not willing to listen to him; and the man seized his concubine and brought her out to them outside; and they knew her, and kept abusing her all night until morning, and they sent her away as morning rose.

**19:26** And the woman came toward dawn, and fell down at the door of the house where her husband was, until it grew light.

**19:27** And her husband rose up in the morning and opened the doors of the house, and went out to go on his way; and look, the woman, his concubine, had fallen at the doors of the house, with her hands upon the threshold.

**19:28** And he said to her, "Get up, and let us go." And she did not answer, because she was dead. And he took her onto the donkey, and went to his place.

**19:29** And he took the sword and took hold of his concubine, and dismembered her into twelve parts, and sent them throughout all the territory of Israel.

**19:30** And it happened that everyone who saw it was saying, "Such a thing as this has not happened and has not been seen from the day the sons of Israel went up from the land of Egypt even until this day; set counsel for yourselves concerning it, and speak."

## Translator Notes

- **19:2** — The Codex Vaticanus Greek reads 'went away from him,' whereas Codex Alexandrinus reads 'became angry with him' and the Hebrew text reads 'played the prostitute against him.'
- **19:8** — The Codex Vaticanus Greek literally reads 'go on campaign' (strateuson), likely an ancient scribal spelling error for 'linger' (stranggeuson), matching the Hebrew text.
- **19:18** — The Greek reads 'to my house,' differing from the Masoretic Hebrew text's 'to the house of the Lord.'
- **19:21** — The Codex Vaticanus Greek reads 'made a place for the donkeys,' whereas the Hebrew text reads 'gave fodder to the donkeys.'
- **19:28** — The Greek explicitly states 'because she was dead,' a clause absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
