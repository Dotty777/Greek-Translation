## Text

**3:1** And these are the nations that the Lord left to test Israel by them, all those who had not known the wars of Canaan,

**3:2** only on account of the generations of the sons of Israel, to teach them war; only those before them did not know them:

**3:3** the five satrapies of the foreigners, and all the Canaanites, and the Sidonians, and the Hivites who lived in Lebanon, from Mount Hermon to Lebo-hamath.

**3:4** And it was to test Israel by them, to know whether they would listen to the commandments of the Lord, which He commanded their fathers by the hand of Moses.

**3:5** And the sons of Israel lived in the midst of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites.

**3:6** And they took their daughters for themselves as wives, and they gave their own daughters to their sons, and they served their gods.

**3:7** And the sons of Israel did what was evil before the Lord, and they forgot the Lord their God, and they served the Baals and the groves.

**3:8** And the Lord became angry with wrath against Israel, and He sold them into the hand of Cushan-rishathaim, king of the rivers of Syria. And the sons of Israel served Cushan-rishathaim eight years.

**3:9** And the sons of Israel cried out to the Lord, and the Lord raised up a savior for Israel, and he saved them: Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb's brother, who was younger than him.

**3:10** And the Spirit of the Lord came upon him, and he judged Israel. And he went out to war against Cushan-rishathaim; and the Lord delivered Cushan-rishathaim, king of Syria of the rivers, into his hand, and his hand prevailed over Cushan-rishathaim.

**3:11** And the land was at rest for forty years; and Othniel the son of Kenaz died.

**3:12** And the sons of Israel again did what was evil before the Lord; and the Lord strengthened Eglon, king of Moab, against Israel, because they had done what was evil before the Lord.

**3:13** And he gathered to himself all the sons of Ammon and Amalek, and he went and struck Israel, and he took possession of the City of Palms.

**3:14** And the sons of Israel served Eglon, king of Moab, eighteen years.

**3:15** And the sons of Israel cried out to the Lord; and He raised up for them a savior, Ehud the son of Gera, the son of Jemini, an ambidextrous man. And the sons of Israel sent gifts by his hand to Eglon, king of Moab.

**3:16** And Ehud made for himself a two-edged dagger, a span in its length, and he strapped it under his cloak on his right thigh.

**3:17** And he went and presented the gifts to Eglon, king of Moab; and Eglon was a very handsome man.

**3:18** And it happened, when Ehud had finished presenting the gifts, that he sent away those who carried the gifts.

**3:19** And he himself turned back from the carved images that were by Gilgal, and Ehud said, "I have a secret word for you, king." And Eglon said to him, "Be silent!" And he sent away from himself all those who stood beside him.

**3:20** And Ehud went in to him; and he was sitting in his own summer upper room completely alone. And Ehud said, "I have a word of God for you, king." And Eglon rose up from the throne near him.

**3:21** And it happened, at the very moment he rose up, that Ehud stretched out his left hand and took the dagger from off his right thigh and thrust it into his belly,

**3:22** and he drove even the hilt in after the flame; and the fat closed over the flame, for he did not pull the dagger out of his belly.

**3:23** And Ehud went out into the porch, and he went out past those who were stationed, and he closed the doors of the upper room upon him, and bolted them.

**3:24** And he went out; and his servants came and looked, and look, the doors of the upper room were bolted. And they said, "Is he perhaps emptying his feet in the summer chamber?"

**3:25** And they waited until they were embarrassed. And look, there was no one opening the doors of the upper room; so they took the key and opened them, and look, their lord had fallen on the ground, dead.

**3:26** And Ehud escaped while they were in confusion, and there was no one paying attention to him; and he passed by the carved images and escaped to Seteirotha.

**3:27** And it happened, when Ehud came into the land of Israel, that he sounded a horn in the hill country of Ephraim; and the sons of Israel went down with him from the hill country, and he was in front of them.

**3:28** And he said to them, "Come down after me, for the Lord God has delivered our enemies, Moab, into our hand." And they went down after him, and they seized beforehand the fords of the Jordan of Moab, and he did not allow a man to cross over.

**3:29** And they struck Moab on that day, about ten thousand men, every stout one and every man of strength, and not a man escaped.

**3:30** And Moab was humbled on that day under the hand of Israel, and the land was at rest for eighty years; and Ehud judged them until he died.

**3:31** And after him rose up Shamgar the son of Dinach, and he struck down the foreigners, up to six hundred men, with the plowshare of the oxen, and he also saved Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **3:15** — "Jemini" transliterates the Greek rendering (uion tou Iemenei) of the Hebrew expression for a Benjaminite; "ambidextrous" (amfoterodexion) is the Septuagint translation where the Hebrew describes Ehud as restricted in his right hand (left-handed).
- **3:17** — Greek asteios ("handsome" or "refined") stands where the Hebrew text describes Eglon as a very fat man.
- **3:22** — Greek flox ("flame") literally renders the Hebrew word lahav, which means both "flame" and "blade"; Codex Vaticanus also lacks the final clause found in the Hebrew ("and the refuse came out").
- **3:26** — "Seteirotha" (Seteirotha) is the distinctive Codex Vaticanus form of the place name corresponding to Seirah in the Hebrew; the preceding clause "and there was no one paying attention to him" is likewise a Septuagint addition.
- **3:31** — "Dinach" (Deinach) is the distinctive Codex Vaticanus name for Shamgar's father (Hebrew: Anath); Greek also reads "foreigners" (allofylous, the standard Septuagint term for the Philistines in Judges) and "plowshare of the oxen" (arotropodi ton boon, Hebrew: oxgoad).
