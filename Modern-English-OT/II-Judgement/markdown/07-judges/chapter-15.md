## Text

**15:1** And it came about after some days, in the days of the wheat harvest, that Samson visited his wife with a kid of goats, and he said, "I will go in to my wife into the inner room." But her father did not allow him to go in.

**15:2** And her father said, "I truly said that you utterly hated her, so I gave her to one from among your friends. Is not her sister, who is younger than her, better than she is? Please let her be yours instead of her."

**15:3** And Samson said to them, "I am blameless even this once regarding foreigners, because I am doing evil to them."

**15:4** And Samson went and caught three hundred foxes, and took torches, and turned tail to tail, and placed one torch between the two tails and bound them.

**15:5** And he kindled fire in the torches and sent them out into the ears of grain of the foreigners; and they were burned from threshing floor even to upright ears of grain, and even to vineyard and olive tree.

**15:6** And the foreigners said, "Who did these things?" And they said, "Samson, the son-in-law of the Timnite, because he took his wife and gave her to the one from among his friends." And the foreigners went up and burned her and her father with fire.

**15:7** And Samson said to them, "If you act like this toward her, surely I will take vengeance on you, and afterward I will cease."

**15:8** And he struck them shin upon thigh, a great blow; and he went down and settled in a cleft of the rock of Etam.

**15:9** And the foreigners went up and encamped in Judah, and they were spread out in Lehi.

**15:10** And the men of Judah said, "Why have you come up against us?" And the foreigners said, "We have come up to bind Samson, and to do to him just as he did to us."

**15:11** And three thousand men from Judah went down to the cleft of the rock of Etam, and they said to Samson, "Do you not know that the foreigners will rule over us? And what is this that you have done to us?" And Samson said to them, "Just as they did to me, so I did to them."

**15:12** And they said to him, "We have come down to bind you, to give you into the hand of foreigners." And Samson said to them, "Swear to me that you yourselves will never fall upon me."

**15:13** And they spoke to him, saying, "No, rather we will certainly bind you and deliver you into their hand, and we will certainly not put you to death." And they bound him with two new ropes and brought him up from that rock.

**15:14** And they came as far as Jawbone, and the foreigners shouted and ran to meet him. And the Spirit of the Lord leaped upon him, and the ropes that were on his arms became like flax that was burned in fire, and his bonds melted away from his hands.

**15:15** And he found a jawbone of a donkey that had been cast aside, and he stretched out his hand and took it, and struck down a thousand men with it.

**15:16** And Samson said, "With a jawbone of a donkey I utterly wiped them out, for with the jawbone of the donkey I struck down a thousand men."

**15:17** And it came about when he finished speaking, that he threw the jawbone out of his hand, and he called that place Removal of a Jawbone.

**15:18** And he was exceedingly thirsty, and he wept to the Lord and said, "You were well pleased with this great deliverance by the hand of Your servant, and now I will die of thirst and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised."

**15:19** And God split open the pit that was in Jawbone, and water came out from it. And he drank, and his spirit returned and he lived. Therefore its name was called Spring of the One Who Calls, which is in Jawbone, until this day.

**15:20** And he judged Israel in the days of the foreigners for twenty years.

## Translator Notes

- **15:3** — The Greek here calls the Philistines allophyloi ("foreigners"), as through most of the Samson narrative; Judges also uses Phylistieim (e.g., 10:6; 13:1; 14:2).
- **15:15** — Codex B describes the donkey's jawbone as "cast aside" (ekrerimmenen, discarded), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "fresh."
- **15:16** — Codex B reads "I utterly wiped them out" (exaleiphon exeleipsa autous), diverging from the Hebrew wordplay "heaps upon heaps."
- **15:17** — Greek translates the Hebrew place name Ramath-lehi as Anairesis siagonos ("Removal of a Jawbone," also capable of meaning "Destruction of a Jawbone"), paralleling the translation of Lehi as "Jawbone" in vv. 14 and 19 (transcribed Leuei in v. 9).
- **15:18** — Codex B reads "wept" (eklausen, also in 16:28) rather than "called," and "were well pleased with" (eudokesas) where the Hebrew text reads "have given."
