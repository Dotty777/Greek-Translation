## Text

**36:1** And the people of the land took Jehoahaz son of Josiah, and they anointed him and appointed him as king in place of his father.

**36:2** Jehoahaz was twenty-three years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. And his mother's name was Abital, daughter of Jeremiah, from Libnah. And he did what was evil in the sight of the Lord, according to all that his fathers did. And Pharaoh Neco bound him at Dablatha in the land of Hamath, so that he would not reign in Jerusalem.

**36:3** And the king removed him to Egypt, and he imposed a tribute on the land of a hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold.

**36:4** And Pharaoh Neco appointed Eliakim son of Josiah, king of Judah, in place of Josiah his father, and changed his name to Jehoiakim. And Pharaoh Neco took Jehoahaz his brother and brought him into Egypt, and he died there. And he gave the silver and the gold to Pharaoh. Then the land began to be taxed in order to give the silver at the command of Pharaoh, and each one according to his ability exacted the silver and the gold from the people of the land, to give to Pharaoh Neco.

**36:5** Jehoiakim was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem, and his mother's name was Zechora, daughter of Neriah, from Ramah. And he did what was evil before the Lord, according to everything that his fathers did. In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came into the land, and he served him three years, and rebelled against him. And the Lord sent against him the Chaldeans and raiding bands of Syrians and raiding bands of Moabites and of the sons of Ammon and of Samaria, and they departed after this word, according to the word of the Lord by the hand of His servants the prophets. Yet the wrath of the Lord was upon Judah, so that it would depart from His presence because of Manasseh, for all that he did, and for the innocent blood that Jehoiakim shed; he filled Jerusalem with innocent blood. And the Lord was not willing to destroy them.

**36:6** And Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up against him, and bound him in bronze fetters and led him away to Babylon.

**36:7** And he carried off part of the vessels of the house of the Lord to Babylon, and placed them in his temple in Babylon.

**36:8** And the rest of the acts of Jehoiakim and all that he did, look, are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah? And Jehoiakim slept with his fathers, and was buried in Ganozae with his fathers. And Jeconiah his son reigned in his place.

**36:9** Jeconiah was eight years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months and ten days in Jerusalem, and he did what was evil in the sight of the Lord.

**36:10** And at the turn of the year King Nebuchadnezzar sent and brought him into Babylon with the desirable vessels of the house of the Lord, and he made Zedekiah, his father's brother, king over Judah and Jerusalem.

**36:11** Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem.

**36:12** And he did what was evil in the sight of the Lord his God; he did not feel shame before Jeremiah the prophet and from the mouth of the Lord,

**36:13** by setting aside the matters with King Nebuchadnezzar that he had made him swear by God; and he stiffened his neck and fortified his heart so as not to return to the Lord, the God of Israel.

**36:14** And all the nobles of Judah and the priests and the people of the land increased in acting unfaithfully with the unfaithful deeds of the abominations of the nations, and they defiled the house of the Lord that was in Jerusalem.

**36:15** And the Lord God of their fathers sent out by hand, rising early and sending His messengers, because He was sparing His people and His sanctuary.

**36:16** And they were deriding His messengers and despising His words and mocking His prophets, until the wrath of the Lord rose up against His people, until there was no healing.

**36:17** And He brought against them the king of the Chaldeans, and he killed their young men with the sword in the house of His sanctuary; and he did not spare Zedekiah, and they had no mercy on their virgins, and they led away their older men. He handed everything over into their hands.

**36:18** And all the vessels of the house of God, the large and the small, and the treasures, and all the treasures of the king and of the high officials, he brought all of them into Babylon.

**36:19** And he burned the house of the Lord, and tore down the wall of Jerusalem, and burned its citadels with fire, and every beautiful vessel to destruction.

**36:20** And he exiled those who remained to Babylon, and they became servants to him and to his sons until the kingdom of the Medes,

**36:21** so that the word of the Lord through the mouth of Jeremiah might be fulfilled, until the land received its Sabbaths to keep Sabbath, to the completion of seventy years.

**36:22** In the first year of the king of the Persians, after the word of the Lord through the mouth of Jeremiah was fulfilled, the Lord roused the spirit of Cyrus king of the Persians, and he gave orders to proclaim throughout all his kingdom in writing, saying,

**36:23** "Thus says Cyrus king of the Persians to all the kingdoms of the earth: 'The Lord God of heaven has given to me, and He Himself has commanded me to build a house for Him in Jerusalem in Judea. Who among you is of all His people? His God will be with him, and let him go up.'"

## Translator Notes

- **36:2** — The Greek expands this verse with material parallel to 2 Kings 23:31-33 and preserves the distinct names Abital (versus Hamutal in 2 Kings 23:31 MT) and Dablatha (versus Riblah in 2 Kings 23:33 MT).
- **36:5** — The Greek includes a long expansion parallel to 2 Kings 23:36-24:4 with distinct names (Zechora daughter of Neriah from Ramah, and Samaria among the raiders), attributes the innocent blood to Jehoiakim, and reads "not willing to destroy them" rather than "forgive."
- **36:8** — The burial notice is a Greek expansion in which "Ganozae" transliterates the Hebrew phrase for "the garden of Uzza" (cf. 2 Kings 21:18, 26); the Greek also names his successor Jeconiah (here and in v. 9) rather than Jehoiachin.
- **36:17** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "young man," the Greek reads "Zedekiah," and likewise has "His sanctuary" (singular) together with plural verbs ("they had no mercy... they led away").
- **36:20** — The Greek reads "until the kingdom of the Medes," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Persia" (though the Greek refers to "the Persians" in vv. 22-23).
- **36:23** — The Greek puts "to all the kingdoms of the earth" in the dative as the addressees of Cyrus's decree, leaving "The Lord God of heaven has given to me" without an expressed direct object (whereas the Hebrew makes "all the kingdoms of the earth" the object of "has given").
