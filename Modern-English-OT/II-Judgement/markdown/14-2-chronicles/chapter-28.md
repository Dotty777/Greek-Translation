## Text

**28:1** Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. And he did not do what was right before the Lord, like David his father.

**28:2** And he walked according to the ways of the king of Israel, for he also made carved images among their idols.

**28:3** He sacrificed in Gaibenthom and led his children through fire, and the abominations of the nations whom the Lord had utterly destroyed from before the sons of Israel.

**28:4** And he burned incense on the high places and on the rooftops and beneath every leafy tree.

**28:5** And the Lord his God delivered him into the hand of the kings of Syria, and He struck him, and they took a great number of captives from them. Indeed, He also delivered him into the hands of the kings of Israel, and he struck himself with a great blow.

**28:6** And Pekah the son of Remaliah, king of Israel, sent in Judah in one day one hundred twenty thousand men mighty in strength, because they had forsaken the Lord, the God of their fathers.

**28:7** And Zichri, the mighty man of Ephraim, killed Maaseiah the son of the king, and Azrikam, ruler of his house, and Elkanah, the successor of the king.

**28:8** And the sons of Israel took captive from their brothers three hundred thousand: women, sons, and daughters; and they plundered much plunder from them, and brought the plunder to Samaria.

**28:9** And there was the prophet of the Lord, Oded by name; and he went out to meet the army of those coming to Samaria and said to them, "Look, the wrath of the Lord, the God of your fathers, was upon Judah, and He delivered them into your hands, and you killed among them in wrath; it has reached as far as the heavens.

**28:10** And now you say you will take possession of the sons of Judah and Jerusalem as male slaves and female slaves. Look, is it not for you with yourselves to bear witness to the Lord our God?

**28:11** And now listen to me and return the captives of your brothers whom you took captive, because the wrath of the Lord is upon you."

**28:12** And leaders from the sons of Ephraim stood up, Udeia the son of Johanan, and Zechariah the son of Meshillemoth, and Hezekiah the son of Shallum, and Amasiah the son of Choad, against those coming from the war,

**28:13** and said to them, "You must by no means bring the captives here to us, for you speak of sinning against the Lord upon us, to add to our sins and to the ignorance; for our sin is great, and the wrath of the Lord God is upon Israel."

**28:14** And the warriors left the captives and the plunder before the leaders and all the assembly.

**28:15** And men who were called by name stood up and helped the captives, and clothed all the naked ones from the plunder; they dressed them and put sandals on them, and gave them both to eat and to anoint themselves, and also helped every weak one on pack animals, and brought them to Jericho, the city of palm trees, to their brothers; and they returned to Samaria.

**28:16** At that time Ahaz sent to the king of Assyria to help him,

**28:17** and in this also, because the Idumeans had attacked and struck in Judah and taken captives.

**28:18** And the foreigners attacked the cities of the lowland and on the south of Judah, and they captured Beth-shemesh, Aijalon, and Galero, and Soco and its villages, and Galezo and its villages; and they settled there.

**28:19** For the Lord humbled Judah because of Ahaz king of Judah, because he had turned away in rebellion from the Lord.

**28:20** And Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria came against him, and they afflicted him.

**28:21** And Ahaz took the things in the house of the Lord and the things in the house of the king and of the leaders, and gave them to the king of Assyria; yet it was not for their help,

**28:22** but only for his affliction. And he went on even more to turn away from the Lord, and the king said,

**28:23** "I will seek the gods of Damascus who strike me." And he said, "The gods of the king of Syria, they will strengthen them; therefore I will sacrifice to them, and they will help me." And they became a stumbling block to him and to all Israel.

**28:24** And Ahaz removed the vessels of the house of the Lord and cut them in pieces, and shut the doors of the house of the Lord, and made for himself altars in every corner,

**28:25** and in every single city in Judah he made high places to burn incense to foreign gods; and they provoked the Lord, the God of their fathers, to anger.

**28:26** And the rest of his matters and his deeds, the first and the last, look, they are written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.

**28:27** And Ahaz slept with his fathers, and he was buried in the city of David, for they did not bring him into the tombs of the kings of Israel. And Hezekiah his son became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **28:3** — Gaibenthom is the Greek transliteration of Hebrew Ge Ben-hinnom ("Valley of the Son of Hinnom"). The Greek also reads "and the abominations" (kai ta bdelygmata), likely an inner-Greek shift from "according to the abominations" (kata ta bdelygmata).
- **28:5** — The Greek reads plural "kings" of Syria and Israel (without mentioning Damascus) and has a reflexive pronoun in the final clause ("struck himself with a great blow").
- **28:6** — The Greek reads "sent" (apesteilen) rather than "killed" (apekteinen), an early Greek scribal variation.
- **28:10** — The Greek closing question literally asks whether it is not for them "with yourselves to bear witness to the Lord our God," diverging from the Hebrew text's reference to "guilts against the LORD your God."
- **28:12** — The Greek names Udeia, Zechariah, Amasiah, and Choad differ from the Hebrew text's Azariah, Berechiah, Amasa, and Hadlai.
- **28:18** — "The foreigners" translates hoi allophyloi, the standard Septuagint designation for the Philistines; the Greek also has the distinct town names Galero and Galezo (corresponding to Gederoth and Gimzo) and omits Timnah.
