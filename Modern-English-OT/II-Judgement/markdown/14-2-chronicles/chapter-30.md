## Text

**30:1** And Hezekiah sent to all Israel and Judah, and he wrote letters to Ephraim and Manasseh to come to the house of the Lord in Jerusalem to observe the Passover to the Lord God of Israel.

**30:2** And the king and the officials and all the assembly in Jerusalem took counsel to observe the Passover in the second month.

**30:3** For they were not able to observe it at that time, because the priests had not been purified in sufficient number, and the people had not been gathered to Jerusalem.

**30:4** And the matter was pleasing before the king and before the assembly.

**30:5** And they established a decree for a proclamation to go throughout all Israel, from Beersheba to Dan, that they should come to observe the Passover to the Lord God of Israel in Jerusalem, because a multitude had not observed it according to what was written.

**30:6** And the runners went with the letters from the king and the officials into all Israel and Judah, according to the command of the king, saying, "Sons of Israel, return to the God of Abraham and Isaac and Israel, and bring back those who have been rescued, who were left from the hand of the king of Assyria.

**30:7** And do not become like your fathers and your brothers, who departed from the Lord, the God of their fathers, and He gave them over to desolation, just as you see.

**30:8** And now do not harden your hearts; give glory to the Lord God, and enter into His sanctuary, which He consecrated forever, and serve the Lord your God, and He will turn away from you the fury of wrath.

**30:9** For when you return to the Lord, your brothers and your children will find compassion before all who took them captive, and He will bring them back to this land; for the Lord our God is merciful and compassionate, and He will not turn His face away from you, if we return to Him."

**30:10** And the runners were passing from city to city in the hill country of Ephraim and Manasseh, and as far as Zebulun; but they were like those laughing at and mocking them.

**30:11** Yet men of Asher and from Manasseh and from Zebulun turned and came to Jerusalem and into Judah.

**30:12** And the hand of the Lord came to give them one heart, to come to act according to the commands of the king and the officials by the word of the Lord.

**30:13** And many people were gathered to Jerusalem to observe the Feast of Unleavened Bread in the second month, a very large assembly.

**30:14** And they rose up and removed the altars that were in Jerusalem, and everything on which they burned incense to the false ones they tore down and threw into the brook Kidron.

**30:15** And they slaughtered the Passover on the fourteenth day of the second month; and the priests and the Levites were ashamed and purified themselves, and they brought burnt offerings into the house of the Lord.

**30:16** And they stood at their station according to their ordinance, according to the commandment of Moses, the man of God, and the priests were receiving the blood from the hand of the Levites.

**30:17** For a multitude of the assembly had not been purified, and the Levites were to slaughter the Passover for everyone who was not able to be purified to the Lord.

**30:18** For most of the people from Ephraim and Manasseh, Issachar, and Zebulun had not purified themselves, yet they ate this Passover contrary to what was written. And Hezekiah prayed for them, saying, "May the good Lord make atonement

**30:19** for every heart that directs itself to seek the Lord, the God of their fathers, though not according to the purification of the sanctuary."

**30:20** And the Lord heard Hezekiah and healed the people.

**30:21** And the sons of Israel who were found in Jerusalem observed the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days with great gladness, singing hymns to the Lord day by day, and the priests and the Levites with instruments to the Lord.

**30:22** And Hezekiah spoke to the heart of all the Levites and of those who showed good understanding for the Lord; and they completed the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days, sacrificing sacrifices of deliverance and giving thanks to the Lord, the God of their fathers.

**30:23** And the assembly took counsel together to observe seven more days; and they observed seven days with gladness.

**30:24** For Hezekiah presented as an offering to Judah, to the assembly, a thousand calves and seven thousand sheep; and the officials presented as an offering to the people a thousand calves and ten thousand sheep, and the holy things of the priests in abundance.

**30:25** And all the assembly rejoiced, the priests and the Levites, and all the assembly of Judah, and those who were found from Israel, and the proselytes who came in from the land of Israel and those dwelling from Judah.

**30:26** And there was great gladness in Jerusalem; from the days of Solomon son of David, king of Israel, there had not been such a feast in Jerusalem.

**30:27** And the priests, the Levites, rose up and blessed the people; and their voice was heard, and their prayer came to His holy dwelling place, to heaven.

## Translator Notes

- **30:6** — The Greek uses a second-person plural imperative with a direct object ("and bring back those who have been rescued") rather than the Hebrew third-person verb ("and he will return to the remnant"), and reads "God" without "Lord" and singular "king of Assyria."
- **30:9** — The Greek preposition ἀντί ("instead of") here functions like ἔναντι ("before"), and the clause shifts grammatical person ("our God," "from you," "if we return"), with third-person singular future ἀποστρέψει ("He will bring [them] back").
- **30:14** — "To the false ones" (τοῖς ψευδέσιν) is a distinctive Greek expression referring to false gods or idols, not present in the Hebrew text.
- **30:22** — The Greek reads "completed the Feast of Unleavened Bread" where the Hebrew reads "ate the festival," and "sacrifices of deliverance" literally translates θυσίας σωτηρίου, the Septuagint equivalent for peace offerings.
- **30:24** — The Greek reads dative "to Judah" (τῷ Ἰούδᾳ) without "king," and closes with the verbless phrase "and the holy things of the priests in abundance" (τὰ ἅγια τῶν ἱερέων εἰς πλῆθος) where the Hebrew states that priests consecrated themselves in great numbers.
