## Text

**12:1** And it came about, when the kingdom of Rehoboam was established and when it was strengthened, that he abandoned the commands of the Lord, and all Israel with him.

**12:2** And it came about in the fifth year of the reign of Rehoboam that Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, because they had sinned before the Lord,

**12:3** with one thousand two hundred chariots and sixty thousand horses, and there was no number to the multitude that came with him out of Egypt: Libyans, Troglodytes, and Ethiopians.

**12:4** And they captured the fortified cities that were in Judah; and he came to Jerusalem.

**12:5** And Shemaiah the prophet came to Rehoboam and to the rulers of Judah who had gathered in Jerusalem from before Shishak, and said to them, "Thus said the Lord: 'You abandoned Me, and I will abandon you in the hand of Shishak.'"

**12:6** And the rulers of Israel and the king were ashamed, and they said, "The Lord is righteous."

**12:7** And when the Lord saw that they felt shame, the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah, saying, "They have felt shame; I will not destroy them, and I will give them, to a small extent, for deliverance, and My wrath will certainly not drip down in Jerusalem,

**12:8** for they will become servants, and they will know My service and the service of the kingdom of the earth."

**12:9** And Shishak king of Egypt came up, and he took the treasures that were in the house of the Lord and the treasures that were in the house of the king; he took everything; and he took the golden shields that Solomon had made.

**12:10** And Rehoboam made bronze shields in their place; and Shishak appointed over him captains of the runners, those guarding the gateway of the king.

**12:11** And it came about, whenever the king entered the house of the Lord, that the guards and the runners and those returning to meet the runners would go in.

**12:12** And when he felt shame, the anger of the Lord turned away from him, and not to complete destruction; for indeed in Judah there were good things.

**12:13** And Rehoboam grew strong in Jerusalem and reigned; and Rehoboam was forty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, in the city that the Lord chose to name His name there out of all the tribes of the sons of Israel; and the name of his mother was Naamah the Ammonite.

**12:14** And he did what was evil, because he did not direct his heart to seek out the Lord.

**12:15** And the acts of Rehoboam, the first and the last, see, are they not written in the words of Shemaiah the prophet and Iddo the seer, and his deeds? And Rehoboam fought against Jeroboam all the days.

**12:16** And Rehoboam died and was buried with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David; and Abijah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — The Greek reads "sixty thousand horses" (Heb. horsemen) and "Troglodytes" (literally cave-dwellers) where the Hebrew names the Sukkiim.
- **12:7** — The Greek verb rendered "felt shame" (also in v. 12) conveys remorseful humility, and "drip down" reflects a distinctive Greek metaphor for wrath being poured out. The Greek also omits the Hebrew phrase "by the hand of Shishak" at the end of the verse.
- **12:10** — Unlike the Hebrew, where Rehoboam entrusts the bronze shields to the captains of the guards, the Greek states that Shishak appointed captains of the runners over him. Verse 11 similarly diverges in naming three groups entering the temple.
- **12:16** — The Greek text repeats "and was buried," reading both "was buried with his fathers" (Heb. slept with his fathers) and "was buried in the city of David."
