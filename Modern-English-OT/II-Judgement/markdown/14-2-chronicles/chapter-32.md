## Text

**32:1** And after these matters and this faithfulness, Sennacherib king of the Assyrians came, and he came against Judah and encamped against the walled cities, and said he would seize them.

**32:2** And Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib had come and that his face was set to wage war against Jerusalem.

**32:3** And he took counsel with his elders and the mighty men to stop up the waters of the springs that were outside the city; and he supported him.

**32:4** And he gathered many people and stopped up the waters of the springs and the river that divides through the city, saying, "May the king of Assyria not come and find much water and grow strong."

**32:5** And Hezekiah grew strong and built all the wall that had been torn down, and towers, and another bulwark outside, and he strengthened the embankment of the city of David, and he made many weapons.

**32:6** And he appointed commanders of war over the people, and they were gathered to him at the square of the gate of the valley, and he spoke to their heart, saying,

**32:7** "Be strong and be courageous; do not be terrified before the king of Assyria and before all the nation that is with him, for there are more with us than with him.

**32:8** With him are arms of flesh, but with us is the Lord our God, to save and to wage our war." And the people took courage at the words of Hezekiah king of Judah.

**32:9** And after these things, Sennacherib king of the Assyrians sent his servants to Jerusalem, while he himself was against Lachish and all the army was with him, and he sent to Hezekiah king of Judah and to all Judah who were in Jerusalem, saying,

**32:10** "Thus says the king of the Assyrians: In what are you trusting? Will you sit under siege in Jerusalem?

**32:11** Is Hezekiah not deceiving you to hand you over to death and to famine and to thirst, saying, 'The Lord our God will save you from the hand of the king of Assyria'?

**32:12** This is Hezekiah, who removed His altars and His high places, and spoke to Judah and to those dwelling in Jerusalem, saying, 'Before this altar you shall worship, and on it you shall burn incense.'

**32:13** Will you not know what I and my fathers did to all the peoples of the lands? Were the gods of the nations of all the earth at all able to save their people from my hand?

**32:14** Who was there among all the gods of these nations that my fathers utterly destroyed? Were they able to save their people from my hand, that your God will be able to save you from my hand?

**32:15** Now do not let Hezekiah deceive you, and do not let him make you trust in this way, and do not believe him; for no god of any nation or kingdom will by any means be able to save his people from my hand and from the hand of my fathers, for your God will by no means save you from my hand."

**32:16** And his servants spoke still further against the Lord God and against Hezekiah His servant.

**32:17** And he wrote a letter to revile the Lord God of Israel, and spoke about Him, saying, "Just as the gods of the nations of the earth did not deliver their peoples from my hand, so the God of Hezekiah will by no means deliver His people from my hand."

**32:18** And he shouted with a loud voice in the language of Judah to the people of Jerusalem who were on the wall, to help them and to pull down, so that they might seize the city.

**32:19** And he spoke against the God of Jerusalem just as also against the gods of the peoples of the earth, works of the hands of men.

**32:20** And Hezekiah the king and Isaiah son of Amoz the prophet prayed about these things and cried out to heaven.

**32:21** And the Lord sent an angel; he wiped out every mighty man and warrior and commander and general in the camp of the king of Assyria, and he returned with shame of face to his own land. And he went into the house of his god, and some of those who came out from his own body struck him down with the sword.

**32:22** And the Lord saved Hezekiah and those dwelling in Jerusalem from the hand of Sennacherib king of Assyria and from the hand of all, and He gave them rest all around.

**32:23** And many brought gifts to the Lord to Jerusalem and presents to Hezekiah king of Judah, and he was highly exalted in the eyes of all the nations after these things.

**32:24** In those days Hezekiah became sick to the point of death, and he prayed to the Lord, and He heard him and gave him a sign.

**32:25** And Hezekiah did not repay according to the benefit that He gave him, but his heart was lifted up; and wrath came upon him and upon Judah and Jerusalem.

**32:26** And Hezekiah humbled himself from the loftiness of his heart, along with those dwelling in Jerusalem, and the wrath of God did not come upon them in the days of Hezekiah.

**32:27** And Hezekiah had very great wealth and glory; and he made for himself treasuries of silver and gold and precious stone, and for spices, and armories, and for desirable vessels,

**32:28** and cities for the produce of grain and oil and wine, and stalls for every kind of livestock, and folds for the flocks,

**32:29** and cities that he built for himself, and possessions of sheep and cattle in abundance, for the Lord gave him very great possessions.

**32:30** Hezekiah himself stopped up the outlet of the upper water of Zion, and directed it down to the west of the city of David; and Hezekiah prospered in all his works.

**32:31** And so, concerning the envoys of the rulers from Babylon who were sent to him to inquire from him about the wonder that had happened in the land, the Lord left him to test him, to know what was in his heart.

**32:32** And the rest of the deeds of Hezekiah and his mercy, see, they are written in the prophecy of Isaiah son of Amoz the prophet and in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.

**32:33** And Hezekiah slept with his fathers, and they buried him in the ascent of the tombs of the sons of David; and all Judah and those dwelling in Jerusalem gave him glory and honor at his death; and Manasseh his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **32:3** — The Greek verb is singular ("he supported him"), whereas the context of the elders and mighty men and the Hebrew text suggest the plural ("they supported him").
- **32:18** — The Greek reads "to help them and to pull down" (βοηθῆσαι αὐτοῖς καὶ κατασπάσαι), likely an early Greek textual corruption of φοβῆσαι ("to frighten"), where the Hebrew reads "to frighten them and terrify them."
- **32:30** — The Greek reads "Zion" (Σειών) where the Hebrew reads "Gihon" (compare 33:14, where the Greek has "Gihon").
