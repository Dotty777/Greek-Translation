## Text

**16:1** And in the thirty-eighth year of the reign of Asa, Baasha king of Israel went up against Judah and built Ramah so as not to allow exit and entrance to Asa king of Judah.

**16:2** And Asa took gold and silver from the treasuries of the house of the Lord and of the house of the king, and sent to the son of Hader, king of Syria, who lived in Damascus, saying,

**16:3** "Make a covenant between me and you, and between my father and your father. Look, I have sent you gold and silver; come and scatter Baasha king of Israel away from me, and let him depart from me."

**16:4** And the son of Hader listened to King Asa, and sent to the commanders of his army against the cities of Israel, and he struck Io and Dano and Abelman and all the surrounding districts of Naphtali.

**16:5** And it happened, when Baasha heard, that he stopped building Ramah any longer and ceased his work.

**16:6** And King Asa took all Judah, and he took the stones of Ramah and its timbers with which Baasha had built, and with them he built Geba and Mizpah.

**16:7** And at that time Hanami the prophet came to Asa king of Judah and said to him, "Because you trusted in the king of Syria and did not trust in the Lord your God, therefore the army of Syria escaped from your hand.

**16:8** Were not the Ethiopians and Libyans a great army, with boldness, with horsemen in a very great multitude? And when you trusted in the Lord, He delivered them into your hands.

**16:9** For the eyes of the Lord look throughout all the earth to show strength in every heart that is whole concerning what is toward Him. You have acted ignorantly in this; from now on there is war with you."

**16:10** And Asa became angry with the prophet, and put him in prison because of this; and Asa did harm among the people at that time.

**16:11** And look, the acts of Asa, the first and the last, are written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.

**16:12** And Asa became ill in his feet in the thirty-ninth year of his reign, until he became exceedingly ill; and in his illness he did not seek the Lord, but the physicians.

**16:13** And Asa slept with his fathers, and died in the thirtieth year of his reign.

**16:14** And they buried him in the tomb that he had dug for himself in the city of David, and they laid him on the bier and filled it with spices and kinds of perfumers' ointments, and they made a very great funeral for him.

## Translator Notes

- **16:1** — The Greek dates Baasha's invasion to the 'thirty-eighth year' of Asa's reign, whereas the Hebrew text reads the 'thirty-sixth year.'
- **16:2** — The Greek renders the Hebrew royal name Ben-hadad literally as 'the son of Hader' (also in v. 4), with the variant spelling Hader, and in v. 3 reads 'scatter Baasha' without mentioning a broken treaty.
- **16:4** — The Greek preserves the distinct town names 'Io,' 'Dano,' and 'Abelman' (corresponding to Hebrew Ijon, Dan, and Abel-maim) and reads 'all the surrounding districts of Naphtali' rather than 'all the store cities.'
- **16:7** — The Greek gives the prophet's name as 'Hanami' (where the Hebrew has 'Hanani the seer'), and in v. 8 reads 'with boldness' (Greek tharsos) where the Hebrew has 'with chariots.'
- **16:9** — The Greek phrase rendered 'concerning what is toward Him' (epi tou pros auton) is syntactically difficult and could alternatively be taken as 'upon the one who is toward Him.'
- **16:13** — The Greek states that Asa died in the 'thirtieth year' of his reign (despite the 'thirty-ninth year' in v. 12; the Hebrew has 'forty-first year'), and in v. 14 reads 'a very great funeral' (Greek ekphoran) rather than a great fire.
