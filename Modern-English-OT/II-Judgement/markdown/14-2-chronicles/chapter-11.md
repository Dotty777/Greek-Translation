## Text

**11:1** And Rehoboam came to Jerusalem, and he assembled Judah and Benjamin, one hundred eighty thousand young men making war; and he was fighting against Israel to return the kingdom to Rehoboam.

**11:2** And the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah, a man of God, saying,

**11:3** "Speak to Rehoboam the son of Solomon and all Judah and Benjamin, saying,

**11:4** 'Thus says the Lord: You shall not go up and you shall not fight against your brothers. Turn back, each one to his house, because this matter has come about from Me.'" And they heeded the word of the Lord, and turned back from going against Jeroboam.

**11:5** And Rehoboam lived in Jerusalem, and he built walled cities in Judea.

**11:6** And he built Bethseem, Apan, and Tekoa,

**11:7** and Beth-zur, Succoth, and Adullam,

**11:8** and Gath, Mareshah, and Zib,

**11:9** and Adorai, Lachish, and Azekah,

**11:10** and Zorah, Aldon, and Hebron, which belongs to Judah and Benjamin, walled cities.

**11:11** And he fortified them as walled cities, and he placed commanders in them and stores of food, oil, and wine,

**11:12** in city after city, shields and spears, and he strengthened them to a very great degree. And Judah and Benjamin belonged to him.

**11:13** And the Levites and the priests who were in all Israel gathered to him from all the territories,

**11:14** for the Levites abandoned the dwellings of their possession and went to Judah, to Jerusalem, because Jeroboam and his sons were driving them out from ministering to the Lord.

**11:15** And he appointed for himself priests of the high places, and for the idols, and for the worthless things, and for the calves that Jeroboam had made.

**11:16** And he drove them out from the tribes of Israel, those who gave their heart to seek the Lord, the God of Israel, and they came to Jerusalem to sacrifice to the Lord, the God of their fathers.

**11:17** And they strengthened the kingdom of Judah; and it strengthened Rehoboam the son of Solomon for three years, because he walked in the ways of David and Solomon for three years.

**11:18** And Rehoboam took for himself as a wife Mollath the daughter of Jerimoth the son of David, Baia the daughter of Elian the son of Jesse.

**11:19** And she bore him sons: Jauth, Shemariah, and Roollam.

**11:20** And after these things he took for himself Maacah the daughter of Absalom, and she bore him Abijah, Jeththi, Ziza, and Emmoth.

**11:21** And Rehoboam loved Maacah the daughter of Absalom above all his wives and his concubines, for he had eighteen wives and thirty concubines; and he fathered twenty-eight sons and sixty daughters.

**11:22** And he appointed Abijah the son of Maacah as ruler, as leader among his brothers, because he intended to make him king.

**11:23** And he grew great beyond all his sons in all the territories of Judah and Benjamin and in the fortified cities, and he gave them provisions in great abundance, and he asked for a multitude of wives.

## Translator Notes

- **11:6** — The Greek text reads "Bethseem" and "Apan" where the Hebrew has Bethlehem and Etam. Elsewhere in this city list (vv. 7-10), the Greek likewise preserves distinct forms: Succoth (Heb. Soco), Zib (Heb. Ziph), Adorai (Heb. Adoraim), and Aldon (Heb. Aijalon).
- **11:16** — The Greek reads "and he drove them out from the tribes of Israel," attributing the expulsion to Jeroboam, where the Hebrew reads "and after them from all the tribes of Israel."
- **11:17** — The Greek text shifts to singular verbs ("it strengthened" and "he walked") where other Greek witnesses and the Hebrew read plural verbs ("they strengthened" and "they walked").
- **11:18** — The Greek preserves distinct forms for these names: Mollath (Heb. Mahalath), Baia (Heb. Abihail, without a preceding "and"), and Elian (Heb. Eliab); similarly in vv. 19-20 it reads Jauth (Jeush), Roollam (Zaham), Jeththi (Attai), and Emmoth (Shelomith).
- **11:21** — The Greek reads "thirty concubines" where the Hebrew has sixty. In v. 23, the Greek also diverges notably, reading "he grew great beyond all his sons" and feminine "them" (referring to the fortified cities) as recipients of provisions.
