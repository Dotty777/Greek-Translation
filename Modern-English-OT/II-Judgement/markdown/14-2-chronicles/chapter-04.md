## Text

**4:1** And he made the altar of bronze, twenty cubits in length, and the width twenty cubits; the height ten cubits.

**4:2** And he made the cast-metal sea, ten cubits in diameter, round all around, and five cubits in height, and the circumference thirty cubits.

**4:3** And a likeness of calves was underneath it; in a circle they encircle it, ten cubits encompass the basin all around; in two kinds they cast the calves in their casting,

**4:4** in which they made them twelve calves, three facing north, and three west, and three south, and three toward the east; and the sea was upon them above; their hindquarters were inward.

**4:5** And its thickness was a handbreadth, and its rim was like the rim of a cup, carved with buds of a lily, holding three thousand measures; and he finished.

**4:6** And he made ten basins, and placed five on the right and five on the left, to wash in them the works of the burnt offerings and to rinse in them; and the sea was for the priests to wash themselves in it.

**4:7** And he made the ten golden lampstands according to their ordinance and placed them in the temple, five on the right and five on the left.

**4:8** And he made ten tables and placed them in the temple, five on the right and five on the left; and he made one hundred golden bowls.

**4:9** And he made the court of the priests and the great court, and doors for the court, and their doorframes plated with bronze.

**4:10** And he placed the sea at the corner of the house on the right, toward the east, opposite.

**4:11** And Hiram made the meat-forks and the firepans and the grate of the altar and all its utensils. And Hiram finished doing all the work that he did for Solomon the king in the house of God,

**4:12** two pillars, and on them globes for the capital on the tops of the two pillars, and two networks to cover the tops of the capitals that are on the tops of the pillars,

**4:13** and four hundred golden bells for the two networks, and kinds of pomegranates on each network to cover the two globes of the capitals that are upon the pillars.

**4:14** And he made the ten stands, and he made the basins upon the stands,

**4:15** and the one sea, and the twelve calves underneath it,

**4:16** and the footed pots and the shovels and the cauldrons and the meat-forks, and all their utensils, which Hiram made of pure bronze and brought up to King Solomon in the house of the Lord.

**4:17** In the region around the Jordan the king cast them in the thickness of the earth, in the house of Succoth and Anamesirdathai.

**4:18** And Solomon made all these utensils in very great abundance, because the weight of the bronze did not fail.

**4:19** And Solomon made all the utensils of the house of the Lord, and the golden altar and the tables, and on them were loaves of presentation,

**4:20** and the lampstands and the lamps of light according to the ordinance and before the inner sanctuary, of pure gold,

**4:21** and their tongs and their lamps, and the bowls and the censers and the firepans, of pure gold,

**4:22** and the inner door of the house to the Holy of Holies, to the golden doors of the house of the temple. And all the work that Solomon did in the house of the Lord was finished.

## Translator Notes

- **4:5** — The Greek noun rendered "measures" is the liquid unit metretes (about ten gallons each). The closing clause "and he finished" reflects a Septuagint reading of the Hebrew verb for "contain" as "finish."
- **4:12** — The Greek uses the Hebrew architectural loanwords golath ("globes") and chothareth ("capitals"), and reads "tops" instead of "two globes" in the second half; similarly, mechonoth ("stands") appears in 4:14 and dabeir ("inner sanctuary") in 4:20, 5:7, and 5:9.
- **4:13** — The Greek reads "four hundred golden bells" (where Hebrew has "pomegranates") and literally "kinds of pomegranates on the one network" (used distributively for "each network").
- **4:17** — The Greek reads "in the house of" (for Hebrew "between") and preserves the Septuagint-distinct place name Anamesirdathai, apparently formed by fusing Greek ana meson ("between") with Hebrew Zeredathah.
- **4:22** — The Greek links the second phrase with "to the golden doors" rather than a coordinating conjunction, and places the closing sentence ("And all the work...") at the end of 4:22, where Hebrew versification begins 5:1.
