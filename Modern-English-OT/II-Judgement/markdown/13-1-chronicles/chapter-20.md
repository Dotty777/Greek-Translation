## Text

**20:1** And it happened in the following year, at the going out of the kings, that Joab led the entire force of the army, and they devastated the country of the sons of Ammon; and he came and besieged Rabbah. Now David was staying in Jerusalem; and he struck Rabbah and demolished it.

**20:2** And David took the crown of Molchol their king from his head, and its weight was found to be a talent of gold, and in it was a precious stone, and it was upon David's head; and he brought out very great spoils of the city.

**20:3** And he brought out the people who were in it and sawed them apart with saws and with iron adzes, and thus David did to the servants, the sons of Ammon; and David and all his people returned to Jerusalem.

**20:4** And it happened after these things that there was war again in Gezer with the foreigners; then Sibbecai Thosathi struck Saphut, of the sons of the giants, and humbled him.

**20:5** And there was war again with the foreigners, and Ellan son of Jair struck Elemee, the brother of Goliath the Gittite, and the shaft of his spear was like a weavers' beam.

**20:6** And there was war again in Gath, and there was a man of immense stature, and his fingers and toes were six and six, twenty-four; and he too was a descendant of giants.

**20:7** And he taunted Israel, and Jonathan son of Shimea, son of David's brother, struck him.

**20:8** This one was born to Rapha in Gath; all of them were four giants, and they fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — Greek reads "Molchol their king," a proper-name rendering corresponding to Hebrew Milcom (or "their king").
- **20:3** — Greek explicitly uses dieprisen ("sawed apart") with only two tools ("saws" and "iron adzes") and reads tois paisin ("the servants"), likely an early scribal variant for pasin ("all"), where Hebrew has "all the cities of the sons of Ammon."
- **20:4** — Greek allophyloi literally means "foreigners" (the Septuagint's regular term for the Philistines); the Greek forms "Thosathi" and "Saphut" correspond to Hebrew "the Hushathite" and "Sippai," and the final verb is singular active ("humbled him").
- **20:5** — The Greek names "Ellan" and "Elemee" correspond to Hebrew "Elhanan" and "Lahmi."
- **20:8** — Greek reads the singular "this one" and adds "all of them were four giants" (compare 2 Samuel 21:22), a clause absent from the Hebrew of 1 Chronicles 20:8.
