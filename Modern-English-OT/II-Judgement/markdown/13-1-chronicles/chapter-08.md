**8:20** Elioliaa, Zillethai, Elieli,

**8:22** And Ishpan, Obde, Eliel,

**8:23** Abdon, Zichri, Hanan,

**8:24** Hananiah, Omri, Elam, Anothaith,

**8:31** Dur, his brother, Zachur, and Mikloth.

**8:35** And the sons of Micah: Pithon, Melchel, Tarea, and Zak.

**8:36** And Zak fathered Jad and Jada; and Jada fathered Salaimath, Salmo, and Zimri; and Zimri fathered Maisa.

**8:40** And the sons of Elim were men strong in might, drawing the bow and multiplying sons and sons of sons, one hundred fifty. All these were from the sons of Benjamin.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — The Greek text preserves numerous distinct proper names throughout this genealogy that differ from the Hebrew Masoretic Text—here reading Beleleel (though Bela in 8:3), Saba, Japhael, and (in 8:2) Joa instead of Bela, Ashbel, Aharah, and Nohah. Similar Greek-distinct name forms appear throughout verses 3-27 and 29-40 (for example, Ali, Abisamas, Saarel, Hada, Alphaad, Obed, Semer, Elchaad, Samaith, Molcha, Abalon, Baalakaim, Zak, Salasath, and Asel).
- **8:7** — Greek reads the proper name Higaam (Ἱγαάμ), reflecting a transliteration of the Hebrew verb meaning 'he deported them', and names his sons Naana and Jachichol where Hebrew has Uzza and Ahihud.
- **8:12** — Greek reads Onan (Ὠνάν) and omits Lod where Hebrew has Ono and Lod; in verse 13 Greek likewise reads Elam (Αἰλάμ) where Hebrew has Aijalon, and in verses 14 and 31 renders Hebrew Ahio as 'his brother'.
- **8:33** — Greek reads Amminadab (Ἀμειναδάβ) where Hebrew has Abinadab, and in verse 34 uses the plural 'sons' before Merib-baal; in verse 36 Greek gives Zak (Hebrew: Ahaz) two sons, Jad and Jada, corresponding to Hebrew Jehoaddah.
- **8:38** — Greek states that Azel had six sons but lists only five names because it translates the Hebrew second name Bocheru as 'his firstborn' (πρωτότοκος αὐτοῦ) after Ezrikai (Hebrew: Azrikam).
