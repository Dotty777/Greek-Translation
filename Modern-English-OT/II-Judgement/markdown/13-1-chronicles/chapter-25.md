## Text

**25:1** And King David and the leaders of the army appointed for the tasks the sons of Asaph, and of Heman, and of Jeduthun, who spoke out with lyres, with harps, and with cymbals; and their count, as they worked at their tasks, was head by head.

**25:2** The sons of Asaph: Sacchus, Joseph, Nathalias, and Erael; the sons of Asaph, next to the king.

**25:3** For Jeduthun, the sons of Jeduthun: Tuna, Zeri, Saia, Shimei, Hashabiah, and Mattithiah, six, following their father Jeduthun, who sounded forth thanksgiving and praise to the Lord with a lyre.

**25:4** For Heman, the sons of Heman: Bukkiah, Mattaniah, Azarel, Shubael, Amsu, Jeremoth, Hananiah, Eliathah, Giddalti, and Romei; the sons of Od, and Jeibasaka, Manthei, Hothir, and Melzoth.

**25:5** All these were sons of Heman, who sounded forth for the king in the words of God to exalt a horn; and God gave to Heman fourteen sons and three daughters.

**25:6** All these were with their father, singing hymns in the house of God with cymbals, with harps, and with lyres, next to the king, and Asaph, Jeduthun, and Heman.

**25:7** And their number along with their brothers, taught to sing to the Lord—everyone with understanding—was two hundred and eighty-eight.

**25:8** And they also cast lots for turns of duty, small and great alike, both the fully trained and the learners.

## Translator Notes

- **25:1** — The Greek 'who spoke out' (apophthengomenous) denotes solemn or inspired utterance (where the Hebrew has 'prophesied'), and 'head by head' preserves a Septuagint-distinct reading.
- **25:2** — Sacchus, Nathalias, and Erael are Septuagint-distinct name forms corresponding to Zaccur, Nethaniah, and Asharelah in the Hebrew text.
- **25:3** — Tuna and Saia are Septuagint-distinct forms (for Hebrew Gedaliah and Jeshaiah), and the Greek includes Shimei to reach the stated total of six; 'sounding forth' (anakrouomenos) literally means striking up a stringed instrument (also in v. 5).
- **25:4** — The Greek lists Azarel (Hebrew: Uzziel), adds Amsu while omitting Hanani, and renders the closing names with Septuagint-distinct forms (Romei, 'the sons of Od,' Jeibasaka, Manthei, and Melzoth) corresponding to Romamti-ezer, Joshbekashah, Mallothi, and Mahazioth.
- **25:9** — Galuia and Heneia reflect a Septuagint-distinct rendering of Hebrew Gedaliah and 'he'; across vv. 10-31, Zacchuth, Nathan, Jeseriel, Josiah, Emei, Azariah, Haria, Bakata, Hananiah (v. 25), Methathei, Aematha, Hethei, Godomathei, and Romelchei preserve the Greek forms (and v. 10 reads singular 'his son').
