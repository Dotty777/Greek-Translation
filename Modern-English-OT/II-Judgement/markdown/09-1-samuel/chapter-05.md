## Text

**5:1** Now the foreigners took the ark of God and brought it from Abenner into Ashdod.

**5:2** And the foreigners took the ark of the Lord and brought it into the house of Dagon, and set it beside Dagon.

**5:3** And the Ashdodites rose early and entered the house of Dagon, and saw, and look, Dagon had fallen on his face before the ark of God. And they raised Dagon up and set him in his place. And the hand of the Lord was heavy upon the Ashdodites, and He tormented them and struck them in their posteriors, Ashdod and its borders.

**5:4** And it happened, when they rose early in the morning, that look, Dagon had fallen on his face before the ark of the covenant of the Lord; and the head of Dagon and both palms of his hands had been severed upon the front of hamapheth, each of them, and both wrists of his hands had fallen upon the entryway; only the spine of Dagon was left.

**5:5** Therefore the priests of Dagon and everyone who enters the house of Dagon do not step on the threshold of the house of Dagon in Ashdod to this day, for they step right over it.

**5:6** And the hand of the Lord was heavy upon Ashdod, and He afflicted them, and it broke out upon them into the ships, and in the midst of its countryside mice sprang up; and there was a great confusion of death in the city.

**5:7** And the men of Ashdod saw that it was so, and they said, "The ark of God shall not remain with us, for His hand is harsh upon us and upon Dagon our god."

**5:8** And they sent and gathered the satraps of the foreigners to themselves and said, "What shall we do with the ark of the God of Israel?" And the Gittites said, "Let the ark of God come over to us." And the ark of God went over to Gath.

**5:9** And it happened after it went over, that the hand of the Lord was against the city, a very great turmoil; and He struck the men of the city from small to great, and He struck them in their posteriors. And the Gittites made seats for themselves.

**5:10** And they sent away the ark of God to Ashkelon. And it happened, as the ark of God entered Ashkelon, that the Ashkelonites cried out, saying, "Why have you turned back to us the ark of the God of Israel, to put us and our people to death?"

**5:11** And they sent out and gathered the satraps of the foreigners and said, "Send away the ark of the God of Israel, and let it settle in its own place, and it will by no means put us and our people to death." For a very heavy confusion came about in the whole city when the ark of the God of Israel entered there.

**5:12** And those who lived and did not die were struck in their posteriors, and the cry of the city went up to heaven.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — The Septuagint reads Abenner here, contrasting with Ebenezer (Greek Abenezer) in 4:1 and in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **5:4** — Greek hamapheth is an untranslated transliteration of Hebrew hammiphtan ("the threshold"); the verse also preserves a double rendering of Dagon's severed hands and reads "the spine of Dagon."
- **5:6** — Greek "into the ships" (eis tas naus) and the explicit mention of mice springing up in the countryside are distinctive Septuagint readings not found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **5:9** — Greek hedrai means both "posteriors" (anatomical seat) and "seats," producing the Septuagint's striking reading that the afflicted Gittites "made seats for themselves."
- **5:10** — The Septuagint reads Ashkelon and the Ashkelonites where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads Ekron and the Ekronites.
