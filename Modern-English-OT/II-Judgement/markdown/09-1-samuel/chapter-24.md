## Text

**24:1** And David rose up from there and stayed in the narrow passes of En-gedi.

**24:2** And it happened, when Saul returned from following the foreigners, that it was reported to him, saying, "David is in the wilderness of En-gedi."

**24:3** And he took with himself three thousand chosen men from all Israel, and went to seek David and his men in front of Eddaiem.

**24:4** And he came to the herds of the flocks that were by the road, and there was a cave there; and Saul went in to prepare himself, and David and his men were sitting farther inside the cave.

**24:5** And David's men said to him, "Look, this is the day of which the Lord spoke to you, to deliver your enemy into your hands, and you will do to him as is good in your eyes." And David rose up and secretly removed the corner of Saul's cloak.

**24:6** And it happened after these things that David's heart struck him, because he had removed the corner of his cloak.

**24:7** And David said to his men, "By no means may it be for me from the Lord that I should do this thing to the anointed of the Lord, to lay my hand against him, because this man is the anointed of the Lord."

**24:8** And David persuaded his men with words, and did not allow them to rise up and slaughter Saul. And Saul rose up and went down the road.

**24:9** And David rose up after him out of the cave; and David called out after Saul, saying, "Lord king!" And Saul looked back behind him, and David bent down with his face to the ground and bowed in homage to him.

**24:10** And David said to Saul, "Why do you listen to the words of the people who say, 'Look, David is seeking your life'?

**24:11** Look, on this day your eyes have seen how the Lord delivered you today into my hand in the cave, and I was not willing to kill you, and I spared you and said, 'I will not lay my hand against my lord, because he is the anointed of the Lord.'

**24:12** And look, the corner of your cloak is in my hand; I have removed the corner and have not killed you. And know and see today that there is no evil in my hand, nor ungodliness and rebellion, and I have not sinned against you; yet you are binding my life to take it.

**24:13** May the Lord judge between me and you, and may the Lord avenge me on you, yet my hand will not be against you;

**24:14** just as the ancient proverb says, 'From the lawless will come forth wrongdoing,' and my hand will not be against you.

**24:15** And now after whom are you going out, king of Israel? After whom are you pursuing? After a dead dog, and after a single flea.

**24:16** May the Lord become judge and arbiter between me and you; may the Lord see and judge my case and vindicate me from your hand."

**24:17** And it happened, when David finished speaking these words to Saul, that Saul said, "Is this your voice, child David?" And Saul lifted up his voice and wept.

**24:18** And Saul said to David, "You are more righteous than I, because you have repaid me good, whereas I have repaid you evil.

**24:19** And you have declared to me today what good things you have done for me, how the Lord shut me up today into your hands and you did not kill me.

**24:20** And because if anyone should find his enemy in distress and send him away on a good road, then the Lord will repay him good, just as you have done today.

**24:21** And now, look, I know that you will certainly reign as king, and the kingdom of Israel will stand in your hands.

**24:22** And now swear to me by the Lord that you will not destroy my seed after me, and will not wipe out my name from the house of my father."

**24:23** And David swore to Saul. And Saul went away to his place, and David and his men went up to the narrow pass of Messara.

## Translator Notes

- **24:3** — Greek preserves the place name Eddaiem, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the rocks of the wild goats."
- **24:4** — Literally "to prepare himself," a Septuagint euphemism for relieving oneself (corresponding to the Hebrew idiom "to cover his feet").
- **24:12** — Greek reads "you are binding," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "you are hunting" or "lying in wait for."
- **24:20** — Greek adds "in distress" and frames the verse as a conditional statement ("if anyone should find... then the Lord will repay him"), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has a rhetorical question.
- **24:23** — Greek transliterates the place name Messara ("the narrow pass of Messara"), corresponding to the Hebrew term for "the stronghold."
