## Text

**4:1** And it happened in those days that foreigners gathered together for battle against Israel; and Israel went out to meet them for battle, and they encamped at Ebenezer, and the foreigners encamped in Aphek.

**4:2** And the foreigners drew up in array for battle against Israel; and the battle turned, and the men of Israel were defeated before the foreigners, and four thousand men were struck down in the battle line in the field.

**4:3** And the people came into the camp, and the elders of Israel said, "Why has the Lord defeated us today before the foreigners? Let us take the ark of our God from Shiloh, and let it go out from our midst, and it will save you from the hand of our enemies."

**4:4** And the people sent to Shiloh, and they carried from there the ark of the Lord who sits on the cherubim. And both of the sons of Eli were with the ark, Hophni and Phinehas.

**4:5** And it happened, as the ark of the Lord came into the camp, that all Israel cried out with a loud voice, and the earth resounded.

**4:6** And the foreigners heard the cry, and the foreigners said, "What is this great cry in the camp of the Hebrews?" And they knew that the ark of the Lord had come into the camp.

**4:7** And the foreigners were afraid and said, "These gods have come to them into the camp! Woe to us! Deliver us, Lord, today, for such a thing has not happened yesterday or the day before.

**4:8** Woe to us! Who will deliver us from the hand of these strong gods? These are the gods who struck Egypt with every plague and in the wilderness.

**4:9** Be strong and become men, and fight them."

**4:10** And they fought them; and the men of Israel were defeated, and each one fled to his tent. And there was a very great slaughter, and thirty thousand ranks fell from Israel.

**4:11** And the ark of God was taken, and both sons of Eli died, Hophni and Phinehas.

**4:12** And a Jeminite man ran from the battle line and came to Shiloh on that day, with his clothes torn and earth on his head.

**4:13** And he came, and look, Eli was on the seat beside the gate, watching the road, for his heart was distraught concerning the ark of God. And the man entered the city to report, and the city cried out.

**4:14** And Eli heard the sound of the outcry and said, "What is the outcry of this sound?" And the man hurried, entered, and reported to Eli.

**4:15** Now Eli was ninety years old, and his eyes were fixed, and he could not see. And Eli said to the men who were standing around him, "What is the sound of this uproar?"

**4:16** And the man hurried, approached Eli, and said to him, "I am the one who has come from the camp, and I have fled from the battle line today." And he said, "What is the thing that has happened, child?"

**4:17** And the young man answered and said, "The men of Israel have fled from before the foreigners, and there has been a great slaughter among the people, and both of your sons have died, and the ark of God was taken."

**4:18** And it happened, when he mentioned the ark of God, that he fell backward from the seat beside the gate, and his back was broken and he died, for the man was old and heavy. And he had judged Israel twenty years.

**4:19** Now his daughter-in-law, the wife of Phinehas, was pregnant and about to give birth. And she heard the report that the ark of God had been taken and that her father-in-law and her husband had died, and she wept and gave birth, for her labor pains turned upon her.

**4:20** And in her time she was dying. And the women who stood beside her said to her, "Do not be afraid, for you have given birth to a son." And she did not answer, and her heart paid no attention.

**4:21** And she named the boy Woe Barchaboth, on account of the ark of God and on account of her father-in-law and on account of her husband.

**4:22** And they said, "The glory of Israel has been carried into exile when the ark of the Lord was taken."

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Greek allophyloi literally means "foreigners" (people of another tribe), the Septuagint's regular designation for the Philistines throughout 1 Kingdoms.
- **4:12** — Greek Iemeinaios ("Jeminite") reflects the Hebrew gentilic Yemini, a shortened designation for a Benjamite.
- **4:15** — The Septuagint gives Eli's age as ninety years (Hebrew Masoretic Text: ninety-eight) and adds Eli's question to the men standing around him.
- **4:18** — The Septuagint reads "his back was broken" and "twenty years" where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "his neck" and "forty years."
- **4:21** — The Septuagint names the child Ouai Barchaboth ("Woe Barchaboth"), corresponding to Hebrew Ichabod ("Where is the glory?").
