## Text

**19:1** And Saul spoke to Jonathan his son and to all his servants to put David to death.

**19:2** And Jonathan, the son of Saul, favored David greatly. And Jonathan reported to David, saying, "Saul is seeking to put you to death. Therefore be on your guard tomorrow morning, and hide yourself and sit in secret.

**19:3** And I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where you are, and I will speak about you to my father; and I will see whatever it may be, and I will report to you."

**19:4** And Jonathan spoke good things about David to Saul his father, and said to him, "Let not the king sin against your servant David, because he has not sinned against you, and his deeds are very good.

**19:5** And he put his life in his hand and struck down the foreigner, and the Lord worked a great deliverance, and all Israel saw and rejoiced. And why are you sinning against innocent blood to put David to death without cause?"

**19:6** And Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan, and Saul swore, saying, "As the Lord lives, he shall not die."

**19:7** And Jonathan called David and reported to him all these words. And Jonathan brought David in to Saul, and he was before him as yesterday and the third day.

**19:8** And the war occurred again against David, and David prevailed and fought the foreigners, and struck a very great blow among them, and they fled from his presence.

**19:9** And an evil spirit of God came upon Saul, and he was sleeping in the house, and a spear was in his hand, and David was playing music with his hands.

**19:10** And Saul was seeking to strike the spear into David, and David moved aside from the presence of Saul, and he struck the spear into the wall, and David withdrew and escaped.

**19:11** And it happened in that night that Saul sent messengers to the house of David to watch him, to put him to death in the morning. And Michal his wife reported to David, saying, "If you do not save your own life this night, tomorrow you will be put to death."

**19:12** And Michal let David down through the window, and he went away and fled and escaped.

**19:13** And Michal took the cenotaphs and placed them on the bed, and she placed a liver of goats at its head, and covered them with a garment.

**19:14** And Saul sent messengers to take David, and they said that he was sick.

**19:15** And he sent for David, saying, "Bring him on the bed to me to put him to death."

**19:16** And the messengers came, and look, the cenotaphs were on the bed, and a liver of goats was at its head.

**19:17** And Saul said to Michal, "Why have you deceived me like this, and sent away my enemy, and he escaped?" And Michal said to Saul, "He himself said, 'Send me away; if not, I will put you to death.'"

**19:18** And David fled and escaped, and came to Samuel at Armathaim, and reported to him all that Saul had done to him. And David and Samuel went and stayed in Auath in Ramah.

**19:19** And it was reported to Saul, saying, "Look, David is in Auath in Ramah."

**19:20** And Saul sent messengers to take David; and they saw the assembly of the prophets, and Samuel was standing appointed over them. And the Spirit of God came upon the messengers of Saul, and they prophesied.

**19:21** And it was reported to Saul, and he sent other messengers, and they also prophesied. And Saul proceeded to send messengers a third time, and they also prophesied.

**19:22** And Saul was furious with anger, and he also went to Armathaim, and came as far as the cistern of the threshing floor that is in Sephi, and asked and said, "Where are Samuel and David?" And someone said, "Look, in Auath in Ramah."

**19:23** And he went from there to Auath in Ramah, and the Spirit of God came upon him also, and he went along prophesying until he came to Auath in Ramah.

**19:24** And he stripped off his garments and prophesied before them, and fell down naked all that day and all the night. Therefore they were saying, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

## Translator Notes

- **19:5** — Greek allophylon literally means 'foreigner,' the Septuagint's regular designation for the Philistines in 1-4 Kingdoms. Unlike the Masoretic Text, the Greek makes 'all Israel' the subject of 'saw and rejoiced.'
- **19:9** — Greek katheudon reads 'sleeping' in the house, whereas the Masoretic Text reads 'sitting.'
- **19:13** — Greek kenotaphia ('cenotaphs' or funerary effigies) renders Hebrew teraphim (household idols), and hepar ton aigon reads 'a liver of goats' (also in v. 16), where the Masoretic Text has a quilt or cushion of goats' hair.
- **19:18** — The Greek distinguishes Armathaim from Ramah in the same verse, and reads Auath (also in vv. 19, 22, 23; 20:1) where the Masoretic Text reads Naioth.
- **19:22** — Greek reads 'the cistern of the threshing floor that is in Sephi' (after 'Saul was furious with anger'), where the Masoretic Text reads 'the great cistern that is in Secu.'
