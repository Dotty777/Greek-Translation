## Text

**21:1** And David rose up and went away, and Jonathan entered the city.

**21:2** And David came to Nob, to Abimelech the priest; and Abimelech was startled at meeting him and said to him, "Why is it that you are alone, and no one is with you?"

**21:3** And David said to the priest, "The king has commanded me a matter today and said to me, 'Let no one know the matter about which I am sending you and concerning which I have commanded you.' And I have given notice to the young men in the place called God's Faithfulness, Phellani Maemoni.

**21:4** And now, are there five loaves of bread under your hand? Give into my hand what is found."

**21:5** And the priest answered David and said, "There are no common loaves under my hand; there are only holy loaves. If the young men have been kept from a woman, then they may eat."

**21:6** And David answered the priest and said to him, "Indeed, we have abstained from a woman yesterday and the third day; when I went out on a journey, all the young men were purified. And is the journey itself common? Because it will be sanctified today on account of my equipment."

**21:7** And Abimelech the priest gave him the loaves of the presentation, because there were no loaves there except the loaves of the presence, which had been removed from the presence of the Lord for warm bread to be set out on the day he took them.

**21:8** And one of the young men of Saul was there on that day, detained, Neessaran, before the Lord; and his name was Doeg the Syrian, tending the mules of Saul.

**21:9** And David said to Abimelech, "See if there is a spear or a sword here under your hand, because I have not taken my sword and the equipment in my hand, for the king's matter was in haste."

**21:10** And the priest said, "Here is the sword of Goliath the foreigner, whom you struck down in the valley of Elah, and it was wrapped in a cloak. If you will take this one, take it for yourself, for there is no other besides this one here." And David said, "Look, there is none like it; give it to me."

**21:11** And he gave it to him. And David rose up and fled on that day from the presence of Saul. And David came to Achish king of Gath.

**21:12** And the servants of Achish said to him, "Is this not David, the king of the land? Did not the dancing women lead the song for him, saying, 'Saul struck down in his thousands, and David in his ten thousands'?"

**21:13** And David placed the words in his heart, and he was greatly afraid before Achish king of Gath.

**21:14** And he altered his face before him and put on a pretense on that day, and he was drumming on the doors of the city and staggering on his hands and falling against the doors of the gate, and his saliva was running down onto his beard.

**21:15** And Achish said to his servants, "Look, see an epileptic man! Why did you bring him in to me?

**21:16** Do I lack epileptics, that you have brought him in to have epileptic fits before me? This man will not enter a house."

## Translator Notes

- **21:2** — Greek reads "Abimelech" here and throughout chs. 21-23, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Ahimelech."
- **21:3** — Greek preserves a doublet for the Hebrew idiom indicating an undisclosed place, rendering it both as a translation ("God's Faithfulness") and as a transliteration ("Phellani Maemoni").
- **21:6** — Greek frames this difficult clause as a question ("And is the journey itself common?") followed by "Because it will be sanctified today on account of my equipment" (Hebrew MT: "even though it is an ordinary journey, how much more today will their vessels be holy").
- **21:8** — "Neessaran" transliterates the Hebrew word for "detained" alongside its Greek translation; Greek also calls Doeg "the Syrian" (MT: "the Edomite") and describes him as "tending the mules of Saul" (MT: "chief of Saul's shepherds").
- **21:10** — Greek allophylos ("foreigner") is the Septuagint's regular designation for the Philistines throughout 1-2 Samuel.
