## Text

**1:1** There was a man from Armathaim Sipha, from Mount Ephraim, and his name was Elkanah, son of Jeremeel, son of Elihu, son of Thoke, in Nasib of Ephraim.

**1:2** And he had two wives: the name of the one was Hannah, and the name of the second was Peninnah. And Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no child.

**1:3** And the man would go up from days to days from his city, from Armathaim, to worship and to sacrifice to the Lord God Sabaoth at Shiloh. And there Eli and his two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were priests of the Lord.

**1:4** And a day came when Elkanah sacrificed, and he gave portions to Peninnah his wife and to her sons;

**1:5** and to Hannah he gave one portion, because she had no child, yet Elkanah loved Hannah more than her. And the Lord had closed off what was around her womb,

**1:6** because the Lord had not given her a child, according to her affliction and according to the despondency of her affliction; and she was despondent on account of this, because the Lord had shut up what was around her womb, so as not to give her a child.

**1:7** So he would do year by year when she went up to the house of the Lord; and she would be despondent and weep, and would not eat.

**1:8** And Elkanah her husband said to her, "Hannah." And she said to him, "Here I am, sir." And he said to her, "What is the matter with you that you are weeping? And why are you not eating? And why does your heart strike you? Am I not better to you than ten children?"

**1:9** And she rose up after they had eaten in Shiloh, and stood before the Lord. Now Eli the priest was on the seat by the doorposts of the temple of the Lord.

**1:10** And she was deeply distressed in soul, and she prayed to the Lord, speaking, and wept.

**1:11** And she vowed a vow to the Lord, saying, "Adonai, Lord, Eloe Sabaoth, if You will indeed look upon the humiliation of Your maidservant, and remember me, and give to Your maidservant male seed, then I will give him before You as one given until the day of his death; and he will not drink wine or strong drink, and no iron will come up upon his head."

**1:12** And it came about, as she continued praying long before the Lord, that Eli the priest watched her mouth.

**1:13** Now she was speaking in her heart, and her lips were moving, but her voice was not heard; so Eli considered her a drunken woman.

**1:14** And Eli's young attendant said to her, "How long will you be drunk? Put away your wine and go away from the presence of the Lord."

**1:15** And Hannah answered and said, "No, sir; I am a woman whose day is hard, and I have not drunk wine or strong drink, but I am pouring out my soul before the Lord.

**1:16** Do not regard your maidservant as a wicked daughter, for out of the abundance of my complaint I have continued until now."

**1:17** And Eli answered and said to her, "Go in peace; may the God of Israel grant you every request of yours that you have asked from Him."

**1:18** And she said, "Your maidservant has found favor in your eyes." And the woman went on her way, and she entered her lodging and ate with her husband and drank, and her face was no longer downcast.

**1:19** And they rose early in the morning and worshiped the Lord, and went on their way. And Elkanah entered his house at Armathaim, and knew Hannah his wife, and the Lord remembered her.

**1:20** And she conceived, and it came about at the time of the days that she gave birth to a son; and she called his name Samuel and said, "Because I asked for him from the Lord God Sabaoth."

**1:21** And the man Elkanah and all his household went up to sacrifice in Shiloh the sacrifice of the days and his vows and all the tithes of his land.

**1:22** But Hannah did not go up with him, for she said to her husband, "Until the boy goes up, when I wean him, then he will appear in the presence of the Lord and remain there forever."

**1:23** And Elkanah her husband said to her, "Do what is good in your eyes; remain until you wean him; only may the Lord establish what has gone out from your mouth." So the woman remained and nursed her son until she weaned him.

**1:24** And she went up with him to Shiloh with a three-year-old bull, and loaves of bread, and an ephah of fine flour, and a skin of wine. And she entered the house of the Lord in Shiloh, and the boy was with them.

**1:25** And they came forward before the Lord, and his father slaughtered the sacrifice that he used to offer from days to days to the Lord. And he brought the boy forward and slaughtered the bull; and Hannah, the mother of the boy, came forward to Eli

**1:26** and said, "Pardon me, sir; as your soul lives, I am the woman who stood before you, with you, when praying to the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The Greek preserves distinct proper names compared to the Hebrew Masoretic Text: Armathaim Sipha (for Ramathaim-zophim, with Armathaim also used in 1:3, 1:19, and 2:11 where the Hebrew has Ramah), Jeremeel (for Jeroham), Thoke (for Tohu), and 'in Nasib of Ephraim' (for 'son of Zuph, an Ephrathite').
- **1:3** — Literally 'from days to days,' a Hebraistic Greek idiom meaning annually ('year by year'); similarly, 'the sacrifice of the days' (1:21; 2:19) refers to the annual sacrifice. 'Sabaoth' is an untranslated transliteration of the Hebrew title meaning 'hosts.'
- **1:5** — The Greek reads 'one portion' (vs. Hebrew 'a double/worthy portion') and in vv. 5-6 attributes Hannah's despondency directly to her childlessness rather than to provocation by Peninnah.
- **1:11** — 'Adonai, Lord, Eloe Sabaoth' preserves the Greek transliteration of Hebrew divine titles ('Lord ... God of hosts'). The Greek literally says 'seed of men' (meaning male offspring), specifies 'until the day of his death' and 'no iron' (vs. Hebrew 'all the days of his life' and 'razor'), and adds the abstention from wine and strong drink.
- **1:14** — The Greek uniquely has 'Eli's young attendant' address Hannah rather than Eli himself, and in 1:15 Hannah literally calls herself a woman 'whose day is hard' (vs. Hebrew 'troubled in spirit').
