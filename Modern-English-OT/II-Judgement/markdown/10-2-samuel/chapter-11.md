## Text

**11:1** And it happened at the return of the year, at the time of the going out of kings, that David sent Joab and his servants with him and all Israel, and they destroyed the sons of Ammon and besieged Rabbah; and David remained in Jerusalem.

**11:2** And it happened toward evening that David rose from his bed and was walking on the roof of the king's house; and from the roof he saw a woman bathing, and the woman was very beautiful in appearance.

**11:3** And David sent and inquired about the woman, and someone said, "Is this not Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliab, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?"

**11:4** And David sent messengers and took her, and he went in to her and lay with her; and she was purifying herself from her uncleanness; and she returned to her house.

**11:5** And the woman conceived, and she sent and reported to David and said, "I am pregnant."

**11:6** And David sent to Joab, saying, "Send Uriah the Hittite to me." And Joab sent Uriah to David.

**11:7** And Uriah came and went in to him, and David asked about the welfare of Joab and about the welfare of the people and about the welfare of the war.

**11:8** And David said to Uriah, "Go down to your house and wash your feet." And Uriah went out from the king's house, and a portion from the king went out after him.

**11:9** And Uriah slept at the door of the king with the servants of his lord, and he did not go down to his house.

**11:10** And they reported to David, saying, "Uriah did not go down to his house." And David said to Uriah, "Are you not coming from a journey? Why did you not go down to your house?"

**11:11** And Uriah said to David, "The ark and Israel and Judah are dwelling in tents, and my lord Joab and the servants of my lord are encamping on the face of the field; and shall I go into my house to eat and to drink and to lie with my wife? How? As your soul lives, I will not do this thing."

**11:12** And David said to Uriah, "Remain here today also, and tomorrow I will send you away." And Uriah remained in Jerusalem on that day and the next day.

**11:13** And David called him, and he ate before him and drank, and he made him drunk; and in the evening he went out to sleep on his bed with the servants of his lord, and he did not go down to his house.

**11:14** And it happened in the morning that David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it by the hand of Uriah.

**11:15** And he wrote in the letter, saying, "Lead Uriah in opposite the fierce battle, and you will turn back from behind him, and he will be struck and die."

**11:16** And it happened, while Joab was keeping watch over the city, that he put Uriah in the place where he knew that men of might were.

**11:17** And the men of the city came out and fought against Joab; and some of the people, of the servants of David, fell and died, and Uriah the Hittite also.

**11:18** And Joab sent and reported to King David all the matters of the war, to speak to the king.

**11:19** And he commanded the messenger, saying, "When you have finished speaking all the matters of the war to the king,

**11:20** if it happens that the wrath of the king rises and he says to you, 'Why did you draw near to the city to fight? Did you not know that they would shoot from atop the wall?

**11:21** Who struck down Abimelech son of Jeroboam son of Ner? Did not a woman throw a piece of a millstone on him from atop the wall, and he died in Thamasi? Why did you approach the wall?' then you will say, 'Uriah your servant, the Hittite, also died.'"

**11:22** And the messenger of Joab went to the king in Jerusalem, and he came and reported to David all that Joab had reported to him, all the matters of the war. And David became angry with Joab, and said to the messenger, "Why did you approach the city to fight? Did you not know that you would be struck from the wall? Who struck down Abimelech son of Jeroboam? Did not a woman throw a piece of a millstone on him from the wall, and he died in Thamasi? Why did you approach the wall?"

**11:23** And the messenger said to David, "Because the men prevailed against us and came out against us into the field, and we were upon them as far as the entrance of the gate;

**11:24** and the archers shot at your servants from atop the wall, and some of the king's servants died, and your servant Uriah the Hittite also died."

**11:25** And David said to the messenger, "Thus you will say to Joab, 'Do not let this thing be evil in your eyes, for the sword devours now in this way and now in that way; strengthen your battle against the city and tear it down.' And strengthen him."

**11:26** And the wife of Uriah heard that Uriah her husband had died, and she mourned for her husband.

**11:27** And the mourning passed, and David sent and brought her into his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. But the thing that David had done appeared evil in the eyes of the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — The Greek names Bathsheba's father "Eliab" rather than the Masoretic Text's "Eliam" (and spells Bathsheba as Bersabee).
- **11:4** — The Greek reads "he went in to her" (masculine subject, feminine object), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "she came to him."
- **11:11** — Instead of the Masoretic Text's double oath "as you live and as your soul lives," the Greek opens with the exclamation "How?" (pos) followed by "as your soul lives."
- **11:21** — The Greek reads "Jeroboam son of Ner" (MT: Jerubbesheth) and "Thamasi" (MT: Thebez), and describes the weapon as "a piece of a millstone" (MT: an upper millstone).
- **11:22** — Unlike the Masoretic Text, the Greek narrates the fulfillment of Joab's prediction, reporting David's anger at Joab and his full rebuke to the messenger (here naming Abimelech's father simply "Jeroboam," without "son of Ner").
