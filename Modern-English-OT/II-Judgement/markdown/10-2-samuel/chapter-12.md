## Text

**12:1** And the Lord sent Nathan the prophet to David, and he went in to him and said to him, "There were two men in one city, one rich and one poor.

**12:2** And the rich man had very many flocks and herds,

**12:3** but the poor man had nothing except one small ewe lamb, which he acquired and kept alive and raised; and it grew up together with him and with his sons. From his bread it would eat, and from his cup it would drink, and in his bosom it would sleep, and it was to him like a daughter.

**12:4** And a traveler came to the rich man, and he held back from taking from his flocks and from his herds to prepare for the traveling guest who had come to him, and he took the poor man's ewe lamb and prepared it for the man who had come to him."

**12:5** And David grew exceedingly furious with anger against the man, and David said to Nathan, "As the Lord lives, surely the man who did this is a son of death,

**12:6** and he shall repay the ewe lamb sevenfold, because he did this thing and because he showed no pity."

**12:7** And Nathan said to David, "You are the man who did this! For this is what the Lord, the God of Israel, says: 'I am the One who anointed you as king over Israel, and I am the One who rescued you from the hand of Saul;

**12:8** and I gave you your master's house and your master's wives into your bosom, and I gave you the house of Israel and Judah; and if it is too little, I will add to you according to these things.'

**12:9** Because you despised the word of the Lord to do what is evil in His eyes: you struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword, and you took his wife for yourself as a wife, and him you killed with the sword of the sons of Ammon;

**12:10** and now a sword will not depart from your house forever, because you showed contempt and took the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your wife.

**12:11** This is what the Lord says: 'Look, I am raising up evils against you out of your house, and I will take your wives before your eyes and give them to your neighbor, and he will lie with your wives in the sight of this sun.

**12:12** For you acted in secret, but I will do this thing before all Israel and in the sight of this sun.'"

**12:13** And David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against the Lord." And Nathan said to David, "The Lord also has put away your sin; you will certainly not die.

**12:14** However, because you have greatly provoked the enemies of the Lord by this thing, even your son who was born to you will surely die."

**12:15** And Nathan departed to his house. And the Lord struck the child whom Uriah's wife bore to David, and he became ill.

**12:16** And David sought God concerning the child, and David fasted a fast and went in and spent the night on the ground.

**12:17** And the elders of the house stood over him to raise him from the ground, and he was not willing; and he did not eat bread with them.

**12:18** And it happened on the seventh day that the child died. And David's servants were afraid to tell him that the child was dead, for they said, "Look, while the child was still alive, we spoke to him, and he did not listen to our voice; and how can we say to him that the child is dead? And he will do evil things."

**12:19** And David perceived that his attendants were whispering, and David realized that the child was dead. And David said to his attendants, "Is the child dead?" And they said, "He is dead."

**12:20** And David arose from the ground, and washed himself and anointed himself and changed his garments, and went into the house of God and worshiped Him; and he went into his house and asked for bread to eat, and they set bread before him, and he ate.

**12:21** And his attendants said to him, "What is this thing that you have done? For the sake of the child while he was still alive, you were fasting and weeping and staying awake; yet when the child died, you arose and ate bread and have drunk."

**12:22** And David said, "While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept, for I said, 'Who knows whether the Lord will have mercy on me, and the child will live?'

**12:23** But now he is dead; why then am I fasting? Can I bring him back anymore? I will go to him, and he will not return to me."

**12:24** And David comforted Bathsheba his wife, and he went in to her and lay with her; and she conceived and bore a son, and called his name Solomon, and the Lord loved him.

**12:25** And He sent by the hand of Nathan the prophet, and he called his name Idedei, for the sake of the Lord.

**12:26** And Joab fought against Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, and captured the city of the kingdom.

**12:27** And Joab sent messengers to David and said, "I have fought against Rabbah and have captured the city of the waters;

**12:28** and now gather the rest of the people, and encamp against the city and capture it first, so that I do not capture the city first and my name be called over it."

**12:29** And David gathered all the people and went to Rabbah, and fought against it and captured it.

**12:30** And he took the crown of Melchol their king from his head, and its weight was a talent of gold and precious stone, and it was on David's head; and he brought out very much plunder of the city.

**12:31** And he brought out the people who were in it and set them to the saw and to iron threshing sledges, and led them through the brickworks; and so he did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon. And David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **12:6** — Greek reads "sevenfold" (kept also in 4QSam^a), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "fourfold."
- **12:9** — Greek begins with "Because" (linking causally into 12:10) rather than the Masoretic interrogative "Why?" and in 12:10 omits "Me" after "showed contempt."
- **12:25** — Greek renders the name as "Idedei" (Idedei, without the divine suffix), corresponding to Hebrew "Jedidiah."
- **12:30** — Greek preserves both the proper name "Melchol" and "their king," where the Masoretic Text has a single word vocalized either "their king" or "Milcom."
