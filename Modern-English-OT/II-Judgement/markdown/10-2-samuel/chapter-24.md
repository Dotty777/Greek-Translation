## Text

**24:1** And the Lord caused wrath to be kindled again against Israel, and He incited David against them, saying, "Go, count Israel and Judah."

**24:2** And the king said to Joab the commander of the force who was with him, "Go through now all the tribes of Israel, from Dan even to Beersheba, and register the people, and I will know the number of the people."

**24:3** And Joab said to the king, "Now may the Lord God add to the people as many as they are and as many as they are a hundredfold, while the eyes of my lord the king are looking on; yet why does my lord the king desire this matter?"

**24:4** And the word of the king prevailed against Joab and against the commanders of the army; and Joab and the commanders of the force went out from before the king to register the people of Israel.

**24:5** And they crossed the Jordan and encamped at Aroel, on the right side of the city that is in the middle of the valley of Gad and Eliezer.

**24:6** And they came to Gilead and to Thabason, which is Nadasai, and they arrived at Dan-idan and Udan, and went around to Sidon.

**24:7** And they came to Mapsar of Tyre and all the cities of the Hivite and the Canaanite; and they came toward the south of Judah to Beersheba.

**24:8** And they traveled throughout all the land; and they arrived at Jerusalem at the end of nine months and twenty days.

**24:9** And Joab gave the number of the registration of the people to the king; and Israel was eight hundred thousand men of valor drawing the sword, and the men of Judah were five hundred thousand fighting men.

**24:10** And David's heart struck him after he had counted the people; and David said to the Lord, "I have sinned greatly in what I have done; now, Lord, please remove the lawlessness of Your servant, for I have acted very foolishly."

**24:11** And David arose in the morning; and the word of the Lord came to Gad the prophet, David's seer, saying,

**24:12** "Go and speak to David, saying, 'Thus says the Lord: Three things I am lifting upon you; choose for yourself one of them, and I will do it to you.'"

**24:13** And Gad went in to David, and reported to him and said to him, "Choose for yourself what is to happen: whether three years of famine shall come to you in your land, or for three months you flee before your enemies while they are pursuing you, or there be three days of death in your land. Now therefore know and see what word I should answer to Him who sent me."

**24:14** And David said to Gad, "I am greatly hemmed in on every side; I will now fall into the hand of the Lord, for His compassions are exceedingly many, but into the hands of man I will by no means fall."

**24:15** And David chose death for himself; and they were the days of the wheat harvest, and the Lord sent death in Israel from early morning until the hour of the midday meal; and the plague began among the people, and there died of the people, from Dan even to Beersheba, seventy thousand men.

**24:16** And the angel of God stretched out his hand toward Jerusalem to destroy it, and the Lord relented concerning the calamity and said to the angel who was bringing destruction among the people, "It is enough now; hold back your hand." And the angel of the Lord was by the threshing floor of Orna the Jebusite.

**24:17** And David spoke to the Lord, when he saw the angel striking among the people, and he said, "Look, it is I who have done wrong, and these sheep, what have they done? Let Your hand now be against me and against my father's house."

**24:18** And Gad came to David on that day and said to him, "Go up and set up an altar to the Lord on the threshing floor of Orna the Jebusite."

**24:19** And David went up according to the word of Gad, in the manner that the Lord had commanded him.

**24:20** And Orna looked out and saw the king and his servants passing along above him; and Orna went out and bowed down to the king with his face to the ground.

**24:21** And Orna said, "Why has my lord the king come to his servant?" And David said, "To buy the threshing floor from you, to build an altar to the Lord." And the plague was restrained from over the people.

**24:22** And Orna said to David, "Let my lord the king take and offer up to the Lord what is good in his eyes; here are the oxen for a burnt offering, and the wheels and the gear of the oxen for wood."

**24:23** All these things Orna gave to the king; and Orna said to the king, "May the Lord your God bless you."

**24:24** And the king said to Orna, "No, rather I will surely buy it from you for a price, and I will not offer up to my Lord, my God, a burnt offering without cost." So David bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver.

**24:25** And David built an altar there to the Lord, and offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings; and Solomon added to the altar afterward, because it was small at first. And the Lord heeded the land, and the plague was restrained from over Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **24:5** — In 24:5-7 the Greek preserves distinctive place names compared to the Masoretic Text: Aroel (MT: Aroer), Eliezer (MT: toward Jazer), Thabason which is Nadasai (MT: land of Tahtim-hodshi), Dan-idan and Udan (MT: Dan-jaan and around), and transliterated Mapsar of Tyre (MT: fortress of Tyre).
- **24:13** — The Septuagint reads 'three years of famine' (matching 1 Chronicles 21:12) rather than 'seven years' as in the Masoretic Text, and renders 'pestilence' literally as 'death' (thanatos) here and in 24:15.
- **24:15** — The Greek includes clauses absent from the Masoretic Text ('And David chose death for himself; and they were the days of the wheat harvest... and the plague began among the people') and reads 'until the hour of the midday meal' (horas aristou) instead of 'until the appointed time'.
- **24:16** — The Greek consistently names the Jebusite 'Orna' (MT: Araunah; 1 Chronicles: Ornan) throughout 24:16-24. In 24:21 the final clause is an indicative statement ('And the plague was restrained') rather than a purpose clause inside David's speech.
- **24:25** — The sentence 'and Solomon added to the altar afterward, because it was small at first' is a Septuagint addition not found in the Masoretic Text.
