## Text

**8:1** And it happened after these things that David struck the foreigners and routed them; and David took the set-apart territory from the hand of the foreigners.

**8:2** And David struck Moab and measured them with cords, having made them lie down on the ground; and the two measured lines were to be put to death, and the two measured lines he kept alive. And Moab became servants to David, bringing gifts.

**8:3** And David struck Hadarezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah, as he was going to set his hand upon the river Euphrates.

**8:4** And David captured from him one thousand chariots and seven thousand horsemen and twenty thousand foot soldiers; and David disabled all the chariots, and reserved for himself one hundred chariots.

**8:5** And Syria of Damascus came to help Hadarezer king of Zobah, and David struck among the Syrians twenty-two thousand men.

**8:6** And David placed a garrison in Syria around Damascus, and the Syrians became servants to David, bringing gifts. And the Lord saved David wherever he went.

**8:7** And David took the golden bracelets that he had made upon the servants of Hadarezer king of Zobah, and he brought them to Jerusalem; and Shishak king of Egypt took them when he went up to Jerusalem in the days of Jeroboam son of Solomon.

**8:8** And from Masbak, from the choice cities of Hadarezer, King David took very much bronze; with it Solomon made the bronze sea and the pillars and the basins and all the vessels.

**8:9** And Tou the king of Hamath heard that David had struck the entire army of Hadarezer,

**8:10** and Tou sent his son Jedduran to King David to ask him concerning peace and to bless him because he had struck Hadarezer; and he struck him, for he was opposed to Hadarezer. And in his hands were silver vessels and gold vessels and bronze vessels.

**8:11** These also the king consecrated to the Lord, along with the silver and the gold that he had consecrated from all the cities that he had subdued:

**8:12** from Idumea, and from the land of Moab, and from the sons of Ammon, and from the foreigners, and from Amalek, and from the spoils of Hadarezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah.

**8:13** And David made a name for himself; and as he was returning, he struck Idumea in Gebelem, up to eighteen thousand.

**8:14** And he placed a garrison in Idumea, in all Idumea, and all the Idumeans became servants to the king. And the Lord saved David wherever he went.

**8:15** And David reigned over Israel, and he was carrying out justice and righteousness for all his people.

**8:16** And Joab son of Zeruiah was over the army, and Jehoshaphat son of Ahijah was over the records,

**8:17** and Zadok son of Ahitub and Ahimelech son of Abiathar were priests, and Asa was the scribe,

**8:18** and Benaiah son of Janak was counselor, and the Cherethites and the Pelethites; the sons of David were court rulers.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Greek allophyloi literally means 'foreigners' (people of another tribe) and is the Septuagint's regular term for the Philistines in 1-2 Kingdoms; 'the set-apart territory' (tēn aphōrismenēn) corresponds to Hebrew Metheg-ammah.
- **8:2** — The Greek reads 'the two measured lines' for both those put to death and those kept alive, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'two lines' to death and 'one full line' kept alive.
- **8:4** — The Greek reads 'one thousand chariots and seven thousand horsemen' (matching 1 Chronicles 18:4), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text of 2 Samuel 8:4 reads 'one thousand seven hundred horsemen.'
- **8:7** — Greek chlidōnas means 'bracelets' (unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text's 'shields'), and the closing clause about Shishak taking them is a Septuagint addition that names Jeroboam son of Solomon (Greek Ieroboam, rather than Rehoboam).
- **8:8** — Masbak (Greek Masbak) is a Septuagint-distinct place name followed by 'from the choice cities' (where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has 'Betah and Berothai, cities'), and the second sentence about Solomon's bronze furnishings is a Septuagint addition matching 1 Chronicles 18:8.
- **8:10** — Jedduran (Greek Ieddouran) is a Septuagint-distinct name closer to 'Hadoram' in 1 Chronicles 18:10 (where the Hebrew Masoretic Text of 2 Samuel 8:10 reads 'Joram'), and keimenos ('opposed') likely reflects or abbreviates antikeimenos ('adversary').
- **8:13** — Gebelem (Greek Gebelem) is a Septuagint-distinct place name transliterating the Hebrew phrase for 'Valley of Salt'; in vv. 12-14 the Greek uses Idumea (Idoumaia, the Septuagint name for Edom), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'Aram' (Syria) in vv. 12-13.
- **8:16** — Ahijah (Greek Acheia) is a Septuagint-distinct name where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'Ahilud.'
- **8:17** — Asa (Greek Asa) is a Septuagint-distinct name where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'Seraiah.'
- **8:18** — Janak (Greek Ianak) is a Septuagint-distinct name where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'Jehoiada'; the Greek also adds symboulos ('counselor') and renders the office of David's sons as aularchai ('court rulers,' vs. Masoretic 'priests').
