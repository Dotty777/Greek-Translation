## Text

**16:1** And David passed a little way beyond Roos, and look, Ziba, the attendant of Mephibosheth, was there to meet him with a pair of saddled donkeys, and on them were two hundred loaves of bread, one hundred clusters of raisins, one hundred dates, and a skin of wine.

**16:2** And the king said to Ziba, "What do you have these for?" And Ziba said, "The pack animals are for the household of the king to ride on, and the loaves of bread and the dates are for food for the attendants, and the wine is to drink for those who are exhausted in the wilderness."

**16:3** And the king said, "And where is the son of your lord?" And Ziba said to the king, "Look, he is staying in Jerusalem, for he said, 'Today the house of Israel will return to me the kingdom of my father.'"

**16:4** And the king said to Ziba, "Look, everything that belongs to Mephibosheth is yours." And Ziba said, "Bowing down, may I find favor in your eyes, my lord the king."

**16:5** And King David came as far as Bahurim; and look, a man was coming out from there, from the clan of the house of Saul, and his name was Shimei, the son of Gera; he came out, walking along and cursing,

**16:6** and throwing stones at David and at all the servants of King David; and all the people and all the mighty men were on his right and on the left of the king.

**16:7** And Shimei was saying this as he cursed: "Get out, get out, man of bloodshed and lawless man!

**16:8** The Lord has returned upon you all the bloodshed of the house of Saul, because you reigned in his place, and the Lord has given the kingdom into the hand of Absalom your son. And look, you are in your calamity, because you are a man of bloodshed."

**16:9** And Abishai the son of Zeruiah said to the king, "Why is this dead dog cursing my lord the king? Let me go over now and take off his head."

**16:10** And the king said, "What do I have to do with you, sons of Zeruiah? Leave him alone, and let him curse like this, because the Lord told him to curse David; and who will say, 'Why have you done so?'"

**16:11** And David said to Abishai and to all his servants, "Look, my own son who came out from my body seeks my life, and how much more now the son of the Jeminite! Leave him alone to curse, because the Lord has told him to;

**16:12** perhaps the Lord will look upon my humiliation, and He will return good to me in place of his curse on this day."

**16:13** And David and his men went along the road; and Shimei kept going along the side of the mountain next to him, going along and cursing, and throwing stones from beside him and scattering dust.

**16:14** And the king and all his people arrived exhausted, and they refreshed themselves there.

**16:15** And Absalom and all the men of Israel entered Jerusalem, and Ahithophel was with him.

**16:16** And it happened when Hushai the Archite, the friend of David, came to Absalom, that Hushai said to Absalom, "Long live the king!"

**16:17** And Absalom said to Hushai, "Is this your kindness toward your friend? Why did you not go with your friend?"

**16:18** And Hushai said to Absalom, "No, but following the one whom the Lord and this people and all the men of Israel have chosen, to him I will belong, and with him I will stay.

**16:19** And secondly, whom will I serve? Is it not before his son? Just as I served before your father, so I will be before you."

**16:20** And Absalom said to Ahithophel, "Give counsel among yourselves as to what we should do."

**16:21** And Ahithophel said to Absalom, "Go in to the concubines of your father, whom he left behind to guard the house, and all Israel will hear that you have disgraced your father, and the hands of all who are with you will grow strong."

**16:22** And they pitched the tent for Absalom on the roof, and Absalom went in to his father's concubines before the eyes of all Israel.

**16:23** And the counsel of Ahithophel, which he gave in the former days, was just as when one inquires by the word of God; so was all the counsel of Ahithophel, both for David and for Absalom.

## Translator Notes

- **16:1** — Greek transliterates Hebrew rosh ("summit") as the place name Roos and renders the dried fruit as "dates" rather than "summer fruit."
- **16:11** — Greek renders Hebrew "the Benjaminite" (ben-hayemini) literally as "the son of the Jeminite."
- **16:23** — Greek reads "in the former days" rather than "in those days."
