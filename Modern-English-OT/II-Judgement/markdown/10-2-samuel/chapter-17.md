## Text

**17:1** And Ahithophel said to Absalom, "Let me choose for myself now twelve thousand men, and I will rise up and pursue after David tonight.

**17:2** And I will come upon him while he is weary and weakened in his hands, and I will throw him into panic, and all the people who are with him will flee, and I will strike down the king all alone;

**17:3** and I will return all the people to you, just as a bride returns to her husband; only you are seeking the life of one man, and for all the people there will be peace."

**17:4** And the word was right in the eyes of Absalom and in the eyes of all the elders of Israel.

**17:5** And Absalom said, "Call now Hushai the Archite also, and let us hear what is in his mouth as well."

**17:6** And Hushai went in to Absalom; and Absalom spoke to him, saying, "It is according to this word that Ahithophel has spoken; should we act according to his word? If not, you speak."

**17:7** And Hushai said to Absalom, "This counsel that Ahithophel has given this time is not good."

**17:8** And Hushai said, "You know your father and his men, that they are very mighty and deeply bitter in their soul, like a bear robbed of her cubs in a field and like a fierce sow in the plain. And your father is a warrior, and he will certainly not let the people rest.

**17:9** For look, he is now hidden in one of the hills or in one of the places; and it will happen, when he falls upon them at the start, that whoever hears of it will say, 'There has been a slaughter among the people who follow Absalom,'

**17:10** then even he, a son of strength, whose heart is like the heart of a lion, will completely melt; for all Israel knows that your father is a mighty man, and those who are with him are sons of strength.

**17:11** For thus I myself firmly counsel: all Israel will surely be gathered to you, from Dan even to Beersheba, like the sand that is by the sea in multitude, and your own presence going in their midst;

**17:12** and we will come to him in one of the places where we may find him, and we will encamp upon him as the dew falls on the earth, and of him and the men who are with him we will not leave even one.

**17:13** And if he withdraws into a city, then all Israel will take ropes to that city, and we will drag it all the way into the brook, so that not even a stone will be left there."

**17:14** And Absalom and all the men of Israel said, "The counsel of Hushai the Archite is better than the good counsel of Ahithophel." And the Lord had commanded to thwart the counsel of Ahithophel, so that the Lord might bring upon Absalom all the evil.

**17:15** And Hushai the Archite said to Zadok and Abiathar the priests, "Thus and thus Ahithophel counseled Absalom and the elders of Israel, and thus and thus I counseled.

**17:16** And now send quickly and report to David, saying, 'Do not spend the night tonight in Araboth of the wilderness, but indeed hurry to cross over, so that he may not persuade the king and all the people who are with him.'"

**17:17** Now Jonathan and Ahimaaz were standing at the spring of Rogel, and the servant girl went and reported to them; and they were to go and report to King David, for they could not be seen entering the city.

**17:18** And a boy saw them, and they reported to Absalom; so the two of them went quickly and entered the house of a man in Bahurim, who had a cistern in his courtyard, and they went down into it.

**17:19** And the woman took and spread the covering over the opening of the cistern and dried araphoth on it, and nothing became known.

**17:20** And the servants of Absalom came to the woman at the house and said, "Where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan?" And the woman said to them, "They went a little past the water." And they searched and did not find them, and they returned to Jerusalem.

**17:21** Now it happened after they departed that they came up out of the cistern, and they went and reported to King David, and said to David, "Rise up and cross the water quickly, for Ahithophel has counseled thus concerning you."

**17:22** And David and all the people who were with him rose up and crossed the Jordan until the light of morning; not even one was missing who had not passed over the Jordan.

**17:23** And Ahithophel saw that his counsel was not carried out, and he saddled his donkey and rose up and went away to his house, to his city; and he gave orders to his household, and he hanged himself and died, and he was buried in the tomb of his father.

**17:24** And David went on to Mahanaim, and Absalom crossed the Jordan, he and all the men of Israel with him.

**17:25** And Absalom appointed Amasa in place of Joab over the army; and Amasa was the son of a man whose name was Jether the Israelite, who went in to Abigail, the daughter of Nahash, brother of Zeruiah, mother of Joab.

**17:26** And all Israel and Absalom encamped in the land of Gilead.

**17:27** And it happened when David came to Mahanaim, Ouesbei the son of Nahash from Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, and Machir the son of Ammiel from Lo-debar, and Barzillai the Gileadite from Rogelim,

**17:28** brought ten beds, and double-sided rugs, and ten basins, and earthenware vessels, and wheat and barley, and flour and barley meal, and beans and lentils,

**17:29** and honey and butter, and sheep and sapphoth of cattle; and they presented them to David and to the people who were with him to eat, for he said, "The people are hungry and exhausted and thirsty in the wilderness."

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — Greek preserves the fuller simile "just as a bride returns to her husband; only you are seeking the life of one man," which differs substantially from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **17:8** — Greek adds the second comparison "and like a fierce sow in the plain," not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **17:16** — "Araboth" transliterates a Hebrew term for plains or fords; "persuade" renders Greek katapeise (Codex Vaticanus), where other Greek witnesses read katapie ("swallow up").
- **17:19** — "Araphoth" transliterates an obscure Hebrew food term, likely crushed grain or groats, which the Greek says the woman "dried" on the covering.
- **17:25** — Greek reads masculine "brother" (adelphou), making Nahash the brother of Zeruiah, whereas the Hebrew reads feminine "sister" referring to Abigail.
- **17:27** — The Greek name "Ouesbei" (Hebrew "Shobi") incorporates the preceding Hebrew conjunction "and" into the name.
- **17:29** — "Sapphoth" transliterates an obscure Hebrew term, likely curds or cheeses of cattle; the Greek also reads singular "he said" rather than plural "they said."
