## Text

**1:1** And it happened after Saul died that David returned, striking Amalek; and David stayed in Ziklag two days.

**1:2** And it happened on the third day that, look, a man came from the camp, from the people of Saul, and his garments were torn and earth was on his head; and it happened when he came in to David that he fell to the ground and bowed down to him.

**1:3** And David said to him, "Where are you coming from?" And he said to him, "From the camp of Israel I have escaped."

**1:4** And David said to him, "What is this matter? Report to me." And he said, "The people fled from the battle, and many from the people have fallen and died; and Saul also died, and Jonathan his son died."

**1:5** And David said to the young man who was reporting to him, "How do you know that Saul and Jonathan his son are dead?"

**1:6** And the young man who was reporting to him said, "By chance I happened to be on Mount Gilboa, and look, Saul was leaning on his spear, and look, the chariots and the cavalry commanders closed in on him.

**1:7** And he looked behind him, and saw and called me, and I said, 'Here I am.'

**1:8** And he said to me, 'Who are you?' And I said, 'I am an Amalekite.'

**1:9** And he said to me, 'Stand over me now and put me to death, for a terrible darkness has seized me, because all my life is in me.'

**1:10** And I stood over him and put him to death, because I knew that he would not live after he fell; and I took the royal crown that was on his head and the armlet that was on his arm, and I have brought them here to my lord."

**1:11** And David took hold of his garments and tore them, and all the men who were with him tore their garments.

**1:12** And they mourned and fasted and wept until evening over Saul and over Jonathan his son, and over the people of Judah, and over the house of Israel, because they were struck down by the sword.

**1:13** And David said to the young man who was reporting to him, "Where are you from?" And he said, "I am the son of a resident alien, an Amalekite man."

**1:14** And David said to him, "How were you not afraid to raise your hand to destroy the anointed of the Lord?"

**1:15** And David called one of his young men and said, "Approach and fall upon him." And he struck him, and he died.

**1:16** And David said to him, "Your blood be on your head, because your mouth answered against you, saying, 'I put to death the anointed of the Lord.'"

**1:17** And David lamented this lament over Saul and over Jonathan his son,

**1:18** and he said to teach the sons of Judah; look, it is written in the Book of the Upright:

**1:19** "Set up a monument, Israel, for those who have died, wounded on your heights! How mighty men have fallen!

**1:20** Do not announce it in Gath, and do not proclaim good news in the thoroughfares of Ashkelon, so that daughters of foreigners may not rejoice, so that daughters of the uncircumcised may not exult.

**1:21** Mountains that are in Gilboa, let no dew come down and no rain upon you, nor fields of firstfruits; for there the shield of mighty men was abhorred; the shield of Saul was not anointed with oil.

**1:22** From the blood of the wounded, from the fat of mighty men, the bow of Jonathan did not turn back empty, and the sword of Saul did not return empty.

**1:23** Saul and Jonathan, the beloved and lovely ones, not separated, graceful in their life, and in their death they were not separated; swifter than eagles, and they were stronger than lions.

**1:24** Daughters of Israel, weep over Saul; weep for the one who clothed you in scarlet with your adornment, the one who placed golden adornment upon your clothing.

**1:25** How mighty men have fallen in the midst of the battle! Jonathan, wounded ones are on your heights!

**1:26** I grieve over you, my brother Jonathan; you were very lovely to me. Your love to me was made marvelous, beyond the love of women.

**1:27** How mighty men have fallen, and weapons of war have perished!"

## Translator Notes

- **1:6** — The Greek verb περιέπεσαν is morphologically third-person plural ("they happened/fell"), here reflecting an early uncial spelling (or dittography before ἐν) of first-person singular περιέπεσα ("I happened by chance"), as required by the speaker's eyewitness narrative in vv. 6-7.
- **1:9** — Greek reads "a terrible darkness has seized me" (σκότος δεινόν), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to "anguish" or "cramp."
- **1:12** — Greek reads "over the people of Judah" (τὸν λαὸν Ἰούδα), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "over the people of the Lord."
- **1:18** — Greek omits the title "the Bow" found in the Hebrew text and translates "Book of Jashar" literally as "the Book of the Upright" (βιβλίου τοῦ εὐθοῦς).
- **1:19** — Greek opens David's lament with the imperative "Set up a monument" (Στήλωσον) rather than "The glory/beauty" of the Hebrew text; similarly, v. 21 has "was abhorred" (προσωχθίσθη) and v. 25 has plural "wounded ones" (τραυματίαι).
