## Text

**4:1** And when all the people had finished crossing the Jordan, the Lord spoke to Joshua, saying,

**4:2** "Taking men from the people, one from each tribe,

**4:3** instruct them, 'Pick up twelve ready stones from the middle of the Jordan, and carrying these across along with you and them, place them in your encampment, wherever you camp there for the night.'"

**4:4** And Joshua, having summoned twelve of the distinguished men from the sons of Israel, one from each tribe,

**4:5** said to them, "Go forward in front of me, before the presence of the Lord, into the middle of the Jordan, and let each one pick up a stone from there and carry it on his shoulders according to the number of the twelve tribes of Israel,

**4:6** so that these may be for you a sign set in place continually; so that when your son asks you tomorrow, saying, 'What are these stones to us?'

**4:7** then you will explain to your son, saying, 'The Jordan River ceased before the ark of the covenant of the Lord of all the earth when it was crossing it.' And these stones will be a memorial for you, the sons of Israel, forever."

**4:8** And the sons of Israel did so, just as the Lord had commanded Joshua; and they took twelve stones from the middle of the Jordan, just as the Lord had instructed Joshua at the completion of the crossing of the sons of Israel, and carried them across with themselves into the camp and set them down there.

**4:9** Now Joshua also set up another twelve stones in the Jordan itself, in the place that had been under the feet of the priests who carried the ark of the covenant of the Lord; and they are there until this day.

**4:10** Now the priests who carried the ark of the covenant were standing in the Jordan until Joshua finished everything that the Lord had commanded to declare to the people; and the people hurried and crossed over.

**4:11** And it came about, when all the people had finished crossing, that the ark of the covenant of the Lord crossed over, and the stones in front of them.

**4:12** And the sons of Reuben and the sons of Gad and half the tribe of Manasseh crossed over armed in front of the sons of Israel, just as Moses had commanded them.

**4:13** Forty thousand ready for battle crossed over before the Lord to war, toward the city of Jericho.

**4:14** On that day the Lord exalted Joshua before the entire nation of Israel, and they feared him just as they did Moses, for as long as he lived.

**4:15** And the Lord spoke to Joshua, saying,

**4:16** "Command the priests who carry the ark of the covenant of the testimony of the Lord to come out of the Jordan."

**4:17** And Joshua commanded the priests, saying, "Come out of the Jordan."

**4:18** And it came about, when the priests who carried the ark of the covenant of the Lord came out of the Jordan and placed their feet on the ground, that the water of the Jordan rushed back to its place and flowed just as yesterday and the day before over the entire bank.

**4:19** And the people came up out of the Jordan on the tenth of the first month, and the sons of Israel encamped in Gilgal on the side toward the sunrise from Jericho.

**4:20** And these twelve stones, which he had taken from the Jordan, Joshua set up in Gilgal,

**4:21** saying, "When your sons ask you, saying, 'What are these stones?'

**4:22** declare to your sons, 'On dry land Israel crossed this Jordan,

**4:23** since the Lord our God dried up the water of the Jordan from before them until they crossed over, just as the Lord our God did to the Red Sea, which the Lord our God dried up before us until we passed through,

**4:24** so that all the nations of the earth may know that the power of the Lord is mighty, and so that you may worship the Lord our God in every work.'"

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — Greek reads "ready stones" (hetoimous) rather than specifying the standing-place of the priests, and includes the unusual phrase "along with you and them" (hama hymin kai autois).
- **4:11** — Greek reads "and the stones" (kai hoi lithoi) in front of them, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "and the priests."
- **4:18** — "yesterday and the day before" renders the Greek idiom echthes kai triten hemeran (literally "yesterday and the third day"), meaning previously.
- **4:24** — Greek reads "the power of the Lord" (he dynamis) and "in every work" (en panti ergo), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "the hand of the Lord" and "all the days."
