## Text

**31:1** I made a covenant with my eyes. And I will not think upon a virgin.

**31:2** And yet God apportioned from above, and an inheritance of the Sufficient One from on high.

**31:3** Woe, destruction for the unrighteous, and alienation for those who practice lawlessness.

**31:4** Will He Himself not see my way, and will not all my steps be numbered?

**31:5** And if I had walked with mockers, and if my foot hurried to deceit,

**31:6** for I have been weighed in a just balance, and the Lord knows my innocence;

**31:7** if my foot turned aside from the way, and if my heart followed my eye, and if with my hands I touched gifts,

**31:8** then may I sow and may others eat, and may I become rootless upon the earth.

**31:9** If my heart followed after the wife of another man, and if I lay in wait at her doors,

**31:10** then may my wife also please another, and may my little children be brought low.

**31:11** For it is an uncontrollable fury of wrath to defile a man's wife;

**31:12** for it is a fire burning over all parts, and wherever it comes, it destroys from the roots.

**31:13** And if I despised the claim of my manservant or maidservant when they contended against me,

**31:14** for what will I do if the Lord makes an examination of me? And if also a visitation, what answer will I make?

**31:15** Just as I myself came into being in the womb, have they not also come into being? And we have come into being in the same womb.

**31:16** And the powerless did not fail to obtain whatever need they ever had, and I did not cause the eye of a widow to waste away;

**31:17** and if I ate my morsel alone and did not share with an orphan;

**31:18** because from my youth I nurtured like a father, and from my mother's womb I guided;

**31:19** and if I overlooked a naked person perishing and did not clothe him;

**31:20** and if the powerless did not bless me, and their shoulders were warmed from the fleece of my lambs;

**31:21** if I lifted a hand against an orphan, trusting that much help is at hand for me,

**31:22** then may my shoulder fall away from the collarbone, and may my arm be shattered from the elbow.

**31:23** For fear gripped me, and because of His burden I will not endure.

**31:24** If I placed gold in my dust, and if I trusted in a precious stone,

**31:25** and if I rejoiced when my wealth became great, and if I laid my hand upon innumerable things;

**31:26** or do we not see the shining sun eclipsing, and the moon waning? For it is not in their power;

**31:27** and if my heart was secretly deceived, and if I placed my hand on my mouth and kissed it,

**31:28** then may this also be counted to me as the greatest lawlessness, because I lied before the Lord Most High.

**31:29** And if I exulted at the downfall of my enemies, and my heart said, "Well done,"

**31:30** then may my ear hear my curse, and then may I be made a byword by my people while being afflicted.

**31:31** And if my maidservants often said, "Who would grant us to be filled with his flesh?" while I was very kind;

**31:32** and outside a stranger did not spend the night, and my door stood open to everyone who came;

**31:33** and if, having sinned unintentionally, I hid my sin,

**31:34** for I did not shrink from a throng of a multitude so as not to confess before them; and if I allowed a powerless person to go out my door with an empty bosom;

**31:35** who would grant someone to hear me? And if I had not feared the hand of the Lord, a written bond that I had against someone,

**31:36** having placed it upon my shoulders as a crown, I would have read it,

**31:37** and if I did not tear it up and give it back, taking nothing from a debtor;

**31:38** if the land ever groaned against me, and if its furrows wept with one accord;

**31:39** and if I ate its strength alone without payment, and if I seized and grieved the soul of the owner of the land;

**31:40** then instead of wheat may nettle come forth for me, and instead of barley, bramble. And Job ceased from words.

## Translator Notes

- **31:2** — The Greek reads kai eti ("And yet") as a statement rather than the interrogative kai ti ("And what?") of some witnesses and the Hebrew. "The Sufficient One" (Hikanos) is the Septuagint's distinctive Greek title rendering Hebrew Shaddai.
- **31:6** — Rather than a wish ("let Him weigh me"), the Greek has a parenthetical perfect passive indicative (hestamai gar, "for I have been weighed"), expressing Job's confidence that he already stands tested.
- **31:10** — The Septuagint recasts the Hebrew imagery ("let my wife grind for another") as "may my wife also please another" and "may my little children be brought low."
- **31:24** — "In my dust" (eis choun mou) preserves the reading of the Greek text (suggesting buried gold or an early inner-Greek shift from ischyn, "strength," corresponding to Hebrew "confidence").
- **31:35** — In 31:35-37, the Septuagint recasts Job's call for a divine indictment into a counterfactual about a written debt-bond (syngraphen) held against a debtor (31:37): had he not feared the Lord's hand, he would have paraded and read it aloud instead of tearing it up and returning it without payment.
