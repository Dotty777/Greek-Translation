## Text

**36:1** Then Elihu, continuing further, says:

**36:2** "Wait for me a little longer so that I may teach you, for there is still speech in me.

**36:3** Taking up my knowledge from afar, and by my works I will speak righteous things in truth,

**36:4** and not unrighteous words; you understand unrighteously.

**36:5** But know that the Lord will by no means reject the innocent; powerful in strength of heart,

**36:6** He will by no means keep an ungodly person alive, and He will give justice to the poor.

**36:7** He will not take away His eyes from the righteous, and with kings on a throne He will indeed seat them to victory, and they will be exalted.

**36:8** And those who have been bound in manacles will be held fast in cords of poverty;

**36:9** and He will declare to them their works and their transgressions, because they will grow strong.

**36:10** But He will listen to the righteous, and He said that they will turn back from unrighteousness.

**36:11** If they listen and serve, they will complete their days in good things, and their years in splendor.

**36:12** But He does not save the ungodly, because they are unwilling to know the Lord, and because, when admonished, they were disobedient.

**36:13** And hypocrites in heart will appoint wrath; they will not cry out, because He bound them.

**36:14** May their soul therefore die in youth, and may their life be wounded by angels,

**36:15** because they afflicted the weak and the powerless; but He will set forth the justice of the meek.

**36:16** And an abyss deceived you even further out of the mouth of an enemy, a pouring down beneath it, and your table went down full of fatness.

**36:17** And justice will not be lacking from the righteous,

**36:18** but wrath will be upon the ungodly, because of the ungodliness of gifts that they were receiving for acts of unrighteousness.

**36:19** Do not let your mind willingly turn you aside from the plea of the powerless who are in distress,

**36:20** and all those who exert strength. Do not draw out the night, for peoples to go up in their place;

**36:21** but guard yourself that you do not do unrighteous things; for you made your choice for this rather than poverty.

**36:22** Look, the Mighty One will prevail in His strength; for who is a ruler like Him?

**36:23** And who is the one who examines His works? Or who is the one who said, 'He did unrighteous things'?

**36:24** Remember that His works are great, over which men ruled;

**36:25** every person has seen within himself, all who are wounded mortals.

**36:26** Look, the Mighty One is great, and we will not know; the number of His years is also boundless.

**36:27** Yet the drops of rain are numbered by Him, and they will be poured out in rain into a cloud;

**36:28** ancient things will flow, and clouds cast shade over countless mortals. He appointed a season for the animals, and they know the order of a resting place. At all these things, is your understanding not astonished, nor is your heart separated from your body?

**36:29** And if one understands the spreadings of a cloud, the equality of His tent,

**36:30** look, the song will extend over Him, and He covered the roots of the sea.

**36:31** For by them He will judge peoples; He will give food to the one who listens.

**36:32** Upon hands He covered light, and He commanded concerning it upon one who comes to meet;

**36:33** He will declare His friend concerning it; the Lord is a possession, even concerning unrighteousness."

## Translator Notes

- **36:3** — The Greek reads "by my works" (ergois de mou), reflecting a different reading of the underlying Hebrew from the Masoretic Text ("to my Maker").
- **36:14** — The Greek reads "wounded by angels" (titroskomene hypo angelon), differing markedly from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("among the cult prostitutes").
- **36:28** — Greek palaiomata literally means "ancient/aged things" (also in 37:18, 21), an etymological rendering of the Hebrew word for "clouds/skies"; the final two sentences of this verse ("He appointed a season... from your body?") are a distinctive Septuagint expansion absent from the Masoretic Text.
- **36:30** — The Greek text reads "the song" (he ode), a notable Septuagint reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "His light."
- **36:33** — The Greek syntax is highly compressed and reflects a literal rendering of an obscure underlying text, reading "His friend," "the Lord is a possession," and "concerning unrighteousness."
