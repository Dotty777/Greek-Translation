## Text

**24:1** "And why have seasons escaped the notice of the Lord,

**24:2** while the ungodly have crossed over a boundary, seizing a flock together with the shepherd?

**24:3** They led away the pack animal of orphans, and they took a widow's ox in pledge.

**24:4** They turned the powerless aside from a righteous way, and all together the meek of the earth hid themselves.

**24:5** And they went off like donkeys in a field, going out from their own station on my behalf; bread was sweetened for him for the younger ones.

**24:6** Before the season they reaped a field that was not theirs; the powerless worked the vineyards of the ungodly without wages and without food.

**24:7** They made many sleep naked without garments, and they took away the clothing of their soul.

**24:8** They are soaked from the showers of the mountains; because they have no shelter for themselves, they wrapped themselves in a rock.

**24:9** They seized an orphan from the breast, and they humbled one who had fallen.

**24:10** And they made them sleep naked unjustly, and they took away the morsel of the hungry.

**24:11** In narrow places they lay in ambush unjustly, and the way of the righteous they did not know.

**24:12** They were being cast out from the city and their own houses, and the soul of infants groaned loudly.

**24:13** And why has He not made an inspection of these things? While they were on the earth, they did not even perceive, and the way of righteousness they did not know, nor did they walk in their paths.

**24:14** Yet knowing their works, He handed them over into darkness, and by night he will be like a thief.

**24:15** And the eye of an adulterer watched for darkness, saying, 'No eye will notice me,' and he put on a covering for his face.

**24:16** In darkness he dug through houses; by day they sealed themselves in; they did not recognize light;

**24:17** for to them all together the morning is the shadow of death, for he will recognize the turmoil of the shadow of death.

**24:18** He is swift upon the face of the water; may their portion be cursed on the earth,

**24:19** and may their plants appear dry on the earth; for they seized the sheaf of orphans.

**24:20** Then his sin was remembered, and like a mist of dew he vanished; and may what he did be repaid to him, and may every unjust person be shattered just like an incurable tree.

**24:21** And he did not do good to a barren woman, and he did not show mercy to one without a wife,

**24:22** and in wrath he overthrew the powerless. Therefore, when he rises up, he will by no means have confidence regarding his own life.

**24:23** When he falls ill, let him not hope to be healed, but he will fall by disease.

**24:24** For his exaltation afflicted many; yet he withered like mallow in the heat, or like an ear of grain falling off from a stalk of its own accord.

**24:25** And if not, who is the one who says that I speak falsehoods, and will turn my words into nothing?"

## Translator Notes

- **24:5** — The Septuagint differs markedly from the Hebrew here, reading "on my behalf" (hyper emou) and "bread was sweetened" (hedynthe, reflecting an alternate sense of the Hebrew root for "wilderness" or "sweet").
- **24:14** — Whereas the Hebrew depicts a murderer rising at dawn, the Septuagint depicts God knowing the works of the wicked and handing them over into darkness.
- **24:17** — Greek tarachos ("turmoil" or "terror") functions here as a heteroclite neuter accusative object of epignosetai ("he will recognize"); some manuscripts read the masculine plural accusative tarachous.
- **24:21** — Greek agynaios literally means "without a wife" or "wifeless," standing where the Hebrew text reads "widow."
