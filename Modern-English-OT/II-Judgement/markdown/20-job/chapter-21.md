## Text

**21:1** Then Job answered and said:

**21:2** "Listen, listen to my words, so that I may not have this comfort from you.

**21:3** Bear with me, and I will speak; then you will not mock me.

**21:4** What then? Is my reproof of a man? Or why should I not be angry?

**21:5** Looking at me, you will be astonished, placing a hand on your cheek.

**21:6** For even if I remember, I am agitated, and pains grip my flesh.

**21:7** Why do the ungodly live, and have they grown old even in wealth?

**21:8** Their offspring is according to their desire, and their children are before their eyes.

**21:9** Their houses prosper, and fear is nowhere, and a scourge from the Lord is not upon them.

**21:10** Their cow did not miscarry, and their pregnant one was brought safely through and did not fail.

**21:11** And they remain like eternal sheep, and their little children play;

**21:12** taking up harp and lyre, they also rejoice at the sound of a song.

**21:13** And they finished their life in good things, and in the rest of Hades they fell asleep.

**21:14** Yet he says to the Lord, 'Depart from me; I do not wish to know Your ways.

**21:15** What is the Sufficient One, that we should serve Him? And what benefit is there that we should meet Him?'

**21:16** For their good things were in their hands, yet He does not look upon the works of the ungodly.

**21:17** Nevertheless, the lamp of the ungodly will also be extinguished, and ruin will come upon them, and pangs will grip them from wrath.

**21:18** And they will be like chaff before the wind, or like dust which a whirlwind has carried off.

**21:19** May his possessions fail his sons; He will repay him, and he will know.

**21:20** May his eyes see his own slaughter, and from the Lord may he not be saved;

**21:21** for his pleasure is in his house with him, and the numbers of his months have been divided.

**21:22** Is it not the Lord who teaches understanding and knowledge? And He Himself will judge murders.

**21:23** This one will die in the strength of his simplicity, wholly at ease and prospering,

**21:24** and his inward parts are full of fat, and his marrow is diffused;

**21:25** while another dies from bitterness of soul, having eaten nothing good;

**21:26** yet together they sleep on the earth, and decay has covered them.

**21:27** So I know you, that with audacity you press upon me,

**21:28** so that you will say, 'Where is the house of a ruler? And where is the shelter of the tents of the ungodly?'

**21:29** Ask those passing along the road, and you will not treat their signs as alien,

**21:30** for the evil man is relieved for a day of destruction; they will be led away for a day of his wrath.

**21:31** Who will declare his way to his face? And what he himself has done, who will repay him?

**21:32** And he himself was carried away to the tombs, and he himself kept watch upon mounds.

**21:33** The pebbles of the torrent have become sweet to him, and after him every man will depart, and before him are countless ones.

**21:34** How then do you comfort me with empty things? And my resting from you is nothing."

## Translator Notes

- **21:2** — Both here ("so that I may not have this comfort") and in 21:3 ("then you will not mock me"), the Greek introduces explicit negations absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **21:15** — "The Sufficient One" translates Greek Ἱκανός, a distinctive Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew divine title Shaddai based on its traditional etymology ("sufficient").
- **21:16** — The Greek affirms that "their good things were in their hands" and adds that God "does not look upon the works of the ungodly," differing substantially from the Masoretic Text; likewise, 21:17 is framed as a statement ("Nevertheless...") rather than a question.
- **21:22** — The Greek reads "He Himself will judge murders" (φόνους), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "those on high."
- **21:30** — The Greek adds a singular pronoun in "a day of his wrath" (ὀργῆς αὐτοῦ) where the Masoretic Text has plural "wraths"; "his" most directly refers back to "the evil man" (either his own wrath or wrath against him), though some take it as referring to God's wrath.
