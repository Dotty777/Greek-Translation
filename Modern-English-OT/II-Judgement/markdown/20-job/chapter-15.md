## Text

**15:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:

**15:2** "Will a wise man give an answer of understanding of wind, and fill the pain of his belly,

**15:3** reproving with words one ought not to use, with speeches in which there is no benefit?

**15:4** Did you not also cast off fear, and carry out such words before the Lord?

**15:5** You are guilty by the words of your mouth, and you did not discern the words of the mighty.

**15:6** May your own mouth reprove you, and not I, and your lips will testify against you.

**15:7** What then? Were you born the first of men? Or were you solidified before the hills?

**15:8** Or have you heard the ordinance of the Lord? And has wisdom reached you?

**15:9** For what do you know that we do not know? Or what do you understand that we also do not?

**15:10** Both an old man and an ancient one are among us, heavier in days than your father.

**15:11** You have been scourged for few of the things you have sinned; you have spoken greatly, exceedingly.

**15:12** What did your heart dare? Or what did your eyes bring forward,

**15:13** that you burst out in wrath before the Lord, and brought out such words from your mouth?

**15:14** For who is a mortal, that he will be blameless, or one born of a woman, as one who will be righteous?

**15:15** If He does not trust in holy ones, and heaven is not pure before Him,

**15:16** let alone one who is abominable and impure, a man drinking injustices like a drink.

**15:17** Now I will declare to you, listen to me; what indeed I have seen I will declare to you,

**15:18** what wise men will say, which they did not hide from their fathers.

**15:19** To them alone the land was given, and no foreigner came upon them.

**15:20** All the life of an ungodly man is in anxiety, and numbered years are given to a mighty man,

**15:21** and his fear is in his ears; whenever he thinks he is already at peace, his overthrow will come.

**15:22** Let him not trust that he will turn back from darkness; for he has already been ordered into the hands of iron,

**15:23** and he has been assigned as food for vultures; and he knows within himself that he remains for a fall. And a dark day will whirl him about,

**15:24** and distress and affliction will seize him, like a general in the front rank falling;

**15:25** because he has lifted up his hands against the Lord, and before the Lord Almighty he stiffened his neck,

**15:26** and he ran against Him with insolence, with the thickness of the back of his shield;

**15:27** because he covered his face with his fat, and made a fold upon his thighs.

**15:28** And may he lodge in desolate cities, and may he enter into uninhabited houses; and what those men prepared, others will carry away.

**15:29** Neither will he become rich, nor will his possessions remain; he will by no means cast a shadow on the earth,

**15:30** nor will he escape the darkness; may a wind wither his shoot, and may his blossom fall off.

**15:31** Let him not trust that he will endure, for empty things will come about for him;

**15:32** his cutting will be destroyed before the season, and his young branch will by no means grow dense;

**15:33** and may he be harvested like an unripe grape before the season, and may he fall off like the blossom of an olive tree.

**15:34** For the testimony of an ungodly man is death, and fire will burn the houses of bribe-takers.

**15:35** And he will conceive pains in the womb, and empty things will come about for him, and his belly will bear deceit."

## Translator Notes

- **15:2** — Greek συνέσεως πνεύματος may mean "understanding of wind" or "understanding of spirit," and ἐνέπλησεν πόνον γαστρός (aorist, lit. "filled the pain of his belly") differs notably from the Masoretic Text ("fill his belly with the east wind").
- **15:11** — The Greek reads "You have been scourged for few of the things you have sinned" (ὀλίγα ὧν ἡμάρτηκας μεμαστίγωσαι), diverging sharply from the Masoretic Text ("Are the consolations of God too small for you?").
- **15:23** — Greek εἰς σῖτα γυψίν ("as food for vultures") reflects a reading of the Hebrew word for "where?" as "vulture/kite."
- **15:27** — Greek περιστόμιον literally denotes a "collar," "rim," or "border" (used here for a roll or fold of fat), likely chosen through an etymological link to the Hebrew word for "mouth."
- **15:34** — Greek μαρτύριον ("testimony") reflects Hebrew edut ("testimony") where the Masoretic Text reads adat ("company").
