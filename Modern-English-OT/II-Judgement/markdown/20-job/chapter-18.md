## Text

**18:1** Then Bildad the Shuhite answered and said,

**18:2** "How long will you not stop? Hold back, so that we ourselves may also speak.

**18:3** Why have we kept silent like four-footed animals before you?

**18:4** Anger has made use of you. What then? If you die, will the earth under heaven be uninhabited? Or will mountains be overturned from their foundations?

**18:5** And the light of the ungodly will be extinguished, and their flame will not come forth.

**18:6** His light is darkness in the dwelling, and the lamp will be extinguished for him.

**18:7** May the least hunt down his possessions, and may his counsel fail.

**18:8** And his foot has been cast into a snare; may he be entangled in a net.

**18:9** And may snares come upon him; He will strengthen thirsty ones against him.

**18:10** His rope has been hidden in the ground, and his capture is upon paths.

**18:11** May pains destroy him all around, and may many come around his foot in severe famine,

**18:12** and a terrible downfall has been prepared for him.

**18:13** May the branches of his feet be eaten, and death will devour his beauty.

**18:14** And may healing be torn away from his dwelling, and may distress seize him on a royal charge.

**18:15** It will dwell in his tent in his night; his splendid things will be strewn with sulfur.

**18:16** From below his roots will dry up, and from above his harvest will fall.

**18:17** May his memory perish from the earth, and there is a name for him on the face farther outside.

**18:18** May He drive him away from light into darkness.

**18:19** He will not be recognized among his people, nor will his house be saved on the earth under heaven,

**18:20** but others will live in what is his. The last groaned over him, and astonishment seized the first.

**18:21** These are the houses of the unrighteous, and this is the place of those who do not know the Lord."

## Translator Notes

- **18:9** — The third-person singular verb κατισχύσει ('will strengthen') has an unexpressed subject, understood as God (compare 18:18) or an indefinite 'one'; 'thirsty ones' (διψῶντας) reflects the Septuagint's distinctive rendering.
- **18:13** — Literally 'branches of his feet' (κλῶνες ποδῶν), an unusual Septuagint expression for the extremities or limbs of his body.
- **18:14** — Greek αἰτίᾳ βασιλικῇ ('on a royal charge' or 'for a royal cause') is a notable Septuagint departure from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's 'king of terrors.'
- **18:17** — The Greek text lacks the negation ('no name') found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and some later witnesses, reading literally 'there is a name for him on the face farther outside.'
