## Text

**30:1** But now they have laughed at me; the least ones now admonish me in turn, whose fathers I disdained, whom I did not consider worthy of the dogs of my flocks.

**30:2** And indeed, what is the strength of their hands to me? Upon them completion has perished.

**30:3** Barren in want and famine, those fleeing a waterless land yesterday, distress and misery;

**30:4** those surrounding saltwort by what resounds, whose food was saltwort; dishonored and despised, in want of every good thing.

**30:5** Thieves rose up against me,

**30:6** whose houses were caverns of rocks;

**30:7** among melodious ones, those who lived under wild brushwood will cry out;

**30:8** sons of fools and a name of the dishonored, and a reputation extinguished from the earth.

**30:9** But now I am their lyre, and they have me as a byword;

**30:10** and they abhorred me, standing far away, and from my face they did not withhold spit.

**30:11** For He opened His quiver and afflicted me, and He sent away the bridle of my face.

**30:12** On the right of a shoot they rose up; they stretched out their foot and made paths of their destruction against me.

**30:13** My paths were wiped out, for they stripped off my robe.

**30:14** With His darts He pierced me; He has treated me as He wills; I am steeped in pains.

**30:15** My pains turn back; my hope departed like wind, and like a cloud my salvation.

**30:16** And now my soul will be poured out upon me, and days of pains hold me;

**30:17** and by night my bones are confounded, and my sinews are loosened.

**30:18** With great strength He took hold of my robe; like the collar of my tunic He encompassed me.

**30:19** And You have considered me equal to clay; in earth and ashes is my portion.

**30:20** And I cry out to You, and You do not hear me; and they stood and observed me,

**30:21** and they came upon me mercilessly; with a mighty hand You scourged me;

**30:22** and You placed me in pains, and You cast me away from salvation.

**30:23** For I know that death will wipe me out; for the earth is a house for every mortal.

**30:24** If only I were able to lay hands on myself, or at least having asked another, and he will do this for me.

**30:25** Yet I wept over every helpless person, and I groaned when I saw a man in distresses.

**30:26** And while I was waiting for good things, look, days of evil things met me instead.

**30:27** My belly boiled over and will not be silent; days of poverty overtook me.

**30:28** Groaning I have walked without a muzzle, and I stand in the assembly crying out.

**30:29** I have become a brother of sirens, and a companion of ostriches.

**30:30** And my skin has been greatly darkened, and my bones from heat.

**30:31** And my lyre turned into grief, and my song into weeping for me.

## Translator Notes

- **30:4** — The Greek pairs halima ("saltwort", a salt-marsh plant) with alima (either a spelling variant or a wordplay suggesting hunger-staving food), while "by what resounds" (and "melodious ones" in 30:7) reflects the Septuagint rendering Hebrew words for bushes as terms of sound.
- **30:20** — Unlike the Hebrew, the Greek shifts here and in 30:21a to third-person plural verbs ("they stood and observed me, and they came upon me mercilessly") before returning to second-person singular address to God in 30:21b.
- **30:24** — In place of a difficult Hebrew line, the Septuagint expresses Job's explicit wish to lay violent hands on himself (cheirosasthai) or to ask someone else to do so for him.
- **30:28** — "Without a muzzle" (aneu phimou) is a distinctive Septuagint reading where the Hebrew has "without sun," and "sirens" in 30:29 is the standard Septuagint rendering of desert creatures where the Hebrew has "jackals."
