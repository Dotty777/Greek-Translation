## Text

**42:1** Then Job answered and said to the Lord,

**42:2** "I know that You are able to do all things, and nothing is impossible for You.

**42:3** For who is the one who hides counsel from You? And holding back words, does he think to hide even from You? And who will declare to me what I did not know, great and marvelous things that I did not understand?

**42:4** Now hear me, Lord, so that I also may speak; I will ask, and You teach me.

**42:5** With the hearing of the ear I heard of You formerly, but now my eye has seen You.

**42:6** Therefore I held myself in contempt and wasted away, and I have regarded myself as earth and ashes."

**42:7** And it happened, after the Lord had spoken all these words to Job, that the Lord said to Eliphaz the Temanite, "You have sinned, you and your friends; for you have spoken nothing true before Me, as My servant Job has.

**42:8** Now then, take seven calves and seven rams, and go to My servant Job, and he will offer burnt offerings on your behalf. And Job, My servant, will pray for you, for I will accept only his face; for had it not been for him, I would have destroyed you; for you have not spoken what is true against My servant Job."

**42:9** So Eliphaz the Temanite and Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Minaean went and did just as the Lord had commanded them; and He pardoned their sin because of Job.

**42:10** And the Lord made Job great; and when he prayed also for his friends, He forgave them their sin; and the Lord gave twice as much as all that belonged to Job before, in double measure.

**42:11** And all his brothers and his sisters heard all the things that had happened to him, and came to him, along with all who had known him from the first. And after eating and drinking with him, they comforted him and marveled at all that the Lord had brought upon him; and each one gave him one ewe lamb and a tetradrachm of gold, unstamped.

**42:12** And the Lord blessed the latter days of Job more than the former; and his livestock were fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, one thousand yoke of oxen, and one thousand grazing female donkeys.

**42:13** And seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

**42:14** And he named the first Day, and the second Cassia, and the third Horn of Amalthea.

**42:15** And none were found comparable to the daughters of Job, better than they, in the earth under heaven; and their father gave them an inheritance among their brothers.

**42:16** And Job lived after the affliction one hundred and seventy years, and all the years he lived were two hundred and forty; and Job saw his sons and the sons of his sons, the fourth generation.

**42:17** And Job died, old and full of days. And it is written that he will rise again with those whom the Lord raises up. This man is explained from the Syriac book as dwelling in the land of Uz, on the borders of Idumea and Arabia; and previously his name was Jobab. And having taken an Arabian wife, he fathered a son whose name was Ennon; and he himself was born of his father Zerah, a son from the sons of Esau, and of his mother Bozrah, so that he was fifth from Abraham. And these are the kings who reigned in Edom, the country over which he himself also ruled: first, Balak the son of Beor, and the name of his city was Dinhabah; and after Balak, Jobab, who is called Job; and after him, Husham, who was governor from the Temanite country; and after him, Hadad the son of Barad, who cut down Midian in the plain of Moab, and the name of his city was Getthaim. And the friends who came to him were Eliphaz, of the sons of Esau, king of the Temanites; Bildad, the ruler of the Shuhites; Zophar, the king of the Minaeans.

## Translator Notes

- **42:6** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text's 'repent in dust and ashes,' the Septuagint reads 'I held myself in contempt and wasted away, and I have regarded myself as earth and ashes.'
- **42:8** — The Greek idiom 'accept only his face' (introduced by a Hebraism for 'for only') means to show favor to Job alone. Unlike the Masoretic Text, the Greek has Job offer the burnt offerings, states 'I would have destroyed you,' and faults the friends for not speaking truthfully 'against My servant Job.'
- **42:9** — The Septuagint calls Zophar 'the Minaean' (and 'king of the Minaeans' in 42:17), referring to an Arabian people rather than the Masoretic Text's 'Naamathite.'
- **42:14** — The Septuagint renders the names of Job's three daughters into Greek as Day (Hemera), Cassia, and Horn of Amalthea (the Greek horn of plenty), corresponding to Hebrew Jemimah, Keziah, and Keren-happuch.
- **42:17** — Following the Septuagint lifespan in 42:16 (one hundred and seventy years after the affliction; two hundred and forty in all), this verse appends an ancient Greek postscript affirming Job's resurrection and identifying him with Jobab of Genesis 36:31-35. It preserves the Septuagint-distinct names Ennon, Balak (Hebrew Bela), Barad (Hebrew Bedad), and Getthaim (Hebrew Avith), and refers to Uz as Ausitis.
