## Text

**12:1** Then Job answered and said:

**12:2** "So then, you are human beings; indeed, wisdom will die with you.

**12:3** I also have a heart like yours.

**12:4** For a righteous and blameless man has become a laughingstock;

**12:5** for he had been prepared for an appointed time to fall by others, and for his houses to be plundered by the lawless.

**12:6** Nevertheless, let no one who is wicked be confident that he will be guiltless, all who provoke the Lord to anger, as though there will not also be an examination of them.

**12:7** But now, ask the four-footed animals if they will tell you, and the birds of heaven if they will report to you;

**12:8** speak to the earth if it will inform you, and the fish of the sea will explain to you.

**12:9** Who then has not known among all these that the hand of the Lord made these things?

**12:10** Is not the soul of all living things and the spirit of every human being in His hand?

**12:11** For the mind discerns words, and the throat tastes food.

**12:12** In much time is wisdom, and in a long life is knowledge.

**12:13** With Him are wisdom and power; His are counsel and understanding.

**12:14** If He tears down, who will build? If He shuts against people, who will open?

**12:15** If He holds back the water, He will dry up the earth; and if He lets it loose, He destroys it by overturning it.

**12:16** With Him are might and strength; His are knowledge and understanding.

**12:17** He leads counselors away captive, and drives the judges of the earth out of their minds.

**12:18** He seats kings upon thrones, and binds their waists with a belt.

**12:19** He sends priests away captive, and overturns the mighty ones of the earth.

**12:20** He changes the lips of the trustworthy, and knows the understanding of the elders.

**12:21** He pours out dishonor upon rulers, and heals the humble.

**12:22** He uncovers deep things out of darkness, and brings out the shadow of death into light.

**12:23** He lays nations low and guides them.

**12:24** He changes the hearts of the rulers of the earth, and leads them astray in a way they did not know.

**12:25** May they grope in darkness and not light, and may they wander astray like a drunkard."

## Translator Notes

- **12:5** — Verses 12:4-6 in the Septuagint present a substantially different line of thought from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, emphasizing the plundering of the righteous man and the certainty of an examination of the wicked.
- **12:10** — The Greek begins literally with ei me ("if not" or "unless"), functioning here as an emphatic rhetorical question ("Is not...?").
- **12:11** — The Greek reads nous ("mind") rather than ous ("ear"), and larynx ("throat") for the organ of taste.
- **12:18** — Throughout 12:17-24, the Greek pairs present participles with gnomic aorists (rendered here in the timeless present) and preserves distinctive Septuagint readings such as "seats kings upon thrones" (12:18), "knows the understanding of the elders" (12:20), and "heals the humble" (12:21).
- **12:25** — Both verbs in the Greek are in the optative mood, expressing a wish ("May they grope... and may they wander astray") rather than a simple indicative statement.
