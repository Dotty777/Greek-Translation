## Text

**20:1** Then Zophar the Minaean answered and said:

**20:2** "I did not suppose that you would reply with these things like this, and you do not understand any more than I also do.

**20:3** I will hear the discipline of my shame, and a spirit from understanding will answer me.

**20:4** Have you known these things from of old, since man was placed on the earth?

**20:5** Now the gladness of the ungodly is a dreadful downfall, and the joy of the lawless is destruction,

**20:6** even if his gifts ascend to heaven, and his sacrifice touches the clouds.

**20:7** For when he thinks he is already firmly established, then he will utterly perish; and those who knew him will say, 'Where is he?'

**20:8** Like a dream that has taken flight, he will by no means be found, and he has flown away like a night vision;

**20:9** an eye caught a glimpse, and will not do so again, and his place will no longer notice him.

**20:10** May inferiors destroy his sons, and may his hands burn with pains.

**20:11** His bones were filled with his youth, and it will sleep with him on the dust.

**20:12** If wickedness becomes sweet in his mouth, he will hide it under his tongue,

**20:13** he will not spare it and will not abandon it, and he will gather it in the middle of his throat,

**20:14** and he will by no means be able to help himself; the gall of an asp is in his belly.

**20:15** Wealth unjustly gathered will be vomited up; an angel will drag it out of his house.

**20:16** May he suck the venom of dragons, and may the tongue of a serpent kill him.

**20:17** May he not see the milking of grazing herds, nor pastures of honey and butter.

**20:18** For empty and futile things he labored, wealth of which he will not taste, like gristle, unchewable, unswallowable.

**20:19** For he crushed the houses of many powerful ones; he seized a dwelling and did not establish it.

**20:20** There is no safety for his possessions; in his desire he will not be saved.

**20:21** There is no remnant of his food; therefore his good things will not flourish.

**20:22** And when he thinks he is already filled, he will be afflicted, and every distress will come upon him.

**20:23** If somehow he should fill his belly, may He send forth upon him the fury of wrath; may He shower pains upon him;

**20:24** and he will by no means be saved from the hand of iron; may a bronze bow wound him;

**20:25** and may an arrow pass through his body, and may stars walk about in his dwellings; terrors are upon him,

**20:26** and may all darkness await him; an unkindled fire will devour him, and may a stranger harm his house.

**20:27** May heaven uncover his lawless deeds, and may the earth rise up against him.

**20:28** May destruction utterly drag away his house; may a day of wrath come upon him.

**20:29** This is the portion of an ungodly man from the Lord, and the possession of what belongs to him from the Overseer."

## Translator Notes

- **20:1** — The Greek text identifies Zophar as "the Minaean" (Μειναῖος), a South Arabian people, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the Naamathite."
- **20:15** — The Greek reads "an angel will drag it out of his house," differing from the Masoretic Text ("from his belly God will drive them out"); the masculine pronoun "it" (αὐτόν) may refer either to "wealth" (πλοῦτος) or to the ungodly man ("him").
- **20:19** — The Greek reads "of many powerful ones" (πολλῶν δυνατῶν), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to "the poor" (some scholars suspect an early Greek transcriptional shift from ἀδυνάτων, "weak ones").
- **20:25** — The Greek literally reads "may stars walk about in his dwellings" (ἄστρα δὲ ἐν διαίταις αὐτοῦ περιπατήσαισαν), diverging sharply from the Masoretic Text's imagery of a glittering point drawn from his gall.
- **20:29** — The Greek concludes with the distinctive divine title "the Overseer" (τοῦ ἐπισκόπου) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "God."
