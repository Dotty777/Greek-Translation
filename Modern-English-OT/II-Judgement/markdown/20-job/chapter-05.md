## Text

**5:1** "Now call out, if anyone will answer you, or if you will see any of the holy angels.

**5:2** For indeed anger kills a fool, and jealousy puts to death one who has gone astray.

**5:3** And I have seen fools putting down a root, but immediately their dwelling was devoured.

**5:4** May their sons be far from safety, and may they be humiliated at the doors of inferiors, and there will be no rescuer.

**5:5** For what those people gathered, the righteous will eat, while they themselves will not be rescued from evils; may their strength be siphoned away.

**5:6** For trouble will certainly not come out from the earth, nor will pain sprout from the mountains;

**5:7** rather, a human being is born for trouble, while the young of a vulture fly to the heights.

**5:8** Nevertheless, I will plead with the Lord, and I will call upon the Lord, the Master of all,

**5:9** who does great and unsearchable things, glorious and extraordinary things, of which there is no number;

**5:10** who gives rain upon the earth, sending water upon what is under heaven;

**5:11** who sets the lowly on high, and raises up those who have perished;

**5:12** who alters the plans of the crafty, and their hands will certainly not accomplish what is true.

**5:13** He is the One who catches the wise in shrewdness, and He confounded the plan of the devious.

**5:14** By day darkness will meet them, and at noon may they grope just as at night.

**5:15** And may they perish in war, and may the powerless escape from the hand of a powerful one;

**5:16** and may there be hope for the powerless, and may the mouth of the unrighteous be stopped.

**5:17** Now blessed is the person whom the Lord reproved; and do not reject the admonition of the Almighty.

**5:18** For He Himself causes pain and restores again; He struck, and His hands healed.

**5:19** Six times He will rescue you from distresses, and in the seventh evil will certainly not touch you.

**5:20** In famine He will deliver you from death, and in war He will release you from the hand of iron.

**5:21** From the lash of the tongue He will hide you, and you will certainly not be afraid of coming evils.

**5:22** You will laugh at the unrighteous and the lawless, and you will certainly not be afraid of wild beasts.

**5:23** For wild beasts will be at peace with you.

**5:24** Then you will know that your house will be at peace, and the dwelling of your tent will certainly not fail.

**5:25** And you will know that your offspring is numerous, and your children will be like all the vegetation of the field.

**5:26** And you will come to the grave like ripe grain reaped in season, or like a heap on a threshing floor gathered in at the right time.

**5:27** Look, we have searched out these things in this way; these are the things we have heard; and as for you, know for yourself if you have done anything."

## Translator Notes

- **5:4** — Greek kolabristheiesan is a rare verb denoting mocking or humiliating treatment, and the Greek specifies "at the doors of inferiors" rather than the Hebrew "in the gate."
- **5:5** — Greek eksiphonistheie is a rare verb meaning literally "may it be siphoned out" or "drained dry."
- **5:7** — The Greek renders the second clause with "the young of a vulture" (neossoi gypos) rather than the Hebrew "sparks" (lit. "sons of Resheph"), just as v. 6 reads "from the mountains."
- **5:24** — Greek diaita can mean "dwelling" (as in v. 3) or "way of life," and hamarte literally means "miss the mark," "fail," or "sin."
- **5:27** — Unlike the shorter Hebrew ending ("know it for yourself"), the Greek adds the pointed accusation "if you have done anything" (ei ti epraxas); similarly, v. 23 lacks the Hebrew clause about a covenant with the stones of the field.
