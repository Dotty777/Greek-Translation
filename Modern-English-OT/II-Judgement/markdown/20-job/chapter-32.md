## Text

**32:1** Now his three friends also ceased speaking against Job any longer, for Job was righteous before them.

**32:2** Then Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite, from the family of Ram, of the land of Uz, became angry; he became very angry with Job because he declared himself righteous before the Lord.

**32:3** And he also became very angry against the three friends, because they were not able to give opposing answers to Job, and they considered him to be ungodly.

**32:4** Now Elihu waited to give an answer to Job, because they were older than he in days.

**32:5** And Elihu saw that there was no answer in the mouth of the three men, and he became furious in his anger.

**32:6** Then Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite responded and said, "I am younger in time, while you are older; therefore I kept silent, fearing to declare to you my own knowledge.

**32:7** And I said, 'It is not time that speaks, and in many years they do not know wisdom;

**32:8** but there is a spirit in mortals, and it is the breath of the Almighty that teaches;

**32:9** it is not the long-lived who are wise, nor do the aged know judgment.'

**32:10** Therefore I said, 'Listen to me, and I will declare to you what I know.'

**32:11** Give ear to my words while you listen, until you examine arguments;

**32:12** and even to you I will pay attention. And look, there was no one refuting Job, answering back his words, from among you,

**32:13** so that you may not say, 'We have found wisdom by joining ourselves to the Lord';

**32:14** yet you permitted a man to speak such words.

**32:15** They were dismayed; they did not answer any longer; they made arguments grow old away from them.

**32:16** I waited, for I did not speak, because they stood still; they did not answer."

**32:17** Then Elihu responds and says:

**32:18** "I will speak again, for I am full of words; for the spirit of my belly is destroying me;

**32:19** and my belly is like a skin of new wine, fermenting and tied shut, or like a coppersmith's bellows that has burst.

**32:20** I will speak so that I may find relief, opening my lips;

**32:21** for I will certainly not feel shame before a man, nor indeed will I defer to a mortal;

**32:22** for I do not know how to admire persons; otherwise, moths will eat me too."

## Translator Notes

- **32:1** — The Greek reads 'his three friends' and 'righteous before them,' whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'these three men' and 'righteous in his own eyes.'
- **32:2** — The Greek adds 'of the land of Uz' (literally 'Ausitis') and reads 'before the Lord' where the Hebrew has 'rather than God.'
- **32:7** — Unlike the Hebrew ('Days should speak, and multitude of years should teach wisdom'), the Greek negates both clauses so that Elihu explicitly rejects age alone as the source of wisdom.
- **32:17** — Instead of continuing Elihu's direct speech as in the Hebrew, the Greek inserts a brief narrative formula introducing the next section of his discourse.
- **32:22** — The Greek literally reads 'admire persons' (an idiom for showing partiality) and concludes with 'moths will eat me too,' where the Hebrew has 'my Maker would soon take me away.'
