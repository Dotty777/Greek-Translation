## Text

**1:1** There was a certain man in the land of Uz whose name was Job; and that man was truthful, blameless, righteous, God-fearing, keeping away from every evil thing.

**1:2** And seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

**1:3** And his livestock was seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yokes of oxen, and five hundred grazing female donkeys. And he had a very large body of servants and great works on the earth; and that man was noble among those from the rising of the sun.

**1:4** And his sons, coming together to one another, would hold a banquet each day, bringing along their three sisters at the same time to eat and drink with them.

**1:5** And whenever the days of the banquet were completed, Job would send and purify them, rising up in the morning, and would offer a sacrifice on their behalf according to their number and one calf for sin on behalf of their souls; for Job would say, "Perhaps my sons have thought evil things in their mind toward God." So then Job would do this all the days.

**1:6** And when this day came, look, the angels of God came to stand before the Lord, and the devil came with them.

**1:7** And the Lord said to the devil, "Where have you come from?" And the devil answered the Lord and said, "Having gone around the earth and walked about what is under heaven, I am here."

**1:8** And the Lord said to him, "Have you given attention in your mind to My servant Job? For there is no one like him among those on the earth, a blameless, truthful, God-fearing man, keeping away from every evil thing."

**1:9** And the devil answered and said before the Lord, "Does Job revere the Lord for nothing?

**1:10** Have You not fenced around what is outside him and what is inside his house and what is outside all that belongs to him all around? You have blessed the works of his hands, and You have made his livestock numerous on the earth.

**1:11** But stretch out Your hand and touch all that he has; surely he will bless You to Your face."

**1:12** Then the Lord said to the devil, "Look, all that belongs to him I give into your hand, but do not touch him." And the devil went out from the presence of the Lord.

**1:13** And it happened on a day like this that the sons of Job and his daughters were drinking wine in the house of their oldest brother.

**1:14** And look, a messenger came to Job and said to him, "The yokes of oxen were plowing, and the female donkeys were grazing beside them,

**1:15** and the captors came and took them captive, and killed the servants with swords; and having been saved, I alone came to report to you."

**1:16** While this one was still speaking, another messenger came and said to Job, "Fire fell from heaven and burned up the sheep, and devoured the shepherds likewise; and having been saved, I alone came to report to you."

**1:17** While this one was still speaking, another messenger came and said to Job, "The horsemen formed three divisions against us, and surrounded the camels and took them captive, and killed the servants with swords; and I alone was saved and came to report to you."

**1:18** While this one was still speaking, another messenger came, saying to Job, "While your sons and your daughters were eating and drinking with their oldest brother,

**1:19** suddenly a great wind came in from the wilderness and touched the four corners of the house; and the house fell upon your children, and they died; and I alone was saved and came to report to you."

**1:20** So Job rose up and tore his garments and shaved the hair of his head, and falling to the ground, he worshiped

**1:21** and said, "I myself came out naked from my mother's womb, and naked I will depart there. The Lord gave, the Lord took away; as it seemed good to the Lord, so it came to be; may the name of the Lord be blessed."

**1:22** In all these things that happened to him, Job did not sin at all before the Lord, and he did not ascribe folly to God.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Greek reads "in the land of Ausitis," the Septuagint geographical form corresponding to Hebrew Uz.
- **1:6** — Greek "the angels of God" corresponds to Hebrew "sons of God," and "the devil" (ho diabolos, literally "the slanderer") renders Hebrew ha-satan ("the adversary").
- **1:11** — Greek "he will bless You" preserves the traditional euphemism for cursing God (so also in 2:5).
- **1:15** — Greek "the captors" here and "the horsemen" in 1:17 are distinctive Septuagint readings where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "the Sabeans" and "the Chaldeans."
- **1:21** — The clause "as it seemed good to the Lord, so it came to be" is a Septuagint reading not found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text (compare also the explicit sin-offering calf added in 1:5).
