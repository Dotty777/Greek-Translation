## Text

**27:1** And continuing further, Job spoke in his prelude:

**27:2** "As God lives, who has judged me in this way, and the Almighty, who has made my soul bitter,

**27:3** surely, while my breath is still in me and the divine Spirit remains in my nostrils,

**27:4** my lips will not speak lawless things, nor will my soul meditate on unjust things.

**27:5** Far be it from me to declare you righteous until I die, for I will not relinquish my innocence;

**27:6** but holding fast to righteousness, I will by no means let go, for I am not conscious in myself of having done wrong things.

**27:7** Nevertheless, may my enemies be like the overthrow of the ungodly, and those who rise up against me like the destruction of lawbreakers.

**27:8** For what hope indeed is there for an ungodly man, that he holds on? Trusting in the Lord, will he then be saved?

**27:9** Or will God hear his plea? Or when distress comes upon him,

**27:10** does he have any boldness before Him? Or as he calls out, will He hear him?

**27:11** But now, I will declare to you what is in the hand of the Lord; I will not lie about the things that are with the Almighty.

**27:12** Look, you all know that you heap empty things upon empty things.

**27:13** This is the portion of an ungodly man from the Lord, and the possession of tyrants will come from the Almighty upon them.

**27:14** And if their sons become many, they will be for slaughter; and even if they grow to manhood, they will beg;

**27:15** and no one will have mercy on their widows.

**27:16** If he gathers silver like earth, and prepares gold equal to clay,

**27:17** the righteous will acquire all these things, and the truthful will possess his wealth.

**27:18** And his house turned out like moths and like a spider.

**27:19** A rich man lay down to sleep and will not do so again; he opened his eyes, and he is no more.

**27:20** Pains met him like water, and by night gloom stole him away.

**27:21** And a scorching wind will take him up, and he will go away, and it will winnow him out of his place.

**27:22** And it will fling him down and will not spare; from its hand he will surely flee.

**27:23** They will clap their hands at him and hiss him out of his place."

## Translator Notes

- **27:5** — Because the Greek places gar ("for") after "until I die," that temporal clause concludes the first statement rather than introducing the second as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **27:8** — The Septuagint reads "that he holds on? Trusting in the Lord, will he then be saved?", diverging markedly from the Hebrew ("when God takes away his soul").
- **27:15** — The Greek lacks the opening line found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("his survivors will be buried by pestilence") and reads "no one will have mercy on their widows" rather than "their widows will not weep."
- **27:22** — The grammatical subject continues from the scorching wind of 27:21, with cheiros autou literally "its hand" (idiomatically "its grasp"), though some take God as the implied subject.
- **27:23** — The Greek uses impersonal singular verbs (krotesei, syriei) alongside the plural pronoun auton ("their hands"), rendered with impersonal "they" for clarity in English.
