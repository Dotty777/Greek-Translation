## Text

**17:1** "I am perishing, carried away by a wind, and I beg for burial and do not obtain it.

**17:2** I plead while weary, and having done what?

**17:3** And strangers have stolen my possessions; who is this? Let him be bound to my hand.

**17:4** Because You have hidden their heart from understanding, therefore You will certainly not exalt them.

**17:5** For the portion he will report evils, and eyes have melted away over sons.

**17:6** Yet You have made me a byword among nations, and I have become a laughingstock to them.

**17:7** For my eyes have grown dim from anger; I have been greatly besieged by all.

**17:8** Astonishment seized the truthful at this, and may a righteous one rise up against a lawless one;

**17:9** and may a faithful one hold to his own way, and may one with clean hands take up courage.

**17:10** However, press on, all of you, and come now, for I do not find anything true among you.

**17:11** My days have passed by in a roar, and the joints of my heart have been torn apart.

**17:12** I have turned night into day; light is near in the face of darkness.

**17:13** For if I wait, Hades is my house, and in gloom my bed has been spread.

**17:14** I have called upon death to be my father, and decay to be my mother and sister.

**17:15** Where then is my hope any longer? Or will I see my good things?

**17:16** Or will they go down with me to Hades, or will we go down together upon dust?"

## Translator Notes

- **17:1** — Greek πνεύματι φερόμενος means 'carried away by a wind' (or 'breath'/'spirit'), reflecting the Septuagint's distinctive poetic phrasing.
- **17:5** — An obscure Septuagint rendering: 'for the portion he will report evils' (τῇ μερίδι ἀναγγελεῖ κακίας), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's 'denounces friends for a share.'
- **17:12** — The Greek has first-person singular ἔθηκα ('I have turned'), unlike the third-person plural ('they turn') in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
