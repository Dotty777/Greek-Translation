## Text

**3:1** After this Job opened his mouth

**3:2** and cursed his day, saying,

**3:3** "May the day perish on which I was born, and that night in which they said, 'Look, a male!'

**3:4** May that night be darkness, and may the Lord not seek after it from above, nor may brightness come into it;

**3:5** and may darkness and the shadow of death seize it; may gloom come upon it.

**3:6** May the day and that night be cursed; may darkness carry it away; may it not be among the days of a year, nor may it be numbered among the days of months.

**3:7** But may that night be pain, and may neither gladness nor joy come upon it;

**3:8** but may the one who curses that day curse it, the one who is about to subdue the great sea monster.

**3:9** May the stars of that night be darkened; may it wait and not come to light, and may it not see the morning star rising,

**3:10** because it did not shut the gates of my mother's womb; for it would have removed trouble from my eyes.

**3:11** For why did I not die in the womb? And did I come out from the womb and not immediately perish?

**3:12** And why did the knees meet me? And why did I nurse at breasts?

**3:13** Now, having lain down, I would have been quiet, and having fallen asleep, I would have rested

**3:14** with kings, counselors of the earth, who gloried in swords,

**3:15** or with rulers whose gold was abundant, who filled their houses with silver,

**3:16** or like a stillborn child coming out from a mother's womb, or like infants who did not see light.

**3:17** There the ungodly have kindled the fury of wrath; there those weary in body have rested;

**3:18** and together the eternal ones have not heard the voice of a tax collector.

**3:19** Small and great are there, and a servant who fears his master.

**3:20** For why has light been given to those in bitterness, and life to souls in pain,

**3:21** who long for death and do not obtain it, digging for it like treasures?

**3:22** And they become overjoyed if they do obtain it.

**3:23** Death is rest for a man, for God has shut him in.

**3:24** For before my food, groaning comes, and I weep, gripped by fear.

**3:25** For the fear that I worried about has come to me, and what I feared has met me.

**3:26** I was neither at peace, nor was I quiet, nor did I rest, yet wrath came to me."

## Translator Notes

- **3:8** — Greek "the great sea monster" (to mega ketos) renders Hebrew Leviathan, and "to subdue" contrasts with the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "to rouse up."
- **3:14** — Greek "who gloried in swords" reflects a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonantal text behind the Masoretic reading "who rebuilt ruins."
- **3:17** — Greek "have kindled the fury of wrath" differs sharply from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "cease from troubling."
- **3:18** — Greek "the eternal ones" (hoi aionioi, the dead of ages past) and "tax collector" stand where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "prisoners" and "taskmaster"; in 3:19 Greek likewise reads "a servant who fears his master" rather than "free from his master."
- **3:23** — The Septuagint reads "Death is rest for a man" in place of the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "to a man whose way is hidden."
