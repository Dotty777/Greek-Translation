## Text

**39:1** "Do you know the time of birth of goat-stags of the rock, and have you watched the birth pangs of deer?

**39:2** And have you counted their months full for birth, and have you loosed their birth pangs?

**39:3** And have you raised their little ones apart from fear? Will you send away their birth pangs?

**39:4** Their offspring will break away, they will multiply in produce; they will go out, and they will by no means return to them.

**39:5** And who is the one who let a wild donkey go free, and who loosed its bonds?

**39:6** And I made its habitation a wilderness, and its dwellings a salt land.

**39:7** Laughing at the throng of a city, and not hearing the reproach of a tax collector,

**39:8** it will survey mountains, its pasture, and it seeks after every green thing.

**39:9** And will a unicorn be willing to serve you, or to sleep at your manger?

**39:10** And will you bind its yoke with straps, or will it draw your furrows in a plain?

**39:11** And do you trust in it because its strength is great, and will you leave your works to it?

**39:12** And will you believe that it will return the seed to you, and bring in your threshing floor?

**39:13** A wing of those who delight, if hasida and nessa conceive;

**39:14** for she will leave her eggs on the ground, and warm them on the dust;

**39:15** and they forgot that a foot will scatter them, and wild beasts of the field will trample them.

**39:16** She treated her own children harshly, so as not herself; in vain she labored without fear.

**39:17** For God kept wisdom silent from her, and did not share with her in understanding.

**39:18** At the right time she will rise on high; she will laugh at a horse and its rider.

**39:19** Or did you place strength upon a horse, and clothe its neck with fear?

**39:20** And did you array it with full armor, and the glory of its chest with boldness?

**39:21** Digging in a plain, it exults, and it goes forth into a plain in strength;

**39:22** Meeting a king, it laughs, and it will by no means turn back from iron.

**39:23** Upon it a bow and a sword exult,

**39:24** and in anger it will make the ground vanish; and it will by no means believe until a trumpet sounds.

**39:25** And when a trumpet sounds, it says, 'Aha!' And from a distance it scents war with a leap and a shout.

**39:26** And by your knowledge does a hawk stand, having spread out its wings motionless, looking down on what lies to the south?

**39:27** And at your command does an eagle rise high, and does a vulture, sitting upon its nest, lodge

**39:28** upon a crag of rock and in a hidden place?

**39:29** Being there, it seeks food; from a distance its eyes keep watch;

**39:30** and its young wallow in blood, and wherever the dead are, immediately they are found."

## Translator Notes

- **39:1** — "Goat-stags" literally translates Greek tragelaphon (a wild mountain goat or ibex), preserving the Greek wordplay with elaphon ("deer") in the parallel clause.
- **39:9** — "Unicorn" literally translates Greek monokeros ("one-horned animal"), the Septuagint's regular rendering of the Hebrew wild ox (re'em).
- **39:13** — Part of the passage (vv. 13-18) supplied in Greek Job from Theodotion, this elliptical verse leaves the Hebrew nouns hasida ("stork") and nessa ("plumage") untransliterated and renders the Hebrew interrogative particle mechanically as "if" (ean).
- **39:16** — The abrupt phrase "so as not herself" literally translates Greek hoste me heauten, a cryptic Greek counterpart to the Hebrew "as though not hers."
