## Text

**22:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite replied and said:

**22:2** "Is it not the Lord who teaches understanding and knowledge?

**22:3** For what does it matter to the Lord if you were blameless in your works? Or is it a benefit that you should reject your way?

**22:4** Or taking account of you, will He reprove you and enter with you into judgment?

**22:5** Is not your wickedness great, and are not your sins innumerable?

**22:6** For you kept taking pledges from your brothers for nothing, and you took away the clothing of the naked.

**22:7** You did not even give the thirsty water to drink, but you deprived the hungry of a morsel.

**22:8** And you showed partiality to some, and you settled those on the earth.

**22:9** And you sent widows away empty, and you afflicted orphans.

**22:10** Therefore snares surrounded you, and extraordinary war dismayed you.

**22:11** The light turned into darkness for you, and when you lay down to sleep, water covered you.

**22:12** Does not He who dwells in the heights observe, and has He not humbled those carried along by arrogance?

**22:13** And you said, 'What does the Mighty One know, or does He judge through the gloom?

**22:14** A cloud of concealment is His, and He will not be seen, and He passes along the circle of heaven.'

**22:15** Will you keep the ancient path that righteous men trod,

**22:16** who were seized before their time? Their foundations are an overflowing river.

**22:17** They are those who say, 'What will the Lord do to us? Or what will the Almighty bring upon us?'

**22:18** Yet He filled their houses with good things, and the counsel of the ungodly is far from Him.

**22:19** Seeing this, the righteous laughed, and the blameless mocked:

**22:20** Surely their substance has been wiped out, and fire will devour their remnant.

**22:21** Be firm now, if you endure; then your fruit will be in good things.

**22:22** And bring out a declaration from His mouth, and take up His words in your heart.

**22:23** And if you turn back and humble yourself before the Lord, you have put what is unjust far from your dwelling;

**22:24** you will place yourself upon a mound in a rock, and as a rock of a torrent of Sophir.

**22:25** Therefore the Almighty will be your helper from enemies, and He will restore you pure like silver refined by fire.

**22:26** Then you will have boldness before the Lord, looking up to heaven cheerfully.

**22:27** And when you pray to Him, He will listen to you, and He will grant you to pay your vows.

**22:28** And He will restore to you a dwelling of righteousness, and upon your ways there will be radiance.

**22:29** For you humbled yourself, and you will say, 'He acted arrogantly,' and He will save the one downcast in his eyes.

**22:30** He will rescue an innocent person, and be saved by your pure hands."

## Translator Notes

- **22:3** — The Greek text reads aposes ("reject" or "push away," attested in Vaticanus and Sinaiticus); some manuscripts and modern editions read haploses ("make straight" or "plain").
- **22:15** — The Greek text reads andres dikaioi ("righteous men," as in Vaticanus and Sinaiticus), whereas other Greek witnesses read adikoi ("unrighteous men").
- **22:20** — Greek ei me (literally "if not") here represents an emphatic affirmative assertion ("surely") rather than a negative condition.
- **22:22** — The Greek text reads ekbale ("bring out" or "draw forth," attested in Vaticanus, Sinaiticus, and Alexandrinus), where some editions emend to eklabe ("receive").
- **22:24** — Greek "Sophir" is the Septuagint proper name corresponding to Hebrew "Ophir." The Greek renders the verse with "in a rock" and "as a rock" where the Hebrew refers to gold of Ophir.
