## Text

**23:1** Then Job answered and said:

**23:2** "Indeed, I know that reproof is out of my hand, and His hand has become heavy upon my groaning.

**23:3** And who then would know that I might find Him and come to the end?

**23:4** And I would state my own case, and He would fill my mouth with reproofs;

**23:5** and I would know the remedies that He will speak to me, and I would perceive what things He will declare to me.

**23:6** And in great strength He will come upon me; then He will not treat me with a threat.

**23:7** For truth and reproof are from Him, and may He bring forth my case to the end.

**23:8** For I will go to the first things, and I am no longer; and of the things at the last, what do I know?

**23:9** When He worked on the left, I did not lay hold; He will turn around on the right, and I will not see.

**23:10** For He already knows my way, and He has tested me like gold.

**23:11** And I will go forth in His commandments, for I kept His ways and will by no means turn aside; from His commandments

**23:12** indeed I will by no means depart, and in my bosom I have hidden His words.

**23:13** And if indeed He Himself judged in this way, who is the one to contradict Him? For what He Himself willed, He also did.

**23:15** Because of this I have been dismayed at Him; and being admonished, I took thought of Him. On this account I will be greatly dismayed before His presence; I will consider, and I will be terrified of Him.

**23:16** And the Lord has weakened my heart, and the Almighty has dismayed me.

**23:17** For I did not know that darkness would come upon me, and before my face gloom covered me."

## Translator Notes

- **23:2** — Literally "out of my hand," an idiomatic Septuagint expression conveying that reproof is beyond Job's reach or control.
- **23:4** — The Greek verb emplesai is a third-person singular aorist optative ("He would fill"), though some manuscripts and editors read or interpret it as first-person ("I would fill").
- **23:5** — The Greek text reads iamata ("remedies" or "healings," attested in Vaticanus and Sinaiticus); other manuscripts and editions read rhemata ("words").
- **23:15** — Verse 14 of the Hebrew numbering is absent from the Greek text, while verse 15 preserves a double translation combining the Old Greek with a later Hexaplaric addition. Greek spoudazo and kataspoudazo here carry the Septuagint sense "dismay" or "alarm."
