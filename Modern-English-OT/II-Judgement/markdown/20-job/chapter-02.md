## Text

**2:1** Now it happened on a day like this that the angels of God came to stand before the Lord, and the devil came in their midst to stand before the Lord.

**2:2** And the Lord said to the devil, "Where are you coming from?" Then the devil said before the Lord, "Having passed through what is under heaven and walked about the whole of it, I am here."

**2:3** And the Lord said to the devil, "Have you then given attention to My servant Job? For there is no one like him among those on the earth, an innocent, truthful, blameless, God-fearing man, keeping away from every evil. And he still holds fast to innocence; yet you said to destroy his possessions without cause."

**2:4** And the devil replied and said to the Lord, "Skin for skin; all that a man has he will pay out for his life.

**2:5** Nevertheless, stretch out Your hand and touch his bones and his flesh; surely he will bless You to Your face."

**2:6** And the Lord said to the devil, "Look, I hand him over to you; only preserve his life."

**2:7** And the devil went out from the Lord, and he struck Job with a malignant sore from his feet to his head.

**2:8** And he took a potsherd to scrape away the discharge, and he was sitting on the dung heap outside the city.

**2:9** And when much time had passed, his wife said to him, "How long will you endure, saying, 'Look, I will wait a little time longer, awaiting the hope of my deliverance'? For look, your memorial has been wiped out from the earth, sons and daughters, the birth pangs and labors of my womb, for whom I toiled in vain with hardships. And you yourself sit in the decay of worms, spending the night in the open air; and I wander about and serve for hire, from place to place and from house to house, awaiting the sun, when it will set, so that I may rest from my hardships and the pains that now grip me. But speak some word against the Lord, and die."

**2:10** And looking intently, he said to her, "You have spoken like one of the foolish women; if we received the good things from the hand of the Lord, will we not bear the evil things?" In all these things that happened to him, Job did not sin at all with his lips before God.

**2:11** Now when his three friends heard of all the evil things that had come upon him, they came to him, each from his own land: Eliphaz, king of the Temanites, Bildad, ruler of the Shuhites, and Zophar, king of the Minaeans; and they came to him with one accord to comfort and visit him.

**2:12** And seeing him from a distance, they did not recognize him, and crying out with a loud voice, they wept, each tearing his own robe, and sprinkling earth on themselves.

**2:13** They sat beside him seven days and seven nights, and none of them spoke; for they saw that the affliction was dreadful and very great.

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — Greek reads "yet you said to destroy his possessions without cause," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "though you incited Me against him to destroy him without cause."
- **2:8** — Greek specifies scraping away "the discharge" (ichor) and places Job "on the dung heap outside the city," where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "among the ashes."
- **2:9** — The Septuagint preserves a substantially longer lament by Job's wife than the Hebrew Masoretic Text, concluding with "speak some word against the Lord, and die."
- **2:11** — The Septuagint gives royal titles to Job's friends (calling Bildad tyrannos, "ruler") and uniquely identifies Zophar as king of the Minaeans rather than the Hebrew Naamathite.
