## Text

**41:1** "Have you not even marveled at the things being said?

**41:2** Do you not even fear because it has been prepared for Me? For who is the one who has stood against Me?

**41:3** Or who will stand against Me and endure? If all that is under heaven is Mine,

**41:4** I will not be silent because of it, and concerning a word of power, it will have mercy on its equal.

**41:5** Who will uncover the face of its garment? And who could enter into the fold of its breastplate?

**41:6** Who will open the gates of its face? Around its teeth is terror.

**41:7** Its inward parts are bronze shields; its bond is like an emery stone.

**41:8** One clings to another, and air will by no means pass through it.

**41:9** Each one will cling to his brother; they hold together, and they will by no means be torn apart.

**41:10** In its sneezing, light flashes forth, and its eyes are the appearance of the morning star.

**41:11** Out of its mouth go burning torches, and braziers of fire are flung about.

**41:12** Out of its nostrils goes the smoke of a furnace burning with a fire of coals.

**41:13** Its soul is coals, and a flame goes out of its mouth.

**41:14** And power lodges in its neck; destruction runs before it.

**41:15** And the fleshy parts of its body cling together. One pours down upon it; it will not be shaken.

**41:16** Its heart is hardened like a stone, and it stands like an unyielding anvil.

**41:17** And when it turns, there is terror for four-footed wild beasts leaping on the earth.

**41:18** If lances meet it, they will accomplish nothing: spear and breastplate.

**41:19** For it regards iron as chaff, and bronze like rotten wood.

**41:20** A bronze bow will by no means wound it; it regards a catapult as grass,

**41:21** hammers are counted like stubble, and it laughs at the shaking of a fire-bearer.

**41:22** Its bed is sharp spikes, and all the gold of the sea under it is like untold mud.

**41:23** It makes the abyss boil like a bronze cauldron; and it regards the sea like an ointment pot,

**41:24** and the Tartarus of the abyss like a captive; it counts the abyss as a walkway.

**41:25** There is nothing on the earth like it, made to be mocked by My angels.

**41:26** It sees everything lofty, and it itself is king of all those in the waters."

## Translator Notes

- **41:2** — Greek "prepared for Me" (ἡτοίμασταί μοι) may also be understood as "prepared by Me."
- **41:4** — The second clause contains two accusatives in Greek (logon dynameos and ton ison autou) and is syntactically difficult; "concerning a word of power" renders the first phrase as an accusative of respect.
- **41:18** — The nouns "spear and breastplate" stand abruptly in the accusative case (dory kai thoraka) at the end of the clause, listing additional weapons that fail against the dragon.
- **41:21** — Greek sphyra here denotes hammers or mallets (a rare neuter plural form), and seismou pyrphorou literally means "shaking of a fire-bearer" (or "a fire-bearing earthquake").
- **41:24** — As in 40:20, the Septuagint uses Greek tartaros ("Tartarus") alongside abyssos ("abyss") to depict the deepest realm of the sea.
