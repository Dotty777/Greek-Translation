## Text

**35:1** Then Elihu answered and said,

**35:2** "What is this you have considered in judgment? Who are you that you said, 'I am righteous before the Lord'?

**35:4** I will give an answer to you and to your three friends.

**35:5** Look up to heaven and see, and observe the clouds, how high they are above you.

**35:6** If you have sinned, what will you do? And even if you have committed many lawless deeds, what can you do?

**35:7** And since, then, you are righteous, what will you give to Him, or what will He receive from your hand?

**35:8** Your ungodliness is for a man like yourself, and your righteousness is for a son of man.

**35:9** Because of a multitude, those who are oppressed will cry out; they will shout because of the arm of many.

**35:10** And no one said, 'Where is God who made me, who appoints night watches,

**35:11** who distinguishes me from the four-footed animals of the earth, and from the birds of heaven?'

**35:12** There they will cry out, and He will by no means hear, also because of the arrogance of the wicked.

**35:13** For the Lord does not wish to see wrong things; for the Almighty Himself is an observer

**35:14** of those who carry out lawless deeds, and He will save me. Now be judged before Him, if you can praise Him as He is.

**35:15** And now, because He is not visiting His wrath, and He has not taken much notice of any trespass,

**35:16** Job also opens his mouth in vain; in ignorance he makes words heavy."

## Translator Notes

- **35:2** — The Greek reads "Who are you that you said, 'I am righteous before the Lord'?" rather than the Hebrew "My righteousness is more than God's," and verse 3 is absent from the Greek text.
- **35:10** — The Greek reads "who appoints night watches" (phylakas nykterinas) where the Hebrew has "who gives songs in the night," and verse 11 links both phrases to the single participle "who distinguishes me."
- **35:14** — The Greek diverges substantially from the Hebrew here, affirming "and He will save me" and challenging Job to "be judged before Him, if you can praise Him as He is."
- **35:16** — The Greek verb barynei literally means "makes heavy," reflecting a different Hebrew root than the Masoretic "multiplies."
