## Text

**4:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:

**4:2** "Have you often been spoken to in trouble? Yet who will endure the strength of your words?

**4:3** For if you admonished many, and encouraged the hands of the weak,

**4:4** and raised up with words those who were weak, and placed courage around powerless knees;

**4:5** but now pain has come upon you and touched you; you have become agitated.

**4:6** Is not your fear in folly, and your hope, and the wickedness of your way?

**4:7** Remember, then: who, being pure, perished, or when did true ones perish root and all?

**4:8** Just as I saw those who plow wrongful things, those who sow them will reap sorrows for themselves.

**4:9** By the command of the Lord they will perish, and by the breath of His anger they will be wiped out.

**4:10** The might of a lion, and the voice of a lioness, and the exultation of dragons were extinguished.

**4:11** An ant-lion perished because it had no food, and lion cubs left one another.

**4:12** But if any true word had been in your speeches, none of these evils would have met you. Will not my ear receive extraordinary things from Him?

**4:13** Now fear and a night echo, fear falling upon people,

**4:14** shuddering and trembling met me and greatly shook my bones,

**4:15** and a spirit came upon my face, and my hair and flesh shuddered.

**4:16** I stood up, and did not recognize it; I looked, and there was no form before my eyes, except that I heard a breeze and a voice:

**4:17** 'What then? Will a mortal be pure before the Lord? Or will a man be blameless from his works?'

**4:18** If He does not put trust in His servants, and against His angels He detected something crooked,

**4:19** then as for those who dwell in houses of clay, of whom we ourselves also are from the same clay, He struck them like a moth.

**4:20** And from morning until evening they are no more; because they were unable to help themselves, they perished.

**4:21** For He breathed on them and they withered; they perished because they had no wisdom."

## Translator Notes

- **4:6** — The Greek diverges sharply from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, rendering Job's fear as "in folly" and referring to "the wickedness of your way" rather than his confidence and integrity.
- **4:11** — Greek myrmekoleon literally denotes an "ant-lion," a legendary hybrid beast in ancient Greek lore; similarly, v. 10 reads "dragons" (drakonton) where the Hebrew refers to young lions.
- **4:12** — The Greek conditional sentence replaces the Hebrew opening about a stealthy word; "from Him" (par' autou) naturally refers to the Lord (cf. v. 9; 5:9), though some take it to refer to the preceding "word" ("from it").
- **4:17** — The Greek reads "from his works" (apo ton ergon autou) rather than the Hebrew "than his Maker."
- **4:19** — The Greek uniquely shifts to the first-person plural ("we ourselves also are from the same clay"), favoring closing the disembodied voice's quotation at v. 17, though many interpreters extend the voice's speech through v. 21.
