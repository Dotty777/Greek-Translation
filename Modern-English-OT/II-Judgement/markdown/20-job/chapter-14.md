## Text

**14:1** "For a mortal born of a woman is short-lived and full of wrath,

**14:2** or like a flower that has blossomed, he falls away; and he flees like a shadow and will never remain.

**14:3** Have You not taken account even of this one, and made this one enter into judgment before You?

**14:4** For who will be clean from filth? No, not one,

**14:5** even if his life on the earth is one day. And his months are numbered by Him; You appointed him for a time, and he will never pass beyond it.

**14:6** Withdraw from him so that he may rest and take pleasure in life, like a hired worker.

**14:7** For there is hope for a tree; for if it is cut down, it will blossom again, and its shoot will never fail.

**14:8** For if its root grows old in the earth, and in rock dies

**14:9** its stump, from the scent of water it will blossom, and it will produce a harvest like a young plant.

**14:10** But when a man dies, he is gone, and when a mortal falls, he is no more.

**14:11** For in time a sea becomes depleted, and a river, left desolate, dries up.

**14:12** And when a man has fallen asleep, he will never rise until heaven is not sewn together, and they will not be awakened from their sleep.

**14:13** If only You had kept me in Hades, and hidden me until Your wrath ceases, and would appoint a time for me in which You will remember me.

**14:14** For if a man dies, he will live, having completed the days of his life; I will wait until I come into being again.

**14:15** Then You will call, and I will answer You; and do not reject the works of Your hands.

**14:16** Yet You numbered my practices, and not one of my sins will pass You by.

**14:17** And You sealed my iniquities in a pouch, and You marked down if I transgressed in anything unwillingly.

**14:18** And yet a falling mountain will fall apart, and a rock will wear away from its place.

**14:19** Waters wear stones smooth, and waters flood the level places of the soil of the earth; and You destroy the endurance of man.

**14:20** You thrust him away to the end, and he is gone; You set Your face against him and send him away.

**14:21** And when his sons become many, he does not know, and if they become few, he does not understand;

**14:22** only his flesh feels pain, and his soul mourns."

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — The Greek renders the mortal's condition as "full of wrath" (πλήρης ὀργῆς), using the same noun that refers to God's wrath in 14:13, rather than the Hebrew "full of turmoil."
- **14:5** — Continuing 14:4, the Greek adds "even if his life on the earth is one day," underscoring universal human impurity, and then shifts from third person ("by Him") to second person ("You appointed").
- **14:9** — The Greek renders the Hebrew word for "boughs" or "harvest" as "harvest" (θερισμόν), and in 14:8 places the dying stump "in rock" (ἐν πέτρᾳ) rather than "in dust."
- **14:12** — The unusual Greek clause "until heaven is not sewn together" (ἕως ἂν ὁ οὐρανὸς οὐ μὴ συνραφῇ) preserves the negation of the Hebrew "until the heavens are no more" while supplying a verb meaning to stitch or join together.
- **14:14** — Punctuated without an interrogative in this Greek text, the opening clause reads as a statement ("he will live") rather than a question, and "until I come into being again" (ἕως πάλιν γένωμαι) expresses renewal as renewed existence.
