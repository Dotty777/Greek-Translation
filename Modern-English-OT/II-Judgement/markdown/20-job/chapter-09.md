## Text

**9:1** Then Job answered and said:

**9:2** "In truth I know that it is so. For how will a mortal be righteous before the Lord?

**9:3** For if he wishes to enter into judgment with Him, he will certainly not answer Him, so that he may not reply to one word of His out of a thousand.

**9:4** For He is wise in mind, both mighty and great; who has become hard before Him and endured?

**9:5** He who makes mountains old—they do not know—He who overturns them in anger;

**9:6** He who shakes the earth under heaven from its foundations, and its pillars totter;

**9:7** He who speaks to the sun and it does not rise, and seals up the stars,

**9:8** He who stretched out heaven alone, walking as on ground upon the sea;

**9:9** He who makes the Pleiades and Hesperus and Arcturus and the chambers of the South;

**9:10** He who does great and unsearchable things, both glorious and extraordinary things, of which there is no number.

**9:11** If He passes over me, I will certainly not see; and if He passes by me, not even so have I known.

**9:12** If He takes away, who will turn Him back? Or who will say to Him, 'What have You done?'

**9:13** For He Himself has turned away anger; by Him the sea monsters under heaven were bowed down.

**9:14** But if He will heed me, surely He will judge my words.

**9:15** For if I am righteous, He will not listen to me; I will plead for His judgment;

**9:16** and if I call and He does not answer, I do not believe that He has listened to me.

**9:17** Could He crush me with gloom? Yet He has made my bruises many for nothing.

**9:18** For He does not allow me to take a breath, but He has filled me with bitterness.

**9:19** For because He is strong, He prevails; who then will stand against His judgment?

**9:20** For if I am righteous, my mouth will be ungodly; and if I am blameless, I will turn out crooked.

**9:21** For even if I acted ungodly, I do not know in my soul, yet my life is being taken away.

**9:22** Therefore I said, 'Anger destroys the great and the mighty,

**9:23** because the worthless are in a dreadful death, yet the righteous are mocked;

**9:24** for they have been handed over into the hands of an ungodly person.' He covers the faces of its judges; and if it is not He, who is it?

**9:25** Now my life is swifter than a runner; they fled away, and they did not see.

**9:26** Or is there indeed a trace of a way for ships, or of a flying eagle seeking prey?

**9:27** For even if I speak, I will forget while speaking; bowing my face down, I will groan.

**9:28** I am shaken in all my limbs, for I know that You will not leave me innocent.

**9:29** But since I am ungodly, why did I not die?

**9:30** For if I wash myself with snow, and cleanse myself with clean hands,

**9:31** You have thoroughly dipped me in filth, and my robe has detested me.

**9:32** For You are not a man like me against whom I may contend, so that we may come together into judgment.

**9:33** If only there were a mediator for us, both reproving and hearing thoroughly between both of us;

**9:34** let Him remove the rod from me, and let the fear of Him not whirl me about,

**9:35** and I will certainly not be afraid, but I will speak; for I do not know myself to be so."

## Translator Notes

- **9:3** — The unexpressed third-person subjects and pronouns in Greek are ambiguous; the second and third clauses can also be read with God as the subject who refuses to heed the mortal or reply to one of his words.
- **9:9** — Greek preserves the distinctive astronomical names Hesperus (the evening star, where Hebrew has Orion) and Arcturus (where Hebrew has the Bear).
- **9:13** — Unlike the Hebrew ("God will not turn back his anger"), the Greek text lacks a negation ("He Himself has turned away anger") and renders "the helpers of Rahab" as "the sea monsters under heaven" (kete).
- **9:24** — Greek has plural "they have been handed over" (referring to the righteous in 9:23) rather than Hebrew "the earth is given," while retaining the feminine singular pronoun "its" (autes) in "its judges."
- **9:33** — Greek expresses an unattainable wish, "If only there were" (eithe en), rather than the Hebrew negative statement ("there is not").
