## Text

**7:1** "Is not the life of man upon the earth a trial, and his life like that of a daily hired worker?

**7:2** Or like a servant fearing his master and having obtained shade, or like a hired laborer waiting for his wages?

**7:3** So I also have endured empty months, and nights of pain have been given to me.

**7:4** If I lie down, I say, 'When will it be day?' And whenever I rise, again, 'When will it be evening?' And I become full of pains from evening until morning.

**7:5** And my body is defiled in the decay of worms, and I dissolve clods of earth with discharge as I scrape.

**7:6** And my life is swifter than speech, and it has perished in empty hope.

**7:7** Remember therefore that my life is a breath, and my eye will no longer return to see good.

**7:8** The eye of one who sees me will not look upon me; Your eyes are upon me, and I am no more,

**7:9** like a cloud cleared away from the sky. For if a man goes down to Hades, he will never again come up,

**7:10** nor will he ever return to his own house, nor will his place ever recognize him again.

**7:11** Therefore neither will I spare my mouth; I will speak, being in distress; I will open the bitterness of my soul, being hard pressed.

**7:12** Am I a sea or a dragon, that You have stationed a guard over me?

**7:13** I said, 'My bed will comfort me, and I will bring up a word privately to myself on my couch';

**7:14** You frighten me with dreams, and in visions You terrify me.

**7:15** You will separate my soul from my spirit, and my bones from death.

**7:16** For I will not live forever, that I should be patient; depart from me, for my life is empty.

**7:17** For what is man, that You have magnified him? Or that You direct Your mind to him?

**7:18** Or will You make a visitation of him until the morning, and judge him until rest?

**7:19** How long will You not leave me alone nor let me go, until I swallow my saliva in pain?

**7:20** If I have sinned, what will I be able to do, You who know the mind of men? Why have You set me as Your accuser, and am I a burden to You?

**7:21** And why have You not made a forgetting of my lawlessness and a cleansing of my sin? But now I will depart to the earth, and rising early, I am no more."

## Translator Notes

- **7:5** — Greek ichoros denotes the watery fluid or discharge oozing from sores, vividly depicting Job dissolving clods of earth with this discharge as he scrapes himself.
- **7:6** — Greek elaphroteros lalias ('swifter than speech', or 'lighter than speech') reflects a Septuagint reading distinct from the weaver's shuttle imagery of the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **7:15** — The Greek reads a 2nd-person future statement addressed to God ('You will separate my soul from my spirit, and my bones from death'), diverging markedly from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **7:18** — Greek eis anapausin ('until rest', or 'for rest') pairs temporally with 'until the morning', reflecting the sense 'rest' rather than 'moment' as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **7:20** — Greek katenteukten ('accuser') is a rare compound noun meaning one who lodges a complaint against another, and epi soi ('to You') preserves a reading differing from 'to myself' in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
