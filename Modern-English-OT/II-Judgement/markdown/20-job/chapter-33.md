## Text

**33:1** "Nevertheless, hear my words, Job, and give ear to my speech;

**33:2** for look, I have opened my mouth, and my tongue has spoken.

**33:3** My heart is pure in words, and the understanding of my lips will perceive pure things.

**33:4** It is the divine spirit that made me, and the breath of the Almighty that teaches me.

**33:5** If you are able, give me an answer to these things; hold firm, stand opposite me, and I opposite you.

**33:6** From clay you have been formed, just as I also have; from the same we have been formed.

**33:7** Fear of me will not unsettle you, nor will my hand be heavy upon you.

**33:8** Yet you spoke in my ears; I have heard the sound of words;

**33:9** because you say, 'I am pure, not having sinned; I am blameless, for I did not act lawlessly;

**33:10** yet He found a complaint against me, and He has regarded me as an adversary;

**33:11** and He put my foot in the stocks, and He watched all my paths.'

**33:12** For how do you say, 'I am righteous, and He has not listened to me'? For the One who is above mortals is eternal.

**33:13** And you say, 'Why has He not listened to me in any word of my case?'

**33:14** For once the Lord may speak, and a second time,

**33:15** in a dream, or in a night meditation, as when dreadful fear falls upon men, during slumbers upon a bed;

**33:16** then He uncovers the mind of men; with such visions of fear He terrifies them,

**33:17** to turn a man away from unrighteousness; and He rescues his body from a fall.

**33:18** And He spares his soul from death, and spares him from falling in war.

**33:19** Again He reproves him in sickness upon a bed, and the multitude of his bones grows numb;

**33:20** and he will certainly not be able to accept any food of grain, and his soul will desire food,

**33:21** until his flesh decays and he shows his bones empty.

**33:22** And his soul draws near to death; this life of his is in Hades.

**33:23** If there are a thousand death-bringing angels, not one of them will by any means wound him. If he intends in his heart to turn back to the Lord, and declares to a man his own fault, and shows his folly,

**33:24** He will uphold him so that he does not fall into death; and He will renew his body like plaster on a wall, and He will fill his bones with marrow;

**33:25** and He will make his flesh tender like that of an infant, and He will restore him, grown to manhood, among men.

**33:26** And when he has prayed to the Lord, it will be acceptable to Him, and he will enter with a cheerful face, with confession; and He will render righteousness to men.

**33:27** Then at that time a man will reproach himself, saying, 'What sorts of things was I doing? And He did not punish me as what I sinned deserved.

**33:28** Save my soul from coming into corruption, and my life will see light.'

**33:29** Look, the Mighty One will work all these things three ways with a man;

**33:30** but He rescued my soul from death, so that my life may praise Him in light.

**33:31** Give ear, Job, and listen to me; be silent, and it is I who will speak.

**33:32** If there is a word, answer me; speak, for I want you to be justified.

**33:33** If not, you listen to me; be silent, and I will teach you."

## Translator Notes

- **33:12** — The Greek reads 'the One who is above mortals is eternal,' differing from the Hebrew 'God is greater than man.'
- **33:14** — The Greek verb is optative ('may speak') and leads directly into the dream imagery of verse 15 without the Hebrew clause 'and man does not perceive it.'
- **33:20** — Whereas the Hebrew describes the sick man's appetite as loathing choice food, the Greek states that he cannot accept grain food even while his soul desires food.
- **33:23** — The Greek speaks of 'a thousand death-bringing angels' unable to wound the sufferer and his confession of fault to another, diverging sharply from the Hebrew's 'angel, a mediator, one of a thousand.'
- **33:24** — The Greek simile 'like plaster on a wall' reflects a different sense of the Hebrew root for 'ransom' (which can also mean 'coat' or 'cover'), paired here with the renewal of the sufferer's body and bones.
