## Text

**40:1** And the Lord God answered Job and said,

**40:2** "Will anyone turn aside judgment with the Sufficient One? And will one who reproves God answer it?"

**40:3** Then Job, responding, said to the Lord,

**40:4** "Why do I still contend in judgment, being admonished and reproving the Lord, hearing such things when I am nothing? And what answer will I give to these things? I will put my hand over my mouth.

**40:5** I have spoken once, and I will not continue a second time."

**40:6** Then the Lord, responding further, said to Job out of the cloud,

**40:7** "No, rather gird your loins like a man; I will question you, and you answer Me.

**40:8** Do not reject My judgment. And do you suppose that I have dealt with you otherwise than that you might appear righteous?

**40:9** Or do you have an arm against the Lord, or do you thunder with a voice against Him?

**40:10** Now take up loftiness and power, and clothe yourself with glory and honor.

**40:11** And send out angels in wrath, and humble every insolent one,

**40:12** and extinguish the proud one; and make the ungodly rot immediately,

**40:13** and hide them in the earth all together, and fill their faces with dishonor;

**40:14** I will acknowledge that your right hand is able to save.

**40:15** But now, look, wild beasts are beside you; they eat grass just like oxen.

**40:16** Look now, its strength is in its loins, and its power is in the navel of its belly.

**40:17** It sets its tail upright like a cypress, and its sinews are intertwined.

**40:18** Its ribs are bronze ribs, and its backbone is cast iron.

**40:19** This is the beginning of the Lord's creation, made to be mocked by His angels.

**40:20** And going up onto a sheer mountain, it causes joy for four-footed creatures in Tartarus.

**40:21** It sleeps under all kinds of trees, beside papyrus and reed and sedge.

**40:22** And great trees with shoots, and branches of the field, are shaded by it.

**40:23** If a flood happens, it will by no means notice; it is confident that the Jordan will dash into its mouth.

**40:24** Will one take it in its eye? Acting craftily, will one pierce its nose?

**40:25** And will you lead a dragon with a hook, and put a halter around its nose?

**40:26** Will you tie a ring in its nostril, and pierce its lip with a bracelet?

**40:27** And will it speak to you with a plea, softly with supplication?

**40:28** Will it make a covenant with you? And will you take it as a perpetual slave?

**40:29** And will you play with it like a bird, or will you tie it like a sparrow for a child?

**40:30** And do nations feed on it, and do nations of the Phoenicians divide it up?

**40:31** And all sailing vessels coming together will by no means carry one hide of its tail, or its head in fishermen's boats.

**40:32** And one will lay a hand on it, remembering the battle that takes place in its body."

## Translator Notes

- **40:2** — Greek Ikanou ("the Sufficient One") renders the Hebrew divine epithet Shaddai, while "turn aside judgment" reflects the Septuagint's distinct reading of the first clause.
- **40:8** — The Septuagint softens the reproach found in the Hebrew text, framing the Lord's dealing (Greek chrematizo) with Job as intended so that Job might appear righteous.
- **40:15** — The Septuagint translates Hebrew Behemoth literally as plural theria ("wild beasts") with a plural verb ("they eat"), before shifting to singular pronouns in verses 16-24.
- **40:20** — Greek tartaros ("Tartarus") is the classical name for the deepest underworld abyss, used by the Septuagint here and again in 41:24.
- **40:32** — The Greek text reads third-person epithesei ("one will lay"), whereas some witnesses read second-person epitheseis ("you will lay"). In the Septuagint, 40:25-32 introduces the dragon (Greek drakon, rendering Hebrew Leviathan) before chapter 41 begins.
