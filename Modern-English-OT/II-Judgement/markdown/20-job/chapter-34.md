## Text

**34:1** Then Elihu answered and said,

**34:2** "Hear me, you who are wise; you who have knowledge, give ear;

**34:3** for the ear tests words, and the throat tastes food.

**34:4** Let us choose judgment for ourselves; let us know among ourselves what is good.

**34:5** For Job has said, 'I am righteous; the Lord has taken away my judgment,

**34:6** and He has dealt falsely with my judgment; violent is my arrow without injustice.'

**34:7** What man is like Job, drinking mockery like water,

**34:8** not having sinned nor acted ungodly, or having shared a way with those who do lawless deeds, to walk with the ungodly?

**34:9** For do not say, 'There will be no visitation of a man,' when there is visitation for him from the Lord.

**34:10** Therefore, you of understanding heart, hear me: Far be it from me to act ungodly before the Lord, and before the Almighty to disturb what is right;

**34:11** but He repays a person according to what each of them does, and in the path of a man He will find him.

**34:12** Now do you suppose that the Lord will do wrong things? Or will the Almighty disturb judgment,

**34:13** He who made the earth? And who is the one who makes what is under heaven and all the things in it?

**34:14** For if He should wish to hold back, and to retain the spirit with Himself,

**34:15** all flesh will die together, and every mortal will depart to the earth from where he was also formed.

**34:16** But if you are not admonished, hear these things; give ear to the sound of words.

**34:17** You, look at the One who hates lawless deeds and the One who destroys the wicked, who is eternally righteous.

**34:18** Ungodly is the one who says to a king, 'You act lawlessly'; 'Most ungodly one,' to rulers,

**34:19** who is not ashamed before the face of an honored man, nor knows how to grant honor to the great, so that their faces might be admired.

**34:20** And crying out and pleading with a man will turn out in vain for them; for they acted lawlessly, while the powerless were turned aside.

**34:21** For He Himself is an observer of the works of people, and nothing of what they do escapes His notice;

**34:22** nor will there be a place for those who do lawless deeds to be hidden;

**34:23** for He will lay no more upon a man.

**34:24** For the Lord watches over all, the One who comprehends unsearchable things, both glorious and marvelous things, of which there is no number,

**34:25** the One who knows their works; and He will overturn the night, and it will be brought low.

**34:26** And He extinguished the ungodly, and they are visible before Him,

**34:27** because they turned aside from the law of God, and they did not acknowledge His ordinances,

**34:28** so as to bring to Him the cry of the needy, and He will hear the cry of the poor.

**34:29** And He Himself will grant quietness, and who will condemn? And He will hide His face, and who will see Him? Both over a nation and over a man alike,

**34:30** making a hypocritical man king because of the perversity of the people.

**34:31** For there is one who says to the Mighty One, 'I have received; I will not take a pledge;

**34:32** apart from myself I will see; You show me; if I have done injustice, I will by no means add to it.'

**34:33** Will He repay it from you, because you will reject it? For you will choose, and not I; and what you know, speak.

**34:34** Therefore those of understanding heart will say these things, and a wise man has heard my word.

**34:35** But Job did not speak with understanding; his words are not with knowledge.

**34:36** Nevertheless, learn, Job: do not give a reply any longer like the foolish,

**34:37** so that we may not add to our sins; and lawlessness will be counted against us when we speak many words before the Lord."

## Translator Notes

- **34:6** — The Greek verb "has dealt falsely" (epseusato) is third-person singular referring to the Lord (unlike the first-person Hebrew), and "arrow" renders belos literally.
- **34:8** — The Greek distinctively prefixes "not having sinned nor acted ungodly" before "or having shared a way," softening Elihu's accusation against Job compared with the Hebrew text.
- **34:10** — In the Greek text, Elihu declares "Far be it from me" to act ungodly before the Lord, whereas the Hebrew reads "Far be it from God" to do wickedness.
- **34:23** — The Greek clause is elliptical (literally, "for He will not place upon a man any longer," without an expressed object) and lacks the second half of the verse present in the Hebrew.
- **34:30** — The Greek renders the participle positively as "making a hypocritical man king" (rather than negatively as in the Hebrew) and reads "because of the perversity of the people" (dyskolias).
