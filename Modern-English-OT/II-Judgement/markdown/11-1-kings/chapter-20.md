## Text

**20:1** And there was a certain vineyard belonging to Naboth the Israelite beside the threshing floor of Ahab king of Samaria.

**20:2** And Ahab spoke to Naboth, saying, "Give me your vineyard, and it shall be a vegetable garden for me, because this one is nearer to my house, and I will give you another vineyard better than it; or if it is pleasing in your sight, I will give you silver in exchange for this vineyard of yours, and it shall be a vegetable garden for me."

**20:3** And Naboth said to Ahab, "May it never happen to me from my God to give the inheritance of my fathers to you."

**20:4** And the spirit of Ahab became troubled, and he lay down on his bed and covered his face, and he did not eat bread.

**20:5** And Jezebel his wife came in to him and spoke to him, "Why is your spirit troubled, and why are you not eating bread?"

**20:6** And he said to her, "Because I spoke to Naboth the Israelite, saying, 'Give me your vineyard for silver; or if you wish, I will give you another vineyard in its place.' And he said, 'I will not give you the inheritance of my fathers.'"

**20:7** And Jezebel his wife said to him, "Is this how you now act the king over Israel? Arise, eat bread, and come to yourself; I will give you the vineyard of Naboth the Israelite."

**20:8** And she wrote a letter in the name of Ahab and sealed it with his seal, and sent the letter to the elders and the free men who lived with Naboth.

**20:9** And it was written in the letters, saying, "Fast a fast, and seat Naboth at the head of the people;

**20:10** and station two men, sons of lawless men."

**20:11** And the men of his city, the elders and the free men who lived in his city, did just as Jezebel had sent to them, and as it was written in the letters that she had sent to them.

**20:12** And they called a fast, and seated Naboth at the head of the people.

**20:13** And they sat down opposite him and testified against him, saying, "You have blessed God and the king." And they led him outside the city and stoned him with stones, and he died.

**20:14** And they sent to Jezebel, saying, "Naboth has been stoned and is dead."

**20:15** And it happened, when Jezebel heard it, she said to Ahab, "Arise, take possession of the vineyard of Naboth the Israelite, who did not give it to you for silver; for Naboth is not alive, because he is dead."

**20:16** And it happened, when Ahab heard that Naboth the Israelite was dead, he tore his garments and put on sackcloth. And it happened after these things, Ahab arose and went down to the vineyard of Naboth the Israelite to take possession of it.

**20:17** And the Lord spoke to Elijah the Tishbite, saying,

**20:18** "Arise and go down to meet Ahab king of Israel, who is in Samaria; for he is in the vineyard of Naboth, because he has gone down there to take possession of it.

**20:19** And you shall speak to him, saying, 'Thus says the Lord: Since you have murdered and taken possession, therefore thus says the Lord: In every place where the swine and the dogs licked up the blood of Naboth, there the dogs will lick up your blood, and the prostitutes will wash themselves in your blood.'"

**20:20** And Ahab said to Elijah, "Have you found me, my enemy?" And he said, "I have found you, because you have been sold in vain to do what is evil before the Lord, to provoke Him to anger.

**20:21** Look, I am bringing calamities upon you, and I will burn up after you, and I will utterly destroy from Ahab anyone who urinates against a wall, both confined and forsaken in Israel.

**20:22** And I will make your house like the house of Jeroboam son of Nebat and like the house of Baasha son of Ahijah, because of the provocations with which you have provoked anger and have caused Israel to sin.

**20:23** And concerning Jezebel the Lord has spoken, saying, 'The dogs will devour her in the outer wall of Israel.'

**20:24** The one of Ahab who dies in the city, the dogs will eat, and the one of his who dies in the field, the birds of the sky will eat."

**20:25** Yet in vain was Ahab, since he was sold to do what is evil before the Lord, as Jezebel his wife turned him aside;

**20:26** and he became exceedingly abominable in going after the abominations, according to all that the Amorite had done, whom the Lord had utterly destroyed from before the sons of Israel.

**20:27** And because of the word, when Ahab was stricken with remorse before the Lord, he went about weeping, and tore his tunic, and fastened sackcloth upon his body, and fasted; and he put on sackcloth on the day that he struck down Naboth the Israelite, and went on.

**20:28** And the word of the Lord came by the hand of His servant Elijah concerning Ahab, and the Lord said,

**20:29** "Have you seen how Ahab has been stricken with remorse before Me? I will not bring the calamity in his days; and in the days of his son I will bring the calamity."

## Translator Notes

- **20:1** — In the Septuagint, the Naboth narrative stands as chapter 20 (corresponding to Hebrew 1 Kings 21). Throughout this chapter the Greek consistently calls Naboth 'the Israelite' (rather than Hebrew 'the Jezreelite'), places his vineyard beside Ahab's 'threshing floor' (halo, rather than 'palace'), and in v. 23 reads 'the outer wall of Israel' (rather than 'of Jezreel').
- **20:10** — This Greek text ends after 'sons of lawless men,' omitting the rest of Jezebel's written instructions about accusing and stoning Naboth; in v. 13 it uses second-person Heulogekas ('You have blessed,' a traditional euphemism for 'cursed').
- **20:16** — The Greek includes an additional notice that Ahab tore his garments and put on sackcloth upon hearing of Naboth's death before going down to take the vineyard (cf. v. 27).
- **20:19** — Rather than a question, the Greek begins with a causal statement ('Since you have murdered...'), includes 'the swine' alongside the dogs in 'every place,' and adds the prophecy that the prostitutes will wash themselves in Ahab's blood.
- **20:25** — The Greek reads plen mataios Achaab ('Yet in vain was Ahab') where the Hebrew has 'Surely there was none like Ahab,' and in v. 27 describes Ahab as 'stricken with remorse' (katenyge, also v. 29) and putting on sackcloth on the day he struck down Naboth.
