## Text

**13:1** And look, a man of God from Judah came by the word of the Lord to Bethel, and Jeroboam was standing by the altar to offer sacrifice.

**13:2** And he cried out to the altar by the word of the Lord, "Altar, altar, thus says the Lord: Look, a son is born to the house of David, Josiah is his name; and he will sacrifice on you the priests of the high places who offer sacrifice on you, and he will burn human bones on you."

**13:3** And he will give a portent on that day, saying, "This is the word that the Lord has spoken, saying, 'Look, the altar is splitting apart, and the fatness that is on it will be poured out.'"

**13:4** And it happened, when King Jeroboam heard the words of the man of God who had cried out against the altar that was in Bethel, that the king stretched out his hand from the altar, saying, "Seize him!" And look, his hand, which he had stretched out against him, withered, and he was not able to bring it back to himself.

**13:5** And the altar split apart, and the fatness was poured out from the altar according to the portent that the man of God had given by the word of the Lord.

**13:6** And King Jeroboam said to the man of God, "Implore your God, and let my hand return to me." And the man of God implored the face of the Lord, and He brought the king's hand back to him, and it became as before.

**13:7** And the king spoke to the man of God, "Come with me into the house and eat a meal, and I will give you a gift."

**13:8** And the man of God said to the king, "If you give me half of your house, I will not go in with you, nor will I eat bread, nor will I drink water in this place.

**13:9** For thus the Lord commanded me by a word, saying, 'Do not eat bread, and do not drink water, and do not return by the way on which you went.'"

**13:10** And he departed by another way, and did not return by the way on which he had come to Bethel.

**13:11** Now a certain old prophet was living in Bethel; and his sons came and recounted to him all the deeds that the man of God had done during the day in Bethel, the words that he had spoken to the king; and they turned the face of their father.

**13:12** And their father spoke to them, saying, "Which way has he gone?" And his sons showed him the way by which the man of God who had come from Judah went up.

**13:13** And he said to his sons, "Saddle the donkey for me." So they saddled the donkey for him, and he mounted it.

**13:14** And he went after the man of God, and found him sitting under an oak, and said to him, "Are you the man of God who has come from Judah?" And he said to him, "I am."

**13:15** And he said to him, "Come with me and eat bread."

**13:16** And he said, "I cannot return with you, nor will I eat bread, nor will I drink water in this place.

**13:17** For thus the Lord has commanded me by a word, saying, 'Do not eat bread there, and do not drink water, and do not return there by the way on which you went.'"

**13:18** And he said to him, "I also am a prophet just as you are, and an angel has spoken to me by the word of the Lord, saying, 'Bring him back to yourself into your house, and let him eat bread and drink water.'" But he lied to him.

**13:19** And he brought him back, and he ate bread in his house and drank water.

**13:20** And it happened, while they were sitting, that the word of the Lord came to the prophet who had brought him back.

**13:21** And he said to the man of God who had come from Judah, saying, "Thus says the Lord: Because you defied the word of the Lord, and did not keep the commandment that the Lord your God commanded you,

**13:22** and returned, and ate bread and drank water in this place of which He spoke to you, saying, 'You must not eat bread and must not drink water,' your body will certainly not enter the tomb of your fathers."

**13:23** And it happened, after eating bread and drinking water, that he saddled the donkey for him, and he turned back and departed.

**13:24** And a lion found him on the road and put him to death; and his body was thrown on the road, and the donkey was standing beside it, and the lion was standing beside the body.

**13:25** And look, men were passing by, and they saw the corpse thrown on the road, and the lion was standing close beside the corpse; and they entered and spoke in the city where the old prophet was living.

**13:26** And the one who had brought him back from the road heard, and he said, "This is the man of God who defied the word of the Lord."

**13:28** And he went and found his body thrown on the road, and the donkey and the lion were standing beside the body; and the lion had not eaten the body of the man of God, and had not crushed the donkey.

**13:29** And the prophet lifted up the body of the man of God and placed it on the donkey, and brought him back into the city,

**13:30** the prophet, to bury him in his own tomb; and they mourned for him, "Alas, brother!"

**13:31** And it happened, after mourning for him, that he said to his sons, saying, "When I die, bury me in this tomb in which the man of God is buried; lay me beside his bones, so that my bones may be saved with his bones.

**13:32** For the word that he spoke by the word of the Lord against the altar in Bethel and against the high houses that are in Samaria will certainly take place."

**13:33** And after this matter Jeroboam did not turn back from his wickedness, and he turned again and made priests of high places from part of the people; whoever wished, he would fill his hand, and he became a priest for the high places.

**13:34** And this matter resulted in sin for the house of Jeroboam, and in destruction and eradication from the face of the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **13:3** — The Greek reads future 'he will give' (literally rendering a Hebrew waw-perfect) even though v. 5 refers to the portent as already given by the man of God. It also reads 'word' (rather than 'sign') and 'fatness' (literally rendering the Hebrew term for fat-soaked altar ashes).
- **13:6** — The Greek uses an active transitive verb ('brought the king's hand back') with an implicit subject, most naturally the Lord answering the plea (though grammatically it could continue 'the man of God'), where the Hebrew reads intransitively ('the king's hand returned').
- **13:11** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'and they told them to their father,' the Greek reads 'and they turned the face of their father.'
- **13:26** — The Greek text lacks the second half of v. 26 (concerning the Lord handing the prophet over to the lion) and all of v. 27 found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **13:33** — 'Fill his hand' is a literal biblical idiom for consecrating or ordaining someone to the priesthood.
