## Text

**17:1** And Elijah the prophet, the Tishbite from Tishbe of Gilead, said to Ahab, "As the Lord, the God of powers, the God of Israel, lives, before whom I have stood, there will surely be no dew or rain during these years, except by the mouth of my word."

**17:2** And the word of the Lord came to Elijah:

**17:3** "Go from here toward the east, and hide yourself in the wadi of Cherith, which is facing the Jordan.

**17:4** And it will be that from the wadi you will drink water, and I will command the ravens to sustain you there."

**17:5** And Elijah did according to the word of the Lord, and he stayed in the wadi of Cherith facing the Jordan.

**17:6** And the ravens kept bringing him loaves of bread in the morning and meat in the evening, and he drank water from the wadi.

**17:7** And it happened after some days that the wadi dried up, because no rain had come upon the land.

**17:8** And the word of the Lord came to Elijah:

**17:9** "Rise and go to Zarephath of Sidon; look, I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain you."

**17:10** And he rose and went to Zarephath, to the gate of the city; and look, there a widow woman was gathering sticks. And Elijah called out after her and said to her, "Please fetch a little water in a vessel, and I will drink."

**17:11** And she went to fetch it, and Elijah called out after her and said, "Please fetch for me a morsel of the bread that is in your hand."

**17:12** And the woman said, "As the Lord your God lives, I surely do not have a baked cake, except as much as a handful of flour in the jar and a little oil in the flask; and look, I am gathering two small sticks, and I will go in and prepare it for myself and for my children, and we will eat and die."

**17:13** And Elijah said to her, "Take courage, go in and do according to your word. Only make for me from it a small baked cake first, and you will bring it out to me, and for yourself and your children you will make some afterward,

**17:14** for thus says the Lord: 'The jar of flour will not fail and the flask of oil will not diminish until the day that the Lord gives rain upon the land.'"

**17:15** And the woman went and did so; and she and he and her children ate.

**17:16** And the jar of flour did not fail, and the flask of oil was not diminished, according to the word of the Lord that He spoke by the hand of Elijah.

**17:17** And it happened after these things that the son of the woman, the mistress of the house, became ill; and his illness was exceedingly severe, until no breath was left in him.

**17:18** And she said to Elijah, "What do I have to do with you, man of God? Have you come in to me to bring my unrighteousness to remembrance and to put my son to death?"

**17:19** And Elijah said to the woman, "Give me your son." And he took him from her bosom and carried him up into the upper room where he himself was staying, and laid him down on the bed.

**17:20** And Elijah cried out and said, "Alas, Lord, the witness of the widow with whom I dwell, You have done harm by putting her son to death."

**17:21** And he breathed upon the boy three times, and he called upon the Lord and said, "Lord my God, please let the soul of this boy return into him."

**17:22** And it happened so, and the boy cried out.

**17:23** And he brought him down from the upper room into the house and gave him to his mother; and Elijah said, "See, your son lives."

**17:24** And the woman said to Elijah, "Look, I know that you are a man of God, and the word of the Lord in your mouth is true."

## Translator Notes

- **17:1** — The Greek adds "the prophet," reads "from Tishbe of Gilead" (vs. Hebrew MT "of the settlers of Gilead"), and includes "the God of powers" (Greek: ton dynameon, corresponding to Hebrew "of hosts"); the closing phrase literally reads "except through the mouth of my word."
- **17:6** — Unlike the Hebrew MT ("bread and meat in the morning and bread and meat in the evening"), the Greek specifies loaves of bread in the morning and meat in the evening.
- **17:12** — Here and in vv. 13 and 15, the Greek reads the plural "children" where the Hebrew MT has the singular "son."
- **17:20** — The Greek addresses the Lord as "the witness of the widow" (reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew) and states Elijah's lament as a direct declaration rather than a question.
- **17:21** — The Greek reads "breathed upon the boy three times" (vs. Hebrew MT "stretched himself upon the child three times"), and v. 22 gives the shorter Greek report ("And it happened so, and the boy cried out").
