## Text

**13:1** Now in the one hundred and forty-ninth year, word reached Judas and his men that Antiochus Eupator had come with multitudes against Judea,

**13:2** and with him Lysias, the guardian and the one in charge of affairs, each having a Greek force of one hundred and ten thousand foot soldiers, and five thousand three hundred horsemen, and twenty-two elephants, and three hundred scythe-bearing chariots.

**13:3** And Menelaus also joined them, and with great duplicity urged Antiochus on, not for the welfare of his homeland, but thinking that he would be established in office.

**13:4** But the King of kings aroused the anger of Antiochus against the wicked wretch, and when Lysias pointed out that this man was the cause of the evils, indeed of all of them, he ordered them to take him to Beroea and put him to death, as is the custom in that place.

**13:5** Now in that place there is a tower fifty cubits high, full of ashes, and it had a circular contrivance sloping steeply on every side down into the ashes.

**13:6** There everyone pushes forward to destruction anyone guilty of sacrilege, or who has also committed an extreme degree of any other evils.

**13:7** By such a doom it happened that the lawless Menelaus died, not even obtaining burial in the earth;

**13:8** and very justly so: for since he had committed many sins concerning the altar, whose fire and ashes were pure, in ashes he received his death.

**13:9** Now the king was coming, weighed down in his thoughts, intending to inflict on the Jews the worst of the things that had happened in the time of his father.

**13:10** But when Judas learned of these things, he ordered the multitude to call upon the Lord day and night, that if ever at any other time, now also He would come to the aid of those who were about to be deprived of the law, their homeland, and the holy temple,

**13:11** and not to allow the people, who had only just briefly been revived, to fall under the power of the blasphemous nations.

**13:12** When all had done the same together and implored the merciful Lord with weeping, fasting, and prostration for three days without ceasing, Judas encouraged them and commanded them to present themselves.

**13:13** And meeting privately with the elders, he resolved that before the king's army could invade Judea and become masters of the city, they would march out and decide the matter with the help of God.

**13:14** So committing the decision to the Lord of the world, and urging those with him to fight nobly even to death for laws, temple, city, homeland, and commonwealth, he stationed his army around Modein.

**13:15** And giving to those with him the watchword, "Victory of God," he attacked the royal pavilion by night with the finest chosen young men, struck down the camp to about two thousand men, and brought down the foremost of the elephants together with the one in its housing.

**13:16** And finally they filled the camp with terror and confusion, and withdrew successfully.

**13:17** Now this had taken place as day was already beginning to dawn, because of the Lord's protection that came to his aid.

**13:18** Now the king, having had a taste of the boldness of the Jews, made attempts on the positions by stratagems,

**13:19** and against Beth-zur, a small fortress of the Jews. He advanced, was routed, met with a setback, was defeated.

**13:20** And Judas sent in what was needed to those inside.

**13:21** But Rhodocus, from the Jewish ranks, reported the secrets to the enemies; he was sought out and caught and locked up.

**13:22** The king negotiated a second time with those in Beth-zur, gave the right hand, received it, departed, attacked Judas and his men, was worsted,

**13:23** learned that Philip, who had been left in charge of affairs, had lost his senses in Antioch, was dismayed, appealed to the Jews, yielded, and swore to all the just terms, came to a settlement and offered a sacrifice, honored the sanctuary and the place, showed kindness,

**13:24** and welcomed Maccabeus, left Hegemonides as governor from Ptolemais as far as the Genneri,

**13:25** came to Ptolemais. The people of Ptolemais were indignant about the agreements, for they were exceedingly afraid; they wanted to annul the stipulations.

**13:26** Lysias stepped up to the platform, made the best possible defense, persuaded and calmed them, made them favorably disposed, broke camp for Antioch. Thus went the affairs of the king's advance and withdrawal.

## Translator Notes

- **13:9** — The Greek text reads bebaremenos ('weighed down' or 'burdened'), whereas other Greek manuscripts read bebarbaromenos ('barbarous' or 'filled with savage intentions').
- **13:15** — The Greek text reads synetheken (literally 'put together' or 'brought down together'), where other manuscripts read synekentesen ('pierced through'); 'in its housing' refers to the wooden battle tower mounted on the elephant's back.
- **13:19** — The Greek text reads oligon ('small'), whereas other Greek manuscripts read ochyron ('strong' or 'fortified').
- **13:24** — The Greek text reads ton Genneron ('the Genneri'), a manuscript variant for ton Gerrenon ('the Gerrenians').
- **13:25** — The Greek text reads edeiliazon ('were afraid'), whereas other Greek manuscripts read edeinopathoun ('were deeply aggrieved' or 'indignant').
