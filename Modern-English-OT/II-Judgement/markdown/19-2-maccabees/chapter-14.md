## Text

**14:1** Now after a three-year period, word reached Judas and those with him that Demetrius, the son of Seleucus, having sailed in through the harbor at Tripolis with a strong force and a fleet,

**14:2** had taken control of the country, having put to death Antiochus and his guardian Lysias.

**14:3** Now a certain Alcimus, who had formerly been high priest, but had willfully defiled himself in the times of separation, realizing that there was no safety for him in any way at all, nor any longer access to the holy altar,

**14:4** came to King Demetrius about the one hundred and fifty-first year, presenting to him a gold crown and a palm branch, and in addition to these, some of the customary olive branches of the temple. And on that day he kept quiet.

**14:5** But having found an opportunity aiding his own folly, when he was summoned to a council by Demetrius and asked in what disposition and intention the Jews stood, in answer to these things he said,

**14:6** "Those among the Jews who are called Hasideans, whom Judas Maccabeus leads, keep up war and stir up sedition, not allowing the kingdom to attain stability.

**14:7** Therefore, deprived of my ancestral glory, meaning the high priesthood, I have now come a second time,

**14:8** first, genuinely concerned for the things pertaining to the king, and second, also having regard for my own fellow citizens; for by the recklessness of the aforementioned men, our entire nation suffers misfortune to no small degree.

**14:9** Now you, king, having ascertained each of these matters, make provision for both the country and our hard-pressed nation, in accordance with the gracious kindness that you have toward all.

**14:10** For as long as Judas remains alive, it is impossible for affairs to attain peace."

**14:11** When such things had been spoken by him, the rest of the friends, being hostilely disposed toward Judas, quickly inflamed Demetrius still further.

**14:12** And immediately selecting Nicanor, who had been commander of the elephants, and appointing him governor of Judea, he sent him out,

**14:13** giving written orders to put Judas himself to death, to scatter those with him, and to establish Alcimus as high priest of the greatest temple.

**14:14** And the Gentiles in Judea who had fled from Judas joined Nicanor in droves, thinking that the misfortunes and calamities of the Jews would be their own prosperity.

**14:15** Now when they heard of the advance of Nicanor and the assault of the Gentiles, sprinkling earth upon themselves, they implored Him who established His people forever and always with a manifestation comes to the aid of His own portion.

**14:16** And when the leader gave the order, immediately setting out from there, he engaged them at the village of Lessau.

**14:17** Now Simon, the brother of Judas, had encountered Nicanor in battle, yet slowly, because of the sudden consternation of the adversaries, had suffered a setback.

**14:18** Nevertheless, Nicanor, hearing of the valor that Judas and those with him possessed and their courage in the struggles for their homeland, was reluctant to make the decision by bloodshed.

**14:19** Therefore he sent Posidonius, Theodotus, and Mattathias to give and receive right-hand pledges.

**14:20** And when lengthy deliberation about these matters had taken place, and the leader had consulted with the troops, and unanimous consent appeared, they assented to the treaty.

**14:21** And they appointed a day on which they would come to the same place privately. And a litter came forward from each one; they set out seats.

**14:22** Judas stationed armed men ready in the strategic places, in case treachery should suddenly arise from the enemies. They held the appropriate conference.

**14:23** Nicanor stayed in Jerusalem and did nothing improper, and he dismissed the crowds that had gathered in droves.

**14:24** And he kept Judas continually in his presence; he had become devoted to the man from his very soul.

**14:25** He urged him to marry and have children; he married, settled down, shared in life.

**14:26** But Alcimus, observing their goodwill toward one another and taking the treaty that had been made, came to Demetrius and kept saying that Nicanor held sentiments alien to the affairs of state, for he had appointed Judas, the conspirator against his kingdom, as successor.

**14:27** Then the king, becoming enraged and provoked by the slanders of that utterly wicked man, wrote to Nicanor, stating that he was greatly displeased about the treaty, and commanding him to send Maccabeus as a prisoner to Antioch quickly.

**14:28** When these things reached Nicanor, he was dismayed and deeply distressed that he would violate what had been stipulated, though the man had done nothing wrong.

**14:29** Yet since it was not possible to oppose the king, he watched for an opportune time to carry this out by a stratagem.

**14:30** But Maccabeus, observing that Nicanor had been acting more harshly in his dealings with him and had become more rude in their customary meeting, realizing that this harshness did not come from the best motive, gathered together not a few of those with him and went into hiding from Nicanor.

**14:31** When the other realized that he had been boldly outmaneuvered by the man, he came to the greatest and holy temple while the priests were offering the prescribed sacrifices, and he commanded them to hand over the man.

**14:32** And when they declared with oaths that they did not know at all where the one being sought was,

**14:33** stretching out his right hand toward the sanctuary, he swore these words: "Unless you hand Judas over to me as a prisoner, I will make this sacred enclosure of God into flat ground, and I will demolish the altar, and here I will erect a splendid temple to Dionysus."

**14:34** Having spoken such words, he departed. And the priests, stretching out their hands toward heaven, called upon the continual defender of our nation, saying,

**14:35** "You, Lord, being in need of nothing at all, were pleased that a sanctuary of Your dwelling should come to be among us.

**14:36** And now, holy Lord of all sanctification, keep undefiled forever this house that has recently been purified."

**14:37** Now a certain Razis, one of the elders from Jerusalem, was denounced to Nicanor, a man devoted to his fellow citizens and very well spoken of, and because of his goodwill called father of the Jews.

**14:38** For in the earlier times of separation he had incurred charges of Judaism, and had risked both body and soul for Judaism with all earnestness.

**14:39** Now Nicanor, wishing to make evident the hostility that he had toward the Jews, sent more than five hundred soldiers to arrest him.

**14:40** For he thought that by arresting that man he would inflict a calamity upon them.

**14:41** When the troops were about to capture the tower and were forcing the courtyard door, ordering fire to be brought and the doors to be set ablaze, being surrounded on every side, he turned the sword upon himself,

**14:42** wishing to die nobly rather than fall into the hands of the wicked and be subjected to outrages unworthy of his own nobility.

**14:43** But having missed his mark with the blow because of the haste of the struggle, and while the crowds were bursting inside the doorways, he ran bravely up onto the wall and hurled himself down courageously into the crowds.

**14:44** And as they quickly stepped back and a space opened up, he landed right in the middle of the empty space.

**14:45** Still breathing and inflamed with rage, he rose up while blood gushed out in torrents and his wounds were severe, and ran through the crowds; and standing upon a steep rock,

**14:46** now becoming completely drained of blood, he pulled out his entrails, and taking them in both hands, hurled them at the crowds; and calling upon Him who rules over life and spirit to give these same things back to him again, in this manner he died.

## Translator Notes

- **14:7** — The Greek text reads the middle participle aphelomenos (here rendered "deprived of," where other manuscripts read apheimenos) and deuteron ("a second time," where other manuscripts read deuro, "here").
- **14:16** — The Greek text preserves the place name Lessau (Lessaou), whereas some Greek manuscripts read Dessau (Dessaou).
- **14:17** — The Greek text reads bradeos ("slowly," where other manuscripts read bracheos, "slightly"), and the rare noun aphasian literally means "speechlessness" or "consternation," with "of the adversaries" variously taken as dismay caused by or occurring among the opponents.
- **14:21** — The Greek word diphrax ("litter") occurs only here in ancient Greek and is understood as a litter, sedan chair, or chariot brought forward from each side.
- **14:44** — The Greek noun keneon ("empty space") matches the preceding "space opened up," though the same word can also denote the anatomical hollow of the flank or abdomen.
