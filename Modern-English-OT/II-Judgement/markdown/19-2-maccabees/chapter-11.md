## Text

**11:1** Now after an exceedingly short time, Lysias, the king's guardian and kinsman and the one in charge of the affairs of state, being greatly displeased over the things that had happened,

**11:2** gathered together about eighty thousand men and all the cavalry and marched against the Jews, reckoning that he would make the city a dwelling place for Greeks,

**11:3** and the temple subject to tribute, just like the other shrines of the nations, and that he would put the high priesthood up for sale year by year;

**11:4** in no way taking into account the might of God, but made overconfident by the tens of thousands of foot soldiers and the thousands of horsemen and the eighty elephants.

**11:5** And having entered Judea and drawn near to Beth-zur, which was a fortified place and about five schoinoi distant from Jerusalem, he pressed it hard.

**11:6** Now when Maccabeus and those with him learned that he was besieging the strongholds, with lamentations and tears they implored the Lord, together with the crowds, to send a good angel for the deliverance of Israel.

**11:7** And Maccabeus himself, being the first to take up weapons, urged the others to risk danger together with him and go to the aid of their brothers; and together and eagerly they rushed out.

**11:8** And while they were right there near Jerusalem, a horseman in white clothing appeared going ahead of them, brandishing golden arms.

**11:9** And all together they blessed the merciful God and were strengthened in their souls, being ready to pierce through not only men, but also the wildest beasts and iron walls;

**11:10** they advanced in battle array, having the ally from heaven, since the Lord had shown mercy to them.

**11:11** And hurling themselves like lions into the enemy, they struck down eleven thousand of them and one thousand six hundred horsemen; and they forced all of them to flee.

**11:12** And most of them escaped wounded and naked; and Lysias himself also escaped by fleeing disgracefully.

**11:13** Now not being without understanding, weighing within himself the defeat that had happened to him and realizing that the Hebrews were invincible because the mighty God was fighting as their ally, he sent to them

**11:14** and persuaded them to come to terms on all just conditions, and that he would also persuade the king, compelling him to become a friend to them.

**11:15** And Maccabeus consented to all that Lysias proposed, having regard for what was beneficial; for whatever Maccabeus submitted to Lysias in writing concerning the Jews, the scribe granted.

**11:16** For letters had been written to the Jews from Lysias, reading in this manner: "Lysias to the multitude of the Jews, greetings.

**11:17** John and Absalom, who were sent from you, having submitted the document written below, made a request concerning the matters indicated in it.

**11:18** Therefore, whatever needed to be brought before the king as well, I explained clearly, and what was admissible he granted.

**11:19** If, therefore, you maintain goodwill toward the affairs of state, in the future as well I will try to be a source of good things.

**11:20** And concerning these matters in detail, I have instructed both these men and those from me to confer with you.

**11:21** Farewell. In the one hundred and forty-eighth year, on the twenty-fourth of Dios Corinthius."

**11:22** Now the king's letter read as follows: "King Antiochus to his brother Lysias, greetings.

**11:23** Now that our father has passed over to the gods, desiring that those of the kingdom, being undisturbed, should attend to the care of the Jews,

**11:24** having heard that the Jews do not approve of our father's change to Greek practices, but preferring their own way of life, request that their lawful customs be granted to them;

**11:25** choosing, therefore, that this nation also be free from disturbance, we decide that both the temple be restored to them and that they live as citizens according to the customs in the time of their ancestors.

**11:26** You will do well, therefore, to send to them and give them pledges, so that knowing our policy they may both be of good cheer and gladly carry on in attending to their own affairs."

**11:27** And the king's letter to the nation was as follows: "King Antiochus to the senate of the Jews and to the other Jews, greetings.

**11:28** Be well; it would be as we wish; and we ourselves also are in good health.

**11:29** Menelaus reported to us, wishing that you return home and attend to your own affairs.

**11:30** Therefore, to those who return home up to the thirtieth of Xanthicus, there will be a pledge with immunity,

**11:31** that the Jews may use their own expenditures and laws, just as before; and none of them will be harassed in any way concerning things done in ignorance.

**11:32** And I have also sent Menelaus to encourage you.

**11:33** Farewell. In the one hundred and forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth of Xanthicus."

**11:34** And the Romans also sent them a letter reading as follows: "Quintus Memmius, Titus Manius, envoys of the Romans, to the people of the Jews, greetings.

**11:35** Concerning the things that Lysias, the kinsman of the king, granted to you, we also approve.

**11:36** But as for the matters that he decided should be referred to the king, send someone immediately after considering these matters, so that we may have them as is fitting for us; for we are proceeding to Antioch.

**11:37** Therefore, make haste and send some men, so that we also may know what your mind is.

**11:38** Be in good health. In the one hundred and forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth of Xanthicus."

## Translator Notes

- **11:5** — A schoinos was an ancient unit of distance equal to roughly three to four miles, making five schoinoi approximately fifteen to twenty miles, matching the distance from Jerusalem to Beth-zur. Some Greek manuscripts read stadia instead.
- **11:15** — The Greek text reads "the scribe" (grammateus, or "secretary"), whereas many other witnesses read "the king" (basileus).
- **11:21** — Dios Corinthius is an unusual month name not otherwise attested in the Macedonian calendar and is often regarded as a variant form of the month Dios or Dioskoros.
- **11:23** — The Greek text reads the genitive participle "desiring" (agreeing with "our father") and "of the Jews," whereas other witnesses read the nominative plural "desiring" (referring to the king) and "of their own affairs."
- **11:31** — The Greek text reads "expenditures" (dapanemasin), whereas other manuscripts read "dietary practices" (diaitemasin).
