## Text

**8:1** Now Judas, who was also called Maccabeus, and those with him, slipping secretly into the villages, summoned their relatives, and enlisting those who had remained in Judaism, they gathered about six thousand.

**8:2** And they called upon the Lord to look upon the people who were oppressed by all, and also to have compassion on the temple that had been profaned by the ungodly men,

**8:3** and also to have mercy on the city that was being destroyed and was about to become level with the ground, and to listen to the blood crying out to Him,

**8:4** and to remember also the lawless destruction of the innocent infants and the blasphemies committed against His name, and to show hatred of wickedness.

**8:5** Now once Maccabeus had formed an organized band, he was already becoming irresistible to the Gentiles, for the wrath of the Lord had turned into mercy.

**8:6** Coming unexpectedly, he would set fire to cities and regions, and seizing the strategic places, routing not a few of the enemies,

**8:7** he took especially the nights as helpers for such attacks; and the report of his valor echoed everywhere.

**8:8** Now when Philip observed that the man was little by little making progress and advancing more and more frequently in successes, he wrote to Ptolemy, the governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, to come to the aid of the king's affairs.

**8:9** And he quickly appointed and sent Nicanor the son of Patroclus, one of the First Friends, placing under his command not fewer than twenty thousand Gentiles of all nationalities to wipe out the entire race of Judea; and they also assigned with him Gorgias, a general well experienced in matters of war.

**8:10** Now Nicanor determined to pay in full for the king the tribute to the Romans, amounting to two thousand talents, from the capture of the Jews.

**8:11** And immediately he sent to the coastal cities, inviting them to the purchase of Jewish slaves, promising to hand over ninety slaves for a talent, not expecting the justice from the Almighty that was about to overtake him.

**8:12** Now word reached Judas concerning Nicanor's advance; and when he informed those with him of the arrival of the army,

**8:13** those who were cowardly and distrusted the justice of God fled and removed themselves from the place,

**8:14** while the others sold everything that remained, and at the same time begged the Lord to rescue those who had been sold by the impious Nicanor before they met him,

**8:15** and if not for their own sake, yet for the sake of the covenants with their fathers, and because of the calling of His revered and majestic name upon them.

**8:16** Now Maccabeus, having gathered those around him, six thousand in number, urged them not to be terrified by the prisoners nor to fear the great multitude of the Gentiles who had come unjustly against them, but to fight nobly,

**8:17** keeping before their eyes the outrage lawlessly perpetrated by them against the holy place, and the torture of the mocked city, and moreover the abolition of the ancestral constitution.

**8:18** "For they trust in weapons and also in daring deeds," he said, "but we trust in the Almighty God, who is able with a single nod to seize both those coming against us and the whole world."

**8:19** And he also recounted to them the acts of help that had occurred in the time of their ancestors: both the one in the time of Sennacherib, how one hundred eighty-five thousand perished,

**8:20** and the battle that took place in Babylonia against the Galatians, how eight thousand in all came to the engagement along with four thousand Macedonians, and when the Macedonians were hard pressed, the six thousand destroyed the one hundred twenty thousand because of the help that came to them from heaven, and took much plunder.

**8:21** By these words, having made them courageous and ready to die for the laws and their homeland, he divided the army into four parts,

**8:22** appointing his brothers as leaders of each division: Simon, Joseph, and Jonathan, placing under each one thousand five hundred,

**8:23** and moreover also Eleazar. Having read aloud the sacred book and given the watchword, "God's Help," leading the first cohort himself, he engaged Nicanor in battle.

**8:24** And with the Almighty becoming their ally, they slaughtered more than nine thousand of the enemies, left the greater part of Nicanor's army wounded and maimed in their limbs, and forced them all to flee.

**8:25** And they took the money of those who had come to purchase them. After pursuing them closely for a considerable distance, they turned back from the region, being hemmed in;

**8:26** for it was the custom before the Sabbath, for which reason they did not prolong chasing after them.

**8:27** Having collected the weapons from them and stripped off the spoils of the enemies, they devoted themselves to the Sabbath, exceedingly blessing and giving thanks to the Lord, who had preserved them to this day, having appointed a beginning of mercy for them.

**8:28** And after the Sabbath, having distributed a share of the spoils to those who had been tortured, to the widows, and to the orphans, they divided the rest among themselves and their children.

**8:29** When they had carried out these things and made a joint supplication, they begged the merciful Lord to be completely reconciled to His servants.

**8:30** And clashing with the forces of Timothy and Bacchides, they killed more than twenty thousand of them, and thoroughly gained control of high strongholds, and divided spoils of greater value, making themselves equal sharers with those who had been tortured, and the orphans, and the widows, and moreover also the elderly.

**8:31** And having carefully collected the weapons from them, they stored them all in the strategic places, and brought the rest of the spoils to Jerusalem.

**8:32** They also killed the phylarch of those with Timothy, a most unholy man who had caused the Jews much grief.

**8:33** And while celebrating the victory in their homeland, they burned those who had set fire to the sacred gates, and Callisthenes, who had fled into a dwelling; and he received the fitting wage of his impiety.

**8:34** Now the thrice-accursed Nicanor, who had brought three thousand merchants for the sale of the Jews,

**8:35** humbled with the help of the Lord by those whom he considered to be the least, put off his glorious robe and, making himself solitary like a runaway slave, came through the inland country to Antioch, having succeeded beyond everything in the destruction of the army.

**8:36** And the one who had undertaken to make good the tribute to the Romans from the capture of those in Jerusalem proclaimed that the Jews had a Defender, and that for this reason the Jews were invulnerable, because they followed His appointed laws.

## Translator Notes

- **8:16** — The Greek text reads "the prisoners" (tois desmiois, as in Codex Alexandrinus), where other witnesses read "the enemies" (tous polemious).
- **8:20** — The Greek text reads "the six thousand" (hoi hexakischilioi, as in Codex Alexandrinus), differing from the "eight thousand" stated earlier in the verse (which other witnesses repeat here).
- **8:23** — The Greek text places "Eleazar" in the accusative as part of the preceding list of leaders, with the nominative participle "having read aloud" referring to Judas; other textual traditions construe Eleazar as the reader of the sacred book.
- **8:26** — The Greek text reads "custom" (dike, as in Codex Alexandrinus), where other witnesses read "hour" (hora) or "afternoon" (deile); similarly in v. 25, the Greek text reads "from the region" (apo tes choras) rather than "by the hour" (hypo tes horas).
- **8:34** — The Greek text reads "three thousand" (trischilious, as in Codex Alexandrinus), whereas other witnesses read "one thousand" (chilious).
