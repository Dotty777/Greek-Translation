## Text

**15:1** Now Nicanor, having learned that Judas and those with him were in the places around Samaria, planned to attack them with complete security on the day of rest.

**15:2** When the Jews who were accompanying him under compulsion said, "By no means destroy them so savagely and barbarously, but give glory to the day that has been preeminently honored with holiness by the One who watches over all things,"

**15:3** the thrice-accursed man asked whether there is in heaven the Sovereign who has commanded that the Sabbath day be kept.

**15:4** When they declared, "There is the living Lord Himself, Sovereign in heaven, who ordered that the seventh day be observed,"

**15:5** the other said, "And I too am a sovereign on the earth, who commands that arms be taken up and the royal duties be carried out." Nevertheless, he did not succeed in carrying out his wicked purpose.

**15:6** And Nicanor, holding his head high with complete security, had resolved to establish a common manner for Judas and those with him.

**15:7** But Maccabeus was unceasingly confident, with all hope, that he would obtain help from the Lord.

**15:8** And he encouraged those with him not to fear the assault of the nations, but, keeping in mind the former acts of help that had come to them from heaven, to expect now as well the victory that would come to them from the Almighty.

**15:9** And comforting them from the Law and the Prophets, and also praising them and the struggles that they had completed, he made them more eager.

**15:10** And rousing them in their spirits, he gave his orders, at the same time pointing out the faithlessness of the nations and the violation of oaths.

**15:11** And arming each one of them not so much with the security of shields and spears as with the encouragement in good words, and relating in addition a trustworthy dream, he gladdened them all exceedingly.

**15:12** Now the vision of it was like this: Onias, who had been high priest, a noble and good man, modest in demeanor, gentle in manner, uttering speech fittingly, and from childhood having thoroughly practiced all things proper to virtue—this man stretched out his hands and was praying for the whole community of the Jews.

**15:13** Then next there appeared a man distinguished by gray hair and glory, and the dignity surrounding him was something marvelous and most majestic.

**15:14** And Onias answered and said, "This is the one who loves his brothers, who prays much for the people and the holy city, Jeremiah, the prophet of God."

**15:15** Then Jeremiah stretched out his right hand and handed to Judas a golden sword, and as he gave it, he spoke these words:

**15:16** "Take the holy sword, a gift from God, through which you will shatter the adversaries."

**15:17** Having been encouraged by the words of Judas, which were altogether noble and able to spur them on to valor and to embolden the souls of young men, they resolved not to carry on a campaign, but gallantly to rush in, and engaging at close quarters with all bravery to decide the matter, because the city and the holy things and the temple were in danger.

**15:18** For their fear for wives and children, and furthermore for brothers and relatives, held a lesser place with them, while greatest and first was their fear for the consecrated sanctuary.

**15:19** And for those confined in the city as well, there was no slight distress, as they were troubled over the clash in the open field.

**15:20** And when all were already awaiting the coming decision, and the enemies had already closed in and the army had been drawn up in array, and the beasts had been stationed in a favorable position, and the cavalry had been arrayed on the flank,

**15:21** Maccabeus, observing the presence of the multitudes and the varied equipment of arms, and the ferocity of the beasts in a favorable position, stretched out his hands to heaven and called upon the wonder-working Lord, knowing that it is not through arms, but that as it is decided by Him, He secures victory for the worthy.

**15:22** And calling upon Him, he spoke in this manner: "You, Master, sent Your angel in the time of Hezekiah, king of Judea, and he killed as many as one hundred eighty-five thousand from the camp of Sennacherib.

**15:23** And now, Sovereign of the heavens, send a good angel before us for dread and trembling;

**15:24** by the greatness of Your arm, may those who come with blasphemy against Your holy people be struck with terror." And he ended with these words.

**15:25** Now Nicanor and those with him were advancing with trumpets and battle songs.

**15:26** But Judas and those with him engaged the enemies with invocation and prayers.

**15:27** And fighting with their hands while praying to God with their hearts, they struck down no fewer than thirty-five thousand, greatly gladdened by the care of God.

**15:28** And when they had finished the engagement and were returning with joy, they recognized Nicanor lying fallen in his full armor.

**15:29** And when a shout and commotion arose, they blessed the Sovereign in their ancestral language.

**15:30** And he who was in everything, in body and soul, the foremost champion on behalf of the citizens, who had preserved the goodwill of his youth toward his countrymen, commanded that they cut off the head of Nicanor, and his arm along with the shoulder, and carry them around to Jerusalem.

**15:31** And when he arrived there and had called together his countrymen and stationed the priests before the altar, he sent for those from the citadel.

**15:32** And having displayed the head of the vile Nicanor and the arm of the blasphemer, which he had stretched out against the holy house of the Almighty as he boasted arrogantly,

**15:33** and having cut out the tongue of the impious Nicanor, he ordered it in pieces for the birds, and the wages of his folly to be hung opposite the sanctuary.

**15:34** And toward heaven they all blessed the manifest Lord, saying, "Blessed is the One who has preserved His own place undefiled."

**15:35** And he hung the severed head of Nicanor from the citadel, clearly visible to all and an evident sign of the help of the Lord.

**15:36** And they all decreed by a public resolution by no means to leave this day unmarked, but to keep as a marked day the thirteenth of the twelfth month (it is called Adar in the Lord's language), one day before the day of Mordecai.

**15:37** Since, then, the affairs concerning Nicanor turned out in this way, and from those times the city was held by the Hebrews, I myself will also bring my account to an end right here.

**15:38** And if it is well done, aptly in its composition, this is what I myself also wished; but if poorly and moderately, this was what was attainable for me.

**15:39** Just as drinking wine by itself, and likewise water on the other hand, is harmful, whereas in the way that wine mixed with water now also produces a delightful pleasure, so also the crafting of the account delights the ears of those who read the composition. And here will be the end.

## Translator Notes

- **15:6** — The Greek text here reads asphaleias ('security') and tropon ('manner'), whereas other witnesses read alazoneias ('arrogance') and tropaion ('trophy/monument').
- **15:17** — The Greek text reads me strateuesthai ('not to carry on a campaign') and enpheresthai ('to rush in'), where other witnesses read me stratopedeuesthai ('not to encamp') and epipheresthai ('to attack').
- **15:27** — The Greek text reads epimeleia ('care'), whereas other witnesses read epiphaneia ('manifestation').
- **15:33** — The Greek text omits an explicit verb before 'for the birds' (where other witnesses add dosein, 'would give') and reads meta meros ('in pieces') for kata meros. The term 'wages' (epicheira, linked to cheir, 'hand/arm') plays on Nicanor's severed arm.
- **15:36** — The Greek text reads kyriake ('the Lord's') for the language naming the month Adar, whereas other witnesses read Syriake ('Syrian,' i.e., Aramaic).
