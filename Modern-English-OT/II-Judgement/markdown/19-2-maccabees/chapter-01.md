## Text

**1:1** To the brothers, the Jews throughout Egypt: the brothers, the Jews in Jerusalem and those in the country of Judea, wish greetings and good peace.

**1:2** And may God do good to you, and remember His covenant with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, His faithful servants.

**1:3** And may He give you all a heart to worship Him and to do His will with a great heart and a willing soul;

**1:4** and may He open your heart in His law and in the statutes, and may He make peace;

**1:5** and may He hear your petitions, and be reconciled to you, and not forsake you in an evil time.

**1:6** And now we are here praying for you.

**1:7** When Demetrius was reigning, in the one hundred sixty-ninth year, we, the Jews, wrote to you in the distress and in the crisis that came upon us in these years, since Jason and those with him revolted from the holy land and the kingdom,

**1:8** and burned the gateway and shed innocent blood. And we petitioned the Lord and were heard; we offered sacrifice and fine flour, and brought out the lamps, and set out the loaves.

**1:9** And now, see that you observe the days of the festival of Booths of the month of Chislev.

**1:10** In the eighty-eighth year, in Jerusalem. And those in Judea and the council of elders and Judas, to Aristobulus, teacher of King Ptolemy, who is from the lineage of the anointed priests, and to the Jews in Egypt, greetings and good health.

**1:11** Having been saved by God from great dangers, we greatly give thanks to Him, as those arrayed in battle against a king.

**1:12** For He Himself drove out into Persia those who were arrayed in battle in the holy city.

**1:13** For when the leader arrived, and the force around him that seemed to be irresistible, they were cut to pieces in the temple of Nanea, since the priests attending Nanea used deception.

**1:14** For as though to marry her, Antiochus and the friends with him came to the place for the sake of taking the large sums of money by way of a dowry.

**1:15** And when the priests of the shrine of Nanea had set them out, and he had approached with a few men into the enclosure of the sacred precinct, they shut up the temple when Antiochus entered,

**1:16** opened the hidden door of the paneled ceiling, and throwing stones, struck down the leader as with a thunderbolt, dismembering them and throwing the heads to those outside.

**1:17** In every way blessed be our God, who gave over those who acted impiously.

**1:18** As we are about to observe on the twenty-fifth of Chislev the purification of the temple, we thought it necessary to inform you, so that you yourselves also may observe the festival of Booths and of the fire, when Nehemiah, who at that time built the temple and the altar, offered sacrifices.

**1:19** For indeed, when our fathers were being led away into Persia, the devout priests secretly took some of the fire of the altar and hid it in a hollow having the form of a waterless cistern, in which they secured it so that the place was unknown to all.

**1:20** And after many years had passed, when it seemed good to God, Nehemiah, having been sent by the king of Persia, sent the descendants of the priests who had hidden it for the fire.

**1:21** When they informed us that they had not found fire, but thick water, he commanded them to draw some out and bring it. And when the materials of the sacrifices had been brought up, Nehemiah commanded the priests to sprinkle with the water both the wood and the things laid upon it.

**1:22** And when it was done and time had passed, and the sun shone out though it was clouded over, a great fire was kindled, so that all marveled.

**1:23** And the priests offered a prayer while the sacrifice was being consumed, both the priests and everyone, with Jonathan leading and the rest responding, just as Nehemiah did.

**1:24** And the prayer was in this manner: "Lord God, Creator of all things, awesome and mighty and righteous and merciful, the only King and gracious One,

**1:25** the only Provider, the only righteous and almighty and eternal One, who delivers Israel from every evil, who made the fathers chosen ones and sanctified them:

**1:26** Accept the sacrifice on behalf of all Your people Israel, and guard Your portion and consecrate it.

**1:27** Gather together our dispersion, free those who are enslaved among the nations, look upon the despised and detested, and let the nations know that You are our God.

**1:28** Torment those who oppress and behave insolently in arrogance;

**1:29** plant Your people in Your holy place, just as Moses said."

**1:30** And the priests were singing the hymns.

**1:31** And when the materials of the sacrifice had been consumed, Nehemiah commanded that the remaining water also be poured over large stones.

**1:32** And when this was done, a flame was kindled; but when the light from the altar shone back, it was consumed.

**1:33** And when the matter became known, and it was reported to the king of the Persians that in the place where the priests who had been carried away hid the fire, the water had appeared, with which Nehemiah and those with him also purified the sacrifices,

**1:34** the king enclosed it and made it sacred, after verifying the matter.

**1:35** And to those from whom the king received many valuables, he also gave a share.

**1:36** And Nehemiah and those with him named this Nephthar, which is translated Purification; and by most people it is called Nephthar.

## Translator Notes

- **1:8** — The Greek text reads ἐξηγάγομεν ("brought out"), whereas other Greek witnesses read ἐξήψαμεν ("lit").
- **1:10** — The Greek text reads "eighty-eighth year" (ἔτους ὀγδοηκοστοῦ καὶ ὀγδόου) and attaches "in Jerusalem" to the date, whereas other Greek witnesses read "one hundred eighty-eighth year" and begin the greeting with "Those in Jerusalem."
- **1:12** — The Greek text places "into Persia" (εἰς τὴν Περσίδα) at the end of verse 12 rather than at the beginning of verse 13 as in some other editions.
- **1:35** — The Greek text omits the verb "showed favor" (ἐχαρίζετο) found in other Greek witnesses, leaving a compressed relative construction.
- **1:36** — The Greek text reads Νεφθάρ ("Nephthar") in both occurrences, whereas other Greek witnesses read Νεφθάι ("Nephthai") in the second.
