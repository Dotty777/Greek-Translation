## Text

**5:1** Now about this time Antiochus made his second expedition into Egypt.

**5:2** And it happened that throughout the whole city, for almost forty days, there appeared horsemen racing through the air, wearing robes interwoven with gold, and fully armed with spears in companies,

**5:3** and the drawing of swords, and squadrons of horses drawn up in order, and attacks and charges taking place on both sides, and movements of shields, and multitudes of toils, and volleys of missiles, and flashes of golden ornaments, and armor of every kind.

**5:4** Therefore everyone prayed that the apparition had occurred for good.

**5:5** Now when a false rumor arose that Antiochus had departed life, Jason took with him not fewer than a thousand men and suddenly carried out an assault against the city; and when those on the wall were driven back, and in the end the city was already being captured, Menelaus fled into the citadel.

**5:6** And Jason carried out slaughters of his own people of the city mercilessly, not realizing that good fortune against kinsmen is the greatest misfortune, but thinking that he was winning trophies over enemies and not over people of his own nation.

**5:7** He did not gain control of the government, but receiving disgrace as the outcome of his plot, he passed again as a fugitive into Ammanitis.

**5:8** In the end, therefore, he met with an evil ruin: imprisoned by Aretas the tyrant of the Arabs, fleeing from city to city, pursued by all, hated as an apostate from the laws and abhorred as the executioner of his homeland and citizens, he was cast ashore into Egypt.

**5:9** He who had exiled many from their homeland perished in a foreign land, having set sail to the Lacedaemonians in the hope of obtaining shelter because of their kinship;

**5:10** and he who had cast out a multitude unburied went unmourned, and had no share in any funeral at all nor in ancestral law.

**5:11** Now when reports concerning what had happened reached the king, he concluded that Judea was in revolt; therefore, breaking camp from Egypt, raging like a wild beast in his soul, he took the city by force of arms.

**5:12** And he ordered the soldiers to cut down mercilessly those they encountered, and to slaughter those climbing up in the houses.

**5:13** And there was a killing of young and old, a wiping out of boys and women and children, and slaughters of virgins and infants.

**5:14** And eighty thousand were destroyed within three days in all, forty thousand in hand-to-hand fighting, and no fewer than those who were slaughtered were sold.

**5:15** And not satisfied with these things, he dared to enter the most holy temple of all the earth, having Menelaus as his guide, the one who had become a traitor both to the laws and to his homeland,

**5:16** and taking the sacred vessels with his polluted hands, and sweeping away with his profane hands the things set up by many kings for the advancement and glory and honor of the place.

**5:17** And Antiochus was lifted up in his mind, not perceiving that because of the sins of those who inhabited the city the Sovereign had been angry for a brief time, and therefore a disregard had occurred concerning the place.

**5:18** For if it had not happened that they were entangled in many sins, just as it was with Heliodorus who was sent by Seleucus the king for the inspection of the treasury, this man, upon advancing, would immediately have been scourged and turned back from his audacity.

**5:19** However, the Lord did not choose the nation because of the place, but the place because of the nation.

**5:20** Therefore the place itself also, having shared in the misfortunes that befell the nation, afterward shared in the benefits; and that which was overtaken in the wrath of the Almighty was restored again with all glory in the reconciliation of the great Sovereign.

**5:21** So Antiochus, having carried off eighteen hundred talents from the temple, hurriedly departed to Antioch, thinking in his arrogance that he could make the land navigable and the sea passable on foot, because of the loftiness of his heart.

**5:22** He also left behind overseers to afflict the race: in Jerusalem, Philip, a Phrygian by birth, yet having a character more barbarous than the one who appointed him;

**5:23** and at Gerizim, Andronicus; and in addition to these, Menelaus exalted himself over the citizens worse than the others, while holding a hostile disposition toward the Jewish citizens.

**5:24** And he sent Apollonius, the commander of the Mysians, with an army of twenty-two thousand, ordering him to slaughter all who were of full age, and to sell the women and the younger ones.

**5:25** When this man arrived in Jerusalem and pretended to be peaceful, he waited until the holy day of the Sabbath; and finding the Jews at rest, he ordered a muster in full armor for those under him.

**5:26** And he stabbed to death all who came out for the spectacle, and rushing into the city with weapons, he stationed great multitudes.

**5:27** But Judas, who is also called Maccabeus, as one of about ten, withdrew and lived in the mountains in the manner of wild beasts along with those who were with him; and they continued feeding on plant food, so as not to share in the defilement.

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — The Greek text reads kamaton ("toils/struggles"), a reading found in Codex Alexandrinus; other manuscripts read kamakon ("spear-shafts" or "pikes").
- **5:8** — The Greek text reads enkleistheis ("imprisoned" or "confined"); other manuscripts read enkletheis ("accused").
- **5:10** — The Greek text reads patroou nomou ("ancestral law"); other manuscripts read patroou taphou ("ancestral tomb").
- **5:20** — The Greek text reads katalephtheis ("overtaken" or "seized"); other manuscripts read kataleiphtheis ("forsaken" or "abandoned").
- **5:26** — The Greek text reads katestesen ("stationed" or "posted"); other manuscripts read katestrosen ("struck down" or "killed"), referring to great multitudes of the city's inhabitants.
