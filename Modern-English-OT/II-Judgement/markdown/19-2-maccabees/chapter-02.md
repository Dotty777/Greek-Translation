## Text

**2:1** Now it is found in the records that Jeremiah the prophet commanded those who were deported to take some of the fire, as has been indicated,

**2:2** and how the prophet charged those who were deported, after giving them the law, that they should not forget the statutes of the Lord, and that they should not be led astray in their minds when seeing images of gold and silver and the adornment around them.

**2:3** And saying other similar things, he urged them not to let the law depart from their heart.

**2:4** And it was in the writing that the prophet, after a divine oracle had come to him, commanded the tabernacle and the ark to follow along, and how he went out to the mountain where Moses went up and viewed the inheritance of God.

**2:5** And when Jeremiah came, he found a cave-like dwelling, and he brought the tabernacle and the ark and the altar of incense in there, and blocked up the entrance.

**2:6** And some of those who were following along came up in order to mark the way, and they were not able to find it.

**2:7** And when Jeremiah learned of it, he rebuked them and said, "Indeed, the place will be unknown until God gathers the assembly of the people and mercy comes.

**2:8** And then the Lord will reveal these things, and the glory of the Lord and the cloud will appear, as it was shown in the time of Moses, and as Solomon also asked, that the place might be greatly consecrated."

**2:9** And it was also made clear how, having wisdom, he offered a sacrifice of dedication and of the completion of the temple.

**2:10** Just as Moses also prayed to the Lord, and fire came down from heaven and consumed the materials of the sacrifice, so Solomon prayed to the Lord, and the fire came down and consumed the burnt offerings.

**2:11** And Moses said, "Because the sin offering had not been eaten, it was consumed."

**2:12** Likewise Solomon also observed the eight days.

**2:13** And the same things were also recounted in the archives and in the memoirs concerning Nehemiah, and how, founding a library, he gathered together the books about the kings and prophets, and those of David, and letters of kings concerning dedicated offerings.

**2:14** And likewise Judas also gathered together these things that had been scattered because of the war that happened to us, and they are with us.

**2:15** Therefore, if you have need of any of these, send those who will bring them to you.

**2:16** As we are about to observe the purification, therefore, we have written to you; you will therefore do well to observe the days.

**2:17** Now God is the One who saved all His people and restored His inheritance to all, and the kingdom and the priesthood and the sanctification,

**2:18** just as He promised through the law. For we hope in God that He will soon have mercy on us and gather us together from under heaven into the holy place, for He has rescued us from great evils and purified the place.

**2:19** Now the events concerning Judas Maccabeus and his brothers, and the purification of the greatest temple, and the dedication of the altar,

**2:20** and furthermore the wars against Antiochus Epiphanes and his son Eupator,

**2:21** and the manifestations that occurred from heaven to those who acted bravely with zeal for Judaism, so that, though few, they plundered the whole country and pursued the barbarian multitudes,

**2:22** and recovered the temple renowned throughout the whole inhabited world, and freed the city, and re-established the laws that were about to be abolished, as the Lord became gracious to them with all clemency,

**2:23** having been set forth by Jason of Cyrene in five books, we will try to abridge in a single volume.

**2:24** For considering the flood of numbers, and the difficulty that exists for those who wish to plunge into the narratives of the history because of the abundance of the material,

**2:25** we have aimed at enjoyment for those who wish to read, ease for those who take pleasure in committing it to memory, and benefit for all who encounter it.

**2:26** And for us who have undertaken the hard labor of the abridgment, it is not easy, but the matter is one of sweat and sleeplessness,

**2:27** just as for the one who prepares a banquet and seeks the advantage of others it is not effortless; nevertheless, for the sake of the gratitude of the many, we will gladly endure the hard labor,

**2:28** leaving the exact inquiry concerning each detail to the author, while growing weary in following the outlines of the abridgment.

**2:29** For just as the architect of a new house must take care for the whole structure, while the one who undertakes to dedicate and paint it must examine what is suitable for ornamentation, so I think it is with us also.

**2:30** To enter in, and to walk about in discourse, and to inquire closely into the particulars belongs to the originator of the history;

**2:31** but to pursue brevity of expression and to decline exhaustive treatment of the subject matter must be granted to the one making the paraphrase.

**2:32** From this point, therefore, let us begin the narrative, having added this much to what has been said before; for it is foolish to be lengthy before the history, while abridging the history.

## Translator Notes

- **2:17** — The Greek opens with a nominative participial phrase (ὁ δὲ θεὸς ὁ σώσας...) that shifts without a finite verb into the causal clause of verse 18 (ἐλπίζομεν γάρ); it is rendered here as a nominal clause.
- **2:25** — The Greek text reads φιλοφρονοῦσιν ("who take pleasure / are favorably inclined"), whereas other Greek witnesses read φιλοπονοῦσιν ("who labor diligently").
- **2:28** — The Greek text reads ἀτονοῦντες ("growing weary / being weak"), whereas other Greek witnesses read διαπονοῦντες ("striving / laboring diligently").
- **2:29** — The Greek text reads ἐνκαινίζειν ("to dedicate"), whereas other Greek witnesses read ἐγκαίειν ("to paint in encaustic").
