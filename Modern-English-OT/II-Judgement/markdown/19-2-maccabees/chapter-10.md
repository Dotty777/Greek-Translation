## Text

**10:1** Now Maccabeus and those with him, with the Lord leading them, recovered the temple and the city.

**10:2** And they tore down the altars that had been constructed in the marketplace by the foreigners, and also the sacred precincts.

**10:3** And after purifying the sanctuary, they made another altar; and striking sparks from stones and taking fire from them, they offered sacrifices after a period of two years, along with incense and lamps, and made the presentation of the loaves.

**10:4** After doing these things, falling on their bellies, they implored the Lord never again to fall into such evils, but if they should ever sin, to be dealt with by Him with gentleness, and not to be handed over to blasphemous and barbarous nations.

**10:5** Now on the day on which the sanctuary had been profaned by foreigners, it happened on that same day that the purification of the sanctuary took place, on the twenty-fifth of that same month, Chislev.

**10:6** And with gladness they observed eight days in the manner of Booths, remembering how a short time before, during the Feast of Booths, they had been living in the mountains and in the caves in the manner of wild beasts.

**10:7** Therefore, holding wreathed staffs and beautiful branches, and also palm branches, they gave thanks to Him who had granted success in purifying His own place.

**10:8** And they decreed with a common ordinance and resolution for the whole nation of the Jews to observe the tenth days every year.

**10:9** Now such were the events concerning the end of Antiochus, who was called Epiphanes.

**10:10** But now we will make known the matters concerning the supreme Antiochus, who was the son of the ungodly man, summarizing the chief evils of the cities.

**10:11** For when he took over the kingdom, he appointed a certain Lysias in charge of affairs, and chief commander of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia.

**10:12** For Ptolemy, who was called Macron, taking the lead in maintaining justice toward the Jews because of the injustice that had been done against them, tried to conduct affairs with them peacefully.

**10:13** For this reason, being accused by the Friends before Eupator and hearing himself called a traitor at every turn because he had abandoned Cyprus, which had been entrusted to him by Philometor, and gone over to Antiochus Epiphanes, and not having maintained his authority with nobility, he poisoned himself and departed life.

**10:14** Now Gorgias, having become commander of the regions, maintained mercenaries and at every turn kept up war against his own people.

**10:15** Along with these, the Idumeans also, being in control of strategic strongholds, harassed the Jews, and taking in those who had fled from Jerusalem, they attempted to keep up war.

**10:16** But Maccabeus and his men, having made supplication and imploring God to become their ally, rushed against the strongholds of the Idumeans.

**10:17** And attacking them vigorously, they gained control of the places, and drove back all those fighting on the wall, and slaughtered those who came in their way, and killed no fewer than twenty thousand.

**10:18** Now when no fewer than nine thousand had fled together into two exceedingly strong towers that had everything for a siege,

**10:19** Maccabeus, leaving behind Simon and Joseph, and also Zacchaeus and those with him, sufficient for the siege of these, departed himself for places requiring urgent attention.

**10:20** But those with Simon, becoming greedy for money, were persuaded with silver by some of those in the towers; and taking seventy thousand drachmas, they allowed some to slip away.

**10:21** And when they reported to Maccabeus about what had happened, having gathered the leaders of the people, he brought an accusation that they had sold their brothers for silver by releasing the enemies against them.

**10:22** Therefore he put to death these men who had become traitors, and immediately captured the two towers.

**10:23** And succeeding in everything with the weapons in his hands, he destroyed more than twenty thousand in the two strongholds.

**10:24** Now Timothy, who had previously been defeated by the Jews, having gathered immense foreign forces and having assembled no small number of horses from Asia, arrived intending to take Judea captive by the spear.

**10:25** But Maccabeus and his men, as he drew near, in supplication to God, having sprinkled earth on their heads and bound their waists with sackcloth,

**10:26** falling down on the step opposite the altar, implored Him to be gracious to them, to be an enemy to their enemies and to oppose those who oppose them, just as the law also declares.

**10:27** And having finished their prayer, taking up their weapons, they advanced a considerable distance from the city; and when they drew near to the enemies, they stayed by themselves.

**10:28** Just as the dawn was spreading, both sides attacked: the one side having as a guarantee of success and victory, along with valor, their refuge in the Lord, while the other side appointed fury as the leader of their struggles.

**10:29** And as a fierce battle took place, five resplendent men on horses with golden bridles appeared from heaven to the adversaries, and two of them, leading the Jews,

**10:30** both taking Maccabeus between them and shielding him with their own full armor, kept him unwounded; while at the opponents they hurled arrows and thunderbolts. Therefore, confounded with blindness, they were cut to pieces, filled with turmoil.

**10:31** And twenty thousand five hundred were slaughtered, and six hundred horsemen.

**10:32** But Timothy himself fled for refuge into a stronghold called Gazara, a very strong fortress, where Chaereas was commander.

**10:33** And Maccabeus and his men gladly besieged the fortress for forty days.

**10:34** But those inside, trusting in the strength of the place, were blaspheming exceedingly and uttering unlawful words.

**10:35** And as the twenty-fifth day was dawning, young men from among those with Maccabeus, inflamed in their fury because of the blasphemies, attacked the wall, and with manly courage and savage fury cut down everyone who came in their way.

**10:36** And others likewise, climbing up against those inside during the distraction, set fire to the towers, and kindling fires, burned the blasphemers alive; while others hacked through the gates, and having let in the rest of the force, captured the city,

**10:37** and they slaughtered Timothy, who was hidden in a certain cistern, and his brother Chaereas, and Apollophanes.

**10:38** And when they had accomplished these things, with hymns and thanksgiving they blessed the Lord, who confers great benefits on Israel and gives them the victory.

## Translator Notes

- **10:4** — The Greek text omits the infinitive "to be disciplined" (paideuesthai) found in some manuscripts after "with gentleness," leaving an elliptical clause supplied here as "to be dealt with."
- **10:8** — The Greek text reads "the tenth days" (tas dekatas hemeras), whereas other witnesses read "these days" (tasde tas hemeras) by a different word division.
- **10:10** — The Greek text reads "the supreme Antiochus" (ton hypaton, a variant for the royal epithet "Eupator" that appears in v. 13) and "of the cities" (ton poleon), where other manuscripts read "of the wars" (ton polemon).
- **10:14** — The Greek text reads "against his own people" (pros tous idious), whereas other manuscripts read "against the Jews" (pros tous Ioudaious).
- **10:33** — The Greek text reads "forty days" here and "the twenty-fifth day" in v. 35 (with no numeral before "young men"), whereas other manuscripts read "four days" here and "the fifth day, twenty young men" in v. 35.
