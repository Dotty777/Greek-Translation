## Text

**7:1** Now it also happened that seven brothers, having been arrested together with their mother, were forced by the king to partake of the unlawful pork, being tormented with whips and thongs.

**7:2** And one of them, becoming spokesman, said, "Are you intending to question and learn from us? For we are ready to die rather than to transgress ancestral laws."

**7:3** And becoming furious, the king commanded pans and cauldrons to be heated red-hot.

**7:4** And when these were heated red-hot at once, he commanded them to cut out the tongue of the one who had become their spokesman and, after scalping him, to cut off his extremities, while the rest of the brothers and their mother looked on.

**7:5** And when he had become helpless in what remained, he ordered him to be brought to the fire while still breathing and to be fried. And as the vapor from the pan spread widely, they encouraged one another together with their mother to die nobly, saying this:

**7:6** "The Lord God looks on, and in truth has compassion on us, just as Moses made clear through the song that bears witness face to face, saying, 'And He will have compassion on His servants.'"

**7:7** Now when the first had passed away in this manner, they brought the second to the mockery; and tearing off the skin of his head along with the hair, they asked, "Will you eat before your body is punished limb by limb?"

**7:8** But answering in the ancestral language, he replied, "No." Therefore also

**7:9** this one received the torture in turn, like the first. And when he was at his last breath, he said, "You indeed, accursed wretch, release us from this present life, but the King of the world will raise us up, who have died for His laws, to an eternal renewal of life."

**7:10** And after him the third was mocked, and when asked for his tongue he quickly put it out, and courageously stretched out his hands;

**7:11** and nobly he said, "From heaven these have been called, and because of His laws I disregard these, and from Him I hope to receive these again."

**7:12** So the king himself and those with him were astonished at the young man's soul, how he counted the pains as nothing.

**7:13** And when this one also had passed away, they tortured the fourth in the same way, tormenting him.

**7:14** And when he was near death, he spoke as follows: "It is preferable, when passing away at the hands of men, to look for the hopes from God of being raised up again by Him; for indeed there will be no resurrection to life for you."

**7:15** And next, bringing forward the fifth, they tormented him.

**7:16** But looking at him, he said, "Having authority among men, you do what you wish, mortal though you are; yet do not suppose that our race has been abandoned by God.

**7:17** But wait patiently, and watch His majestic power, how He will torture you and your seed."

**7:18** And after him they brought the sixth, and when he was about to die, he said, "Do not be deceived any further, for we suffer these things because of ourselves, having sinned against our own God; things worthy of astonishment have happened;

**7:19** but as for you, do not think that you will go unpunished, having attempted to fight against God."

**7:20** Now the mother was exceedingly good and worthy of good remembrance, who, looking on as seven sons perished within the span of a single day, bore it with good courage because of her hopes in the Lord.

**7:21** And she encouraged each of the men in the ancestral language, filled with noble resolve and stirring up her womanly reasoning with manly courage, saying to them:

**7:22** "I do not know how you appeared in my womb, nor was it I who granted you spirit and life, and it was not I who arranged the elements of each of you.

**7:23** Therefore the Creator of the world, who formed the origin of man and devised the origin of all things, gives back to you both spirit and life again with mercy, just as you now disregard yourselves because of His laws."

**7:24** Now Antiochus, thinking he was being held in contempt and suspecting the reproachful voice, while the youngest was still alive, not only made his appeal through words, but also assured him with oaths that he would at once make him rich and enviable, if indeed he turned away from the ancestral customs, and that he would have him as a friend and entrust him with offices.

**7:25** But since the young man paid no attention at all, the king summoned the mother and urged her to become an adviser to the youth for his safety.

**7:26** And after he had urged her at length, she agreed to persuade her son.

**7:27** And leaning down toward him, deriding the cruel tyrant, she spoke as follows in the ancestral language: "Son, have mercy on me, who carried you in my womb for nine months, and nursed you for three years, and reared you and brought you to this age and nurtured you.

**7:28** I implore you, child, to look up to heaven and earth and, seeing all things in them, to recognize that God did not make them out of things that exist, and that the race of men comes into being in this way.

**7:29** Do not fear this executioner, but proving worthy of your brothers, accept death, so that in that mercy I may receive you back together with your brothers."

**7:30** While she was still concluding, the young man said, "What are you waiting for? I do not obey the command of the king, but I heed the command of the law that was given to our fathers through Moses.

**7:31** And you, who have become the contriver of every evil against the Hebrews, will by no means escape the hands of God.

**7:32** For we are suffering because of our own sins;

**7:33** and if for the sake of rebuke and discipline our living Lord has become angry for a little while, He will yet again be reconciled with His own servants.

**7:34** But you, unholy and most vile of all men, do not lift yourself up in vain, acting insolently with uncertain hopes, having raised your hand against the heavenly children;

**7:35** for you have not yet escaped the judgment of the Almighty, all-seeing God.

**7:36** For our brothers now, having endured a brief pain, have fallen under God's covenant of everlasting life; but you, by the judgment of God, will receive the just penalties for your arrogance.

**7:37** And I, just as my brothers did, give up both body and fortune for the ancestral laws, as we call upon God quickly to become gracious to the nation, and for you, with trials and whips, to confess that He alone is God,

**7:38** and in me and my brothers to halt the wrath of the Almighty, which has been justly brought upon our whole race."

**7:39** And becoming furious, the king dealt with this one most cruelly, beyond the others, bitterly resenting the derision.

**7:40** So this one also passed away undefiled, trusting completely in the Lord.

**7:41** And last, after her sons, the mother died.

**7:42** Now then, let the matters concerning the sacrificial meals and the excessive torments be set forth to this extent.

## Translator Notes

- **7:11** — The Greek text reads "have been called" (kekletai), whereas many manuscripts read "I have acquired" or "received" (kektemai).
- **7:18** — The Greek text reads "any further" (mallon), where other manuscripts read "in vain" (maten).
- **7:21** — The Greek text reads "each of the men" (ton anthropon), whereas other manuscripts read "each of them" (auton).
- **7:28** — Literally "not out of things that exist" (ouk ex onton), a phrase frequently discussed in relation to creation out of nothing.
- **7:37** — The Greek text reads "fortune" (tychen) and the plural participle "as we call upon" (epikaloumenoi), whereas other manuscripts read "soul" (psychen) and the singular participle (epikaloumenos).
