## Text

**3:1** While the holy city was inhabited in complete peace, and the laws were kept most excellently because of the piety and hatred of wickedness of Onias the high priest,

**3:2** it came about that even the kings themselves honored the place and glorified the temple with the finest gifts,

**3:3** so that even Seleucus, the king of Asia, supplied from his own revenues all the expenses falling due for the services of the sacrifices.

**3:4** But a certain Simon from the tribe of Benjamin, who had been appointed overseer of the temple, had a dispute with the high priest concerning the administration of the market in the city.

**3:5** And being unable to overcome Onias, he went to Apollonius son of Thraseas, who at that time was governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia.

**3:6** And he reported that the treasury in Jerusalem was full of untold money, so that the amount of the funds was beyond counting, and that they did not contribute to the account of the sacrifices, and that it was possible for these to fall under the authority of the king.

**3:7** When Apollonius met with the king, he informed him about the money that had been disclosed to him; and the king, having chosen Heliodorus, who was in charge of state affairs, sent him, giving orders to carry out the removal of the aforementioned money.

**3:8** Immediately Heliodorus set out on the journey, in appearance as though to inspect the cities throughout Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, but in reality to carry out the purpose of the king.

**3:9** When he arrived in Jerusalem and was warmly received by the high priest of the city, he told him about the information that had been given and made clear for what reason he was present; and he inquired whether in truth these things actually were so.

**3:10** When the high priest explained that these were deposits of widows and orphans,

**3:11** and that some also belonged to Hyrcanus son of Tobias, a man of very high standing (not as the impious Simon was falsely reporting), and that in all there were four hundred talents of silver and two hundred of gold,

**3:12** and that it was altogether impossible to wrong those who had trusted in the holiness of the place and in the majesty and inviolability of the temple honored throughout the entire world,

**3:13** the other man, because of the royal orders he had, kept saying that these must by all means be taken into the royal treasury.

**3:14** Having appointed a day, he went in to direct the inspection concerning these things; and there was no small anguish throughout the whole city.

**3:15** And the priests, throwing themselves down before the altar in their priestly robes, called out toward heaven to the One who gave the law concerning deposits to keep these safe for those who had deposited them.

**3:16** And to see the appearance of the high priest was to be wounded in mind; for his face and his altered color revealed the anguish in his soul.

**3:17** For a certain dread and shuddering of body had enveloped the man, through which the pain present in his heart became clearly evident to those looking on.

**3:18** And people were rushing out of their houses in crowds for public supplication, so that the place would not be about to come into contempt.

**3:19** And the women, with sackcloth wrapped beneath their breasts, thronged along the streets; and of the virgins who were kept indoors, some ran together to the gates, others to the walls, and some peered out through the windows.

**3:20** And all of them, stretching out their hands toward heaven, were making their petition.

**3:21** And one had to pity the mingled prostration of the multitude and the suspense of the greatly anguished high priest.

**3:22** So they were calling upon the almighty Lord to keep the things entrusted safe for those who had entrusted them, with complete security.

**3:23** But Heliodorus was carrying out what had been decided.

**3:24** Now when he was already present right there with his bodyguards by the treasury, the Sovereign of spirits and of all authority made a great manifestation, so that all who had dared to come along, struck with terror at the power of God, were turned to faintness and cowardice.

**3:25** For a horse appeared to them, having a terrifying rider and adorned with the finest trappings; and rushing fiercely, it struck at Heliodorus with its front hooves, while the one seated upon it appeared to have full armor of gold.

**3:26** And two other young men appeared to him, remarkable in strength, finest in speech, and splendid in apparel; and standing on either side, they flogged him unceasingly, inflicting many blows upon him.

**3:27** And when he suddenly fell to the ground and was enveloped in deep darkness, they snatched him up and placed him in a litter,

**3:28** and they carried away the man who had just entered the aforementioned treasury with a great retinue and all his bodyguard, him who had now become completely helpless and had clearly recognized the sovereign power.

**3:29** And because of the divine working he lay cast down, speechless and deprived of all hope and deliverance;

**3:30** while they were blessing the Lord, who marvelously glorifies His own place. And the temple, which a little before was full of dread and turmoil, now that the Lord Almighty had appeared, was filled with joy and gladness.

**3:31** Quickly some of Heliodorus's companions begged Onias to call upon the Most High and to grant life to the one lying altogether at his last breath.

**3:32** And the high priest, becoming apprehensive that the king might form a suspicion that some foul play had been perpetrated against Heliodorus by the Jews, offered a sacrifice for the man's deliverance.

**3:33** While the high priest was making the atonement, the same young men appeared again to Heliodorus, dressed in the same garments, and standing there, they said, "Give great thanks to Onias the high priest, for because of him the Lord has granted you life.

**3:34** And you, having been flogged from heaven, declare to all the majestic might of God." And having said these things, they vanished from sight.

**3:35** Then Heliodorus, having offered a sacrifice to God, and having vowed the greatest vows to the One who had preserved his life, and having warmly acknowledged Onias, marched back with his forces to the king;

**3:36** and he testified openly to all about the deeds of the supreme God that he had seen before his eyes.

**3:37** When the king asked Heliodorus what sort of man would be suitable to be sent once more to Jerusalem, he said,

**3:38** "If you have any enemy or plotter against state affairs, send him there, and you will receive him back flogged, if indeed he even survives, because there is truly a certain power of God around the place.

**3:39** For He Himself who has His dwelling in heaven is the watcher and helper of that place, and He strikes and destroys those who come to do harm."

**3:40** And the events concerning Heliodorus and the preservation of the treasury turned out in this way.

## Translator Notes

- **3:11** — The Greek text contains an elliptical participial clause ("οὐχ οὕτως διαβάλλων ὁ δυσσεβὴς Σίμων", lit. "not thus falsely accusing, the impious Simon"), contrasting Onias's account with Simon's report; other manuscripts read "οὕτως δ' ἦν διαβάλλων" ("thus the impious Simon was slandering") or "οὐχ ὡς διέβαλλεν" ("not as ... was slandering").
- **3:18** — The Greek text retains the negative "μή" ("διὰ τὸ μὴ μέλλειν"), giving a purpose sense ("so that the place would not be about to come into contempt"), whereas some manuscripts omit "μή" ("because the place was about to come into contempt").
- **3:21** — The Greek text has the bare infinitive "ἐλεεῖν δέ" (lit. "and to pity..."), rendered "And one had to pity...", whereas other manuscripts read "ἐλεεινὸν δ' ἦν" ("and it was pitiable").
- **3:26** — The Greek text reads "κάλλιστοι δὲ τὴν γλῶσσαν" ("finest in speech" or "in tongue"), where other manuscripts read "τὴν δόξαν" ("in splendor" or "in glory").
- **3:28** — The Greek text uses the accusative singular participle "ἐπεγνωκότα" (referring to Heliodorus) and reads simply "τὴν δυναστίαν" ("the sovereign power"), whereas other manuscripts read the plural "ἐπεγνωκότες" and add "τοῦ θεοῦ" ("of God").
