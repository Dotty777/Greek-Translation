## Text

**12:1** Now when these agreements had been made, Lysias departed to the king, and the Jews occupied themselves with farming.

**12:2** But among the local governors, Timothy and Apollonius the son of Gennaeus, and also Hieronymus and Demophon, and in addition to these Nicanor the Cypriarch, did not allow them to remain settled and live in quietness.

**12:3** And the people of Joppa committed so great an act of impiety, inviting the Jews living among them to board the boats provided by them, together with their wives and children, as though no hostility existed toward them;

**12:4** and after the public decree of the city, when these also had accepted, as people wanting to live in peace and having no suspicion, once they had been led out, they drowned them, being no fewer than two hundred.

**12:5** Now when Judas learned of the cruelty that had been done to his fellow countrymen, he gave orders to the men with him,

**12:6** and calling upon God, the righteous Judge, he came against the murderers of his brothers, set the harbor on fire by night, burned up the boats, and stabbed to death those who had fled there for refuge.

**12:7** Because the town was shut up, he withdrew, intending to come again and uproot the entire citizenry of the people of Joppa.

**12:8** But when he learned that those in Jamnia also wanted to deal in the same way with the Jews living there,

**12:9** he attacked the people of Jamnia by night as well and set fire to the harbor together with the fleet, so that the glow of the light was visible as far as Jerusalem, a distance of two hundred and forty stadia.

**12:10** And when they had marched away nine stadia from there, as they were making their march against Timothy, Arabs attacked him, no fewer than five thousand, and five hundred horsemen.

**12:11** Now when a fierce battle took place, and Judas and his men succeeded through the help from God, the nomads, having been defeated, asked Judas to give them his right hand, promising both to give livestock and to benefit them in other respects.

**12:12** And Judas, supposing that they would truly be useful in many ways, yielded to keep peace with them; and having received right-hand pledges, they departed to their tents.

**12:13** He also attacked a certain city built with causeways, fortified and enclosed with walls, and inhabited by mixed nations; its name was Caspin.

**12:14** And those inside, trusting in the strength of the walls and their store of food, treated Judas and his men quite insolently, reviling them, and moreover blaspheming and speaking things that are not permissible.

**12:15** But Judas and his men, calling upon the great Sovereign of the world, who without battering rams and siege engines cast Jericho down in the times of Joshua, charged ferociously against the wall.

**12:16** And capturing the city by the will of God, they carried out an untold slaughter, so that the adjacent lake, having a width of two stadia, seemed to stream with blood and to be filled.

**12:17** And marching away seven hundred and fifty stadia from there, they made their way to the stockade and to the Jews called Tubieni.

**12:18** And they did not overtake Timothy in those places, for he had withdrawn from those places at that time without accomplishing anything, though he had left behind a very strong garrison in a certain place.

**12:19** But Dositheus and Sosipater, among the commanders with Maccabeus, marched out and destroyed those who had been left by Timothy in the stronghold, more than ten thousand men.

**12:20** And Maccabeus, having organized the army with him into divisions, set them over the divisions; and they advanced rapidly against Timothy, who had with him one hundred and twenty thousand foot soldiers, and two thousand horsemen in addition to the seven thousand.

**12:21** Now when Timothy learned of the advance of Judas, he sent ahead the women and the rest of the baggage to the place called Carnion; for the place was hard to besiege and difficult to approach because of the narrowness of all the places.

**12:22** And when Judas's division appeared first, and terror fell upon the enemies, and fear came upon them from the appearing of the One who oversees all things, they rushed into flight, one running one way and another another, so that they were often injured by their own men and impaled on the points of their swords.

**12:23** And Judas pressed the pursuit all the more vigorously, stabbing the wicked wretches, and he destroyed about thirty thousand men.

**12:24** And Timothy himself, falling among Dositheus and Sosipater and their men, begged them with great cunning to let him go in safety, because he held the parents of many and the brothers of others, and it would happen that these would be shown no regard.

**12:25** And when he had confirmed with many assurances his pledge to restore them unharmed, they released him for the sake of the safety of their brothers.

**12:26** Then marching out against Carnion and the temple of Atargatis, he slaughtered twenty-five thousand persons.

**12:27** And after the rout and destruction of these, he also campaigned against Ephron, a fortified city in which there were multitudes of all tribes; and strong young men stationed before the walls fought back vigorously, where there were abundant stores of war engines and missiles.

**12:28** But calling upon the Sovereign who with might shatters the forces of the enemies, they brought the city under their power, and struck down about twenty-five thousand of those inside.

**12:29** And breaking camp from there, they pushed on toward Scythopolis, which is six hundred stadia from Jerusalem.

**12:30** But when the Jews settled there testified to the goodwill that the people of Scythopolis had toward them, and their gentle treatment in the times of misfortune,

**12:31** they gave thanks and further urged them to remain favorably disposed toward the nation in the future as well; then they came to Jerusalem, since the Feast of Weeks was close at hand.

**12:32** And after what is called Pentecost, they pushed on against Gorgias, the governor of Idumea.

**12:33** And he came out with three thousand foot soldiers and four hundred horsemen.

**12:34** And when they drew up for battle, it happened that a few of the Jews fell.

**12:35** But a certain Dositheus, one of Bacenor's men, a strong horseman, kept hold of Gorgias, and seizing his cloak, was dragging him vigorously; and while he wanted to take the accursed man alive, one of the Thracian horsemen rushed upon him and cut off his shoulder, so Gorgias escaped to Marisa.

**12:36** Now when Esdris and his men had been fighting for a long time and were exhausted, Judas called upon the Lord to appear as their ally and to appear as leader of the battle;

**12:37** and beginning in the ancestral language the cry with hymns, he charged unexpectedly against Gorgias and his men and put them to rout.

**12:38** Then Judas gathered the army and came to the city of Adullam; and as the seventh day was coming on, after they had purified themselves according to custom, he spent the Sabbath there.

**12:39** On the next day, Judas and his men came, as by that time the need had arisen, to recover the bodies of those who had fallen and to bring them back with their relatives to the ancestral tombs.

**12:40** And under the tunics of each of the dead they found sacred tokens of the idols from Jamnia, from which the law barred the Jews; and it became clear to all that for this reason these men had fallen.

**12:41** Therefore, all of them, blessing the ways of the Lord, the righteous Judge, who makes hidden things manifest,

**12:42** turned to supplication, asking that the sin that had been committed might be completely blotted out. And the noble Judas urged the multitude to keep themselves free from sin, since they had seen before their eyes what had happened because of the sin of those who had fallen.

**12:43** And having made a collection man by man amounting to two thousand drachmas of silver, he sent it to Jerusalem to offer a sacrifice for sin, acting very well and honorably as he took resurrection into account.

**12:44** For if he had not expected that those who had fallen would rise again, it would have been superfluous and foolish to pray for the dead.

**12:45** Or if he was looking to the finest gracious reward laid up for those who fall asleep in godliness, the thought was holy and godly. Therefore he made the atonement for those who had died, that they might be released from the sin.

## Translator Notes

- **12:13** — The Greek word gephyroun ('built with causeways/bridges') is textually difficult; other manuscripts and editors read 'with bridges/earthworks' or interpret it as a place name, Gephyrun.
- **12:20** — The Greek text reads literally 'two thousand and in addition to the seven thousand,' whereas other Greek manuscripts read 'two thousand five hundred.'
- **12:27** — Unlike some Greek manuscripts that mention Lysias dwelling in Ephron, the Greek text translated here mentions only the multitudes of all tribes in the city.
- **12:28** — The Greek text reads holkas (literally 'weights' or 'forces'), an early manuscript variant for alkas ('strength' or 'might').
- **12:45** — The Greek text reads eite ('or if') and eucharisterion (literally 'thank-offering' or 'gracious reward'), a variant for charisterion ('reward').
