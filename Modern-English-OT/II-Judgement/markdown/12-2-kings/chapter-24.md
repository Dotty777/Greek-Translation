## Text

**24:1** In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon went up, and Jehoiakim became his servant for three years; then he turned and rebelled against him.

**24:2** And he sent against him the raiders of the Chaldeans and the raiders of Syria and the raiders of Moab and the raiders of the sons of Ammon, and sent them out into the land of Judah to prevail, according to the word of the Lord that He spoke by the hand of His servants the prophets.

**24:3** However, it was because of the wrath of the Lord against Judah, to remove it from His presence for the sins of Manasseh, according to all that he had done.

**24:4** And also he shed innocent blood and filled Jerusalem with innocent blood, and the Lord was not willing to be appeased.

**24:5** And the rest of the acts of Jehoiakim and all that he did—look, are these not written in the book of the records of the days of the kings of Judah?

**24:6** And Jehoiakim slept with his fathers, and Jehoiakim his son became king in his place.

**24:7** And the king of Egypt did not again go out from his land, for the king of Babylon had taken, from the brook of Egypt to the Euphrates River, all that belonged to the king of Egypt.

**24:8** Jehoiakim was eighteen years old when he became king, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem; and his mother's name was Nehushta, daughter of Elnathan from Jerusalem.

**24:9** And he did what was evil in the eyes of the Lord, according to all that his father had done.

**24:10** At that time Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon went up to Jerusalem, and the city came under siege.

**24:11** And Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon entered into the city, and his servants were besieging it.

**24:12** And Jehoiakim king of Judah went out to the king of Babylon, he and his servants and his mother and his officials and his eunuchs; and the king of Babylon took him in the eighth year of his reign.

**24:13** And he brought out from there all the treasures of the house of the Lord and the treasures of the house of the king, and cut in pieces all the golden vessels that Solomon king of Israel had made in the temple of the Lord, according to the word of the Lord.

**24:14** And he exiled Jerusalem and all the officials and the mighty in strength, capturing ten thousand captives, and every craftsman and the locksmith; and no one was left except the poor of the land.

**24:15** And he exiled Jehoiakim to Babylon, and the king's mother and the king's wives and his eunuchs; and the strong of the land he led away into exile from Jerusalem to Babylon.

**24:16** And all the men of might, seven thousand, and the craftsman and the locksmith, one thousand, all of them mighty, making war—the king of Babylon brought them into deportation to Babylon.

**24:17** And the king of Babylon made Matthan his son king in his place, and set his name as Zedekiah.

**24:18** Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem; and his mother's name was Mitat, daughter of Jeremiah.

**24:19** And he did what was evil before the Lord, according to all that Jehoiakim had done.

**24:20** For it was because of the wrath of the Lord upon Jerusalem and in Judah, until He cast them away from His presence. And Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

## Translator Notes

- **24:2** — Greek omits an explicit subject ("the Lord" in Hebrew) for "sent," leaving Nebuchadnezzar as the apparent grammatical subject, and reads "to prevail" (Hebrew "to destroy it").
- **24:3** — Greek reads "because of the wrath of the Lord" (Hebrew "at the command of the Lord"), matching the wording of v. 20.
- **24:6** — Here and in vv. 8, 12, and 15, the Greek text names the new king "Jehoiakim," identical to his father's name, whereas the Hebrew distinguishes the son as "Jehoiachin."
- **24:10** — Greek states that Nebuchadnezzar himself went up to Jerusalem (omitting "the servants of" found in Hebrew) and in v. 11 that he "entered into the city" (Hebrew "came against the city").
- **24:17** — Greek reads "Matthan his son" (Hebrew "Mattaniah his uncle"). In v. 18, Greek names Zedekiah's mother "Mitat" (Hebrew "Hamutal," compare "Hamitai" in 23:31) and omits "from Libnah."
