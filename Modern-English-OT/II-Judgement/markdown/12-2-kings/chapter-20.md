## Text

**20:1** In those days Hezekiah became ill to the point of death. And Isaiah son of Amoz the prophet came in to him and said to him, "Thus says the Lord: Give orders to your house; you are dying and will not live."

**20:2** And Hezekiah turned toward the wall, and prayed to the Lord, saying,

**20:3** "Please, O Lord, remember now how I have walked before You in truth and with a full heart, and have done Your good in Your eyes." And Hezekiah wept with a great weeping.

**20:4** And Isaiah was in the middle court, and the word of the Lord came to him, saying,

**20:5** "Return, and you will say to Hezekiah, the leader of My people, 'This is what the Lord, the God of David your father, says: I have heard your prayer; I have seen your tears. Look, I will heal you; on the third day you will go up to the house of the Lord.

**20:6** And I will add fifteen years to your days, and from the hand of the kings of the Assyrians I will save you and this city; and I will shield this city for My own sake and for the sake of David My servant.'"

**20:7** And he said, "Let them take a cake of figs and put it on the boil, and he will recover."

**20:8** And Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "What is the sign that the Lord will heal me, and that I will go up to the house of the Lord on the third day?"

**20:9** And Isaiah said, "This is the sign from the Lord that the Lord will do the word that He has spoken: the shadow will go ten steps, if it returns ten steps."

**20:10** And Hezekiah said, "It is a light thing for the shadow to decline ten steps; no, rather let the shadow return on the stairway ten steps backward."

**20:11** And Isaiah the prophet cried out to the Lord, and the shadow returned on the stairway ten steps backward.

**20:12** At that time Merodach-baladan son of Balaan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah, for he had heard that Hezekiah had been ill.

**20:13** And Hezekiah rejoiced over them, and he showed them the entire house of nechotha, the silver and the gold, the spices and the good oil, and the house of the vessels, and all that was found in his treasuries; there was not a thing that Hezekiah did not show them in his house and in all his dominion.

**20:14** And Isaiah the prophet came in to King Hezekiah and said to him, "What did these men say, and from where have they come to you?" And Hezekiah said, "From a distant land they have come to me, from Babylon."

**20:15** And he said, "What did they see in your house?" And he said, "All that is in my house they saw; there was nothing in my house that I did not show them, and even the things in my treasuries."

**20:16** And Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Hear the word of the Lord:

**20:17** Look, days are coming when everything that is in your house and all that your fathers have stored up until this day will be taken to Babylon; and not a word that the Lord has spoken will be left behind.

**20:18** And your sons who will come forth from you, whom you will father, he will take, and they will be eunuchs in the house of the king of Babylon."

**20:19** And Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "Good is the word of the Lord that He has spoken."

**20:20** And the rest of the matters of Hezekiah, and all his might, and all that he made, the spring and the aqueduct, and brought the water into the city, are these not written in the book of the records of the days of the kings of Judah?

**20:21** And Hezekiah slept with his fathers, and Manasseh his son became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **20:3** — Greek adds the possessive in 'Your good in Your eyes'; in 20:4 it states simply 'Isaiah was in the middle court' rather than Hebrew 'had not gone out of the middle court/city.'
- **20:7** — Greek omits Isaiah's name and makes the entire instruction part of his spoken command ('and put it on the boil, and he will recover') rather than narrative past action.
- **20:9** — Greek renders Hebrew im ('or' in an alternative question) literally as ean ('if'), forming a conditional statement; in 20:11 'the shadow' is the grammatical subject and the reference to the steps of Ahaz is absent.
- **20:12** — Greek reads Merodach-baladan son of Balaan (MT Berodach-baladan son of Baladan) and uses the Hebrew loanword manaan (minchah, 'gift'); in 20:13 Greek leaves the obscure Hebrew term nekhot untranslated as nechotha, likely referring to a royal storehouse or treasury.
- **20:17** — Greek adds the relative pronoun 'that' (ho), reading 'not a word that the Lord has spoken will be left behind' (MT 'nothing shall be left, says the Lord'); in 20:19 Greek reads 'He has spoken' and omits Hezekiah's following remark about peace and truth in his days.
