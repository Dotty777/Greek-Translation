## Text

**5:1** Now Naaman, the commander of the army of Syria, was a great man before his master and admired in person, for by him the Lord had given deliverance to Syria; and the man was mighty in strength, a leper.

**5:2** And Syria went out lightly armed and took captive from the land of Israel a little girl, and she was before the wife of Naaman.

**5:3** And she said to her mistress, "If only my master were before the prophet of God who is in Samaria, then he would cure him from his leprosy."

**5:4** And she went in and reported to her lord and said, "Thus and thus spoke the girl who is from the land of Israel."

**5:5** And the king of Syria said to Naaman, "Go, enter, and I will send out a letter to the king of Israel." And he went and took in his hand ten talents of silver and six thousand gold pieces and ten changes of robes.

**5:6** And he brought the letter to the king of Israel, saying, "And now, when this letter comes to you, look, I have sent to you Naaman my servant, and you shall cure him from his leprosy."

**5:7** And it happened, when the king of Israel read the letter, that he tore his garments and said, "Am I God, to put to death and to make alive, that this man sends to me to cure a man from his leprosy? For only know now and see that this man is seeking a pretext against me."

**5:8** And it happened, when Elisha heard that the king of Israel had torn his own garments, that he sent to the king of Israel, saying, "Why have you torn your garments? Let Naaman now come to me, and let him know that there is a prophet in Israel."

**5:9** And Naaman came with horse and chariot, and he stood at the door of the house of Elisha.

**5:10** And Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying, "Go, wash yourself seven times in the Jordan, and your flesh shall return to you, and you shall be cleansed."

**5:11** And Naaman became angry and went away and said, "Look, I said, 'He will surely come out to me and call upon the name of his God, and place his hand upon the place, and cure the leprous spot.'

**5:12** Are not Abana and Pharpar, rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? Could I not go and wash myself in them and be cleansed?" And he turned aside and went away in anger.

**5:13** And his servants came near and spoke to him, "Had the prophet spoken a great word to you, would you not do it? And how much more when he said to you, 'Wash yourself and be cleansed'!"

**5:14** And Naaman went down and immersed himself in the Jordan seven times, according to the word of Elisha; and his flesh returned like the flesh of a little boy, and he was cleansed.

**5:15** And he returned to Elisha, he and all his company, and he came and stood and said, "Look, I have come to know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel; and now, take the blessing from your servant."

**5:16** And Elisha said, "As the Lord lives, before whom I stand, I will certainly not take it." And he urged him strongly to take it, and he refused.

**5:17** And Naaman said, "And if not, let there now be given to your servant a gomor, pairs of mules; for your servant will no longer make a burnt offering and a sacrifice to other gods, except to the Lord.

**5:18** In this matter may the Lord be merciful to your servant: when my master enters into the house of Rimmon to bow down there, and he leans upon my hand, and I bow down in the house of Rimmon when he bows down in the house of Rimmon, may the Lord now be merciful to your servant in this matter."

**5:19** And Elisha said to Naaman, "Go in peace." And he went away from him to a debratha of the land.

**5:20** And Gehazi the servant of Elisha said, "Look, my master has spared this Naaman the Syrian, by not taking from his hand what he has brought. As the Lord lives, I will surely run after him and take something from him."

**5:21** And Gehazi pursued after Naaman. And Naaman saw him running after him, and he turned back from the chariot to meet him.

**5:22** And he said, "Peace. My master has sent me, saying, 'Look, just now two young men have come to me from Mount Ephraim, from the sons of the prophets; give now to them a talent of silver and two changes of robes.'"

**5:23** And Naaman said, "Take two talents of silver." And he took it in two bags, and two changes of robes, and he placed them upon two of his servants, and they carried them before him.

**5:24** And they came to the dark place, and he took them from their hands and stored them away in a house, and he sent the men away.

**5:25** And he himself went in and stood beside his master. And Elisha said to him, "From where, Gehazi?" And Gehazi said, "Your servant has not gone here and there."

**5:26** And Elisha said to him, "Did not my heart go with you when the man turned back from the chariot to meet you? And now you have taken the silver, and now you have taken the garments and olive groves and vineyards and sheep and oxen and male servants and female servants;

**5:27** and the leprosy of Naaman shall cling to you and to your offspring forever." And he went out from his presence leprous like snow.

## Translator Notes

- **5:4** — The Greek has the feminine reflexive pronoun heautes ("her lord"), making Naaman's wife the one who reports to her husband rather than Naaman reporting to the Syrian king as in the Hebrew.
- **5:17** — The Greek reads "a gomor, pairs of mules" (gomor, zeuge hemionon), using the dry measure gomor (possibly a variant for gomos, "load") and omitting the Hebrew reference to earth or soil.
- **5:19** — "Debratha" is an untranslated Greek transliteration of a Hebrew term for a stretch of distance (kibrat, apparently read with d for k).
- **5:24** — The Greek reads "to the dark place" (eis to skoteinon), rendering the Hebrew word for a hill or citadel (ha-opel) as though from a similar root meaning darkness.
- **5:26** — Instead of the Hebrew question ("Is it a time to accept silver...?"), the Greek reads declarative statements ("And now you have taken the silver, and now you have taken the garments..."), reading "time" as "now" and the infinitive as a second-person verb.
