## Text

**6:1** And the sons of the prophets said to Elisha, "Look now, the place in which we live before you is too narrow for us.

**6:2** Let us go now as far as the Jordan, and let us take from there each man one beam, and let us build for ourselves there to live there." And he said, "Go."

**6:3** And one said, "Please, come with your servants." And he said, "I will go."

**6:4** And he went with them, and they came to the Jordan and were cutting the trees.

**6:5** And look, as one was felling the beam, the iron fell off into the water; and he cried out, "Oh, master! And it is hidden!"

**6:6** And the man of God said, "Where did it fall?" And he showed him the place; and he broke off a piece of wood and threw it there, and the iron floated.

**6:7** And he said, "Lift it up for yourself." And he stretched out his hand and took it.

**6:8** And the king of Syria was waging war against Israel, and he took counsel with his servants, saying, "At such and such a place, Elimoni, I will encamp."

**6:9** And Elisha sent to the king of Israel, saying, "Be on your guard not to pass through this place, for Syria is hidden there."

**6:10** And the king of Israel sent to the place of which Elisha had told him, and he was on his guard from there not once nor twice.

**6:11** And the soul of the king of Syria was agitated concerning this matter, and he called his servants and said to them, "Will you not tell me who is betraying me to the king of Israel?"

**6:12** And one of his servants said, "No, my lord the king, for Elisha the prophet who is in Israel tells the king of Israel all the words that you speak in the inner chamber of your bedroom."

**6:13** And he said, "Go, see where he is, and I will send and seize him." And they told him, saying, "Look, he is in Dothan."

**6:14** And he sent there horses and chariots and a heavy force, and they came by night and surrounded the city.

**6:15** And the attendant of Elisha woke early to get up and went out, and look, a force was surrounding the city, and horses and chariots; and the young man said to him, "Oh, master! What shall we do?"

**6:16** And Elisha said, "Do not be afraid, for those who are with us are more than those who are with them."

**6:17** And Elisha prayed and said, "Lord, open the eyes of the young man, and let him see." And the Lord opened his eyes, and he saw; and look, the mountain was full of horses, and chariots of fire were all around Elisha.

**6:18** And they came down to him; and he prayed to the Lord and said, "Please strike this nation with blindness." And He struck them with blindness according to the word of Elisha.

**6:19** And Elisha said to them, "This is not the city, nor is this the way; come after me, and I will lead you to the man whom you are seeking." And he led them away to Samaria.

**6:20** And it happened, when they entered into Samaria, that Elisha said, "Please open, Lord, their eyes, and let them see." And He opened their eyes, and they saw; and look, they were in the middle of Samaria.

**6:21** And the king of Israel said, when he saw them, "Shall I surely strike, father?"

**6:22** And he said, "You shall not strike, unless you yourself strike those whom you took captive with your sword and your bow; set loaves of bread and water before them, and let them eat and drink, and let them depart to their master."

**6:23** And he set before them a great feast, and they ate and drank; and he sent them away to their master. And raiding troops of Syria no longer continued to come into the land of Israel.

**6:24** And it happened after these things that Son of Hader, king of Syria, gathered all his army and went up, and they besieged Samaria.

**6:25** And there was a great famine in Samaria, and look, they were besieging it until a donkey's head came to be fifty pieces of silver, and a quarter of a kab of doves' dung, five pieces of silver.

**6:26** And the king of Israel was passing along upon the wall, and a woman cried out to him, saying, "Save me, lord king!"

**6:27** And he said to her, "May the Lord not save you! From where shall I save you? Surely not from a threshing floor or from a winepress?"

**6:28** And the king said to her, "What is the matter with you?" And the woman said, "This woman said to me, 'Give your son, and we will eat him today; and my son, we will eat him tomorrow.'

**6:29** And we boiled my son and ate him, and I said to her on the second day, 'Give your son, and let us eat him.' And she hid her son."

**6:30** And it happened, when the king heard the words of the woman, he tore his garments; and he was passing along upon the wall, and the people saw the sackcloth on his flesh underneath.

**6:31** And he said, "May God do so to me, and so may He add, if the head of Elisha remains on him today."

**6:32** And Elisha was sitting in his house, and the elders were sitting with him; and he sent a man ahead of him. Before the messenger came to him, he said to the elders, "Did you know that the son of the murderer has sent to take off my head? Look, when the messenger comes, shut the door and press him back at the door. Is not the sound of his master's feet behind him?"

**6:33** While he was still speaking with them, look, a messenger came down to him and said, "Look, this evil is from the Lord; why should I wait for the Lord any longer?"

## Translator Notes

- **6:5** — The Greek reads "hidden" (kekrymmenon), likely an early inner-Greek shift from kechremenon ("borrowed," matching the Hebrew).
- **6:8** — "Elimoni" is a Greek transliteration of the Hebrew indefinite term almoni ("unnamed" or "such and such"), placed alongside the translated phrase "such and such."
- **6:24** — "Son of Hader" renders the Greek huios Hader, which translates the Ben- element ("son of") and reflects a resh/dalet variant of the Hebrew name Ben-hadad.
- **6:25** — The Greek gives the price of a donkey's head as fifty pieces of silver, whereas the Hebrew reads eighty.
- **6:27** — The Greek uses a negative optative (me se sosai Kyrios, "May the Lord not save you!"), expressing a negative wish rather than a conditional clause ("If the Lord does not save you").
