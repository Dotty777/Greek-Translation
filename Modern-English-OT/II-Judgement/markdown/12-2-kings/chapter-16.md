## Text

**16:1** In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of Jotham, king of Judah, became king.

**16:2** Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem; and he did not do what was right in the eyes of the Lord God faithfully, like David his father.

**16:3** And he walked in the way of the king of Israel, and indeed he led his son through fire, and the abominations of the nations that the Lord removed from before the sons of Israel.

**16:4** And he sacrificed and burned incense at the high places and on the hills and under every leafy tree.

**16:5** Then Rezin king of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up to Jerusalem for war, and they laid siege against Ahaz, and they were not able to fight.

**16:6** At that time Rezin king of Syria restored Elath to Syria, and drove out the Judeans from Elath; and the Idumeans came to Elath and lived there to this day.

**16:7** And Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser king of the Assyrians, saying, "I am your servant and your son. Come up, save me from the hand of the king of Syria and from the hand of the king of Israel, who are rising up against me."

**16:8** And Ahaz took silver and gold that was found in the treasuries of the house of the Lord and of the house of the king, and sent gifts to the king.

**16:9** And the king of the Assyrians listened to him; and the king of the Assyrians went up to Damascus, and captured it and deported it, and put Rezin to death.

**16:10** And King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet him, Tiglath-pileser king of the Assyrians, and he saw the altar in Damascus; and King Ahaz sent to Uriah the priest the likeness of the altar and its design for all its construction.

**16:11** And Uriah the priest built the altar according to all that King Ahaz sent from Damascus.

**16:12** And the king saw the altar, and went up onto it,

**16:13** and offered up in smoke his burnt offering and his sacrifice and his drink offering, and poured out the blood of his peace offerings upon the altar

**16:14** of bronze that was before the Lord; and he brought near the front of the house of the Lord from between the altar and from between the house of the Lord, and showed it on the side of the altar toward the north.

**16:15** And King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest, saying, "Upon the great altar offer the morning burnt offering and the evening sacrifice, and the burnt offering of the king and his sacrifice, and the burnt offering of all the people and their sacrifice and their drink offering; and all the blood of a burnt offering and all the blood of a sacrifice you shall pour out upon it; upon the bronze altar it shall be for me for the morning."

**16:16** And Uriah the priest did according to all that King Ahaz commanded him.

**16:17** And the king cut off the panels of the stands and removed the basin from them, and he took down the sea from the bronze oxen that were underneath it and put it upon a stone base.

**16:18** And the foundation of the seat he built in the house of the Lord, and the outer entrance of the king he turned to the house of the Lord because of the king of the Assyrians.

**16:19** And the rest of the acts of Ahaz, all that he did, are these not written in the book of the records of the days of the kings of Judah?

**16:20** And Ahaz slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David; and Hezekiah his son became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **16:3** — The Greek reads singular "king of Israel" and "and the abominations" rather than "kings of Israel" and "according to the abominations" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **16:14** — In the Greek, "of bronze that was before the Lord" directly modifies the altar at the end of v. 13, followed by "brought near the front of the house of the Lord" and "showed it" (edeixen, where the Hebrew has "placed it").
- **16:15** — The Greek reads "upon the bronze altar it shall be for me for the morning" (eis to proi), reflecting a reading of the Hebrew consonants as "morning" rather than "to inquire."
- **16:17** — "Stands" translates the Greek loanword mechonoth, a transliteration of the Hebrew term for the temple's wheeled bronze stands.
- **16:18** — The Greek reads "the foundation of the seat he built" and "turned to the house of the Lord," diverging from the Hebrew reference to removing the covered Sabbath structure from the house of the Lord.
