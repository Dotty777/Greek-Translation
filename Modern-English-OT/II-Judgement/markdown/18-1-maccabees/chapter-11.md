## Text

**11:1** And the king of Egypt gathered numerous forces, like the sand along the shore of the sea, and many ships; and he sought to gain control of the kingdom of Alexander by deceit and to add it to his own kingdom.

**11:2** And he went out into Syria with peaceful words, and the people of the cities opened to him and met him, because it was the command of King Alexander to meet him, since he was his father-in-law.

**11:3** And as he entered the cities of Ptolemais, he stationed his forces as a garrison in each city.

**11:4** And as he approached Ashdod, they showed him the temple of Dagon burned down, and Ashdod and its surrounding towns torn down, and the bodies cast out, and those who had been burned, whom he had burned in the war; for they had made heaps of them along his way.

**11:5** And they recounted to the king what Jonathan had done, in order to find fault with him; and the king kept silent.

**11:6** And Jonathan met the king at Joppa with splendor, and they greeted one another and slept there.

**11:7** And Jonathan went with the king as far as the river called Eleutherus, and he returned to Jerusalem.

**11:8** Now King Ptolemy took control of the cities of the coast as far as Seleucia by the sea, and he was devising evil designs concerning Alexander.

**11:9** And he sent envoys to King Demetrius, saying, "Come, let us make a covenant with one another, and I will give you my daughter whom Alexander has, and you will reign over the kingdom of your father;

**11:10** for I regret having given him my daughter, for he sought to kill me."

**11:11** And he found fault with him because he desired his kingdom.

**11:12** And taking his daughter away from him, he gave her to Demetrius; and the face of Alexander was changed, and their enmity became evident.

**11:13** And Ptolemy entered Antioch and put on the diadem of Asia; and he put two diadems around his head, that of Asia and Egypt.

**11:14** Now King Alexander was in Cilicia during those times, because the people of those places were revolting.

**11:15** And Alexander heard and came against him in war; and Ptolemy marched out and met him with a strong hand, and he routed him.

**11:16** And Alexander fled into Arabia to be sheltered there, and King Ptolemy was exalted.

**11:17** And Zabdiel the Arab cut off the head of Alexander and sent it to Ptolemy.

**11:18** And King Ptolemy died on the third day, and those who were in his strongholds were destroyed by those in the strongholds.

**11:19** And Demetrius became king in the one hundred sixty-seventh year.

**11:20** In those days Jonathan gathered those from Judea to attack the citadel in Jerusalem, and they made many siege engines against it.

**11:21** And some who hated their own nation, lawless men, went to the king and reported to him that Jonathan was laying siege to the citadel.

**11:22** And on hearing, he became angry; and when he heard, he immediately broke camp and came to Ptolemais, and he wrote to Jonathan not to continue laying siege, and to meet him to confer at Ptolemais as quickly as possible.

**11:23** And when Jonathan heard, he gave orders to continue laying siege; and he selected some of the elders of Israel and of the priests, and he put himself in danger.

**11:24** And taking silver and gold and clothing and many other gifts, he went to the king at Ptolemais, and he found favor before him.

**11:25** And some lawless people from the nation were lodging complaints against him.

**11:26** And the king did for him just as those before him had done for him, and he exalted him before all his friends.

**11:27** And he confirmed the high priesthood to him, and whatever other honors he had previously, and he made him chief of the First Friends.

**11:28** And Jonathan requested the king to make Judea and the three districts and Samaria free from tribute, and they promised him three hundred talents.

**11:29** And the king consented, and he wrote letters to Jonathan concerning all these things to this effect:

**11:30** "King Demetrius to his brother Jonathan, greetings, and to the Jewish nation.

**11:31** We have also written to you the copy of the letter that we wrote to our kinsman Lasthenes concerning you, so that you may see it:

**11:32** 'King Demetrius to his father Lasthenes, greetings.

**11:33** We have decided to do good to the nation of the Jews, who are our friends and keep what is right toward us, because of their goodwill toward us.

**11:34** Therefore we have confirmed to them the territory of Judea and the three districts, Aphairema and Lydda and Rathamin (they were added to Judea from Samaria), and all things belonging to them, for all those who offer sacrifice in Jerusalem, in place of the royal dues which the king previously received from them each year from the produce of the land and the fruit of the trees.

**11:35** And the other things due to us from now on, from the tithes and the tolls due to us, and the salt pools, and the crowns due to us, we will grant all of them to them.

**11:36** And none of these things will be annulled from now on for all time.

**11:37** Now therefore take care to make a copy of these things, and let it be given to Jonathan, and let it be placed on the holy mountain in a suitable, conspicuous place.'"

**11:38** And King Demetrius saw that the land was quiet before him and nothing opposed him, and he dismissed all his forces, each one to his own place, except the foreign forces which he had recruited from the islands of the nations; and all the forces from the time of the fathers became hostile to them.

**11:39** Now Trypho was previously one of those with Alexander, and he saw that all the forces were grumbling against Demetrius, and he went to Sinmalkue the Arab, who was raising Antiochus, the young boy of Alexander;

**11:40** and he pressed him persistently so that he would hand him over to him, so that he might reign in place of his father; and they reported to him all that Demetrius had done and the enmity with which his forces were hostile to him; and he remained there many days.

**11:41** And Jonathan sent word to King Demetrius to remove those from the citadel out of Jerusalem and those in the strongholds, for they were fighting against Israel.

**11:42** And Demetrius sent word to Jonathan, saying, "Not only will I do these things for you and your nation, but with honor I will honor you and your nation, if I find an opportunity.

**11:43** Now therefore you will do well to send us men who will fight alongside me, because all my forces have deserted."

**11:44** And Jonathan sent three thousand men, mighty in their strength, to Antioch, and they came to the king, and the king rejoiced at their arrival.

**11:45** And the people of the city gathered together into the middle of the city, about one hundred twenty thousand men, and they wanted to kill the king.

**11:46** And the king fled into the palace, and the people of the city seized the thoroughfares of the city and began to attack.

**11:47** And the king called the Jews for help, and they all gathered together to him at once; and they spread out in the city and killed in the city on that day about one hundred thousand.

**11:48** And they burned the city, and they took much plunder on that day, and they saved the king.

**11:49** And the people of the city saw that the Jews had gained control of the city as they wished, and they grew weak in their minds, and they cried out to the king with pleading, saying,

**11:50** "Give us the right hand, and let the Jews stop fighting against us and the city."

**11:51** And they threw down their weapons and made peace; and the Jews were honored before the king and in the sight of all those in his kingdom, and they returned to Jerusalem having much plunder.

**11:52** And King Demetrius sat on the throne of his kingdom, and the land was quiet before him;

**11:53** and he proved false to all that he had said, and he became estranged from Jonathan, and he did not repay the acts of goodwill that he had rendered to him, and he afflicted him severely.

**11:54** Now after these things Trypho returned, and Antiochus with him, a very young boy; and he became king and put on a diadem.

**11:55** And all the forces whom Demetrius had dismissed in disgrace gathered together to him, and they fought against him, and he fled and was routed;

**11:56** and Trypho captured the elephants and gained control of Antioch.

**11:57** And Antiochus the younger wrote to Jonathan, saying, "I confirm the high priesthood to you, and I appoint you over the four districts and to be one of the friends of the king."

**11:58** And he sent him gold vessels and a table service, and he gave him authority to drink from gold vessels, and to wear purple, and to have a gold buckle.

**11:59** And he appointed his brother Simon governor from the Ladder of Tyre to the borders of Egypt.

**11:60** And Jonathan went out and traveled beyond the river and among the cities, and all the forces of Syria gathered to him as allies; and he came to Ashkelon, and the people of the city met him with honor.

**11:61** And he departed from there to Gaza, and the people of Gaza shut him out; and he laid siege around it, and burned its surrounding towns with fire, and plundered them.

**11:62** And the people of Gaza appealed to Jonathan, and he gave them the right hand; and he took the sons of their rulers as hostages and sent them away to Jerusalem, and he passed through the country as far as Damascus.

**11:63** And Jonathan heard that the commanders of Demetrius had arrived at Kedesh, which is in Galilee, with a large force, wanting to remove him from office.

**11:64** And he went to meet them, while he left his brother Simon in the country.

**11:65** And Simon encamped at Beth-zur, and he fought against it many days and shut it in.

**11:66** And they asked him that they might receive the right hand, and he gave it to them; and he expelled them from there, and seized the city, and placed a garrison over it.

**11:67** And Jonathan and his army encamped by the water of Gennesar, and they rose early in the morning and went to the plain of Nasor;

**11:68** and look, an army of foreigners was coming to meet him in the plain, and they had sent out an ambush against him in the mountains, while they themselves met him from the front.

**11:69** And those in ambush rose up from their places and joined battle; and all those with Jonathan fled;

**11:70** not even one of them was left behind, except Mattathias the son of Absalom and Judas the son of Chalphi, commanders of the army of the forces.

**11:71** And Jonathan tore his garments and put earth on his head, and he prayed.

**11:72** And he turned back to them in battle and routed them, and they fled.

**11:73** And those who had fled from him saw it and returned to him, and they pursued with him as far as Kedesh, as far as their camp, and they encamped there.

**11:74** And about three thousand men of the foreigners fell on that day; and Jonathan returned to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — The Greek text reads the genitive "of Ptolemais" rather than the nominative "Ptolemy" found in some witnesses as the subject of the verb.
- **11:12** — The Greek text reads "the face of Alexander was changed," a Semitic idiom for turning hostile; some witnesses instead read that Ptolemy became estranged from Alexander.
- **11:18** — The repeated phrase "in the strongholds" refers first to Ptolemy's garrison troops stationed in the fortified cities and second to the inhabitants of those same strongholds.
- **11:28** — The Greek text reads accusative "and Samaria" rather than genitive "of Samaria" and has the plural verb "they promised," referring to Jonathan together with the elders and priests.
- **11:34** — Aphairema and Rathamin are Greek Septuagint forms corresponding to Hebrew Ophrah (or Ephraim) and Ramathaim.
- **11:38** — The Greek text reads the plural pronoun "them" (referring to the foreign forces or Demetrius and his mercenaries), whereas some witnesses read singular "him" (Demetrius).
- **11:39** — Sinmalkue is the spelling in this Greek text (as in Codex Alexandrinus); other witnesses read Imalkue or Simalcue.
- **11:50** — Literally "right hands," the standard idiom for granting pledges of peace or terms of truce (also in 11:62, 66).
- **11:56** — The Greek word literally means "beasts" and is the standard Hellenistic military term for war elephants.
- **11:67** — Gennesar is the Greek form corresponding to Gennesaret, and Nasor is the Septuagint form corresponding to Hazor.
