## Text

**8:1** Now Judas heard the name of the Romans, that they are mighty in strength, and that they are well pleased with all who have joined themselves to them, and that with as many as join themselves to them, and as many as approach them, they establish friendship,

**8:2** and that they are mighty in strength. And they recounted to him their wars and the valiant deeds that they perform among the Gauls, and that they gained control over them and brought them under tribute;

**8:3** and all that they did in the territory of Spain, to gain control of the mines of silver and gold there,

**8:4** and they gained control of the entire place by their counsel and patience (and the place was very far away from them), and of the kings who came against them from the end of the earth, until they crushed them and struck them with a great blow, and the rest pay them tribute year by year;

**8:5** and Philip, and Perseus king of the Kittim, and those who had risen up against them, they crushed in war and gained control over them;

**8:6** and Antiochus the Great, king of Asia, who went against them to war having one hundred and twenty elephants, and cavalry, and chariots, and a very great force, was crushed by them;

**8:7** and they took him alive, and established for them that both he and those who would reign after him should pay a great tribute, and give hostages and a settlement,

**8:8** and the territory of India, and Media, and Lydia, and some of their finest territories; and having taken them from him, they gave them to King Eumenes;

**8:9** and that those from Greece planned to come and destroy them,

**8:10** and the matter became known to them, and they sent one general against them, and they waged war against them, and many of them fell wounded, and they took captive their wives and their children, and plundered them, and gained control of their land, and tore down their strongholds, and plundered them, and enslaved them to this day.

**8:11** And the remaining kingdoms and the islands, as many as ever rose up against them, they destroyed and enslaved to themselves; but with their friends and those who relied on them they maintained friendship,

**8:12** and they gained control of the kingdoms near and far; and as many as heard their name were afraid of them.

**8:13** Those whom they wish to help and to make kings will reign, and those whom they wish, they depose; and they were greatly exalted.

**8:14** Yet in all these things not one of them put on a diadem, nor did they clothe themselves in purple so as to be magnified by it.

**8:15** And they made a council chamber for themselves, and every day three hundred and twenty deliberated, taking counsel continually concerning the people, so as to keep themselves in good order.

**8:16** And they trust one man to rule over them year by year and to have dominion over all their land, and all obey that one, and there is neither envy nor jealousy among them.

**8:17** And Judas chose Eupolemus son of John, son of Hakkoz, and Jason son of Eleazar, and sent them to Rome to establish friendship and an alliance with them,

**8:18** and to remove their yoke, because they saw the kingdom of the Greeks enslaving Israel in bondage.

**8:19** And they went to Rome, and the journey was very long; and they entered the council chamber and spoke:

**8:20** "Judas, who is also called Maccabeus, and his brothers and the people of the Jews sent us to you to establish an alliance and peace with you, and for us to be enrolled as your allies and friends."

**8:21** And the proposal was pleasing before them.

**8:22** And this is the copy of the document which they wrote in reply on bronze tablets and sent to Jerusalem to be there with them as a memorial of peace and alliance:

**8:23** "May it go well with the Romans and with the nation of the Jews at sea and on land forever, and may sword and enemy be kept far from them.

**8:24** Now if war comes first to Rome or to any of their allies in all their dominion,

**8:25** the nation of the Jews will fight as allies, as the occasion may be prescribed for them, with a full heart.

**8:26** And to those waging war they will neither give nor supply grain, weapons, silver, or ships, as it seemed good to Rome; and they will keep their obligations, receiving nothing.

**8:27** And in the same way, if war happens first to the nation of the Jews, the Romans will fight as allies from the soul, as the occasion may prescribe for them.

**8:28** And to those fighting as allies no grain, weapons, or silver will be given, as it seemed good to Rome; and they will keep these obligations, and not with deceit.

**8:29** According to these terms, the Romans thus established it with the people of the Jews.

**8:30** Now if, after these terms, both parties decide to add or to take away, they will act by their own choice, and whatever they add or take away will be binding.

**8:31** And concerning the evils that King Demetrius is committing against them, we have written to him, saying, 'Why have you made your yoke heavy upon our friends, our allies, the Jews?

**8:32** If, therefore, they appeal against you again, we will execute justice for them, and we will wage war against you by sea and by land.'"

## Translator Notes

- **8:5** — Greek Kitieon ('Kittim,' originally inhabitants of Citium in Cyprus) is used in 1 Maccabees for the Macedonians, over whom Philip V and Perseus ruled.
- **8:7** — Greek diastole literally means 'separation, division, or specification' and is variously understood here as a stipulated settlement or a territorial partition ceded by Antiochus III (cf. v. 8).
- **8:8** — The Greek text names India and Media (alongside Lydia) rather than the Anatolian regions actually transferred to Eumenes II after the Peace of Apamea (188 BC), probably reflecting early confusion for Ionia and Mysia.
- **8:10** — The clause 'and plundered them' appears twice in the Greek text of this verse.
- **8:26** — The phrase 'to those waging war' is debated, referring either to enemies of Rome (who must not be supplied) or to allied forces fighting on Rome's behalf (without Roman provisions; compare 'those fighting as allies' in v. 28, where the Greek text omits 'ships').
