## Text

**12:1** And Jonathan saw that the time was working in his favor, and he chose men and sent them to Rome to establish and renew the friendship with them.

**12:2** And to the Spartans and to other places he sent letters to the same effect.

**12:3** And they went to Rome, and they went into the senate house and said, "Jonathan the high priest and the nation of the Jews sent us to renew the friendship and the alliance for them as before."

**12:4** And they gave them letters to those in each place, so that they might escort them to the land of Judah in peace.

**12:5** And this is a copy of the letters that Jonathan wrote to the Spartans:

**12:6** "Jonathan the high priest of the nation, and the council of elders, and the priests, and the rest of the people of the Jews, to the Spartans, their brothers, greetings.

**12:7** Already earlier, letters were sent to Onias the high priest from Areus, who was reigning among you, stating that you are our brothers, as the copy below shows.

**12:8** And Onias welcomed with honor the man who had been sent, and received the letters in which it was clearly declared concerning alliance and friendship.

**12:9** Therefore, though we have no need of these things, having as encouragement the holy books that are in our hands,

**12:10** we undertook to send to renew the brotherhood and friendship with you, so that we might not become estranged from you; for many seasons have passed since you sent to us.

**12:11** We therefore at every time without ceasing, on the festivals and on the other appointed days, remember you at the sacrifices that we offer and in our prayers, as it is right and fitting to remember brothers.

**12:12** And we rejoice over your glory.

**12:13** Yet many afflictions and many wars surrounded us, and the kings around us made war against us.

**12:14** Therefore we did not wish to trouble you and the rest of our allies and friends in these wars.

**12:15** For we have the help from heaven helping us, and we were rescued from our enemies, and our enemies were brought low.

**12:16** Therefore we chose Numenius son of Antiochus and Antipater son of Jason, and have sent them to the Romans to renew the former friendship and alliance with them.

**12:17** Therefore we commanded them to go to you as well and greet you, and to deliver to you the letters from us concerning the renewal and our brotherhood.

**12:18** And now you will do well to answer us concerning these things."

**12:19** And this is the copy of the letters that they sent to Onias:

**12:20** "Ares, king of the Spartans, to Onias the great priest, greetings.

**12:21** It was found in a writing concerning both the Spartans and the Jews that they are brothers, and that they are of the lineage of Abraham.

**12:22** And now, since we have come to know these things, you will do well to write to us concerning your peace.

**12:23** And we also write back to you, 'Your livestock and your property are ours, and ours are yours.' Therefore we command them to report to you to the same effect."

**12:24** And Jonathan heard that the commanders of Demetrius had returned with a greater force than before to make war against him.

**12:25** And he departed from Jerusalem and met them in the country of Hamath, for he did not give them opportunity to invade his country.

**12:26** And he sent spies into his camp, and they returned and reported to him that thus they were positioning themselves to fall upon them during the night.

**12:27** And when the sun set, Jonathan ordered his men to keep watch and be at their weapons, to keep themselves ready for battle throughout the whole night; and he sent out sentries around the camp.

**12:28** And the adversaries heard that Jonathan and his men were ready for battle, and they feared and cowered in their hearts, and they kindled fires in their camp.

**12:29** But Jonathan and his men did not know until morning, for they saw the lights burning.

**12:30** And he pursued after them and did not overtake them, for they had crossed the Eleutherus River.

**12:31** And Jonathan turned aside against the Arabs called Zabadeans, and he struck them down and took their plunder.

**12:32** And breaking camp, he came to Damascus and marched through the whole country.

**12:33** And Simon went out and marched as far as Ashkelon and through the neighboring strongholds, and he turned aside to Joppa and seized it beforehand.

**12:34** For he had heard that they wanted to hand over the stronghold to Demetrius's men, and he stationed a garrison there so that they might guard it.

**12:35** And Jonathan returned and convened the elders of the people, and he took counsel with them to build strongholds in Judea,

**12:36** and to raise higher the walls of Jerusalem, and to raise a great height between the citadel and the city in order to separate it from the city, so that it would be by itself, so that they might neither buy nor sell.

**12:37** And they gathered together to build, and part of the wall of the brook on the east fell, and he repaired what was called Chaphenatha.

**12:38** And Simon built Adida in the Shephelah and fortified gates and bars.

**12:39** And Trypho sought to become king over Asia and to put on the diadem and to stretch out his hands against Antiochus the king.

**12:40** And he was apprehensive that Jonathan might not allow him and might make war against him, and he was seeking a way to seize him in order to kill him; and departing, he came to Beth-shan.

**12:41** And Jonathan went out to meet him with forty thousand men chosen for battle array, and he came to Beth-shan.

**12:42** And Trypho saw that he had arrived with a great force, and he was apprehensive about stretching out his hands against him.

**12:43** And he welcomed him with honor, and commended him to all his friends, and gave him gifts, and ordered his friends and his forces to obey him in the same way.

**12:44** And he said to Jonathan, "Why have you wearied all these people when no war is upon us?

**12:45** And now send them to their homes, and choose for yourself a few men who will be with you; and come with me to Ptolemais, and I will hand it over to you, and the rest of the strongholds, and the many forces, and all those in charge of affairs, and I will turn back and depart; for this is why I am here."

**12:46** And trusting him, he did just as he had said, and he sent away the forces, and they departed to the land of Judah.

**12:47** But he kept with himself three thousand men; about two thousand he left in Galilee, and one thousand went with him.

**12:48** And when Jonathan entered Ptolemais, the people of Ptolemais shut the gates and seized him, and they killed with the sword all those who had come with him.

**12:49** And Trypho sent forces and cavalry into Galilee, to the Great Plain, to destroy all of Jonathan's men.

**12:50** And they learned that he had been seized and had perished, and those with him; and they encouraged one another and marched in close formation, ready for battle.

**12:51** And the pursuers saw that it was a matter of life for them, and they turned back.

**12:52** And they all came in peace to the land of Judah, and they mourned for Jonathan and those with him, and they were greatly afraid; and Israel mourned a great mourning.

**12:53** And all the nations around them sought to wipe them out, for they said, "They have no man leading and helping them; now therefore let us make war against them, and we will remove their remembrance from among mankind."

## Translator Notes

- **12:20** — The Greek text spells the Spartan king's name 'Ares' here (referring to Areus I, called 'Areus' in 12:7) and styles Onias 'great priest,' reflecting the Hebrew title for the high priest.
- **12:26** — The Greek text reads singular 'his camp' (referring to Demetrius's army), where some manuscripts read plural 'their camp.'
- **12:28** — The Greek text ends with the lighting of the campfires, leaving the enemy's nighttime withdrawal implicit until 12:29-30; some later witnesses explicitly add 'and they departed.'
- **12:37** — 'The brook on the east' refers to the Kidron Valley east of Jerusalem; 'Chaphenatha' is an otherwise unattested name for a section of the wall or adjacent quarter.
- **12:45** — The Greek text reads 'the many forces,' where some other witnesses read 'the remaining forces.'
