## Text

**13:1** And Simon heard that Trypho had gathered a large force to come into the land of Judah to destroy it.

**13:2** And he saw that the people were trembling and terrified, and he went up to Jerusalem and assembled the people.

**13:3** And he encouraged them and said to them, "You yourselves know all that I and my brothers and the house of my father have done for the laws and the sanctuary, and the wars and the distresses.

**13:4** For this reason all my brothers perished for the sake of Israel, and I alone was left.

**13:5** And now, far be it from me to spare my life in any time of affliction, for I am no better than my brothers.

**13:6** Rather, I will take vengeance for my nation and for the sanctuary and for our wives and children, because all the nations have gathered to destroy us out of hostility."

**13:7** And the spirit of his people revived upon hearing these words.

**13:8** And they answered with a loud voice, saying, "You are our leader in place of Judas and Jonathan your brother.

**13:9** Fight our battle, and everything that you say to us we will do."

**13:10** And he gathered all the fighting men, and hurried to finish the walls of Jerusalem, and fortified it all around.

**13:11** And he sent Jonathan the son of Absalom, and with him a considerable force, to Joppa; and he drove out those who were in it and remained there in it.

**13:12** And Trypho departed from Ptolemais with a large force to enter the land of Judah, and Jonathan was with him under guard.

**13:13** Now Simon encamped at Adida, facing the plain.

**13:14** And Trypho learned that Simon had risen in place of Jonathan his brother and that he was about to engage him in battle, and he sent envoys to him, saying,

**13:15** "Because of silver that Jonathan your brother owed to the royal treasury for the offices he held, we are detaining him.

**13:16** And now send one hundred talents of silver and two of his sons as hostages, so that when released he will not revolt against us, and we will release him."

**13:17** And Simon knew that they were speaking to him with deceit, yet he sent the silver and the boys so that he would not incur great hostility with the people,

**13:18** saying, "Because I did not send him the silver and the boys, he perished."

**13:19** And he sent the boys and the one hundred talents, but he lied and did not release Jonathan.

**13:20** And after these things Trypho came to invade the city and destroy it, and he circled around by the road to Adora. And Simon and his army marched opposite him to every place where he went.

**13:21** Meanwhile, those from the citadel kept sending envoys, urging him to come to him through the wilderness and to send them provisions.

**13:22** And Trypho prepared all his cavalry to come, but on that night there was very heavy snow, and he did not come because of the snow. And he departed and came into Gilead.

**13:23** And when he approached Baskama, he killed Jonathan, and he was buried there.

**13:24** And Trypho turned back and went away to his own land.

**13:25** And Simon sent and took the bones of Jonathan his brother, and buried him in Modein, the city of his fathers.

**13:26** And all Israel lamented him with a great lamentation, and mourned for him many days.

**13:27** And Simon built over the tomb of his father and his brothers, and raised it high to view with polished stone on the back and on the front.

**13:28** And he set up seven pyramids, one opposite another, for his father and his mother and four brothers.

**13:29** And for these he made elaborate structures, setting great pillars around them; and on the pillars he made suits of armor for an everlasting name, and beside the suits of armor, carved ships, to be seen by all who sail the sea.

**13:30** This tomb that he made is in Modein to this day.

**13:31** Now Trypho dealt deceitfully with Antiochus the young king and killed him,

**13:32** and became king in his place, and put on the diadem of Asia, and brought a great calamity upon the land.

**13:33** And Simon built the stronghold of Judea, and walled it around with high towers and great walls and towers and gates and bars, and placed food supplies in the strongholds.

**13:34** And Simon chose men and sent them to Demetrius the king to grant relief to the country, because all the deeds of Trypho were to plunder.

**13:35** And Demetrius the king sent to him according to these words, and answered him, and wrote to him a letter like this:

**13:36** "King Demetrius to Simon, high priest and friend of kings, and to the elders and nation of the Jews: greetings.

**13:37** We have received the golden crown and the palm branch that you sent, and we are ready to make a great peace with you and to write to those in charge of affairs to grant you remissions.

**13:38** And whatever we established with you stands, and let the strongholds that you have built belong to you.

**13:39** And we remit to you oversights and offenses up to this day, and the crown that you owed; and if anything else was taxed in Jerusalem, let it no longer be taxed.

**13:40** And if any of you are qualified to be enrolled among those around us, let them be enrolled, and let there be peace between us."

**13:41** In the one hundred seventieth year the yoke of the nations was lifted from Israel.

**13:42** And the people of Israel began to write in the documents and contracts, "In the first year under Simon, great high priest and commander and leader of the Jews."

**13:43** In those days he encamped against Gaza and surrounded it with camps; and he made a siege tower and brought it up to the city, and struck one tower and captured it.

**13:44** And those in the siege tower leaped out into the city, and there was a great commotion in the city.

**13:45** And those in the city went up onto the wall with their wives and children, having torn their garments, and they cried out with a loud voice, begging Simon to give them the right hand.

**13:46** And they said, "Do not treat us according to our wicked deeds, but according to your mercy."

**13:47** And Simon came to terms with them and did not fight against them; but he drove them out of the city and cleansed the houses in which the idols were, and so he entered it singing hymns and giving blessing.

**13:48** And he cast out all uncleanness from it and settled in it men who practice the law; and he further fortified it and built for himself a residence in it.

**13:49** Now those from the citadel in Jerusalem were prevented from going out into the country and buying and selling; and they became very hungry, and a considerable number of them perished from famine.

**13:50** And they cried out to Simon to receive the right hand, and he gave it to them; and he drove them out from there and cleansed the citadel from the defilements.

**13:51** And he entered into it on the twenty-third day of the second month, in the one hundred seventy-first year, with praise and palm branches, and with lyres and cymbals and harps, and with hymns and songs, because a great enemy had been crushed out of Israel.

**13:52** And he established that this day be observed every year. And he further fortified the mountain of the temple that was beside the citadel, and he lived there, he and those with him.

**13:53** And Simon saw that John his son was a man, and he appointed him leader of all the forces; and he lived in Gazara.

## Translator Notes

- **13:18** — The Greek text reads first-person apesteila ("I did not send"), reflecting Simon's perspective in anticipating the people's accusation, whereas other manuscripts read third-person apesteilen ("he did not send").
- **13:21** — The Greek text reads singular pros auton ("to him") rather than plural pros autous ("to them") found in other manuscripts, though the following clause uses plural "them."
- **13:33** — The Greek text begins with singular "stronghold" (to ochyroma) and repeats "towers" (pyrgois), whereas other manuscripts read plural "strongholds" throughout and omit the second "towers."
- **13:43** — The Greek text reads "Gaza" (Gazan), though the reference to "Gazara" in 13:53 (and elsewhere in 1 Maccabees and Josephus) indicates that the fortified city besieged here was Gazara (Gezer).
- **13:45** — "Give them the right hand" (Greek dexias didonai, literally "give right hands"; compare "receive the right hand" in 13:50) is an idiom for granting a pledge of safety or terms of peace.
