## Text

**6:1** And King Antiochus was passing through the upper regions, and he heard that there was in Elymais in Persia a city renowned for wealth, silver, and gold;

**6:2** and the temple in it was exceedingly rich, and golden coverings, breastplates, and weapons were there, which Alexander son of Philip, the Macedonian king, who first reigned over the Greeks, had left there.

**6:3** And he came and sought to capture the city and plunder it, but he was not able, because the matter became known to those of the city.

**6:4** And they rose up against him for battle, and he fled and departed from there with great grief to return to Babylon.

**6:5** And someone came to him in Persia reporting that the armies that had gone into the land of Judah had been routed,

**6:6** and Lysias had gone with a strong force at first and had been turned back from before them, and they had grown strong with the weapons they took from the armies they cut down;

**6:7** and they had torn down the abomination that he had built upon the altar in Jerusalem, and they had surrounded the sanctuary with high walls as before, and Beth-zur, his city.

**6:8** And it happened, when the king heard these words, he was astounded and greatly shaken; and he fell onto his bed and fell into sickness from grief, because it had not turned out for him as he had planned.

**6:9** And he was there many days, because great grief was renewed upon him, and he concluded that he was dying.

**6:10** And he called all his friends and said to them, "Sleep departs from my eyes, and my heart has collapsed from anxiety.

**6:11** And I said to my heart, 'To what distress have I come, and to what a great flood, in which I now am? For I was kind and beloved in my authority.'

**6:12** But now I remember the evils that I did in Jerusalem; and I took all the silver and gold vessels that were in it, and I sent to destroy the inhabitants of Judah without cause.

**6:13** I know that on account of these things these evils have found me; and look, I am perishing from great grief in a foreign land."

**6:14** And he called Philip, one of his friends, and appointed him over all his kingdom.

**6:15** And he gave him the diadem, his robe, and the signet ring, to guide Antiochus his son and raise him to reign.

**6:16** And King Antiochus died there in the one hundred forty-ninth year.

**6:17** And Lysias learned that the king was dead, and he appointed Antiochus his son, whom he had raised while young, to reign in his place, and he called his name Eupator.

**6:18** And those from the citadel were hemming Israel in around the sanctuary, continually seeking evil, and they were a support for the nations.

**6:19** And Judas resolved to destroy them, and he assembled all the people to besiege them

**6:20** in the one hundred fiftieth year; and he built siege mounds and siege engines against them.

**6:21** And they went out from it, from the blockade, and some of the ungodly from Israel joined them.

**6:22** And they went to the king and said, "How long will you not execute justice and avenge our brothers?

**6:23** We were pleased to serve your father, and to walk by the things spoken by him, and to follow his decrees;

**6:24** for on account of this, those of our people became estranged from us; moreover, as many of us as were found were put to death, and our inheritances were plundered.

**6:25** And they did not stretch out a hand against us only, but also against all their borders.

**6:26** And look, today they have encamped against the citadel in Jerusalem to capture it, and they have fortified the sanctuary and Beth-zur.

**6:27** And if you do not forestall them quickly, they will do greater things than these, and you will not be able to restrain them."

**6:28** And the king became angry when he heard; and he gathered all his friends, commanders of his force, and those in charge of the reins.

**6:29** And from other kings and from islands of the seas, mercenary forces came to him.

**6:30** And the number of his forces was one hundred thousand foot soldiers, and twenty thousand horses, and thirty-two elephants skilled in war.

**6:31** And they came through Idumea, and encamped against Beth-zur, and fought for many days, and built siege engines; and they went out and burned them with fire, and fought courageously.

**6:32** And Judas departed from the citadel and encamped at Beth-zechariah, opposite the camp of the king.

**6:33** And the king rose early in the morning and moved the army in its rush along the road to Beth-zechariah, and the forces were drawn up for battle, and they sounded the trumpets.

**6:34** And they showed the elephants the blood of grapes and mulberries, to rouse them for battle.

**6:35** And they distributed the beasts among the phalanxes, and they stationed beside each elephant one thousand men armored in chain mail, and bronze helmets were on their heads; and five hundred chosen cavalry were assigned to each beast.

**6:36** These were there beforehand, wherever the beast was; and wherever it went, they went together; they did not leave it.

**6:37** And strong, covered wooden towers were upon them, on each beast, strapped onto them with contrivances; and upon each were thirty-two men who fought upon them, and its Indian.

**6:38** And he stationed the remaining cavalry on this side and on that, on the two sides of the army, striking terror and being protected among the phalanxes.

**6:39** And when the sun shone upon the golden shields, the mountains shone from them and blazed like torches of fire.

**6:40** And part of the army of the king was spread out upon the high mountains, and some upon the low ground; and they advanced steadily and in order.

**6:41** And all who heard the sound of their multitude, and the marching of the multitude, and the clashing of the weapons were shaken. For the army was exceedingly great and strong.

**6:42** And Judas and his army drew near for battle, and six hundred men from the army of the king fell.

**6:43** And Eleazar Sauaran saw one of the beasts armored with royal breastplates, and it towered above all the beasts, and it appeared that the king was on it.

**6:44** And he gave himself to save his people and to acquire for himself an eternal name.

**6:45** And he ran at it boldly into the middle of the phalanx, and he killed to the right and to the left, and they parted from him this way and that.

**6:46** And he slipped under the elephant, and thrust beneath it and killed it; and it fell to the ground upon him, and he died there.

**6:47** And they saw the strength of the kingdom and the onslaughts of the forces, and they turned away from them.

**6:48** And from the army of the king they went up to Jerusalem to meet them, and the king encamped in Judea and at Mount Zion.

**6:49** And he dealt with those of Beth-zur; and he went out from the city, because they had no provisions there to be shut up in it, because it was a sabbath for the land.

**6:50** And the king captured Beth-zur and stationed a garrison there to guard it.

**6:51** And he encamped against the sanctuary for many days, and he set up there siege mounds and siege engines, and fire-throwers and stone-throwers, and scorpions to shoot arrows, and slings.

**6:52** And they also built siege engines against their siege engines, and they fought for many days.

**6:53** But there was no food in the sanctuary, because it was the seventh year, and those who had been rescued into Judea from the nations had eaten up the remainder of the supplies;

**6:54** and few men were left in the sanctuary, because the famine prevailed over them; and they scattered, each to his own place.

**6:55** And Lysias heard that Philip, whom King Antiochus while still alive had appointed to raise Antiochus his son to reign,

**6:56** had returned from Persia and Media, and with him the forces of the king that had gone out, and that he was seeking to take over the affairs of state.

**6:57** And they hastened and were spurred on to depart; and he said to the king and the leaders of the force and the men, "We are failing day by day, and we have little food, and the place where we are encamped is strong, and the affairs of the kingdom press upon us.

**6:58** Now therefore let us give the right hand to these people, and let us make peace with them and with all their nation;

**6:59** and let us grant them to walk by their laws as before; for on account of their laws, which we abolished, they became angry and did all these things."

**6:60** And the word was pleasing before the king and the commanders, and he sent to them to make peace, and they accepted.

**6:61** And the king and the commanders swore to them upon these terms; he went out from the stronghold,

**6:62** and the king entered Mount Zion, and saw the fortification of the place; and he broke the oath that he had sworn, and gave orders and tore down the wall all around.

**6:63** And they departed in haste, and he returned to Antioch, and he found Philip in control of the city; and he fought against him and captured the city by force.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — The Greek reads "in Elymais" (en Elymais), referring to the region in Persia; other manuscripts omit the preposition and identify Elymais itself as the city.
- **6:2** — Greek kalymmata chrysa literally means "golden coverings," often understood in this military context as golden shields or protective armor.
- **6:20** — This Greek text connects the date directly to the preceding verse; other manuscripts insert "and they gathered together and besieged them" before "in the one hundred fiftieth year."
- **6:21** — The Greek reads "from it, from the blockade" (ex autou ek tou synkleismou); other manuscripts read "some of them went out from the blockade."
- **6:28** — Greek tous epi ton henion ("those in charge of the reins") is a military idiom referring to cavalry or chariot commanders.
- **6:37** — The Greek reads "thirty-two men" per elephant (a well-known textual crux, sometimes thought to reflect a different underlying Hebrew term or number); "its Indian" refers to the elephant's Indian driver.
- **6:43** — This Greek text reads the surname "Sauaran" (other manuscripts read "Avaran") and "it appeared" (ophthe; other manuscripts read oiethe, "he supposed").
- **6:49** — This Greek text omits "peace" after "made/dealt" and reads singular "he went out," whereas other manuscripts read "made peace" and plural "they went out," referring to the garrison of Beth-zur.
- **6:53** — This Greek text reads "in the sanctuary" (en tois hagiois, matching 6:54); other manuscripts read "in the storage vessels" (en tois angeiois).
- **6:61** — This Greek text reads singular "he went out" (exelthen); other manuscripts read plural "they went out," referring to the defenders leaving the stronghold.
