## Text

**1:1** And it happened after Alexander, son of Philip, the Macedonian, who came out from the land of Kittim, had struck down Darius, king of the Persians and Medes, that he reigned in his place, first over Greece.

**1:2** And he waged many wars, seized strongholds, and slaughtered kings,

**1:3** and he passed through to the ends of the earth and took plunder from a multitude of nations. And the earth became quiet before him, and he was exalted, and his heart was lifted up.

**1:4** And he gathered an exceedingly strong army, and ruled over countries, nations, and dominions, and they became tributary to him.

**1:5** And after these things he fell onto his bed, and he knew that he was dying.

**1:6** And he called his honored servants who had been brought up with him from youth, and he divided his kingdom among them while he was still alive.

**1:7** And Alexander reigned twelve years, and he died.

**1:8** And his servants took control, each in his own place;

**1:9** and they all put on diadems after he died, and their sons after them for many years; and they multiplied evils on the earth.

**1:10** And there came out from them a sinful root, Antiochus Epiphanes, son of Antiochus the king, who had been a hostage in Rome. And he began to reign in the one hundred and thirty-seventh year of the kingdom of the Greeks.

**1:11** In those days there came out from Israel a lawless son, and they persuaded many, saying, "Let us go and make a covenant with the nations around us, for from the time we were separated by them, many evils have found us."

**1:12** And the word seemed good in their eyes.

**1:13** And some of the people were eager and went to the king; and they gave them authority to observe the ordinances of the nations.

**1:14** And they built a gymnasium in Jerusalem according to the customs of the nations.

**1:15** And they made foreskins for themselves, and turned away from the holy covenant; and they yoked themselves to the nations, and sold themselves to do evil.

**1:16** And the kingdom was established before Antiochus, and he intended to reign over the land of Egypt, so that he might reign over the two kingdoms.

**1:17** And he entered Egypt with a heavy force, with chariots and elephants, and with horsemen and a great fleet.

**1:18** And he waged war against Ptolemy, the king of Egypt; and Ptolemy turned back from before him and fled, and many fell wounded.

**1:19** And they captured the fortified cities in the land of Egypt, and he took the plunder of the land of Egypt.

**1:20** And Antiochus returned after striking down Egypt in the one hundred and forty-third year, and he went up against Israel and Jerusalem with a heavy force.

**1:21** And he entered the sanctuary in arrogance, and he took the golden altar, the lampstand for the light and all its utensils,

**1:22** and the table of the presentation, the libation cups, the bowls, the golden censers, the curtain, the crowns, and the golden ornamentation on the front of the temple; and he stripped off everything.

**1:23** And he took the silver and the gold, and the precious vessels; and he took the hidden treasures that he found.

**1:24** And having taken everything, he departed to his own land. And they committed slaughter, and spoke with great arrogance.

**1:25** And there was great mourning upon Israel in every place of theirs.

**1:26** And rulers and elders groaned, virgins and young men grew weak, and the beauty of the women was altered.

**1:27** Every bridegroom took up a lament; she who sat in the bridal chamber was in mourning.

**1:28** And the land was shaken for those who lived in it, and the whole house of Jacob clothed itself with shame.

**1:29** After two full years, the king sent a chief collector of tribute to the cities of Judah, and he came to Jerusalem with a heavy force.

**1:30** And he spoke peaceful words to them in deceit, and they trusted him, and he fell upon the city suddenly, and struck it with a great blow, and destroyed many people from Israel.

**1:31** And he took the plunder of the city and burned it with fire, and tore down its houses and its walls all around,

**1:32** and he took the women and the children captive, and they took possession of the livestock.

**1:33** And they built up the city of David with a great and strong wall, with strong towers, and it became a citadel for them.

**1:34** And they placed there a sinful nation, lawless men, and they grew strong in it.

**1:35** And they stored up weapons and food, and having gathered the plunder of Jerusalem, they deposited it there; and it became a great snare,

**1:36** and it became an ambush against the sanctuary and an evil adversary to Israel continually.

**1:37** And they shed innocent blood all around the sanctuary, and they polluted the sanctuary.

**1:38** And the inhabitants of Jerusalem fled because of them, and she became a dwelling place of strangers; and she became a stranger to her offspring, and her children were forsaking her.

**1:39** Her sanctuary was made desolate like a wilderness; her festivals were turned into mourning, her Sabbaths into reproach, her honor into contempt.

**1:40** According to her glory, her dishonor was multiplied, and her exaltation was turned into mourning.

**1:41** And the king wrote to his whole kingdom that all should be one people,

**1:42** and that each one should forsake his own customs; and all the nations agreed according to the word of the king.

**1:43** And many from Israel were pleased with his worship, and sacrificed to the idols, and profaned the Sabbath.

**1:44** And the king sent letters by the hand of messengers to Jerusalem and the cities of Judah, to go after customs foreign to the land,

**1:45** and to forbid burnt offerings and sacrifices and a drink offering from the sanctuary, and to profane Sabbaths and festivals,

**1:46** and to defile the sanctuary and holy ones;

**1:47** to build altars and sacred precincts and idols, and to sacrifice swine and unclean animals,

**1:48** and to leave their sons uncircumcised, to make their souls abominable with every impure thing and to profane them,

**1:49** so as to forget the law and change all the ordinances.

**1:50** And whoever does not act according to the word of the king will die.

**1:51** According to all these words he wrote to his kingdom, and he appointed overseers over all the people; and he commanded the cities of Judah to offer sacrifices city by city.

**1:52** And many from the people gathered together to them, everyone who forsook the law; and they committed evils in the land,

**1:53** and they forced Israel into hiding places, in every place of refuge of theirs.

**1:54** And on the fifteenth day of Chislev, in the forty-fifth year, they built an abomination of desolation upon the altar; and in the cities of Judah all around they built altars,

**1:55** and at the windows of the houses and in the streets they burned incense.

**1:56** And the books of the law that they found, they tore to pieces and burned with fire.

**1:57** And wherever a book of the covenant was found with anyone, and if anyone consented to the law, the sentence of the king put him to death.

**1:58** In their strength they kept doing so to Israel, to those who were found month by month in the cities.

**1:59** And on the twenty-fifth of the month they were offering sacrifices upon the altar that was on top of the altar.

**1:60** And according to the decree they put to death the women who had circumcised their children,

**1:61** and they hung the infants from their necks, and their households, and those who had circumcised them.

**1:62** And many in Israel were hanged. And they were fortified within themselves not to eat unclean things,

**1:63** and they chose to die, so that they would not be defiled by the foods and would not profane the holy covenant; and they died.

**1:64** And exceedingly great wrath came upon Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Kittim (Greek Chettiim) refers here to Macedonia or the Greek world; the closing phrase "first over Greece" can mean either that Alexander had previously reigned over Greece or that he reigned as the first king of the Greek empire.
- **1:4** — The Greek text reads "dominions" (tyrannion, or "principalities"), whereas other manuscripts read "tyrants" or "rulers" (tyrannon).
- **1:11** — The Greek text uniquely combines the singular "a lawless son" with plural verbs ("they persuaded ... saying") and reads "separated by them" (hyp' auton), where other witnesses read plural "lawless sons" and "separated from them" (ap' auton).
- **1:13** — The Greek text uses the plural "they gave" (referring to the royal court), whereas other witnesses read singular "he gave"; a similar shift to plural verbs occurs in 1:24 ("they committed ... spoke").
- **1:29** — "Two full years" translates the literal Hebraic expression "two years of days" (dyo ete hemeron).
- **1:48** — The Greek text reads the verb "to profane" (bebelosai) rather than the adjective "profane" (bebelo) found in other witnesses; similarly 1:47 reads "idols" (eidola) rather than "idol shrines" (eidolia).
- **1:54** — The Greek text reads "the forty-fifth year," omitting "one hundredth" found in other manuscripts that date the event to the one hundred and forty-fifth year of the Seleucid era (167 BC).
- **1:55** — The Greek text reads "windows" (thyridon) rather than "doors" (thyron) found in other witnesses.
- **1:59** — The Greek uses two distinct words for altar here, referring to the newly erected pagan altar (bomos) placed on top of the temple's sacrificial altar (thysiasterion).
- **1:62** — The Greek text reads "were hanged" (ekremasthesan), whereas other manuscripts read "stood firm" (ekrataiothesan).
