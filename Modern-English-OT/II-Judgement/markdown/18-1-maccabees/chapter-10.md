## Text

**10:1** And in the one hundred and sixtieth year, Alexander Epiphanes, the son of Antiochus, went up and seized Ptolemais, and they received him, and he reigned there.

**10:2** And King Demetrius heard of it, and gathered very numerous forces, and went out to meet him for war.

**10:3** And Demetrius sent letters to Jonathan with peaceful words, so as to magnify him.

**10:4** For he said, "Let us act first to make peace with them before he makes peace with Alexander against us.

**10:5** For he will remember all the evils that we perpetrated against him, and against his brothers, and against his nation."

**10:6** And he gave him authority to gather forces, and to prepare weapons, and for him to be his ally; and he ordered that the hostages who were in the citadel be handed over to him.

**10:7** And Jonathan came to Jerusalem and read the letters in the hearing of all the people and of those from the citadel.

**10:8** And they feared with a great fear when they heard that the king had given them authority to gather forces.

**10:9** And those from the citadel handed over the hostages to Jonathan, and they returned them to their parents.

**10:10** And Jonathan settled in Jerusalem, and began to build and renew the city.

**10:11** And he told those who were doing the work to build the walls and Mount Zion all around with squared stones for fortification, and they did so.

**10:12** And the foreigners who were in the strongholds that Bacchides had built fled.

**10:13** And each one left his place and departed to his own land.

**10:14** Only in Beth-zur were some of those who had abandoned the law and the ordinances left behind, for it was a place of refuge for them.

**10:15** And King Alexander heard of all the promises that Demetrius had sent to Jonathan, and they recounted to him the wars and the brave deeds that he and his brothers had done, and the hardships that they had endured.

**10:16** And he said, "Will we find even one man like this? And now, let us make him our friend and ally."

**10:17** And he wrote letters and sent them to him according to these words, saying:

**10:18** "King Alexander to his brother Jonathan, greetings.

**10:19** We have heard about you, that you are a man excellent in strength and fit to be our friend.

**10:20** And now we have appointed you today as high priest of your nation, and to be called Friend of the King" (and they sent him a purple robe and a golden crown) "and to take our side and keep friendship with us."

**10:21** And Jonathan put on the holy robe in the seventh month of the one hundred and sixtieth year, at the Feast of Booths, and he gathered forces and prepared many weapons.

**10:22** And Demetrius heard these words, and was grieved, and said,

**10:23** "What is this that we have done, that Alexander has gotten ahead of us in securing friendship with the Jews for support?

**10:24** I also will write to them words of encouragement and exaltation and gifts, so that they may be with me for help."

**10:25** And he sent to them according to these words: "King Demetrius to the nation of the Jews, greetings.

**10:26** Since you kept the agreements with us, and remained in our friendship, and did not go over to our enemies, we heard and rejoiced.

**10:27** And now continue still to keep faith with us, and we will repay you with good things in return for what you do with us.

**10:28** And we will grant you many remissions, and we will give you gifts.

**10:29** And now I release you and exempt all the Jews from the tributes, and from the price of salt, and from the crowns.

**10:30** And instead of the third of the sown crop, and instead of the half of the fruit of the trees that falls to me to receive, I remit from today and onward taking them from the land of Judah and from the three districts added to it from Samaria and Galilee, from this day and for all time.

**10:31** And let Jerusalem be holy and exempt, and its borders, the tithes and the taxes.

**10:32** I also relinquish authority over the citadel that is in Jerusalem, and give it to the high priest, so that he may appoint in it men whom he himself may choose, to guard it.

**10:33** And every soul of the Jews who was taken captive from the land of Judah into all my kingdom I release free without payment; and let all remit the tributes even of their livestock.

**10:34** And all the feasts and the Sabbaths and new moons and appointed days, and three days before a feast and three days after a feast, let all the days be days of exemption and release for all the Jews who are in all my kingdom.

**10:35** And no one shall have authority to exact payment from or trouble any of them concerning any matter.

**10:36** And let there be enrolled from the Jews into the forces of the king up to thirty thousand men, and allowances shall be given to them as is due to all the forces of the king.

**10:37** And some of them shall be stationed in the great strongholds of the king, and some of these shall be appointed over offices of the kingdom that require trust; and let those over them and the commanders be from among them, and let them walk by their own laws, just as the king has also commanded in the land of Judah.

**10:38** And as for the three districts that have been added to Judea from the country of Samaria, let them be added to Judea so as to be reckoned as being under one ruler, so as not to obey any other authority except the high priest.

**10:39** Ptolemais and the territory adjoining it I have given as a gift to the sanctuary in Jerusalem, for the proper expense of the sanctuary.

**10:40** And I also give every year fifteen thousand shekels of silver from the accounts of the king, from the appropriate places.

**10:41** And all the surplus that they were not paying out from the offices, as in the former years, from now on they shall give for the works of the house.

**10:42** And in addition to these, five thousand shekels of silver, which they used to take from the offices, as in the former years, from the account of the sanctuary every year, these also are remitted, because they belong to the priests who minister.

**10:43** And as many as flee to the temple in Jerusalem and within all its borders, being liable for royal dues or any matter, let them be released, along with everything that belongs to them in my kingdom.

**10:44** And for the building and renewing of the works of the sanctuary, the expense also shall be given from the account of the king.

**10:45** And for the building of the walls of Jerusalem and fortifying it all around, the expense also shall be given from the account of the king, and for building the walls in Judea."

**10:46** Now when Jonathan and the people heard these words, he did not believe them, nor did they receive them, because they remembered the great evil that he had done in Israel, and how he had afflicted them greatly.

**10:47** And they were well pleased with Alexander, because he had become to them the originator of peaceful words, and they fought as allies with him all the days.

**10:48** And King Alexander gathered great forces and encamped opposite Demetrius.

**10:49** And the two kings joined battle, and the army of Demetrius fled, and Alexander pursued him and prevailed over them.

**10:50** And he pressed the battle fiercely until the sun went down, and Demetrius fell on that day.

**10:51** And Alexander sent envoys to Ptolemy, king of Egypt, according to these words, saying:

**10:52** "Since I have returned to my kingdom, and sat on the throne of my fathers, and gained control of the rule, and crushed Demetrius, and gained mastery over our country;

**10:53** and I joined battle with him, and he and his army were crushed by us, and we sat on the throne of his kingdom;

**10:54** and let us establish friendship with him; and now give me your daughter as a wife, and I will make a marriage alliance with you, and I will give gifts to you and to her worthy of you."

**10:55** And King Ptolemy answered, saying, "Good is the day on which you returned to the land of your fathers and sat on the throne of their kingdom.

**10:56** And now I will do for you what you have written; but meet me at Ptolemais, so that we may see one another, and I will make a marriage alliance with you just as you have said."

**10:57** And Ptolemy went out from Egypt, he and his daughter Cleopatra, and came to Ptolemais in the one hundred and sixty-second year.

**10:58** And King Alexander met him, and he gave him his daughter Cleopatra in marriage, and celebrated her wedding in Ptolemais, as kings do, with great splendor.

**10:59** And King Alexander wrote to Jonathan to come to meet him.

**10:60** And he went with splendor to Ptolemais and met the two kings; and he gave them silver and gold, and to their friends also, and many gifts, and he found favor before them.

**10:61** And pestilent men from Israel, lawless men, gathered against him to bring charges against him; and the king paid no attention to them.

**10:62** And the king commanded them, and he removed Jonathan's garments and clothed him with a purple robe; and they did so.

**10:63** And the king seated him with him, and said to his officials, "Go out with him into the middle of the city, and proclaim that no one is to bring charges against this man concerning any matter, and let no one trouble him for any reason."

**10:64** And it happened, when those who were bringing charges saw his honor as he proclaimed it, and him clothed in fine linen, that they all fled.

**10:65** And the king honored him, and enrolled him among the First Friends, and made him general and district governor.

**10:66** And Jonathan returned to Jerusalem with peace and gladness.

**10:67** And in the one hundred and sixty-fifth year, Demetrius, the son of Demetrius, came from Crete to the land of his fathers.

**10:68** And King Alexander heard of it, and was exceedingly grieved, and returned to Antioch.

**10:69** And Demetrius appointed Apollonius, who was over Coele-Syria, and he gathered a great force and encamped at Jamnia.

**10:70** And he sent to Jonathan the high priest, saying, "You all alone lift yourself up against us, and I have become a laughingstock and a reproach because of you. And why do you assert authority against us in the mountains?

**10:71** Now therefore, if you trust in your forces, come down to us into the plain, and let us measure ourselves against one another there, for with me is the force of the cities.

**10:72** Ask and learn who I am and who the rest are who help us; and they say, 'You have no footing before us, because twice your fathers were routed in their own land.'

**10:73** And now you will not be able to withstand the cavalry and such a force in the plain, where there is neither stone nor pebble nor place to flee."

**10:74** Now when Jonathan heard the words of Apollonius, he was stirred in his mind, and he chose ten thousand men and went out from Jerusalem, and his brother Simon met him to help him.

**10:75** And he encamped against Joppa, and those from the city shut him out, because a garrison of Apollonius was in Joppa; and they fought against it.

**10:76** And being afraid, those from the city opened up, and Jonathan became master of Joppa.

**10:77** And Apollonius heard of it, and he marshaled three thousand cavalry and a large force, and went toward Ashdod as though passing through, and at the same time he advanced into the plain, even because he had a multitude of cavalry and trusted in it.

**10:78** And he pursued after him to Ashdod, and the armies joined battle behind him.

**10:79** And Apollonius had left one thousand cavalry secretly behind them.

**10:80** And Jonathan knew that there was an ambush behind him. And they surrounded his army and shot the arrows into the people from morning until evening.

**10:81** But the people stood fast, just as Jonathan had commanded, and their horses grew weary.

**10:82** And Simon drew out his force and engaged the phalanx, for the cavalry was exhausted; and they were crushed by him and fled.

**10:83** And the cavalry was scattered in the plain, and they fled to Ashdod and entered Beth-dagon, their idol temple, to be saved.

**10:84** And Jonathan burned Ashdod and the cities around it, and took their spoils, and the temple of Dagon and its temple he burned with fire.

**10:85** And those who had fallen by the sword, together with those who were burned, amounted to eight thousand men.

**10:86** And Jonathan departed from there and encamped at Ashkelon, and those from the city came out to meet him with great honor.

**10:87** And Jonathan returned with those who were with him, having many spoils.

**10:88** And it happened, when King Alexander heard these words, that he honored Jonathan still more.

**10:89** And he sent him a golden buckle, as is the custom to be given to the Kinsmen of the Kings; and he gave him Ekron and all its borders as an inheritance.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — Greek ho Epiphanes ("Epiphanes") is nominative, modifying Alexander rather than Antiochus; Alexander Balas claimed descent from Antiochus IV and adopted his royal epithet.
- **10:20** — The clause "(and they sent him a purple robe and a golden crown)" is a third-person narrative parenthesis inserted directly within the quoted letter, with the plural verb apesteilan ("they sent") in the Greek text.
- **10:29** — "The price of salt" refers to the Seleucid salt tax, and "the crowns" refers to the customary gold crown levies paid to Hellenistic kings.
- **10:42** — The phrase "as in the former years" appears here in this Greek text (as in Codex Alexandrinus and Codex Venetus, echoing v. 41), separating "from the offices" from "of the sanctuary from the account."
- **10:54** — This Greek text reads singular pros auton ("with him"), where other witnesses read pros heautous ("with one another").
- **10:64** — This Greek text reads sindona ("fine linen," as in Codex Alexandrinus) rather than porphyran ("purple," as in other witnesses), and active ekeryxen ("he proclaimed") rather than passive ekerychthe ("it was proclaimed").
- **10:84** — This Greek text reads kai to hieron autes ("and its temple," as in Codex Alexandrinus), where other witnesses read kai tous symphygontas eis auto ("and those who had fled into it").
