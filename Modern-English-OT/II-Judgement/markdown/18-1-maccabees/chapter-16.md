## Text

**16:1** And John went up from Gazara and reported to Simon his father what Cendebeus was carrying out.

**16:2** And Simon called his two older sons, Judas and John, and said to them, "I and my brothers and the house of my father have fought the wars of Israel from youth until this day, and it has prospered in our hands to deliver Israel many times.

**16:3** But now I have grown old, and you, by mercy, are sufficient in years; take the place of me and my brother, and go out and fight in defense of our nation, and may the help from heaven be with you."

**16:4** And he chose from the country twenty thousand fighting men and horsemen, and they marched against Cendebeus and spent the night in Modein.

**16:5** And rising in the morning, they marched into the plain; and look, a large force was coming to meet them, infantry and horsemen, and a stream was between them.

**16:6** And he encamped facing them, he and his people; and he saw that the people were afraid to cross the stream, so he crossed over first; and the men saw him and crossed over after him.

**16:7** And he divided the people, with the horsemen in the middle of the infantry; and the cavalry of the adversaries was very numerous.

**16:8** And they sounded the trumpets, and Cendebeus and his army were routed, and many on his side fell wounded; and those who remained fled into the fortress.

**16:9** Then Judas, the brother of John, was wounded; but John pursued them until they came to Kedron, which he had built.

**16:10** And they fled into the towers that were in the countryside of Ashdod, and he burned it with fire; and about one thousand men of them fell, and he returned to Judea in peace.

**16:11** Now Ptolemy, the son of Abubus, had been appointed commander over the plain of Jericho, and he had much silver and gold,

**16:12** for he was the son-in-law of the high priest.

**16:13** And his heart was lifted up, and he desired to gain control of the country, and he plotted treacherously against Simon and against his sons to do away with them.

**16:14** Now Simon was inspecting the cities in the country and attending to their needs, and he went down to Jericho, he and Mattathias and Judas and his sons, in the one hundred seventy-seventh year, in the eleventh month; this is the month of Shebat.

**16:15** And the son of Abubus deceitfully received them into the small fortress called Dok, which he had built, and he made a great banquet for them; and he hid men there.

**16:16** And when Simon and his sons were drunk, Ptolemy and those with him rose up, and took their weapons, and burst in upon Simon in the banquet hall, and killed him and his sons and some of his servants.

**16:17** And he committed great godlessness, and he repaid evil for good.

**16:18** And Ptolemy wrote these things and sent them to the king, so that he would send him forces for help and he would hand over their country and the cities.

**16:19** And he sent others to Gazara to do away with John; and to the commanders of thousands he sent letters to come to him, so that he might give them silver and gold and gifts.

**16:20** And he sent others to capture Jerusalem and the mountain of the temple.

**16:21** And someone ran ahead and reported to John at Gazara that his father and his brothers had perished, and said, "He has sent to kill you also."

**16:22** And when he heard this, he was deeply shocked, and he seized the men who had come to destroy him and killed them, for he knew that they were seeking to destroy him.

**16:23** Now the rest of the acts of John, and his wars, and the valiant exploits that he performed, and the building of the walls that he built, and his deeds,

**16:24** look, these are written in the book of the days of his high priesthood, from the time he became high priest after his father.

## Translator Notes

- **16:3** — "By mercy" (en to eleei) and "from heaven" are reverent circumlocutions for God characteristic of 1 Maccabees.
- **16:9** — The unexpressed subject of "had built" is Cendebeus, who built Kedron in 15:41.
- **16:10** — Our Greek text reads "one thousand" men (where some other witnesses read "two thousand") and uses the feminine singular pronoun "it" referring to Ashdod (Greek Azotos).
- **16:14** — Our Greek text reads "and his sons" (kai hoi huioi autou), whereas some other manuscripts omit "and," identifying Mattathias and Judas as his sons.
- **16:17** — Our Greek text reads atheian ("godlessness" or "impiety"), whereas other Greek witnesses read athesian ("treachery").
