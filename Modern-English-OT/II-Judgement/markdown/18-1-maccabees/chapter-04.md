## Text

**4:1** And Gorgias took five thousand men and one thousand chosen cavalry, and the army set out by night,

**4:2** so as to fall upon the camp of the Jews and strike them suddenly; and the sons of the citadel were guides for him.

**4:3** And Judas heard of it, and he and the mighty men set out to strike the king's force that was in Emmaus,

**4:4** while the forces were still scattered away from the camp.

**4:5** And Gorgias came into the camp of Judas by night and found no one; and he was searching for them in the mountains, for he said, "These men are fleeing from us."

**4:6** And at daybreak Judas appeared in the plain with three thousand men; however, they did not have armor and sword as they wished.

**4:7** And they saw an army of Gentiles, strong and clad in armor, and cavalry encircling it, and these were trained in war.

**4:8** And Judas said to the men who were with him, "Do not fear their multitude, and do not be terrified of their onslaught.

**4:9** Remember how our fathers were saved at the Red Sea, when Pharaoh pursued them with an army.

**4:10** And now let us cry out to Heaven, to see if He will favor us and remember the covenant of the fathers and crush this army before us today.

**4:11** And all the nations will know that there is One who redeems and saves Israel."

**4:12** And the foreigners lifted up their eyes and saw them close by, directly opposite,

**4:13** and they came out from the camp to battle; and those with Judas sounded the trumpets.

**4:14** And they joined battle, and the Gentiles were crushed and fled into the plain.

**4:15** And all those in the rear fell by the sword; and they pursued them as far as Gazara and as far as the plains of Judea and Ashdod and Jamnia, and about three thousand men of them fell.

**4:16** And Judas and the force turned back from pursuing after them,

**4:17** and he said to the people, "Do not covet the spoils, for battle is directly before us,

**4:18** and Gorgias and the force are on the mountain near us. But stand now against our enemies and fight them, and after these things take spoils, and with boldness."

**4:19** While Judas was still finishing these things, a certain detachment appeared peering out from the mountain.

**4:20** And it saw that the army had been put to flight and that the camp was being burned; for the smoke that was seen made plain what had happened.

**4:21** And realizing these things, they were greatly terrified; and perceiving also the army of Judas in the plain ready for battle array,

**4:22** they all fled into the land of the foreigners.

**4:23** And Judas returned to the plundering of the camp; and he took gold and much silver, and blue and purple and sea-purple, and great wealth.

**4:24** And as they returned, they were singing hymns and blessing Heaven, "For His mercy is good forever."

**4:25** And there was a great salvation for Israel on that day.

**4:26** Now as many of the foreigners as escaped came and reported to Lysias all that had happened.

**4:27** And when he heard everything, he was dismayed and discouraged, because such things as he wished had not happened to Israel, and such things as the king had commanded him had not come to pass.

**4:28** And in the following year he mustered sixty thousand select men and five thousand cavalry, so as to subdue them.

**4:29** And they came into Idumea and encamped at Beth-zur, and Judas met them with ten thousand men.

**4:30** And he saw that the army was strong, and he prayed and said, "Blessed are You, Savior of Israel, who crushed the onslaught of the mighty warrior by the hand of Your servant David, and You handed over the army of the foreigners into the hands of Jonathan son of Saul and his armor-bearer.

**4:31** Deliver this army into the hands of Your people Israel, and let them be put to shame over their force and their cavalry.

**4:32** Give them cowardice, and melt the audacity of their strength, and let them be shaken by their destruction.

**4:33** Strike them down by the sword of those who love You, and let all who have seen Your name praise You with hymns."

**4:34** And they clashed with one another, and about five thousand men fell from the army of Lysias, and they fell directly before them.

**4:35** Now when Lysias saw the rout that had happened to his division, and the courage that had come to that of Judas, and how they were ready either to live or to die nobly, he set out for Antioch; and he was recruiting mercenaries, so as to become even greater in number and come again into Judea.

**4:36** Now Judas and his brothers said, "Look, our enemies have been crushed; let us go up to cleanse the sanctuary and dedicate it."

**4:37** And the entire army was gathered together, and they went up to Mount Zion.

**4:38** And they saw our sanctuary desolate, and the altar profaned, and the gates burned down, and in the courts plants growing as in a forest or as on one of the mountains, and the chambers torn down.

**4:39** And they tore their garments, and lamented with a great lamentation, and put ashes on their heads.

**4:40** And they fell on their faces to the ground, and sounded the signal trumpets, and cried out to Heaven.

**4:41** Then Judas ordered men to fight those in the citadel until he cleansed the sanctuary.

**4:42** And he selected blameless priests who delighted in the law;

**4:43** and they cleansed the sanctuary and carried out the stones of defilement to an unclean place.

**4:44** And they took counsel concerning the altar of burnt offering that had been profaned, what they should do with it.

**4:45** And a good plan occurred to them, to tear it down, so that it would never become a reproach to them, because the Gentiles had defiled it; and they tore down the altar.

**4:46** And they stored the stones on the mountain of the house in a suitable place, until a prophet should come to give an answer concerning them.

**4:47** And they took whole stones according to the law and built a new altar like the former one.

**4:48** And they built the sanctuary and the interior of the house, and he consecrated the courts.

**4:49** And they made the holy vessels new, and they brought the lampstand and the altar of burnt offerings and incense and the table into the temple.

**4:50** And they burned incense on the altar and lit the lamps that were on the lampstand, and they gave light in the temple.

**4:51** And they placed loaves on the table and hung the curtains; and they finished all the works that they had done.

**4:52** And they rose early in the morning on the twenty-fifth day of the ninth month, that is, the month of Chislev, of the one hundred and forty-eighth year.

**4:53** And they offered sacrifice according to the law upon the new altar of burnt offerings that they had made.

**4:54** At the season, on the day on which the Gentiles had profaned it, on that day it was dedicated with songs and lyres and harps and with cymbals.

**4:55** And all the people fell on their faces, and worshiped, and blessed Heaven, the One who had given them success.

**4:56** And they celebrated the dedication of the altar for eight days, and presented burnt offerings with gladness, and offered a sacrifice of deliverance and praise.

**4:57** And they adorned the front of the temple with golden crowns and small shields, and he renewed the gates and the chambers, and they fitted them with doors.

**4:58** And there was very great gladness among the people, and the reproach of the Gentiles was turned away.

**4:59** And Judas and his brothers and all the assembly of Israel decreed that the days of dedication of the altar should be observed in their seasons year by year for eight days, from the twenty-fifth day of the month of Chislev, with gladness and joy.

**4:60** And at that time they built up Mount Zion with high walls and strong towers all around, so that the Gentiles would never come and trample them down as they had done before.

**4:61** And they stationed a force there to guard it, and they fortified it to guard Beth-zur, so that the people might have a stronghold facing Idumea.

## Translator Notes

- **4:12** — This Greek text reads echomenous ("close by / positioned"), whereas some manuscripts read erchomenous ("coming").
- **4:15** — This Greek text reads "Judea" (Ioudaias), whereas some manuscripts read "Idumea" (Idoumaias).
- **4:23** — This Greek text reads kai porphyran kai thalassian ("and purple and sea-purple"), whereas some manuscripts omit the second kai to read "sea-purple" (costly marine-dyed purple cloth).
- **4:24** — This Greek text has a single clause, "For His mercy is good forever" (Hoti kalon eis ton aiona to eleos autou), whereas some manuscripts repeat hoti ("For it is good, for His mercy is forever"), echoing Psalm 136:1 (135:1 LXX).
- **4:30** — Greek allophyloi ("foreigners," also in 4:12, 22, 26) is the standard Septuagint rendering for the Philistines, here recalling Jonathan's victory in 1 Samuel 14.
- **4:33** — This Greek text reads idontes ("those who have seen"), whereas other manuscripts read eidotes ("those who know").
- **4:38** — This Greek text includes "our" (hemon) with "sanctuary," whereas some manuscripts omit the pronoun.
- **4:49** — This Greek text reads "the altar of burnt offerings and incense" (ton holokautomaton kai thymiamaton), whereas other manuscripts read only "the altar of incense" (ton thymiamaton).
- **4:61** — The Greek phrasing literally reads "they fortified it to guard Beth-zur" (ochyrosan auto terein ten Bethsoura), which many interpreters understand in the sense of fortifying Beth-zur to guard it.
