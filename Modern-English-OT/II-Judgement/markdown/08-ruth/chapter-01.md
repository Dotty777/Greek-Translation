## Text

**1:1** And it happened, while the judges were judging, that there was a famine in the land. And a man went from Bethlehem of Judah to live as a foreigner in the country of Moab, he and his wife and his sons.

**1:2** And the name of the man was Abimelech, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion, Ephrathites from Bethlehem of Judah. And they came into the country of Moab and were there.

**1:3** And Abimelech, the husband of Naomi, died, and she was left, and her two sons.

**1:4** And they took for themselves Moabite wives; the name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the second was Ruth. And they lived there about ten years.

**1:5** And both of them also died, Mahlon and Chilion; and the woman was left without her husband and without her two sons.

**1:6** And she and her two daughters-in-law arose and returned from the country of Moab, because they heard in the country of Moab that the Lord had visited His people to give them bread.

**1:7** And she went out from the place where she was, and her two daughters-in-law with her; and they were going along the road to return to the land of Judah.

**1:8** And Naomi said to her daughters-in-law, "Go now, return, each one to her mother's house. May the Lord show mercy to you, just as you have done with the dead and with me.

**1:9** May the Lord grant it to you, and may you find rest, each one in her husband's house." And she kissed them, and they lifted up their voice and wept.

**1:10** And they said to her, "With you we will return to your people."

**1:11** And Naomi said, "Return now, my daughters; and why are you going with me? Do I still have sons in my womb, that they may become husbands for you?

**1:12** Return now, my daughters, because I have grown too old to belong to a husband. For I said, 'There is hope for me to belong to a husband, and I will bear sons,'

**1:13** would you wait for them until they grow up? Or would you be held back for them so as not to belong to a husband? No indeed, my daughters, for it has been made more bitter for me than for you, because the hand of the Lord has gone out against me."

**1:14** And they lifted up their voice and wept again; and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law and returned to her people, but Ruth followed her.

**1:15** And Naomi said to Ruth, "Look, your sister-in-law has returned to her people and to her gods; return now, you also, after your sister-in-law."

**1:16** But Ruth said, "May it not happen to me to leave you or to turn back from behind you, because wherever you go, I will go, and wherever you lodge, I will lodge; your people are my people, and your God is my God;

**1:17** and wherever you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the Lord do such things to me and add such things as well, for death will separate between me and you."

**1:18** And when Naomi saw that she was determined to go with her, she stopped speaking to her anymore.

**1:19** So both of them went until they arrived at Bethlehem. And the whole city resounded about her, and they said, "Is this Naomi?"

**1:20** And she said to them, "Do not call me Naomi; call me Bitter, because the Sufficient One has been exceedingly bitter toward me.

**1:21** I went away full, and the Lord has brought me back empty. And why do you call me Naomi, when the Lord has humbled me and the Sufficient One has afflicted me?"

**1:22** And Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabite, her daughter-in-law, returning from the country of Moab; and they arrived at Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — The Greek text names Naomi's husband Abimelech (rather than Hebrew Elimelech) and omits the clause introducing Naomi by name here (she is first named in 1:3). Greek agros ("country") literally means "field" or "countryside."
- **1:12** — Greek hoti eipa reads "for I said" where the Hebrew has a hypothetical clause ("even if I said"); the Greek also lacks "tonight," found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **1:16** — Greek reads "May it not happen to me" (optative of apantao) rather than the Hebrew "Do not urge me."
- **1:17** — Literally "for death will separate between me and you"; in this oath formula the Greek renders the Hebrew idiom elliptically, meaning that only death will part them.
- **1:20** — The Greek translates Hebrew Mara into the Greek name Pikran ("Bitter"), preserving the wordplay with "has been exceedingly bitter," and renders Hebrew Shaddai as ho hikanos ("the Sufficient One"; also in 1:21).
