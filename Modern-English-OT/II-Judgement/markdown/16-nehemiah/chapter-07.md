## Translator Notes

- **7:2** — Greek gives both men the name Hananiah (where Hebrew has Hanani for Nehemiah's brother) and uses the loanword beira (from Hebrew birah, 'citadel, fortress').
- **7:3** — Greek heos hama to helio (lit. 'until with the sun') is an idiom for sunrise, where Hebrew reads 'until the sun is hot'; the commands to shut and bolt the doors are also passive in Greek.
- **7:5** — Greek renders Hebrew genealogical enrollment with synodia ('company, traveling party') and reads 'God' rather than Hebrew 'my God'.
- **7:7** — The Greek list has fourteen names—adding Ezra and transliterating Hebrew mispar ('number') as the name Masphar—with distinct forms Naamiah, Maldocheus, Maspheran, and Batoei, followed by 'the men of the son of Israel' (Hebrew: 'the number of the men of the people of Israel').
- **7:33** — Here and in 7:34 Greek transliterates Hebrew aher ('other') as aar (Nabia Aar; Elam Aar, with 'men' and 1,250 vs. Hebrew 'sons' and 1,254). Earlier Greek-distinct names and totals also differ from Hebrew: Jobab (7:11), Zathuia (7:13; 840), Zathu (7:14; 860), Azgad (7:17; 2,328), Adikam (7:18), Batoei (7:19), Bethlehem and Atopha split as 'sons' (7:26; 123 and 56), Kiriath-jearim's group (7:29; 721), Tamal (7:30; Hebrew Geba), and Halia (7:32; Hebrew Ai).
- **7:40** — Greek omits the family name Immer (Hebrew: 'the sons of Immer, 1,052'). Elsewhere in 7:37-45 and 7:61 Greek preserves distinct readings including Hadia (7:37), Sananat with 930 vs. Hebrew 3,930 (7:38), Jodae (7:39), Phasedur (7:41), Kabdiel and Thuduia (7:43), repeated 'Ater, son of Ater' and Aku (7:45), and Thelmeleth, Harsha, Eron, and Jemer (7:61).
- **7:46** — Nethinim transliterates Greek Natheineim (Hebrew for temple servants); in 7:46-59 Greek omits Gahar (7:49), splits Pochereth and Zebaim into two families (7:59), and preserves distinct name forms including Gabaoth, Kira, Asuia, Uzzi, Mesinom, Nephosasi, Bakbu, Harum, Basaoth, Hadasan, Barkue, Sedirath, Hemath, Aseia, Jelel, Hegel, and Emim.
- **7:64** — Greek enchisteuthesan (passive of anchisteuo, 'act as next of kin, redeem') applies the translator's standard equivalent for Hebrew g-'-l ('redeem') to its homonym meaning 'exclude as defiled.' Greek also reads 'of the company' (synodias, as in 7:5) and singular 'it was not found.'
- **7:65** — Hasersatha transliterates the Persian title ha-tirshata ('the governor') without an article, and 'to give light' (photison) renders Hebrew 'with Urim and Thummim' based on the root meaning 'light.'
- **7:70** — Greek mechonoth (also 7:72) is an untranslated transliteration (likely confusing Hebrew kothnoth, 'tunics', with mekonoth, 'stands'), with 'thirty' vs. Hebrew 'five hundred thirty' and Nehemiah as recipient rather than 'the governor' as donor. Greek also differs notably in 7:66-72: totals of 42,308 (7:66), 337 (7:67), and 2,700 donkeys (7:69, with 7:68 and camels absent); 'treasury of the year' (7:71, etous vs. ergou, 'work'); and the omission of the first part of 7:72.
