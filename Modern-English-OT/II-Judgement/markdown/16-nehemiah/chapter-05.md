## Text

**5:1** And there was a great outcry of the people and their wives against their brothers the Jews.

**5:2** And there were some saying, "With our sons and with our daughters we are many; and we will get grain, and we will eat and live."

**5:3** And there are some saying, "Our fields and our vineyards and our houses, we are pledging them; and we will get grain and eat."

**5:4** And there are some saying, "We have borrowed silver for the taxes of the king—our fields and our vineyards and our houses.

**5:5** Now like the flesh of our brothers is our flesh, like our sons are their sons; and look, we are subjugating our sons and our daughters to be female slaves, and some of our daughters are being subjugated; and there is no power in our hand, and our fields and our vineyards belong to the nobles."

**5:6** And I was very grieved when I heard their outcry and these words.

**5:7** And my heart took counsel within me, and I contended against the nobles and the rulers and said to them, "You are demanding an exaction, each man from his brother." And I held a great assembly against them,

**5:8** and I said to them, "We have bought our brothers the Jews who were being sold to the nations of our own free will; and are you selling our brothers?" And they kept silent and did not find a word.

**5:9** And I said, "The thing that you are doing is not good; not in this way, in the fear of our God, will you depart from the reproach of our enemies.

**5:10** And my brothers and my acquaintances and I placed silver and grain with them, yet we gave up this exaction.

**5:11** Now you yourselves return this very day their fields, their vineyards, their olive trees, and their houses, and from the silver, the grain and the wine, and bring them out for yourselves."

**5:12** And they said, "We will give it back and will require nothing from them; thus we will do just as you say." And I called the priests and made them swear to do according to this word.

**5:13** And I shook out my cloak and said, "Thus may God shake out every man who will not uphold this word from his house and from his labor, and thus he will be shaken out and empty." And all the assembly said, "Amen," and they praised the Lord; and the people performed this word.

**5:14** From the day that he commanded me to be their ruler in the land of Judah, from the twentieth year even until the thirty-second year of Arsenatha, twelve years, I and our brothers did not eat their extortion.

**5:15** And the former extortions that were before me, they laid heavily upon them, and they took from them in bread and in wine, lastly silver, forty didrachmas, and their shaken-out ones exercise authority over the people; and I did not do so because of the fear of God.

**5:16** And in the work of the wall of these people I did not take hold; I did not buy a field; and all those who were gathered there were at the work.

**5:17** And the Jews, one hundred and fifty men, and those coming to us from the nations around us, were at my table.

**5:18** And there was prepared for one day one calf, and choice sheep and a goat were prepared for me, and every ten days, among all things, wine in abundance; and along with these things I did not demand the bread of extortion, because the servitude was heavy upon this people.

**5:19** Remember me, God, for good, for all that I have done for this people.

## Translator Notes

- **5:5** — Greek uses the feminine plural "female slaves" (doulas) for both sons and daughters, and reads "belong to the nobles" (tois entimois) where the Hebrew text reads "belong to others."
- **5:11** — Greek reads nominative "yourselves" (autoi) instead of "to them," has "from the silver" (apo tou argyriou) rather than "the hundredth part," omits "oil," and ends with the imperative "bring them out for yourselves."
- **5:14** — Greek preserves the distinct proper name "Arsenatha" (corresponding to King Artaxerxes) and renders the Hebrew term for "governor" as "extortion" (bia, also in vv. 15 and 18).
- **5:15** — Greek uses "forty didrachmas" (the standard Septuagint equivalent for shekels) and again renders servants as "shaken-out ones" (ektetinagmenoi; see 4:10).
- **5:16** — Greek has the negative "I did not take hold" (ouk ekratesa) and plural "of these people" (touton), differing from the positive Hebrew reading ("I held fast in the work of this wall").
