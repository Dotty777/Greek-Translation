## Text

**2:1** And it happened in the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, that wine was before me; and I took the wine and gave it to the king, and there was no one else before him.

**2:2** And the king said to me, "Why is your face sad, and you are not unwell? This is nothing other than sadness of heart." And I was very greatly afraid.

**2:3** And I said to the king, "May the king live forever. Why should my face not be sad, because the city, the house of the tombs of my fathers, has been laid waste, and its gates were devoured by fire?"

**2:4** And the king said to me, "What is this that you are asking about?" And I prayed to the God of heaven.

**2:5** And I said to the king, "If it is good to the king, and if your servant is pleasing before you, that you send him to Judah, to the city of the tombs of my fathers, then I will rebuild it."

**2:6** And the king, and the concubine who was sitting beside him, said to me, "How long will your journey be, and when will you return?" And it was pleasing before the king, and he sent me, and I gave him a set time.

**2:7** And I said to the king, "If it is good to the king, let him give me letters to the governors beyond the River, so that they may let me pass through until I come to Judah,

**2:8** and a letter to Asaph, keeper of the park that belongs to the king, so that he may give me timber to roof the gates, and for the wall of the city, and for the house that I will enter." And the king gave them to me, according to the good hand of God.

**2:9** And I came to the governors beyond the River, and gave them the letters of the king; and the king sent with me commanders of an army and horsemen.

**2:10** And Sanballat the Horonite heard, and it was displeasing to them that a man had come to seek good for the sons of Israel.

**2:11** And I came to Jerusalem, and I was there three days.

**2:12** And I arose by night, I and a few men with me, and I did not tell anyone what God was putting into my heart to do with Israel, and there was no animal with me except the animal on which I was riding.

**2:13** And I went out by the Gate of Golela, and toward the mouth of the Spring of the Figs, and to the Dung Gate; and I was crushing at the wall of Jerusalem, which they are tearing down, and its gates were devoured by fire.

**2:14** And I passed on to the Gate of Aina and to the Pool of the King, and there was no place for the animal under me to pass through.

**2:15** And I was going up along the wall, up the torrents, by night, and I was crushing at the wall; and I was at the Valley Gate, and I returned.

**2:16** And the guards did not know why I had gone and what I was doing; and until then I had not told the Jews, or the priests, or the nobles, or the rest who were doing the works.

**2:17** And I said to them, "You see the distress in which we are, how Jerusalem is desolate and its gates were given to fire. Come, and let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, and we will no longer be a reproach."

**2:18** And I told them of the hand of God, which is good upon me, and also about the words of the king that he spoke to me; and I said, "Let us rise and build." And their hands were strengthened for good.

**2:19** And Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the Ammonite servant, and Geshem the Arab heard, and they mocked us, and came upon us and said, "What is this thing that you are doing? Or are you rebelling against the king?"

**2:20** And I returned word to them and said to them, "The God of heaven, He will grant us success, and we are His pure servants, and let us build; but for you there is no portion, nor righteousness, nor memorial in Jerusalem."

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Greek text reads 'before me' (Hebrew 'before him') and 'there was no one else before him' (Hebrew 'I had not been sad in his presence'), and spells Artaxerxes as Arsathertha.
- **2:2** — Greek metriazon (lit. 'being moderate') is used in Hellenistic Greek as a euphemism for being slightly unwell or indisposed; poneron and poneria (lit. 'bad/evil') here denote a sad countenance and distress of heart.
- **2:10** — This Septuagint text omits Tobiah the Ammonite servant here while retaining the plural 'to them', and in 2:12 reads 'with Israel' where the Hebrew has 'for Jerusalem'.
- **2:13** — Distinctive Septuagint readings here include 'Golela' (Hebrew 'Valley'), 'Spring of the Figs' (Hebrew 'Jackal/Dragon Spring'), and syntribon ('crushing', from reading the Hebrew verb for 'inspect' as 'shatter', also in 2:15; in 2:15 'along the wall, up the torrents' likewise reflects a distinctive Greek reading).
- **2:20** — Greek katharoi ('pure/clean') reflects a variant reading of the Hebrew consonant group translated 'we will arise' in the Masoretic Text.
