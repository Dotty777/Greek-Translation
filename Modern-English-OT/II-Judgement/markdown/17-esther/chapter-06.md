## Text

**6:1** Now the Lord removed sleep from the king that night, and he told his instructor to bring in memorial records of the days to read to him.

**6:2** And he found the records written concerning Mordecai, how he had reported to the king concerning the two eunuchs of the king, when they were keeping guard and sought to lay hands on Artaxerxes.

**6:3** And the king said, "What honor or favor have we done for Mordecai?" And the servants of the king said, "You have done nothing for him."

**6:4** Now while the king was inquiring about Mordecai's goodwill, look, Haman was in the courtyard. And the king said, "Who is in the courtyard?" Now Haman had come in to tell the king to hang Mordecai on the pole that he had prepared.

**6:5** And the servants of the king said, "Look, Haman is standing in the courtyard." And the king said, "Call him."

**6:6** And the king said to Haman, "What should I do for the man whom I wish to honor?" And Haman said within himself, "Whom does the king wish to honor except me?"

**6:7** And he said to the king, "For a man whom the king wishes to honor,

**6:8** let the king's attendants bring a fine linen robe that the king wears, and a horse on which the king rides,

**6:9** and let him give them to one of the illustrious friends of the king, and let him clothe the man whom the king loves, and mount him on the horse, and let him proclaim through the square of the city, saying, 'Thus it will be for every man whom the king honors.'"

**6:10** And the king said to Haman, "You have spoken well. Do so for Mordecai the Jew, who serves in the courtyard, and let no word of yours fail from what you have spoken."

**6:11** So Haman took the robe and the horse, and clothed Mordecai and mounted him on the horse, and went through the square of the city and kept proclaiming, saying, "Thus it will be for every man whom the king wishes to honor."

**6:12** Then Mordecai returned to the courtyard, while Haman went back to his own home, grieving with his head covered.

**6:13** And Haman recounted what had happened to him to Zosara his wife and to his friends. And his friends and his wife said to him, "If Mordecai is of the race of the Jews, you have begun to be humbled before him; you will surely fall. You will by no means be able to defend yourself against him, because a living God is with him."

**6:14** While they were still speaking, the eunuchs arrived, hurrying Haman to the drinking feast that Esther had prepared.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("sleep fled from the king"), the Septuagint explicitly credits the Lord with removing the king's sleep; "his instructor" translates Greek didaskalos ("teacher"), denoting the royal reader or chronicler.
- **6:2** — Throughout the Septuagint of Esther, the Persian king is named Artaxerxes rather than Ahasuerus as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **6:12** — "With his head covered" renders the idiomatic Greek phrase kata kephales (literally "down over [his] head"), an elliptical expression for covering one's head in grief or shame.
- **6:13** — "Because a living God is with him" is a theological statement distinctive to the Septuagint; the anarthrous Greek phrase theos zon ("a living God") can also be rendered "the living God."
