## Text

**9:1** For in the twelfth month, on the thirteenth of the month, which is Adar, the letters written by the king arrived.

**9:2** On that very day the opponents of the Jews perished; for no one resisted, fearing them.

**9:3** For the rulers of the satraps and the governors and the royal scribes honored the Jews; for the fear of Mordecai lay upon them.

**9:4** For the decree of the king had arrived to be spoken of throughout the whole kingdom.

**9:6** And in that very city the Jews killed five hundred men,

**9:7** both Pharsan and Nestain and Delphon and Phasga,

**9:8** and Pharadatha and Barsa and Sarbacha,

**9:9** and Marmasima and Rouphaeus and Arsaeus and Zabouthaeus,

**9:10** the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the Bugaean, the enemy of the Jews; and they took plunder

**9:11** on that very day; and the number of those who had perished in Susa was submitted to the king.

**9:12** And the king said to Esther, "The Jews have destroyed five hundred men in the city of Susa; and in the surrounding region, how do you think they have acted? What more, then, do you petition, and it will be yours?"

**9:13** And Esther said to the king, "Let it be granted to the Jews to act likewise tomorrow, so as to hang the ten sons of Haman."

**9:14** And he permitted it to be done so, and he handed over to the Jews of the city the bodies of the sons of Haman to hang.

**9:15** And the Jews in Susa gathered together on the fourteenth of Adar and killed three hundred men, and they plundered nothing.

**9:16** And the rest of the Jews who were in the kingdom gathered together and came to their own aid, and rested from their foes; for they destroyed fifteen thousand of them on the thirteenth of Adar, and they plundered nothing.

**9:17** And they rested on the fourteenth of the same month, and observed it as a day of rest with joy and gladness.

**9:18** But the Jews who were in the city of Susa gathered together on the fourteenth as well and did not rest; and they also observed the fifteenth with joy and gladness.

**9:19** For this reason, therefore, the Jews who are scattered in every outlying province observe the fourteenth of Adar as a good day with gladness, sending portions each to his neighbor. And those who dwell in the metropolises also observe the fifteenth of Adar as a good festivity, sending out portions to their neighbors as well.

**9:20** And Mordecai wrote these words in a book, and sent them out to all the Jews who were in the kingdom of Artaxerxes, to those near and to those far away,

**9:21** to establish these days as good days, and to observe the fourteenth and the fifteenth of Adar;

**9:22** for on these days the Jews rested from their enemies; and to observe the whole month in which it was turned for them, which was Adar, from mourning into joy and from grief into a good day, as good days of wedding feasts and gladness, sending out portions to friends and to the poor.

**9:23** And the Jews accepted just as Mordecai had written to them,

**9:24** how Haman son of Hammedatha, the Macedonian, waged war against them, just as he had set a decree and a lot to annihilate them,

**9:25** and how he went in to the king, saying to hang Mordecai; but all the evils that he undertook to bring upon the Jews came upon him, and both he himself and his children were hanged.

**9:26** Therefore these days were called Phrourai because of the lots, because in their language they are called Phrourai, because of the words of this letter, and all that they had suffered because of these things, and all that had happened to them,

**9:27** and he established it; and the Jews accepted it upon themselves and upon their seed and upon those joined to them, and they will certainly not act otherwise. And these days are a memorial observed generation after generation, and in every city, family, and province.

**9:28** And these days of Phrourai will be observed for all time, and their memorial will never fail from the generations.

**9:29** And Queen Esther, daughter of Amminadab, and Mordecai the Jew wrote all that they had done and the confirmation of the letter of Phrourai.

**9:31** And Mordecai and Queen Esther established it for themselves upon themselves, and at that time established their counsel as well, against their own health.

**9:32** And Esther established it by word forever, and it was written for a memorial.

## Translator Notes

- **9:4** — The Greek compresses this verse ("For the decree of the king had arrived to be spoken of throughout the whole kingdom") and omits verse 5 entirely compared to the Hebrew text.
- **9:10** — Verses 7-9 list eleven Greek forms for the names of Haman's "ten sons" (splitting the first name into Pharsan and Nestain) and call Haman "the Bugaean"; uniquely, the Greek text also reads "and they took plunder" (kai dierpasan) without a negation.
- **9:16** — The Septuagint reads "fifteen thousand" (myrious pentakischilious), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads seventy-five thousand.
- **9:26** — "Phrourai" is the Septuagint form of the festival name Purim (and in 9:29 Esther's father is named "Amminadab" rather than Abihail).
- **9:31** — Verse 30 is absent in the Septuagint, and verse 31 has compressed Greek syntax stating that Mordecai and Esther established their counsel "against their own health" (kata tes hygieias heauton), corresponding to the Hebrew mention of fasting and lamentation.
