## Text

**8:1** And on that very day King Artaxerxes bestowed on Esther all that belonged to Haman the slanderer, and Mordecai was summoned by the king; for Esther had revealed that he was related to her.

**8:2** And the king took the signet ring that he had taken away from Haman and gave it to Mordecai, and Esther appointed Mordecai over all the property of Haman.

**8:3** And she spoke once more to the king, and fell at his feet, and petitioned him to remove the malice of Haman and all that he had done to the Jews.

**8:4** And the king extended the golden scepter to Esther; and Esther rose up to stand before the king.

**8:5** And Esther said, "If it seems good to you and I have found favor, let word be sent that the letters sent by Haman be revoked, those written that the Jews who are in your kingdom should perish.

**8:6** For how will I be able to see the affliction of my people, and how will I be able to be saved in the destruction of my homeland?"

**8:7** And the king said to Esther, "If I have given and graciously granted to you all the possessions of Haman, and have hanged him on a wooden pole because he laid hands on the Jews, what more do you seek?

**8:8** You also write in my name as seems good to you, and seal it with my signet ring; for whatever is written at the king's command and is sealed with my signet ring cannot be contradicted."

**8:9** And the scribes were summoned in the first month, which is Nisan, on the twenty-third of the same year, and it was written to the Jews, all that he commanded to the stewards and to the rulers of the satraps from India to Ethiopia, to one hundred twenty-seven satrapies, province by province, according to their own language.

**8:10** And it was written through the king and sealed with his signet ring, and they sent out the letters by couriers,

**8:11** as he commanded them to observe their laws in every city, and to come to their own aid and to deal with their adversaries and their opponents as they wished,

**8:12** on one day throughout the whole kingdom of Artaxerxes, on the thirteenth of the twelfth month, which is Adar. Of these things, the following is the copy of the letter: "The Great King Artaxerxes to the rulers of the provinces in the one hundred twenty-seven satrapies from India to Ethiopia, and to those who are loyal to our interests, greetings. Many, being honored more frequently through the abundant kindness of those who confer benefits, have grown too proud, and not only do they seek to harm those who are subject to us, but being unable to bear their excess, they even undertake to scheme against their own benefactors; and not only removing gratitude from among people, but also puffed up by the boasts of those ignorant of goodness, they suppose that they will escape the evil-hating justice of God, who always sees all things. Often also, the persuasion of friends entrusted to manage affairs has made many of those appointed to positions of authority partakers of innocent blood and plunged them into irremediable calamities, when with the false reasoning of malice they misled the sincere goodwill of those who hold power. Now it is possible to observe, not so much from the older histories as we have handed down, as when you examine what lies right at hand, how many things have been impiously perpetrated by the pestilent wickedness of those who wield power unworthily, and to pay attention to what comes afterward, so that we may render the kingdom undisturbed for all people with peace, making use of the changes, and always judging the matters that come before our eyes with a more equitable response. For thus Haman son of Hammedatha, a Macedonian, in truth an alien to Persian blood and far removed from our kindness, having been received as a guest by us, obtained the benevolence that we hold toward every nation to such a degree that he was publicly proclaimed our father and, bowed down to by all, continued as the second person to the royal throne; yet, unable to restrain his arrogance, he endeavored to deprive us of our rule and our life, demanding for destruction, through intricate false reasonings of craftiness, both Mordecai, our savior and continual benefactor, and Esther, the blameless partner of the kingdom, together with their entire nation; for by these means he thought, having caught us isolated, to transfer the dominion of the Persians to the Macedonians. But we find that the Jews, who were handed over to annihilation by that thrice-wicked man, are not evildoers, but live under the most righteous laws and are sons of the Most High, Greatest, Living God, who directs the kingdom for both us and our ancestors in the finest order. You will therefore do well not to act upon the letters sent by Haman son of Hammedatha, because the very one who perpetrated these things has been crucified at the gates of Susa with his entire household, since God, who rules over all things, has swiftly rendered to him the judgment he deserved; and having posted the copy of this letter openly in every place, permit the Jews to observe their own customs, and lend them support so that they may defend themselves against those who attack them in the time of distress on the thirteenth of the twelfth month, Adar, on that very day; for God, who holds dominion over all things, has made this day gladness for them instead of destruction for the chosen race. Therefore you also, among your named festivals, observe a notable day with all feasting, so that both now and afterward it may be deliverance for us and for the well-disposed Persians, but for those who plot against us, a memorial of destruction. And every city or province without exception that does not act according to these things will be consumed with spear and fire in wrath; not only impassable to humans, it will also be rendered most hateful to wild beasts and birds for all time."

**8:13** And the copies were to be posted in full view throughout the whole kingdom, and all the Jews were to be ready for this day to fight against their foes.

**8:14** So the horsemen went out, hastening to carry out what was spoken by the king; and the decree was also posted in Susa.

**8:15** And Mordecai went out clothed in the royal robe, wearing a golden crown and a purple diadem of fine linen; and when those in Susa saw him, they rejoiced.

**8:16** And for the Jews there was light and gladness,

**8:17** in every city and province wherever the decree was posted, wherever the proclamation was posted: joy and gladness for the Jews, a banquet and gladness; and many of the nations were circumcised and lived as Jews because of the fear of the Jews.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — The Septuagint consistently names the Persian king "Artaxerxes" rather than "Ahasuerus."
- **8:9** — The Septuagint dates the decree to "the first month, which is Nisan" (Greek Nisa), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the third month, which is Sivan."
- **8:12** — This verse incorporates the full text of Artaxerxes's royal letter (known as Addition E in the Greek tradition), which identifies Haman as "a Macedonian" rather than an Agagite and has no counterpart in the Hebrew text.
- **8:17** — The Septuagint preserves a doublet ("wherever the decree was posted, wherever the proclamation was posted") and explicitly adds that many of the nations "were circumcised" (perietemonto) and "lived as Jews" (ioudaizon).
