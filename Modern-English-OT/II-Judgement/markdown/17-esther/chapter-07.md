## Text

**7:1** Now the king and Haman went in to drink with the queen.

**7:2** And the king said to Esther on the second day during the drinking banquet, "What is it, Queen Esther, and what is your request, and what is your petition? And let it be yours, up to half of my kingdom."

**7:3** And answering, she said, "If I have found favor before the king, let my life be given at my request, and my word at my petition.

**7:4** For we have been sold, both I and my people, for destruction and plunder and slavery, we and our children as male and female servants, and I disregarded it; for the slanderer is not worthy of the king's court."

**7:5** And the king said, "Who is this who dared to do this thing?"

**7:6** And Esther said, "A hostile man, this wicked Haman." And Haman was terrified before the king and the queen.

**7:7** And the king rose up from the banquet and went into the garden; and Haman was pleading with the queen, for he saw that he was in trouble.

**7:8** And the king returned from the garden, and Haman had fallen upon the couch, petitioning the queen; and the king said, "So are you even assaulting my wife in my house?" And when Haman heard this, he was confounded in his face.

**7:9** And Bougathan, one of the eunuchs, said to the king, "Look, Haman has even prepared a wooden pole for Mordecai, the one who spoke concerning the king, and a wooden pole of fifty cubits stands upright on Haman's property." And the king said, "Let him be crucified on it."

**7:10** And Haman was hanged on the wooden pole that had been prepared for Mordecai. And then the king ceased from his wrath.

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — The Greek text reads ho logos mou ("my word") rather than ho laos mou ("my people"), and has he psyche ("my life") without an explicit possessive pronoun.
- **7:4** — The Septuagint distinctively adds "and slavery" and renders Esther's statement "and I disregarded it; for the slanderer (ho diabolos) is not worthy of the king's court," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **7:8** — The Greek reads "he was confounded in his face" (dietrape to prosopo), rather than the Hebrew "they covered Haman's face."
- **7:9** — "Bougathan" is the Septuagint name of the eunuch (corresponding to Harbonah in the Hebrew); Greek xylon ("wooden pole") and stauroo ("be crucified/impaled") reflect Hellenistic execution terminology.
