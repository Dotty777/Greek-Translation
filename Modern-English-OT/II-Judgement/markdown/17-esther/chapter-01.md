## Text

**1:1** In the second year of the reign of Artaxerxes the great king, on the first day of Nisan, Mordecai, son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, from the tribe of Benjamin—a Jewish man living in the city of Susa, a great man serving in the court of the king—saw a dream. Now he was from the captivity that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon took captive from Jerusalem with Jeconiah the king of Judea. And this was his dream: Look, cries and uproar, thunder and earthquake, turmoil upon the earth. And look, two great dragons came forward, both ready to wrestle; and a great cry came from them, and at their cry every nation was made ready for war so as to make war against a nation of the righteous. And look, a day of darkness and gloom, tribulation and distress, affliction and great turmoil upon the earth; and the whole righteous nation was thrown into turmoil, fearing their own calamities, and they prepared to perish; and they cried out to God. And from their cry there came, as if from a small spring, a great river, much water; light and the sun rose, and the lowly were exalted and devoured the honored ones. And when Mordecai awoke, having seen this dream and what God had purposed to do, he kept it in his heart, and in every way he wanted to understand it until night. And Mordecai rested in the court with Gabatha and Tharra, the two eunuchs of the king who guarded the court; and he heard their reasonings and investigated their concerns, and learned that they were preparing to lay hands on Artaxerxes the king. And he informed the king about them; and the king examined the two eunuchs, and after they confessed, they were led away. And the king wrote these matters as a memorial, and Mordecai wrote about these matters; and the king ordered Mordecai to serve in the court, and gave him gifts for these things. And Haman son of Hammedatha, a Bugaean, was honored before the king, and he sought to harm Mordecai and his people because of the two eunuchs of the king. And it happened after these events, in the days of Artaxerxes—this Artaxerxes ruled over one hundred twenty-seven provinces from India—

**1:2** in those very days, when King Artaxerxes was enthroned in the city of Susa,

**1:3** in the third year of his reign, he held a banquet for his friends and the rest of the nations and the honored men of the Persians and Medes and the rulers of the satraps.

**1:4** And after these things, after he had shown them the wealth of his kingdom and the glory of the gladness of his wealth for one hundred eighty days;

**1:5** when the days of the wedding were completed, the king held a drinking feast for the nations who were found in the city for six days in the court of the king's house,

**1:6** adorned with fine linen and cotton stretched on cords of fine linen and purple, on cubes of gold and silver, on pillars of Parian marble and stone; couches of gold and silver on a stone pavement of emerald stone and mother-of-pearl and Parian stone, and transparent coverings variously adorned with flowers, with roses strewn all around;

**1:7** cups of gold and silver, and a small goblet of carbuncle set out, worth thirty thousand talents; abundant and sweet wine, which the king himself drank.

**1:8** Now this drinking feast did not take place according to an established law; rather, so the king wished, and he ordered the stewards to carry out his wish and that of the people.

**1:9** And Astin the queen held a drinking feast for the women in the royal palace where King Artaxerxes was.

**1:10** And on the seventh day, having become merry, the king told Aman, Mazan, Tharra, Boraze, Zatholtha, Abataza, and Tharaba, the seven eunuchs who were attendants of King Artaxerxes,

**1:11** to bring the queen in to him, for her to reign as queen and to put the diadem on her, and to show her to the rulers and to the nations, her beauty, because she was beautiful.

**1:12** And Astin the queen did not listen to him to come with the eunuchs; and the king was grieved and became angry,

**1:13** and he said to his friends, "Astin has spoken in this way; therefore make a law and a judgment concerning this."

**1:14** And Arkesaeus, Sarsathaeus, and Malesear came to him, the rulers of the Persians and Medes who were near the king, the first ones sitting beside the king,

**1:15** and they reported to him according to the laws what must be done to Astin the queen, because she did not do the things commanded by the king through the eunuchs.

**1:16** And Muchaeus said to the king and the rulers, "Astin the queen has wronged not only the king, but also all the rulers and the leaders of the king."

**1:17** For indeed he recounted to them the words of the queen, and how she had contradicted the king. "Just as, then, she contradicted King Artaxerxes,

**1:18** so today the rest of the noble ladies of the rulers of the Persians and Medes, having heard the things spoken to the king by her, will dare likewise to dishonor their husbands.

**1:19** If, therefore, it seems good to the king, let him issue a royal decree, and let it be written according to the laws of the Medes and Persians, and let him not act otherwise, nor let the queen come in to him anymore; and let the king give her royal position to a woman better than she.

**1:20** And let the law from the king, whichever he makes, be heard in his kingdom; and thus all the women will bestow honor on their own husbands, from poor to rich."

**1:21** And the word pleased the king and the rulers; and the king did just as Muchaeus had spoken,

**1:22** and he sent to all the kingdom, province by province, according to their own language, so that they might be feared in their houses.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Includes the Septuagint prologue (Addition A) narrating Mordecai's dream and his discovery of the plot by Gabatha and Tharra (distinct from Bigthan and Teresh in Hebrew 2:21). Throughout the book, the Greek names the king Artaxerxes (Hebrew Ahasuerus) and calls Haman a Bugaean (Greek Bougaios, Hebrew Agagite), and here notes one hundred twenty-seven provinces "from India" without adding "to Ethiopia."
- **1:5** — The Greek specifies "the days of the wedding" (Hebrew "these days") and gives the banquet's duration as "six days" (Hebrew "seven days"), although 1:10 refers to "the seventh day."
- **1:9** — Greek Astin is the Septuagint form of the queen's name, corresponding to Vashti in the Hebrew text.
- **1:10** — The Greek names of the seven eunuchs (Aman, Mazan, Tharra, Boraze, Zatholtha, Abataza, and Tharaba) differ markedly from the Hebrew list; likewise 1:14 names only three rulers (Arkesaeus, Sarsathaeus, and Malesear) instead of seven, and 1:16 names Muchaeus (Hebrew Memucan).
- **1:17** — The opening clause is a narratorial parenthesis explaining that Muchaeus recounted the queen's words to the rulers before his direct speech resumes in the second clause.
