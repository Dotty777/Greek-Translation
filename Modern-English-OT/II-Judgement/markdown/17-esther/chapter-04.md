## Text

**4:1** Now when Mordecai learned what was being done, he tore his garments and put on sackcloth and sprinkled ashes on himself; and rushing out through the square of the city, he cried out with a loud voice, "A nation that has done no wrong is being done away with!"

**4:2** And he came as far as the king's gate and stopped, for it was not permitted for him to enter the courtyard wearing sackcloth and ashes.

**4:3** And in every province where the letters were posted, there was outcry and lamentation and great mourning among the Jews; they spread sackcloth and ashes for themselves.

**4:4** And the maids and the eunuchs of the queen came in and reported to her; and she was troubled when she heard what had happened, and she sent to clothe Mordecai and to take away his sackcloth, but he was not persuaded.

**4:5** Then Esther summoned Hachrathaeus her eunuch, who attended her, and sent him to learn for her from Mordecai the exact facts.

**4:7** And Mordecai showed him what had happened and the promise of ten thousand talents that Haman had promised to the king for the treasury, in order to destroy the Jews.

**4:8** And he gave him the copy that was posted in Susa for them to be destroyed, to show to Esther; and he told him to charge her to go in and appeal to the king and petition him on behalf of the people: "Remember the days of your lowliness, how you were brought up by my hand, because Haman, who is second to the king, has spoken against us for death; call upon the Lord and speak to the king on our behalf, and deliver us from death."

**4:9** So Hachrathaeus went in and spoke all these words to her.

**4:10** And Esther said to Hachrathaeus, "Go to Mordecai and say,

**4:11** 'All the nations of the kingdom know that for every man or woman who goes in to the king into the inner courtyard without being called, there is no deliverance; only the one to whom the king extends the golden scepter, this one will be saved. And I have not been called to go in to the king, these thirty days.'"

**4:12** And Hachrathaeus reported to Mordecai all the words of Esther.

**4:13** And Mordecai said to Hachrathaeus, "Go and say to her, 'Esther, do not say to yourself that you alone will be saved in the kingdom above all the Jews;

**4:14** for if you refuse to listen at this time, from another place help and protection will come for the Jews, but you and your father's house will perish. And who knows whether you became queen for this time?'"

**4:15** And Esther sent the one who had come to her back to Mordecai, saying,

**4:16** "Go, assemble the Jews who are in Susa, and fast for me, and do not eat or drink for three days, night and day; and I also and my maids will abstain from food; and then I will go in to the king contrary to the law, even if I must perish."

**4:17** And Mordecai went and did everything that Esther had commanded him. And he prayed to the Lord, remembering all the works of the Lord, and said, "Lord, Lord, King ruling over all, for all things are in Your authority, and there is no one who opposes You when You will to save Israel; for You made heaven and earth and every wondrous thing in the realm under heaven, and You are Lord of all, and there is no one who will resist You, the Lord. You know all things; You know, Lord, that not in insolence nor in pride nor in love of glory did I do this: not bowing down to proud Haman. For I would gladly have kissed the soles of his feet for the deliverance of Israel, but I did this so that I would not place the glory of a man above the glory of God. And I will not bow down to anyone except You, my Lord, and I will not do these things in pride. And now, Lord God, the King, the God of Abraham, spare Your people, for they are looking upon us for ruin and have desired to destroy Your inheritance from the beginning. Do not overlook Your portion, which You redeemed for Yourself out of the land of Egypt. Hear my prayer and be merciful to Your heritage, and turn our mourning into feasting, so that living we may sing praise to Your name, Lord, and do not wipe out the mouth of those who praise You." And all Israel cried out with their strength, because their death was before their eyes. And Esther the queen fled for refuge to the Lord, seized in the agony of death. And taking off her garments of glory, she put on garments of distress and mourning; and instead of the proud perfumes, she filled her head with ashes and dung; and she greatly humbled her body, and every place of her joyful adornment she filled with her tangled hair. And she prayed to the Lord God of Israel and said, "My Lord, our King, You are the only One; help me, who am alone and have no helper except You, because my danger is in my hand. I heard from my birth in the tribe of my paternal family that You, Lord, took Israel out of all the nations, and our fathers out of all their ancestors, for an eternal inheritance, and You did for them all that You spoke. And now we have sinned before You, and You have handed us over into the hands of our enemies, because we glorified their gods. You are righteous, Lord. And now they have not been satisfied with the bitterness of our slavery, but they have set their hands to remove the decree of Your mouth, and to wipe out Your inheritance, and to stop up the mouth of those who praise You, and to extinguish the glory of Your house and Your altar, and to open the mouth of the nations for the praises of worthless things, and for a king of flesh to be marveled at forever. Do not hand over, Lord, Your scepter to those who do not exist, and do not let them laugh at our fall, but turn their counsel upon themselves; and make a public example of the one who began this against us. Remember, Lord; make Yourself known in the time of our affliction, and give me courage, King of the gods, ruling over every dominion. Put well-ordered speech into my mouth before the lion, and turn his heart to hatred of the one who fights against us, to the destruction of him and of those who agree with him. But deliver us by Your hand, and help me, who am alone and have no one except You, Lord. You have knowledge of all things, and You know that I have hated the glory of the lawless, and I abhor the bed of the uncircumcised and of every foreigner. You know my necessity, that I abhor the sign of my pride that is on my head on the days of my appearance; I abhor it like a menstrual cloth, and I do not wear it on the days of my quiet. And Your maidservant has not eaten at Haman's table, and I have not honored a king's banquet, nor have I drunk the wine of libations; and Your maidservant has not rejoiced from the day of my change until now, except in You, Lord God of Abraham. God, who has power over all, hear the voice of those in despair, and deliver us from the hand of the evildoers, and deliver me from my fear."

## Translator Notes

- **4:5** — "Hachrathaeus" is the Septuagint name (Greek Hachrathaios) of Esther's eunuch, corresponding to Hathach in the Hebrew Masoretic Text. The Greek text also omits verse 4:6.
- **4:14** — The Greek reads "if you refuse to listen" (parakouses), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "if you remain silent."
- **4:16** — Literally "even if it should be for me to perish," reflecting the Septuagint wording rather than the Hebrew "if I perish, I perish."
- **4:17** — In the Septuagint, verse 4:17 incorporates the prayers of Mordecai and Esther (traditionally called Addition C). In Esther's prayer, "my change" refers to her change of status upon entering the palace, and this text reads simply "they have set their hands" without the longer clause found in some manuscripts.
