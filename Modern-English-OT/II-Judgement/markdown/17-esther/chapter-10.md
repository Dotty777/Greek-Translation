## Text

**10:1** Now the king wrote to the kingdom of the land and of the sea.

**10:2** And as for his strength and valor, both the wealth and the glory of his kingdom, look, it is written in the book of the kings of the Persians and Medes as a memorial.

**10:3** And Mordecai acted as deputy to King Artaxerxes, and was great in the kingdom and honored by the Jews; and being loved, he set forth the way of life to all his nation. And Mordecai said, "These things came from God. For I remembered the dream that I saw concerning these matters, for not even a single matter among them passed away: the little spring that became a river, and there was light and sun and much water. Esther is the river, whom the king married and made queen; and the two dragons are I and Haman; and the nations are those who were gathered together to destroy the name of the Jews; and as for my nation, this is Israel, who cried out to God and were saved. And the Lord saved His people, and the Lord delivered us from all these evils; and God performed the signs and the great wonders that have not happened among the nations. For this reason He made two lots, one for the people of God and one for all the nations; and these two lots came to the hour and time and to the day of judgment before God and for all the nations, and God remembered His people and vindicated His own inheritance. And these days will be for them in the month of Adar, on the fourteenth and on the fifteenth of the same month, with assembly and joy and gladness before God throughout the generations forever among His people Israel." In the fourth year of the reign of Ptolemy and Cleopatra, Dositheus, who said he was a priest and a Levite, and Ptolemy his son brought in the preceding letter of Phrourai; they said it was genuine and that Lysimachus, son of Ptolemy, one of those in Jerusalem, had translated it.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The Greek text here has no explicit direct object after "wrote" (literally "wrote to/upon the kingdom"). Some Septuagint manuscripts add "taxes" here (compare the Hebrew, "laid tribute upon the land").
- **10:3** — As throughout Greek Esther, the king is named Artaxerxes (Hebrew Ahasuerus), and the Greek extends the verse with Mordecai's dream interpretation and the translator's colophon; "set forth the way of life" reflects a debated Greek phrase differing from the Hebrew. In the colophon, "Phrourai" is the Septuagint form of Purim, and "was genuine" renders the Greek verb "to be" used absolutely.
