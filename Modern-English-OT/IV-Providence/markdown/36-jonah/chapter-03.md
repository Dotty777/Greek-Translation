## Text

**3:1** And the word of the Lord came to Jonah a second time, saying,

**3:2** "Rise, go to Nineveh, the great city, and proclaim in it according to the former proclamation that I spoke to you."

**3:3** And Jonah rose and went to Nineveh, just as the Lord had spoken. Now Nineveh was a great city to God, about a journey of three days' walk.

**3:4** And Jonah began to enter into the city about one day's journey, and he proclaimed and said, "Three more days, and Nineveh will be overthrown."

**3:5** And the men of Nineveh believed God, and they proclaimed a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them.

**3:6** And the word reached the king of Nineveh; and he rose up from his throne, and took off his robe from himself and wrapped himself in sackcloth, and sat on ashes.

**3:7** And it was proclaimed and spoken in Nineveh from the king and from his nobles, saying, "Let the people and the livestock and the cattle and the sheep taste nothing, nor graze, nor drink water."

**3:8** And the people and the livestock wrapped themselves in sackcloth, and they cried out to God earnestly; and each one turned away from his wicked way and from the injustice that was in their hands, saying,

**3:9** "Who knows if God will relent and turn away from the anger of His wrath, and we will by no means perish?"

**3:10** And God saw their deeds, that they turned away from their wicked ways; and God relented concerning the evil that He had spoken to do to them, and He did not do it.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — The Greek specifies "according to the former proclamation that I spoke to you" in the past tense, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to the proclamation God is about to tell Jonah.
- **3:3** — "A great city to God" (πόλις μεγάλη τῷ θεῷ) preserves the literal Semitic idiom expressing extraordinary size ("great even in God's sight").
- **3:4** — The Septuagint reads "three days" (τρεῖς ἡμέραι) rather than "forty days" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **3:8** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text, where the king's decree continues through this verse, the Greek shifts to past-tense indicative verbs describing the Ninevites' repentance and ends with plural "saying" (λέγοντες), attributing verse 9 to the people.
