## Text

**4:1** And Jonah was grieved with great grief, and he was agitated.

**4:2** And he prayed to the Lord and said, "Oh, Lord, were not these my words while I was still in my land? For this reason I acted beforehand to flee to Tarshish, because I knew that You are merciful and compassionate, slow to anger and abundant in mercy, and relenting over calamities.

**4:3** And now, Sovereign Lord, take my life from me, because it is better for me to die than for me to live."

**4:4** And the Lord said to Jonah, "Are you deeply grieved?"

**4:5** And Jonah went out from the city and sat down opposite the city; and he made a shelter for himself there and was sitting underneath it in the shade, until he could see what would become of the city.

**4:6** And the Lord God commanded a gourd, and it came up over Jonah's head to be shade above his head, to shade him from his troubles; and Jonah rejoiced over the gourd with great joy.

**4:7** And God commanded a worm at dawn on the next day, and it struck the gourd, and it withered away.

**4:8** And it happened, as soon as the sun rose, that God commanded a burning wind of scorching heat, and the sun struck upon Jonah's head; and he grew faint and despaired of his life, and said, "Better for me to die than to live."

**4:9** And God said to Jonah, "Are you deeply grieved over the gourd?" And he said, "I am deeply grieved, to the point of death."

**4:10** And the Lord said, "You had pity on the gourd, for which you did not toil and which you did not nurture, which came into being overnight and overnight perished.

**4:11** And shall I not have pity on Nineveh, the great city, in which more than 120,000 people dwell, who do not know their right hand or their left hand, and many animals?"

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — The Greek describes Jonah's reaction as intense grief and agitation rather than anger, establishing the motif of grief that continues in verses 4 and 9.
- **4:4** — The Greek asks whether Jonah is deeply or exceedingly grieved, taking the underlying Hebrew idiom adverbially rather than asking whether it is right for him to be angry.
- **4:6** — The Greek identifies the plant specifically as a gourd (kolokynthe) and reads "to shade him" rather than "to deliver him" as in the Hebrew text.
- **4:8** — The Greek reads "a burning wind of scorching heat" without specifying an east wind, and says Jonah "despaired of his life" (renounced or gave up on his life) rather than asking to die.
- **4:10** — The Greek verb pheidomai ("spare") with hyper carries the Septuagintal sense "have pity on," connecting Jonah's pity for the gourd with God's sparing of Nineveh in verse 11.
