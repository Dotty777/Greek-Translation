## Text

**2:1** And the Lord commanded a great sea monster to swallow Jonah; and Jonah was in the belly of the sea monster three days and three nights.

**2:2** And Jonah prayed to the Lord his God out of the belly of the sea monster,

**2:3** and said, "I cried out in my distress to the Lord my God, and He listened to me. Out of the belly of Hades You heard my cry, my voice; You hurled me into the depths of the heart of the sea,

**2:4** and rivers surrounded me; all Your swells and Your waves passed over me.

**2:5** And I said, 'I have been driven away from Your eyes; shall I indeed look again toward Your holy temple?'

**2:6** Water poured around me even to the soul, the uttermost abyss surrounded me, my head sank into the clefts of the mountains;

**2:7** I went down into a land whose bars are everlasting barriers; and let the corruption of my life come up, Lord my God.

**2:8** When my soul was failing from me, I remembered the Lord, and may my prayer come to You, into Your holy temple.

**2:9** Those who observe empty and false things have forsaken their mercy.

**2:10** But I, with a voice of praise and thanksgiving, will sacrifice to You; all that I have vowed I will pay to You, for deliverance to the Lord."

**2:11** And the sea monster was commanded, and it cast Jonah out onto the dry land.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — Greek κῆτος denotes a huge sea creature or sea monster rather than an ordinary fish; in the Septuagint versification, this verse opens chapter 2 (corresponding to 1:17 in most English Bibles).
- **2:3** — The Greek places both "my cry" (κραυγῆς μου) and "my voice" (φωνῆς μου) in the genitive as objects of "You heard," and renders Sheol as Hades (ᾅδης).
- **2:6** — The Septuagint reads "uttermost abyss" (ἄβυσσος ... ἐσχάτη, reading the Hebrew word for "reeds" as "end/uttermost") and "my head sank into the clefts of the mountains" rather than seaweed wrapping around Jonah's head.
- **2:7** — The Greek text has a third-person imperative with "corruption" as subject (ἀναβήτω φθορὰ ζωῆς μου, "let the corruption of my life come up") rather than "You brought up my life from the pit" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **2:10** — The closing phrase σωτηρίου τῷ κυρίῳ uses the genitive ("for deliverance to the Lord") rather than the independent clause "salvation belongs to the Lord" found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
