## Text

**11:1** At dawn they were cast off; the king of Israel was cast off. For Israel was a young child, and I loved him, and out of Egypt I summoned his children.

**11:2** Just as I summoned them, so they departed from My presence; they kept sacrificing to the Baals and burning incense to the carved images.

**11:3** And I bound the feet of Ephraim; I took him up onto My arm; they did not know that I have healed them.

**11:4** In the destruction of people, I stretched them out with the bonds of My love, and I will be to them like a man slapping on his cheeks; and I will look toward him, I will prevail over him.

**11:5** Ephraim lived in Egypt, and Assyria itself was his king, because he was unwilling to return.

**11:6** And he grew weak by the sword in his cities, and he ceased in his hands; and they will eat from their counsels.

**11:7** And his people hang suspended from his dwelling; and God will be enraged at his precious things, and will by no means exalt him.

**11:8** What shall I do with you, Ephraim? Shall I shield you, Israel? What shall I do with you? I will make you like Admah and like Zeboiim. My heart turned around at the same time; My regret was thrown into turmoil.

**11:9** I will by no means act according to the anger of My wrath; I will by no means abandon Ephraim to be blotted out, because I am God and not a man, the Holy One in you, and I will not enter into a city.

**11:10** After the Lord I will walk; like a lion He will bellow, because He Himself will roar, and children of the waters will be astounded.

**11:11** They will fly out like a bird out of Egypt, and like a dove out of the land of the Assyrians; and I will restore them to their homes, says the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — Following Septuagint versification, the opening sentence about the king of Israel corresponds to the end of 10:15 in Hebrew; the verse also reads "his children" (plural) where the Hebrew has "my son."
- **11:3** — The Greek verb synepodisa literally means "I bound the feet of," differing from the Hebrew "I taught to walk."
- **11:4** — The Greek diverges sharply from the Hebrew here, reading "in the destruction of people," "like a man slapping on his cheeks," and "I will prevail over him" instead of "with cords of a man," "like those who lift a yoke," and "I fed them."
- **11:9** — The Greek reads "abandon Ephraim to be blotted out" and translates the final phrase "into a city," taking the Hebrew consonants as "in a city" rather than "in wrath."
- **11:10** — The Greek shifts to the first person singular ("I will walk") and renders the Hebrew "from the west/sea" as "children of the waters."
