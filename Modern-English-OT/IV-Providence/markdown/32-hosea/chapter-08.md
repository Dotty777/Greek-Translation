## Text

**8:1** Into their bosom like earth, like an eagle against the house of the Lord, because they transgressed My covenant and against My law.

**8:2** To Me they will cry out, "God, we have known You."

**8:3** Because Israel rejected good things, they pursued an enemy.

**8:4** They reigned for themselves, and not through Me; they ruled, and did not inform Me; their silver and their gold they made into idols for themselves, so that they might be utterly destroyed.

**8:5** Rid yourself of your calf, Samaria! My wrath has been provoked against them; how long will they be unable to be cleansed

**8:6** in Israel? And a craftsman made it, and it is not a god; because your calf was a deceiver, Samaria.

**8:7** For they sowed wind-blighted crops, and their ruin will receive them—a sheaf having no strength to produce flour; and even if it does produce, foreigners will devour them.

**8:8** Israel has been swallowed up; now he has become among the nations like a useless vessel.

**8:9** For they themselves went up to the Assyrians; Ephraim flourished by himself, they loved gifts;

**8:10** therefore they will be handed over among the nations. Now I will take them in, and they will cease for a little while from anointing a king and rulers.

**8:11** Because Ephraim multiplied altars, beloved altars became sins for him.

**8:12** I will write down a multitude for him, and My statutes were counted as foreign things—the beloved altars.

**8:13** For if they offer a sacrifice and eat meat, the Lord will not accept them; now He will remember their iniquities and punish their sins. They returned to Egypt, and among the Assyrians they will eat unclean things.

**8:14** And Israel forgot the One who made him, and they built shrines; and Judah multiplied walled cities; and I will send out fire into his cities, and it will devour their foundations.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — The Septuagint opens with "Into their bosom like earth" (reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew consonants behind MT's "To your lips a trumpet") and leaves the final phrase "and against My law" elliptical without a second verb.
- **8:3** — The Greek makes "enemy" the direct object of a plural verb ("they pursued an enemy"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text makes the enemy the subject ("an enemy shall pursue him").
- **8:6** — The Greek participle "a deceiver" (planon, lit. "leading astray") diverges from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "broken in pieces / splinters."
- **8:7** — The Septuagint renders the imagery as "wind-blighted crops" and "their ruin" rather than the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "wind" and "whirlwind."
- **8:12** — The Greek divides the clauses differently from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, reading "a multitude" separately from "My statutes" and closing the verse with "the beloved altars" (corresponding to the opening words of MT 8:13).
