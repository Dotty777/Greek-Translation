## Text

**14:1** Samaria will be wiped out, because she opposed her God; they themselves will fall by the sword, and their nursing infants will be dashed to the ground, and their pregnant women will be torn open.

**14:2** Return, Israel, to the Lord your God, because you have grown weak in your injustices.

**14:3** Take words with you and return to the Lord your God; speak to Him so that you may not receive injustice and may receive good things, and we will repay the fruit of our lips.

**14:4** Assyria will by no means save us; we will not mount a horse; we will no longer say, "Our gods," to the works of our hands; the one who is in You will show mercy to an orphan.

**14:5** I will heal their dwellings, I will love them openly, because he has turned My anger away from him.

**14:6** I will be like dew to Israel; he will blossom like a lily, and he will put forth his roots like Lebanon.

**14:7** His branches will go forth, and he will be like an olive tree laden with fruit, and his fragrance will be like that of Lebanon.

**14:8** They will return and sit under his shelter; they will live and be intoxicated with grain; and a vine will blossom forth as his memorial, like wine of Lebanon.

**14:9** As for Ephraim, what does he still have to do with idols? I humbled him, and I will strengthen him; I am like a dense juniper; from Me your fruit has been found.

**14:10** Who is wise and will understand these things? Or who is discerning and will recognize them? For the ways of the Lord are straight, and the righteous will walk in them, but the ungodly will grow weak in them.

## Translator Notes

- **14:3** — The Greek renders the clause after "speak to Him" as a second-person plural purpose clause ("so that you may not receive injustice") before shifting to first-person plural ("we will repay the fruit of our lips," contrasting with the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "bulls of our lips").
- **14:4** — The Greek closes with an active construction ("the one who is in You will show mercy to an orphan"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text uses a passive verb ("in You the orphan finds mercy").
- **14:5** — Following the Greek text, this verse reads "dwellings" (MT "apostasy"), "openly" (homologos; MT "freely"), and a transitive third-person verb ("he has turned My anger away from him," where MT makes "My anger" the subject).
- **14:8** — The Greek omits a comparative particle before "vine," linking "vine" directly to "his memorial"; "be intoxicated with grain" translates the Greek verb methyskomai literally in its sense of abundant satiety.
- **14:9** — The Greek reads "I humbled him, and I will strengthen him" and specifies a "juniper" (arkeuthos), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "I answer and look after him" and "cypress."
