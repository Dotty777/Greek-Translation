## Text

**1:1** An oracle of the word of the Lord concerning Israel by the hand of His messenger: Lay it now upon your hearts.

**1:2** "I have loved you," says the Lord. "Yet you said, 'In what way have You loved us?' Was not Esau Jacob's brother?" says the Lord. "Yet I loved Jacob,

**1:3** but Esau I hated, and I appointed his borders for desolation and his inheritance for gifts of the wilderness."

**1:4** Because one will say, "Idumea has been overthrown, and let us return and rebuild the desolate places," this is what the Lord Almighty says: "They will build, and I will overthrow; and they will be called borders of lawlessness, and a people against whom the Lord stands arrayed forever.

**1:5** And your eyes will see, and you yourselves will say, 'The Lord has been magnified above the borders of Israel.'"

**1:6** "A son honors a father, and a servant his master. And if I am a father, where is My honor? And if I am a master, where is the fear of Me?" says the Lord Almighty. "You priests who despise My name—yet you said, 'In what way have we despised Your name?'

**1:7** By offering defiled loaves at My altar. Yet you said, 'In what way have we defiled them?' By your saying, 'The table of the Lord is defiled,' and you have held in contempt the things placed upon it.

**1:8** For if you offer a blind animal for a sacrifice, is it not evil? And if you offer a lame or sick one, is it not evil? Offer it now to your governor: will he accept you? Will he show you favor?" says the Lord Almighty.

**1:9** "And now, entreat the face of your God and plead with Him! By your hands these things have happened; will I show favor to any of you?" says the Lord Almighty.

**1:10** "Because even among you doors will be shut, and My altar will not be kindled in vain. I have no pleasure in you," says the Lord Almighty, "and I will not accept a sacrifice from your hands.

**1:11** For from the rising of the sun even to its setting, My name has been glorified among the nations, and in every place incense is offered to My name, and a pure sacrifice; for My name is great among the nations," says the Lord Almighty.

**1:12** "But you profane it by your saying, 'The table of the Lord is defiled,' and the things placed upon it, His foods, are held in contempt.

**1:13** And you said, 'These things are from hardship.' And I blew them away," says the Lord Almighty. "And you were bringing in stolen things and the lame and the afflicted; and if you bring the sacrifice, will I accept them from your hands?" says the Lord Almighty.

**1:14** "And cursed is the one who was able, and there was a male in his flock, and his vow was upon it, yet he sacrifices a marred one to the Lord. For I am a great King," says the Lord Almighty, "and My name is illustrious among the nations."

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The Greek reads 'by the hand of His messenger' (rendering Hebrew Malachi as a common noun rather than a proper name) and includes the closing exhortation 'Lay it now upon your hearts,' which is absent from the Hebrew MT.
- **1:3** — The Greek reads 'his borders' (horia) rather than Hebrew 'his mountains,' and 'for gifts of the wilderness' (domata) rather than Hebrew 'for jackals of the wilderness.'
- **1:4** — Idumea (Idoumaia) is the Greek name for Edom; here the Greek makes Idumea the subject of the passive verb 'has been overthrown,' contrasting with the Hebrew first-person plural 'Edom says, We have been shattered.'
- **1:10** — Whereas the Hebrew expresses a wish ('Oh that one among you would shut the doors'), the Greek states a future passive prediction: 'doors will be shut, and My altar will not be kindled in vain.'
- **1:13** — The Greek reads the first-person singular 'I blew them away' (exephysesa), whereas the Hebrew has the second-person plural 'you sniffed at it.'
