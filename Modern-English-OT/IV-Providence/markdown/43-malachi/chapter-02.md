## Text

**2:1** "And now this command is for you, priests.

**2:2** If you do not listen, and if you do not lay it to your heart to give glory to My name," says the Lord Almighty, "then I will send out the curse upon you, and I will curse your blessing, and I will curse it; and I will scatter your blessing, and it will not be among you, because you yourselves do not lay it to your heart.

**2:3** Look, I am separating the shoulder from you, and I will scatter offal upon your faces, offal of festivals, and I will take you to the same place;

**2:4** and you will know that I have sent out this command to you, so that My covenant may be with the Levites," says the Lord Almighty.

**2:5** "My covenant with him was one of life and peace, and I granted him to fear with reverence and to stand in awe before My name.

**2:6** A law of truth was in his mouth, and injustice was not found on his lips; in peace, keeping a straight course, he walked with Me, and he turned many back from injustice.

**2:7** For the lips of a priest will guard knowledge, and they will seek out the law from his mouth, because he is a messenger of the Lord Almighty.

**2:8** But you turned aside from the way and weakened many in the law; have you corrupted the covenant of Levi?" says the Lord Almighty.

**2:9** "And I also have given you over, held in contempt and cast aside, among all the nations, because you yourselves do not keep My ways, but were showing favoritism in the law."

**2:10** Did not one God create you? Is there not one Father of you all? Why is it that you have abandoned, each one his brother, so as to profane the covenant of your fathers?

**2:11** Judah has been abandoned, and an abomination has occurred in Israel and in Jerusalem, because Judah profaned the holy things of the Lord, in that he loved and pursued foreign gods.

**2:12** The Lord will utterly destroy the man who does these things, until he is even humbled from the tents of Jacob and from among those who offer a sacrifice to the Lord Almighty.

**2:13** "And these things which I hated, you were doing: you were covering the altar of the Lord with tears, and with weeping and groaning from toils. Is it still worthy to look upon a sacrifice or to receive what is acceptable from your hands?

**2:14** Yet you said, 'For what reason?' Because the Lord testified between you and the wife of your youth, whom you abandoned; and she is your companion and the wife of your covenant.

**2:15** And did He not make what is good, and a remnant of His Spirit? And you said, 'What else but seed does God seek?' So guard yourselves in your spirit, and do not abandon the wife of your youth.

**2:16** But if you hate her and send her away," says the Lord God of Israel, "then ungodliness will cover over your thoughts," says the Lord Almighty. "So guard yourselves in your spirit, and by no means abandon her,

**2:17** you who provoke God with your words. Yet you said, 'In what way have we provoked Him?' By your saying, 'Everyone who does evil is good before the Lord, and in them He Himself is well pleased,' and, 'Where is the God of righteousness?'"

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — Instead of Hebrew 'rebuke your offspring,' the Greek reads 'separating the shoulder from you' and uses enystron ('offal,' specifically the fourth stomach or maw of a sacrificial animal), echoing the priestly portions in Deuteronomy 18:3. The closing verb is also first-person singular ('I will take you to the same place').
- **2:11** — The Greek opens with a passive verb ('Judah has been abandoned,' vs. Hebrew 'Judah has been faithless') and reads 'foreign gods' without the Hebrew phrase 'daughter of a foreign god.' In 2:12, 'until he is even humbled' likewise diverges from the Hebrew idiom 'awake and answering.'
- **2:15** — A notoriously difficult verse; the Greek text translated here reads 'make what is good' (kalon epoiesen, where some Greek witnesses read 'another' and the Hebrew has 'one') and introduces the following question with 'And you said.'
- **2:16** — The Greek frames the opening as a conditional ('if you hate her and send her away'), where the Hebrew has a difficult clause about hating and sending away, and reads 'ungodliness will cover over your thoughts' instead of Hebrew 'violence on his garment.'
