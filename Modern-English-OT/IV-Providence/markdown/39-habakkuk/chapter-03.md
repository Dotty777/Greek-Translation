## Text

**3:1** A prayer of Habakkuk the prophet, with a song.

**3:2** Lord, I have heard the report of You, and I feared; I considered Your works, and I was astounded. In the midst of two living creatures You will be known; when the years draw near, You will be recognized; when the appointed time arrives, You will be revealed; when my soul is troubled, in wrath You will remember mercy.

**3:3** God will come from Teman, and the Holy One from Mount Paran, shaded and densely wooded. Interlude. His excellence covered the heavens, and the earth is full of His praise.

**3:4** And His radiance will be like light; horns are in His hands, and He established a mighty love of His strength.

**3:5** Before His face a word will go, and it will go out into the plains at His feet.

**3:6** He stood, and the earth was shaken; He looked on, and nations melted away; the mountains were shattered by force, eternal hills melted at His eternal march.

**3:7** Instead of troubles I saw the dwellings of the Ethiopians; the tents of the land of Midian will also be terrified.

**3:8** Were You angry at rivers, Lord? Or was Your fury at rivers? Or was Your onslaught at the sea? For You will mount Your horses, and Your riding is salvation.

**3:9** Surely You drew Your bow against scepters, says the Lord. Interlude. The earth will be split open with rivers.

**3:10** Peoples will see You and be in birth pangs, as You scatter waters of passage; the abyss gave its voice, the height of its appearance.

**3:11** The sun was lifted up, and the moon stood in its station; Your shafts will go into light, into the radiance of the lightning of Your weapons.

**3:12** In threat You will diminish the earth, and in fury You will bring down nations.

**3:13** You went out for the salvation of Your people, to save Your anointed one; You will cast death upon the heads of lawless ones; You raised up bonds up to the neck. Interlude.

**3:14** You cut through the heads of rulers in astonishment; they will be shaken in it. They will open wide their bridles like a poor person eating in secret.

**3:15** And You drive Your horses into the sea, stirring up much water.

**3:16** I kept watch, and my belly was terrified at the sound of the prayer of my lips, and trembling entered into my bones, and beneath me my frame was troubled. I will rest in the day of affliction, so as to go up to the people of my sojourn.

**3:17** For a fig tree will not bear fruit, and there will be no produce on the vines; the work of the olive tree will prove false, and the fields will not produce food; sheep have failed from food, and there are no oxen at the mangers.

**3:18** Yet I will exult in the Lord; I will rejoice in God my Savior.

**3:19** The Lord God is my strength, and He will order my feet to completion; He sets me upon the high places, to triumph in His song.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — The Septuagint reads "in the midst of two living creatures You will be known" (vocalizing the Hebrew consonants as shenayim chayyim rather than MT "in the midst of years revive it"), a famous reading widely discussed in early Christian interpretation.
- **3:3** — "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, a musical pause corresponding to Hebrew selah (also in 3:9 and 3:13).
- **3:4** — The Greek reads "He established a mighty love of His strength" where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "there is the hiding place of His power."
- **3:5** — The Greek vocalizes the Hebrew consonants as davar ("a word," Greek logos) rather than MT dever ("pestilence").
- **3:19** — The Greek reads "to completion" (rather than MT "like deer's feet") and translates the Hebrew musical colophon ("For the choirmaster, on my stringed instruments") as the closing clause "to triumph in His song."
