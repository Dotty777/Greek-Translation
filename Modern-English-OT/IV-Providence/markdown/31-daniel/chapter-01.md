## Text

**1:1** In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and laid siege to it.

**1:2** And the Lord gave Jehoiakim king of Judah into his hand, and part of the vessels of the house of God, and he brought them into the land of Shinar, of the house of his god, and the vessels he brought into the treasury house of his god.

**1:3** And the king told Ashpenaz his chief eunuch to bring in from the sons of the captivity of Israel, and from the royal seed, and from the nobles,

**1:4** young men in whom there is no blemish, and handsome in appearance, and having understanding in all wisdom, and knowing knowledge, and comprehending insight, and in whom there is strength to stand in the king's house, and to teach them the writings and language of the Chaldeans.

**1:5** And the king appointed for them the day's portion day by day from the king's table and from the wine of his drinking, and to nourish them for three years, and after these things to stand before the king.

**1:6** And there were among them, from the sons of Judah, Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.

**1:7** And the chief eunuch gave names to them: to Daniel, Baltasar; and to Hananiah, Shadrach; and to Mishael, Meshach; and to Azariah, Abednego.

**1:8** And Daniel set it upon his heart that he would not be defiled by the king's table and by the wine of his drinking, and he entreated the chief eunuch that he would not be defiled.

**1:9** And God gave Daniel into mercy and compassion before the chief eunuch.

**1:10** And the chief eunuch said to Daniel, "I fear my lord the king, who appointed your food and your drink, in case he sees your faces gloomy compared to the youths of your own age, and you condemn my head to the king."

**1:11** And Daniel said to Amelsad, whom the chief eunuch had appointed over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, Azariah:

**1:12** "Please test your servants for ten days, and let them give us some of the seeds, and we will eat, and we will drink water;

**1:13** and let our appearances be seen before you, and the appearances of the youths who eat from the king's table, and just as you see, deal with your servants."

**1:14** And he listened to them, and tested them for ten days.

**1:15** And after the end of the ten days, their appearances were seen to be good, and they were strong in their flesh, more than the youths who were eating from the king's table.

**1:16** And Amelsad continued taking away their dinner and the wine of their drink, and giving them seeds.

**1:17** And as for these youths, the four of them, God gave them understanding and insight in all literature and wisdom; and Daniel had understanding in every vision and dreams.

**1:18** And after the end of the days that the king had said to bring them in, then the chief eunuch brought them in before Nebuchadnezzar.

**1:19** And the king spoke with them, and among all of them there were not found any like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah; and they stood before the king.

**1:20** And in every matter of wisdom and knowledge about which the king inquired from them, he found them tenfold beyond all the enchanters and the magicians who were in all his kingdom.

**1:21** And Daniel remained until the first year of Cyrus the king.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — The Greek uses the genitive "of the house of his god" (οἴκου τοῦ θεοῦ αὐτοῦ) without a preposition after "the land of Shinar," linking the temple with the land rather than marking a second destination as in the Hebrew MT ("to the house of his god").
- **1:3** — φορθομμείν transliterates the Hebrew partemim ("nobles," a Persian loanword); it is rendered by its meaning.
- **1:5** — Here and in verses 8, 13, and 15, the Greek consistently reads "the king's table" (τράπεζα) where the Hebrew MT has "the king's choice food" (pat-bag); verses 13 and 15 literally read "eat the king's table."
- **1:11** — The Greek treats the Hebrew title hammelsar ("the steward" or "the overseer") as the personal name "Amelsad" (Ἀμελσάδ; spelled Ἁμελσάδ in v. 16).
- **1:12** — The Greek renders the Hebrew term for vegetable or pulse fare literally as "seeds" (σπερμάτων; also σπέρματα in v. 16), denoting plant foods grown from seed.
