## Text

**64:1** as wax melts from fire, and fire will burn up the adversaries, and Your name will be manifest among the adversaries; before Your presence nations will be thrown into turmoil,

**64:2** when You do glorious things; trembling will seize mountains from You.

**64:3** From ancient times we have not heard, nor have our eyes seen a god besides You, and Your works that You will do for those who wait for mercy.

**64:4** He will meet those who do what is right, and they will remember Your ways. Look, You were angry, and we sinned; because of this we went astray,

**64:5** and all of us became like unclean ones, all our righteousness is like a rag of a menstruating woman; and we fell away like leaves because of our lawless deeds; so a wind will carry us away.

**64:6** And there is no one who calls upon Your name and who remembers to take hold of You; for You have turned Your face away from us, and handed us over because of our sins.

**64:7** And now, Lord, You are our Father, and we are clay; all of us are the works of Your hands.

**64:8** Do not be greatly angry with us, and do not remember our sins in time; and now look down, for all of us are Your people.

**64:9** The city of Your sanctuary became a wilderness, Zion; Jerusalem became like a wilderness; for a curse

**64:10** the house, our sanctuary, and the glory that our fathers blessed, became burned with fire, and all our glorious things collapsed.

**64:11** And at all these things You held back, Lord, and kept silent, and humbled us greatly.

## Translator Notes

- **64:1** — In the Septuagint versification, 64:1 begins mid-sentence as a direct continuation of the clause at the end of 63:19.
- **64:4** — The Greek verb synantēsetai is third-person singular ('He will meet,' or potentially 'it will meet,' referring to mercy or Your works), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads second-person 'You meet.'
- **64:5** — Greek apokathēmenēs literally means 'of a woman sitting apart,' the standard Septuagint term for a menstruating woman in ceremonial separation (cf. Leviticus 15).
- **64:8** — Greek en kairō ('in time') differs from 'forever' in the Hebrew Masoretic Text, likely reflecting a reading of the Hebrew as 'at a time' rather than 'forever.'
- **64:9** — The phrase 'for a curse' (eis kataran) at the end of 64:9 has no counterpart in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and continues directly into the opening clause of 64:10.
