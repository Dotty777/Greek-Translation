## Text

**38:1** Now it happened at that time that Hezekiah became ill to the point of death; and Isaiah son of Amoz, the prophet, came to him and said to him, "This is what the Lord says: 'Give orders concerning your house, for you are dying and you will not live.'"

**38:2** And Hezekiah turned his face toward the wall and prayed to the Lord,

**38:3** saying, "Remember, Lord, how I walked before You with truth, in a true heart, and did what is pleasing before You." And Hezekiah wept with great weeping.

**38:4** And the word of the Lord came to Isaiah, saying,

**38:5** "Go and say to Hezekiah, 'This is what the Lord, the God of David your father, says: I have heard your prayer and seen your tears; look, I am adding fifteen years to your time,

**38:6** and from the hand of the king of the Assyrians I will rescue you and this city, and I will defend this city.

**38:7** And this is the sign to you from the Lord that God will do this thing:

**38:8** Look, I am turning the shadow of the steps that the sun went down, the ten steps of the house of your father—I will turn back the sun the ten steps.'" And the sun went up the ten steps that the shadow had gone down.

**38:9** A prayer of Hezekiah, king of Judea, when he became ill and recovered from his illness.

**38:10** I said, "In the height of my days, in the gates of Hades, I will leave behind the remaining years."

**38:11** I said, "No longer will I see the salvation of God in the land of the living; no longer will I see the salvation of Israel on the earth; no longer will I see a human being."

**38:12** It failed from my kindred; I left behind the remainder of my life; it went out and went away from me like one taking down a tent after pitching it. My spirit within me became like the web of a weaver drawing near to cut it off.

**38:13** In that day I was handed over until morning as to a lion; it crushed all my bones, for from the day until night I was handed over.

**38:14** Like a swallow, so I will cry out, and like a dove, so I moan; for my eyes failed from looking to the height of heaven, to the Lord, who rescued me

**38:15** and took away the pain of my soul.

**38:16** Lord, for concerning it, it was declared to You, and You raised up my breath, and having been comforted, I lived.

**38:17** For You chose my soul so that it would not perish, and You cast behind me all the sins.

**38:18** For those in Hades will not praise You, nor will those who have died bless You, nor will those in Hades hope for Your mercy.

**38:19** The living will bless You, just as I also do; for from today I will produce children, who will declare Your righteousness,

**38:20** God of my salvation; and I will not cease blessing You with a harp all the days of my life before the house of God.

**38:21** And Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Take a cake of figs and crush it and apply it as a poultice, and you will be well."

**38:22** And Hezekiah said, "This is a sign to Hezekiah that I will go up to the house of God."

## Translator Notes

- **38:1** — The Greek spells Isaiah's father Ἀμώς (identical to the prophet Amos), rendered consistently with the standard modern English form Amoz throughout Isaiah.
- **38:8** — The Greek text reads "of the house of your father" where the Hebrew text has "the steps of Ahaz," and describes the sun itself going up the steps after the shadow descended.
- **38:17** — The Greek text reads "behind me all the sins" (ὀπίσω μου πάσας τὰς ἁμαρτίας) rather than the Hebrew "behind Your back all my sins."
- **38:19** — Unlike the Hebrew text ("the father to the children makes known Your faithfulness"), the Greek text has Hezekiah promise to produce children from that day forward who will declare God's righteousness.
- **38:22** — The Greek text renders this verse as a declarative statement naming Hezekiah in the third person rather than a question as in the Hebrew text, and verse 21 omits mention of the boil.
