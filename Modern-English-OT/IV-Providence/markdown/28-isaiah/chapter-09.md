## Text

**9:1** People walking in darkness, see a great light; you who dwell in a land, a shadow of death, light will shine upon you.

**9:2** The greater part of the people whom You brought down in Your joy—they will rejoice before You like those who rejoice in harvest, and just as those who divide spoils.

**9:3** For the yoke lying upon them has been taken away, and the rod that was upon their neck; for He shattered the rod of the exactors, as in the day against Midian.

**9:4** For every robe amassed by deceit, and cloak with recompense, they will pay back, and they will wish that they had been burned with fire.

**9:5** For a child was born to us, a son was given to us, whose rule came to be upon His shoulder, and His name is called Messenger of Great Counsel; for I will bring peace upon the rulers and health to Him.

**9:6** Great is His rule, and of His peace there is no boundary, upon the throne of David and his kingdom, to set it upright and sustain it in judgment and in righteousness, from now on and forever; the zeal of the Lord Sabaoth will do these things.

**9:7** The Lord sent death upon Jacob, and it came upon Israel.

**9:8** And all the people of Ephraim and those residing in Samaria will know, who say in arrogance and a lofty heart,

**9:9** "Bricks have fallen, but come, let us hew stones, and let us cut down sycamores and cedars, and let us build a tower for ourselves."

**9:10** And God will dash upon him those who rise up against Mount Zion, and He will scatter the enemies,

**9:11** Syria from the rising of the sun and the Greeks from the setting of the sun, who devour Israel with a full mouth. For all these things the fury has not turned away, but the hand is still uplifted.

**9:12** And the people did not turn back until they were struck, and they did not seek the Lord.

**9:13** And the Lord took away from Israel head and tail, great and small in one day, an elder and those who admire faces—this is the beginning,

**9:14** and a prophet teaching lawless things—this one is the tail.

**9:15** And those who call this people blessed will be leading them astray, and they lead astray so that they may swallow them up.

**9:16** Therefore the Lord will not rejoice over their young men, and He will not have mercy on their orphans and their widows; for all are lawless and wicked, and every mouth speaks unjust things. For all these things the fury has not turned away, but the hand is still uplifted.

**9:17** And lawlessness will burn like fire, and like dry grass it will be eaten by fire; and it will burn in the thickets of the forest, and it will devour together everything around the hills;

**9:18** because of the fury of the wrath of the Lord, the whole land has been burned up, and the people will be as though burned up by fire. A man will not have mercy on his brother,

**9:19** but he will turn aside to the right, because he will be hungry, and he will eat from the left, and a man eating the flesh of his arm will by no means be filled.

**9:20** For Manasseh will eat of Ephraim, and Ephraim of Manasseh, because together they will besiege Judah. For all these things the fury has not turned away, but the hand is still uplifted.

## Translator Notes

- **9:4** — The Septuagint diverges sharply from the Hebrew here, rendering the imagery of bloody battle garments burned in fire as garments amassed by deceit that must be repaid with recompense, so that their owners wish they had been burned instead.
- **9:5** — Whereas the Hebrew gives four compound royal titles and uses present/perfect verbs ('is born... is given'), the Septuagint uses aorists ('was born... was given') and condenses the titles into 'Messenger of Great Counsel,' followed by God's first-person promise of peace and health. Pronouns for the child ('His/Him' here and in v. 6) are capitalized following traditional messianic reading; Greek makes no case distinction.
- **9:6** — 'Sabaoth' transliterates Greek σαβαώθ (from Hebrew ṣĕbāʾôt, 'of hosts/armies'), which the Septuagint of Isaiah regularly leaves untranslated.
- **9:7** — The Septuagint reads 'death' (Θάνατον, reflecting Hebrew deber 'pestilence') where the Masoretic Text reads 'a word' (dābār).
- **9:11** — Where the Hebrew names 'Aram' and 'the Philistines,' the Septuagint has 'Syria' and 'the Greeks,' reflecting later Hellenistic-era geopolitical identification.
