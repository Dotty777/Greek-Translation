## Text

**42:1** Jacob is My servant; I will take hold of him; Israel is My chosen one; My soul has accepted him. I have put My Spirit upon him; he will bring forth judgment to the nations.

**42:2** He will not cry out nor let up, nor will his voice be heard outside.

**42:3** A bruised reed he will not shatter, and smoldering flax he will not extinguish; rather, he will bring forth judgment for truth.

**42:4** He will shine out and will not be broken until he establishes judgment on the earth; and in his name nations will hope.

**42:5** Thus says the Lord God, who made the heaven and fixed it, who established the earth and the things in it, and who gives breath to the people on it and spirit to those who tread on it:

**42:6** "I, the Lord God, called you in righteousness, and I will grasp your hand and strengthen you, and I gave you for a covenant of a race,

**42:7** to open the eyes of the blind, to bring out from bonds those who are bound, and from a prison house those sitting in darkness.

**42:8** I am the Lord God; this is My name; My glory I will not give to another, nor My excellences to carved images.

**42:9** The things from the beginning, look, have come, and new things are what I declare; and before declaring them, they were made known to you."

**42:10** Sing a new hymn to the Lord; its beginning: glorify His name from the end of the earth, you who go down to the sea and sail it, the islands and those who inhabit them.

**42:11** Rejoice, wilderness and its villages, encampments and those who inhabit Kedar. Those who inhabit the rock will rejoice; from the top of the mountains they will shout;

**42:12** they will give glory to God; His excellences they will declare in the islands.

**42:13** The Lord God of powers will go forth and shatter war; He will stir up zeal and shout against His enemies with strength.

**42:14** I kept silent; will I even keep silent forever and hold back? Like the woman giving birth, I endured; I will dismay and dry up at once.

**42:15** I will lay waste mountains and hills, and all their grass I will dry up; and I will turn rivers into islands, and I will dry up marshes.

**42:16** And I will lead the blind in a way that they did not know, and paths that they had not known I will make them tread; I will make the darkness into light for them, and the crooked things straight. These things I will do, and I will not forsake them;

**42:17** but they themselves turned back. Be ashamed with shame, you who trust in carved images, who say to cast images, "You are our gods."

**42:18** You deaf, hear, and you blind, look up to see!

**42:19** And who is blind except My servants, and deaf except those who rule over them? And the servants of God were blinded.

**42:20** You saw many times, and did not take heed; your ears were open, and you did not hear.

**42:21** The Lord God purposed that He might be justified and magnify praise.

**42:22** And I saw, and the people became plundered and pillaged; for the snare is in the inner rooms everywhere, and in houses alike, where they hid them, they became plunder; and there was no one rescuing the spoil, and there was no one saying, "Give back!"

**42:23** Who among you will give ear to these things? Listen to the things to come

**42:24** upon those to whom He gave Jacob and Israel for pillaging, to those who plundered him. Was it not God, against whom they sinned, and they were not willing to walk in His ways nor to listen to His law?

**42:25** And He brought upon them the wrath of His fury, and war overpowered them, and those burning them up all around; and not one of them knew, nor did they lay it upon their soul.

## Translator Notes

- **42:1** — The Septuagint explicitly identifies the servant and chosen one as "Jacob" and "Israel," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text leaves them unnamed; accordingly, third-person pronouns in vv. 1-4 refer to Jacob/Israel.
- **42:6** — The Greek source text ends v. 6 at "a covenant of a race" (εἰς διαθήκην γένους) without the phrase "a light to the nations" found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and some Greek manuscripts.
- **42:10** — The phrase ἡ ἀρχὴ αὐτοῦ literally means "its beginning" (referring to the new hymn) or "His dominion," where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "His praise."
- **42:19** — The Greek differs substantially from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, speaking of "My servants" (plural), "those who rule over them" (rather than "My messenger"), and "the servants of God were blinded."
- **42:24** — The Greek text begins with the dative relative pronoun οἷς ("upon those to whom"), connecting syntactically with "the things to come" in v. 23, whereas other Greek manuscripts and the Hebrew Masoretic Text read "Who gave...?"
