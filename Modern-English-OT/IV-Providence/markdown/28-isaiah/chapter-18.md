## Text

**18:1** Woe, wings of a land of ships beyond the rivers of Ethiopia,

**18:2** the one who sends hostages on the sea and papyrus letters upon the water. For swift messengers will go to a lofty nation, and a strange and harsh people; who is beyond it? A nation without hope and trampled. Now the rivers of the earth

**18:3** will all be inhabited like an inhabited country; their country may be lifted up as for a signal from a mountain; like the sound of a trumpet, it will be audible.

**18:4** Because thus the Lord said to me, "Security will be in My city like a light of burning heat at midday, and it will be like a cloud of dew on a day of reaping.

**18:5** Before the harvest, when the blossom is finished and an unripe grape will put forth a blossom while forming unripe grapes; and He will remove the small grape clusters with the pruning hooks, and the shoots He will remove and cut off,

**18:6** and He will leave them together to the birds of the sky and the wild beasts of the earth; and the birds of the sky will be gathered together upon them, and all the wild beasts of the earth will come upon him."

**18:7** At that time gifts will be brought up to the Lord Sabaoth from an afflicted and plucked people, and from a great people from now and for eternal time, a nation that hopes and is trampled, which is in a part of a river of its country, to the place where the name of the Lord Sabaoth is, Mount Zion.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — The Greek reads "wings of a land of ships" where the Masoretic Text has "land of whirring wings" (and in 18:2 has "hostages" where the Masoretic Text has "envoys", along with a future indicative "will go" rather than an imperative).
- **18:3** — The Greek uses an aorist passive subjunctive (arthe, "may be lifted up") following hos ("as"), expressing a prophetic wish or condition paired with "it will be audible."
- **18:7** — The Greek retains the untranslated Hebrew title Sabaoth ("hosts" or "armies") rather than translating it, and characterizes the nation as "that hopes" (elpizon) in contrast to "without hope" (anelpiston) in 18:2.
