## Text

**30:1** "Woe, apostate children," says the Lord, "you made a plan not through Me, and treaties not through My Spirit, to add sins upon sins,

**30:2** who set out to go down to Egypt, yet they did not inquire of Me, to be helped by Pharaoh and to be sheltered by the Egyptians.

**30:3** For the shelter of Pharaoh will be for shame to you, and a reproach to those who trust in Egypt.

**30:4** For in Tanis there are leaders, evil messengers;

**30:5** in vain they will weary themselves toward a people that will not profit them for help, but for shame and reproach."

**30:6** The vision of the four-footed beasts in the wilderness. In affliction and anguish: a lion and a lion's cub, from there also asps and offspring of asps, who carried their wealth on donkeys and camels to a nation that will not profit them.

**30:7** Egyptians will profit you with futile and empty things; report to them, "This consolation of yours is futile."

**30:8** Now therefore, sit down and write these things on a boxwood tablet and in a book, because these things will be for days, in time, and even forever.

**30:9** For it is a disobedient people, lying sons who were unwilling to hear the law of God,

**30:10** who say to the prophets, "Do not declare to us," and to those who see visions, "Do not speak to us; rather, speak to us and declare to us another delusion,

**30:11** and turn us away from this way; remove from us this path, and remove from us the oracle of Israel."

**30:12** Therefore, thus says the Holy One of Israel: "Because you disobeyed these words and hoped in falsehood, and because you grumbled and came to trust in this word,

**30:13** therefore this sin will be to you like a wall of a fortified city that has been captured, falling at once, whose collapse is present at once;

**30:14** and its collapse will be like the breaking of an earthen vessel, fine pieces from a clay jar, so that no shard is found among them in which you will take up fire, and in which you will draw off a little water."

**30:15** Thus says the Lord, the Lord, the Holy One of Israel: "When you turn back and groan, then you will be saved, and you will know where you were; when you trusted in futile things, your strength became futile. And you were unwilling to listen,

**30:16** but you said, 'On horses we will flee'—therefore you will flee; and, 'On swift riders we will be'—therefore those who pursue you will be swift.

**30:17** A thousand will flee because of the voice of one, and because of the voice of five many will flee, until you are left like a mast on a mountain, and like one carrying a banner on a hill."

**30:18** And again God will wait to have compassion on you, and because of this He will be exalted to have mercy on you, because the Lord your God is a judge; blessed are those who abide in Him.

**30:19** Because a holy people will dwell in Zion; and Jerusalem wept with weeping, "Have mercy on me." He will have mercy on you; when He saw the voice of your cry, He listened to you.

**30:20** And the Lord will give you bread of affliction and scant water, and no longer will those who lead you astray come near you; because your eyes will see those who lead you astray,

**30:21** and your ears will hear the words of those behind who led you astray, who say, "This is the way; let us walk in it, whether to the right or to the left."

**30:22** And you will defile the idols overlaid with silver and overlaid with gold; you will break them into fine pieces and scatter them like the water of a menstruating woman, and like dung you will push them away.

**30:23** Then the rain will be for the seed of your land, and the bread of the produce of your land will be plentiful and rich; and your livestock will graze in that day in a fertile and spacious place,

**30:24** your bulls and the oxen that work the ground will eat chaff mixed with winnowed barley.

**30:25** And upon every high mountain and upon every lofty hill there will be water flowing through in that day, when many perish, when towers fall.

**30:26** And the light of the moon will be like the light of the sun, and the light of the sun will be sevenfold, in the day when the Lord heals the fracture of His people, and He will heal the pain of your wound.

**30:27** Look, the name of the Lord comes after a long time, burning fury; with glory is the oracle of His lips, the oracle full of wrath, and the wrath of fury will devour like fire;

**30:28** and His breath, like water sweeping along in a ravine, will come up to the neck, and it will be divided to trouble nations in futile delusion, and delusion will pursue them and seize them before their face.

**30:29** Must you continually rejoice, and continually enter into My sanctuary as those keeping a festival, and as those rejoicing to enter with a flute into the mountain of the Lord, to the God of Israel?

**30:30** And the Lord will make the glory of His voice heard, and show the fury of His arm with fury and wrath and devouring flame, with a violent thunderblast, and like water and hail sweeping down together with violence.

**30:31** For through the voice of the Lord the Assyrians will be defeated, by the blow with which He strikes them.

**30:32** And it will happen to him from all around, from where their hope of help was, in which he had trusted; they themselves with tambourines and a lyre will fight against him in a reversal.

**30:33** For before days you will be called to account; was it also prepared for you to reign—a deep ravine, laid wood, fire and much wood? The fury of the Lord is like a ravine burning with sulfur.

## Translator Notes

- **30:4** — Greek Tanis is the Septuagint name corresponding to Hebrew Zoan; the Septuagint also reads "leaders, evil messengers" where the Hebrew text has "his officials are at Zoan and his envoys reach Hanes."
- **30:15** — Greek doubles the divine title as kyrios Kyrios ("the Lord, the Lord," reflecting Hebrew Adonai YHWH), and reads "When you turn back and groan" rather than the Hebrew "In returning and rest."
- **30:20** — Greek renders the Hebrew term for "your Teacher" as "those who lead you astray" (hoi planōntes se), continuing in 30:21 with the plural exhortation "let us walk in it."
- **30:29** — Rather than a promise of festive song as in the Hebrew text, the Septuagint frames this verse as a rhetorical rebuke questioning their continual feasting and entry into the sanctuary.
- **30:33** — The Septuagint renders the Hebrew phrase involving Topheth as "you will be called to account" and reads "to reign" (basileuein) where the Hebrew text has "for the king."
