## Text

**31:1** Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help, who trust in horses and in chariots, for they are many, and on horses there is an exceedingly great multitude; and they did not trust in the Holy One of Israel, and they did not seek the Lord.

**31:2** Yet He, being wise, brought evils upon them, and His word will certainly not be set aside, and He will rise up against houses of wicked people and against their futile hope,

**31:3** an Egyptian, a human being and not God, flesh of horses, and there is no help; and the Lord will bring His hand upon them, and those who help will grow weary, and all together they will perish.

**31:4** For thus the Lord said to me: "In the same way as the lion or the cub roars over the prey that it has caught, and cries out over it until the mountains are filled with its voice, and they are defeated, and the multitude of fury is terrified, so the Lord Sabaoth will come down to wage war upon Mount Zion, upon its mountains.

**31:5** Like flying birds, so the Lord Sabaoth will shield; He will shield Jerusalem and rescue it, and He will preserve and save it."

**31:6** Turn back, you who plan the deep and lawless plan, sons of Israel.

**31:7** For in that day people will renounce their silver handmade things and the gold handmade things, which their hands made.

**31:8** And Assyria will fall; not a sword of a man nor a sword of a human being will devour it, and it will flee not from the face of a sword; and the young men will be for defeat,

**31:9** for they will be surrounded by a rock as by a palisade and will be defeated, and the one who flees will be captured. Thus says the Lord: "Blessed is the one who has seed in Zion and relatives in Jerusalem."

## Translator Notes

- **31:4** — Greek Sabaōth (also in 31:5) transliterates the Hebrew title meaning "of hosts" or "of armies"; in the simile, the Septuagint portrays the enemies as defeated and terrified rather than the lion being undaunted.
- **31:8** — Greek uses the singular Assour (Assyria) with masculine singular pronouns and distinguishes between "man" (anēr) and "human being" (anthrōpos).
- **31:9** — Where the Hebrew text speaks of the Lord "whose fire is in Zion, and whose furnace is in Jerusalem," the Septuagint reads "who has seed in Zion and relatives in Jerusalem."
