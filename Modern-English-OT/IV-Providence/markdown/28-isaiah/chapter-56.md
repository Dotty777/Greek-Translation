## Text

**56:1** Thus says the Lord: "Keep justice and do righteousness, for My salvation has drawn near to come, and My mercy to be revealed.

**56:2** Blessed is the man who does these things, and the person who holds fast to them, and keeps the Sabbaths so as not to profane them, and guards his hands from doing unrighteous things."

**56:3** Do not let the foreigner who joins himself to the Lord say, "Surely the Lord will separate me from His people"; and do not let the eunuch say, "I am a dry tree."

**56:4** Thus says the Lord to the eunuchs: "All who keep My Sabbaths and choose the things that I desire and hold fast to My covenant,

**56:5** I will give them in My house and in My wall a renowned place, better than sons and daughters; I will give them an everlasting name, and it will not fail.

**56:6** And as for the foreigners who join themselves to the Lord to serve Him, and to love the name of the Lord so as to be male and female servants to Him, and all who keep My Sabbaths so as not to profane them and hold fast to My covenant,

**56:7** I will bring them into My holy mountain, and I will make them glad in My house of prayer; their burnt offerings and their sacrifices will be acceptable upon My altar. For My house will be called a house of prayer for all the nations,"

**56:8** said the Lord who gathers the dispersed of Israel, "I will gather a gathering to him."

**56:9** All you wild beasts, come, eat, all you beasts of the forest.

**56:10** See that they have all been blinded; they have not known; mute dogs, they will not be able to bark, dreaming of a bed, loving to slumber.

**56:11** And the dogs are shameless in soul, not knowing satisfaction; and they are wicked, not knowing understanding; all have followed their own ways, each one according to what is his own.

## Translator Notes

- **56:6** — The Greek specifies both genders ("male and female servants," δούλους καὶ δούλας) and shifts from the dative ("as for the foreigners") to the accusative ("and all who keep...") anticipating "I will bring them" in v. 7.
- **56:8** — The Septuagint uses the cognate noun συναγωγήν ("a gathering") and singular "him" (αὐτόν, referring to Israel).
- **56:10** — Greek ἴδετε ("See") reads the Hebrew consonants as an imperative rather than "his watchmen," and treats κοίτην ("bed") as the object or scene of their dreaming.
- **56:11** — Greek πονηροί ("wicked") reflects reading the Hebrew consonants rʿym as "evil" rather than "shepherds."
