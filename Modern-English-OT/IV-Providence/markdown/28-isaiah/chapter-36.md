## Text

**36:1** And it happened in the fourteenth year, while Hezekiah was reigning, that Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, went up against the fortified cities of Judea and took them.

**36:2** And the king of the Assyrians sent Rabshakeh from Lachish to Jerusalem to King Hezekiah with a great army; and he stood at the aqueduct of the upper pool, on the road of the fuller's field.

**36:3** And Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, the steward, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah the son of Asaph, the recorder, went out to him.

**36:4** And Rabshakeh said to them, "Say to Hezekiah, 'Thus says the great king, the king of the Assyrians: What are you trusting in?

**36:5** Does battle array come about through counsel and words of the lips? And now, in whom are you trusting, that you disobey me?

**36:6** Look, you are trusting in this crushed reed staff, in Egypt; whoever leans on it, it will enter into his hand. So is Pharaoh king of Egypt, and all who trust in him.'

**36:7** And if you say, 'We trust in the Lord our God,'

**36:8** now join with my master, the king of the Assyrians, and I will give you two thousand horses, if you will be able to put riders on them.

**36:9** And how can you turn back in the face of the governors? Those who trust in Egypt are household servants, for horse and rider.

**36:10** And now, did we come up against this country without the Lord to fight against it? The Lord said to me, 'Go up against this land and destroy it.'"

**36:11** And Eliakim and Shebna and Joah said to him, "Speak to your servants in Syrian, for we understand, and do not speak to us in Judean; and why are you speaking in the ears of the people on the wall?"

**36:12** And Rabshakeh said to them, "Has my master sent me to your master or to you to speak these words? Was it not to the people sitting on the wall, so that they may eat dung and drink urine together with you?"

**36:13** And Rabshakeh stood and cried out with a loud voice in Judean and said, "Hear the words of the great king, the king of the Assyrians.

**36:14** Thus says the king: 'Do not let Hezekiah deceive you with words; he will by no means be able to deliver you.

**36:15** And do not let Hezekiah say to you, "God will deliver you, and this city will by no means be handed over into the hand of the king of the Assyrians."'

**36:16** Do not listen to Hezekiah. Thus says the king of the Assyrians: 'If you wish to be blessed, come out to me, and each of you will eat his vine and the fig trees, and you will drink water from your bronze vessel,

**36:17** until I come and take you to a land like your land, a land of grain and wine and bread and vineyards.

**36:18** Do not let Hezekiah deceive you, saying, "God will deliver you." Did the gods of the nations each deliver his own country from the hand of the king of the Assyrians?

**36:19** Where is the god of Hamath and Arpad? And where is the god of the city of Epharvaim? Were they able to deliver Samaria from my hand?

**36:20** Who among all the gods of these nations is there who delivered his land from my hand, that God will deliver Jerusalem from my hand?'"

**36:21** And they were silent, and no one answered him a word, because the king had commanded no one to answer.

**36:22** And Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, the steward, and Shebna the scribe of the army, and Joah the son of Asaph, the recorder, came in to Hezekiah with their tunics torn, and reported to him the words of Rabshakeh.

## Translator Notes

- **36:7** — LXX omits the second half of the verse found in the Hebrew MT regarding Hezekiah removing the high places and altars.
- **36:9** — LXX diverges markedly from the Hebrew MT, reading 'in the face of the governors' without mention of a captain of the master's servants, and treating 'those who trust in Egypt' as the subject of 'are household servants.'
- **36:11** — Greek 'Syristi' ('in Syrian') refers to Aramaic, and 'Ioudaisti' ('in Judean') refers to the Hebrew dialect spoken in Judea (also in 36:13).
- **36:16** — LXX reads 'if you wish to be blessed' (where MT has 'make peace with me') and 'water from your bronze vessel' (Greek 'tou chalkou hymon'), where MT has 'his cistern.'
- **36:19** — LXX reads 'Epharvaim' (Greek 'Eppharouaim'), an LXX form corresponding to Hebrew Sepharvaim (also in 37:13).
