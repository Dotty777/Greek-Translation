## Text

**26:1** On that day they will sing this song in the land of Judea: "Look, a strong city! And He will set salvation as the wall and outer wall.

**26:2** Open gates! Let a people enter that keeps righteousness and keeps truth,

**26:3** holding fast to truth and keeping peace; for in You, with hope,

**26:4** they have hoped, Lord, forever, the great, eternal God,

**26:5** who brought low and brought down those who dwell in high places; fortified cities You will cast down and bring down to the ground,

**26:6** and the feet of the gentle and humble will trample them.

**26:7** The way of the godly has become straight; the way of the godly is also prepared.

**26:8** For the way of the Lord is judgment; we have hoped in Your name and in the remembrance

**26:9** for which our soul longs. Out of the night my spirit rises early to You, God, because Your ordinances are light upon the earth. Learn righteousness, you who dwell on the earth.

**26:10** For the ungodly has ceased; everyone who will by no means learn righteousness on the earth will by no means practice truth; let the ungodly be taken away, so that he may not see the glory of the Lord.

**26:11** Lord, Your arm is high, and they did not know; yet when they come to know, they will be put to shame. Zeal will seize an untaught people, and now fire will devour the adversaries.

**26:12** Lord our God, give peace to us, for You have rendered all things to us.

**26:13** Take possession of us, Lord; apart from You we know no other; we name Your name.

**26:14** But the dead will by no means see life, nor will physicians raise them up; therefore You brought it upon them and destroyed them, and took away every male of them.

**26:15** Add evils to them, Lord; add evils to the glorious ones of the earth.

**26:16** Lord, in distress I remembered You; in a small distress was Your discipline to us.

**26:17** And as the woman in labor draws near to give birth—in her labor pain she cries out—so we became to Your beloved.

**26:18** Because of the fear of You, Lord, we conceived and were in labor and gave birth; we made a spirit of Your salvation on the earth, but all who dwell on the earth will fall.

**26:19** The dead will rise, and those in the tombs will be raised, and those in the earth will be glad; for the dew that is from You is healing to them, but the land of the ungodly will fall."

**26:20** Go, My people, enter into your inner rooms, shut your door, hide yourself for a very little while, until the wrath of the Lord passes by.

**26:21** For look, the Lord is bringing wrath from the holy place upon those who dwell on the earth; and the earth will uncover its blood, and will not cover up those who have been killed.

## Translator Notes

- **26:3** — The Septuagint connects vv. 3-4 with the cognate phrase "with hope they have hoped" (elpidi elpisan), differing from the Masoretic Text's "steadfast of mind You keep in peace."
- **26:13** — The Septuagint reads an imperative addressed to God, "Take possession of us, Lord," where the Masoretic Text reads "Other lords besides You have ruled over us."
- **26:14** — The Septuagint renders the Hebrew "rephaim" (departed shades) as "physicians" (iatroi) and "all remembrance of them" as "every male of them" (pan arsen auton).
- **26:17** — The Septuagint reads "to Your beloved" (to agapeto sou) where the Masoretic Text has "before You, O LORD."
- **26:19** — The Septuagint reads "those in the tombs" instead of the Masoretic Text's "my dead body," and translates the Hebrew "rephaim" as "the ungodly."
