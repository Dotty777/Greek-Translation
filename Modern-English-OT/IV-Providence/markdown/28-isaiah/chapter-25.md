## Text

**25:1** Lord God, I will glorify You, I will sing praise to Your name, because You did wonderful things, an ancient, true counsel; may it be so.

**25:2** For You made cities into a mound, fortified cities so that their foundations might not fall; the city of the ungodly will by no means be built for eternity.

**25:3** Therefore the poor people will bless You, and cities of wronged people will bless You.

**25:4** For You became a helper to every humble city, and a shelter to those who lost heart because of need; from wicked people You will rescue them—a shelter of those who thirst, and a spirit of wronged people,

**25:5** like fainthearted people thirsting in Zion because of ungodly people, to whom You handed us over.

**25:6** And the Lord Sabaoth will act for all the nations; on this mountain they will drink gladness, they will drink wine;

**25:7** they will anoint themselves with ointment on this mountain. Hand over all these things to the nations, for this counsel is upon all the nations.

**25:8** Death, having prevailed, swallowed up, and again the Lord God took away every tear from every face; the reproach of the people He took away from all the earth, for the mouth of the Lord has spoken.

**25:9** And they will say on that day, "Here is our God, in whom we were hoping, and He will save us; this is the Lord—we waited for Him and exulted, and we will be glad in our salvation."

**25:10** God will give rest upon this mountain, and the Moabite land will be trampled down just as they trample a threshing floor with wagons;

**25:11** and he will relax his hands, just as he himself brought low in order to destroy, and He will bring low his arrogance over the things on which he laid his hands;

**25:12** and the height of the refuge of your wall He will bring low, and they will come down to the ground.

## Translator Notes

- **25:2** — The Septuagint literally reads "fortified cities so that their foundations might not fall," reinterpreting the Hebrew "fortified city into a ruin."
- **25:6** — "Sabaoth" is the Septuagint's Greek transliteration of the Hebrew title meaning "of hosts/armies" (compare Rom 9:29; James 5:4). The Septuagint also lacks "a feast of rich food" here, rendering the feast root in the following clause as "they will drink gladness."
- **25:8** — In contrast to the Masoretic Text ("He will swallow up death forever") and 1 Cor 15:54, the Septuagint makes "death" (ho thanatos) the nominative subject: "Death, having prevailed, swallowed up."
- **25:9** — The Septuagint reads "in our salvation" (te soteria hemon) where the Masoretic Text has "in His salvation."
- **25:11** — The Septuagint diverges sharply from the Masoretic Text ("as a swimmer spreads his hands to swim"), reading "he will relax his hands, just as he himself brought low in order to destroy."
