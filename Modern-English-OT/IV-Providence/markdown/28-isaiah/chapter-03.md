## Text

**3:1** Look now, the Master, the Lord Sabaoth will take away from Jerusalem and from Judea a strong man and a strong woman, strength of bread and strength of water,

**3:2** a giant and a strong man and a fighting man and a judge and a prophet and a diviner and an elder,

**3:3** and a captain of fifty and a marvelous counselor and a wise master builder and an intelligent listener.

**3:4** And I will appoint youths as their rulers, and mockers will rule over them.

**3:5** And the people will clash, person against person, and a person against his neighbor; the child will strike against the old man, the dishonored against the honored.

**3:6** For a man will take hold of his brother or of a relative of his father, saying, "You have a cloak; become our leader, and let my food be under you."

**3:7** And answering, he will say in that day, "I will not be your leader, for there is neither bread nor cloak in my house; I will not be a leader of this people."

**3:8** For Jerusalem has been abandoned and Judea has collapsed, and their tongues are with lawlessness, disobeying what concerns the Lord; because now their glory has been humbled,

**3:9** and the shame of their face stood against them; and they declared and displayed their sin like that of Sodom. Woe to their soul, because they have counseled an evil counsel against themselves,

**3:10** saying, "Let us bind the righteous one, because he is troublesome to us." Therefore they will eat the produce of their works.

**3:11** Woe to the lawless one! Evil things according to the works of his hands will happen to him.

**3:12** My people, your exactors glean you, and those who make demands rule over you. My people, those who call you blessed lead you astray and disturb the path of your feet.

**3:13** But now the Lord will take His stand for judgment, and will bring His people to judgment;

**3:14** the Lord Himself will come to judgment with the elders of the people and with its rulers. But you, why have you burned My vineyard, and the plunder of the poor is in your houses?

**3:15** Why do you wrong My people, and put the face of the poor to shame?

**3:16** Thus says the Lord, "Because the daughters of Zion have been exalted, and have walked with a high neck and with winks of the eyes, and in the gait of their feet trailing their tunics at the same time, and dancing with their feet at the same time,

**3:17** God will humble the ruling daughters of Zion. And the Lord will uncover their form

**3:18** in that day; and the Lord will take away the glory of their clothing, the hair braids and the hairnets and the crescents,

**3:19** and the pendant and the adornment of their face,

**3:20** and the array of the adornment of glory, and the armlets and the bracelets and the hair braid and the finger rings and the wristbands and the earrings,

**3:21** and the purple-bordered garments and the purple-striped garments,

**3:22** and the wraps for the house and the transparent Laconian garments,

**3:23** and the fine linen garments and the hyacinth-blue and scarlet ones, and the fine linen, interwoven with gold and hyacinth-blue, and flowing summer veils.

**3:24** And instead of sweet fragrance there will be dust, and instead of a belt you will gird yourself with a rope, and instead of the golden adornment of the head you will have baldness because of your works; and instead of the purple-striped tunic you will wrap yourself with sackcloth.

**3:25** And your most handsome son whom you love will fall by the sword, and your strong men will fall by the sword and be humbled;

**3:26** and the cases of your adornment will mourn, and you will be left alone and dashed to the ground."

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Sabaoth (σαβαώθ) is a Greek transliteration of the Hebrew title meaning 'of hosts/armies,' retained consistently in LXX Isaiah. Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'support and staff,' the LXX renders 'a strong man and a strong woman.'
- **3:10** — The LXX reads 'Let us bind the righteous one, because he is troublesome to us' (echoed in Wisdom 2:12), differing sharply from the Masoretic Text ('Say to the righteous that it will go well with him').
- **3:22** — Laconian (Λακωνικά) refers to fine, sheer Spartan-style garments and is an LXX-distinct geographic designation not present in the Masoretic Text.
- **3:25** — The LXX reads the first clause in the singular ('your most handsome son whom you love'), whereas the Masoretic Text has plural 'your men'; similarly in 3:26, the LXX has 'the cases of your adornment' rather than 'her gates.'
