## Text

**7:1** And it came about in the days of Ahaz, son of Jotham, son of Uzziah, king of Judah, that Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up against Jerusalem to make war on it, and they were not able to besiege it.

**7:2** And it was reported to the house of David, saying, "Aram has made an agreement with Ephraim." And his soul and the soul of his people were dismayed, just as a tree in a forest is shaken by the wind.

**7:3** And the Lord said to Isaiah, "Go out to meet Ahaz, you and Jashub who is left, your son, to the pool of the upper road of the fuller's field,

**7:4** and say to him, 'Take care to remain calm and do not be afraid, and do not let your soul grow weak because of these two pieces of wood, these smoking firebrands; for when the anger of My wrath comes, I will heal again.

**7:5** And the son of Aram and the son of Remaliah, because they have planned an evil plan:

**7:6** "We will go up into Judea, and having spoken with them, we will turn them to ourselves, and we will make the son of Tabeel king over it,"

**7:7** thus says the Lord Sabaoth, "This plan will by no means endure, nor will it come about.

**7:8** Rather, the head of Aram is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin; yet within sixty-five years more, the kingdom of Ephraim will cease from being a people.

**7:9** And the head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is the son of Remaliah; and if you do not believe, neither will you by any means understand."'"

**7:10** And the Lord spoke again to Ahaz, saying,

**7:11** "Ask for yourself a sign from the Lord your God, in depth or in height."

**7:12** And Ahaz said, "I will by no means ask, nor will I test the Lord."

**7:13** And he said, "Listen now, house of David: Is it a small thing for you to cause trouble for people, and how is it that you cause trouble for the Lord?

**7:14** Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign: Look, the virgin will conceive in the womb and give birth to a son, and you will call His name Immanuel.

**7:15** He will eat butter and honey before He knows or prefers evil things, to choose the good;

**7:16** for before the child knows good or evil, He refuses wickedness to choose the good, and the land that you fear because of the presence of the two kings will be forsaken.

**7:17** But God will bring upon you and upon your people and upon your father's house days that have not yet come since the day He took away Ephraim from Judah—the king of the Assyrians."

**7:18** And it will happen in that day that the Lord will whistle to flies, which will rule over part of the river of Egypt, and to the bee that is in the country of the Assyrians;

**7:19** and they will all come in the ravines of the country and in the crevices of the rocks, and into the caves and into every fissure.

**7:20** In that day the Lord will shave the head with the razor hired beyond the river of the king of the Assyrians, and He will take away the hair of the feet and the beard.

**7:21** And it will happen in that day, a man will raise a heifer of cattle and two sheep;

**7:22** and it will happen, from the producing of abundant milk, that everyone who is left on the land will eat butter and honey.

**7:23** And it will happen in that day, every place where there are a thousand vines worth a thousand shekels will become barren ground and thorns;

**7:24** with arrow and bow they will enter there, because all the land will be barren ground and thorns.

**7:25** And every mountain that is plowed will be plowed; fear will by no means come there, for from the barren ground and thorns it will become pasture for sheep and trampling for cattle.

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — Greek renders the symbolic name Shear-jashub ("A remnant will return") half in translation ("who is left") and half in transliteration ("Jashub," Greek Iasoub).
- **7:4** — Greek ends with "for when the anger of My wrath comes, I will heal again" and begins 7:5 with "the son of Aram," diverging from the MT ("because of the fierce anger of Rezin and Aram").
- **7:9** — Greek transliterates Samaria here as Somoron (unlike Samareias in 8:4) and renders the closing clause "neither will you by any means understand" (MT: "you will not be established").
- **7:14** — Greek renders the noun with the definite article as ἡ παρθένος ("the virgin," cf. Matt 1:23) and uses second-person singular "you will call" (MT: "she will call").
- **7:25** — Greek turns the MT's warning ("you will not go there for fear of briars and thorns") into a promise that every plowed mountain will be plowed and fear will not come there.
