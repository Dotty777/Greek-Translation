## Text

**20:1** In the year when Tanathan entered into Ashdod, when he was sent by Arna, king of the Assyrians, and he fought against Ashdod and took it,

**20:2** then the Lord spoke to Isaiah son of Amoz, saying, "Go and remove the sackcloth from your waist, and untie your sandals from your feet, and do so, going naked and barefoot."

**20:3** And the Lord said, "Just as My servant Isaiah has gone naked and barefoot for three years, three years will be for signs and wonders to the Egyptians and Ethiopians;

**20:4** for thus the king of the Assyrians will lead the captives of Egypt and of the Ethiopians, young men and old men, naked and barefoot alike, having the shame of Egypt covered.

**20:5** And having been defeated, they will be ashamed over the Ethiopians, in whom the Egyptians had trusted, who were a glory to them.

**20:6** And those who dwell on this island will say on that day, 'Look, we had trusted to flee to them for help, who were not able to be saved from the king of the Assyrians; and how will we be saved?'"

## Translator Notes

- **20:1** — The Greek preserves the distinct proper names Tanathan (corresponding to the Assyrian military title "Tartan" in Hebrew) and Arna (corresponding to "Sargon" in Hebrew), and spells Ashdod with its standard Greek form Azotus (Ἄζωτος), modernized here to Ashdod.
- **20:3** — The Greek repeats "three years" twice and uses the plural "signs and wonders" where the Masoretic Text has "three years" once with singular "a sign and a portent."
- **20:4** — The Greek literally reads "having the shame of Egypt covered" (kekalymmenous), in contrast to the Masoretic Text's "with buttocks uncovered, the shame of Egypt."
