## Text

**35:1** Be glad, thirsty desert; let the desert exult and blossom like a lily,

**35:2** and the desert places of the Jordan will blossom forth and exult; the glory of Lebanon has been given to it, and the honor of Carmel, and My people will see the glory of the Lord and the majesty of God.

**35:3** Be strong, drooping hands and paralyzed knees.

**35:4** Comfort, you who are fainthearted in mind; be strong, do not fear; look, our God renders judgment and will render it; He Himself will come and save us.

**35:5** Then the eyes of the blind will be opened, and the ears of the deaf will hear.

**35:6** Then the lame one will leap like a deer, and the tongue of those who speak with difficulty will be clear, because water has broken forth in the desert, and a ravine in a thirsty land,

**35:7** and the waterless land will become marshes, and in the thirsty land there will be a spring of water; there will be gladness of birds, dwellings of reeds and marshes.

**35:8** There will be a clean way there, and it will be called a holy way, and an unclean person will by no means pass there, nor will there be an unclean way there; but the scattered ones will walk upon it; they will by no means go astray.

**35:9** And no lion will be there, nor will any of the evil beasts go up onto it, nor ever be found there, but the redeemed will walk on it,

**35:10** and those gathered because of the Lord; and they will return and come to Zion with gladness, and eternal gladness will be over their head, praise and exultation, and gladness will overtake them; pain and sorrow and groaning have fled away.

## Translator Notes

- **35:2** — The Greek includes 'the desert places of the Jordan' and 'My people,' both absent from the Masoretic Text.
- **35:4** — Greek 'Comfort' (parakalesate) addresses the 'fainthearted in mind' directly, whereas the Masoretic Text reads 'Say to those of anxious heart.'
- **35:8** — The Septuagint adds 'nor will there be an unclean way there' and reads 'the scattered ones' (hoi diesparmenoi) where the Masoretic Text has 'fools.'
- **35:10** — The Septuagint adds 'praise and exultation' and concludes with three nouns and an aorist verb ('pain and sorrow and groaning have fled away'), contrasting with the Masoretic Text's future 'sorrow and sighing shall flee away.'
