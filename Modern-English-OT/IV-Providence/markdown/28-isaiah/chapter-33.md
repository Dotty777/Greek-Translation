## Text

**33:1** Woe to those who bring misery upon you, yet no one makes you miserable; and the one who breaks faith with you does not break faith; those who break faith will be captured and handed over, and like a moth on a garment, so they will be defeated.

**33:2** Lord, have mercy on us, for in You we have trusted; the seed of the disobedient has come to destruction, but our salvation is in a time of distress.

**33:3** Because of the sound of fear, peoples were dismayed at the fear of You, and the nations were scattered.

**33:4** But now your spoils will be gathered, of small and great; just as when someone gathers locusts, so they will mock you.

**33:5** Holy is God, who dwells on high; Zion has been filled with justice; and in righteousness

**33:6** by law they will be handed over; in treasures is our salvation; there has come wisdom and knowledge and godliness toward the Lord; these are treasures of righteousness.

**33:7** Look now, in your fear these will be afraid; those whom you used to fear will cry out because of you; messengers will be sent, weeping bitterly, pleading for peace.

**33:8** For the ways of these will be made desolate; the fear of the nations has ceased, and the covenant with these is taken away, and you will by no means consider them human beings.

**33:9** The land mourned, Lebanon was put to shame, Sharon became marshes; Galilee and Carmel will be exposed.

**33:10** "Now I will arise," says the Lord, "now I will be glorified, now I will be exalted;

**33:11** now you will see, now you will perceive; futile will be the strength of your spirit, fire will devour you,

**33:12** and nations will be burned up like a thornbush cast out in a field and burned up.

**33:13** Those who are far off will hear what I have done; those who draw near will know My strength."

**33:14** The lawless in Zion have departed; trembling will seize the ungodly. Who will announce to you that fire is burning? Who will announce to you the eternal place?

**33:15** One who walks in righteousness, speaking a straight way, hating lawlessness and unrighteousness, and shaking his hands away from gifts, dulling his ears so that he may not hear a judgment of blood, shutting his eyes so that he may not see unrighteousness,

**33:16** this one will dwell in a high cave of a strong rock; bread will be given to him, and his water is sure.

**33:17** You will see a king with glory; your eyes will see a land from afar.

**33:18** Our soul will meditate on fear. Where are the scribes? Where are the counselors? Where is the one who counts those being nourished,

**33:19** a small and great people? One with whom he did not take counsel, nor did he know a deep-voiced one, so that a despised people did not hear, and there is no understanding for the one who hears.

**33:20** Look, Zion the city, our salvation; your eyes will see Jerusalem, a rich city, tents that will by no means be shaken, nor will the stakes of its tent be moved for eternal time, nor will its ropes ever be broken.

**33:21** For the name of the Lord is great for you. There will be a place for you, rivers and canals wide and spacious; you will not go this way, nor will a rowing ship go on it.

**33:22** For my God is great; He will not pass me by; the Lord is our judge, the Lord is our ruler, the Lord is our king, the Lord, He will save us.

**33:23** Your ropes were broken, because they were not strong; your mast tilted; it will not slacken the sails, it will not raise a standard, until it is handed over for plunder; therefore many lame people will take plunder.

**33:24** And the people dwelling among them will by no means say, "I am weary," for their sin has been forgiven.

## Translator Notes

- **33:1** — The Septuagint uniquely reads 'like a moth on a garment, so they will be defeated,' differing from the Masoretic Text ('when you finish betraying').
- **33:5** — The Greek critical text punctuates after 'justice' (genitive) so that 'and in righteousness' (dative) carries directly into v. 6 ('by law they will be handed over'), whereas the Masoretic Text joins 'and righteousness' to v. 5.
- **33:9** — The Greek reads 'Galilee' (Galilaia) where the Masoretic Text has 'Bashan,' and renders the preceding phrase as 'Sharon became marshes.'
- **33:19** — Continuing the question from v. 18 ('those being nourished,' vs. MT 'the towers'), the Greek syntax is notably compressed: 'One with whom' refers back to the people, and 'a despised people' stands in the nominative as the subject of the infinitive 'did not hear.'
- **33:22** — The Septuagint renders 'For my God is great; He will not pass me by,' whereas the Masoretic Text refers to 'a majestic ship' that cannot pass through the waters of v. 21.
