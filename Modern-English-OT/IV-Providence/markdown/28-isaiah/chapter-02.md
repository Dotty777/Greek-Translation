## Text

**2:1** The word that came to Isaiah son of Amoz concerning Judea and concerning Jerusalem.

**2:2** For in the last days the mountain of the Lord will be clearly visible, and the house of God on the top of the mountains, and it will be exalted above the hills, and all the nations will come to it.

**2:3** And many nations will go and say, "Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the Lord and to the house of the God of Jacob, and He will declare to us His way, and we will walk in it." For from Zion law will go forth, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem;

**2:4** and He will judge between the nations, and will reprove a numerous people; and they will beat their swords into plows and their spears into sickles, and nation will not take a sword against nation, and they will never again learn to wage war.

**2:5** And now, house of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord.

**2:6** For He has forsaken His people, the house of Israel, because their country was filled, as from the beginning, with divinations, like that of the foreigners, and many foreign children became theirs.

**2:7** For their country was filled with silver and gold, and there was no number to their treasures; and the land was filled with horses, and there was no number to their chariots;

**2:8** and the land was filled with abominations, the works of their hands, and they worshiped what their fingers made;

**2:9** and a human being bowed down, and a man was brought low, and I will certainly not forgive them.

**2:10** And now, enter into the rocks and hide yourselves in the earth from before the terror of the Lord and from the glory of His strength, when He rises to shatter the earth.

**2:11** For the eyes of the Lord are high, while humanity is low; and the loftiness of human beings will be brought low, and the Lord alone will be exalted in that day.

**2:12** For the day of the Lord Sabaoth is against everyone who is arrogant and proud, and against everyone who is high and lofty, and they will be brought low,

**2:13** and against every cedar of Lebanon, of the high and lofty ones, and against every oak tree of Bashan,

**2:14** and against every mountain, and against every high hill,

**2:15** and against every high tower, and against every high wall,

**2:16** and against every ship of the sea, and against every sight of ships of beauty;

**2:17** and every human being will be brought low, and the arrogance of human beings will fall, and the Lord alone will be exalted in that day.

**2:18** And they will hide away all the handmade things,

**2:19** carrying them into the caves, and into the clefts of the rocks, and into the holes of the earth, from before the terror of the Lord and from the glory of His strength, when He rises to shatter the earth.

**2:20** For on that day a human being will throw out his silver and gold abominations, which they made to worship, to the worthless things and to the bats,

**2:21** in order to enter into the holes of the solid rock and into the clefts of the rocks, from before the terror of the Lord and from the glory of His strength, when He rises to shatter the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — Where the Hebrew text has a single construct phrase ("the mountain of the house of the Lord"), the Septuagint divides it into two parallel subjects: "the mountain of the Lord" (which will be "clearly visible") and "the house of God" ("on the top of the mountains").
- **2:6** — "He has forsaken" translates a third-person verb (unlike the second-person address in Hebrew), and "as from the beginning" interprets Hebrew miqqedem temporally rather than geographically ("from the east"); "foreigners" renders allophyloi, often used in the Septuagint for the Philistines.
- **2:11** — "For the eyes of the Lord are high, while humanity is low" translates the Septuagint's contrast between God and humanity, diverging from the Hebrew text ("The haughty eyes of humanity will be brought low").
- **2:20** — "To the worthless things" renders Greek tois mataiois (futile idols) where the Hebrew text has "to the moles/shrews"; Septuagint Isaiah 2 also ends at verse 21 without the additional verse found at 2:22 in the Hebrew text.
