## Text

**23:1** The word of Tyre. Wail, ships of Carthage, for she has perished, and they no longer come from the land of the Citians; she has been led away captive.

**23:2** To whom have those who live on the island become like, traders of Phoenicia, crossing over the sea

**23:3** in much water, seed of traders? Like a harvest being brought in are the traders of the nations.

**23:4** "Be ashamed, Sidon," said the sea; and the strength of the sea said, "I was not in labor, nor did I give birth, nor did I rear young men, nor raise up virgins."

**23:5** And when it is heard by Egypt, pain will seize them concerning Tyre.

**23:6** Go away to Carthage; wail, you who dwell on this island.

**23:7** Was this not your insolence from the beginning, before she was handed over?

**23:8** Who planned these things against Tyre? Surely she is not inferior, or is she not strong? Her merchants are glorious, rulers of the earth.

**23:9** The Lord Sabaoth planned to undo all the insolence of the glorious ones, and to dishonor every glorious thing upon the earth.

**23:10** Work your land, for ships no longer come from Carthage.

**23:11** And your hand is no longer strong over the sea, which provokes kings; the Lord Sabaoth has commanded concerning Canaan to destroy her strength.

**23:12** And they will say, "You will by no means continue to treat insolently and wrong the daughter of Sidon; and if you go away to the Citians, not even there will there be rest for you;

**23:13** and to the land of the Chaldeans—even it has been made desolate by the Assyrians; its wall has fallen."

**23:14** Wail, ships of Carthage, for your stronghold has perished.

**23:15** And it will be in that day that Tyre will be forsaken for seventy years, like the time of a king, like the time of a man; and it will be after seventy years that Tyre will be like the song of a prostitute.

**23:16** Take a harp, roam about, forgotten prostitute city; play the harp well, sing many songs, so that there may be remembrance of you.

**23:17** And it will be after the seventy years that God will make a visitation of Tyre, and again she will be restored to the ancient state, and she will be a marketplace for all the kingdoms of the inhabited world upon the face of the earth.

**23:18** And her trade and wages will be holy to the Lord; it will not be gathered for them, but for those who dwell before the Lord, all her trade, to eat and to drink and to be filled, and for a contribution, a memorial before the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **23:1** — Throughout this chapter, the Septuagint identifies MT 'Tarshish' with 'Carthage' (Karchedon) and renders MT 'Kittim' with the Greek gentilics 'Citians' (Kitiaioi / Kitieis, inhabitants of Citium in Cyprus).
- **23:2** — Greek names 'Phoenicia' (Phoinikes) where the Hebrew MT has 'Sidon,' and reads 'become like' (homoioi gegonasin) for MT 'be silent.'
- **23:8** — Greek asks 'Surely she is not inferior, or is she not strong?' where the Hebrew MT describes Tyre as 'the bestower of crowns.'
- **23:17** — Greek reads 'restored to the ancient state' instead of MT 'return to her harlot's wages,' and in v. 18 renders MT 'durable clothing' as 'a contribution (symbolen), a memorial before the Lord.'
