## Text

**2:2** And He said, "Thus says the Lord: 'I remembered the mercy of your youth and the love of his perfection, your following after the Holy One of Israel,' says the Lord.

**2:3** 'Holy is Israel to the Lord, the beginning of His produce; all who eat him will commit offense; evils will come upon them,' declares the Lord."

**2:4** Hear the word of the Lord, house of Jacob and every family of the house of Israel.

**2:5** Thus says the Lord: "What offense did your fathers find in Me, that they departed far from Me, and went after worthless things and became worthless?

**2:6** And they did not say, 'Where is the Lord who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, who led us in the wilderness, in a boundless and untrodden land, in a waterless and fruitless land, in a land through which nothing passed and where no human being lived?'

**2:7** And I brought you into Carmel, for you to eat its fruits and its good things; and you entered and defiled My land, and you made My inheritance into an abomination.

**2:8** The priests did not say, 'Where is the Lord?' And those who hold fast to the law did not know Me, and the shepherds were acting impiously against Me, and the prophets were prophesying by Baal and went after what is unprofitable.

**2:9** Therefore I will still enter into judgment with you, and with the sons of your sons I will enter into judgment.

**2:10** For go to the islands of Kittim and see, and send to Kedar and consider carefully, and see if such things have happened,

**2:11** whether nations will exchange their gods, and yet these are not gods; but My people have exchanged their glory, from which they will not profit.

**2:12** Heaven was appalled at this and shuddered exceedingly much," says the Lord.

**2:13** "For My people have done two evil things: they forsook Me, a spring of water of life, and dug for themselves broken cisterns that will not be able to hold water.

**2:14** Is Israel a slave, or is he homeborn? Why has he become plunder?

**2:15** Lions roared against him and gave forth their voice, who turned his land into a wasteland, and his cities were torn down so as not to be inhabited.

**2:16** And the sons of Memphis and Tahpanhes knew you and were mocking you;

**2:17** has not your forsaking Me done these things to you?" says the Lord your God.

**2:18** "And now what do you have to do with the way of Egypt, to drink the water of Gihon? And what do you have to do with the way of the Assyrians, to drink the water of rivers?

**2:19** Your apostasy will discipline you, and your wickedness will reprove you; and know and see that it is bitter for you to forsake Me," says the Lord your God; "and I have not taken pleasure in you," says the Lord your God.

**2:20** "For from of old you broke your yoke; you tore apart your bonds, and you said, 'I will not serve You, but I will go upon every high hill and underneath every shady tree; there I will stretch myself out in my prostitution.'

**2:21** Yet I planted you as a fruitful vine, wholly genuine; how did you turn into bitterness, O foreign vine?

**2:22** Even if you wash with lye and multiply soap for yourself, you are stained in your iniquities before Me," says the Lord.

**2:23** "How will you say, 'I have not been defiled, and I have not gone after Baal'? See your ways in the burial ground, and know what you have done. At evening her voice wailed,

**2:24** she widened her ways to the waters of the wilderness; in the desires of her soul she was carried along by the wind; she was given over. Who will turn her back? All who seek her will not grow weary; in her humiliation they will find her.

**2:25** Turn back your foot from a rough road, and your throat from thirst. But she said, 'I will be strong,' for she had loved foreigners and was going after them.

**2:26** Like the shame of a thief when he is caught, so the sons of Israel will be put to shame, they and their kings and their rulers and their priests and their prophets.

**2:27** To the wood they said, 'You are my father,' and to the stone, 'You gave birth to me.' And they turned their backs to Me and not their faces; yet in the time of their troubles they will say, 'Arise and save us!'

**2:28** And where are your gods that you made for yourself? Will they arise and save in the time of your affliction? For according to the number of your cities were your gods, Judah, and according to the number of the streets of Jerusalem they sacrificed to Baal.

**2:29** Why do you speak to Me? All of you have acted impiously, and all of you have acted lawlessly against Me," says the Lord.

**2:30** "In vain I struck your children; you did not accept discipline. A sword devoured your prophets like a destroying lion, and you were not afraid."

**2:31** Hear the word of the Lord. Thus says the Lord: "Have I become a wilderness to Israel, or a barren land? Why did My people say, 'We will not be ruled over, and we will not come to You anymore'?

**2:32** Will a bride forget her ornament, and a virgin her breastband? Yet My people have forgotten Me for days without number.

**2:33** What good thing will you still devise in your ways to seek love? Not so; rather, you yourself have acted wickedly to defile your ways,

**2:34** and on your hands was found the blood of innocent souls; I did not find them in housebreakings, but at every oak.

**2:35** And you said, 'I am innocent; rather, let His wrath turn away from me.' Look, I am entering into judgment with you because you say, 'I have not sinned.'

**2:36** Why have you been so exceedingly contemptuous as to repeat your ways? By Egypt also you will be put to shame, just as you were put to shame by Assyria,

**2:37** for from here also you will go out with your hands on your head, because the Lord has rejected your hope, and you will not prosper in it."

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Septuagint omits MT 2:1–2a and diverges sharply here, reading "the love of his perfection" (τελειώσεως αὐτοῦ) and "the Holy One of Israel" where MT refers to bridal devotion in the wilderness.
- **2:7** — The Greek renders Hebrew ha-karmel ("the fruitful land") as the proper name "Carmel" (τὸν Κάρμηλον).
- **2:8** — Here and in vv. 23 and 28, the Greek uses the feminine article with Baal (τῇ Βάαλ), reflecting the practice of reading Hebrew boshet ("shame," feminine) in place of Baal.
- **2:18** — The Greek uses the river name "Gihon" (Γηών, cf. Gen 2:13) where MT has "Shihor" (the Nile), and plural "rivers" where MT has singular "the River" (the Euphrates).
- **2:23** — Verses 23–24 differ substantially from MT, reading "in the burial ground" (πολυανδρίῳ, MT "in the valley"), "at evening her voice wailed," "she was carried along by the wind," and "in her humiliation" (v. 24, MT "in her month").
