## Text

**18:1** The word that came from the Lord to Jeremiah, saying:

**18:2** "Rise up and go down to the potter's house, and there you will hear My words."

**18:3** And I went down to the potter's house, and look, he was doing work on the stones.

**18:4** And the vessel that he was making fell in his hands; and again he made it into another vessel, just as it was pleasing before him to make.

**18:5** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying:

**18:6** "Just as this potter does, will I not be able to do with you, house of Israel? Look, like the potter's clay, you are in My hands.

**18:7** In the end I will speak concerning a nation or concerning a kingdom, to remove them and to destroy,

**18:8** and should that nation turn from all their evils, I will change My mind concerning the evils that I planned to do to them.

**18:9** And in the end I will speak concerning a nation and a kingdom, to be rebuilt and to be planted,

**18:10** and should they do wicked things before Me, so as not to listen to My voice, I will change My mind concerning the good things that I spoke of doing for them.

**18:11** And now speak to the men of Judah and to those who dwell in Jerusalem: 'Look, I am forming evils against you, and I am devising a plan against you. Let each one turn back now from his wicked way, and you will make your practices better.'"

**18:12** And they said, "We will act courageously, for we will walk after our turnings away, and each of us will do the pleasing things of his wicked heart."

**18:13** Therefore thus says the Lord: "Ask now among the nations, 'Who has heard such horrifying things as virgin Israel has exceedingly done?'

**18:14** Will breasts fail from a rock, or snow from Lebanon? Will water carried violently by the wind turn aside?

**18:15** For My people have forgotten Me; they have burned incense in vain, and they will grow weak in their ways—eternal cords—so as to tread paths that have no road for travel,

**18:16** to set their land for desolation and an eternal hissing; all who pass through it will be astounded and will shake their heads.

**18:17** Like a scorching wind I will scatter them before the face of their enemies; I will show them the day of their destruction."

**18:18** And they said, "Come, let us devise a plan against Jeremiah, for law will not perish from a priest, nor counsel from a discerning man, nor a word from a prophet. Come, and let us strike him with the tongue, and we will listen to all his words."

**18:19** Listen to me, Lord, and listen to the voice of my just cause.

**18:20** Is evil repaid for good? For they have spoken words together against my life, and they have hidden their punishment for me. Remember my standing before Your face to speak good things on their behalf, to turn away Your wrath from them.

**18:21** Therefore give their sons over to famine, and gather them into the hands of the sword; let their wives become childless and widows, and let their husbands become killed by death, and their young men fallen by the sword in war.

**18:22** Let an outcry arise in their houses; You will bring raiders upon them suddenly, because they took up a word for my capture and hid snares against me.

**18:23** And You, Lord, know all their counsel against me for death; do not acquit their injustices, and do not blot out their sins from before Your face. Let their weakness be before You; in the time of Your wrath, deal with them.

## Translator Notes

- **18:3** — Greek "on the stones" refers to the two stone disks of a potter's wheel. In 18:4 the Septuagint reads "fell" (epesen) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "was spoiled."
- **18:7** — The Greek adverbial peras ("in the end" or "finally," also in 18:9) corresponds to the Hebrew rega' ("at one moment" or "suddenly").
- **18:12** — The Septuagint reads "We will act courageously" (andrioumetha) and "our turnings away" where the Hebrew reads "It is hopeless" and "our plans."
- **18:14** — The Septuagint reads "breasts from a rock" (mastoi, reflecting a reading of the Hebrew word for "field" as "breasts") and "water carried violently by the wind," differing markedly from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **18:15** — Greek schoinous aiōnious ("eternal cords," perhaps denoting boundary lines or tracks) stands where the Hebrew reads "ancient paths." In 18:18 the Septuagint also lacks a negative before "we will listen to all his words."
