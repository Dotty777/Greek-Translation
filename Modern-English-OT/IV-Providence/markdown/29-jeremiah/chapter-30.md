## Text

**30:1** Concerning Idumea. Thus says the Lord: "There is no longer wisdom in Teman; counsel has perished from the discerning, their wisdom has vanished,

**30:2** their place has been deceived. Go deep to settle, you who dwell in Dedan, for he has done grievous things; I brought them upon him in the time when I called him to account.

**30:3** For grape gatherers have come who will not leave you a remnant; like thieves in the night they will put forth their hand.

**30:4** For I have stripped Esau; I have uncovered their hidden places, and they will by no means be able to hide. They have perished by the hand of his brother, My neighbor, and it is not possible

**30:5** to leave your orphan behind so that he may live; and I will live, and widows have trusted in Me."

**30:6** For thus said the Lord: "Those for whom there was no law to drink the cup have drunk it; and you will by no means be acquitted,

**30:7** for by Myself I have sworn," says the Lord, "that you will become an untrodden waste and a reproach and a curse in her midst, and all her cities will be desolate forever."

**30:8** I have heard a report from the Lord, and He has sent messengers to the nations: "Gather together and come against her; rise up for war."

**30:9** "I have made you small among the nations, despised among people.

**30:10** Your playfulness has set upon you; the audacity of your heart has lodged in the clefts of the rocks and has seized the stronghold of a high hill. Because he has raised his nest high like an eagle, from there I will bring you down.

**30:11** And Idumea will become an untrodden waste; everyone who passes by her will hiss.

**30:12** Just as Sodom and Gomorrah and her neighboring cities were overthrown," said the Lord Almighty, "no man will ever settle there, and no son of man will dwell there.

**30:13** Look, like a lion he will come up from the midst of the Jordan to the place of Etham, for I will quickly drive them out from her; and set the young men over her. For who is like Me? And who will withstand Me? And who is this shepherd who will stand before My face?"

**30:14** Therefore hear the counsel of the Lord which He has formed against Idumea, and His plan which He has devised against those who dwell in Teman: "Surely the least of the sheep will be swept away; surely their dwelling place will be made untrodden over her;

**30:15** for at the sound of their fall the earth was afraid, and a cry of the sea was not heard."

**30:16** Now, like an eagle he will look and stretch out his wings over her strongholds; and the heart of the mighty ones of Idumea in that day will be like the heart of a woman in labor.

**30:17** Concerning the sons of Ammon. Thus said the Lord: "Are there no sons in Israel, or is there no one to take possession for them? Why has Melchol taken possession of Gilead, and why will their people dwell in their cities?

**30:18** Therefore look, days are coming," declares the Lord, "and I will make the uproar of wars heard against Rabbah; and they will become an untrodden waste and a destruction, and her altars will be burned up with fire, and Israel will take possession of his dominion.

**30:19** Wail, Heshbon, for it has perished; cry out, daughters of Rabbah! Wrap yourselves in sackcloth and lament, for Melchol will go into exile, his priests and his rulers together.

**30:20** Why do you exult in the plains of the Anakim? Daughter of audacity, who trusts in her treasures, who says, 'Who will come against me?'

**30:21** Look, I am bringing terror upon you," said the Lord, "from all your surrounding region, and you will be scattered, each one before his own face; there is no one who gathers."

**30:23** Concerning Kedar, queen of the court, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon struck. Thus said the Lord: "Rise up and go up against Kedar, and fill the sons of Kedem.

**30:24** Their tents and their sheep they will take; their garments and all their goods and their camels they will take for themselves; and summon destruction against them on every side.

**30:25** Flee! You have gone very deep to settle, you who dwell in the court, for the king of Babylon has formed a counsel against you and has devised a plan.

**30:26** Rise up and go up against a tranquil nation, settled at ease, who have no doors, no bolts, no bars; they dwell alone.

**30:27** And their camels will become plunder, and the multitude of their livestock for destruction; and I will winnow them to every wind, those who are clipped before their faces; from every side beyond them I will bring their rout," said the Lord.

**30:28** And the court will become a haunt of ostriches and untrodden forever; no man will ever settle there, and no son of man will dwell there.

**30:29** Concerning Damascus. Themar and Arpad have been put to shame, for they have heard an evil report; they were dismayed, they were enraged; they will by no means be able to rest.

**30:30** Damascus has grown feeble; she has turned to flight; trembling has seized her.

**30:31** "How has she not forsaken My city? They loved a village.

**30:32** Therefore young men will fall in your streets, and all your men of war will fall," declares the Lord,

**30:33** "and I will kindle a fire in the wall of Damascus, and it will devour the quarters of the son of Hader."

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — Chapter 30 in the Septuagint gathers four oracles against the nations (Idumea, Ammon, Kedar, and Damascus) in a different order from the Hebrew text (MT 49:1-33). Idumea is the standard Greek name for Edom; in 30:2, Greek "their place has been deceived" and "he has done grievous things" diverge markedly from the Hebrew.
- **30:4** — The Greek reads "by the hand of his brother, My neighbor" and links the end of 30:4 syntactically to the infinitive in 30:5 ("it is not possible to leave your orphan behind"), followed by "I will live" where the Hebrew reads "I will keep them alive."
- **30:13** — Greek Aitham ("Etham") renders the Hebrew adjective "perennial/enduring" as a proper place name, and "set the young men over her" reflects a different reading from the Hebrew. Similarly, in 30:15-16, the Greek reads "a cry of the sea was not heard" and "he will look" (for Hebrew "swoop") over "her strongholds" (for Hebrew "Bozrah").
- **30:17** — Greek Melchol (also 30:19) is the Septuagint form of the Ammonite deity Malcam/Milcom. In 30:18-20, the Greek reads "her altars" (Hebrew "her daughters/villages"), "his dominion," and "the plains of the Anakim" (Greek Enakeim), and omits MT 49:6 (no verse 30:22).
- **30:23** — Greek aulē ("court," also 30:25, 28) translates the Hebrew place name Hazor as a common noun, Kedem transliterates the Hebrew word for "east," and plēsate ("fill") stands where the Hebrew has "devastate." In 30:27, Greek kekramenous is a metathesized form of kekarmenous ("clipped/shaved"), referring to trimming the hair at the temples.
- **30:29** — Greek Themar is a distinct Septuagint proper name where the Hebrew text reads Hamath, and in 30:33 Greek huiou Hader ("the son of Hader") corresponds to Hebrew Ben-hadad. In 30:31, the Greek reads active "How has she not forsaken My city? They loved a village."
