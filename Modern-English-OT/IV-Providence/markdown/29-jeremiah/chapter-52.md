## Text

**52:1** Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem; and his mother's name was Hamutal, daughter of Jeremiah from Libnah.

**52:4** And it happened in the ninth year of his reign, in the ninth month, on the tenth of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and all his army came against Jerusalem, and they surrounded it with a palisade and built four-footed siegeworks all around it.

**52:5** And the city came under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah,

**52:6** on the ninth of the month; and the famine grew severe in the city, and there was no bread for the people of the land.

**52:7** And the city was breached, and all the men of war went out by night by the way of the gate between the wall and the outer wall, which was by the king's garden, while the Chaldeans were all around the city. And they went by the way to the Arabah,

**52:8** and the army of the Chaldeans pursued after the king, and they overtook him on the far side of Jericho, and all his servants were scattered from him.

**52:9** And they captured the king and brought him to the king of Babylon at Deblatha, and he spoke to him with judgment.

**52:10** And the king of Babylon slaughtered the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes, and he slaughtered all the rulers of Judah in Deblatha.

**52:11** And he blinded the eyes of Zedekiah and bound him in fetters; and the king of Babylon brought him to Babylon and put him in a millhouse until the day he died.

**52:12** And in the fifth month, on the tenth of the month, Nebuzaradan the chief cook, who stood before the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem,

**52:13** and he burned the house of the Lord and the house of the king, and all the houses of the city and every great house he burned with fire.

**52:14** And the army of the Chaldeans that was with the chief cook tore down every wall of Jerusalem all around.

**52:16** And the chief cook left the remnant of the people as vinedressers and as farmers.

**52:17** And the Chaldeans broke in pieces the bronze pillars that were in the house of the Lord, and the stands, and the bronze sea that was in the house of the Lord, and they took their bronze and carried it away to Babylon.

**52:18** and the rim, and the bowls, and the meat forks, and all the bronze vessels with which they ministered,

**52:19** and the sapphoth, and the masmaroth, and the pouring vessels, and the lampstands, and the censers, and the ladles—whatever was gold as gold and whatever was silver as silver—the chief cook took.

**52:20** And the two pillars, and the one sea, and the twelve bronze calves beneath the sea, which King Solomon made for the house of the Lord—there was no weighing of their bronze.

**52:21** And as for the pillars, the height of the one pillar was thirty-five cubits, and a cord of twelve cubits encircled it, and its thickness was four fingers all around,

**52:22** and a bronze capital was upon them, and five cubits was the length, the height of the one capital, and network and pomegranates were upon the capital all around, all of bronze; and it was the same for the second pillar: eight pomegranates to the cubit for the twelve cubits.

**52:23** And the pomegranates were ninety-six on one side, and all the pomegranates were one hundred upon the network all around.

**52:24** And the chief cook took the first priest and the second priest and the three who guarded the way,

**52:25** and one eunuch who was overseer of the men of war, and seven renowned men who were in the presence of the king, who were found in the city, and the scribe of the armies who served as scribe for the people of the land, and sixty men from the people of the land who were found in the midst of the city.

**52:26** And Nebuzaradan the chief cook took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Deblatha;

**52:27** and the king of Babylon struck them down in Deblatha in the land of Hamath.

**52:31** And it happened in the thirty-seventh year after Jehoiakim king of Judah was taken into exile, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-fourth of the month, that Evil-merodach king of Babylon, in the year in which he became king, lifted up the head of Jehoiakim king of Judah and cut his hair, and brought him out of the house where he was guarded,

**52:32** and spoke kindly to him, and set his throne above the kings who were with him in Babylon,

**52:33** and they changed his prison garment, and he ate bread continually before him all the days that he lived.

**52:34** And his allowance was given to him continually from the king of Babylon, from day to day, until the day he died.

## Translator Notes

- **52:4** — Verses 52:2-3 of the Hebrew numbering are absent from the Septuagint. Greek reads "in the ninth month" (Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "tenth month"); "four-footed siegeworks" renders Greek tetrapodois, understood either as wooden siege barricades or (as a spelling of tetrapedois) four-foot stone blocks.
- **52:9** — Greek Deblatha (also in 52:10, 26, 27) corresponds to Hebrew Riblah, reflecting a common daleth/resh consonant variation.
- **52:12** — Greek archimageiros ("chief cook," also in 52:14, 16, 19, 24, 26) is the Septuagint's literal rendering of the Hebrew royal-military title rab-tabbachim ("captain of the guard" or "chief of the slaughterers").
- **52:19** — Greek sapphoth and masmaroth are untranslated transliterations of Hebrew temple vessel terms (likely "basins" and "wick trimmers/snuffers").
- **52:21** — Greek reads "thirty-five cubits" (matching 2 Chronicles 3:15; Hebrew Jeremiah 52:21 reads "eighteen cubits"); in 52:22, Greek also uniquely adds "eight pomegranates to the cubit for the twelve cubits."
- **52:31** — Verses 52:28-30 of the Hebrew numbering (the exile deportation totals) are absent from the Septuagint. Greek reads "Jehoiakim" (Ioakeim, the form the Septuagint regularly uses for both Jehoiakim and Jehoiachin), "twenty-fourth" (Hebrew "twenty-fifth"), transcribes Evil-merodach as Oulaimadachar, and uniquely adds "and cut his hair" (kai ekeiren auton) after "lifted up" (literally "took") his head.
