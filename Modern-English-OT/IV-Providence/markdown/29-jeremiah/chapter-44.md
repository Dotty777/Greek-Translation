## Text

**44:1** And Zedekiah son of Josiah reigned instead of Jehoiakim, whom Nebuchadnezzar made king to reign over Judah.

**44:2** And he and his servants and the people of the land did not listen to the words of the Lord that He spoke by the hand of Jeremiah.

**44:3** And King Zedekiah sent Jehucal son of Shelemiah and Zephaniah son of Maaseiah, the priest, to Jeremiah, saying, "Please pray for us to the Lord."

**44:4** And Jeremiah came and passed through the midst of the city, and they had not put him into the prison house.

**44:5** And the army of Pharaoh came out of Egypt, and the Chaldeans heard the report of them, and they went up against Jerusalem.

**44:6** And the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah, saying,

**44:7** "Thus says the Lord: Thus you shall say to the king of Judah who sent to you to seek Me: 'Look, the army of Pharaoh that came out to help you will return to the land of Egypt,

**44:8** and the Chaldeans themselves will come back and fight against this city, and they will capture it and burn it with fire.

**44:9** For thus says the Lord: Do not suppose in your souls, saying, "The Chaldeans will surely depart from us," for they will certainly not depart.

**44:10** And even if you strike down the entire army of the Chaldeans who are fighting against you, and some who have been pierced through are left behind, each one in his place, these will rise up and burn this city with fire.'"

**44:11** And it happened, when the army of the Chaldeans went up from Jerusalem from before the army of Pharaoh,

**44:12** that Jeremiah went out from Jerusalem to go to the land of Benjamin, to buy from there in the midst of the people.

**44:13** And he was at the Gate of Benjamin, and there was a man with whom he was lodging, Saruiah son of Shelemiah son of Hananiah, and he seized Jeremiah, saying, "Are you fleeing to the Chaldeans?"

**44:14** And he said, "A lie! I am not fleeing to the Chaldeans." And he did not listen to him, and Saruiah seized Jeremiah and brought him in to the officials.

**44:15** And the officials became embittered against Jeremiah and struck him, and they sent him to the house of Jonathan the scribe, because they had made it into a prison house.

**44:16** And Jeremiah went into the house of the pit and into the chereth, and he remained there many days.

**44:17** And Zedekiah sent and called him, and the king secretly asked him, saying, "Is there a word from the Lord?" And he said, "There is; into the hands of the king of Babylon you will be handed over."

**44:18** And Jeremiah said to the king, "What wrong have I done to you and to your servants and to this people, that you are putting me into a prison house?

**44:19** And where are your prophets who prophesied to you, saying, 'The king of Babylon will certainly not come against this land'?

**44:20** And now, lord king, let my mercy fall before you; and why are you returning me to the house of Jonathan the scribe, and will I not die there?"

**44:21** And the king gave orders, and they threw him into the prison house, and they were giving him one loaf of bread a day from outside where they bake, until the loaves ran out from the city; and Jeremiah remained in the court of the prison.

## Translator Notes

- **44:5** — The Greek literally reads "went up against Jerusalem" (ἐπὶ Ἰερουσαλήμ), differing from the Hebrew text (Jer 37:5) and v. 11 below, where the Chaldeans withdraw "from Jerusalem."
- **44:12** — The Greek reads "to buy from there" (τοῦ ἀγοράσαι ἐκεῖθεν), rendering a difficult Hebrew verb often understood as taking possession of a portion of property.
- **44:13** — The Septuagint describes the official as "a man with whom he was lodging" (rather than a captain of the guard) and names him "Saruiah" (Σαρουιά, here and in v. 14) instead of the Hebrew "Irijah."
- **44:16** — The word "chereth" (χερέθ) is an untranslated Greek transliteration corresponding to the Hebrew term for vaulted cells or chambers.
- **44:20** — As in 43:7, "my mercy" (τὸ ἔλεός μου) reflects the Hebrew idiom for a plea for mercy; the Septuagint also frames the second half of the verse as a question ("why are you returning me ... and will I not die there?") rather than a negative petition.
