## Text

**41:1** The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord—and Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and all his army and all the land of his dominion were fighting against him, against Jerusalem and against all the cities of Judah—saying,

**41:2** "Thus said the Lord: Go to Zedekiah king of Judah, and you shall say to him, 'Thus said the Lord: This city will surely be handed over into the hands of the king of Babylon, and he will capture it and burn it with fire,

**41:3** and you will by no means escape from his hand, and you will surely be captured and be given into his hands, and your eyes will see his eyes, and you will enter Babylon.

**41:4** Yet hear the word of the Lord, Zedekiah king of Judah. Thus says the Lord:

**41:5** You will die in peace; and just as they wept for your fathers who reigned before you, they will weep for you also, and even to Hades they will mourn for you; for I have spoken a word, said the Lord.'"

**41:6** And Jeremiah spoke all these words to King Zedekiah in Jerusalem.

**41:7** And the force of the king of Babylon was fighting against Jerusalem and against the cities of Judah, and against Lachish and against Azekah, for these were left among the cities of Judah as fortified cities.

**41:8** The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord after King Zedekiah concluded a covenant with the people to proclaim a release,

**41:9** that each one should send away his male servant and each one his female servant, the Hebrew man and the Hebrew woman, free, so that no man from Judah should serve as a slave.

**41:10** And all the nobles and all the people who had entered into the covenant that each one should send away his male servant and his female servant turned back, and they left

**41:11** them as male servants and female servants.

**41:12** And the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah, saying,

**41:13** "Thus said the Lord: I established a covenant with your fathers on the day when I delivered them from the land of Egypt, from the house of slavery, saying,

**41:14** 'When six years are completed, you shall send away your brother, the Hebrew, who will be sold to you and will work for you six years, and you shall send him away free.' And they did not listen to Me, and they did not incline their ear.

**41:15** And they turned back today to do what is right before My eyes, for each one to proclaim a release of his neighbor, and they concluded a covenant before Me in the house upon which My name was called.

**41:16** And you turned back and profaned My name by bringing back each one his male servant and each one his female servant, whom you had sent away free to their own will, to be male servants and female servants for you."

**41:17** Therefore thus said the Lord: "You did not listen to Me to proclaim a release, each one to his neighbor; look, I am proclaiming a release for you to the sword and to death and to famine, and I will give you over to dispersion among all the kingdoms of the earth.

**41:18** And I will give over the men who have transgressed My covenant, who did not uphold My covenant that they made before Me, the calf that they made to serve it,

**41:19** the rulers of Judah and the officials and the priests and the people,

**41:20** and I will give them to their enemies, and their corpses will be food for the birds of heaven and for the wild beasts of the earth.

**41:21** And I will give Zedekiah king of Judea and their rulers into the hands of their enemies, and the force of the king of Babylon is for those who are running away from them.

**41:22** Look, I am giving a command," says the Lord, "and I will bring them back to this land, and they will fight against it and take it and burn it down with fire, and the cities of Judah, and I will make them desolate, without inhabitants."

## Translator Notes

- **41:1** — The Greek literally reads "were fighting against him" before "against Jerusalem," a difficult reading likely arising from rendering a Hebrew phrase meaning "with him" as an object pronoun or anticipating Judah or Zedekiah.
- **41:5** — The Septuagint reads "wept for" and "even to Hades they will mourn for you," differing from the Hebrew text's burning of spices and lament "Alas, lord!"
- **41:10** — The Septuagint moves directly to the people turning back, omitting the initial compliance reported in the Hebrew text. The printed verb form (from eao, "left") can alternatively be accented as an aorist of otheo ("forced" or "drove"), matching the Hebrew sense of subjugating them.
- **41:18** — The Greek reads "the calf that they made to serve it," departing from the Hebrew covenant-cutting ritual ("the calf that they cut in two and passed between its parts").
- **41:21** — The Greek shifts to a nominative noun phrase paired with a dative participle ("and the force of the king of Babylon [is] for those who are running away from them"), whereas the Hebrew coordinates the army under "into the hand of" as those who have withdrawn.
