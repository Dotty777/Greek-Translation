## Text

**49:1** And all the commanders of the force, and Johanan, and Azariah son of Maaseiah, and all the people from the least even to the greatest came near

**49:2** to Jeremiah the prophet and said to him, "Please let our mercy fall before your face, and pray to the Lord your God for this remnant, for we have been left few out of many, just as your eyes see.

**49:3** And let the Lord your God tell us the way in which we should go and the thing that we should do."

**49:4** And Jeremiah said to them, "I have heard; look, I will pray to the Lord our God according to your words. And it will be, whatever word the Lord answers, I will tell you; I will by no means hide a word from you."

**49:5** And they said to Jeremiah, "May the Lord be a righteous and faithful witness among us, if according to every word that the Lord sends to us we do not do so.

**49:6** Whether good or evil, we will listen to the voice of the Lord our God, to whom we are sending you, so that it may be better for us, because we will listen to the voice of the Lord our God."

**49:7** And it happened after ten days that the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah.

**49:8** And he called Johanan and the commanders of the force and all the people from the least even to the greatest,

**49:9** and said to them, "Thus says the Lord:

**49:10** 'If you indeed stay in this land, I will build you up and will by no means tear down, and I will plant you and will by no means uproot, for I have ceased from the evils that I did to you.

**49:11** Do not be afraid of the king of Babylon, of whom you are afraid; do not be afraid, says the Lord, for I am with you to deliver you and to save you from their hand.

**49:12** And I will give you mercy, and I will have mercy on you and return you to your land.'

**49:13** And if you say, 'We will by no means stay in this land,' so as not to listen to the voice of the Lord,

**49:14** 'for we will enter into the land of Egypt, and we will by no means see war, and we will by no means hear the sound of a trumpet, and we will by no means be hungry for bread, and there we will dwell'—

**49:15** therefore hear the word of the Lord. Thus says the Lord: 'If you set your face toward Egypt and enter there to dwell,

**49:16** then it will be that the sword of which you are afraid will find you in the land of Egypt, and the famine about which you are concerned will overtake you behind you in Egypt, and there you will die.

**49:17** And it will be that all the people and all the foreigners who set their face toward the land of Egypt to dwell there will perish by the sword and by the famine, and there will be no one of them escaping from the evils that I am bringing upon them.'

**49:18** For thus says the Lord: 'Just as My wrath poured down upon those who dwell in Jerusalem, so My wrath will pour down upon you when you enter into Egypt; and you will become a desolation, and subjugated, and a curse, and a reproach, and you will by no means see this place any longer.'

**49:19** What the Lord has spoken concerning you, the remnant of Judah, is, 'Do not enter into Egypt.' And now you will certainly know

**49:20** that you have acted wickedly in your souls when you sent me, saying, 'Pray for us to the Lord,' and, 'According to all that the Lord speaks to you, we will do.'

**49:21** And you did not listen to the voice of the Lord, with which He sent me to you.

**49:22** And now by sword and by famine you will perish in the place where you desire to enter to dwell."

## Translator Notes

- **49:1** — The Greek reads Azariah son of Maaseiah here (as in 50:2), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has Jezaniah son of Hoshaiah.
- **49:10** — Greek anapepaumai literally means "I have rested" or "I have ceased," here rendering the Hebrew verb for "relent."
- **49:12** — All three verbs in the Greek are first person ("I will give... I will have mercy... and return"), making the Lord the subject of each action, whereas in the Hebrew text the last two refer to the king of Babylon.
- **49:17** — The phrase "and all the foreigners" is distinctive to the Greek text and does not appear in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
