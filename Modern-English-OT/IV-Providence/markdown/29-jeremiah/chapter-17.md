## Text

**17:5** Cursed is the man who has his hope in man, and rests the flesh of his arm upon him, and his heart turns away from the Lord;

**17:6** and he will be like the wild tamarisk in the wilderness; he will not see when good things come, and he will dwell in salt flats and in a wilderness, in a salty land that is not inhabited.

**17:7** And blessed is the man who trusts in the Lord, and the Lord will be his hope;

**17:8** and he will be like a thriving tree beside waters, and toward moisture it will cast its root; it will not fear when heat comes, and upon it there will be leafy branches; in a year of drought it will not fear, and it will not cease bearing fruit.

**17:9** Deep is the heart beyond all things, and it is a man; and who will know him?

**17:10** I am the Lord, examining hearts and testing kidneys, to give to each one according to his ways and according to the fruits of his practices.

**17:11** A partridge cried out; it gathered what it did not bear. One making his wealth not with justice—in the middle of his days they will forsake him, and at his end he will be foolish.

**17:12** An exalted throne of glory is our sanctuary.

**17:13** Hope of Israel, Lord, let all who forsake You be put to shame; let those who have departed be written on the earth, because they have forsaken the spring of life, the Lord.

**17:14** Heal me, Lord, and I will be healed; save me, and I will be saved, because You are my boast.

**17:15** Look, they say to me, "Where is the word of the Lord? Let it come!"

**17:16** But I did not grow weary following behind You, and the day of man I did not desire, You know; the things going out through my lips are before Your face.

**17:17** Do not become an alienation to me, You who spare me in an evil day.

**17:18** Let those who pursue me be put to shame, and may I not be put to shame; may they be terrified, and may I not be terrified; bring upon them an evil day; with a double shattering, shatter them.

**17:19** Thus says the Lord: "Go and stand in the gates of the sons of your people, through which kings of Judah enter and through which they go out, and in all the gates of Jerusalem,

**17:20** and you will say to them the word of the Lord: 'Kings of Judah and all Judea and all Jerusalem, who enter through these gates,

**17:21** thus says the Lord: Guard your souls, and do not carry burdens on the Sabbath day, and do not go out through the gates of Jerusalem,

**17:22** and do not bring out burdens from your houses on the Sabbath day, and you shall not do any work; sanctify the Sabbath day, just as I commanded your fathers. And they did not listen and did not incline their ear,

**17:23** and they stiffened their neck more than their fathers so as not to listen to Me and not to receive discipline.

**17:24** And it will be, if you heed Me, says the Lord, so as not to bring in burdens through the gates of this city on the Sabbath day, and to sanctify the Sabbath day so as not to do any work,

**17:25** then kings and rulers will enter through the gates of this city, sitting on the throne of David and mounted on their chariots and horses, they and their rulers, men of Judah and those who dwell in Jerusalem; and this city will be inhabited forever;

**17:26** and they will come from the cities of Judah, and from around Jerusalem, and from the land of Benjamin, and from the lowland, and from the mountain, and from the land toward the south, bringing burnt offerings and sacrifice and incenses and manna and frankincense, bringing praise into the house of the Lord.

**17:27** And it will be, if you do not heed Me to sanctify the Sabbath day, not to carry burdens and not to enter through the gates of Jerusalem on the Sabbath day, then I will kindle a fire in its gates, and it will devour the streets of Jerusalem, and it will not be quenched.'"

## Translator Notes

- **17:5** — Verses 17:1-4 are absent from the Old Greek (Septuagint) text of Jeremiah, which begins the chapter here without the Hebrew MT's "Thus says the Lord."
- **17:9** — Greek reads "deep" (batheia) rather than "deceitful," and translates the Hebrew word for "incurable" as anthropos ("a man"), followed by the masculine pronoun "him."
- **17:16** — As in 17:9, Greek renders the Hebrew term for "woeful/incurable" as anthropou ("the day of man"), and reads "I did not grow weary following behind You" rather than the Hebrew MT's shepherd imagery.
- **17:26** — Greek transliterates Hebrew minhah ("grain offering") as manna and includes an additional offering term (thymiamata, "incenses").
- **17:27** — Greek amphoda ("streets" or "city blocks") corresponds to "citadels/palaces" in the Hebrew MT.
