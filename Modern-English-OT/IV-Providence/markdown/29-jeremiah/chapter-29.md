## Text

**29:1** Against the foreigners. Thus says the Lord:

**29:2** "Look, waters are rising from the north, and they will become an overflowing torrent, and it will overflow the land and its fullness, the city and those who dwell in it; and the people will cry out, and all who dwell in the land will wail

**29:3** at the sound of his rushing, at the hooves of his feet, and at the shaking of his chariots, the roar of his wheels. Fathers did not turn back for their sons because of the feebleness of their hands,

**29:4** on the day that is coming to destroy all the foreigners. And I will wipe out Tyre and Sidon and all who remain of their help, for the Lord will utterly destroy those who remain of the islands.

**29:5** Baldness has come upon Gaza; Ashkelon has been cast away, and those who remain of the Anakim."

**29:6** How long will you slash, sword of the Lord? How long will you not be quiet? Return to your sheath; rest and be lifted up.

**29:7** How can it be quiet, when the Lord has commanded it to rise up against Ashkelon and against the coastal regions, against those that remain?

## Translator Notes

- **29:1** — Greek allophyloi ("foreigners") is the Septuagint's standard designation for the Philistines. In 29:4, the Greek speaks directly of wiping out Tyre and Sidon and "those who remain of the islands" (without Hebrew "Caphtor").
- **29:5** — The Greek reads Enakeim ("Anakim") where the Hebrew text has "their valley," and aperiphē ("has been cast away") where the Hebrew has "is silenced/destroyed."
- **29:6** — The Greek joins "How long will you slash" directly to the address to the sword of the Lord, and ends with eparthēti ("be lifted up" or "removed") where the Hebrew reads "be still."
