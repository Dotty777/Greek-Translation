## Text

**24:1** The Lord showed me two baskets of figs set before the temple of the Lord, after Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had exiled Jeconiah son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, and the officials and the craftsmen and the prisoners and the neighbors from Jerusalem, and brought them to Babylon.

**24:2** One basket had very good figs, like the early figs, and the other basket had very bad figs, which cannot be eaten because of their badness.

**24:3** And the Lord said to me, "What do you see, Jeremiah?" And I said, "Figs: the good ones, very good, and the bad ones, very bad, which cannot be eaten because of their badness."

**24:4** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**24:5** "Thus says the Lord God of Israel: Like these good figs, so I will regard the exiled Judeans, whom I have sent away from this place into the land of the Chaldeans, for good.

**24:6** And I will fix My eyes upon them for good, and I will restore them to this land for good; and I will build them up and will by no means tear them down, and I will plant them and will by no means uproot them.

**24:7** And I will give them a heart to know Me, that I am the Lord; and they will be a people to Me, and I will be God to them, because they will turn back to Me with their whole heart;

**24:8** and like the bad figs that cannot be eaten because of their badness—thus says the Lord—so I will hand over Zedekiah king of Judah and his nobles and the remnant of Jerusalem, those who are left behind in this land and those who dwell in Egypt.

**24:9** And I will give them over to scattering among all the kingdoms of the earth, and to reproach and a byword and hatred and a curse in every place where I drove them out;

**24:10** and I will send against them famine and death and the sword, until they vanish from the land that I gave to them."

## Translator Notes

- **24:1** — Greek reads "the prisoners" (where the Hebrew has "the smiths") and "the neighbors" (plesious; some Septuagint manuscripts read plousious, "the rich," a phrase absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text).
- **24:9** — Greek reads "to scattering" (where the Hebrew has "a horror") and uses the past tense "where I drove them out."
- **24:10** — Greek orders the triad as "famine and death and the sword," rendering the Hebrew term for "pestilence" literally as "death" (thanatos).
