## Text

**5:1** And I turned again and lifted up my eyes and saw, and look, a flying sickle.

**5:2** And he said to me, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a flying sickle twenty cubits in length and ten cubits in width."

**5:3** And he said to me, "This is the curse going out over the face of all the earth; for every thief will be punished from this to death, and every perjurer will be punished from this.

**5:4** 'And I will bring it out,' says the Lord Almighty, 'and it will enter into the house of the thief and into the house of the one swearing by My name falsely, and it will lodge in the middle of his house, and it will consume it and its timbers and its stones.'"

**5:5** And the angel speaking in me went out and said to me, "Look up with your eyes and see this thing that is going out."

**5:6** And I said, "What is it?" And he said, "This is the measure that is going out." And he said, "This is their unrighteousness in all the earth."

**5:7** And look, a talent of lead was being lifted up; and look, one woman was sitting in the middle of the measure.

**5:8** And he said, "This is lawlessness." And he threw her into the middle of the measure, and he threw the stone of lead into her mouth.

**5:9** And I lifted up my eyes and saw, and look, two women going out, and wind was in their wings, and they had the wings of a hoopoe; and they lifted up the measure between the earth and heaven.

**5:10** And I said to the angel speaking in me, "Where are they carrying the measure away?"

**5:11** And he said to me, "To build a house for it in the land of Babylon and to make preparation, and they will set it there upon its prepared place."

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — The Greek reads "a flying sickle" (drepanon petomenon) rather than the Masoretic Text's "flying scroll," reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew consonants.
- **5:6** — "The measure" (to metron) renders the Hebrew ephah measuring container; Greek "their unrighteousness" (he adikia auton) reflects a different textual reading from the Masoretic Text's "their eye/appearance."
- **5:8** — The pronoun "her" (autes) is feminine in Greek, grammatically agreeing with the woman (lawlessness) rather than the neuter noun "measure" (metron), though it mirrors the feminine gender of the Hebrew ephah.
- **5:9** — Greek epopos specifically names a hoopoe, whereas the Masoretic Text reads "stork."
- **5:11** — The Greek specifies "the land of Babylon" (ge Babylonos) rather than the Masoretic Text's "land of Shinar."
