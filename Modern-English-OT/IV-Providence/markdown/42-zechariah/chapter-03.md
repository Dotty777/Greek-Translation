## Text

**3:1** And the Lord showed me Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the Lord, and the devil was standing at his right hand to oppose him.

**3:2** And the Lord said to the devil, "May the Lord rebuke you, devil, and may the Lord who chose Jerusalem rebuke you! Look, is this not like a burning stick snatched from the fire?"

**3:3** And Joshua was clothed in filthy garments, and he was standing before the angel.

**3:4** And he answered and spoke to those standing before him, saying, "Remove the filthy garments from him." And he said to him, "Look, I have removed your lawless deeds, and clothe him in a full-length robe,

**3:5** and place a clean turban upon his head." And they placed a clean turban upon his head, and dressed him in garments. And the angel of the Lord was standing by.

**3:6** And the angel of the Lord solemnly testified to Joshua, saying,

**3:7** "Thus says the Lord Almighty: You walk in My ways and will keep watch in My ordinances, and you will judge My house; and if you guard My court, then I will give you those who walk about in the midst of these standing here.

**3:8** Listen now, Joshua the high priest, you and your companions who sit in front, for they are men who observe wonders; for look, I am bringing My servant Rising.

**3:9** For the stone that I set before Joshua—upon the one stone are seven eyes. Look, I am digging a pit, says the Lord Almighty, and I will feel out all the unrighteousness of that land in one day.

**3:10** In that day, says the Lord Almighty, each of you will invite his neighbor under a vine and under a fig tree."

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Greek renders Hebrew hasatan ("the adversary/Satan") as ho diabolos ("the devil," literally "the slanderer/accuser"), and explicitly names "the Lord" as the subject of "showed me."
- **3:4** — Continuing seamlessly into 3:5, the Greek shifts to plural imperatives ("clothe him ... and place") addressed to the attendants, without the first-person interruption ("And I said") present in the Hebrew MT at 3:5.
- **3:7** — Following the Greek text as written (without an initial "if," ean, likely omitted before en in early transmission), the opening clauses are indicative rather than conditional. The Greek also renders the promised gift personally as "those who walk about" (anastrephomenous, attendants) rather than "right of access."
- **3:8** — The Greek renders Hebrew "men of a sign" as "men who observe wonders" (teratoskopoi) and translates the messianic title Tsemach ("Branch/Shoot") as Anatole ("Rising," evoking both a sprouting shoot and the rising sun/dawn, as echoed in Luke 1:78).
- **3:9** — The Greek reads "I am digging a pit" (orysso bothron) and "I will feel out" (pselapheso, literally "grope/touch"), diverging from the Hebrew MT's "I will engrave its inscription" and "I will remove."
