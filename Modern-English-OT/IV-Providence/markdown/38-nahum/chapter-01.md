## Text

**1:1** Pronouncement concerning Nineveh, the book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite.

**1:2** The Lord is a jealous God, taking vengeance with fury; the Lord takes vengeance on His adversaries, and He Himself removes His enemies.

**1:3** The Lord is slow to anger, and great is His strength, and the Lord will not acquit as innocent; His way is in complete destruction and in a tempest, and clouds are the dust of His feet,

**1:4** threatening the sea and drying it up, and making all the rivers desolate. Bashan and Carmel diminished, and the blossoms of Lebanon failed.

**1:5** The mountains were shaken before Him, and the hills reeled; and the earth recoiled from His presence, the entire world, and all who dwell in it.

**1:6** Before His wrath, who will stand firm? And who will withstand in the wrath of His fury? His fury melts dominions, and the rocks were shattered before Him.

**1:7** The Lord is good to those who wait for Him in a day of distress, and knows those who revere Him.

**1:8** And with an advancing flood He will bring about complete destruction; darkness will pursue those who rise up and His enemies.

**1:9** What are you scheming against the Lord? He Himself will bring about complete destruction; He will not take vengeance twice at the same time in distress,

**1:10** for down to its foundation it will be laid waste, and like intertwined bindweed it will be devoured, and like stubble full of dryness.

**1:11** Out of you will come forth a scheme against the Lord, devising evil, contrary things.

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — Greek ἀθῷον οὐκ ἀθῳώσει (literally "as innocent He will not acquit") reproduces the Hebrew emphatic construction ("will by no means acquit"). Likewise, "in complete destruction" translates Greek συντελείᾳ, connecting Hebrew suphah ("whirlwind") with the root suph ("come to an end").
- **1:6** — The Greek reads "melts dominions" (τήκει ἀρχάς), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "is poured out like fire."
- **1:7** — The Greek reads "to those who wait for Him" and "those who revere Him," where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "a stronghold" and "those who take refuge in Him."
- **1:12** — The Greek "who rules over many waters" (κατάρχων ὑδάτων πολλῶν) differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("Though they are at full strength and likewise many") through a different division and vocalization of the Hebrew consonants.
- **1:14** — The closing words "For swift" (ὅτι ταχεῖς) render Hebrew qallota ("you are vile") as a plural adjective linked syntactically to "the feet" at the opening of 2:1. The Greek punctuation also places "cast images" as the object of "I will make... your grave."
