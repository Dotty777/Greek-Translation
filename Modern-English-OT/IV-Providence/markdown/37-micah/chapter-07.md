## Text

**7:1** Woe to me, for I have become like one gathering straw at harvest and like gleanings at the vintage, when there is no cluster to eat the first-grown fruits. Woe to me, soul,

**7:2** for a devout person has perished from the earth, and there is no one among people who acts uprightly. All of them go to law for bloodshed; each one oppresses his neighbor with oppression.

**7:3** They prepare their hands for evil. The ruler asks, and the judge spoke peaceful words; it is the desire of his soul. And I will take away their goods

**7:4** like a moth eating away and walking on a measuring rod on a day of watching. Woe, woe, your punishments have come; now will be their weeping.

**7:5** Do not trust in friends, and do not hope in leaders; guard yourself from her who shares your bed, so as not to confide anything to her,

**7:6** because a son dishonors a father, a daughter will rise up against her mother, a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law; a man's enemies are all those in his house.

**7:7** But I will look to the Lord; I will wait patiently for God my Savior; my God will listen to me.

**7:8** Do not gloat over me, my enemy, because I have fallen, yet I will rise; for if I sit in the darkness, the Lord will give light to me.

**7:9** I will bear the wrath of the Lord because I have sinned against Him, until He vindicates my cause; and He will execute judgment for me, and You will bring me out into the light; I will see His righteousness.

**7:10** And my enemy will see and will be clothed with shame, she who says to me, "Where is the Lord your God?" My eyes will look upon her; now she will be trampled down like mud in the streets.

**7:11** On a day of plastering brick, that day is your blotting out, and that day will rub away your ordinances.

**7:12** And your cities will yield to leveling and to partitioning by the Assyrians, and your fortified cities to partitioning from Tyre to the river, and from sea to sea, and from mountain to the mountain.

**7:13** And the land will become a desolation together with those who dwell in it, because of the fruits of their practices.

**7:14** Shepherd Your people with Your rod, the sheep of Your inheritance, who dwell by themselves in a forest in the midst of Carmel; they will graze in Bashan and Gilead as in the days of old.

**7:15** And according to the days of your departure from Egypt, you will see marvelous things.

**7:16** Nations will see and be ashamed even of all their strength; they will put hands over their mouth; their ears will become deaf.

**7:17** They will lick dust like a serpent, crawling on the ground; they will be thrown into confusion in their confinement. They will be astounded at the Lord our God, and they will be afraid of You.

**7:18** Who is a God like You, removing lawless deeds and passing over acts of ungodliness for the remnant of His inheritance? And He has not retained His wrath as a testimony, because He is one who desires mercy.

**7:19** He will return and have compassion on us; He will submerge our unrighteous deeds, and they will be cast away into the depths of the sea, all our sins.

**7:20** He will give in truth to Jacob, mercy to Abraham, just as You swore to our fathers in the former days.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The Greek renders the harvest imagery as gathering straw (kalamēn) and takes "the first-grown fruits" (ta prōtogona) as the object of "to eat," closing with a second exclamation ("Woe to me, soul,") rather than the Hebrew "my soul desires the first-ripe fig."
- **7:4** — Continuing from v. 3b ("I will take away their goods"), the Greek portrays judgment as a moth eating away and walking on a measuring rod (kanonos) on a day of watching, diverging markedly from the Hebrew ("the best of them is like a brier").
- **7:12** — The Greek text reads eixousin ("will yield," from eikō) rather than hēxousin ("will come"), continuing v. 11's judgment oracle in which the cities are leveled and partitioned by the Assyrians, whereas the Hebrew promises people coming to Zion.
- **7:18** — The Greek reads "as a testimony" (eis martyrion) where the Hebrew has "forever" (lāʿad), reflecting a different vocalization of the same Hebrew consonants.
- **7:20** — The Greek text shifts from third person ("He will give," dōsei) to second person ("You swore," ōmosas) and reads dōsei eis alētheian ("He will give in truth"), whereas other Greek witnesses and the Hebrew read second-person "You will give truth" (dōseis alētheian).
