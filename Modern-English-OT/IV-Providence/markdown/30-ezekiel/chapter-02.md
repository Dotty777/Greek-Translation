## Text

**2:1** This was the vision of the likeness of the glory of the Lord. And I saw, and I fell upon my face, and I heard a voice of One speaking, and He said to me, "Son of man, stand upon your feet, and I will speak to you."

**2:2** And the Spirit came upon me, and took me up and lifted me up and stood me upon my feet; and I heard Him speaking to me,

**2:3** and He said to me, "Son of man, I am sending you out to the house of Israel, those who provoke Me, who have provoked Me, they and their fathers, to this day,

**2:4** and you shall say to them, 'Thus says the Lord,'

**2:5** whether indeed they hear or are terrified, because it is a provoking house, and they will know that you are a prophet in their midst.

**2:6** And you, son of man, do not fear them, nor be dismayed before their face, because they will rage and band together against you all around, and in the midst of scorpions you dwell. Do not fear their words, and do not be dismayed before their face, because it is a provoking house.

**2:7** And you shall speak My words to them, whether indeed they hear or are terrified, for it is a provoking house.

**2:8** And you, son of man, listen to the One speaking to you; do not become provoking like the provoking house. Open your mouth wide and eat what I give to you."

**2:9** And I looked, and look, a hand was stretched out to me, and in it was a scroll of a book.

**2:10** And He unrolled it before me, and in it the front and the back were written on; and there was written lamentation and melody and woe.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — In the Septuagint versification, the opening sentence ("This was the vision... One speaking") begins chapter 2 rather than concluding 1:28 as in the Hebrew text.
- **2:3** — Greek "parapikraino" ("provoke," literally "embitter") renders the Hebrew root for rebellion throughout chapters 2-3.
- **2:5** — Greek reads "are terrified" (also in 2:7), whereas the Hebrew text reads "refuse" or "forbear."
- **2:6** — Greek translates the Hebrew nouns for "briers and thorns" as verbs, reading "they will rage and band together against you all around."
