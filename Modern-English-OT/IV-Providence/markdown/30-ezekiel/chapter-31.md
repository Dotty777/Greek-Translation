## Text

**31:1** And it happened in the eleventh year, in the third month, on the first day of the month, that the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**31:2** "Son of man, say to Pharaoh king of Egypt and to his multitude, 'To whom have you compared yourself in your height?

**31:3** Look, Assyria was a cypress in Lebanon, beautiful in its boughs and tall in stature; its top reached into the midst of clouds.

**31:4** Water nourished it; the abyss exalted it; it led its own rivers around its plants, and sent out its channels to all the trees of the plain.

**31:5** Because of this its stature was exalted above all the trees of the plain, and its branches spread wide from much water.

**31:6** In its boughs all the birds of the sky nested, and underneath its branches all the wild beasts of the plain gave birth; in its shadow lived every multitude of nations.

**31:7** And it became beautiful in its height because of the multitude of its branches, for its roots reached into much water.

**31:8** And such cypresses were in the paradise of God, and the pines were not like its boughs, and firs did not become like its branches; no tree in the paradise of God became like it in its beauty

**31:9** because of the multitude of its branches, and the trees of the paradise of the delight of God envied it.

**31:10** Therefore, thus says the Lord: Because you became great in stature and put your top into the midst of clouds, and I saw when it was exalted,

**31:11** I handed it over into the hands of a ruler of nations, and he carried out its destruction,

**31:12** and pestilent foreigners from the nations utterly destroyed it and cast it down upon the mountains; in all the ravines its branches fell, and its limbs were shattered on every plain of the earth, and all the peoples of the nations came down from their shelter and dashed it to the ground.

**31:13** Upon its ruin all the birds of the sky rested, and upon its limbs were all the wild beasts of the field,

**31:14** so that even all the trees that are in the water might not be exalted in their stature; and they put their top into the midst of clouds, and they did not stand in their height beside them. All those who drink water, all have been given over to death, to the depth of the earth, in the midst of the sons of men, to those going down into the pit.

**31:15** This is what the Lord, the Lord says: On the day it went down to Hades, the abyss mourned for it, and I held back its rivers and restrained an abundance of water, and Lebanon grew dark over it; all the trees of the plain fainted over it.

**31:16** At the sound of its fall the nations shook, when I brought it down to Hades with those going down into the pit, and all the trees of delight and the choice ones of Lebanon, all those drinking water, comforted it in the earth.

**31:17** For they also went down with it to Hades among those wounded by the sword, and its seed, those who live under its shelter, perished in the midst of their life.

**31:18** To whom have you been compared? Go down and be brought down with the trees of delight to the depth of the earth; in the midst of the uncircumcised you will lie down with those wounded by the sword. So it is with Pharaoh and the multitude of his strength, says the Lord, the Lord.'"

## Translator Notes

- **31:3** — Greek kyparissos ("cypress") corresponds to Hebrew "cedar", and "clouds" (nephelōn, also in vv. 10, 14) reflects reading the Hebrew consonants as "clouds" rather than "thick boughs".
- **31:8** — The Greek lacks a negative in the opening clause ("such cypresses were in the paradise of God"), unlike the Hebrew ("cedars could not rival it in the garden of God"). In v. 9 (and vv. 16, 18), "delight" (tryphē) translates Hebrew Eden.
- **31:10** — The Greek shifts from second person to third person and reads "I saw when it was exalted" (eidon en tō hypsōthēnai auton) where the Hebrew text has "its heart was lifted up in its height".
- **31:14** — The middle clauses in Greek shift to indicative verbs ("and they put their top into the midst of clouds, and they did not stand in their height beside them"), differing from the continued negative purpose clause in Hebrew.
- **31:17** — "Hades" (vv. 15-17) is the Septuagint's Greek name for Hebrew Sheol; here the Greek also reads "its seed" (sperma, vs. Hebrew "arm") and "perished in the midst of their life" (vs. Hebrew "in the midst of the nations").
