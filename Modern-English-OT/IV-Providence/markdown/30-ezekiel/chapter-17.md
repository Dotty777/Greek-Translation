## Text

**17:1** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**17:2** "Son of man, recount a tale and speak a parable to the house of Israel,

**17:3** and you shall say, 'Thus says the Lord: The great eagle, great-winged, long in span, full of claws, who has the design to enter Lebanon, took the choice parts of the cedar.

**17:4** He plucked off the tips of the tender growth and brought them to the land of Canaan; in a walled city he placed them.

**17:5** And he took from the seed of the land and put it into the field as a plant beside much water; he set it as something watched over.

**17:6** And it sprouted and became a weak and small vine, so that it showed its branches over it, and its roots were underneath it. And it became a vine and produced offshoots and stretched out its climbing shoots.

**17:7** And there was another great eagle, great-winged, with many claws; and look, this vine was entwined toward him, and its roots were toward him, and it sent out its branches to him to water it together with the clod of its planting.

**17:8** In a good field beside much water it grows lush, to produce shoots and bear fruit, to become a great vine.'

**17:9** Therefore say, 'Thus says the Lord: Will it prosper? Will not the roots of its tender growth and the fruit rot, and all its early sprouts dry up—and not with a great arm nor with many people to pull it out from its roots?

**17:10** And look, it grows lush; will it prosper? Will it not dry up as soon as the scorching wind touches it? Together with the clod of its sprouting it will dry up.'"

**17:11** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**17:12** "Son of man, say now to the provoking house, 'Do you not know what these things were?' Say, 'When the king of Babylon comes against Jerusalem, he will take its king and its rulers and will lead them to himself into Babylon.

**17:13** And he will take from the seed of the kingdom, and will make a covenant with him and bring him under a curse, and he will take the leaders of the land,

**17:14** so that it may become a weak kingdom, not to lift itself up at all, to keep his covenant and make it stand.

**17:15** And he will revolt from him by sending out his messengers to Egypt, to give him horses and many people. Will he prosper? Will the one who does contrary things be delivered? And one who transgresses a covenant, will he be delivered?

**17:16** As I live, says the Lord, surely in the place of the king who made him king, he who dishonored My curse and who transgressed My covenant will die with him in the midst of Babylon.

**17:17** And not with a great force nor with a large multitude will Pharaoh make war against him, with the throwing up of a rampart and with the building of siege-works to wipe out souls.

**17:18** And he dishonored an oath to transgress a covenant, and look, I have given his hand, and he did all these things to him; will he be saved?'

**17:19** Therefore say, 'Thus says the Lord: As I live, surely My oath which he dishonored and My covenant which he transgressed, I will put it upon his head.

**17:20** And I will spread out the net over him, and he will be caught in his enclosure.

**17:21** In all his battle array they will fall by the sword, and the remaining ones I will scatter to every wind; and you will know that I, the Lord, have spoken.

**17:22** For thus says the Lord: I Myself will also take from the choice parts of the cedar, from the top; I will pluck off their hearts, and I Myself will plant on a high mountain,

**17:23** and I will hang it on a lofty mountain of Israel and plant it, and it will put forth a shoot and produce fruit and become a great cedar; and underneath it every bird will rest, and every winged creature will rest under its shadow; its branches will be restored.

**17:24** And all the trees of the field will know that I am the Lord who brings low a high tree and exalts a low tree, and dries up a green tree and makes a dry tree flourish. I, the Lord, have spoken, and I will do it.'"

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — The Greek reads "full of claws" (pleres onychon, cf. v. 7) and "who has the design to enter Lebanon" (hos echei to hegema eiselthein), diverging from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("full of plumage of varied colors came to Lebanon").
- **17:12** — The Septuagint casts the interpretation across vv. 12-15 in the future tense ("When the king of Babylon comes... he will take"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text narrates it in the past tense.
- **17:16** — The Greek contains a nominative anacoluthon (literally "in the place, the king who made him king"), rendered here as "in the place of the king who made him king," and reads "My curse" and "My covenant" rather than referring to the king's oath and covenant as in the Hebrew.
- **17:18** — The Greek critical text reads the first-person singular perfect verb dedoka ("I have given his hand") rather than the third-person singular ("he gave his hand") found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **17:23** — The Greek kremaso auton ("I will hang it," lit. "him") reflects reading Hebrew talul ("lofty") as a form of the verb "to hang," and the closing clause reads "its branches will be restored" rather than describing birds dwelling in its branches.
