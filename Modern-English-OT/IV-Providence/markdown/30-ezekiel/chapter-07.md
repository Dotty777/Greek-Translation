## Text

**7:1** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**7:2** "And you, son of man, say, 'Thus says the Lord to the land of Israel: An end has come; the end has come upon the four wings of the land.

**7:3** The end has come

**7:4** upon you who dwell in the land; the time has come, the day has drawn near, not with tumults nor with birth pains.

**7:5** Now soon I will pour out My wrath upon you, and I will complete My fury against you, and I will judge you in your ways, and I will put all your abominations upon you.

**7:6** My eye will not spare, nor will I show mercy; because I will put your ways upon you, and your abominations will be in your midst, and you will know that I am the Lord who strikes.

**7:7** Now the end is upon you, and I will send against you and execute vengeance in your ways, and I will put all your abominations upon you.

**7:8** My eye will not spare, nor will I show mercy; because I will put your way upon you, and your abominations will be in your midst, and you will know that I am the Lord.

**7:9** For thus says the Lord: Look, the end has come,

**7:10** look, the day of the Lord. Even though the rod has blossomed, arrogance has risen up.

**7:11** And He will shatter the support of a lawless one, and not with tumult nor with haste.

**7:12** The time has come; look, the day. Let the buyer not rejoice, and let the seller not mourn,

**7:13** for the buyer will no longer return to the seller, and a man will not hold fast in the eye of his life.

**7:14** Sound a trumpet and judge all things.

**7:15** War with the sword is outside, and famine and death are inside; the one in the plain will die by the sword, and famine and death will finish off those in the city.

**7:16** And those escaping from them will escape and be on the mountains; I will kill them all, each one in his injustices.

**7:17** All hands will grow limp, and all thighs will be soiled with moisture,

**7:18** and they will gird themselves with sackcloth, and horror will cover them; and on every face there will be shame upon them, and on every head baldness.

**7:19** Their silver will be thrown in the streets, and their gold will be disregarded; their souls will certainly not be satisfied, and their bellies will certainly not be filled, because it became a torment of their injustices.

**7:20** Choice things of adornment, they set them for pride, and they made images of their abominations out of them; because of this I have given them to them for uncleanness.

**7:21** And I will hand them over into the hands of foreigners to plunder them, and to the pestilent ones of the earth for spoil, and they will profane them.

**7:22** And I will turn My face away from them, and they will defile My oversight, and they will enter into them unguardedly and profane them,

**7:23** and they will cause confusion, because the land is full of peoples, and the city is full of lawlessness.

**7:24** And I will turn back the insolence of their strength, and their holy places will be defiled.

**7:25** Propitiation will come and seek peace, and there will be none.

**7:26** Woe upon woe will come, and report upon report will come; and a vision will be sought from a prophet, and law will perish from a priest, and counsel from elders.

**7:27** A ruler will clothe himself with desolation, and the hands of the people of the land will be paralyzed; according to their ways I will do to them, and in their judgments I will execute vengeance on them, and they will know that I am the Lord.'"

## Translator Notes

- **7:4** — The Septuagint arranges 7:3-9 in a different sequence from the Hebrew Masoretic Text and reads "not with tumults nor with birth pains" rather than the Hebrew "tumult, and not joyful shouting of mountains."
- **7:7** — The Greek reads literally "I will send against you" without an expressed object, reflecting a variant reading ("I") where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "My anger."
- **7:13** — The Greek phrase "in the eye of his life" reflects a textual difference from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "in his iniquity."
- **7:23** — The Greek reads "confusion" rather than the Hebrew "chain," and "peoples" rather than the Hebrew "bloody crimes" (compare 9:9).
- **7:25** — The Greek reads "propitiation" (exilasmos) as the subject that comes and seeks peace, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "anguish."
