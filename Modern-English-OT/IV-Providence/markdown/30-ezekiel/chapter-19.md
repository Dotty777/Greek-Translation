## Text

**19:1** And you, take up a lamentation over the ruler of Israel,

**19:2** and you shall say, "What was your mother? A lion cub; in the midst of lions she came to be, in the midst of lions she multiplied her cubs.

**19:3** And one of her cubs leaped forth; he became a lion, and he learned to seize prey; he ate people.

**19:4** And nations heard against him; in their destruction he was captured, and they led him in a muzzle to the land of Egypt.

**19:5** And she saw that he had been driven away from her; her expectation perished; and she took another of her cubs; she appointed him a lion.

**19:6** And he moved about in the midst of lions; he became a lion, and he learned to seize prey; he ate people.

**19:7** And he fed in his boldness and laid waste their cities, and he ruined the land and its fullness at the sound of his roaring.

**19:8** And nations from regions all around set themselves against him, and they spread out their nets over him; in their destruction he was captured;

**19:9** and they put him in a muzzle and in a cage; he came to the king of Babylon, and he brought him into prison, so that his voice might not be heard upon the mountains of Israel.

**19:10** Your mother was like a vine and like a blossom on a pomegranate tree, planted in water; her fruit and her shoot came from much water.

**19:11** And she became a rod over a tribe of leaders, and she was exalted in her stature in the midst of trunks, and she saw her stature in the multitude of her branches.

**19:12** And she was broken down in wrath; to the ground she was cast, and the scorching wind dried up her choice parts; they were punished, and the rod of her strength was dried up; fire consumed her.

**19:13** And now they have planted her in the wilderness, in a waterless land.

**19:14** And fire went out from a rod of her choice parts and devoured her, and there was no rod of strength in her; a tribe is for a parable of lamentation, and it will be for a lamentation."

## Translator Notes

- **19:2** — The Greek calls the mother a skymnos ("lion cub") rather than "lioness" as in the Hebrew, and reads "she came to be" (egenethe) and "she multiplied her cubs."
- **19:4** — Here and in v. 8 the Greek translates Hebrew shachat ("pit") by its other sense diaphthora ("destruction"), and renders "with hooks" as en kemo ("in a muzzle," cf. v. 9).
- **19:7** — The Greek reads kai enemeto to thrasei autou ("and he fed in his boldness"), diverging notably from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("and he knew [or ravaged] their widows [or citadels]").
- **19:10** — The Greek reads "and like a blossom on a pomegranate tree" (kai hos anthos en roa), diverging from the difficult Hebrew reading usually rendered "in your blood" or "in your likeness."
- **19:14** — Reading Hebrew shevet limshol ("a scepter for ruling") as phyle eis parabolen ("a tribe for a parable"), the Greek concludes with "a tribe is for a parable of lamentation, and it will be for a lamentation."
