## Text

**15:1** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**15:2** "And you, son of man, what could become of the wood of the vine out of all the woods that are among the trees of the forest?

**15:3** Will they take wood from it to make for work? Will they take a peg from it to hang any vessel upon it?

**15:4** Apart from that, it has been given to fire for consumption; the fire consumes the yearly pruning from it, and it fails completely. Will it be useful for work?

**15:5** Not even while it is still whole will it be for work? How much less, if fire also consumes it completely, will it still be for work?

**15:6** Therefore say, 'Thus says the Lord: Just as the wood of the vine among the trees of the forest, which I have given to fire for consumption, so I have given the inhabitants of Jerusalem.

**15:7** And I will set My face against them; from the fire they will come out, and fire will devour them, and they will know that I am the Lord, when I set My face against them.

**15:8** And I will give the land over to desolation, because they committed a transgression, says the Lord.'"

## Translator Notes

- **15:4** — Greek parex ("Apart from that") stands where the Hebrew has "behold," and "the yearly pruning from it" (ten kat' eniauton katharsin ap' autes) reflects a different reading of the Hebrew phrase "both its ends."
- **15:5** — Greek me hoti ("how much less") introduces the a fortiori rhetorical question following the negative first clause.
- **15:7** — Greek exeleusontai ("they will come out") is future rather than past, and epignosontai ("they will know") is third person plural rather than second person.
