## Text

**13:1** A shrewd son is obedient to his father, but a disobedient son is in ruin.

**13:2** A good person will eat from the fruits of righteousness, but the souls of the lawless will perish prematurely.

**13:3** The one who guards his own mouth preserves his own soul, but the one who is rash with his lips will terrify himself.

**13:4** Every idle person is in desires, but the hands of the courageous are in diligence.

**13:5** A righteous person hates an unjust word, but an ungodly person is ashamed and will not have boldness.

**13:7** There are those who make themselves rich while having nothing, and there are those who humble themselves in great wealth.

**13:8** A man's own wealth is a ransom for his soul, but a poor person does not endure a threat.

**13:9** There is light for the righteous continually, but the light of the ungodly is extinguished. Deceitful souls go astray in sins, but the righteous have compassion and show mercy.

**13:10** An evil person does evil with arrogance, but those who are judges of themselves are wise.

**13:11** Wealth hastily acquired with lawlessness becomes less, but the one who gathers for himself with godliness will be multiplied; a righteous person has compassion and lends.

**13:12** One who heartily helps those who are beginning is better than the one who promises and leads into hope; for a good desire is a tree of life.

**13:13** The one who despises a matter will be despised by it, but the one who fears a commandment, he is well. For a deceitful son there will be nothing good, but for a wise household servant deeds will be prosperous, and his way will be directed straight.

**13:14** The law of a wise person is a spring of life, but the senseless person will die by a snare.

**13:15** Good understanding gives favor, and to know the law belongs to a good mind, but the ways of those who despise are in ruin.

**13:16** Every shrewd person acts with knowledge, but the fool spreads out his own wickedness.

**13:17** A reckless king will fall into evils, but a wise messenger will rescue him.

**13:18** Discipline removes poverty and dishonor, and the one who guards reproofs will be honored.

**13:19** The desires of the godly sweeten the soul, but the works of the ungodly are far from knowledge.

**13:20** Walking with the wise, you will be wise, but the one who walks with fools will be known.

**13:21** Evils will pursue those who sin, but good things will overtake the righteous.

**13:22** A good man will leave an inheritance to his sons' sons, and the wealth of the ungodly is stored up for the righteous.

**13:23** The righteous will spend many years in wealth, but the unjust will perish swiftly.

**13:24** The one who spares the rod hates his son, but the one who loves him diligently disciplines him.

**13:25** A righteous person eats and fills his soul, but the souls of the ungodly are in want.

## Translator Notes

- **13:5** — Verse 13:6 is absent from the Greek Septuagint text, so the verse numbering moves directly from 13:5 to 13:7.
- **13:9** — The second sentence is an additional couplet preserved in the Greek Septuagint that is not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text (similar expansions appear in 13:11 and 13:13).
- **13:12** — The Greek text differs markedly from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ('Hope deferred makes the heart sick...'), contrasting sincere help for beginners with mere promises that raise hopes.
- **13:17** — The Greek reads 'king' (basileus) in the first clause rather than the Hebrew 'messenger,' contrasting a reckless king with the wise messenger who rescues him.
