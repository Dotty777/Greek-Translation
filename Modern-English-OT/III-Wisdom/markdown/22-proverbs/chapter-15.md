## Text

**15:1** Anger destroys even the prudent, and a submissive answer turns away wrath, but a painful word stirs up anger.

**15:2** The tongue of the wise knows good things, but the mouth of fools will declare evil things.

**15:3** In every place are the eyes of the Lord; they keep watch over both the evil and the good.

**15:4** Healing of the tongue is a tree of life, and the one who guards it will be filled with spirit.

**15:5** A fool mocks a father's discipline, but the one who keeps commands is more shrewd. In abounding righteousness there is great strength, but the ungodly will perish root and all from the earth.

**15:6** In the houses of the righteous there is great strength, but the fruits of the ungodly will perish.

**15:7** The lips of the wise are bound with perception, but the hearts of fools are not secure.

**15:8** The sacrifices of the ungodly are an abomination to the Lord, but the prayers of those who act uprightly are acceptable to Him.

**15:9** The ways of an ungodly person are an abomination to the Lord, but He loves those who pursue righteousness.

**15:10** The discipline of an innocent person is recognized by those who pass by, but those who hate reproofs die shamefully.

**15:11** Hades and destruction are manifest before the Lord; how could the hearts of people not be as well?

**15:12** An undisciplined person will not love those who reprove him, and he will not associate with the wise.

**15:13** When a heart is glad, the face blooms, but when it is in sorrows, it looks gloomy.

**15:14** An upright heart seeks perception, but the mouth of the undisciplined will know evil things.

**15:15** All the time the eyes of the evil expect evil things, but the good will be at rest continually.

**15:16** Better is a small portion with the fear of the Lord than great treasures with a lack of fear.

**15:17** Better is hospitality with vegetables in friendship and favor than a serving of calves with enmity.

**15:18** A hot-tempered man prepares quarrels, but one who is slow to anger calms even an impending one; a man slow to anger will extinguish disputes, but the ungodly person stirs them up all the more.

**15:19** The ways of the idle are strewn with thorns, but those of the courageous are worn smooth.

**15:20** A wise son gladdens a father, but a foolish son mocks his mother.

**15:21** The paths of a senseless person are lacking in understanding, but a prudent man walks straight.

**15:22** Those who do not honor councils postpone plans, but in the hearts of those who take counsel, counsel remains.

**15:23** The evil person will by no means obey it, nor will he say anything timely and good for the community.

**15:24** The thoughts of a discerning person are ways of life, so that, turning aside from Hades, he may be saved.

**15:25** The Lord tears down the houses of the arrogant, but He has established the boundary of a widow.

**15:26** An unrighteous thought is an abomination to the Lord, but the sayings of the pure are honorable.

**15:27** The bribe-taker destroys himself, but the one who hates taking bribes is saved. By acts of mercy and faithfulness sins are cleansed away, and by the fear of the Lord everyone turns aside from evil.

**15:28** The hearts of the righteous meditate on faithfulness, but the mouth of the ungodly answers evil things. The ways of righteous people are acceptable to the Lord, and through them even enemies become friends.

**15:29** God is far away from the ungodly, but He listens to the prayers of the righteous. Better is a small gain with righteousness than much produce with unrighteousness. And let a man's heart consider righteous things, so that his steps may be set straight by God.

**15:30** An eye that sees good things gladdens a heart, and a good report fattens the bones.

**15:32** Whoever rejects discipline hates himself, but the one who keeps reproofs loves his soul.

**15:33** The fear of God is discipline and wisdom, and the beginning of glory will answer it.

## Translator Notes

- **15:1** — The Greek opens with an additional clause ("Anger destroys even the prudent") absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, and uses the participle hypopiptousa, literally "yielding" or "submissive," for the answer that turns away wrath.
- **15:5** — πανουργότερος ('more shrewd') is used here in a positive sense. The second half of this verse and verse 6 give two parallel sayings on the strength of the righteous and the ruin of the ungodly.
- **15:23** — The Greek continues verse 22 directly, referring back to "counsel" with "it" and stating that the evil person will say nothing timely and good for the public community (to koino).
- **15:27** — In the Septuagint, verses 27-29 incorporate sayings corresponding to Masoretic 16:6-9, which accounts for the shorter verse sequence at the opening of chapter 16 in the Greek text.
- **15:33** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("before honor is humility"), the Greek reads "the beginning of glory will answer it," referring back to wisdom or discipline.
