## Text

**30:1** Fear my words, son, and having received them, repent. These things the man says to those who believe in God, and I cease.

**30:2** For I am the most foolish of all people, and the understanding of people is not in me.

**30:3** God has taught me wisdom, and I have known the knowledge of the holy ones.

**30:4** Who has ascended into heaven and descended? Who has gathered winds in his bosom? Who has wrapped up water in a garment? Who has taken hold of the ends of the earth? What is his name, or what is the name of his children?

**30:5** For all the words of God are refined by fire, and He Himself shields those who revere Him.

**30:6** Do not add to His words, so that He may not reprove you and you may not become a liar.

**30:7** Two things I ask from You; do not take away grace from me before I die:

**30:8** Put a vain and false word far from me, and do not give me wealth or poverty, but appoint for me what is necessary and what is sufficient,

**30:9** so that, being filled, I may not become a liar and say, "Who sees me?" or, becoming poor, steal and swear by the name of God.

**30:10** Do not hand over a servant into the hands of a master, or he may curse you and you will be destroyed.

**30:11** An evil offspring curses its father, and does not bless its mother.

**30:12** An evil offspring judges itself righteous, yet has not washed away its discharge.

**30:13** An evil offspring has lofty eyes, and lifts itself up with its eyelids.

**30:14** An evil offspring has swords for its teeth and knives for its molars, so as to consume and devour the humble from the earth and their needy from among people.

**30:15** The leech had three daughters loved with love, and these three did not satisfy her. And the fourth was not content to say, "Enough":

**30:16** Hades, and the passion of a woman, and Tartarus, and earth not satisfied with water, and water, and fire will never say, "It is enough."

**30:17** An eye that mocks a father and dishonors the old age of a mother, may ravens from the ravines peck it out, and may the young of eagles devour it.

**30:18** Now there are three things impossible for me to understand, and the fourth I do not comprehend:

**30:19** the tracks of a flying eagle, and the ways of a serpent on a rock, and the paths of a ship sailing the sea, and the ways of a man in youth.

**30:20** Such is the way of an adulterous woman, who, when she acts, washes herself and says she has done nothing wrong.

**30:21** Because of three things the earth is shaken, and the fourth it cannot bear:

**30:22** if a servant becomes king, and a fool is filled with food,

**30:23** and if a female servant drives out her own mistress, and if a hated woman obtains a good husband.

**30:24** Now there are four very small things on the earth, yet these are wiser than the wise:

**30:25** the ants, which have no strength, yet prepare their food in summer;

**30:26** and the rock badgers, a people not strong, who make their own homes in the rocks;

**30:27** the locust is without a king, yet marches out in good order at a single command;

**30:28** and a gecko, supporting itself with its hands and easily caught, dwells in the strongholds of kings.

**30:29** Now there are three things that walk steadily, and a fourth that strides well:

**30:30** a lion's cub, stronger than beasts, which does not turn back nor cower before any beast;

**30:31** and a high-spirited rooster walking about among hens, and a male goat leading a herd of goats; and a king speaking publicly in a nation.

**30:32** If you give yourself over in gladness, and stretch out your hand with quarreling, you will be dishonored.

**30:33** Draw milk, and there will be butter; and if you squeeze nostrils, blood will come out; and if you draw out words, judgments and quarrels will come out.

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — The Septuagint translates the Hebrew heading's proper names (Agur, Jakeh, Ithiel, and Ucal) as common words and verbs, producing an exhortation to a son and the closing words "and I cease."
- **30:3** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("I have not learned wisdom"), the Greek states affirmatively, "God has taught me wisdom."
- **30:12** — Greek exodos (literally "going out, issue, discharge") renders Hebrew tso'ah ("filth/excrement") via an etymological or euphemistic link to "going out."
- **30:16** — The Septuagint expands the list with Tartarus (the Greek name for the underworld abyss) alongside Hades, and includes both "water" and "fire."
- **30:19** — Greek "in youth" (en neoteti) reflects reading the Hebrew consonantal text as "in youth" rather than "with a young woman."
