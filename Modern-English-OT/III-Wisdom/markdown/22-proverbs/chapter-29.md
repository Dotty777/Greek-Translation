# Proverbs 29 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**29:1** Better is a man who reproves than a stiff-necked man, for when he is suddenly burned, there is no healing.

**29:2** When the righteous are praised, peoples will rejoice, but when the ungodly rule, men groan.

**29:3** The father of a man who loves wisdom rejoices, but the one who shepherds prostitutes will lose wealth.

**29:4** A righteous king raises up a land, but a lawless man tears it down.

**29:5** Whoever prepares a net before the face of his own friend casts it around his own feet.

**29:6** For a man who sins there is a great snare, but a righteous man will be in joy and in gladness.

**29:7** A righteous man knows how to judge for the needy, but the ungodly man does not understand knowledge, and a poor man does not have a discerning mind.

**29:8** Lawless men set a city ablaze, but the wise turn away wrath.

**29:9** A wise man judges nations, but a worthless man, when he becomes angry, is laughed at and does not cower.

**29:10** Men who share in bloodshed hate a devout man, but the upright will seek out his soul.

**29:11** A fool brings out all his anger, but a wise man dispenses it bit by bit.

**29:12** When a king listens to an unjust word, all those under him are lawless.

**29:13** When a lender and a debtor meet one another, the Lord exercises oversight over both.

**29:14** When a king judges the poor in truth, his throne will be established for a testimony.

**29:15** Blows and reproofs give wisdom, but a child who goes astray puts his parents to shame.

**29:16** When the ungodly are many, sins become many, but when those people fall, the righteous become greatly afraid.

**29:17** Discipline your son, and he will give you rest, and he will give adornment to your soul.

**29:18** There will by no means be an interpreter for a lawless nation, but the one who keeps the law is blessed.

**29:19** A stubborn servant will not be disciplined by words; for even if he understands, yet he will not obey.

**29:20** If you see a man hasty in words, know that a fool has more hope than he does.

**29:21** Whoever lives in luxury from childhood will be a servant, and in the end he will grieve over himself.

**29:22** A hot-tempered man stirs up strife, and an angry man digs up sins.

**29:23** Arrogance humbles a man, but the Lord supports the humble-minded with glory.

**29:24** Whoever shares with a thief hates his own soul; and if, when an oath is put forth, they hear it and do not report it,

**29:25** fearing and feeling shame before people, they are tripped up; but the one who trusts in the Lord will rejoice. Ungodliness brings a fall to a man, but whoever trusts in the Master will be saved.

**29:26** Many court the favor of rulers, but justice for a man comes from the Lord.

**29:27** A righteous man is an abomination to an unjust man, and a way that goes straight is an abomination to a lawless man.

## Translator Notes

- **29:1** — Greek frames this as a comparison ("Better is a man who reproves than a stiff-necked man") and uses the verb meaning "burned" or "inflamed," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "broken."
- **29:7** — The third clause ("and a poor man does not have a discerning mind") is distinctive to the Septuagint, likely arising from a double rendering in which Hebrew rasha' ("wicked") was read as rash ("poor").
- **29:14** — Greek "for a testimony" (eis martyrion) reflects reading the Hebrew consonantal text as "for a witness/testimony" rather than "forever."
- **29:16** — Greek "become greatly afraid" reflects reading the Hebrew verb as "fear" rather than "see."
- **29:25** — The opening clause completes the conditional sentence begun in 29:24, and the Septuagint preserves a double rendering of the verse using both Kyrios ("the Lord") and despotes ("the Master").
