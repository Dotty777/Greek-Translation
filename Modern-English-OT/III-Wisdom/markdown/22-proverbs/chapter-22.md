## Text

**22:1** A good name is preferable to great wealth, and good favor is above silver and gold.

**22:2** A rich person and a poor person meet one another, and the Lord made them both.

**22:3** When a shrewd person sees a wicked person severely punished, he himself is disciplined, but the foolish pass by and are punished.

**22:4** The offspring of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and wealth, and glory, and life.

**22:5** Thistles and snares are in crooked ways, but he who guards his own soul will keep away from them.

**22:7** The rich will rule over the poor, and household servants will lend to their own masters.

**22:8** He who sows worthless things will reap evil things, and he will bring to an end the blow of his works. God blesses a cheerful man and a giver, and He will bring to an end the futility of his works.

**22:9** He who shows mercy to a poor person will himself be nourished, for he gave of his own loaves to the poor person. He who gives gifts acquires victory and honor, yet he takes away the soul of those who possess them.

**22:10** Cast out a pest from a council, and strife will go out with him; for whenever he sits in a council, he dishonors everyone.

**22:11** The Lord loves devout hearts, and all blameless people are acceptable to Him; a king shepherds with his lips.

**22:12** And the eyes of the Lord preserve perception, but a transgressor despises words.

**22:13** A sluggard makes excuses and says, "There is a lion in the roads, and murderers are in the squares."

**22:14** The mouth of a transgressor is a deep pit, and he who is hated by the Lord will fall into it. There are evil ways before a man, and he does not love to turn away from them; yet one must turn away from a crooked and evil way.

**22:15** Folly is bound to the heart of a youth, but a rod and discipline are far from him.

**22:16** He who defrauds a needy person makes his own possessions many, yet he gives to a rich person for less.

**22:17** Turn your ear to the words of the wise and hear my word, and apply your heart, so that you may know that they are good.

**22:18** And if you place them into your heart, they will gladden you together upon your lips,

**22:19** so that your hope may be in the Lord, and He may make your way known to you.

**22:20** And you also, write them down for yourself threefold for counsel and knowledge upon the breadth of your soul.

**22:21** Therefore I teach you a true word and good knowledge to heed, so as to answer words of truth to those who put questions to you.

**22:22** Do not oppress a needy person, for he is poor, and do not dishonor a weak person at the gates.

**22:23** For the Lord will judge his case, and you will rescue your inviolable soul.

**22:24** Do not be a companion to a wrathful man, and do not lodge with a quick-tempered friend,

**22:25** so that you do not learn of his ways and take nooses for your soul.

**22:26** Do not give yourself as a pledge, feeling shame before someone's face.

**22:27** For if he does not have the means to repay, they will take the bedding that is under your sides.

**22:28** Do not remove eternal boundaries that your fathers set.

**22:29** A man who is observant and sharp in his works must stand before kings, and not stand before sluggish men.

## Translator Notes

- **22:7** — The Greek active verb daniousin literally means "will lend," reversing the sense of the Masoretic Text ("the borrower is the slave of the lender").
- **22:8** — The second sentence ("God blesses a cheerful man and a giver...") is a Septuagint addition absent from the Masoretic Text and echoed in 2 Corinthians 9:7.
- **22:15** — Greek paidia is an ancient spelling of paideia ("discipline"); unlike the Masoretic Text ("drives it far from him"), the Septuagint states that a rod and discipline "are far from him."
- **22:23** — Greek rhuse sen asulon psuchen ("you will rescue your inviolable soul") diverges sharply from the Masoretic Text ("will despoil of life those who despoil them").
- **22:29** — The Septuagint renders the opening Hebrew verb ("Do you see?") as the adjective horatikon ("observant") and reads nothrois ("sluggish") instead of the Masoretic Text's "obscure."
