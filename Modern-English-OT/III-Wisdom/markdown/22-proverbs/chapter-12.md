# Proverbs 12 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**12:1** The one who loves discipline loves discernment, but the one who hates reproofs is a fool.

**12:2** Better is the one who has found favor with the Lord, but a lawless man will be passed over in silence.

**12:3** A person will not prosper from what is lawless, but the roots of the righteous will not be uprooted.

**12:4** A valiant wife is a crown to her husband; but like a worm in wood, so a wife who does evil destroys her husband.

**12:5** The thoughts of the righteous are judgments, but the ungodly direct deceits.

**12:6** The words of the ungodly are deceitful, but the mouth of the upright will rescue them.

**12:7** Wherever an ungodly person turns, he vanishes, but the houses of the righteous remain.

**12:8** The mouth of an understanding person is praised by a man, but a sluggish-hearted person is derided.

**12:9** Better is a man in dishonor serving himself than one who puts honor on himself and lacks bread.

**12:10** A righteous person has compassion on the lives of his livestock, but the affections of the ungodly are merciless.

**12:11** The one who works his own land will be filled with bread, but those who pursue vain things are lacking sense. Whoever delights in lingering over wine will leave behind dishonor in his own strongholds.

**12:12** The desires of the ungodly are evil, but the roots of the godly are in strongholds.

**12:13** Because of the sin of the lips a sinner falls into snares, but a righteous person escapes from them. The one who looks gently will be shown mercy, but the one who confronts others in the gates will crush souls.

**12:14** From the fruits of the mouth a man's soul will be filled with good things, and the repayment of his lips will be given to him.

**12:15** The ways of fools are right before them, but a wise person listens to counsel.

**12:16** A fool declares his anger on the very same day, but a shrewd person conceals his own dishonor.

**12:17** A righteous person declares demonstrated faithfulness, but the witness of the unrighteous is deceitful.

**12:18** There are those who, as they speak, wound like swords, but the tongues of the wise heal.

**12:19** Truthful lips establish testimony, but a hasty witness has an unrighteous tongue.

**12:20** Deceit is in the heart of one who devises evil things, but those who desire peace will rejoice.

**12:21** Nothing unrighteous will please the righteous person, but the ungodly will be filled with evil things.

**12:22** Lying lips are an abomination to the Lord, but the one who does faithful deeds is acceptable with Him.

**12:23** An understanding man is a throne of discernment, but the heart of fools will meet with curses.

**12:24** The hand of the chosen will rule easily, but the deceitful will be subject to plunder.

**12:25** A fearful word troubles the heart of a righteous man, but a good message gladdens him.

**12:26** A fair-minded righteous person will be a friend to himself, but evil things will pursue those who sin, and the way of the ungodly will lead them astray.

**12:27** A deceitful person will not catch game, but a pure man is a precious possession.

**12:28** In the ways of righteousness is life, but the ways of the resentful lead to death.

## Translator Notes

- **12:11** — The second couplet is a Septuagint addition absent from the Hebrew MT; 'delights in lingering over wine' literally reads 'is pleasant in wine-pastimes' (ἡδὺς ἐν οἴνων διατριβαῖς).
- **12:13** — The second couplet is a Septuagint addition absent from the Hebrew MT; 'looks gently' literally translates the Greek idiom βλέπων λεῖα ('looking smooth things').
- **12:18** — The Greek text prints the nominative plural μάχαιραι ('swords'), taken metaphorically in apposition to the speakers ('like swords'); some Greek manuscripts read the dative singular μαχαίρᾳ ('with a sword').
- **12:23** — The Greek reads 'throne of discernment' (θρόνος αἰσθήσεως), reflecting a Hebrew consonantal reading for 'throne' rather than the MT's 'conceals.'
- **12:26** — The rare Greek word ἐπιγνώμων can function either as an adjective ('fair-minded / discerning') modifying 'righteous person' or as a noun ('a righteous arbiter').
