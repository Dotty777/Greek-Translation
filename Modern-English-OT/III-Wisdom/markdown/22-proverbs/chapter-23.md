## Text

**23:1** If you sit down to dine at the table of rulers, consider intelligently the things set before you,

**23:2** and put out your hand, knowing that you must prepare such things. But if you are rather insatiable,

**23:3** do not desire his delicacies, for these belong to a false life.

**23:4** Do not measure yourself against a rich man when you are poor, but by your own insight hold back.

**23:5** If you fix your eye upon him, it will fall nowhere; for wings have been prepared for him like those of an eagle, and he returns to the house of the one set over him.

**23:6** Do not dine with a grudging man, nor desire his foods;

**23:7** for just as if someone were to swallow a hair, so he eats and drinks. And do not bring him in to yourself and eat your morsel with him;

**23:8** for he will vomit it up and ruin your fine words.

**23:9** Say nothing into the ears of a fool, so that he may never sneer at your discerning words.

**23:10** Do not move eternal boundaries, and do not enter into the property of orphans;

**23:11** for the One who redeems them is the Lord; He is mighty, and He will judge their case with you.

**23:12** Give your heart to discipline, and prepare your ears for words of perception.

**23:13** Do not hold back from disciplining a young child, because if you strike him with a rod, he will by no means die;

**23:14** for you will strike him with a rod, yet you will rescue his soul from death.

**23:15** Son, if your heart becomes wise, you will gladden my heart also,

**23:16** and your lips will dwell in words with my lips, if they are upright.

**23:17** Do not let your heart envy sinners, but be in the fear of the Lord all day long.

**23:18** For if you keep them, you will have offspring, and your hope will not depart.

**23:19** Listen, son, and become wise, and direct the thoughts of your heart.

**23:20** Do not be a wine-drinker, nor reach out for shared feasts, purchases of meat;

**23:21** for every drunkard and fornicator will become poor, and every drowsy person will clothe himself in torn and ragged clothing.

**23:22** Listen, son, to the father who fathered you, and do not show contempt because your mother has grown old.

**23:24** A righteous father nurtures well, and over a wise son his soul is glad.

**23:25** Let your father and mother be glad over you, and let her who gave birth to you rejoice.

**23:26** Give me your heart, son, and let your eyes keep my ways.

**23:27** For a foreign house is a perforated jar and a narrow foreign well.

**23:28** For this one will swiftly perish, and every lawless person will be consumed.

**23:29** Who has woe? Who has uproar? Who has judgment? And who has unpleasantness and chatter? Who has bruises for nothing? Whose eyes are black-and-blue?

**23:30** Is it not those who linger over wines? Is it not those who track down where drinking-parties take place? Do not get drunk on wines, but converse with righteous people, and converse on walks;

**23:31** for if you give your eyes to the bowls and the cups, afterward you will walk around more naked than a pestle;

**23:32** but in the end, like one struck by a serpent, he is stretched out, and as by a horned viper, the venom spreads through him.

**23:33** When your eyes see a foreign woman, then your mouth will speak crooked things,

**23:34** and you will lie down as in the heart of the sea, and like a helmsman in a great surge.

**23:35** And you will say, "They are striking me, and I felt no pain, and they mocked me, yet I did not know it; when will it be dawn, so that I may go and seek those with whom I will meet?"

## Translator Notes

- **23:5** — The Greek reads οὐδαμοῦ πεσεῖται ("it will fall nowhere," where some manuscripts read φανεῖται, "will appear") and refers to τοῦ προεστηκότος αὐτοῦ ("the one set over him"), which may denote a patron or master, or metaphorically God.
- **23:22** — Verse 23:23 of the Hebrew text is not present in the Greek Septuagint tradition, so numbering proceeds directly from 23:22 to 23:24.
- **23:27** — The Greek uses the classical image of a "perforated jar" (πίθος τετρημένος) and speaks of a "foreign house" (ἀλλότριος οἶκος) and "narrow foreign well" (φρέαρ στενὸν ἀλλότριον), differing markedly from the Hebrew.
- **23:31** — The expression "more naked than a pestle" (γυμνότερος ὑπέρου) is an ancient Greek proverb describing total destitution.
