# Proverbs 16 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**16:2** All the works of the humble are manifest before God, but the ungodly will perish on an evil day.

**16:5** Everyone who is proud in heart is unclean before God, and the one who places hands in a hand unjustly will not be acquitted.

**16:7** The beginning of a good way is doing righteous things, and they are more acceptable to God than offering sacrifices.

**16:8** The one who seeks the Lord will find knowledge with righteousness, and those who seek Him rightly will find peace.

**16:9** All the works of the Lord are with righteousness, and the ungodly person is kept for an evil day.

**16:10** An oracle is on the lips of a king, and in judgment his mouth will by no means err.

**16:11** The tipping of a scale is righteousness before the Lord, and His works are just weights.

**16:12** The one who does evil things is an abomination to a king, for with righteousness a throne of rule is prepared.

**16:13** Righteous lips are acceptable to a king, and he loves upright words.

**16:14** The wrath of a king is a messenger of death, but a wise man will appease it.

**16:15** In the light of life is a king's son, and those acceptable to him are like a late cloud.

**16:16** Nests of wisdom are more desirable than gold, and nests of prudence are more desirable than silver.

**16:17** The paths of life turn aside from evils, and the ways of righteousness are length of life. The one who receives discipline will be among good things, and the one who keeps reproofs will become wise. Whoever keeps his own ways guards his own soul, and one who loves his life will spare his mouth.

**16:18** Before ruin arrogance leads the way, and before a fall, evil-mindedness.

**16:19** Better is a gentle-spirited person with humility than one who divides spoils with the arrogant.

**16:20** One who is discerning in affairs is a finder of good things, and one who trusts in God is blessed.

**16:21** They call the wise and discerning worthless, but those who are sweet in speech will hear more.

**16:22** Insight is a fountain of life to those who possess it, but the discipline of fools is evil.

**16:23** The heart of a wise person will understand the things from his own mouth, and on his lips it will bear good judgment.

**16:24** Good words are combs of honey, and its sweetness is healing of the soul.

**16:25** There are ways that seem to be right to a man, yet their end looks to the bottom of Hades.

**16:26** A man in labors toils for himself and forces away his own destruction; yet the crooked man bears destruction on his own mouth.

**16:27** A foolish man digs up evil things for himself, and on his own lips he stores up fire.

**16:28** A crooked man sends out evil things, and he will kindle a torch of deceit for evil people, and he separates friends.

**16:29** A lawless man tests friends, and leads them away along ways that are not good.

**16:30** And fixing his eyes, he devises perverse things, and with his lips he determines all evil things; this one is a furnace of wickedness.

**16:31** Old age is a crown of boasting, and it is found in the ways of righteousness.

**16:32** Better is a man who is slow to anger than a strong man, and the one who masters anger is better than one who captures a city.

**16:33** All things come into the laps of the unrighteous, but from the Lord are all righteous things.

## Translator Notes

- **16:15** — The Greek reads "a king's son" (huios basileos) rather than the Masoretic "a king's face," and "a late cloud" (nephos opsimon) refers to the clouds bringing the welcome late spring rains.
- **16:16** — The Greek literally reads nossiai ("nests"), reflecting an Old Greek rendering distinct from the Masoretic "getting" or "acquiring."
- **16:21** — Rather than stating that the wise of heart will be called discerning, the Greek states that people call the wise and discerning "worthless" (phaulous), contrasting them with those sweet in speech who "will hear more."
- **16:26** — The verb ekbiazetai ("forces away") sets up a direct contrast with the following clause ("yet"), opposing the hardworking man who staves off ruin to the crooked man who bears destruction on his own mouth.
- **16:33** — Unlike the Masoretic Text's reference to casting the lot into the lap, the Greek makes no mention of lots, contrasting all things that come into the laps of the unrighteous with all righteous things coming from the Lord.
