# Proverbs 17 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**17:1** Better is a morsel with pleasure in peace than a house of many good things and unjust sacrifices with strife.

**17:2** A discerning household servant will rule over foolish masters, and among brothers he will divide portions.

**17:3** Just as silver and gold are tested in a furnace, so hearts are chosen in the sight of the Lord.

**17:4** An evil person listens to the tongue of the lawless, but a righteous person does not pay attention to false lips.

**17:5** The one who mocks a poor person provokes the One who made him, and the one who rejoices over someone perishing will not go unpunished; but the one who has compassion will be shown mercy.

**17:6** Children's children are a crown of old men, and their fathers are the boast of children. To the faithful person belongs the whole world of wealth, but to the faithless not even an obol.

**17:7** Faithful lips will not suit a fool, nor false lips a righteous person.

**17:8** Discipline is a reward of favors to those who use it, and wherever it turns, it will prosper.

**17:9** The one who conceals wrongdoings seeks friendship, but the one who hates concealing separates friends and relatives.

**17:10** A threat crushes the heart of a prudent person, but a fool, though flogged, does not feel it.

**17:11** Every evil person stirs up disputes, but the Lord will send out a merciless messenger to him.

**17:12** Anxiety will fall upon a discerning man, but fools will ponder evil.

**17:13** The one who repays evil for good—evil will not be removed from his house.

**17:14** The beginning of righteousness gives authority to words, but dissension and strife precede poverty.

**17:15** The one who judges the unrighteous to be righteous, and the righteous to be unrighteous, is unclean and detestable in the sight of God.

**17:16** Why does wealth belong to a fool? For one without sense will not be able to acquire wisdom. The one who makes his own house high seeks ruin, and the one who turns crookedly from learning will fall into evil.

**17:17** Let a friend be yours at all times, and let brothers be useful in hardships, for they are born for this reason.

**17:18** A foolish man claps and rejoices over himself, just as the one who pledges a guarantee for his own friends also does.

**17:19** A lover of sin rejoices in quarrels,

**17:20** and the hard-hearted person does not meet with good things. A man changeable in tongue will fall into evil,

**17:21** and the heart of a fool is grief to the one who possesses it. A father does not rejoice over an undisciplined son, but a prudent son gladdens his mother.

**17:22** A joyful heart produces good health, but the bones of a sorrowful man dry up.

**17:23** The ways of one who unjustly receives gifts in his bosom do not prosper, and an ungodly person turns aside the ways of righteousness.

**17:24** The face of a wise man is perceptive, but the eyes of the fool are on the ends of the earth.

**17:25** A foolish son is anger to a father, and grief to her who bore him.

**17:26** To penalize a righteous man is not good, nor is it godly to plot against righteous rulers.

**17:27** The one who refrains from uttering a harsh word is judicious, and a prudent man is patient.

**17:28** To one without understanding who asks for wisdom, wisdom will be credited, and someone who makes himself mute will seem to be prudent.

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — The Greek reads "hearts are chosen in the sight of the Lord" (ἐκλεκταὶ καρδίαι παρὰ Κυρίῳ), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the Lord tests hearts."
- **17:6** — The second sentence is unique to the Septuagint; an "obol" (ὀβολός) was a small Greek coin of minimal value.
- **17:8** — The Greek reads "discipline" (παιδεία) where the Hebrew has "a bribe"; the singular verbs in the second clause may refer either to discipline itself or to the one who uses it.
- **17:12** — The Greek differs sharply from the Hebrew ("Let a bear robbed of her cubs meet a man..."), reading "Anxiety will fall upon a discerning man."
- **17:19** — In the Septuagint, material parallel to Hebrew 17:19b appears at the end of 17:16, causing the verse boundaries across 17:19–21 to divide sentences mid-clause.
