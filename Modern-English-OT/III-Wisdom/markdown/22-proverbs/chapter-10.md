# Proverbs 10 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**10:1** A wise son gladdens a father, but a foolish son is grief to his mother.

**10:2** Treasures will not profit the lawless, but righteousness will deliver from death.

**10:3** The Lord will not starve a righteous soul, but He will overturn the life of the ungodly.

**10:4** Poverty humbles a man, but the hands of courageous men make rich. A disciplined son will be wise, and he will use the foolish one as a servant.

**10:5** A sensible son is saved from scorching heat, but a lawless son becomes wind-blighted in the harvest.

**10:6** The blessing of the Lord is upon the head of a righteous person, but untimely mourning will cover the mouth of the ungodly.

**10:7** The memory of the righteous is with praises, but the name of an ungodly person is extinguished.

**10:8** One who is wise in heart will receive commandments, but the one who is unrestrained with his lips, acting crookedly, will be tripped up.

**10:9** Whoever walks straightforwardly walks confidently, but the one who perverts his ways will be known.

**10:10** The one who winks his eyes with deceit gathers griefs for men, but the one who reproves with openness makes peace.

**10:11** A spring of life is in the hand of a righteous person, but destruction will cover the mouth of an ungodly person.

**10:12** Hatred stirs up strife, but friendship covers all those who do not love strife;

**10:13** with a rod it strikes a senseless man.

**10:14** The wise will conceal discernment, but the mouth of a rash person draws near to ruin.

**10:15** The property of the rich is a fortified city, but the ruin of the ungodly is poverty.

**10:16** The works of the righteous produce life, but the fruits of the ungodly, sins.

**10:17** Discipline guards righteous ways of life, but discipline without reproof goes astray.

**10:18** Righteous lips cover hostility, but those who utter insults are most foolish.

**10:19** From much talking you will not escape sin, but by sparing your lips you will be sensible.

**10:20** The tongue of a righteous person is silver refined by fire, but the heart of an ungodly person will fail.

**10:21** The lips of the righteous know lofty things, but the foolish die in want.

**10:22** The blessing of the Lord is upon the head of a righteous person; it makes rich, and grief in the heart will by no means be added to it.

**10:23** In laughter a foolish person practices evil, but wisdom gives birth to prudence for a man.

**10:24** In destruction an ungodly person is carried about, but the desire of a righteous person is acceptable.

**10:25** When a storm passes by, an ungodly person vanishes, but a righteous person, having turned aside, is saved forever.

**10:26** Just as an unripe grape is harmful to teeth and smoke to eyes, so is lawlessness to those who practice it.

**10:27** The fear of the Lord adds days, but the years of the ungodly will be diminished.

**10:28** Gladness remains long with the righteous, but the hope of the ungodly will perish.

**10:29** The fear of the Lord is a stronghold of a devout person, but ruin to those who work evil.

**10:30** A righteous person will never give way, but the ungodly will not inhabit the earth.

**10:31** The mouth of a righteous person distills wisdom, but the tongue of an unrighteous person will perish completely.

**10:32** The lips of righteous men distill graces, but the mouth of the ungodly turns away.

## Translator Notes

- **10:4** — The second sentence ("A disciplined son will be wise...") is a Septuagint addition not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **10:10** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text repeats the warning of 10:8b, the Greek reads "the one who reproves with openness makes peace" (μετὰ παρρησίας εἰρηνοποιεῖ).
- **10:13** — The Greek text here contains only this clause continuing from 10:12 (other witnesses and the Hebrew include an opening line about wisdom on the lips); "senseless" literally renders ἀκάρδιον ("without heart").
- **10:18** — The Greek reads "Righteous lips cover hostility" (χείλη δίκαια), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of lying lips concealing hatred.
