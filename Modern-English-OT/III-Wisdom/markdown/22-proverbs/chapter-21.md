## Text

**21:1** Just as a rush of water, so is the heart of a king in the hand of God; wherever He wishes to turn, there He inclines it.

**21:2** Every man appears righteous to himself, but the Lord directs hearts.

**21:3** Doing what is righteous and being truthful are more pleasing to God than the blood of sacrifices.

**21:4** A bold-hearted person is haughty in insolence, and the lamp of the ungodly is sin.

**21:6** He who produces treasures with a lying tongue pursues futile things to the snares of death.

**21:7** Destruction will lodge as a guest with the ungodly, for they do not wish to practice what is righteous.

**21:8** To the crooked, God sends crooked ways, for His works are pure and upright.

**21:9** It is better to dwell on a corner in the open air than in whitewashed rooms with unrighteousness and in a shared house.

**21:10** The soul of an ungodly person will not be shown mercy by any human being.

**21:11** When an undisciplined person is punished, the evil person becomes more shrewd, and when a wise person understands, he will receive knowledge.

**21:12** A righteous person understands the hearts of the ungodly and despises the ungodly in evil things.

**21:13** He who stops up his ears so as not to listen to a weak person will himself also call out, and there will be no one who listens.

**21:14** A secret gift overturns anger, but he who is sparing with gifts stirs up strong wrath.

**21:15** The gladness of the righteous does justice, but a devout person is unclean among evildoers.

**21:16** A man wandering from the way of righteousness will rest in the assembly of giants.

**21:17** A man in want loves gladness, loving wine and oil for wealth.

**21:18** And a lawless person is an expiation for a righteous person.

**21:19** It is better to dwell in the wilderness than with a woman who is sharp-tongued and quarrelsome and quick-tempered.

**21:20** A desirable treasure will rest upon the mouth of a wise person, but foolish men will swallow it up.

**21:21** The way of righteousness and mercy will find life and glory.

**21:22** A wise person scaled fortified cities and tore down the stronghold in which the ungodly had trusted.

**21:23** He who guards his mouth and tongue preserves his soul from distress.

**21:24** One who is brazen and self-willed and boastful is called a pest, and he who holds a grudge is a transgressor.

**21:25** Desires kill a sluggard, for his hands do not choose to do anything.

**21:26** An ungodly person desires evil desires all day long, but the righteous person shows mercy and has compassion unsparingly.

**21:27** Sacrifices of the ungodly are an abomination to the Lord, for indeed they offer them unlawfully.

**21:28** A false witness will perish, but an obedient man will speak guardedly.

**21:29** An ungodly man shamelessly stands firm in countenance, but the upright person himself understands his ways.

**21:30** There is no wisdom, there is no courage, there is no counsel for the ungodly.

**21:31** A horse is prepared for the day of battle, but help is from the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **21:7** — The Greek verb epixenothesetai literally means "will lodge as a guest with," reflecting a Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew verb as "sojourn" rather than "sweep away."
- **21:9** — The Septuagint reads "in whitewashed rooms with unrighteousness" where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "with a quarrelsome woman."
- **21:16** — Greek giganton ("giants") is the standard Septuagint rendering of Hebrew Rephaim ("shades" or "the dead").
- **21:18** — Greek perikatharma ("expiation") literally denotes a ritual cleansing or expiatory offering, used here to render Hebrew "ransom."
- **21:30** — The Septuagint reads "courage" and "for the ungodly," diverging notably from the Masoretic Text ("understanding" and "against the LORD").
