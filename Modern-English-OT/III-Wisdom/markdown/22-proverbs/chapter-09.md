## Text

**9:1** Wisdom built a house for herself and set seven pillars underneath.

**9:2** She slaughtered her sacrifices, mixed her wine into a mixing bowl, and prepared her table.

**9:3** She sent out her servants, summoning to the mixing bowl with a lofty proclamation, saying,

**9:4** "Whoever is foolish, let him turn aside to me." And to those lacking sense she said,

**9:5** "Come, eat of my bread, and drink wine that I have mixed for you;

**9:6** abandon foolishness, so that you may reign forever, and seek prudence, and direct understanding in knowledge."

**9:7** The one who disciplines evil people will receive dishonor for himself, and one who reproves the ungodly person will reproach himself.

**9:8** Do not reprove evil people, so that they will not hate you; reprove a wise person, and he will love you.

**9:9** Give a wise person an opportunity, and he will be wiser; inform a righteous person, and he will continue to receive.

**9:10** The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and the counsel of holy ones is understanding; for to know the law belongs to a good mind.

**9:11** For in this way you will live a long time, and years of your life will be added to you.

**9:12** Son, if you become wise for yourself, you will be wise for your neighbors as well; but if you turn out evil, you alone will endure evils. Whoever leans on falsehoods, this one shepherds winds, and the same one will chase flying birds; for he abandoned the ways of his own vineyard, and he has strayed from the tracks of his own farmland; and he passes through a waterless wilderness and a land assigned to parched places, and he gathers barrenness with his hands.

**9:13** A foolish and brazen woman comes to lack a morsel, one who does not know shame.

**9:14** She sat at the doors of her house on a seat, openly in the streets,

**9:15** calling out to those passing by and going straight in their ways:

**9:16** "Whoever among you is most foolish, let him turn aside to me; and I urge those lacking prudence, saying,

**9:17** 'Partake with pleasure of secret bread, and of sweet stolen water.'"

**9:18** But he does not know that the earthborn perish with her, and he comes upon the plank of Hades. Instead, leap away, do not linger in the place, nor fix your eye upon her; for in this way you will cross over foreign water; but abstain from foreign water, and do not drink from a foreign spring, so that you may live a long time, and years of life may be added to you.

## Translator Notes

- **9:6** — The Greek reads "reign forever" (βασιλεύσητε) and adds "and seek prudence," where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "live" without the additional clause.
- **9:10** — The Greek reads "counsel of holy ones" (βουλὴ ἁγίων) and includes the third clause about knowing the law, which is not found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **9:12** — The verb rendered "endure" (ἀντλήσεις) literally means "draw" or "bail out," an idiom for suffering hardship; the extended saying from "Whoever leans on falsehoods" onward is a distinctive Septuagint expansion.
- **9:16** — Unlike 9:4 ("she said"), the Greek shifts to the first person ("I urge, saying," παρακελεύομαι λέγουσα), placing the entire verse in the direct speech of the foolish woman.
- **9:18** — Greek γηγενεῖς ("the earthborn") renders the Hebrew Rephaim (shades of the dead), and πέτευρον ("plank") can denote a perch or trap-board; the admonition from "Instead, leap away" to the end is an extended Septuagint passage.
