# Proverbs 11 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**11:1** Deceitful balances are an abomination before the Lord, but a just weight is acceptable to Him.

**11:2** Wherever arrogance enters, there also is dishonor; but the mouth of the humble meditates on wisdom.

**11:3** When a righteous person dies, he leaves behind regret, but the destruction of the ungodly is swift and rejoiced over.

**11:5** Righteousness cuts blameless paths straight, but ungodliness falls into unrighteousness.

**11:6** The righteousness of upright men rescues them, but the lawless are caught by their destruction.

**11:7** When a righteous man dies, hope does not perish, but the boast of the ungodly perishes.

**11:8** A righteous person escapes from a trap, and in his place the ungodly one is handed over.

**11:9** In the mouth of the ungodly is a snare for citizens, but the discernment of the righteous is prosperous.

**11:10** By the good things of the righteous a city prospers,

**11:11** but by the mouths of the ungodly it is overthrown.

**11:12** One lacking sense derides citizens, but a prudent man keeps quiet.

**11:13** A double-tongued man reveals counsels in an assembly, but one faithful in spirit conceals matters.

**11:14** Those who have no guidance fall like leaves, but there is safety in much counsel.

**11:15** An evil person does harm whenever he associates with a righteous person, and he hates the sound of security.

**11:16** A gracious woman raises up glory for her husband, but a woman who hates what is righteous is a throne of dishonor. The lazy come to lack wealth, but the valiant are supported by wealth.

**11:17** A merciful man does good to his soul, but the merciless one destroys his body.

**11:18** An ungodly person does unrighteous deeds, but the seed of the righteous is a reward of truth.

**11:19** A righteous son is born for life, but the pursuit of an ungodly person leads to death.

**11:20** Crooked ways are an abomination to the Lord, but all who are blameless in their ways are acceptable to Him.

**11:21** One who places hands in a hand unjustly will not go unpunished, but the one who sows righteousness will receive a trustworthy reward.

**11:22** Like an earring in a pig's snout, so is beauty to an evil-minded woman.

**11:23** Every desire of the righteous is good, but the hope of the ungodly will perish.

**11:24** There are those who, by sowing what is their own, make more; and there are also those who, by gathering, are diminished.

**11:25** Every blessed soul is sincere, but a wrathful man is not dignified.

**11:26** May the one who withholds grain leave it behind for the nations; but a blessing is on the head of the one who shares.

**11:27** One who devises good things seeks good favor, but evil things will overtake the one who seeks them out.

**11:28** The one who trusts in wealth, he will fall; but the one who supports the righteous, he will spring up.

**11:29** The one who does not get along with his own household will inherit wind, and a fool will serve a prudent person.

**11:30** From the fruit of righteousness a tree of life grows, but the souls of the lawless are taken away prematurely.

**11:31** If the righteous person is barely saved, where will the ungodly and the sinner appear?

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — The Greek text differs markedly from the Hebrew MT here (and omits verse 4). 'Regret' (μετάμελον) refers to the sorrow left among the living at a righteous person's death, while 'swift' (πρόχειρος) literally means 'ready at hand.'
- **11:10** — In the Greek text, verses 10 and 11 form a single couplet, omitting the second half of verse 10 and the first half of verse 11 found in the Hebrew MT.
- **11:15** — The phrase 'sound of security' (ἦχον ἀσφαλείας) reflects the distinctive Greek reading, contrasting with the Hebrew MT's reference to those who strike hands in pledge.
- **11:25** — The Greek clause ψυχὴ εὐλογουμένη πᾶσα ἁπλῆ ('Every blessed soul is sincere') may also be construed 'Every sincere soul is blessed.'
- **11:31** — This Greek rendering—differing from the Hebrew MT's 'repaid on the earth'—is quoted verbatim in 1 Peter 4:18.
