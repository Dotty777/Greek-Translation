## Text

**7:1** Son, keep my words, and hide my commandments with yourself. Son, honor the Lord and you will be strong, and besides Him do not fear another.

**7:2** Keep my commandments and you will live, and my words like the pupils of your eyes.

**7:3** Place them around your fingers, and inscribe them on the breadth of your heart.

**7:4** Say that wisdom is your sister, and acquire prudence as an acquaintance for yourself,

**7:5** so that she may guard you from a strange and wicked woman, if she assails you with words meant to win favor.

**7:6** For peering out from a window of her house into the streets,

**7:7** whomever she sees among the senseless children, a young man lacking sense,

**7:8** passing by near a corner in the passages of her houses and speaking

**7:9** in evening darkness, whenever there is nightly and gloomy stillness,

**7:10** then the woman meets him, having the appearance of a prostitute, who makes the hearts of young men take flight.

**7:11** She is flighty and dissolute, and her feet do not stay still at home.

**7:12** For at one time she roams outside, and at another time she lies in ambush in the streets near every corner.

**7:13** Then seizing him, she kissed him, and with a shameless face she said to him:

**7:14** "I have a peace offering; today I am paying my vows.

**7:15** For this reason I came out to meet you; longing for your face, I have found you.

**7:16** I have strung my bed with cord, and I have spread it with double-sided coverlets from Egypt.

**7:17** I have sprinkled my couch with saffron, and my house with cinnamon.

**7:18** Come, and let us enjoy love until dawn; come here, and let us wrap ourselves in passion.

**7:19** For my husband is not present at home, but he has gone on a long journey,

**7:20** having taken a bundle of silver in his hand; after many days he will return to his house."

**7:21** She led him astray with much talk, and with the snares from her lips she drove him off course.

**7:22** And he followed after her, lured on, just as an ox is led to slaughter, and just as a dog to bonds,

**7:23** or like a stag struck in the liver by an arrow; and he hurries like a bird into a trap, not knowing that he is running for his life.

**7:24** Now therefore, son, listen to me, and pay attention to the words of my mouth.

**7:25** Do not let your heart turn aside into her ways.

**7:26** For having wounded many, she has cast them down, and countless are those whom she has murdered.

**7:27** Her house is the ways of Hades, leading down into the chambers of death.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The second sentence ("Son, honor the Lord...") is a Septuagint-distinctive expansion not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **7:6** — Unlike the Hebrew (where the narrator looks through his own window), the Greek depicts the woman herself peering from her house, and 7:8 uniquely describes the young man as speaking in the passages of her houses.
- **7:22** — Greek κεπφωθείς ("lured on") is a rare verb derived from κέπφος, a gullible seabird easily lured into a trap. The Greek also distinctively compares the youth to a dog led to bonds and, in 7:23, a stag struck in the liver by an arrow.
- **7:27** — Greek ᾅδου ("Hades") is the Septuagint rendering for the realm of the dead (corresponding to Hebrew Sheol).
