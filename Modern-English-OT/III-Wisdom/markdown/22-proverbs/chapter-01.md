## Text

**1:1** Proverbs of Solomon, son of David, who reigned in Israel,

**1:2** to know wisdom and discipline, and to comprehend words of prudence,

**1:3** and to receive turns of words, and to comprehend true righteousness and to make judgment straight,

**1:4** so that he may give shrewdness to the innocent, and both discernment and thoughtfulness to a young child.

**1:5** For having heard these things, a wise person will be wiser, and the intelligent one will acquire guidance,

**1:6** and will comprehend a parable and an obscure word, and sayings of the wise and riddles.

**1:7** The beginning of wisdom is the fear of God, and good understanding belongs to all who practice it; and piety toward God is the beginning of discernment, but the ungodly will despise wisdom and discipline.

**1:8** Hear, son, the discipline of your father, and do not reject the ordinances of your mother;

**1:9** for you will receive a wreath of graces on your head, and a golden collar around your neck.

**1:10** Son, do not let ungodly men lead you astray, nor consent

**1:11** if they urge you, saying, "Come with us, share in blood, and let us hide a righteous man in the earth unjustly,

**1:12** and let us swallow him alive like Hades, and let us remove his memory from the earth;

**1:13** let us seize his costly property, and let us fill our houses with spoils;

**1:14** and cast your lot among us, and let us all acquire a common purse, and let there be one pouch for us."

**1:15** Do not walk in a way with them, but turn your foot away from their paths;

**1:17** for nets are not spread out unjustly for winged creatures.

**1:18** For those themselves who share in murder store up evils for themselves, and the overthrow of lawless men is evil.

**1:19** These are the ways of all who carry out lawless deeds; for by ungodliness they are deprived of their own life.

**1:20** Wisdom is praised in song in the thoroughfares, and in the squares she speaks with boldness;

**1:21** and on the tops of walls she is proclaimed, and at the gates of rulers she sits in attendance, and at the gates of the city she says with confidence,

**1:22** "As long as the innocent hold fast to righteousness, they will not be ashamed; but the foolish, being desirers of insolence, having become ungodly, hated discernment,

**1:23** and became liable to reproofs. Look, I will put forth to you an utterance of my breath, and I will teach you my word.

**1:24** Since I was calling and you did not listen, and I was stretching out words and you were not paying attention,

**1:25** but you were making my counsels invalid, and you disobeyed my reproofs;

**1:26** therefore I also will laugh at your ruin, and I will exult whenever destruction comes upon you,

**1:27** and when turmoil suddenly arrives upon you, and overthrow is present like a storm, and when affliction and siege come upon you, or when destruction comes upon you.

**1:28** For it will be that when you call upon me, I will not hear you; evil people will seek me, and will not find me.

**1:29** For they hated wisdom, and they did not choose the word of the Lord,

**1:30** nor were they willing to pay attention to my counsels, and they sneered at my reproofs.

**1:31** Therefore they will eat the fruits of their own way, and they will be filled with their own ungodliness;

**1:32** for because they wronged infants, they will be murdered, and scrutiny will destroy the ungodly.

**1:33** But the one who listens to me will dwell in hope, and will rest without fear from every evil."

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — Greek στροφὰς λόγων ("turns of words") refers to intricate, subtle, or artfully turned expressions.
- **1:7** — The Greek text reads "fear of God" rather than "fear of the Lord" and contains an expanded double couplet echoing Psalm 110:10 LXX (111:10 MT).
- **1:17** — Verse 1:16 is absent in the Septuagint tradition, which proceeds directly from 1:15 to 1:17 and reads ἀδίκως ("unjustly") rather than "in vain."
- **1:22** — The Septuagint diverges markedly from the Hebrew here, opening with a conditional promise for the innocent ("As long as...") rather than a rebuke of the simple.
- **1:32** — Greek νηπίους ("infants") can also mean "the naive," and ἐξετασμός ("scrutiny") denotes judicial examination or inquiry.
