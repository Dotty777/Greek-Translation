## Text

**6:1** Son, if you become guarantor for your friend, you will deliver your hand to an enemy.

**6:2** For his own lips are a strong snare to a man, and he is caught by the lips of his own mouth.

**6:3** Do, son, what I command you, and save yourself; for you have come into the hands of evil men because of your friend. Go without growing faint, and also spur on your friend for whom you became guarantor.

**6:4** Do not give sleep to your eyes, nor slumber with your eyelids,

**6:5** so that you may save yourself like a gazelle from nooses and like a bird from a snare.

**6:6** Go to the ant, you lazy one, and, seeing its ways, emulate it, and become wiser than it;

**6:7** for though it has no farmland, neither having anyone who compels it nor being under a master,

**6:8** it prepares its food in summer, and makes its store abundant in the harvest. Or go to the bee and learn what a worker it is, and how noble it makes its work; kings and common people partake of its labors for health, and it is desired by all and renowned. Although it is weak in strength, having honored wisdom, it was advanced.

**6:9** How long, lazy one, are you lying down? And when will you rise from sleep?

**6:10** A little you sleep, and a little you sit, and a brief while you slumber, and a little you clasp your chest with your hands;

**6:11** then poverty comes upon you like an evil traveler, and want like a good runner. But if you are not lazy, your harvest will come like a spring, and want will desert like a bad runner.

**6:12** A foolish and lawless man walks ways that are not good;

**6:13** and the same man winks with his eye, and signals with his foot, and teaches by gestures of his fingers;

**6:14** with a perverted heart he devises evil things at all times; such a one stirs up disturbances for a city.

**6:15** For this reason, his destruction comes suddenly, a cutting off and an incurable crushing,

**6:16** because he rejoices in all things that God hates, and he is crushed because of impurity of soul:

**6:17** the eye of an insolent man, an unjust tongue, hands shedding the blood of a just man,

**6:18** and a heart devising evil thoughts, and feet hurrying to do evil.

**6:19** An unjust witness kindles falsehoods, and sends disputes among brothers.

**6:20** Son, keep the laws of your father, and do not reject the ordinances of your mother;

**6:21** and bind them upon your soul continually, and fasten them around your neck.

**6:22** Whenever you walk, bring it along and let it be with you; and when you sleep, let it guard you, so that when you wake, it may converse with you.

**6:23** For a commandment of the law is a lamp and a light; both reproof and discipline are a way of life,

**6:24** to guard you from a married woman and from the slander of a strange tongue.

**6:25** Do not let desire for beauty conquer you, nor be ensnared by your eyes, nor be carried away by her eyelids.

**6:26** For the price of a prostitute is as much as one loaf of bread, but a woman ensnares the precious souls of men.

**6:27** Will anyone bind up fire in his lap, and not burn up his garments?

**6:28** Or will anyone walk on coals of fire, and not burn up his feet?

**6:29** So is the one who goes in to a married woman; he will not go unpunished, nor anyone who touches her.

**6:30** It is not surprising if someone is caught stealing, for he steals so that he may fill his soul when he is hungry;

**6:31** yet if he is caught, he will repay sevenfold, and by giving all his possessions he will rescue himself.

**6:32** But the adulterer, because of a lack of sense, acquires destruction for his soul,

**6:33** and endures both pains and disgraces; and his reproach will never be wiped out.

**6:34** For the rage of her husband is full of jealousy; he will not spare in the day of judgment;

**6:35** he will not exchange the enmity for any ransom, nor will he at all be appeased by many gifts.

## Translator Notes

- **6:3** — Here and in 6:6, the Greek form isthi represents a manuscript spelling of ithi ('Go!'), rather than the imperative of 'be' (compare 5:20).
- **6:8** — The Greek text contains an extended comparison to the bee (from 'Or go to the bee' to the end of the verse) not found in MT, as well as an additional couplet on diligence in 6:11.
- **6:16** — Instead of MT's numerical saying ('six things... seven'), the Greek connects this verse directly to the wicked man of 6:12-15 ('because he rejoices in all things that God hates, and he is crushed because of impurity of soul').
- **6:22** — After the plural 'laws' and 'ordinances' of 6:20-21, the Greek shifts abruptly to a feminine singular pronoun (auten, here 'it'), echoing the Hebrew singular antecedent and anticipating 'commandment' in 6:23; in 6:23, 'and a light' could alternatively be construed as the predicate of 'the way of life.'
- **6:26** — The genitive andron ('of men') stands between 'woman' and 'precious souls' and can alternatively be construed with 'woman' ('a wife/woman of men ensnares precious souls'), echoing MT's 'a man's wife.'
