# Proverbs 18 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**18:1** A man wishing to separate himself from friends seeks pretexts, and at all times he will be open to reproach.

**18:2** One lacking sense has no need of wisdom, for he is led rather by folly.

**18:3** When an ungodly person comes into a depth of evils, he shows contempt, and dishonor and reproach come upon him.

**18:4** A word in a man's heart is deep water, and a river springs up, and a spring of life.

**18:5** To show partiality to an ungodly person is not good, nor is it godly to turn aside justice in judgment.

**18:6** The lips of a fool lead him into evil, and his rash mouth calls upon death.

**18:7** The mouth of a fool is ruin to him, and his lips are a snare to his soul.

**18:8** Fear casts down the lazy, and the souls of the effeminate will go hungry.

**18:9** The one who does not heal himself in his works is a brother of the one who destroys himself.

**18:10** The name of the Lord is from greatness of strength, and the righteous, running to it, are exalted.

**18:11** The property of a rich man is a fortified city, and its glory casts a great shadow.

**18:12** Before ruin a man's heart is lifted up, and before glory it is humbled.

**18:13** The one who answers a matter before hearing—it is folly and reproach to him.

**18:14** A prudent servant calms a man's anger, but who will endure a fainthearted man?

**18:15** The heart of a prudent person acquires discernment, and the ears of the wise seek understanding.

**18:16** A person's gift makes room for him, and seats him beside rulers.

**18:17** A righteous person is his own accuser in the opening speech; and when the adversary attacks, he is refuted.

**18:18** A silent person puts an end to disputes, and among powers he sets boundaries.

**18:19** A brother helped by a brother is like a fortified and high city, and is strong just like a firmly founded palace.

**18:20** From the fruits of the mouth a man fills his stomach, and from the fruits of his lips he will be filled.

**18:21** Death and life are in the hand of the tongue, and those who master it will eat its fruits.

**18:22** The one who has found a good wife has found favors, and has received cheerfulness from God; the one who casts out a good wife casts out good things, and the one who keeps an adulteress is foolish and ungodly.

## Translator Notes

- **18:8** — The Greek differs completely from the Hebrew ("The words of a whisperer..."), closely paralleling Greek Proverbs 19:15; "the effeminate" (ἀνδρογύνων) denotes soft, unmanly, or timid persons.
- **18:9** — The Greek reads "does not heal himself" (μὴ ἰώμενος αὐτόν), reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew root rendered "is slack" in the Masoretic Text.
- **18:10** — The Greek reads "from greatness of strength" (ἐκ μεγαλωσύνης ἰσχύος) rather than the Hebrew "a strong tower," and the dative pronoun αὐτῷ ("to it") may refer either to the name of the Lord or directly to the Lord ("to Him").
- **18:18** — The Greek reads "a silent person" (σιγηρός) where the Hebrew reads "the lot."
- **18:22** — The second half of this verse ("the one who casts out...") is an addition in the Septuagint, which concludes chapter 18 here without the equivalent of Hebrew 18:23–24.
