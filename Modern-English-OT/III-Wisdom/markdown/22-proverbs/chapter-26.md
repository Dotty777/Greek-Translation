## Text

**26:1** Just as dew in harvest and just as rain in summer, so there is no honor for a fool.

**26:2** Just as birds and sparrows fly, so an empty curse will not come upon anyone.

**26:3** Just as a whip for a horse and a goad for a donkey, so is a rod for a lawless nation.

**26:4** Do not answer a fool in line with his folly, so that you do not become like him.

**26:5** But answer a fool according to his folly, so that he does not appear wise to himself.

**26:6** From his own ways, the one who sends a word through a foolish messenger brings reproach on himself.

**26:7** Take away the walking of legs, and lawlessness from the mouth of fools.

**26:8** The one who ties up a stone in a sling is like the one who gives glory to a fool.

**26:9** Thorns grow in the hand of a drunkard, and slavery in the hand of fools.

**26:10** All the flesh of fools is greatly storm-tossed, for their frenzy is crushed.

**26:11** Just as a dog, when it goes to its own vomit and becomes detestable, so is a fool who by his own wickedness returns to his own sin. There is a shame that brings sin, and there is a shame that is glory and grace.

**26:12** I saw a man who seemed to himself to be wise, yet a fool had more hope than he.

**26:13** A sluggard, when sent on the road, says, "A lion is on the roads."

**26:14** Just as a door turns on its hinge, so does a sluggard on his bed.

**26:15** Having hidden his hand in his bosom, a sluggard will not be able to bring it to his mouth.

**26:16** A sluggard appears wiser to himself than the one who brings back a report in fullness.

**26:17** Just as the one who takes hold of a dog's tail, so is the one who presides over another's dispute.

**26:18** Just as those who heal put forward words to people, and the first one who encounters the word will be tripped up;

**26:19** so are all those who lie in ambush for their own friends, and when they are seen, they say, "I was joking when I did it."

**26:20** With much wood a fire flourishes, and where there is no double-minded person, a quarrel grows quiet.

**26:21** A hearth for coals and wood for fire, and a reviling man is for the turmoil of a quarrel.

**26:22** The words of tricksters are soft, yet these strike into the chambers of the inward parts.

**26:23** Silver given with deceit must be regarded like a potsherd; smooth lips cover a grievous heart.

**26:24** With his lips he agrees to everything while lamenting enemies, but in his heart he devises words.

**26:25** If the enemy pleads with you with a loud voice, do not be persuaded; for there are seven evils in his soul.

**26:26** The one who conceals enmity contrives deceit, and, being well-known in councils, he covers up his own sins.

**26:27** The one who digs a pit for his neighbor will fall into it, and the one who rolls a stone rolls it onto himself.

**26:28** A false tongue hates truth, and an unguarded mouth causes disturbances.

## Translator Notes

- **26:7** — The Greek uses a second-person imperative ('Take away') and reads 'lawlessness' rather than 'a proverb,' presenting a markedly different saying from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **26:11** — The second sentence ('There is a shame that brings sin, and there is a shame that is glory and grace') is a Septuagint addition nearly identical to Sirach 4:21.
- **26:16** — The Greek reads en plesmone ('in fullness' or 'in satiety'), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonants from the Masoretic Text's 'seven.'
- **26:18** — The Greek reads hoi iomenoi ('those who heal') and logous ('words'), differing sharply from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's image of a madman throwing firebrands and arrows.
- **26:26** — The Greek source text reads enkalyptei ('covers up') rather than the variant ekkalyptei ('uncovers'), portraying the deceitful person as trying to conceal his sins even though he is well-known in councils.
