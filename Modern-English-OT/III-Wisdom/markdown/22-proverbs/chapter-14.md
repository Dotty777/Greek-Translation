## Text

**14:1** Wise women build houses, but the foolish woman demolishes them with her hands.

**14:2** The one who walks uprightly fears the Lord, but the one who is crooked in his ways will be dishonored.

**14:3** Out of the mouth of fools comes a rod of arrogance, but the lips of the wise guard them.

**14:4** Where there are no oxen, the mangers are clean, but where there are abundant crops, the strength of an ox is evident.

**14:5** A faithful witness does not lie, but an unjust witness kindles lies.

**14:6** You will seek wisdom among evil people and will not find it, but perception is easy among the prudent.

**14:7** All things are contrary to a foolish man, but wise lips are weapons of perception.

**14:8** The wisdom of the shrewd will recognize their ways, but the folly of fools is in error.

**14:9** The houses of the lawless will owe purification, but the houses of the righteous are acceptable.

**14:10** A man's heart is sensitive; his soul is sorrowful, yet when he rejoices, he does not mingle with arrogance.

**14:11** The houses of the ungodly will be destroyed, but the tents of those who act uprightly will stand.

**14:12** There is a way that seems to be right among people, but its end comes to the bottom of Hades.

**14:13** In times of gladness sorrow does not mingle, yet joy in the end comes to mourning.

**14:14** A reckless-hearted person will be filled with his own ways, and a good man from his thoughts.

**14:15** An innocent person believes every word, but a shrewd person comes to repentance.

**14:16** A wise person fears and turns away from evil, but the fool, trusting in himself, mingles with a lawless person.

**14:17** A quick-tempered person acts without counsel, but a prudent man endures many things.

**14:18** Fools will receive wickedness as their share, but the shrewd will hold fast to perception.

**14:19** Evil people will slip before the good, and the ungodly will attend the doors of the righteous.

**14:20** Friends will hate poor friends, but the friends of the rich are many.

**14:21** The one who dishonors the needy sins, but one who shows mercy to the poor is blessed.

**14:22** Those who go astray craft evil, but good people craft mercy and truth; craftsmen of evil do not know mercy and faithfulness, but acts of mercy and faithfulness are with good craftsmen.

**14:23** In everyone who takes thought there is abundance, but the easygoing and unfeeling person will be in want.

**14:24** The crown of the wise is a shrewd person, but the pastime of fools is evil.

**14:25** A faithful witness will rescue a soul from evils, but a deceitful person kindles lies.

**14:26** In the fear of the Lord there is hope of strength, and for his children he leaves behind a support.

**14:27** The command of the Lord is a spring of life, and it causes one to turn away from a snare of death.

**14:28** In a numerous nation is the glory of a king, but in a lack of people is the shattering of a ruler.

**14:29** A patient man is great in prudence, but the impatient person is strongly foolish.

**14:30** A gentle-spirited man is a physician of the heart, but a sensitive heart is a moth to the bones.

**14:31** The one who oppresses a needy person provokes the One who made him, but the one who honors Him shows mercy to a poor person.

**14:32** In his wickedness an ungodly person will be thrust away, but the one who trusts in his own holiness is righteous.

**14:33** In the good heart of a man is wisdom, but in the heart of fools it is not discerned.

**14:34** Righteousness lifts up a nation, but sins diminish tribes.

**14:35** A discerning servant is acceptable to a king, and by his own adroitness he removes dishonor.

## Translator Notes

- **14:10** — The Greek expression 'sensitive heart' (kardia aisthetike, recurring in 14:30) reflects a distinctive Septuagint reading linking heightened sensitivity with sorrow, and 'arrogance' (hybris) stands where the Hebrew reads 'stranger.'
- **14:15** — The Greek reads 'comes to repentance' (metanoia, also meaning 'change of mind' or 'reconsideration'), differing from the Hebrew ('considers his steps').
- **14:22** — The Greek text contains a four-line proverb built on artisan wordplay (tektaino 'craft' and tekton 'craftsman'), expanding the two-line Hebrew proverb.
- **14:26** — The unexpressed subject of 'leaves behind' and the pronoun 'his' are ambiguous in Greek; they may refer to the God-fearing person, to the Lord Himself ('He'/'His'), or to the fear of the Lord ('it').
- **14:32** — The Greek reads 'in his own holiness' (hosiotes), reflecting a different textual reading from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ('in his death').
