## Text

**27:1** Do not boast about the things for tomorrow, for you do not know what the coming day will bring forth.

**27:2** Let a neighbor praise you, and not your own mouth; a stranger, and not your own lips.

**27:3** A stone is heavy and sand is hard to carry, but the anger of a fool is heavier than both.

**27:4** Wrath is merciless and anger is sharp, but jealousy tolerates no one.

**27:5** Open reproofs are better than hidden friendship.

**27:6** The wounds of a friend are more trustworthy than the willing kisses of an enemy.

**27:7** A soul that is full scoffs at honeycombs, but to a soul in need even bitter things seem sweet.

**27:8** Just as when a bird flies down from its own nest, so a man is enslaved when he is estranged from his own places.

**27:9** A heart is delighted by perfumes and wines and incense, but a soul is torn apart by misfortunes.

**27:10** Do not forsake your friend or a friend of your father, and do not enter into the house of your brother when you suffer misfortune; better is a friend nearby than a brother living far away.

**27:11** Become wise, son, so that your heart may be glad, and turn away reproachful words from yourself.

**27:12** A shrewd man hides himself when evils approach, but fools press on and will pay the penalty.

**27:13** Take away his garment, for he has passed by; whoever ruins what belongs to others is an insolent man.

**27:14** Whoever blesses a friend early in the morning with a loud voice will seem no different from one who curses.

**27:15** Drops drive a man out of the house on a wintry day; likewise also a reviling woman out of his own house.

**27:16** The north wind is a harsh wind, yet by name it is called favorable.

**27:17** Iron sharpens iron, and a man sharpens the face of a companion.

**27:18** He who plants a fig tree will eat its fruits, and he who guards his own master will be honored.

**27:19** Just as faces are not like faces, so neither are the minds of people.

**27:20** Hades and destruction are not filled; likewise also the eyes of people are insatiable. An abomination to the Lord is one who fixes his eye, and the undisciplined are unrestrained in tongue.

**27:21** A test for silver and gold is smelting, and a man is tested through the mouth of those who praise him. The heart of a lawless man seeks out evils, but an upright heart seeks knowledge.

**27:22** If you flog a fool in the midst of a council, dishonoring him, you will by no means remove his folly.

**27:23** You shall thoroughly know the lives of your flock, and you shall set your heart on your herds;

**27:24** for a man does not have power and strength forever, nor does he hand them down from generation to generation.

**27:25** Care for the green plants in the field, and you will cut grass, and gather mountain hay,

**27:26** so that you may have sheep for clothing; value a field, so that you may have lambs.

**27:27** Son, from me you have strong sayings for your life and for the life of your servants.

## Translator Notes

- **27:4** — In the Greek text, jealousy (zelos) is the grammatical subject of hyphistatai with oudena ('no one') as its direct object, literally 'jealousy bears with/tolerates no one,' rather than the Hebrew question 'who can stand before jealousy?'
- **27:11** — The Greek text reads second-person pronouns ('your heart' and 'from yourself') rather than the first-person pronouns found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **27:16** — The Greek presents an ironic saying about the north wind, rendering the Hebrew term for 'right hand' with epidexios ('on the right, auspicious, favorable').
- **27:20** — Both verses 20 and 21 contain an additional Septuagint couplet not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **27:27** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text mentions goats' milk as food for the household, the Septuagint closes the chapter with a didactic statement about 'strong sayings' for life.
