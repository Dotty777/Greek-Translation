## Text

**19:3** The foolishness of a man destroys his ways, yet he blames God in his heart.

**19:4** Wealth adds many friends, but the poor man is abandoned even by the friend he has.

**19:5** A false witness will not remain unpunished, and the one who accuses unjustly will not escape.

**19:6** Many court the faces of kings, but every evil person becomes a reproach to a man.

**19:7** Everyone who hates a poor brother will also be far from friendship. Good insight will draw near to those who know it, and a prudent man will find it. The one who does much evil brings wickedness to completion, and the one who provokes words will not be saved.

**19:8** The one who acquires prudence loves himself, and the one who keeps prudence will find good things.

**19:9** A false witness will not remain unpunished, and whoever kindles wickedness will perish by it.

**19:10** Luxury is not beneficial for a fool, nor if a household servant begins to rule with insolence.

**19:11** A merciful man is patient, and his boast comes upon lawbreakers.

**19:12** A king's threat is like the roaring of a lion, and just as dew upon grass, so is his cheerfulness.

**19:13** A foolish son is a shame to a father; vows from the wages of a prostitute are not pure.

**19:14** Fathers apportion house and property to children, but from God a wife is joined to a husband.

**19:15** Cowardice grips an effeminate man, and the soul of an idle person will hunger.

**19:16** The one who keeps a commandment guards his own soul, but the one who despises his own ways will perish.

**19:17** The one who shows mercy to a poor man lends to God, and according to his gift it will be repaid to him.

**19:18** Discipline your son, for in this way he will be hopeful, yet do not be carried away in your soul to abuse.

**19:19** An evil-minded man will be penalized; and if he acts like a scoundrel, he will also add his own soul.

**19:20** Listen, son, to the discipline of your father, so that you may become wise in your latter days.

**19:21** Many thoughts are in a man's heart, but the counsel of the Lord remains forever.

**19:22** Mercy is fruit for a man, and a righteous poor man is better than a lying rich man.

**19:23** The fear of the Lord leads to life for a man, but the one without fear will lodge in places where knowledge is not attended to.

**19:24** The one who hides his hands in his bosom unjustly will by no means even bring them to his mouth.

**19:25** When a scoundrel is whipped, a fool becomes shrewder; and if you reprove a prudent man, he will understand discernment.

**19:26** The one who dishonors his father and drives away his mother will be put to shame and will be an object of reproach.

**19:27** A son who ceases to keep a father's discipline will meditate on evil sayings.

**19:28** The one who stands guarantor for a foolish child treats a just decree with insolence, and the mouth of the ungodly will swallow down judgments.

**19:29** Whips are prepared for the unrestrained, and punishments likewise for fools.

## Translator Notes

- **19:3** — Verses 19:1-2 of the Hebrew numbering are absent from the Septuagint, so this chapter opens at verse 3; nothing has been omitted from the Greek text.
- **19:6** — The Septuagint reads 'every evil person becomes a reproach to a man' (apparently reading Hebrew ra', 'evil,' rather than rea', 'friend'), where the Masoretic Text has 'everyone is a friend to a giver of gifts.'
- **19:7** — The Septuagint includes two additional proverbial couplets in this verse ('Good insight...' and 'The one who does much evil...') that do not appear in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **19:11** — The Greek 'his boast comes upon lawbreakers' reflects a literal rendering of the Hebrew idiom 'his glory is to pass over transgression.'
- **19:13** — In the second clause the Septuagint reads 'vows from the wages of a prostitute are not pure' (cf. Deut 23:18), diverging from the Hebrew 'a wife's quarreling is a continual dripping.'
- **19:23** — The Septuagint's 'where knowledge is not attended to' reflects reading a Hebrew form meaning 'knowledge' rather than 'evil/harm' as in the Masoretic Text.
