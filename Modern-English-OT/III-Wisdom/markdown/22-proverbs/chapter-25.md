## Text

**25:1** These are the unarranged instructions of Solomon, which the friends of Hezekiah, the king of Judea, copied out.

**25:2** The glory of God conceals a word, but the glory of a king honors matters.

**25:3** Heaven is high, and the earth is deep, and the heart of a king is beyond scrutiny.

**25:4** Strike impure silver, and all of it will be purified clean.

**25:5** Kill the ungodly from the presence of a king, and his throne will prosper in righteousness.

**25:6** Do not boast before a king, nor take your stand in the places of rulers.

**25:7** For it is better for you to be told, "Come up to me," than for you to be humbled in the presence of a ruler. Speak what your eyes have seen.

**25:8** Do not rush into a quarrel quickly, so that you do not regret it in the end, whenever your own friend reproaches you.

**25:9** Withdraw backward; do not show contempt,

**25:10** otherwise your friend may reproach you, and your quarrel and your enmity will not depart, but will be equal to death for you. Grace and friendship set free; keep them for yourself, so that you do not become subject to reproach, but guard your ways amicably.

**25:11** A golden apple in a necklace of sardius—so it is to speak a word.

**25:12** A wise word is fastened to an attentive ear as a golden earring and a precious sardius.

**25:13** Just as a going out in harvest during scorching heat helps, so a faithful messenger helps those who sent him; for he helps the souls of those who employ him.

**25:14** Just as winds and clouds and rains are most conspicuous, so is the one who boasts of a false gift.

**25:15** In patience there is prosperity for kings, and a soft tongue crushes bones.

**25:16** When you find honey, eat what is sufficient, so that you do not become sated and vomit.

**25:17** Rarely bring your foot to your own friend, so that he does not become sated with you and hate you.

**25:18** A club and a sword and a pointed arrow—so also is a man who gives false testimony against his friend.

**25:19** The way of an evil person and the foot of a lawless person will perish on an evil day.

**25:20** Just as vinegar is harmful to a wound, so an affliction falling on the body grieves the heart. Just as a moth in a garment and a worm in wood, so the grief of a man harms the heart.

**25:21** If your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he is thirsty, give him a drink.

**25:22** For by doing this you will pile coals of fire on his head, and the Lord will repay you with good things.

**25:23** A north wind stirs up clouds, and a shameless face provokes a tongue.

**25:24** It is better to live on a corner of a roof than with a reviling woman in a shared house.

**25:25** Just as cold water is pleasant to a thirsty soul, so is a good report from a land far away.

**25:26** Just as if someone were to block up a spring and ruin an outlet of water, so it is unseemly for a righteous person to have fallen before an ungodly person.

**25:27** To eat much honey is not good, but one must honor glorious words.

**25:28** Just as a city with its walls torn down and unwalled, so is a man who does anything without counsel.

## Translator Notes

- **25:1** — Greek hai adiakritoi literally means 'the undistinguished' or 'the unseparated,' often understood as 'unarranged' or 'obscure,' and has no counterpart in the Hebrew Masoretic Text. The Greek also reads 'friends' (a royal court title) rather than 'men' and 'Judea' rather than 'Judah.'
- **25:12** — The Greek syntax is compact, literally reading 'is fastened for a golden earring and a precious sardius, a wise word to an attentive ear,' using a predicative construction to equate a wise word with precious adornment.
- **25:13** — The Greek reads exodos ('a going out,' 'exit,' or 'outlet,' compare 25:26) rather than 'snow' as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **25:20** — The Greek diverges substantially from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, pairing the comparison of vinegar on a wound with a second saying about a moth in a garment and a worm in wood.
