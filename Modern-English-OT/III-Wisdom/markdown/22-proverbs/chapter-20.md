## Text

**20:1** Wine is unrestrained and drunkenness is insolent, and every fool gets entangled in such things.

**20:2** A king's threat does not differ from a lion's wrath, and the one who provokes him sins against his own soul.

**20:3** It is glory for a man to turn away from insults, but every fool gets entangled in such things.

**20:4** When reproached, a lazy man is not ashamed; likewise also the one who borrows grain at harvest.

**20:5** Counsel in a man's heart is deep water, yet a prudent man will draw it out.

**20:6** A human being is a great thing, and a merciful man is precious, but a faithful man is a hard task to find.

**20:7** The one who conducts himself blamelessly in righteousness will leave his children blessed.

**20:8** Whenever a righteous king sits on a throne, nothing evil stands in opposition before his eyes.

**20:9** Who will boast of having a pure heart? Or who will boldly claim to be clean from sins? The lamp of the one who speaks evil of father or mother will be extinguished, and the pupils of his eyes will see darkness. A portion hastily pursued at first will not be blessed in the end. Do not say, "I will take vengeance on the enemy," but wait for the Lord, so that He may help you.

**20:10** A large weight and a small one, and double measures: both of them are unclean before the Lord.

**20:11** And the one who makes them will be tripped up in his practices; a young man is with a devout person, and his way is straight.

**20:12** An ear hears and an eye sees; both of them are works of the Lord.

**20:13** Do not love to slander, so that you may not be removed; open your eyes wide and be filled with bread.

**20:23** A double weight is an abomination to the Lord, and a deceitful scale is not good before Him.

**20:24** From the Lord a man's steps are directed, and how could a mortal understand his ways?

**20:25** It is a snare for a man to consecrate something of his own hastily, for after making a vow, regret occurs.

**20:26** A wise king is a winnower of the ungodly, and he will lay a wheel upon them.

**20:27** The breath of human beings is the light of the Lord, who searches the inner chambers of the belly.

**20:28** Mercy and truth are a guard for a king, and they will surround his throne in righteousness.

**20:29** Wisdom is an ornament for young men, and gray hair is the glory of older men.

**20:30** Bruises and fractures meet evil people, and blows reach into the inner chambers of the belly.

## Translator Notes

- **20:6** — Greek 'a human being is a great thing' renders Hebrew 'many a man' literally; 'a hard task' translates the Greek idiom ergon with an infinitive.
- **20:9** — In the Septuagint, the three sayings corresponding to Hebrew 20:20-22 are placed immediately after 20:9, while Hebrew 20:14-19 are absent from the Greek text.
- **20:11** — The Greek syntax and punctuation are difficult; following the critical text's punctuation, 'the one who makes them' is the subject of 'will be tripped up,' though other editions attach that phrase to verse 10 and take 'a young man with a devout person' as the subject.
- **20:27** — The Greek masculine singular relative pronoun ('who') specifically identifies the Lord as the one who searches the inner chambers, whereas the Hebrew phrasing can refer to the breath or lamp.
