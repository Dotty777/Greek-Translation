## Text

**8:1** You will proclaim wisdom, so that prudence may heed you.

**8:2** For she is on the high summits, and she stands in the midst of the paths;

**8:3** for beside the gates of rulers she sits, and at the entrances she is praised.

**8:4** "To you, O men, I call, and I send out my voice to the sons of men.

**8:5** Understand shrewdness, you naive ones, and you uninstructed ones, place a heart within yourselves.

**8:6** Listen to me, for I will speak noble things, and I will bring out right things from my lips.

**8:7** Because my throat will meditate on truth, and lying lips are detested before me.

**8:8** All the words of my mouth are with righteousness; there is nothing crooked or twisted in them.

**8:9** All are plain to those who understand, and right to those who find knowledge.

**8:10** Receive instruction and not silver, and knowledge rather than tested gold.

**8:11** For wisdom is better than costly stones, and nothing precious is worthy of her.

**8:12** I, wisdom, dwelt in counsel, and I called upon knowledge and thought; for wisdom is better than costly stones.

**8:13** The fear of the Lord hates unrighteousness, both insolence and pride, and the ways of wicked people; and I have hated the perverted ways of evil people.

**8:14** Mine are counsel and security; mine is prudence, and mine is strength.

**8:15** Through me kings reign, and rulers decree righteousness.

**8:16** Through me nobles are made great, and sovereigns through me rule the earth.

**8:17** I love those who cherish me, and those who seek me will find me.

**8:18** Wealth and glory belong to me, and the possession of many things and righteousness.

**8:19** It is better to reap fruit from me than gold and precious stone, and my produce is better than choice silver.

**8:20** I walk in the ways of righteousness, and in the midst of the paths of justice I go about,

**8:21** so that I may apportion property to those who love me, and fill their treasuries with good things. If I declare to you the things that happen day by day, I will remember to recount the things from of old.

**8:22** The Lord created me as the beginning of His ways for His works;

**8:23** before the age He founded me in the beginning, before He made the earth,

**8:24** and before He made the depths, before the springs of the waters emerged,

**8:25** before mountains were settled, and before all hills He brings me forth.

**8:26** The Lord made lands and uninhabited places, and the inhabited heights of the world under the heavens.

**8:27** When He was preparing heaven, I was present with Him, and when He was setting apart His own throne upon the winds.

**8:28** And as He was making the clouds above strong, and as He was making the springs of the world under heaven secure,

**8:29** and as He was making the foundations of the earth strong,

**8:30** I was beside Him, fitting things together; I was the one in whom He delighted, and day by day I rejoiced in His presence at every time,

**8:31** when He rejoiced, having completed the inhabited world, and He rejoiced in the sons of men.

**8:32** Now therefore, son, listen to me.

**8:34** Blessed is the man who will listen to me, and the person who will keep my ways, staying awake at my doors day by day, guarding the posts of my entrances.

**8:35** For my exits are exits of life, and goodwill is prepared by the Lord.

**8:36** But those who sin against me act impiously against their own souls, and those who hate me love death."

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Unlike the Hebrew rhetorical question ("Does not wisdom call?"), the Greek addresses the reader directly with a second-person statement.
- **8:21** — The second sentence ("If I declare to you...") is a Septuagint-distinctive transition into Wisdom's creation discourse; verse 8:33 is absent in the Greek text.
- **8:22** — Greek ἔκτισεν means "created" (whereas the corresponding Hebrew verb can also mean "acquired/possessed"), and in 8:25 Greek γεννᾷ ("brings forth") notably uses the present tense.
- **8:30** — Greek ἁρμόζουσα means "fitting things together" (as an artisan) or "being in harmony." In 8:31, the masculine participle συντελέσας makes the Lord, rather than Wisdom, the one rejoicing after completing the inhabited world.
- **8:35** — Greek ἔξοδοι ("exits" or "ways out") mirrors "entrances" in 8:34 and reflects reading the underlying Hebrew root as "go out" rather than "find."
