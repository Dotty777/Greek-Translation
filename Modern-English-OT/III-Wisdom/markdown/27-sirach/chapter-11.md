## Text

**11:1** The wisdom of a humble person lifts up his head, and will seat him in the midst of nobles.

**11:2** Do not praise a man for his beauty, and do not detest a person for his appearance.

**11:3** The bee is small among flying creatures, yet its fruit is the chief of sweet things.

**11:4** Do not boast in the wearing of garments, and in a day of glory do not exalt yourself; for the works of the Lord are wonderful, and His works are hidden among people.

**11:5** Many monarchs have sat on the ground, and the unsuspected one has worn a diadem.

**11:6** Many mighty men have been greatly dishonored, and renowned men have been delivered into the hands of companions.

**11:7** Before you investigate, do not find fault; understand first, and then rebuke.

**11:8** Before you listen, do not answer, and in the midst of words do not interrupt.

**11:9** Do not quarrel about a matter of which you have no need, and do not sit in council in the judgment of sinners.

**11:10** Child, let your activities not be about many things; if you multiply them, you will not be held guiltless; and if you pursue, you will by no means overtake, and you will by no means escape by fleeing.

**11:11** There is one who toils and labors and hurries, and so much the more falls short.

**11:12** There is one who is sluggish and in need of help, lacking in strength, and he abounds in poverty; yet the eyes of the Lord looked upon him for good, and He raised him up from his humiliation,

**11:13** and He lifted up his head, and many marveled at him.

**11:14** Good and bad, life and death, poverty and wealth are from the Lord.

**11:17** The gift of the Lord remains with the godly, and His good pleasure will prosper forever.

**11:18** There is one who grows rich from his carefulness and stinginess, and this is the portion of his reward:

**11:19** When he says, "I have found rest, and now I will eat from my good things," he does not know what time will pass, and he will leave them to others and die.

**11:20** Stand firm in your covenant and attend to it, and grow old in your work.

**11:21** Do not marvel at the works of a sinner; trust in the Lord and remain in your labor; for it is an easy thing in the eyes of the Lord to make a poor person rich swiftly, suddenly.

**11:22** The blessing of the Lord is in the reward of the godly, and in a swift hour He causes His blessing to flourish.

**11:23** Do not say, "What is my need, and what good things will be mine from now on?"

**11:24** Do not say, "I have enough, and how will I be harmed from now on?"

**11:25** In a day of good things there is forgetfulness of bad things, and in a day of bad things one will not remember good things.

**11:26** For it is an easy thing before the Lord on the day of death to repay a person according to his ways.

**11:27** The affliction of an hour causes forgetfulness of luxury, and at a person's end is the revelation of his works.

**11:28** Before death do not call anyone blessed, and a man will be known by his children.

**11:29** Do not bring every person into your house, for many are the ambushes of the deceitful.

**11:30** A decoy partridge in a basket: so is the heart of the arrogant, and like a spy he watches for a fall.

**11:31** For he lies in ambush, turning good things into bad, and on choice things he will place a blemish.

**11:32** From a spark of fire, burning coals will be multiplied, and a sinful person lies in ambush for blood.

**11:33** Beware of an evildoer, for he devises wicked things, so that he may not give you a blemish forever.

**11:34** Lodge a stranger, and he will upset you with troubles and estrange you from your own people.

## Translator Notes

- **11:6** — The Greek text reads hetairon ("companions"), whereas other witnesses read the near-homophonous heteron ("others").
- **11:14** — The pair "good and bad" refers here to good fortune and misfortune rather than moral evil. Verses 15-16, found in later expanded Greek manuscripts, are absent from this shorter Greek text.
- **11:28** — The Greek reads "by his children" (en teknois autou), reflecting a rendering of the Hebrew term for "end" or "posterity" as offspring.
- **11:30** — Literally "a hunting partridge in a basket," referring to a caged decoy bird used by fowlers to lure wild partridges into a snare.
