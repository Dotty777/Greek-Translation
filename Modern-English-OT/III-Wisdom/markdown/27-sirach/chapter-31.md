## Text

**31:1** Sleeplessness over wealth wastes away the flesh, and anxiety about it drives away sleep.

**31:2** Anxiety of sleeplessness will demand slumber, and a severe sickness will dispel sleep.

**31:3** A rich man has labored in gathering riches, and in rest he is filled with his luxuries.

**31:4** A poor man has labored in scarcity of livelihood, and in rest he becomes needy.

**31:5** The one who loves gold will not be justified, and the one who pursues corruption will himself be filled.

**31:6** Many have been given over to downfall for the sake of gold, and their destruction came before their face.

**31:7** It is a wooden stumbling block for those who sacrifice to it, and every fool will be caught in it.

**31:8** Blessed is a rich man who has been found blameless, and who has not gone after gold.

**31:9** Who is he, and we will call him blessed? For he has done wondrous things among his people.

**31:10** Who has been tested by it and made perfect? And let it be for boasting. Who was able to transgress and did not transgress, and to do evil things and did not do them?

**31:11** His goods will be established, and the assembly will recount his acts of charity.

**31:12** Have you sat at a great table? Do not open your throat over it, and do not say, "Many indeed are the things on it!"

**31:13** Remember that an evil eye is a bad thing; what has been created more evil than an eye? Therefore it weeps from every face.

**31:14** Wherever he looks, do not stretch out your hand, and do not crowd against him in the dish.

**31:15** Understand the things of your neighbor from yourself, and reflect on every matter.

**31:16** Eat the things set before you like a human being, and do not chew greedily, so that you may not be hated.

**31:17** Stop first for the sake of discipline, and do not be insatiable, so that you may never give offense.

**31:18** And if you have sat down among many, do not stretch out your hand before them.

**31:19** How sufficient is a little for a disciplined person, and on his bed he does not gasp for breath.

**31:20** Healthy sleep comes upon a moderate stomach; he rises early, and his soul is with him. The pain of sleeplessness and bilious nausea, and colic, are with an insatiable man.

**31:21** And if you have been pressed with delicacies, get up and leave midway, and you will find rest.

**31:22** Listen to me, child, and do not despise me, and in the end you will find my words. In all your works be diligent, and no sickness will ever meet you.

**31:23** Lips will bless one who is generous with bread, and the testimony to his excellence is trustworthy.

**31:24** A city will grumble against one who is stingy with bread, and the testimony to his stinginess is accurate.

**31:25** Do not act the hero with wine, for wine has destroyed many.

**31:26** A furnace tests tempered steel in the quenching, so wine tests hearts in the strife of the arrogant.

**31:27** Wine is equal to life for a person, if you drink it in its measure. What life is there for one lacking in wine? And it has been created for gladness for people.

**31:28** Exultation of heart and gladness of soul is wine drunk at the right time in sufficient amount.

**31:29** Bitterness of soul is wine drunk in abundance, with provocation and conflict.

**31:30** Drunkenness multiplies the anger of a fool to stumbling, diminishing strength and adding wounds.

**31:31** At a banquet of wine do not rebuke your neighbor, and do not despise him in his gladness; do not speak a word of reproach to him, and do not distress him with a demand.

## Translator Notes

- **31:2** — The Greek text reads 'apaitesei' ('will demand [slumber]'), whereas other witnesses read a verb meaning 'drives away'; 'eknepsei' literally means 'will sober away' or 'dispel.'
- **31:5** — The Greek text reads 'diaphthoran' ('corruption') and nominative 'autos plesthesetai' ('he himself will be filled'), while other witnesses read 'will be filled with it' or 'will be led astray.'
- **31:13** — 'An evil eye' is an idiom for greed or stinginess; 'from every face' literally renders a Hebrew idiom meaning 'at everything' or 'in every direction.'
- **31:21** — Greek 'mesoporon' literally means 'going midway,' an expression for excusing oneself in the middle of a banquet to find relief from overeating.
- **31:24** — Greek 'poneros' and 'poneria' (literally 'evil') carry the idiomatic sense of 'stingy' and 'stinginess' when used in connection with sharing bread.
