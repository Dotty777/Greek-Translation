## Text

**6:3** You will devour your leaves and destroy your fruits, and you will leave yourself like a dry tree.

**6:4** An evil soul will destroy the one who possesses it, and will make him an object of gloating to enemies.

**6:5** A sweet throat will multiply its friends, and a well-spoken tongue will multiply courteous greetings.

**6:6** Let those who are at peace with you be many, but let your counselors be one out of a thousand.

**6:7** If you gain a friend, gain him through testing, and do not quickly trust him.

**6:8** For there is a friend in his own time, and he will by no means remain in the day of your distress.

**6:9** And there is a friend who turns to enmity, and he will uncover a quarrel to your reproach.

**6:10** And there is a friend who is a companion at tables, and he will by no means remain in the day of your distress.

**6:11** And in your prosperity he will be like you, and he will speak boldly over your household servants.

**6:12** If you are brought low, he will be against you, and he will hide himself from your face.

**6:13** Separate yourself from your enemies, and beware of your friends.

**6:14** A faithful friend is a strong shelter, and the one who has found him has found a treasure.

**6:15** For a faithful friend there is no equivalent, and there is no weight for his excellence.

**6:16** A faithful friend is a medicine of life, and those who fear the Lord will find him.

**6:17** The one who fears the Lord will direct his friendship rightly, because as he is, so also is his neighbor.

**6:18** Child, from your youth choose instruction, and until gray hairs you will find wisdom.

**6:19** Like the one who plows and the one who sows, approach her, and wait for her good fruits; for in her work you will toil a little, and quickly you will eat of her produce.

**6:20** How exceedingly swift she is to the uninstructed, and one without sense will not remain in her.

**6:21** Like a heavy stone of testing she will be upon him, and he will not delay to cast her aside.

**6:22** For wisdom is according to her name, and she is not manifest to many.

**6:23** Listen, child, and receive my judgment, and do not reject my counsel.

**6:24** And put your feet into her fetters, and your neck into her collar.

**6:25** Put your shoulder under and carry her, and do not chafe at her bonds.

**6:26** With all your soul approach her, and with all your might keep her ways.

**6:27** Search her out and seek her, and she will be made known to you; and when you have taken hold of her, do not let her go.

**6:28** For in the end you will find her rest, and she will be turned into gladness for you.

**6:29** And the fetters will become for you a shelter of strength, and her branches a robe of glory.

**6:30** For a golden ornament is upon her, and her bonds are a blue cord.

**6:31** You will put her on as a robe of glory, and you will place a crown of exultation upon yourself.

**6:32** If you are willing, child, you will be instructed, and if you give your soul, you will be shrewd.

**6:33** If you love to listen, you will receive, and if you incline your ear, you will be wise.

**6:34** Stand in the company of elders; and who is wise? Cling to him.

**6:35** Be willing to listen to every godly discourse, and do not let proverbs of understanding escape you.

**6:36** If you see a man of understanding, rise early to go to him, and let your foot wear out the steps of his doors.

**6:37** Reflect on the statutes of the Lord, and meditate on His commandments continually; He Himself will establish your heart, and your desire for wisdom will be given to you.

## Translator Notes

- **6:20** — The Greek text reads tacheia ('swift', as in major uncials such as Vaticanus), whereas other witnesses and editions read tracheia ('rough/harsh'). The adjective akardios literally means 'without heart', an idiom for lacking sense or understanding.
- **6:22** — The statement that wisdom is 'according to her name' preserves literally a Hebrew wordplay in the underlying text (linking discipline/instruction with what is hidden or turned aside) that does not carry over directly into Greek.
- **6:29** — The Greek text reads kladoi ('branches'), whereas other witnesses read kloioi ('collars', echoing v. 24).
- **6:37** — The Greek text includes the possessive pronoun sou in he epithymia tes sophias sou, translated 'your desire for wisdom' (taking the possessive with the whole phrase, as in Semitic construct idioms); some witnesses omit sou.
