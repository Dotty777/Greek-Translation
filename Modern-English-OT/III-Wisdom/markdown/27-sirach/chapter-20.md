## Text

**20:1** There is a reproof that is not timely, and there is one who keeps silent, and he is prudent.

**20:2** How good it is to reprove rather than to be angry,

**20:3** and the one who confesses will be kept from loss.

**20:4** A eunuch's desire to deflower a young woman—so is the one who executes judgments by violence.

**20:5** There is one who keeps silent and is found wise, and there is one who is hated because of much talk.

**20:6** There is one who keeps silent, for he has no answer, and there is one who keeps silent, knowing the right time.

**20:7** A wise person will remain silent until the right time, but the braggart and senseless one will overstep the right time.

**20:8** The one who is excessive in speech will be detested, and the one who assumes authority for himself will be hated.

**20:9** There is success in misfortunes for a man, and there is a find that leads to loss.

**20:10** There is a gift that will not profit you, and there is a gift whose repayment is double.

**20:11** There is loss on account of glory, and there is one who has lifted his head from humiliation.

**20:12** There is one who buys many things for little and pays for them sevenfold.

**20:13** The wise person will make himself endearing in speech, but the favors of fools will be poured out.

**20:14** The gift of a senseless person will not profit you, for his eyes are many instead of one.

**20:15** He will give little and reproach much, and he will open his mouth like a herald; today he will lend and tomorrow he will demand it back—such a person is hateful.

**20:16** A fool will say, "I have no friend, and there is no gratitude for my good deeds; those who eat my bread are wicked in tongue."

**20:17** How often and how many will laugh at him?

**20:18** A slip on the floor rather than with the tongue; so the fall of the wicked will come swiftly.

**20:19** An ungracious person is an untimely tale; it will be continually in the mouth of the undisciplined.

**20:20** From the mouth of a fool a proverb will be rejected, for he will by no means speak it at its right time.

**20:21** There is one who is kept from sinning by poverty, and in his rest he will not be stung with remorse.

**20:22** There is one who destroys his soul through shame, and because of a senseless person he will destroy it.

**20:23** There is one who makes a promise to a friend out of shame, and has gained him as an enemy for nothing.

**20:24** A lie is an evil blemish in a person; it will be continually in the mouth of the undisciplined.

**20:25** A thief is preferable to one who persists in lying, yet both will inherit destruction.

**20:26** The character of a lying person is dishonor, and his shame is with him continually.

**20:27** Words of proverbs. The wise person will advance himself in words, and a prudent person will please the great.

**20:28** The one who works the land will raise high his heap of grain, and the one who pleases the great will atone for wrongdoing.

**20:29** Presents and gifts blind the eyes of the wise, and like a muzzle in the mouth, they turn away reproofs.

**20:30** Hidden wisdom and unseen treasure—what benefit is there in both?

**20:31** Better is a person who hides his folly than a person who hides his wisdom.

## Translator Notes

- **20:8** — "Assumes authority for himself" translates the rare Greek compound middle participle ἐνεξουσιαζόμενος, denoting one who arrogates authority to himself.
- **20:14** — Literally "his eyes are many instead of one," a vivid idiom depicting a stingy giver who greedily expects multiple returns for a single gift (cf. v. 15).
- **20:22** — "Because of a senseless person" translates ἀπὸ ἄφρονος προσώπου (literally "from a senseless face/person"), which may refer either to deference to a fool or to foolish concern for appearances.
- **20:27** — "Words of proverbs." renders the Greek section heading Λόγοι παραβολῶν, which is embedded directly in verse 27 in major Septuagint witnesses.
