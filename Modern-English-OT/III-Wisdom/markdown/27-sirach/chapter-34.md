## Text

**34:1** Empty and false hopes belong to a man without understanding, and dreams give wings to fools.

**34:2** Like one grasping a shadow and chasing the wind, so is the one who pays attention to dreams.

**34:3** This opposite that is the vision of dreams: the likeness of a face in front of a face.

**34:4** From what is unclean, what will be made clean? And from what is false, what will be true?

**34:5** Divinations and omens and dreams are futile, and the heart forms fancies like that of a woman in labor.

**34:6** Unless they are sent from the Most High in a visitation, do not give your heart to them.

**34:7** Dreams have led many astray, and they fell away while hoping in them.

**34:8** Without falsehood the law will be fulfilled, and wisdom in a faithful mouth is fulfillment.

**34:9** An instructed man knows many things, and one of much experience will declare understanding.

**34:10** He who has not been tested knows little, but the one who has traveled will multiply shrewdness.

**34:11** I have seen many things in my travels, and my understanding is more than my words.

**34:12** Many times I was in danger to the point of death, and I was rescued because of these things.

**34:13** The spirit of those who fear the Lord will live, for their hope is upon the One who saves them.

**34:14** The one who fears the Lord will never be afraid and will never lose heart, because He Himself is his hope.

**34:15** Blessed is the soul of the one who fears the Lord. On whom does he rely? And who is his support?

**34:16** The eyes of the Lord are upon those who love Him: a protection of might and a support of strength, a shelter from scorching heat and a shelter from midday, a guard from stumbling and a help from falling.

**34:17** He raises up the soul and enlightens the eyes, giving healing, life, and blessing.

**34:18** When one sacrifices from what is unjust, the offering is mocked, and the mockeries of the lawless are not pleasing.

**34:19** The Most High is not pleased with the offerings of the ungodly, nor by a multitude of sacrifices does He forgive sins.

**34:20** One who sacrifices a son before his father is the one who brings a sacrifice from the property of the poor.

**34:21** The bread of the needy is the life of the poor; the one who withholds it is a man of bloodshed.

**34:22** One who murders a neighbor is the one who takes away a livelihood, and one who sheds blood is the one who withholds the wages of a hired worker.

**34:23** When one builds and one tears down, what have they gained more than toil?

**34:24** When one prays and one curses, whose voice will the Master hear?

**34:25** If one washes himself from a dead body and touches it again, what has he gained by his washing?

**34:26** So is a man who fasts for his sins and goes again and does the same things: who will hear his prayer? And what has he gained by humbling himself?

## Translator Notes

- **34:3** — Literally "this opposite that," comparing a dream to an insubstantial mirror reflection of a face opposite a face.
- **34:9** — The Greek text reads "instructed" (πεπαιδευμένος), whereas some witnesses read "traveled" (πεπλανημένος), matching the vocabulary of vv. 10-11.
- **34:10** — Greek πεπλανημένος (and ἀποπλάνησις in v. 11) literally denotes wandering or roaming, used here in the positive sense of traveling widely abroad.
- **34:18** — The Greek text reads μεμωκημένη and μωκήματα ("mocked" and "mockeries," from μωκάομαι), while some manuscripts read μεμωμημένη and μωμήματα ("blemished" and "blemishes").
