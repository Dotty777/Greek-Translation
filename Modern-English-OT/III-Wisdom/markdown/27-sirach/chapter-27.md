## Text

**27:1** For the sake of a trifling thing many have sinned, and the one who seeks to increase will turn away his eye.

**27:2** Between the joints of stones a peg will be fixed fast, and between selling and buying sin will be squeezed in.

**27:3** Unless he holds fast with diligence in the fear of the Lord, his house will quickly be overthrown.

**27:4** In the shaking of a sieve the rubbish remains; so does a man's refuse in his reasoning.

**27:5** A furnace tests a potter's vessels, and the test of a man is in his discussion.

**27:6** The fruit of a tree reveals its cultivation; so does the speech of the thought of a man's heart.

**27:7** Do not praise a man before his reasoning, for this is the test of people.

**27:8** If you pursue justice, you will overtake it, and you will put it on like a long robe of glory.

**27:9** Birds will roost with their own kind, and truth will return to those who practice it.

**27:10** A lion lies in wait for prey; so do sins for those who practice injustice.

**27:11** The discourse of a godly man is always wisdom, but the foolish man changes like the moon.

**27:12** In the company of those without understanding, watch the time, but in the company of a thoughtful man, stay continually.

**27:13** The discourse of fools is an offense, and their laughter is in the wantonness of sin.

**27:14** The talk of one who swears many oaths will make hair stand on end, and their strife is a stopping of the ears.

**27:15** The strife of the proud is a shedding of blood, and their mutual reviling is a grievous thing to hear.

**27:16** The one who reveals secrets has destroyed trust, and he will never find a friend after his own soul.

**27:17** Love a friend and keep faith with him; but if you reveal his secrets, never pursue after him.

**27:18** For just as a man has destroyed his enemy, so you have destroyed the friendship of your neighbor.

**27:19** And as you have released a bird from your hand, so you have let your neighbor go, and you will not catch him.

**27:20** Do not pursue him, for he has gone far away, and has escaped like a gazelle from a snare.

**27:21** For a wound can be bound up, and for reviling there is reconciliation; but the one who has revealed secrets has lost hope.

**27:22** One who winks with the eye devises evil things, and no one will withdraw himself from him.

**27:23** Before your eyes he will sweeten your mouth and he will marvel at your words, but afterward he will twist his mouth and in your words he will set a stumbling block.

**27:24** I have hated many things, yet I have compared none to him, and the Lord will hate him.

**27:25** The one who throws a stone up high throws it upon his own head, and a treacherous blow will deal out wounds.

**27:26** The one who digs a pit will fall into it, and the one who sets a snare will be caught in it.

**27:27** The one who does evil—it will roll upon him, and he will never know from where it has come to him.

**27:28** Mockery and reproach belong to the proud, and vengeance will lie in wait for him like a lion.

**27:29** Those who rejoice at the fall of the godly will be caught in a snare, and pain will consume them before their death.

**27:30** Resentment and anger, these too are abominations, and a sinful man will hold fast to them.

## Translator Notes

- **27:1** — The Greek text reads adiaphorou ('an indifferent/trifling thing'); some manuscripts read diaphorou ('profit/gain'), which directly continues the commercial theme of 26:29-27:2.
- **27:2** — The Greek verb syntribēsetai normally means 'be crushed' or 'rubbed together,' here used in the sense of being tightly squeezed or wedged in, parallel to the peg between stones.
- **27:6** — The Greek text reads the genitive enthymēmatos ('the speech of the thought of a man's heart'), implying that speech reveals inner cultivation; some manuscripts read the accusative enthymēma ('so does speech reveal the thought of a man's heart').
- **27:22** — The Greek text reads the reflexive hauton ('no one will withdraw himself from him'); reading non-reflexive auton gives 'no one will turn him away from it' (i.e., from evil).
- **27:23** — The Greek text reads 'your mouth' (stoma sou), whereas some manuscripts read 'his mouth' (to stoma autou), matching the second half of the verse.
