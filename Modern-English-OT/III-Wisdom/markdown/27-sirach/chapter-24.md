## Text

**24:1** Praise of Wisdom. Wisdom will praise her own soul, and in the midst of her people she will boast.

**24:2** In the assembly of the Most High she will open her mouth, and before His host she will boast:

**24:3** "I came forth from the mouth of the Most High, and like a mist I covered the earth.

**24:4** I made my dwelling in the heights, and my throne was in a pillar of cloud.

**24:5** I alone went around the circuit of heaven, and in the depth of the abysses I walked.

**24:6** In the waves of the sea and in all the earth, and among every people and nation I gained possession.

**24:7** Among all these I sought a resting place, and in whose inheritance will I lodge?

**24:8** Then the Creator of all things commanded me, and He who created me caused my tent to rest and said, 'Make your dwelling in Jacob, and receive your inheritance in Israel.'

**24:9** Before the age, from the beginning, He created me, and forever I will never cease.

**24:10** In the holy tent I ministered before Him, and so in Zion I was established.

**24:11** In the beloved city He likewise caused me to rest, and in Jerusalem is my authority.

**24:12** And I took root among an honored people, in the portion of the Lord, His inheritance.

**24:13** Like a cedar I grew tall in Lebanon, and like a cypress on the mountains of Hermon.

**24:14** Like a palm tree I grew tall on the shores, and like rose plants in Jericho; like a stately olive tree in a plain, and I grew tall like a plane tree.

**24:15** Like cinnamon and aspalathus I have given forth a scent of spices, and like choice myrrh I spread abroad a sweet fragrance, like galbanum and onycha and stacte, and like the vapor of frankincense in the tent.

**24:16** I, like a terebinth, stretched out my branches, and my branches are branches of glory and grace.

**24:17** I am like a vine that sprouted grace, and my blossoms are fruit of glory and wealth.

**24:19** Come to me, you who desire me, and be filled from my produce.

**24:20** For the remembrance of me is sweeter than honey, and my inheritance than a honeycomb.

**24:21** Those who eat me will still hunger, and those who drink me will still thirst.

**24:22** The one who obeys me will not be put to shame, and those who work in me will not sin."

**24:23** All these things are the book of the covenant of the Most High God, the law that Moses commanded as an inheritance for the congregations of Jacob,

**24:25** which overflows with wisdom like the Pishon, and like the Tigris in the days of new fruits;

**24:26** which runs full with understanding like the Euphrates, and like the Jordan in the days of harvest;

**24:27** which shows forth instruction like light, like the Gihon in the days of grape harvest.

**24:28** The first one did not finish knowing her, and so the last one did not trace her out.

**24:29** For her thought is more abundant than the sea, and her counsel than the great abyss.

**24:30** And I, like a canal from a river and like a water conduit, went out into an orchard.

**24:31** I said, "I will water my garden, and I will drench my garden bed." And look, the canal became a river for me, and my river became a sea.

**24:32** Still I will make instruction shine like the dawn, and I will show these things forth even far away.

**24:33** Still I will pour out teaching like prophecy, and I will leave it for eternal generations.

**24:34** See that I have not labored for myself alone, but for all who seek it out.

## Translator Notes

- **24:2** — Greek dynameos can mean either "host" (the heavenly army, parallel to "assembly") or "power."
- **24:6** — The Greek text reads ektesamen ("I gained possession"); some commentators emend to hegesamen ("I held sway").
- **24:14** — The Greek text reads en aigialois ("on the shores"), whereas some later manuscripts and versions read the place name "En-gedi."
- **24:25** — The masculine participles in vv. 25-27 resume "the law" (Greek nomos, masculine) from v. 23, though some refer them to God; v. 24 is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
- **24:27** — The Greek reads hos phos ("like light"), often thought to reflect Hebrew ke'or ("like light") rather than ki'or ("like the Nile").
