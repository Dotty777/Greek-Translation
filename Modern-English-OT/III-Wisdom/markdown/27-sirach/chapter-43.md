## Text

**43:1** The pride of the height is the expanse of purity, the appearance of heaven in a vision of glory.

**43:2** The sun, in its appearing, proclaiming at its going out, is a marvelous vessel, a work of the Most High.

**43:3** At its noon it dries up the land, and before its heat, who will endure?

**43:4** One tends a furnace in works of heat; three times more the sun burns up mountains, breathing out fiery vapors, and shining out rays, it dims the eyes.

**43:5** Great is the Lord who made it, and by His words He hastened its course.

**43:6** And the moon in all things is at its appointed time, an indication of times and an everlasting sign.

**43:7** From the moon is the sign of a festival, a luminary waning upon completion.

**43:8** The month is according to its name, increasing marvelously in its changing; a vessel of parables in the height, shining out in the expanse of heaven.

**43:9** The beauty of heaven is the glory of the stars, an illuminating ornament; in the highest places is the Lord.

**43:10** By holy words they will stand according to judgment, and they will never grow weary in their watches.

**43:11** Look at the rainbow, and bless Him who made it; it is exceedingly beautiful in its radiance.

**43:12** It encircled heaven in a circle of glory; the hands of the Most High stretched it out.

**43:13** By His command He caused the snow to cease, and He speeds the lightnings of His judgment.

**43:14** Therefore storehouses were opened, and clouds flew out like birds.

**43:15** In His majesty He strengthened the clouds, and stones of hail were shattered.

**43:16** And at His appearings mountains will be shaken; by His will the south wind will blow.

**43:17** The voice of His thunder reproached the earth, as did a tempest of the north and a whirling of wind. Like birds flying down He sprinkles snow, and like a locust settling down is its descent.

**43:18** At the beauty of its whiteness the eye will marvel, and at its rain the heart will be astonished.

**43:19** And He pours out frost like salt upon the earth, and when frozen, it becomes the points of thorns.

**43:20** A cold north wind will blow, and ice will freeze from water; upon every gathering of water it will settle, and the water will clothe itself as with a breastplate.

**43:21** It will devour mountains and burn up the wilderness, and it will extinguish greenery like fire.

**43:22** A healing of all things in haste is a mist; dew arriving after scorching heat will bring cheer.

**43:23** By His counsel He stilled the abyss, and Jesus planted it.

**43:24** Those who sail the sea recount its danger, and at the hearing of our ears we marvel.

**43:25** And there are extraordinary and marvelous works, a variety of every living creature, the creation of sea monsters.

**43:26** Because of Him a sweet aroma is His end, and by His word all things hold together.

**43:27** We will say many things and will never reach the end, and the conclusion of words is: "He is all."

**43:28** In glorifying Him, where will we have strength? For He is the Great One beyond all His works.

**43:29** Awesome is the Lord and exceedingly great, and marvelous is His might.

**43:30** Glorifying the Lord, exalt Him as much as you are able, for He will surpass even more; and exalting Him, increase in strength; do not grow weary, for you will never reach the end.

**43:31** Who has seen Him and will describe Him? And who will magnify Him as He is?

**43:32** Many hidden things are greater than these, for we have seen few of His works.

**43:33** For the Lord made all things, and to the godly He gave wisdom.

## Translator Notes

- **43:8** — The Greek text reads "a vessel of parables" (skeuos parabolon), following major uncial manuscripts, whereas other Greek witnesses read "of the armies" or "encampments" (parembolon).
- **43:9** — The Greek text reads "the Lord" in the nominative case (Kyrios), yielding "in the highest places is the Lord," whereas some manuscripts read the genitive (Kyriou), "in the heights of the Lord."
- **43:17** — The Greek text reads "reproached" (oneidisen), whereas some critical editions adopt the variant "made writhe in labor" (odinesen).
- **43:23** — The Greek text literally reads "and Jesus planted it" (ephyteusen auten Iesous), a well-known reading of major Septuagint manuscripts arising from an early scribal misreading of "planted islands in it" (en aute nesous).
- **43:26** — The Greek text reads "a sweet aroma is His end" (euodia telos autou), matching major uncial manuscripts, whereas other witnesses read "His messenger prospers" (euodoi angelos autou).
