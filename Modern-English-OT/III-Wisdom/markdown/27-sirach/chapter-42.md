## Text

**42:1** Do not be ashamed about these things, and do not show partiality so as to sin:

**42:2** about the law of the Most High and the covenant, and about judgment to justify the ungodly,

**42:3** about an accounting with a partner and travelers, about the giving of an inheritance of companions,

**42:4** about accuracy of scales and weights, about acquiring much and little,

**42:5** about indifference in selling and with merchants, and about much discipline of children, and making the side of a wicked servant bleed;

**42:6** over a wicked wife a seal is good, and where there are many hands, lock up;

**42:7** whatever you hand over, let it be by number and weight, and giving and receiving for everyone in writing;

**42:8** about the discipline of a senseless person and a fool, and of one in extreme old age who contends with the young; and you will be truly disciplined and approved before every living one.

**42:9** A daughter is a secret sleeplessness to a father, and anxiety over her drives away sleep: in her youth, for fear that she may pass her prime, and when married, for fear that she may be hated;

**42:10** in virginity, for fear that she may be defiled and become pregnant in her father's house; when with a husband, for fear that she may transgress, and when married, for fear that she may be barren.

**42:11** Over a headstrong daughter strengthen the watch, so that she may not make you a laughingstock to enemies, talk in the city and a public summons of the people, and she will put you to shame in a crowd of many.

**42:12** Do not gaze at any person for beauty, and do not sit in the midst of women;

**42:13** for from garments a moth comes out, and from a woman, the wickedness of a woman.

**42:14** Better is the wickedness of a man than a woman who does good, and a woman who brings shame to reproach.

**42:15** Now I will remember the works of the Lord, and what I have seen I will recount; by the words of the Lord are His works.

**42:16** The shining sun looks upon all things, and His work is full of His glory.

**42:17** The Lord has not enabled the holy ones to recount all His wonders, which the Lord Almighty established, for the universe to be made firm in His glory.

**42:18** He has searched out the abyss and the heart, and I have reflected on their cunning devices; for the Lord knows all knowledge and has looked into the sign of the age,

**42:19** declaring the things that have passed and the things that are to come, and revealing the traces of secret things;

**42:20** no thought has passed Him by, not even one word has been hidden from Him.

**42:21** He has set in order the great works of His wisdom; and as long as He exists, before the age and forever, He has neither been added to nor diminished, and He has not needed any counselor.

**42:22** How desirable are all His works, and like a spark they are to look upon!

**42:23** All these things live and remain forever in all needs, and all things obey;

**42:24** all things are twofold, one opposite the other, and He has made nothing incomplete;

**42:25** one establishes the good things of the other, and who will be satisfied seeing His glory?

## Translator Notes

- **42:2** — Greek dikaiosai ton asebe ('to justify the ungodly') is legal language for declaring someone in the right; in context it either connects back to 'do not show partiality so as to sin' (42:1) or refers to judging impartially even when the verdict favors an ungodly person in a particular dispute.
- **42:5** — Greek adiaphorou (lit. 'indifferent' or 'an indifferent thing') is used in Sirach (cf. 7:18) for impartial dealing or monetary price/gain in trade, closely related to diaphoron ('price, profit').
- **42:18** — The Greek text reads the first-person dienoethen ('I have reflected on'), a well-attested early manuscript reading alongside the third-person dienoethe ('He reflected on/understood') in other witnesses.
- **42:21** — The Greek text reads kai heos estin ('and as long as He exists'), a manuscript reading corresponding to hos estin ('as He is') in other Greek witnesses.
- **42:22** — The Greek phrase hos spintheros estin theoresai (lit. 'as of a spark it is to look upon') is difficult; the genitive spintheros may express that human observation catches only a spark-like glimpse of God's works or may reflect heos ('even down to a spark').
