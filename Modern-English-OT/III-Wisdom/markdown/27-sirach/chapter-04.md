## Text

**4:1** Child, do not withhold the livelihood of the poor person, and do not keep needy eyes waiting.

**4:2** Do not grieve a hungry soul, and do not provoke a man to anger in his distress.

**4:3** Do not further trouble a heart provoked to anger, and do not delay a gift to one in need.

**4:4** Do not reject an afflicted suppliant, and do not turn your face away from a poor person.

**4:5** From one in need do not turn your eye away, and do not give a person occasion to curse you;

**4:6** for when he curses you in the bitterness of his soul, the One who made him will hear his petition.

**4:7** Make yourself dear to the assembly, and lower your head to a great man.

**4:8** Incline your ear to a poor person, and answer him peaceably in gentleness.

**4:9** Rescue one who is wronged from the hand of a wrongdoer, and do not be fainthearted when you judge.

**4:10** Be like a father to orphans, and in place of a husband to their mother; and you will be like a son of the Most High, and He will love you more than your mother does.

**4:11** Wisdom has exalted sons for herself, and takes hold of those who seek her.

**4:12** The one who loves her loves life, and those who rise early for her will be filled with gladness.

**4:13** The one who holds fast to her will inherit glory, and wherever he enters, the Lord will bless.

**4:14** Those who serve her will minister to the Holy One, and the Lord loves those who love her.

**4:15** The one who obeys her will judge nations, and the one who comes near to her will dwell in confidence.

**4:16** If you trust, you will inherit her, and his generations will be in possession;

**4:17** for she walks with him crookedly at first, and she will bring fear and dread upon him, and she will torment him with her discipline until she trusts his soul; and she will test him with her ordinances;

**4:18** and again she will return straight to him and will gladden him, and will reveal to him her hidden things.

**4:19** If he goes astray, she will forsake him, and will deliver him into the hands of his downfall.

**4:20** Observe the proper time and guard yourself from evil, and do not be ashamed concerning your soul;

**4:21** for there is a shame that brings sin, and there is a shame that is glory and favor.

**4:22** Do not show partiality against your soul, and do not feel shame to your downfall.

**4:23** Do not hold back speech in a time of salvation;

**4:24** for in speech wisdom will be known, and discipline in the utterance of the tongue.

**4:25** Do not speak against the truth, and feel shame concerning your lack of discipline.

**4:26** Do not be ashamed to confess your sins, and do not force the current of a river.

**4:27** And do not spread yourself under a foolish person, and do not show partiality to a ruler.

**4:28** Until death contend for the truth, and the Lord God will fight for you.

**4:29** Do not be harsh in your tongue, and sluggish and slack in your works.

**4:30** Do not be like a lion in your house, and given to wild fancies among your household servants.

**4:31** Do not let your hand be stretched out to receive, and drawn back when repaying.

## Translator Notes

- **4:16** — The Greek text shifts abruptly from second person singular ("If you trust, you will inherit her") to third person singular ("his generations"), continuing in third person through vv. 17-19.
- **4:22** — "Show partiality" renders the Greek idiom λάβῃς πρόσωπον (literally "accept a face," also in v. 27), meaning to show deference or favoritism.
- **4:29** — The Greek text reads τραχὺς ("harsh" or "rough"); several other manuscripts read ταχύς ("quick" or "hasty").
- **4:30** — The rare Greek compound φαντασιοκοπῶν ("given to wild fancies") refers to behaving capriciously, wildly, or with paranoid suspicions toward one's household.
