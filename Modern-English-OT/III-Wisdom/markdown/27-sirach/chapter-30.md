## Text

**30:1** Concerning children. The one who loves his son will continually apply lashes to him, so that he may be glad at his end.

**30:2** The one who disciplines his son will benefit from him, and among acquaintances he will boast about him.

**30:3** The one who teaches his son will provoke the enemy to jealousy, and before friends he will exult in him.

**30:4** His father died, and even so he did not die, for he left behind one like himself after him.

**30:5** In his life he saw and was glad, and at his death he was not grieved.

**30:6** Against enemies he left behind an avenger, and for friends one who repays favor.

**30:7** For the souls of sons he will bind up his wounds, and at every cry his inward parts will be troubled.

**30:8** An untamed horse turns out stubborn, and an unrestrained son becomes headstrong.

**30:9** Pamper a child, and he will terrify you; play with him, and he will grieve you.

**30:10** Do not laugh with him, so that you may not suffer anguish with him, and in the end you will have your teeth set on edge.

**30:11** Do not give him authority in youth.

**30:12** Bruise his sides while he is a young child, so that he may never become hardened and disobey you.

**30:13** Discipline your son and work on him, so that he may not stumble in your disgrace.

**30:14** Better is a poor man who is healthy and strong in constitution than a rich man scourged in his body.

**30:15** Health and a sound constitution are better than all gold, and a robust body than immeasurable riches.

**30:16** There is no wealth better than health of body, and there is no gladness above joy of heart.

**30:17** Better is death than a bitter life or a lasting sickness.

**30:18** Good things poured out upon a closed mouth are a portion of food placed upon a grave.

**30:19** What does a burnt offering benefit an idol? For it will neither eat nor smell at all; so is the one who is driven away by the Lord,

**30:20** looking with his eyes and groaning, just like a eunuch embracing a virgin and groaning.

**30:21** Do not give your soul to grief, and do not distress yourself in your counsel.

**30:22** Gladness of heart is a person's life, and a man's exultation is length of days.

**30:23** Love your soul and comfort your heart, and put grief far away from yourself; for grief has killed many, and there is no profit in it.

**30:24** Jealousy and anger shorten days, and anxiety brings old age before its time.

**30:25** A bright and good heart over delicacies will attend to its food.

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — The Greek manuscript text includes the section heading 'Concerning children' at the start of the verse.
- **30:4** — The Greek text reads accented 'kai hos' ('and even so'); some editions accent it as 'hos' ('as though he did not die').
- **30:7** — The Greek text reads 'peri psychon huion' ('for the souls of sons'), reflecting an ancient word-division difference from 'peripsychon huion' ('pampering a son').
- **30:13** — The Greek text reads 'in your disgrace he may stumble'; other textual witnesses read 'at his shameful conduct you may stumble.'
- **30:25** — Greek 'lampra kardia' ('a bright heart') denotes a cheerful or generous disposition, and 'epimelesetai' ('will attend to') conveys caring for or enjoying its food. From here through the end of chapter 36 the English keeps the Saints.Bible verse slots shared with the Latin edition (the restored chapter order); the Saints Greek Critical Text follows the manuscript order (Swete), so its verse numbers differ throughout this section.
