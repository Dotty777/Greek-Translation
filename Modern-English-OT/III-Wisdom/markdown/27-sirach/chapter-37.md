## Text

**37:1** Every friend will say, "I too became a friend to him"; but there is a friend who is a friend in name only.

**37:2** Is not a companion and friend turning to enmity a grief even to death?

**37:3** O evil imagination, from where were you rolled in to cover the dry land with deceit?

**37:4** A companion takes delight in a friend's gladness, and in a time of affliction he will be in opposition.

**37:5** A companion labors together with a friend for the sake of the belly; in the face of war he will take up a shield.

**37:6** Do not forget a friend in your soul, and do not be unmindful of him in your wealth.

**37:7** Every counselor extols counsel, but there is one who gives counsel for himself.

**37:8** Guard your soul from a counselor, and know first what his need is—for indeed he will take counsel for himself—for fear that he may cast a lot against you,

**37:9** and say to you, "Your way is good," and he will stand opposite to see what will happen to you.

**37:10** Do not take counsel with the one who looks suspiciously at you, and hide counsel from those who are jealous of you:

**37:11** with a woman concerning her rival, and with a coward about war, and with a merchant about trade, and with a buyer about a sale, with an envious person about gratitude, and with a merciless person about kindness, with a lazy person about any work, and with a nonresident hired worker about completion, an idle household servant about much labor—do not rely on these for any counsel.

**37:12** Rather, associate continually with a godly man, whom you recognize as keeping commandments, who in his soul is like your soul, and if you stumble, he will grieve with you.

**37:13** And establish the counsel of your heart, for there is no one more faithful to you than it.

**37:14** For a man's soul is sometimes accustomed to report better than seven watchmen sitting on a high place at a lookout.

**37:15** And above all these things, pray to the Most High that He may make your way straight in truth.

**37:16** The beginning of every work is a word, and before every action is counsel.

**37:17** A trace of change is in the heart;

**37:18** four parts spring up: good and evil, life and death, and what continually rules over them is the tongue.

**37:19** There is a man who is shrewd and an instructor of many, yet to his own soul he is useless.

**37:20** There is one who acts wise in words and is hated; this one will lack all food.

**37:21** For grace was not given to him from the Lord, because he was deprived of all wisdom.

**37:22** There is one who is wise for his own soul, and the fruits of his understanding are trustworthy upon his mouth.

**37:23** A wise man will instruct his own people, and the fruits of his understanding are trustworthy.

**37:24** A wise man will be filled with blessing, and all who see him will call him blessed.

**37:25** The life of a man is in a number of days, and the days of Israel are innumerable.

**37:26** The wise man will inherit trust among his people, and his name will live forever.

**37:27** Child, in your life test your soul, and see what is bad for it, and do not give that to it.

**37:28** For not all things are beneficial for all, and not every soul takes pleasure in everything.

**37:29** Do not be insatiable in any luxury, and do not pour yourself out over foods.

**37:30** For in many foods there will be pain, and insatiability will draw near to bilious nausea.

**37:31** Because of insatiability many have died, but the one who takes heed will add life.

## Translator Notes

- **37:6** — The Greek text reads "in your soul," whereas some witnesses and the Hebrew read "in battle" (reflecting two readings of the underlying Hebrew term).
- **37:11** — The Greek omits the preposition "concerning" before "her rival," using a bare genitive of reference. "Nonresident hired worker" renders Greek misthiou aphestiou ("away from the hearth/home"), where other manuscripts read epeteiou ("yearly").
- **37:14** — The Greek omits an explicit comparative adverb ("better" or "more") before "than," forming an elliptical comparative idiom.
- **37:17** — The Greek critical text prints the dative kardia ("in the heart"), yielding "A trace of change is in the heart," while some editions accent it as nominative ("the heart is the trace of change").
- **37:30** — Greek cholera here denotes a violent bilious illness or colic rather than modern Asiatic cholera.
