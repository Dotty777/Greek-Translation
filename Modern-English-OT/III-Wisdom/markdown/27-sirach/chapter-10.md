## Text

**10:1** A wise judge will discipline his people, and the leadership of a person of understanding will be well-ordered.

**10:2** As the judge of his people is, so also are his ministers, and as the leader of the city is, so are all who inhabit it.

**10:3** An undisciplined king will destroy his people, and a city will be populated through the understanding of rulers.

**10:4** In the hand of the Lord is authority over the earth, and He will raise up the suitable one over it at the proper time.

**10:5** In the hand of the Lord is the success of a man, and upon the face of a scribe He will place His glory.

**10:6** For every wrong, do not hold a grudge against your neighbor, and do nothing in deeds of insolence.

**10:7** Arrogance is hateful before the Lord and people, and from both it will commit unjust things.

**10:8** A kingdom is transferred from nation to nation because of injustices and acts of insolence and money.

**10:9** Why are earth and ashes arrogant? Because in life I cast out his inward parts.

**10:10** A long illness—a physician mocks it; and one is a king today, and tomorrow he will die.

**10:11** For when a person dies, he will inherit creeping things and wild beasts, worms.

**10:12** The beginning of a person's arrogance is departing from the Lord, and his heart has departed from Him who made him.

**10:13** For the beginning of arrogance is sin, and the one who holds fast to it will pour forth abomination. Because of this, the Lord made the calamities extraordinary and overthrew them completely.

**10:14** The Lord pulled down the thrones of rulers and seated the meek in their place.

**10:15** The Lord plucked up the roots of nations and planted the humble in their place.

**10:16** The Lord overthrew the lands of nations and destroyed them to the foundations of the earth.

**10:17** He withered some of them and destroyed them, and caused their memory to cease from the earth.

**10:18** Arrogance was not created for human beings, nor furious anger for those born of women.

**10:19** What kind of offspring is honorable? Human offspring. What kind of offspring is honorable? Those who fear the Lord. What kind of offspring is dishonorable? Human offspring. What kind of offspring is dishonorable? Those who transgress commandments.

**10:20** In the midst of brothers, their leader is honorable, and those who fear the Lord are honorable in His eyes.

**10:22** Rich and distinguished and poor—their boast is the fear of the Lord.

**10:23** It is not right to dishonor a poor person of understanding, and it is not fitting to glorify a sinful man.

**10:24** A nobleman and a judge and a ruler will be glorified, and not one of them is greater than the one who fears the Lord.

**10:25** Free people will serve a wise slave, and a man of knowledge will not grumble.

**10:26** Do not pretend to be wise in doing your work, and do not glorify yourself in the time of your distress.

**10:27** Better is one who works in all things than one who walks about, or one who glorifies himself and lacks bread.

**10:28** Child, in meekness glorify your soul, and give it honor according to its worth.

**10:29** Who will justify the one who sins against his own soul? And who will glorify the one who dishonors his own life?

**10:30** A poor person is glorified because of his knowledge, and a rich person is glorified because of his wealth.

**10:31** Now the one who is glorified in poverty, how much more in wealth? And the one who is without glory in wealth, how much more in poverty?

## Translator Notes

- **10:7** — The Greek text given here reads plemmelesi adika ("it will commit unjust things"), a difficult variant alongside plemmeles he adikia ("injustice is offensive") found in some other editions.
- **10:9** — The Greek literally uses the first-person singular erripsa ("I cast out"), either portraying God as speaker or reflecting a difficult Greek rendering of the Hebrew thought that a person's inward parts decay even in life.
- **10:10** — The terse Greek clause makron arrostema, skoptei iatros literally reads "a long illness, a physician mocks" (or "scoffs at"), differing from the Hebrew manuscript tradition, which speaks of a slight illness baffling the physician.
- **10:20** — Verse 10:21 is absent from the main Greek text (appearing only in the expanded Greek tradition), so the verse numbering proceeds directly from 10:20 to 10:22.
- **10:27** — This Greek text reads "works in all things" and "or one who glorifies himself," whereas some other witnesses add "and abounds" before "in all things" and read "and" before "glorifies himself."
