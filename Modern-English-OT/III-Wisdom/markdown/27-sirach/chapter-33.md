## Text

**33:1** Evil will not come upon the one who fears the Lord, but in testing he will be delivered yet again.

**33:2** A wise man will not hate the law, but the one who acts hypocritically in it is like a ship in a storm.

**33:3** A discerning man will trust in the law, and the law is trustworthy to him like an inquiry of the righteous.

**33:4** Prepare your speech and then you will be heard; bind instruction together and answer.

**33:5** The heart of a fool is a cart wheel, and his reasoning is like a turning axle.

**33:6** A breeding stallion is like a mocking friend; he neighs under everyone who sits on him.

**33:7** Why does one day surpass another, when all the daylight of the year is from the sun?

**33:8** By the knowledge of the Lord they were distinguished, and He varied seasons and festivals.

**33:9** Some of them He exalted and sanctified, and some of them He appointed to the number of days.

**33:10** And all people are from the ground, and Adam was created from the earth.

**33:11** In the abundance of knowledge the Lord distinguished them and varied their ways.

**33:12** Some of them He blessed and exalted, and some of them He sanctified and brought near to Himself; some of them He cursed and brought low, and overturned them from their position.

**33:13** As a potter's clay is in his hand, all its ways according to his good pleasure, so are people in the hand of the One who made them, to render to them according to His judgment.

**33:14** Opposite evil is good, and opposite death is life; so opposite a godly person is a sinner.

**33:15** And in this way look upon all the works of the Most High: two by two, one opposite the other.

**33:17** Like one gleaning behind grape-gatherers, by the blessing of the Lord I got ahead,

**33:18** and like a grape-gatherer I filled a winepress. Consider that I did not labor for myself alone, but for all who seek instruction.

**33:19** Hear me, great men of the people, and leaders of the assembly, give ear.

**33:20** To son and wife, brother and friend, do not give authority over yourself in your lifetime; and do not give your possessions to another, so that you do not feel regret and beg for them.

**33:21** While you are still alive and breath is in you, do not exchange yourself for any flesh.

**33:22** For it is better for your children to beg from you than for you to look to the hands of your sons.

**33:23** In all your works be preeminent; do not put a blemish on your honor.

**33:24** On the day of the completion of the days of your life, and at the time of death, distribute your inheritance.

**33:25** Fodder and a rod and burdens for a donkey; bread and discipline and work for a household servant.

**33:26** Put a servant to work, and you will find rest; leave his hands free, and he will seek freedom.

**33:27** A yoke and a strap will bend a neck, and for a wicked household servant, racks and tortures.

**33:28** Put him to work so that he may not be idle, for idleness teaches much wickedness.

**33:29** Set him to tasks as is fitting for him, and if he does not obey, make his shackles heavy.

**33:30** And do not go to excess with any flesh, and without judgment do nothing.

**33:31** If you have a household servant, let him be like you, because you acquired him with blood.

**33:32** If you have a household servant, treat him as yourself, because, like your soul, there is need for him; if you mistreat him and he departs and runs away, on what road will you seek him?

## Translator Notes

- **33:1** — Greek exeleitai is a middle form that can be read passively ('he will be delivered') or actively with the Lord as implied subject ('He will deliver him').
- **33:3** — The Greek text here reads dikaion ('of the righteous'), whereas some editions adopt the reading delon ('of the Urim').
- **33:17** — In this critical text the line numbered 33:16 in some editions ('And I was the last to keep watch') stands at the seam of the transposed sections and is rendered at 36:10, so 33:17 opens directly with the participial comparison.
- **33:31** — Greek en haimati ('with blood') literally renders a Hebrew idiom for 'with money' or 'at a price,' while in Greek also conveying a costly acquisition.
- **33:32** — This critical text reads the noun epideesis with the dative auto ('there is need for him'), whereas many other witnesses read the verb epideeseis autou ('you will have need of him'). In the Saints.Bible numbering, this verse (native Greek 30:40) spans 33:32–33.
