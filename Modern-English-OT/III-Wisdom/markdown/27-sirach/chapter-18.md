## Text

**18:1** He who lives forever created all things together.

**18:2** The Lord alone will be justified.

**18:4** He has enabled no one to declare His works, and who will trace out His great deeds?

**18:5** Who will count the might of His majesty? And who will go on to recount His mercies?

**18:6** It is not possible to diminish or to add, and it is not possible to trace out the wonders of the Lord.

**18:7** When a man finishes, then he begins, and when he stops, then he will be perplexed.

**18:8** What is man, and what is his usefulness? What is his good, and what is his evil?

**18:9** The number of a man's days is at most a hundred years.

**18:10** Like a drop of water from the sea and a grain of sand, so are a few years in a day of eternity.

**18:11** For this reason the Lord was patient with them, and poured out His mercy upon them.

**18:12** He saw and recognized that their ruin was grievous; for this reason He multiplied His atonement.

**18:13** The mercy of a man is upon his neighbor, but the mercy of the Lord is upon all flesh; He reproves and disciplines and teaches and brings back, as a shepherd does his flock.

**18:14** He has mercy on those who accept discipline and those who hasten to His judgments.

**18:15** Child, in good deeds do not bring a blemish, nor in any gift grief of words.

**18:16** Will not dew relieve the scorching heat? So a word is better than a gift.

**18:17** Look, is not a word above a good gift? And both are with a gracious man.

**18:18** A fool will reproach ungraciously, and the gift of a grudging man wastes away the eyes.

**18:19** Before speaking, learn, and before sickness, take care of yourself.

**18:20** Before judgment, examine yourself, and in the hour of visitation you will find atonement.

**18:21** Before you become sick, humble yourself, and in a time of sins, show repentance.

**18:22** Do not be hindered from paying a vow in due time, and do not wait until death to be justified.

**18:23** Before making a vow, prepare yourself, and do not become like a man testing the Lord.

**18:24** Remember wrath in the days of death, and the time of vengeance at the turning away of the face.

**18:25** Remember a time of famine in a time of plenty, poverty and need in days of wealth.

**18:26** From morning until evening time changes, and all things are swift before the Lord.

**18:27** A wise man will be cautious in everything, and in days of sins he will guard against wrongdoing.

**18:28** Everyone with understanding has come to know wisdom, and to the one who finds it he will give praise.

**18:29** Those with understanding in words have themselves also become wise, and have poured forth precise proverbs.

**18:30** Self-control of the soul. Do not go after your desires, and restrain yourself from your appetites.

**18:31** If you supply your soul with the pleasure of desire, it will make you an object of gloating to your enemies.

**18:32** Do not delight in great luxury; do not be bound to its expense.

**18:33** Do not become poor by feasting on borrowed money, when you have nothing in your purse.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — Greek koine ('in common' or 'together') likely reflects Hebrew yahdaw ('all alike' or 'without exception'), and was also traditionally understood to mean 'all at once.'
- **18:9** — Greek literally reads 'many, a hundred years' (polla ete hekaton), an idiom meaning 'at most a hundred years' or 'many if they reach a hundred years.'
- **18:23** — Greek euxasthai can mean 'to make a vow' (continuing the subject of v. 22) or 'to pray.'
- **18:30** — 'Self-control of the soul.' is an ancient section heading preserved directly in the Greek manuscript tradition.
- **18:32** — Greek symbolei refers to a 'contribution' or 'share of the expense' (especially for a shared feast; cf. symbolokopon, 'feasting,' in v. 33), though it can also mean 'association.'
