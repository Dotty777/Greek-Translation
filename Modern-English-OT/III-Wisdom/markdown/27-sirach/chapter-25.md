## Text

**25:1** In three things I was made beautiful, and I stood forth beautiful before the Lord and people: harmony of brothers, and friendship of neighbors, and a wife and a husband who accommodate each other.

**25:2** But my soul hated three kinds, and I greatly loathed their life: an arrogant poor man, and a lying rich man, an old adulterer lacking in understanding.

**25:3** In youth you have not gathered, and how could you find anything in your old age?

**25:4** How beautiful is judgment for gray hair, and for elders to know counsel!

**25:5** How beautiful is the wisdom of the aged, and thought and counsel for those who are honored!

**25:6** The crown of the aged is much experience, and their boast is the fear of the Lord.

**25:7** Nine thoughts I called blessed in my heart, and the tenth I will speak on my tongue: a man rejoicing over children, living and looking upon the fall of enemies.

**25:8** Blessed is the one who lives with an understanding wife, and he who has not slipped with his tongue, and he who has not served one unworthy of him.

**25:9** Blessed is he who has found prudence, and the one who speaks into the ears of those who listen.

**25:10** How great is the one who has found wisdom, yet there is no one above the one who fears the Lord.

**25:11** The fear of the Lord has surpassed everything; to whom will the one who holds fast to it be compared?

**25:13** Any wound, but not a wound of the heart; and any wickedness, but not the wickedness of a woman;

**25:14** any affliction, but not the affliction of those who hate; and any vengeance, but not the vengeance of enemies.

**25:15** There is no head above the head of a serpent, and there is no wrath above the wrath of an enemy.

**25:16** I would rather live with a lion and a dragon than dwell with a wicked woman.

**25:17** The wickedness of a woman alters her appearance, and darkens her face like sackcloth.

**25:18** Among his neighbors her husband will recline, and when he hears, he groans bitterly.

**25:19** Any evil is small compared to the evil of a woman; may the lot of a sinner fall upon her!

**25:20** A sandy climb under the feet of an elderly man, so is a talkative wife to a quiet husband.

**25:21** Do not fall before the beauty of a woman, and do not long for a woman.

**25:22** There is anger and shamelessness and great shame if a wife supports her husband.

**25:23** A downcast heart and a gloomy face and a wound of the heart is a wicked wife; drooping hands and feeble knees is she who will not make her husband happy.

**25:24** From a woman is the beginning of sin, and because of her we all die.

**25:25** Do not give water an outlet, nor a wicked wife authority.

**25:26** If she does not walk according to your hand, cut her off from your flesh.

## Translator Notes

- **25:1** — Greek horaia ("beautiful") is feminine singular, probably referring to the speaker's soul (cf. v. 2) or to personified Wisdom from ch. 24.
- **25:7** — Although ten beatitudes are announced, the Greek text lists nine items across vv. 7-10, culminating in the fear of the Lord; v. 12 is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
- **25:15** — Greek kephale ("head") renders Hebrew rosh, which means both "head" and "venom/poison."
- **25:17** — The Greek text reads hos sakkon ("like sackcloth"), whereas the Hebrew and some witnesses read "like a bear."
- **25:18** — The Greek text reads akousas ("when he hears"), whereas some manuscripts read akousios ("involuntarily").
