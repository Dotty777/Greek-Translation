## Text

**14:1** Blessed is the man who has not slipped with his mouth, and has not been pierced by the grief of sin.

**14:2** Blessed is the one whom his soul has not condemned, and who has not fallen from his hope.

**14:3** Wealth is not fitting for a stingy man, and what is the use of possessions to a grudging person?

**14:4** The one who gathers from his own soul gathers for others, and others will live in luxury on his good things.

**14:5** The one who is evil to himself, to whom will he be good? And he will by no means rejoice in his possessions.

**14:6** There is no one more evil than the one who begrudges himself, and this is the repayment of his wickedness.

**14:7** And even if he does good, he does it in forgetfulness, and in the end he reveals his wickedness.

**14:8** Evil is the one who begrudges with his eye, turning away his face and overlooking souls.

**14:9** The eye of a greedy person is not satisfied with a portion, and evil injustice will dry up a soul.

**14:10** An evil eye is envious over bread, and is lacking at his table.

**14:11** Child, according to what you have, do good to yourself, and bring offerings to the Lord worthily.

**14:12** Remember that death will not delay, and the decree of Hades has not been shown to you.

**14:13** Before you die, do good to a friend, and according to your strength reach out and give to him.

**14:14** Do not miss out on a good day, and may a portion of a good desire not pass you by.

**14:15** Will you not leave your labors to another, and your toils for division by lot?

**14:16** Give and receive, and indulge your soul, because it is not possible in Hades to seek luxury.

**14:17** All flesh grows old like a garment; for the decree from of old is, "You will surely die."

**14:18** Like a flourishing leaf on a thick tree, some it sheds, and others it sprouts; so is a generation of flesh and blood: one dies, and another is born.

**14:19** Every decaying work fails, and the one who makes it will depart with it.

**14:20** Blessed is the man who will die in wisdom, and who will reason in his understanding.

**14:21** The one who considers her ways in his heart will also gain understanding in her secrets.

**14:22** Go out after her like a tracker, and lie in wait at her entrances.

**14:23** The one who peers through her windows will also listen at her doorways.

**14:24** The one who lodges close to her house will also drive a peg into her walls.

**14:25** He will pitch his tent beside her, and he will lodge in a lodging place of good things.

**14:26** He will place his children in her shelter, and he will camp beneath her branches.

**14:27** He will be sheltered by her from heat, and he will lodge in her glory.

## Translator Notes

- **14:4** — Greek synagon apo tes psyches autou (lit. 'gathering from his own soul') is an idiom for hoarding by depriving or stinting oneself.
- **14:16** — Greek apateson (ordinarily 'deceive' or 'beguile') is used here in its Hellenistic sense of 'indulge,' 'entertain,' or 'treat' oneself.
- **14:20** — The Greek text reads teleutesei ('will die' or 'will end his life'), whereas some Greek witnesses and the Hebrew read 'will meditate' (meletesei).
- **14:25** — Greek kata cheiras autes (lit. 'by her hands') is a Semitic idiom meaning 'beside her' or 'at her side.'
