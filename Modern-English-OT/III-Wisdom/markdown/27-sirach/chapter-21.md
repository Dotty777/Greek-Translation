## Text

**21:1** Child, have you sinned? Do not add to it anymore, and pray concerning your former sins.

**21:2** As from the face of a serpent, flee from sin; for if you approach, it will bite you. Its teeth are the teeth of a lion, destroying the souls of people.

**21:3** All lawlessness is like a two-edged sword; for its wound there is no healing.

**21:4** Intimidation and insolence will lay waste to wealth; so the house of a proud person will be laid waste.

**21:5** The prayer of a poor person reaches from the mouth to His ears, and His judgment comes swiftly.

**21:6** One who hates reproof is in the footsteps of a sinner, but the one who fears the Lord will turn back in heart.

**21:7** The one who is mighty in tongue is known from afar, but the discerning person knows when he slips.

**21:8** The one who builds his house with the money of others is like one who gathers his stones for winter.

**21:9** Tow gathered together is a gathering of lawless ones, and their end is a flame of fire.

**21:10** The way of sinners is made smooth with stones, yet at its end is the pit of Hades.

**21:11** The one who keeps the law masters his thinking, and the completion of the fear of the Lord is wisdom.

**21:12** One who is not shrewd will not be instructed; there is a shrewdness that multiplies bitterness.

**21:13** The knowledge of a wise person will increase like a flood, and his counsel is like a spring of life.

**21:14** The inward parts of a fool are like a shattered vessel, and he will not hold any knowledge.

**21:15** If a knowledgeable person hears a wise word, he will praise it and add to it; the self-indulgent person heard it and it displeased him, and he cast it behind his back.

**21:16** The discourse of a fool is like a burden on a journey, but grace will be found on the lip of an understanding person.

**21:17** The mouth of a prudent person will be sought in the assembly, and one will ponder his words in the heart.

**21:18** Like a ruined house, so is wisdom to a fool, and the knowledge of one lacking understanding is unexamined words.

**21:19** Discipline is fetters on senseless feet, and like shackles on the right hand.

**21:20** A fool raises his voice high in laughter, but a shrewd man will scarcely smile quietly.

**21:21** Like a golden ornament to a prudent person is discipline, and like a bracelet on the right arm.

**21:22** The foot of a fool is quick into a house, but a well-experienced person will show modesty before another's face.

**21:23** A senseless person peers from the door into a house, but a disciplined man will stand outside.

**21:24** It is lack of discipline for a person to listen by the door, but the prudent person will be weighed down with dishonor.

**21:25** The lips of strangers will be weighed down in these things, but the words of the prudent will be weighed on a scale.

**21:26** In the mouth of fools is their heart, but the heart of the wise is their mouth.

**21:27** When an ungodly person curses Satan, he himself curses his own soul.

**21:28** A whisperer defiles his own soul, and he will be hated in the neighborhood.

## Translator Notes

- **21:5** — Neither occurrence of αὐτοῦ has an explicit divine antecedent in Greek; the first ("His ears") refers to God, while the second ("His judgment") may refer either to God's swift judgment or to the poor person's own vindication.
- **21:8** — Greek εἰς χειμῶνα literally reads "for winter" (possibly referring to stockpiling stones uselessly for winter storms, or reflecting an underlying Hebrew wordplay or variant for a burial mound).
- **21:25** — "Will be weighed down" translates βαρυνθήσεται (repeating the verb of v. 24 and contrasting with "weighed on a scale"); some Greek manuscripts instead read διηγήσεται ("will recount").
- **21:26** — The critical Greek text reads the nominative καρδία ("the heart of the wise is their mouth"), whereas some manuscripts read the dative ἐν καρδίᾳ ("in the heart of the wise is their mouth").
- **21:27** — Greek τὸν σατανᾶν (literally "the adversary" or "Satan") transliterates Hebrew שָׂטָן and may refer either to Satan or to a personal adversary.
