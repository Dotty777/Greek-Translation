## Text

**2:1** Child, if you come forward to serve the Lord God, prepare your soul for testing.

**2:2** Direct your heart and be steadfast, and do not be hasty in a time of calamity.

**2:3** Cling to Him and do not depart, so that you may be increased at your end.

**2:4** Accept whatever is brought upon you, and be patient in the changes of your humiliation.

**2:5** For gold is tested in fire, and acceptable people in the furnace of humiliation.

**2:6** Trust Him, and He will help you; direct your ways and hope in Him.

**2:7** You who fear the Lord, wait for His mercy, and do not turn aside, so that you do not fall.

**2:8** You who fear the Lord, trust Him, and your reward will certainly not fail.

**2:9** You who fear the Lord, hope for good things, and for gladness of eternity and of mercy.

**2:10** Look at the ancient generations and see: who trusted in the Lord and was put to shame? Or who remained in His fear and was forsaken? Or who called upon Him, and He overlooked him?

**2:11** For the Lord is compassionate and merciful, and He forgives sins and saves in a time of distress.

**2:12** Woe to timid hearts and slack hands, and to a sinner treading on two paths.

**2:13** Woe to a slack heart, because it does not trust; therefore it will not be sheltered.

**2:14** Woe to you who have lost endurance; and what will you do when the Lord visits?

**2:15** Those who fear the Lord will not disobey His words, and those who love Him will keep His ways.

**2:16** Those who fear the Lord will seek His good pleasure, and those who love Him will be filled with the law.

**2:17** Those who fear the Lord will prepare their hearts, and before Him they will humble their souls.

**2:18** We will fall into the hands of the Lord and not into the hands of people; for as His majesty is, so also is His mercy.

## Translator Notes

- **2:4** — 'Changes' renders ἀλλάγμασιν, referring to shifting circumstances or reversals during humiliation.
- **2:9** — Both 'eternity' and 'mercy' (αἰῶνος καὶ ἐλέους) are Greek genitives modifying 'gladness,' expressing enduring joy grounded in God's mercy.
- **2:14** — 'Visits' renders ἐπισκέπτηται, a biblical idiom for God coming in inspection, reckoning, or judgment.
- **2:18** — Greek uses the future indicative ἐμπεσούμεθα ('We will fall'), expressing firm resolve (often understood cohortatively as 'Let us fall'), echoing 2 Samuel 24:14.
