## Text

**40:1** Great toil has been created for every person, and a heavy yoke is upon the sons of Adam, from the day of coming forth from their mother's womb until the day for burial into the mother of all—

**40:2** their thoughts and fear of heart, contemplation of expectation, the day of death—

**40:3** from the one sitting on a throne in glory even to the one humbled in earth and ashes,

**40:4** from the one wearing blue-purple and a crown even to the one clothed in raw linen:

**40:5** fury and jealousy and turmoil and agitation and fear of death and resentment and strife. And in the time of rest on a bed, the sleep of night alters his knowledge.

**40:6** Little, as nothing, is he in rest, and from then on in sleep he is as on a day of watch, troubled in the vision of his heart like one who has escaped from the face of battle;

**40:7** at the time of his deliverance he wakes up, and marvels that there is no fear.

**40:8** With all flesh, from human to beast, and upon sinners sevenfold in addition to these:

**40:9** death and blood and strife and sword—

**40:10** for the lawless all these things were created, and because of them the flood came about.

**40:11** All things that are from earth return to earth, and what is from waters turns back to the sea.

**40:12** Every bribe and injustice will be blotted out, and faithfulness will stand forever.

**40:13** The wealth of the unjust will dry up like a river, and like great thunder in rain it will resound.

**40:14** When he opens his hands he will rejoice; so those who transgress will come to a complete end.

**40:15** The offspring of the ungodly will not multiply branches, and unclean roots are upon sheer rock.

**40:16** Reed grass beside every water and riverbank will be plucked up before all grass.

**40:17** Kindness is like a garden in blessings, and almsgiving will endure forever.

**40:18** The life of a self-sufficient laborer will be sweet, and above both is the one who finds a treasure.

**40:19** Children and the building of a city establish a name, and above both a blameless wife is esteemed.

**40:20** Wine and music gladden the heart, and above both is the love of wisdom.

**40:21** Flute and harp make melodies pleasant, and above both is a pleasant tongue.

**40:22** Your eye will desire grace and beauty, and above both, the green shoots of a crop.

**40:23** A friend and a companion meet at the right time, and above both is a wife with a husband.

**40:24** Brothers and help are for a time of distress, and above both, almsgiving will deliver.

**40:25** Gold and silver will make the foot stand firm, and above both a wife is approved.

**40:26** Wealth and strength will lift up the heart, and above both is the fear of the Lord. There is no lack in the fear of the Lord, and in it there is no need to seek help.

**40:27** The fear of the Lord is like a garden of blessing, and above all glory they have covered it.

**40:28** Child, do not live a life of begging; it is better to die than to beg.

**40:29** A man looking to another's table—his living is not in the reckoning of life; he will pollute his soul with another's foods. But a knowledgeable and instructed man will guard himself.

**40:30** In the mouth of a shameless person begging will be sweet, and in his belly a fire will burn.

## Translator Notes

- **40:1** — This Greek text reads "for burial" (epi taphe), whereas some other witnesses read "of return" (epistrophes).
- **40:9** — This Greek text ends after "sword," without the additional list of calamities found in some other witnesses.
- **40:14** — The obscure Greek phrase "when he opens his hands" likely arose from a Greek rendering of a Hebrew image about a torrent lifting rocks, reading the Hebrew word for "rocks" as "hands."
- **40:18** — This Greek text lacks "and" between "self-sufficient" and "laborer," even though the second clause refers to "both."
- **40:25** — This Greek text reads "a wife" (gyne), whereas other witnesses read "counsel" (boule).
- **40:27** — This Greek text reads third-person plural "they have covered it," an impersonal plural or variant for singular "it covers" found in some other witnesses.
