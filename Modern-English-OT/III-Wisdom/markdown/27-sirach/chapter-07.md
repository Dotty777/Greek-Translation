## Text

**7:1** Do not do evil things, and evil will never overtake you.

**7:2** Keep away from what is unjust, and it will turn aside from you.

**7:3** Son, do not sow upon furrows of injustice, and you will never reap them sevenfold.

**7:4** Do not seek authority from the Lord, nor a seat of honor from a king.

**7:5** Do not justify yourself before the Lord, and do not show yourself wise in the presence of a king.

**7:6** Do not seek to become a judge, in case you are not strong enough to remove injustices; or else you may be intimidated before a powerful man, and you will place a stumbling block in your uprightness.

**7:7** Do not sin against the assembly of a city, and do not bring yourself down in a crowd.

**7:8** Do not bind up sin twice, for in the one you will not be guiltless.

**7:9** Do not say, "He will look upon the abundance of my gifts, and when I offer to God Most High, He will accept it."

**7:10** Do not grow fainthearted in your prayer, and do not neglect to give alms.

**7:11** Do not laugh at a man who is in the bitterness of his soul, for there is One who humbles and exalts.

**7:12** Do not plow a lie against your brother, nor do the same to a friend.

**7:13** Do not wish to tell any lie, for persistence in it is not for good.

**7:14** Do not babble in an assembly of elders, and do not repeat a word in your prayer.

**7:15** Do not hate laborious work and farming created by the Most High.

**7:16** Do not count yourself among the multitude of sinners.

**7:17** Humble your soul greatly. Remember that wrath will not delay, for the punishment of an ungodly person is fire and worm.

**7:18** Do not exchange a friend for money, nor a true brother for the gold of Souphir.

**7:19** Do not forfeit a wise and good wife, for her grace is above gold.

**7:20** Do not mistreat a household servant who works in truth, nor a hired worker who gives his soul.

**7:21** Let your soul love a good household servant; do not deprive him of freedom.

**7:22** Do you have livestock? Look after them, and if they are useful to you, let them remain with you.

**7:23** Do you have children? Discipline them, and bend their necks from youth.

**7:24** Do you have daughters? Watch over their bodies, and do not make your face cheerful toward them.

**7:25** Give a daughter in marriage, and you will have completed a great work, and bestow her on a man of understanding.

**7:26** Do you have a wife dear to your soul? Do not cast her out.

**7:27** With all your heart honor your father, and do not forget a mother's birth pains.

**7:28** Remember that through them you came into being, and what will you repay them as they have done for you?

**7:29** With all your soul revere the Lord, and hold His priests in high regard.

**7:30** With all your strength love Him who made you, and do not forsake His ministers.

**7:31** Fear the Lord and honor a priest, and give him his portion just as has been commanded you from the beginning: the offering for guilt, and the gift of the shoulders, and the sacrifice of sanctification, and the firstfruits.

**7:32** And stretch out your hand to a poor person, so that your blessing may be made complete.

**7:33** Let the grace of a gift be before every living person, and toward a dead person do not withhold grace.

**7:34** Do not stay away from those who weep, and mourn with those who mourn.

**7:35** Do not hesitate to visit a sick person, for because of such things you will be loved.

**7:36** In all your words remember your end, and you will never sin.

## Translator Notes

- **7:12** — Greek literally reads "plow a lie," reflecting a Hebrew verb (charash) that can mean both "to plow" and "to devise" or "plot."
- **7:17** — In this Greek text, "Remember that wrath will not delay" appears within v. 17 rather than at the end of v. 16 as in some editions; "fire and worm" preserves the Greek singular, echoing Isaiah 66:24.
- **7:18** — "Money" renders Greek adiaphorou (literally "an indifferent thing"), an idiom used in Sirach for financial gain or price; "Souphir" is the Septuagint name corresponding to Hebrew Ophir, renowned for fine gold.
- **7:31** — This Greek text reads ap' arches ("from the beginning"), where some manuscripts read aparchen ("firstfruits") in this position; "the offering for guilt" renders the Septuagint Levitical phrase peri plemmelias.
- **7:33** — The first clause is verbless in Greek (literally "grace of a gift before every living person"); "grace" (charis) here encompasses gracious kindness, including proper care and burial rites for the dead.
