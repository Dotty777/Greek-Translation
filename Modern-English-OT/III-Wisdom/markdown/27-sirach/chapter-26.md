## Text

**26:1** Blessed is the husband of a good wife, and the number of his days is double.

**26:2** A capable wife gladdens her husband, and he will complete his years in peace.

**26:3** A good wife is a good portion; she will be given in the portion of those who fear the Lord.

**26:4** Whether rich or poor, his heart is good, and at all times his face is cheerful.

**26:5** Of three things my heart was afraid, and at the fourth face I pleaded: slander of a city, and an assembly of a mob, and false accusation; all are more grievous than death.

**26:6** Pain of heart and mourning is a rival wife against another wife, and a scourge of the tongue communicating with all.

**26:7** A shaking ox-yoke is an evil wife; the one who takes hold of her is like the one who grasps a scorpion.

**26:8** Great anger is a drunken woman, and she will not cover up her shame.

**26:9** The sexual immorality of a woman is in the lifting up of the eyes, and it will be known by her eyelids.

**26:10** Over a daughter, unswervingly keep a firm watch, so that, finding liberty, she may not indulge herself.

**26:11** Keep watch after a shameless eye, and do not be surprised if she does wrong against you.

**26:12** As a thirsty traveler opens his mouth and will drink from any water nearby, opposite every peg she will sit, and before an arrow she will open her quiver.

**26:13** The grace of a wife will delight her husband, and her skill will fatten his bones.

**26:14** A gift of the Lord is a silent wife, and there is no equivalent for a disciplined soul.

**26:15** Grace upon grace is a modest wife, and no weight at all is worthy of a self-controlled soul.

**26:16** Like the sun rising in the heights of the Lord, so is the beauty of a good wife in the ordering of his house.

**26:17** Like a lamp shining out upon a holy lampstand, so is the beauty of a face upon a stately stature.

**26:18** Like golden pillars upon a silver base, so are beautiful feet upon the breast of a steadfast woman.

**26:28** Over two things my heart has been grieved, and at the third, wrath came upon me: a warrior suffering lack because of poverty, and men of understanding if they are treated as refuse; one who turns back from righteousness to sin—the Lord will prepare him for the sword.

**26:29** Hardly will a merchant keep himself free from wrongdoing, and a shopkeeper will not be acquitted of sin.

## Translator Notes

- **26:5** — The Greek phrase 'at the fourth face I pleaded' (epi tō tetartō prosōpō edeēthēn) is notoriously difficult; it likely reflects a literal rendering or shift involving 'in the presence of' and 'I feared' or 'I entreated.'
- **26:10** — The Greek text reads the adverb adiatreptōs ('unswervingly'); some manuscripts read the dative adjective adiatreptō ('over a headstrong/shameless daughter').
- **26:16** — The Greek text reads 'his house' (oikias autou, i.e., her husband's house), whereas some manuscripts read 'her house' (autēs).
- **26:18** — The Greek text literally reads 'upon the breast/chest (sternois) of a steadfast woman'; many scholars suspect an early scribal or translational shift from 'heels' (pternais), which would better parallel 'feet' and 'silver base.'
