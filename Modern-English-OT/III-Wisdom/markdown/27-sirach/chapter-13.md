## Text

**13:1** The one who touches pitch will be defiled, and the one who associates with an arrogant person will become like him.

**13:2** Do not lift a burden beyond you, and do not associate with one stronger and richer than you. What will a clay pot have in common with a cauldron? It will collide, and it itself will be shattered.

**13:3** A rich person did wrong, and he himself grew indignant as well; a poor person has been wronged, and he himself will plead as well.

**13:4** If you are useful, he will put you to work; and if you fall short, he will abandon you.

**13:5** If you have anything, he will live with you and empty you out, and he himself will feel no pain.

**13:6** He has had need of you, and he will lead you astray, and he will smile at you and give you hope; he will speak good things to you and say, "What is your need?"

**13:7** And he will put you to shame with his meals until he empties you out twice or three times, and in the end he will mock you; after these things he will see you and abandon you, and he will shake his head at you.

**13:8** Take care that you are not led astray, and do not be humiliated in your gladness.

**13:9** When a ruler invites you, keep drawing back, and so much the more he will invite you.

**13:10** Do not rush in, so that you may not be pushed away, and do not stand far off, so that you may not be forgotten.

**13:11** Do not presume to speak as an equal with him, and do not trust his many words; for through much talk he will test you, and as if smiling, he will examine you.

**13:12** Merciless is the one who does not guard words, and he will by no means hold back from affliction and chains.

**13:13** Be on guard and take great care, because you are walking with your downfall.

**13:15** Every living creature loves what is like itself, and every person his neighbor.

**13:16** All flesh gathers according to kind, and a man will cling to one like himself.

**13:17** What will a wolf have in common with a lamb? So is a sinner with a godly person.

**13:18** What peace does a hyena have with a dog? And what peace does a rich person have with a needy person?

**13:19** Wild donkeys in the wilderness are the prey of lions; so the poor are the pastures of the rich.

**13:20** Humility is an abomination to an arrogant person; so a poor person is an abomination to a rich person.

**13:21** A rich person who staggers is supported by friends, but a lowly person who falls is even pushed away by friends.

**13:22** When a rich person slips, there are many helpers; he spoke things not to be spoken, and they justified him. A lowly person slipped, and they even rebuked him; he uttered understanding, and no place was given to him.

**13:23** A rich person spoke, and all kept silent, and they exalted his word to the clouds; a poor person spoke, and they said, "Who is this?" And if he stumbles, they will overthrow him as well.

**13:24** Wealth is good in which there is no sin, and poverty is evil in the mouths of a godly person.

**13:25** A person's heart changes his face, whether for good or for evil.

**13:26** The trace of a heart in good things is a cheerful face, and the discovery of proverbs is thoughts with toil.

## Translator Notes

- **13:2** — Greek repeats the feminine demonstrative pronoun (haute ... haute), both referring to the fragile clay pot (chytra) rather than the masculine cauldron (lebes).
- **13:4** — Greek ergatai en soi (lit. 'will work in/with you') reflects a Hebrew idiom meaning to put someone to work or exploit their labor.
- **13:8** — The Greek text reads 'in your gladness' (en euphrosyne sou), whereas the Hebrew text reads 'in your folly.'
- **13:24** — The Greek text reads plural 'mouths' (stomasin) and 'a godly person' (eusebous), whereas the Hebrew and some witnesses read 'an ungodly person' (asebous).
