## Prologue

Since many great things have been given to us through the Law and the Prophets and the others who followed after them—for which Israel ought to be praised for instruction and wisdom, and since not only ought the readers themselves to become knowledgeable, but lovers of learning should also be able to be useful to outsiders both in speaking and in writing—my grandfather Jesus, after devoting himself extensively to the reading of the Law and the Prophets and the other ancestral books, and having acquired considerable proficiency in them, was himself also led to compose something pertaining to instruction and wisdom, so that lovers of learning, becoming committed to these writings as well, might make much greater progress in living according to the Law. You are urged, therefore, to read with goodwill and attention, and to show forbearance wherever we may seem to fall short in some of the expressions we have labored over in translation; for things spoken in Hebrew do not have the same force in themselves as when they are carried over into another language. And not only these, but the Law itself, and the prophecies, and the rest of the books have no small difference when spoken in their original language. For when I arrived in Egypt in the thirty-eighth year under King Euergetes and stayed for a time, I found a copy of no small instruction; I judged it most necessary myself to apply some diligence and labor to translating this book. Indeed, I have devoted much sleepless effort and skill over that span of time to bringing the book to completion and publishing it also for those living abroad who wish to love learning, preparing their character to live according to the Law.

## Text

**1:1** All wisdom is from the Lord, and is with Him forever.

**1:2** Who will count the sand of the seas, and the drops of rain, and the days of eternity?

**1:3** Who will trace out the height of heaven, and the breadth of the earth, and the abyss, and wisdom?

**1:4** Before all things wisdom was created, and understanding of prudence is from eternity.

**1:6** To whom was the root of wisdom revealed? And who knew her subtle designs?

**1:8** There is One who is wise, greatly to be feared, seated upon His throne.

**1:9** The Lord Himself created her, and saw and counted her, and poured her out upon all His works,

**1:10** with all flesh according to His gift, and He supplied her to those who love Him.

**1:11** The fear of the Lord is glory and a boast, and gladness and a crown of exultation.

**1:12** The fear of the Lord will delight the heart, and will give gladness and joy and length of days.

**1:13** For the one who fears the Lord it will be well at the end, and on the day of his death he will find favor.

**1:14** The beginning of wisdom is to fear God, and with the faithful she was created together with them in the womb.

**1:15** And with people she made her nest as an eternal foundation, and with their seed she will be trusted.

**1:16** The fullness of wisdom is to fear the Lord, and she intoxicates them from her fruits.

**1:17** She will fill her whole house with desirable things, and the storehouses from her produce.

**1:18** The crown of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, causing peace and the health of healing to flourish.

**1:19** And He saw and counted her; He rained down insight and knowledge of understanding, and He exalted the glory of those who hold fast to her.

**1:20** The root of wisdom is to fear the Lord, and her branches are length of days.

**1:22** Unjust anger cannot be justified, for the weight of his anger is a downfall to him.

**1:23** For a time a patient person will hold out, and afterward gladness will spring up for him.

**1:24** For a time he will hide his words, and the lips of the faithful will recount his understanding.

**1:25** In the treasuries of wisdom is a proverb of insight, but godliness is an abomination to a sinner.

**1:26** If you desire wisdom, keep the commandments, and the Lord will supply her to you.

**1:27** For the fear of the Lord is wisdom and discipline, and His good pleasure is faithfulness and gentleness.

**1:28** Do not disobey the fear of the Lord, and do not approach it with a double heart.

**1:29** Do not be a hypocrite in the mouths of people, and be careful with your lips.

**1:30** Do not exalt yourself, so that you do not fall and bring dishonor upon your soul; and the Lord will reveal your secrets, and in the midst of the assembly He will cast you down, because you did not approach the fear of the Lord, and your heart is full of deceit.

## Translator Notes

- **Prologue** — The Greek translator's prologue by the grandson of Jesus ben Sira, dated to the thirty-eighth year of Ptolemy VIII Euergetes II (132 BC), is the earliest witness to the threefold division of Israel's Scriptures ('the Law and the Prophets and the other ancestral books') and reflects on the inherent challenges of translating Hebrew into Greek.
- **1:1** — In the Saints Greek Critical Text database, the translator's Prologue and 1:1 are stored together in entry 1:1; this Modern English edition displays the Prologue separately above (corresponding to Saints.Bible chapter 0) and places the opening maxim here at 1:1 (Saints.Bible 1:1).
- **1:13** — The critical Greek text reads 'he will find favor' (εὑρήσει χάριν), whereas some manuscripts read 'he will be blessed.'
- **1:15** — Greek literally reads 'she nested an eternal foundation,' portraying Wisdom making a lasting home among humanity; 'she will be trusted' translates future passive ἐμπιστευθήσεται (also carrying the sense of proving faithful).
- **1:19** — The implied subject of the verbs in this verse is the Lord, echoing 1:9 ('saw and counted her'), while 'her' refers to Wisdom.
- **1:22** — Greek pairs ἄδικος ('unjust') with δικαιωθῆναι ('be justified') in a wordplay; 'weight' renders ῥοπή, literally the downward tipping of a balance scale.
- **1:29** — Greek literally reads 'in the mouths of people' (ἐν στόμασιν ἀνθρώπων), referring to public speech or acting before the scrutiny of others.
