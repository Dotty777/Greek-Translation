## Text

**17:1** The Lord created man from earth, and again He returned him to it.

**17:2** He gave them numbered days and a time, and He gave them authority over the things upon it.

**17:3** According to themselves He clothed them with strength, and according to His image He made them;

**17:4** and He placed the fear of him upon all flesh, and appointed them to have dominion over wild beasts and birds.

**17:6** He gave them deliberation and a tongue and eyes, ears and a heart to think.

**17:7** He filled them with knowledge of understanding, and He showed them good and evil.

**17:8** He set His eye upon their hearts, to show them the majesty of His works,

**17:10** so that they might recount the splendors of His works. And they will praise the name of holiness.

**17:11** He added knowledge to them, and He gave them a law of life as an inheritance.

**17:12** He established an everlasting covenant with them, and He showed them His judgments.

**17:13** Their eyes saw the majesty of glory, and their ear heard the glory of their voice.

**17:14** And He said to them, "Beware of every unrighteous thing," and He commanded each of them concerning his neighbor.

**17:15** Their ways are before Him continually; they will not be hidden from His eyes.

**17:17** For every nation He appointed a ruler, and the Lord's portion is Israel.

**17:19** All their works are like the sun before Him, and His eyes are continually upon their ways.

**17:20** Their unrighteous deeds were not hidden from Him, and all their sins are before the Lord.

**17:22** A man's act of mercy is like a signet ring with Him, and He will guard a person's kindness like the pupil of an eye.

**17:23** After these things He will rise up and repay them, and He will render their repayment upon their head.

**17:24** Yet to those who repent He gave a return, and He encouraged those failing in endurance.

**17:25** Turn back to the Lord and forsake sins; plead before His face and lessen offense.

**17:26** Return to the Most High and turn away from unrighteousness, and greatly hate abomination.

**17:27** Who will praise the Most High in Hades instead of the living and those who give thanksgiving?

**17:28** From one who is dead, as from one who does not even exist, thanksgiving perishes; one who is alive and healthy will praise the Lord.

**17:29** How great is the mercy of the Lord, and pardon for those who turn back to Him!

**17:30** For not all things can be in human beings, because a son of man is not immortal.

**17:31** What is brighter than the sun? Yet even this fails; and an evil person will ponder flesh and blood.

**17:32** He Himself inspects the host of the height of heaven, and all human beings are earth and ashes.

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — The Greek text reads plural καθ᾽ ἑαυτούς ('according to themselves,' as in major uncials B, A, and S), whereas some other witnesses read singular καθ᾽ ἑαυτόν ('like Himself').
- **17:4** — 'The fear of him' (τὸν φόβον αὐτοῦ) refers to the fear of humanity placed upon the creatures (echoing Genesis 9:2), rather than the fear of God.
- **17:10** — Following the order of the Greek text here (as in Codex Vaticanus and Rahlfs), the purpose clause ('so that they might recount...') precedes 'and they will praise the name of holiness'; some editions reverse the two lines.
- **17:13** — The Greek text reads 'their voice' (φωνῆς αὐτῶν, as in Codex Vaticanus), while other Greek witnesses read 'His voice' (φωνῆς αὐτοῦ), referring to God's voice at Sinai.
- **17:31** — The Greek text places 'an evil person' (πονηρός) in the nominative as subject and 'flesh and blood' (σάρκα καὶ αἷμα) in the accusative as object; other witnesses reverse the cases to read 'flesh and blood will ponder evil.'
