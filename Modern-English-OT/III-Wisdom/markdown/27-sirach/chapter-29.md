# Sirach 29 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**29:1** The one who shows mercy will lend to his neighbor, and the one who gives support with his hand keeps the commandments.

**29:2** Lend to your neighbor in the time of his need, and in turn repay your neighbor on time.

**29:3** Keep your word firm and be trustworthy with him, and at every time you will find what you need.

**29:4** Many have regarded a loan as a windfall, and have caused trouble for those who helped them.

**29:5** Until he receives it, he will kiss his hand, and concerning his neighbor's money he will lower his voice; and at the time of repayment he will delay the time, and he will pay back words of weariness and blame the time.

**29:6** If he is able, he will barely recover half and will count it as a windfall; but if not, he has defrauded him of his money, and he has acquired him as an enemy not for nothing; he will pay back to him curses and insults, and instead of honor he will pay back to him dishonor.

**29:7** Many have turned away because of wickedness; they have feared being defrauded for nothing.

**29:8** Nevertheless, be patient with a humble person, and you will delay him for almsgiving.

**29:9** Because of the commandment, help a needy person, and in view of his lack do not turn him away empty.

**29:10** Lose silver for the sake of a brother and a friend, and do not let it rust under the stone to ruin.

**29:11** Place your treasure according to the commandments of the Most High, and it will profit you more than gold.

**29:12** Lock up almsgiving in your storehouses, and it will deliver you from every affliction.

**29:13** Better than a shield of might and better than a spear of strength, it will fight for you against an enemy.

**29:14** A good man will stand surety for his neighbor, and the one who has lost shame will abandon him.

**29:15** Do not forget the kindnesses of a guarantor, for he has given his life for you.

**29:16** A sinner will overturn the goods of a guarantor,

**29:17** and one who is ungrateful in mind will forsake his rescuer.

**29:18** Suretyship has destroyed many who were prospering and has tossed them like a wave of the sea; it has sent powerful men into exile, and they have wandered among foreign nations.

**29:19** A sinner who falls into suretyship and pursues contracts will fall into lawsuits.

**29:20** Help your neighbor according to your ability, and watch yourself so that you do not fall.

**29:21** The beginning of life is water and bread and clothing, and a house covering indecency.

**29:22** Better is the life of a poor person under a shelter of beams than sumptuous foods among strangers.

**29:23** With little and with much, be content.

**29:24** It is a miserable life from house to house, and where he sojourns he will not open his mouth.

**29:25** You will entertain and serve drink for no thanks, and in addition to these things you will hear bitter words:

**29:26** "Come forward, sojourner, set a table, and if there is anything in your hand, feed me."

**29:27** "Go out, sojourner, from the presence of honor; my brother has come as my guest; there is need of the guest room."

**29:28** These things are heavy for a person who has understanding: the rebuke of a household and the reproach of a lender.

## Translator Notes

- **29:6** — The primary Greek uncial text reads 'not for nothing' (οὐ δωρεάν), emphasizing ironically that the lender paid for an enemy with his lost loan; some witnesses omit 'not' and read 'for nothing' (without cause).
- **29:8** — The Greek text here lacks a negative particle and uses the future indicative ('you will delay him for almsgiving'); several later manuscripts and versions read a negative prohibition ('do not keep him waiting for almsgiving').
- **29:23** — The primary Greek uncials preserve only this single clause; some manuscripts and versions add a second clause ('and you will not hear the reproach of your sojourning').
- **29:27** — 'From the presence of honor' (ἀπὸ προσώπου δόξης) is a Semitic idiom meaning to yield one's place to a more honored person.
