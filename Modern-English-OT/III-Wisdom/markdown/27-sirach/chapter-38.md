## Text

**38:1** Honor a physician according to the needs with the honors due him, for indeed the Lord created him.

**38:2** For healing is from the Most High, and from a king he will receive a gift.

**38:3** The skill of a physician will lift up his head, and before great men he will be admired.

**38:4** The Lord created medicines from the earth, and a sensible man will not despise them.

**38:5** Was not water made sweet by wood so that its power might be known?

**38:6** And He Himself gave skill to people so that He might be glorified in His marvelous works.

**38:7** With them he heals and takes away his pain,

**38:8** with these a pharmacist will make a compound; and He will never bring His works to an end, and peace is from Him upon the face of the earth.

**38:9** Child, in your illness do not be negligent, but pray to the Lord, and He Himself will heal you.

**38:10** Put away wrongdoing and make your hands straight, and cleanse your heart from all sin.

**38:11** Give a fragrant aroma and a memorial of fine flour, and make an offering rich, as one who does not exist.

**38:12** And give a place to a physician, for indeed the Lord created him; and let him not depart from you, for indeed there is need of him.

**38:13** There is a time when success is also in their hands,

**38:14** for indeed they themselves will pray to the Lord that He may grant them success in relief and healing for the sake of continued life.

**38:15** May the one who sins before Him who made him fall into the hands of a physician.

**38:16** Child, shed tears over a dead person, and as one suffering grievously begin a lamentation; according to his due shroud his body, and do not neglect his burial.

**38:17** Make weeping bitter and make wailing fervent, and observe the mourning according to his worth, one day and two because of slander, and then be comforted because of grief.

**38:18** For from grief death comes forth, and grief of heart will bend strength.

**38:19** In being carried off, grief also passes by, and the life of a poor person is against the heart.

**38:20** Do not give your heart to grief; put it away, remembering the end.

**38:21** Do not forget, for there is no return; and you will not benefit this one, and you will harm yourself.

**38:22** Remember that his judgment is thus, just as yours is also: "For me yesterday, and for you today."

**38:23** In the rest of a dead person, put his remembrance to rest, and be comforted concerning him at the departure of his spirit.

**38:24** The wisdom of a scribe is in the opportunity of leisure, and the one who is reduced in his business will become wise.

**38:25** How will he become wise who holds the plow and boasts in the shaft of a goad, driving oxen and occupied in their works, and their talk is about sons of bulls?

**38:26** He will give his heart to turning out furrows, and his wakefulness is for the fodder of heifers.

**38:27** So also is every craftsman and master builder who passes his time by night as by day; those who engrave engravings of signets, and his perseverance is to vary the pattern; he will give his heart to making a painting lifelike, and his wakefulness is to complete the work.

**38:28** So also is the smith sitting near the anvil and closely examining unworked iron; the vapor of fire will harden his flesh, and in the heat of the furnace he will contend; the sound of the hammer will renew his ear, and his eyes are opposite the model of the vessel; he will give his heart to the completion of works, and his wakefulness is to adorn them at completion.

**38:29** So also is the potter sitting at his work and turning a wheel with his feet, who is continually in anxiety over his work, and all his labor is counted by number.

**38:30** With his arm he will mold clay, and before his feet he will bend its strength; he will apply his heart to complete the gift, and his wakefulness is to clean the kiln.

**38:31** All these have trusted in their hands, and each one is wise in his own work.

**38:32** Without them a city will not be inhabited, and people will neither sojourn nor walk about.

**38:33** And in the assembly they will not rise to prominence, and they will not understand a covenant of judgment; they will not sit on a judge's seat, nor will they declare righteousness and judgment, and they will not be found among parables;

**38:34** but they will preserve the creation of the age, and their prayer is in the work of a craft. Not so with the one who applies his soul and reflects on the law of the Most High:

## Translator Notes

- **38:5** — Or 'His power'; Greek autou may refer to the wood or to the Lord (v. 4).
- **38:7** — The Greek leaves the subject of "heals" unexpressed (implicitly the physician, or possibly the Lord from v. 6), with "his pain" referring to the sufferer's pain. In v. 8, ou me syntelese erga autou may also be taken intransitively as "His works will never come to an end."
- **38:11** — Literally "as one who does not exist" (hos me hyparchon), perhaps understood as "as if near death" or "sparing nothing"; the extant Hebrew differs, reading "according to your means."
- **38:19** — A notoriously difficult Greek line: apagoge literally means "carrying off" (calamity or bereavement), and parabainei means "passes by" (other manuscripts read paramenei, "remains"). "Against the heart" (kata kardias) conveys a burden or affliction to the heart.
- **38:28** — The critical text reads "unworked iron" (argo sidero, vs. variant "work of iron"), "will harden" (pexei, vs. variant "will melt"), and "will renew his ear" (kainiei, ringing afresh in his ear, where some scholars conjecture a textual error for "deafens" or "inclines").
- **38:30** — "Its strength" (ischyn autou) refers to the stiffness of the masculine noun "clay" (or possibly "his own strength"). "The gift" translates the manuscript reading to charisma, which many editors emend to to chrisma ("the glaze").
- **38:34** — Following the traditional Greek versification, the final clause of 38:34 opens the sentence that continues directly into 39:1.
