## Text

**15:1** But You, our God, are kind and true, patient and governing all things with mercy.

**15:2** For even if we sin, we are Yours, knowing Your might; we will not sin, knowing that we are counted as Yours.

**15:3** For to know You is complete righteousness, and to know Your might is the root of immortality.

**15:4** For neither has the wickedly skilled devising of people led us astray, nor the fruitless labor of painters, a form stained with varied colors,

**15:5** the sight of which turns to reproach for the foolish, and one longs for the breathless form of a dead image.

**15:6** Lovers of evil things, and worthy of such hopes, are those who make them, and those who long for them, and those who worship them.

**15:7** For a potter also, pressing soft earth, laboriously molds each article for our service; yet out of the same clay he molds both the vessels serving clean uses and the opposite ones, all alike; and what the use is of each one of either kind of these, the clay-worker is the judge;

**15:8** and in wicked toil he molds a futile god out of the same clay, he who a little while ago came into being from earth and after a little while goes back to that from which he was taken, when the debt of his soul is demanded back.

**15:9** Yet his concern is not that he is about to fall ill, nor that he has a brief life, but he rivals goldsmiths and silversmiths, and imitates bronze-molders, and considers it a glory that he molds counterfeits.

**15:10** His heart is ashes, and his hope is of less worth than earth, and his life is of less honor than clay,

**15:11** because he was ignorant of the One who molded him, and the One who breathed into him an active soul and blew into him a vital spirit;

**15:12** but he regarded our life as a game and our living as a profitable festival, for he says one must make gain from any source, even from evil.

**15:13** For this man knows above all others that he is sinning, crafting fragile vessels and carved images out of earthy material.

**15:14** Yet most foolish and more wretched than the soul of an infant are all the enemies of Your people who oppressed them,

**15:15** because they even regarded all the idols of the nations as gods, which have neither the use of eyes for seeing, nor nostrils for drawing in air, nor ears to hear, nor fingers on their hands for feeling, and their feet are useless for walking.

**15:16** For a human being made them, and one who has borrowed his spirit molded them; for no human being is able to mold a god like himself;

**15:17** yet being mortal, he produces a dead thing with lawless hands; for he is better than the objects of his worship, since he himself lived, whereas they never did.

**15:18** And they even worship the most hateful animals; for when compared in senselessness, they are worse than the others.

**15:19** Nor are they beautiful enough to be desired, even as far as the appearance of animals goes, but they have escaped both the praise of God and His blessing.

## Translator Notes

- **15:4** — Greek skiagraphon ('painters') literally refers to 'shadow-painters,' artists who used shading and perspective to create lifelike illusions.
- **15:5** — Following the Greek reading oneidos ('reproach'); other witnesses read orexin ('desire'). The abrupt shift from plural 'the foolish' to the singular verb pothei ('one longs for') is a known syntactic difficulty in the Greek text.
- **15:9** — Greek kamnein ('fall ill') can mean 'grow weary,' 'fail in strength,' or 'fall ill,' pointing here to the potter's approaching frailty and mortality.
- **15:17** — The Greek connecting relative hon (lit. 'of which') is syntactically compressed, functioning here in a causal or contrastive sense ('since he himself lived, whereas they never did').
