## Text

**12:1** For Your imperishable Spirit is in all things.

**12:2** Therefore, little by little, You reprove those who stumble, and admonish them by reminding them of the things in which they sin, so that, being freed from wickedness, they may believe in You, Lord.

**12:3** For indeed, the ancient inhabitants of Your holy land,

**12:4** whom You hated for practicing most detestable works of sorcery and unholy rites,

**12:5** merciless murderers of children, and a banquet of entrail-eaters of human flesh and blood, initiates from the midst of a cultic revel,

**12:6** and parents who murdered helpless souls, You willed to destroy by the hands of our fathers,

**12:7** so that the land most precious of all to You might receive a worthy colony of God's children.

**12:8** Yet even these You spared as human beings, and You sent wasps as forerunners of Your army, so that they might exterminate them little by little;

**12:9** not that You were unable in pitched battle to give the ungodly into the hands of the righteous, or to wipe them out all at once with fierce wild beasts or with a stern word;

**12:10** but judging them little by little, You gave a place for repentance, not unaware that their origin was evil and their wickedness inborn, and that their way of thinking would never be changed for all eternity,

**12:11** for they were a cursed seed from the beginning; nor was it out of fear of anyone that You granted immunity for the things in which they sinned.

**12:12** For who will say, "What have You done?" Or who will resist Your judgment? And who will accuse You for nations that have perished, which You made? Or who will come to confront You as an avenger for unrighteous people?

**12:13** For neither is there any god besides You, whose care is for all things, so that You should show that You did not judge unrighteously;

**12:14** nor will a king or a tyrant be able to look You in the face concerning those whom You punished.

**12:15** But being righteous, You order all things righteously, considering it alien to Your power to condemn the very one who does not deserve to be punished.

**12:16** For Your strength is the beginning of righteousness, and Your being Master of all makes You spare all.

**12:17** For You display strength when You are disbelieved concerning the perfection of Your power, and among those who know, You rebuke insolence.

**12:18** But You, being Master of strength, judge with gentleness, and with great forbearance You govern us; for power is present with You whenever You wish.

**12:19** And You taught Your people through such works that the righteous person must be humane; and You made Your sons full of good hope, because You give repentance for sins.

**12:20** For if You punished the enemies of Your children, who were also deserving of death, with such great attention and entreaty, giving them times and a place through which they might be freed from wickedness,

**12:21** with what great exactness did You judge Your sons, to whose fathers You gave oaths and covenants of good promises?

**12:22** Therefore, while disciplining us, You scourge our enemies ten-thousandfold, so that when we judge we may ponder Your goodness, and when we are judged we may expect mercy.

**12:23** For this reason also, through their own abominations, You tormented the unrighteous who lived in folly of life.

**12:24** For indeed they went farther astray than the ways of error, supposing to be gods those creatures that even among animals are despised by their enemies, being deceived like foolish infants.

**12:25** Because of this, as to unreasoning children, You sent judgment as a mockery;

**12:26** but those who were not admonished by mocking rebukes will experience a judgment worthy of God.

**12:27** For regarding those things at which they themselves were indignant while suffering, being punished by these very ones whom they thought to be gods, they saw and recognized as the true God Him whom they had formerly refused to know; therefore the culmination of condemnation also came upon them.

## Translator Notes

- **12:5** — The Greek syntax is compressed and textually debated: the accusative singular noun "banquet" (thoinan) is coordinated directly with personal nouns ("murderers," "initiates"), and the genitive plural splanchnophagon ("of entrail-eaters") appears where some manuscripts read the accusative singular splanchnophagon ("an entrail-eating banquet").
- **12:12** — The preposition kata with the genitive carries a Hellenistic legal sense of "concerning" or "for" in both clauses, and katastasis denotes a formal appearance or confrontation in court.
- **12:20** — Translates the Greek text deeseos ("entreaty" or "supplication"); some critical editions adopt the conjecture dieseos ("leniency" or "indulgence").
- **12:24** — The phrase ta kai en zoois ton echthron atima ("those creatures that even among animals are despised by their enemies") is difficult; ton echthron may also be taken as neuter ("of hateful creatures"), and some editions conjecture aischron ("vile").
