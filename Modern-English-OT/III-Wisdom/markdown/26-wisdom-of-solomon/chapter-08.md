## Text

**8:1** And she extends strongly from end to end, and governs all things well.

**8:2** Her I loved and sought out from my youth, and I sought to take her as a bride for myself, and I became a lover of her beauty.

**8:3** She glorifies her noble birth by having companionship with God, and the Master of all loved her.

**8:4** For she is an initiate of the knowledge of God, and a chooser of His works.

**8:5** And if wealth is a desirable possession in life, what is wealthier than wisdom, who works all things?

**8:6** And if understanding is at work, who more than she is an artisan of the things that exist?

**8:7** And if anyone loves righteousness, her labors are virtues; for she teaches self-control and understanding, righteousness and courage; nothing in life is more useful to people than these.

**8:8** And if anyone also longs for wide experience, she knows the things of old and how to surmise the things to come; she understands turns of speech and solutions of riddles; she foreknows signs and wonders and the outcomes of seasons and times.

**8:9** Therefore I decided to take her for companionship, knowing that she would be a counselor of good things to me, and an encouragement in cares and grief.

**8:10** Because of her I will have glory among the crowds, and honor among the elders, though I am young.

**8:11** I will be found keen in judgment, and in the sight of rulers I will be admired.

**8:12** When I am silent they will wait for me, and when I speak they will pay attention; and when I talk at greater length, they will place a hand upon their mouth.

**8:13** Because of her I will have immortality, and I will leave behind an eternal memory to those after me.

**8:14** I will govern peoples, and nations will be subjected to me.

**8:15** Terrifying tyrants will fear me when they hear of me; among the multitude I will appear good, and in war courageous.

**8:16** When I enter my house, I will find rest with her; for association with her has no bitterness, nor does companionship with her have pain, but gladness and joy.

**8:17** Having considered these things within myself, and having pondered in my heart that there is immortality in kinship with wisdom,

**8:18** and in friendship with her is good delight, and in the labors of her hands is unfailing wealth, and in the exercise of her company is understanding, and renown in the fellowship of her words, I went about seeking how I might take her to myself.

**8:19** Now I was a gifted child, and I received a good soul as my portion;

**8:20** or rather, being good, I entered an undefiled body.

**8:21** But knowing that I would not otherwise possess her unless God gave her—and this too belonged to understanding, to know whose the gift is—I appealed to the Lord and pleaded with Him, and said from my whole heart:

## Translator Notes

- **8:4** — Greek mystis ("initiate") borrows from Hellenistic mystery terminology to portray wisdom as one initiated into the inner secrets of God's knowledge.
- **8:7** — The four virtues listed here—self-control (sōphrosynē), understanding/prudence (phronēsis), righteousness/justice (dikaiosynē), and courage (andreia)—are the classical Greek cardinal virtues.
- **8:20** — Scholars debate whether "being good, I entered an undefiled body" reflects a Hellenistic/Platonic view of the preexistence of the soul or is a rhetorical self-correction locating personal identity in the soul rather than the body.
- **8:21** — Greek enkratēs appears without an explicit object and can mean either "in possession [of wisdom]" (fitting 8:18 and 9:4, 17) or "self-controlled/continent" (as rendered in the Latin Vulgate).
