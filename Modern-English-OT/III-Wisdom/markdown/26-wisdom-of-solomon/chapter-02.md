## Text

**2:1** For they said among themselves, not reasoning rightly, "Our life is short and sorrowful, and there is no remedy at a person's end, and no one has been known to return from Hades.

**2:2** For we came into being by chance, and after this we will be as though we had not existed; for the breath in our nostrils is smoke, and reason is a spark in the movement of our heart,

**2:3** and when it is extinguished, the body will turn into ashes, and the spirit will be dispersed like thin air.

**2:4** And our name will be forgotten in time, and no one will remember our works; and our life will pass away like the traces of a cloud, and will be scattered like a mist driven away by the rays of the sun and weighed down by its heat.

**2:5** For our life is the passing of a shadow, and there is no reversal of our end, because it has been sealed, and no one returns.

**2:6** Come, therefore, and let us enjoy the good things that exist, and let us eagerly make use of creation as in youth.

**2:7** Let us be filled with costly wine and perfumes, and let no flower of the air pass us by;

**2:8** let us crown ourselves with rosebuds before they wither.

**2:9** Let none of us be without a share in our revelry; everywhere let us leave tokens of gladness, for this is our portion, and this is our lot.

**2:10** Let us oppress a righteous poor man; let us not spare a widow, nor respect the aged gray hairs of an old man.

**2:11** But let our strength be the law of righteousness, for what is weak is proved useless.

**2:12** And let us lie in wait for the righteous man, because he is inconvenient to us and opposes our works, and reproaches us for sins against the law, and charges us with sins against our discipline.

**2:13** He professes to have knowledge of God, and calls himself a child of the Lord.

**2:14** He has become to us a reproof of our thoughts;

**2:15** he is burdensome to us even to look at, because his life is unlike that of others, and his paths are strange.

**2:16** We have been considered counterfeit by him, and he keeps away from our ways as from impurities; he calls the final end of the righteous blessed, and boasts of God as his father.

**2:17** Let us see whether his words are true, and let us test what happens at his departure.

**2:18** For if the righteous man is a son of God, He will help him and rescue him from the hand of those who oppose him.

**2:19** With insult and torment let us examine him, so that we may know his gentleness and judge his patient endurance.

**2:20** Let us condemn him to a shameful death, for according to his words, there will be a visitation for him."

**2:21** These things they reasoned, and they were led astray; for their wickedness blinded them, and they did not know the mysteries

**2:22** of Him, nor did they hope for a reward of holiness, nor did they discern a prize for blameless souls.

**2:23** For God created man for incorruption, and made him an image of His own nature;

**2:24** but by the envy of the devil, death entered into the world, and those who belong to his portion experience it.

## Translator Notes

- **2:7** — The Greek text reads aeros ('of the air,' attested in major early uncial manuscripts); other witnesses and emendations read earos ('of spring').
- **2:13** — Greek paida can mean either 'child' (connecting with vv. 16 and 18) or 'servant' (echoing Isaiah 52:13 LXX).
- **2:19** — The Greek text reads dikasomen ('judge'), as in Codices Vaticanus and Sinaiticus; other witnesses read dokimasomen ('test' or 'prove').
- **2:22** — The Greek text reads autou ('of Him,' referring to God), continuing 'the mysteries' from the end of v. 21; many manuscripts read theou ('of God').
- **2:23** — The Greek text reads idiotetos ('proper nature' or 'character'); a notable variant reads aidiotetos ('eternity').
