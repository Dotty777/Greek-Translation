## Text

**10:1** She protected the first-formed father of the world when he was created alone, and she delivered him from his own transgression,

**10:2** and gave him strength to rule over all things.

**10:3** But when an unrighteous man departed from her in his wrath, he perished together with his brother-killing fury.

**10:4** Because of him, wisdom again saved the flooded earth, steering the righteous man by means of humble wood.

**10:5** She also, when the nations were thrown into confusion in unanimity of wickedness, found the righteous man, and preserved him blameless before God, and kept him strong in the face of compassion for his child.

**10:6** She rescued a righteous man while the ungodly were perishing, as he fled the fire descending on Pentapolis,

**10:7** of whose wickedness a smoking wasteland still stands as a testimony, and plants bearing fruit in untimely seasons, and a standing pillar of salt, a monument of an unbelieving soul.

**10:8** For by passing by wisdom, they were not only harmed so as not to know what is good, but also left to human life a memorial of their folly, so that in the things in which they stumbled they might not even be able to go unnoticed.

**10:9** But wisdom rescued those who served her from hardships.

**10:10** She guided a righteous man, a fugitive from his brother's wrath, on straight paths; she showed him the kingdom of God, and gave him knowledge of holy things; she prospered him in his toils, and multiplied the fruits of his labors.

**10:11** In the greed of those who overpowered him she stood by him, and made him rich.

**10:12** She protected him from enemies, and kept him safe from those lying in ambush; and she decided a fierce contest in his favor, so that he might know that godliness is more powerful than everything.

**10:13** She did not abandon a righteous man when he was sold, but rescued him from sin.

**10:14** She went down with him into the pit, and in chains she did not leave him, until she brought him the scepter of a kingdom and authority over those who tyrannized him; she also showed those who had found fault with him to be liars, and gave him eternal glory.

**10:15** She rescued a devout people and a blameless seed from a nation of oppressors.

**10:16** She entered into the soul of a servant of the Lord, and withstood fearsome kings with wonders and signs.

**10:17** She repaid the devout the wages of their labors; she guided them on a marvelous way, and became for them a shelter by day and a flame of stars through the night.

**10:18** She brought them across the Red Sea, and led them through much water;

**10:19** but their enemies she engulfed, and cast them up from the depth of the abyss.

**10:20** Therefore the righteous plundered the ungodly, and sang hymns to Your holy name, Lord, and praised with one accord Your defending hand,

**10:21** because wisdom opened the mouth of the mute, and made the tongues of infants articulate.

## Translator Notes

- **10:3** — Greek synapoleto (lit. 'perished together with') indicates that the unrighteous man (Cain) brought about his own ruin along with his fratricidal fury.
- **10:6** — Pentapolis (lit. 'Five Cities') is the Greek name for the five cities of the plain—Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, Zeboiim, and Zoar (cf. Gen 14:2; 19:24-26).
- **10:10** — 'Holy things' translates an ambiguous Greek plural (hagion) that can also mean 'holy ones' (i.e., angels, alluding to Jacob's vision at Bethel in Gen 28:12). 'The fruits of his labors' renders tous ponous autou (lit. 'his labors'), a Septuagint idiom for the yield or earnings of work.
- **10:12** — Greek ebrabeusen uses athletic umpire imagery ('decided the contest in his favor' or 'awarded the prize'), alluding to Jacob's wrestling match at the Jabbok (Gen 32:24-30).
