## Text

**11:1** She prospered their works by the hand of a holy prophet.

**11:2** They journeyed through an uninhabited wilderness, and pitched tents in trackless places.

**11:3** They withstood foes, and repelled enemies.

**11:4** They grew thirsty and called upon You, and water was given to them out of flinty rock, and a remedy for thirst out of hard stone.

**11:5** For through the things by which their enemies were punished, through these they themselves were benefited in their need.

**11:6** Whereas, instead of the spring of an ever-flowing river, the others were troubled with defiled blood

**11:7** as a rebuke for the infant-killing decree, You gave them abundant water unexpectedly,

**11:8** showing through the thirst at that time how You punished the adversaries.

**11:9** For when they were tested, although disciplined in mercy, they came to know how the ungodly, judged in wrath, were tormented.

**11:10** For these You tested like a father who admonishes, but those You examined like a stern king who condemns.

**11:11** And both when absent and when present, they were equally distressed.

**11:12** For a double grief seized them, and a groaning over memories of the past.

**11:13** For when they heard that through their own punishments the others were being benefited, they perceived the Lord.

**11:14** For the one who long ago was cast out in exposure they rejected with mockery; at the end of events they marveled at him, having thirsted unlike the righteous.

**11:15** And in return for the senseless reasonings of their unrighteousness, in which they went astray and worshiped irrational reptiles and worthless creatures, You sent upon them a multitude of irrational animals for vengeance,

**11:16** so that they might know that through the things by which a person sins, through these he is punished.

**11:17** For Your all-powerful hand, which created the world out of formless matter, was not at a loss to send upon them a multitude of bears or fierce lions,

**11:18** or newly created, unknown wild beasts full of fury, either blowing out fiery breath, or scattering roaring blasts of smoke, or flashing terrible sparks from their eyes—

**11:19** beasts whose harm could not only utterly crush them, but whose very sight could terrify and destroy them.

**11:20** And even apart from these, they could have fallen by a single breath, pursued by justice and scattered by the breath of Your power; but You set all things in order by measure and number and weight.

**11:21** For to have great strength is always present with You, and who will withstand the might of Your arm?

**11:22** Because the whole world before You is like a tipping of the balance scales, and like a drop of morning dew that has come down upon the earth.

**11:23** Yet You have mercy on all, because You are able to do all things, and You overlook the sins of people for repentance.

**11:24** For You love all existing things, and You detest nothing of what You made; for You would not have fashioned anything if You hated it.

**11:25** And how would anything have remained if You had not willed it, or that which was not called by You have been preserved?

**11:26** Yet You spare all things, because they are Yours, Master, lover of life.

## Translator Notes

- **11:6** — The Greek text reads the masculine plural participle tarachthentes ('having been troubled'), a syntactic shift (anacoluthon) referring to the enemies of v. 5 in contrast to 'them' (Israel) in v. 7. Other manuscripts read the singular genitive tarachthentos ('of a river fouled with defiled blood').
- **11:14** — Alludes to the infant exposure of Moses (Exod 2:3). The Greek text reads the finite verb apeipon ('they rejected') rather than a participle.
- **11:17** — 'Out of formless matter' (ex amorphou hyles) employs Hellenistic philosophical vocabulary to describe the primordial state of creation in Gen 1:2.
- **11:18** — Greek bromous ('roars' or 'roaring blasts') is spelled with omicron here; some interpreters take it as an alternate spelling of bromos with omega ('foul fumes/stench').
- **11:26** — Greek philopsyche (lit. 'lover of soul/life') reclaims a classical term normally meaning 'clinging to one's own life' to express God's loving care for all living beings.
