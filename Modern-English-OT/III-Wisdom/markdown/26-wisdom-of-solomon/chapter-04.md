## Text

**4:1** Better is childlessness with virtue, for immortality is in the memory of it, because it is known both by God and by people.

**4:2** When it is present they imitate it, and when it has departed they long for it; and throughout eternity it marches in triumph wearing a crown, having won the contest for undefiled prizes.

**4:3** But the prolific multitude of the ungodly will be of no use, and from illegitimate shoots it will not send down a deep root nor establish a secure foundation.

**4:4** For even if they flourish in branches for a time, standing insecurely they will be shaken by wind, and by the violence of winds they will be uprooted.

**4:5** Immature branches will be broken off, and their fruit is useless, unripe for eating and fit for nothing.

**4:6** For children born from lawless unions are witnesses of wickedness against parents at their examination.

**4:7** But a righteous person, even if he dies early, will be at rest.

**4:8** For honorable old age is not that of long duration, nor is it measured by a count of years.

**4:9** Rather, gray hair for people is prudence, and the maturity of old age is an unblemished life.

**4:10** Having become pleasing to God, he was loved, and while living among sinners he was transferred.

**4:11** He was snatched away so that evil might not alter his understanding, or deceit delude his soul.

**4:12** For the fascination of baseness obscures what is good, and the roving of desire undermines an innocent mind.

**4:13** Having been perfected in a short time, he fulfilled long years.

**4:14** For his soul was pleasing to the Lord; therefore he hastened out of the midst of wickedness.

**4:15** But the peoples saw and did not understand, nor did they take such a thing to mind, that grace and mercy are upon His chosen ones, and watchful care is upon His devout ones.

**4:16** But a righteous person who has died will condemn the living ungodly, and youth quickly completed will condemn the long old age of an unrighteous person.

**4:17** For they will see the end of a wise person, and they will not understand what He purposed concerning him, and for what purpose the Lord kept him safe.

**4:18** They will see and show contempt, but the Lord will laugh at them in derision; and after this they will become a dishonored corpse and a disgrace among the dead forever.

**4:19** For He will dash them down speechless and headlong, and He will shake them from their foundations; and to the very last they will be laid waste, and they will be in anguish, and their memory will perish.

**4:20** They will come fearful at the reckoning of their sins, and their lawless deeds will convict them to their face.

## Translator Notes

- **4:6** — Greek reads literally "from lawless sleeps," a poetic euphemism for illicit sexual unions.
- **4:12** — Greek uses vivid, rare imagery: "fascination" (baskania) denotes a bewitching spell, "roving" (rhembasmos) restless wandering, and "undermines" (metalleuei) literally means "mines under" (also sometimes taken as "alters" or "perverts").
- **4:14** — The verb "hastened" (espeusen) has no explicit object in Greek; it may be taken intransitively of the righteous person ("he hastened") or, as in the Latin Vulgate, with the Lord as subject ("He hastened [to remove him]").
- **4:15** — The Greek constructs this verse with participles and no finite main verb (an anacoluthon), rendered with finite verbs here for clarity.
