## Text

**7:1** I also am a mortal, equal to all, and a descendant of the earthborn one who was first formed.

**7:2** And in a mother's womb I was sculpted into flesh during a ten-month period, congealed in blood from the seed of a man and the pleasure that accompanied sleep.

**7:3** And when I too was born, I drew in the common air and fell upon the earth of like nature, weeping with a first cry the same as all alike.

**7:4** I was raised in swaddling cloths and with cares.

**7:5** For no king had a different beginning of birth;

**7:6** but there is one entrance into life for all, and an equal departure.

**7:7** Therefore I prayed, and understanding was given to me; I called out, and a spirit of wisdom came to me.

**7:8** I preferred her to scepters and thrones, and I considered wealth nothing in comparison with her.

**7:9** Nor did I liken a priceless stone to her, because all gold in her sight is a little sand, and silver will be counted as clay before her.

**7:10** I loved her more than health and beauty, and I chose to have her instead of light, because the brightness from her is sleepless.

**7:11** Yet all good things came to me together with her, and countless wealth in her hands.

**7:12** And I rejoiced over them all, because wisdom leads them, yet I did not know that she was the origin of these things.

**7:13** I learned without deceit, and I share ungrudgingly; I do not hide her wealth.

**7:14** For she is an unfailing treasure for people; those who make use of it secure friendship with God, being commended because of the gifts that come from instruction.

**7:15** May God grant to me to speak with judgment and to think worthily of the things that have been given, because He Himself is both the guide of wisdom and the corrector of the wise.

**7:16** For in His hand are both we and our words, and all understanding and knowledge of crafts.

**7:17** For He Himself gave to me unerring knowledge of the things that exist, to know the structure of the world and the working of the elements,

**7:18** the beginning and end and middle of times, the alternations of the solstices and the changes of the seasons,

**7:19** the cycles of the years and the positions of the stars,

**7:20** the natures of animals and the tempers of wild beasts, the forces of winds and the reasonings of people, the varieties of plants and the powers of roots.

**7:21** And all things, both hidden and manifest, I came to know.

**7:22** For wisdom, the artisan of all things, taught me. For in her is a spirit that is intelligent, holy, unique, manifold, subtle, mobile, clear, unstained, distinct, unharmed, loving the good, keen, unhindered, beneficent,

**7:23** loving humanity, steadfast, secure, free from anxiety, all-powerful, overseeing all, and penetrating through all spirits that are intelligent, pure, and most subtle.

**7:24** For wisdom is more mobile than any motion, and she pervades and penetrates through all things because of her purity.

**7:25** For she is a vapor of the power of God, and an unalloyed outflowing of the glory of the Almighty; because of this, nothing defiled slips into her.

**7:26** For she is a radiance of everlasting light, and an unblemished mirror of God's working, and an image of His goodness.

**7:27** And being one, she can do all things, and remaining in herself, she renews all things; and generation by generation passing into holy souls, she fashions friends of God and prophets.

**7:28** For God loves nothing except the one who dwells with wisdom.

**7:29** For she is more splendid than the sun, and above every arrangement of stars. Compared with light, she is found superior;

**7:30** for night succeeds it, but wickedness does not prevail against wisdom.

## Translator Notes

- **7:2** — "Ten-month period" reflects the ancient reckoning of human gestation by ten lunar months; "congealed in blood" echoes ancient embryological imagery (cf. Job 10:10).
- **7:3** — Greek homoiopathē (lit. "of like nature/experiences") applied to the earth either points to the earth's shared nature with earthborn humanity (7:1) or acts as a transferred epithet for the common mortal lot of all born onto it.
- **7:12** — Following the critical Greek reading genesin ("origin" or "source"); some later witnesses and versions read genetin ("mother" or "begetter").
- **7:20** — Greek pneumatōn can mean either "winds" (fitting the natural-philosophy catalog) or "spirits" (reflecting Hellenistic Jewish traditions of Solomon's knowledge over spiritual forces).
- **7:26** — Greek apaugasma can denote either active "radiance/effulgence" shining forth from light or passive "reflection" (cf. Hebrews 1:3).
