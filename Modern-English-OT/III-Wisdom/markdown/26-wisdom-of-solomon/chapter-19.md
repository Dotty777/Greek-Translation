## Text

**19:1** But upon the ungodly merciless fury came until the end; for He knew beforehand even their future,

**19:2** that after they themselves had relented so as to let them depart and had sent them off with haste, they would change their minds and pursue them.

**19:3** For while still having their mourning in hand and lamenting at the tombs of the dead, they drew upon themselves another thought of folly, and those whom they had driven out with entreaties, these they pursued as fugitives.

**19:4** For the deserved necessity was dragging them to this end and implanted forgetfulness of what had happened, so that they might fill up the punishment still lacking to their torments,

**19:5** and that Your people might experience an extraordinary journey, while those others might find a strange death.

**19:6** For the whole creation in its own kind was fashioned anew from the beginning, serving its own commands, so that Your children might be kept unharmed.

**19:7** The cloud overshadowing the camp was seen, and out of water that stood before, the emergence of dry land; out of the Red Sea, an unobstructed road, and a grass-bearing plain out of a violent surge,

**19:8** through which those who were sheltered by Your hand passed as a whole nation, seeing marvelous wonders.

**19:9** For they grazed like horses and leaped about like lambs, praising You, Lord, who delivered them;

**19:10** for they still remembered the events of their sojourn, how instead of the birth of living creatures the earth brought forth gnats, and instead of aquatic creatures the river disgorged a multitude of frogs.

**19:11** And afterward they also saw a new birth of birds, when, led on by craving, they asked for foods of luxury;

**19:12** for as a comfort, quail came up to them from the sea.

**19:13** And the punishments came upon the sinners not without the signs that had occurred through the force of the thunderbolts; for they were suffering justly for their own wicked deeds, since they indeed practiced a more grievous hatred of strangers.

**19:14** For the former did not receive those unacquainted with them when they arrived, whereas these enslaved guests who were benefactors.

**19:15** And not only that, but surely there will be some visitation of them, since they received foreigners with hostility;

**19:16** whereas these, after welcoming them with festivities, afflicted with dreadful hardships those who had already shared in the same rights.

**19:17** And they too were struck with blindness, just like those at the doors of the righteous man, when, enveloped in yawning darkness, each one sought the passage through his own doors.

**19:18** For the elements changed harmony among themselves, just as on a harp the notes alter the name of the rhythm while always remaining constant in tone, which one can infer accurately from the sight of what took place.

**19:19** For land creatures were changed into aquatic creatures, and swimming creatures passed over onto land;

**19:20** fire in water surpassed its own power, and water forgot its quenching power.

**19:21** Conversely, flames did not waste away the flesh of perishable living creatures walking about in them, nor the melting, easily melted ice-like kind of immortal food.

**19:22** For in every way, Lord, You magnified Your people and glorified them, and You did not overlook them, standing beside them at every time and in every place.

## Translator Notes

- **19:2** — The Greek text reads 'epistrepsantes tou apeinai' ('having relented/turned around so that they might be gone'), attested in major uncials; other witnesses and editions read 'epitrepsantes tou apienai' ('having permitted them to depart').
- **19:6** — This text reads 'tais idiais epitagais' ('its own commands', following Vaticanus and Sinaiticus), whereas other witnesses read 'tais sais epitagais' ('Your commands').
- **19:15** — The text reads 'all' ē tis episkopē estai autōn' ('but surely there will be some visitation of them'). Because uncial script had no spaces or accents ('ALLĒTIS'), many editors read 'all' ei tis' ('but if there will be some allowance/regard for them').
- **19:18** — The author compares the miraculous interchange of the four elements to harp notes shifting the character ('name') of a rhythm while keeping their pitch; the neuter participle 'menonta' ('remaining constant') agrees grammatically with 'ta stoicheia' ('the elements').
- **19:21** — The Greek text has the double adjective 'tēkton eutēkton' ('melting, easily melted', as in Vaticanus), which many editors regard as an early scribal corruption of 'etēkon eutēkton' ('nor did they melt the easily melted...').
