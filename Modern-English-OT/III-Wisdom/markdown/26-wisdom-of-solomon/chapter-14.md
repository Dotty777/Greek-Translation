## Text

**14:1** Again, someone preparing for a voyage and about to pass through wild waves calls upon a piece of wood more decayed than the ship carrying him.

**14:2** For a desire for gain devised that ship, and wisdom as craftswoman constructed it;

**14:3** but Your providence, Father, steers it, because You gave a way even in the sea and a safe path among the waves,

**14:4** showing that You are able to save from everything, so that someone may embark even without skill.

**14:5** Yet You desire that the works of Your wisdom not be idle; therefore people entrust their lives even to the smallest piece of wood, and passing through the surge on a raft, they are brought safely through.

**14:6** For in the beginning also, when the arrogant giants were perishing, the hope of the world took refuge on a raft and, steered by Your hand, left to the age a seed of generation.

**14:7** For blessed is the wood through which righteousness comes;

**14:8** but what is made by hands is accursed, both it and the one who made it, because the one fashioned it, while the perishable thing was named a god.

**14:9** For equally hateful to God are both the ungodly one and his ungodliness;

**14:10** for indeed what was done will be punished together with the one who did it.

**14:11** Therefore there will also be a visitation upon the idols of the nations, because in God's creation they became an abomination, and stumbling blocks for the souls of people, and a trap for the feet of the foolish.

**14:12** For the beginning of sexual immorality is the devising of idols, and their inventions are the corruption of life.

**14:13** For neither did they exist from the beginning, nor will they exist forever;

**14:14** for by the vainglory of people they entered into the world, and therefore a swift end was devised for them.

**14:15** For a father worn down by untimely grief, having made an image of the child quickly taken away, now honored as a god the one who was then a dead human being, and handed down mysteries and rites to those under his authority;

**14:16** then in time the ungodly custom, having grown strong, was kept as a law,

**14:17** and by commands of tyrants carved images were worshiped. Unable to honor them face to face because they lived far away, people pictured the appearance from afar and made a visible image of the honored king, so that through their zeal they might flatter the absent one as though present.

**14:18** And the ambition of the craftsman urged even those who were ignorant to an intensification of worship.

**14:19** For he, perhaps wishing to please the ruler, forced the likeness by his skill toward greater beauty;

**14:20** and the multitude, drawn in by the charm of the workmanship, now regarded as an object of worship the man who a little while before had been honored.

**14:21** And this became a snare for life, because people, enslaved either to misfortune or to tyranny, bestowed the incommunicable name upon stones and wood.

**14:22** Then it was not enough for them to go astray concerning the knowledge of God, but living also in a great war of ignorance, they call such great evils peace.

**14:23** For celebrating either child-murdering rites, or secret mysteries, or frenzied revelries of strange ordinances,

**14:24** they no longer keep either their lives or their marriages pure, but one either kills another by lying in wait, or grieves him by adultery.

**14:25** And all things are mixed together indiscriminately: blood and murder, theft and deceit, corruption, faithlessness, tumult, perjury,

**14:26** disturbance of the good, forgetfulness of favor, defilement of souls, reversal of generation, disorder of marriages, adultery and debauchery.

**14:27** For the worship of nameless idols is the beginning and cause and end of every evil;

**14:28** for either while rejoicing they go mad, or they prophesy falsehoods, or they live unjustly, or they quickly commit perjury;

**14:29** for trusting in lifeless idols, having sworn wickedly, they do not expect to be harmed.

**14:30** Yet both just penalties will overtake them, because they thought wrongly about God by paying heed to idols, and they swore unjustly in deceit, having despised holiness.

**14:31** For it is not the power of the things sworn by, but the penalty for those who sin that always pursues the transgression of the unjust.

## Translator Notes

- **14:12** — Greek porneia encompasses both literal sexual immorality (developed in vv. 24-26) and the biblical metaphor of idolatry as spiritual unfaithfulness. The Greek text here reads the plural heureseis ('inventions').
- **14:21** — Greek akoinoneton ('incommunicable') literally means 'not to be shared with any other,' referring to God's exclusive divine name.
- **14:24** — Greek notheuon ('by adultery') literally means 'corrupting' or 'making illegitimate,' referring to marital betrayal that undermines a household's lineage.
- **14:26** — Greek geneseos enallage ('reversal of generation') is debated, understood either as unnatural sexual inversion or as confusion of birth and lineage through adultery.
- **14:27** — Greek anonymon ('nameless') can also carry the sense 'unmentionable' or 'not to be named' (cf. Exod 23:13 LXX), contrasting with God's 'incommunicable name' in v. 21.
