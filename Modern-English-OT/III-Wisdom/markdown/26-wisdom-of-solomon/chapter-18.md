## Text

**18:1** But for Your devout ones there was a very great light. Hearing their voice yet not seeing their form, they counted them blessed because indeed they too had suffered,

**18:2** and because, though previously wronged, they did not harm them, they gave thanks and begged pardon for having been at variance.

**18:3** In place of these things, You provided a pillar blazing with fire, as a guide for an unknown journey and a harmless sun for an honorable sojourn.

**18:4** For those people deserved to be deprived of light and imprisoned in darkness, who had kept Your sons locked up, through whom the imperishable light of the law was about to be given to the world.

**18:5** When they had planned to kill the infants of the devout ones, and one child was exposed and saved, as a reproof You took away the multitude of their children and destroyed them all together in violent water.

**18:6** That night was made known beforehand to our fathers, so that, knowing with certainty the oaths they had trusted, they might take heart.

**18:7** The salvation of the righteous and the destruction of the enemies were awaited by Your people.

**18:8** For as You punished the adversaries, by this You called us to Yourself and glorified us.

**18:9** For in secret the devout children of good men were offering sacrifice, and in harmony they established the divine law that the holy ones would share alike in the same good things and dangers, while the fathers were already singing praises beforehand.

**18:10** But the discordant cry of the enemies echoed back, and a pitiable cry was carried abroad for the children being lamented.

**18:11** And with like penalty a slave was punished together with a master, and a commoner suffered the same things as a king;

**18:12** and all of them together, under one name of death, had innumerable dead; for the living were not even sufficient to bury them, since in a single moment their most honored offspring was destroyed.

**18:13** For though disbelieving everything because of their sorceries, at the destruction of the firstborn they confessed the people to be God's son.

**18:14** For while quiet silence encompassed all things, and night in its own swiftness was in the midst of all things,

**18:15** Your all-powerful word leaped from the heavens, from royal thrones, a stern warrior into the midst of the doomed land, carrying Your unfeigned command as a sharp sword,

**18:16** and taking its stand, filled all things with death; it touched heaven, yet stood upon the earth.

**18:17** Then immediately visions of dreams dreadfully agitated them, and unexpected terrors came upon them,

**18:18** and cast down half-dead, one here and another there, each declared the reason why he was dying.

**18:19** For the dreams that troubled them had disclosed this beforehand, so that they might not perish without knowing why they were suffering evil.

**18:20** Now a trial of death touched the righteous as well, and a plague came upon a multitude in the wilderness; yet the wrath did not remain for long.

**18:21** For a blameless man hurried to fight as their champion, bringing the weapon of his own ministry, prayer and the atonement of incense; he withstood the fury and put an end to the calamity, showing that he was Your servant.

**18:22** He conquered the crowd not by strength of body, not by the power of weapons, but by word he subdued the punisher, recalling the oaths and covenants of the fathers.

**18:23** For when the dead had already fallen in heaps upon one another, he stood between them, drove back the wrath, and severed the path toward the living.

**18:24** For upon his full-length robe was the whole world, and the glories of the fathers were upon the four-rowed engraving of stones, and Your majesty was upon the diadem of his head.

**18:25** To these the destroyer yielded, and these they feared; for the trial of wrath alone was sufficient.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — The Greek text translated here reads 'hoti men oun kakeinoi epepontheisan' (without a negative particle, as in the major uncials). Other witnesses and ancient versions read 'ou' ('not') instead of or with 'oun', yielding 'because those others had not also suffered'.
- **18:9** — This text reads the genitive absolute 'paterōn ēdē proanamelpontōn ainous' ('while the fathers were already singing praises beforehand'); other witnesses read nominative 'proanamelpontes' ('already singing the praises of the fathers').
- **18:14** — The Greek text repeats 'ta panta' ('all things') after 'mesazousēs' ('was in the midst of all things'), whereas some witnesses omit the repeated phrase ('was midway [in its course]').
- **18:22** — The principal manuscripts read 'ton ochlon' ('the crowd'); many commentators emend this by letter transposition to 'ton cholon' ('the wrath/anger').
- **18:25** — The major manuscripts shift abruptly from singular 'ho olothreuōn' ('the destroyer') to plural 'ephobēthēsan' ('they feared'); some witnesses smooth this to singular 'ephobēthē' ('he feared').
