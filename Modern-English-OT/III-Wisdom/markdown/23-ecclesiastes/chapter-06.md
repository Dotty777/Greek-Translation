## Text

**6:1** There is an evil that I saw under the sun, and it is great under mankind:

**6:2** a man to whom God will give wealth and possessions and honor, and he lacks nothing for his soul of all that he will desire; yet God will not grant him authority to eat from it, for a foreign man will eat it. This is futility and an evil sickness.

**6:3** If a man fathers a hundred and lives many years, and however abundant the days of his years will be, yet his soul will not be satisfied with the goodness, and he even had no burial, I said, "Better than him is the stillborn child";

**6:4** for it came in futility and goes in darkness, and in darkness its name will be covered,

**6:5** moreover, it did not see the sun, and it did not know; there is more rest for this one than for that one.

**6:6** And if he lived cycles of a thousand years, yet did not see goodness, does he not go to one place?

**6:7** All the toil of a person is for his mouth, and yet the soul will not be filled.

**6:8** For there is an advantage to the wise over the fool, because the poor person knows how to walk before life.

**6:9** Better is a sight of the eyes than what goes in the soul; this too is futility and choice of spirit.

**6:10** Whatever has come into being, its name has already been called, and it is known what a human being is, and he will not be able to contend in judgment with the one who is stronger than him;

**6:11** for there are many words that multiply futility.

**6:12** What advantage is there for a person? For who knows what is good for a person in life, during the number of the days of his life of futility? And he spent them in a shadow; for who will report to a person what will be after him under the sun?

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — The Greek reads ὑπὸ τὸν ἄνθρωπον ("under mankind"), whereas the Hebrew and some Greek witnesses read "upon mankind."
- **6:6** — Greek καθόδους literally means "descents" or "returns" (rendered here "cycles," where the Hebrew has "twice"), and the final verb πορεύεται is singular without "all."
- **6:8** — Unlike the Hebrew's two rhetorical questions referring to "the living," the Greek forms a declarative statement ("For there is an advantage... because...") and uses the singular "life" (τῆς ζωῆς).
- **6:12** — The opening question belongs to verse 11 in the Hebrew numbering, and Greek ἐποίησεν αὐτὰ ἐν σκιᾷ means "he spent [literally made] them in a shadow" (where the Hebrew reads "like a shadow").
