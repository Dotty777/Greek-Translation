## Text

**10:1** Deadly flies will spoil a preparation, oil of sweet spice; the word of wisdom is more precious than the glory of great foolishness.

**10:2** The heart of a wise man is toward his right, and the heart of a fool is toward his left.

**10:3** And even when a fool walks on the road, his heart will be lacking, and all that he will consider is foolishness.

**10:4** If the spirit of the ruler rises against you, do not leave your place, for healing will put an end to great sins.

**10:5** There is an evil that I saw under the sun, like an unintentional error that proceeded from the presence of a ruler.

**10:6** The fool was set in great heights, and the rich will sit in a low place.

**10:7** I saw slaves on horses, and princes walking like slaves on the ground.

**10:8** The one who digs a pit will fall into it, and a snake will bite one who tears down a fence.

**10:9** One who removes stones will be hurt by them; one who splits logs will be endangered by them.

**10:10** If the iron falls off, and he has troubled the face, then he will strengthen forces, and the advantage for the man is not wisdom.

**10:11** If a snake bites when there is no whispering, then there is no advantage for the charmer.

**10:12** The words of a wise man's mouth are grace, and the lips of a fool will drown him.

**10:13** The beginning of the words of his mouth is foolishness, and his end is evil madness.

**10:14** And the fool multiplies words. A man does not know what has happened and what will be; what is after him, who will tell him?

**10:15** The toil of fools will afflict them—one who does not know how to go to a city.

**10:16** Woe to you, city, whose king is a youth, and your princes eat in the morning.

**10:17** Blessed are you, land, whose king is a son of free men, and your princes will eat at the proper time in strength and will not be put to shame.

**10:18** Through laziness the ceiling will sink low, and through idleness of hands the house will leak.

**10:19** For laughter they make bread, and wine and oil so that the living may rejoice, and all things will answer to the humbling of money.

**10:20** Even in your conscience do not curse a king, and in the inner rooms of your bedrooms do not curse a rich man; for a bird of the sky will carry away your voice, and the one with wings will report your word.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The Greek reads active "deadly flies" (where the Hebrew has "dead flies") and "the word of wisdom is more precious than the glory of great foolishness," following the textual reading ho logos ("the word") rather than oligon ("a little") and reversing the sense of the Hebrew text.
- **10:10** — The Greek diverges sharply from the Hebrew, reading "he has troubled the face" and the manuscript reading "the advantage for the man is not wisdom" (to andri ou sophia, contrasting with the variant tou andreiou sophia, "the advantage of the courageous man is wisdom").
- **10:17** — The Greek reads "will not be put to shame" (ouk aischynthesontai), differing from the Hebrew text's "not for drunkenness."
- **10:19** — The Greek adds "and oil" and combines two senses of the underlying Hebrew verb in "will answer to the humbling of money" (tou argyriou tapeinosei epakousetai), which can also be construed as "in humility all things will obey money."
