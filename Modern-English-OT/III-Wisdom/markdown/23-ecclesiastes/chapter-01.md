## Text

**1:1** Words of the Assembler, son of David, king of Israel in Jerusalem.

**1:2** "Futility of futilities," said the Assembler, "futility of futilities; all things are futility."

**1:3** What advantage does a man have in all his toil at which he toils under the sun?

**1:4** A generation goes and a generation comes, and the earth stands forever.

**1:5** And the sun rises, and the sun sets, and draws to its place;

**1:6** rising there, it goes toward the south and circles toward the north; circling, it circles; the wind goes, and to its circuits the wind returns.

**1:7** All the streams go into the sea, and the sea will not be filled; to the place the streams go, there they return to go.

**1:8** All words are wearisome; a man will not be able to speak, and an eye will not be satisfied with seeing, and an ear will not be filled from hearing.

**1:9** What is that which has come to be? It is that which will come to be. And what is that which has been done? It is that which will be done. And there is nothing fresh under the sun.

**1:10** Whoever speaks and says, "See, this is new"—it has already come to be in the ages that were before us.

**1:11** There is no remembrance of the first ones, and indeed of the last ones who have come to be; there will be no remembrance of them among those who will come to be at the last.

**1:12** I, the Assembler, became king over Israel in Jerusalem.

**1:13** And I gave my heart to seek out and to examine in wisdom concerning all the things that happen under heaven; for God has given an evil preoccupation to the sons of men to be preoccupied with it.

**1:14** I saw all the works that have been done under the sun, and look, all things are futility and a choice of spirit.

**1:15** What is crooked will not be able to be put in order, and a deficiency will not be able to be counted.

**1:16** I spoke in my heart, saying, "Look, I have grown great and have added wisdom beyond all who were before me in Jerusalem."

**1:17** And my heart saw much—wisdom and knowledge—and I gave my heart to know wisdom and knowledge; parables and understanding I came to know. For this too is a choice of spirit,

**1:18** because in an abundance of wisdom is an abundance of knowledge, and the one who adds knowledge will add pain.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — "The Assembler" translates Greek Ekklesiastes ("one who convenes or addresses an assembly").
- **1:6** — In the Greek text, "rising there" opens v. 6 and continues describing the sun's circuit before turning to the wind; in v. 5, "draws" renders Greek helkei (compare MT "pants/hastens").
- **1:14** — Greek proairesis pneumatos ("choice of spirit," also in 1:17; 2:11, 17, 26) reflects taking the Hebrew phrase as "desire/will of spirit" rather than "chasing after wind." Here and in 2:17; 3:10, 17, Greek syn with the accusative functions as a Hebraizing direct-object marker rather than the preposition "with."
- **1:17** — Greek reads "parables and understanding" (parabolas kai epistemen) where the Hebrew MT reads "madness and folly"; likewise in v. 18 Greek reads "abundance of knowledge" (plethos gnoseos, vs. MT "much vexation").
