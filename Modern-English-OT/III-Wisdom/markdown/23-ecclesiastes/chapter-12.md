## Text

**12:1** And remember the One who created you in the days of your youth, before the days of trouble come and years arrive in which you will say, "There is no delight for me in them";

**12:2** before the sun and the light and the moon and the stars become dark, and the clouds return following the rain;

**12:3** in the day when the guards of the house are shaken, and the men of strength are bent, and the grinding women cease because they have grown few, and those who look through the openings will grow dark;

**12:4** and they will shut the doors in the marketplace in the weakness of the sound of the grinding woman, and one will rise up at the sound of the sparrow, and all the daughters of song will be brought low,

**12:5** and they will look to the height, and terrors will be in the road; and the almond tree will blossom, and the locust will grow fat, and the caperberry will be scattered, because man has gone to his eternal home, and the mourners have gone around in the marketplace;

**12:6** before the cord of silver is overturned, and the floral ornament of gold is shattered, and a pitcher is shattered at the spring, and the wheel rolls down into the cistern;

**12:7** and the dust returns to the earth just as it was, and the spirit returns to the God who gave it.

**12:8** "Futility of futilities," said the Assembler, "all things are futility."

**12:9** And moreover, because the Assembler became wise, because he taught knowledge to mankind, an ear will also trace out the adornment of parables.

**12:10** The Assembler sought much to find words of delight, and what is written of uprightness, words of truth.

**12:11** The words of the wise are like oxgoads and like planted nails, those from the collections; they were given from one shepherd,

**12:12** and more than these. My son, beware of making many books; there is no end, and much study is weariness of the flesh.

**12:13** The end of the matter, hear it all: Fear God and keep His commandments, for this is the whole man.

**12:14** For God will bring every work into judgment, with every overlooked thing, whether good or evil.

## Translator Notes

- **12:5** — The Greek reads "they will look to the height" (opsontai) where the Hebrew has "they will be afraid of a height," and names the caperberry (kapparis), associated in antiquity with appetite or desire.
- **12:6** — The Greek uses distinctive imagery here: "is overturned" (anatrape), "floral ornament of gold" (anthemion, where the Hebrew has "bowl"), and "rolls down" (syntrochase, a rare compound forming a wordplay on trochos, "wheel").
- **12:9** — The Assembler (Greek ekklesiastes, "one who speaks in an assembly") is followed by a repeated "because" (hoti, shifted from eti, "still/also"), "mankind" rather than "the people," and "an ear will also trace out the adornment of parables," where the Greek construes underlying Hebrew verbs as nouns.
- **12:12** — Following the Greek punctuation, "and more than these" concludes v. 11 and "beware" governs "making many books," whereas the Hebrew connects "beyond these" to "my son, be warned."
- **12:13** — The Greek uses an imperative, "hear it all" (akoue), where the Hebrew has passive "all has been heard," and concludes literally with "this is the whole man" (pas ho anthropos).
