## Text

**2:1** I said in my heart, "Come now, I will test you with gladness, and look upon good." And look, this too is futility.

**2:2** To laughter I said, "Madness," and to gladness, "What is this you are doing?"

**2:3** And I examined whether my heart would draw my flesh like wine—and my heart guided in wisdom—and to take hold of gladness, until I should see what is the good for the sons of men that they will do under the sun for the number of the days of their life.

**2:4** I made my work great; I built houses for myself; I planted vineyards for myself;

**2:5** I made gardens and parks for myself, and I planted in them every fruit tree;

**2:6** I made pools of water for myself, to water from them a sprouting forest;

**2:7** I acquired male slaves and female slaves, and homeborn ones became mine; and also a great possession of herd and flock became mine, beyond all who were before me in Jerusalem;

**2:8** I gathered for myself both silver and gold, and treasures of kings and of the regions; I provided for myself male singers and female singers, delights of the sons of men, a male cupbearer and female cupbearers;

**2:9** And I grew great and increased beyond all who were before me in Jerusalem; and indeed my wisdom stood with me.

**2:10** And everything that my eyes asked for I did not withhold from them; I did not keep my heart back from all my gladness, for my heart was glad in all my toil; and this became my portion from all my toil.

**2:11** And I looked upon all my works that my hands had made, and upon the toil at which I toiled to do so, and look, all things are futility and a choice of spirit, and there is no advantage under the sun.

**2:12** And I looked to see wisdom and madness and folly; for who is the man who will come after the counsel? All the things that he did to it.

**2:13** And I saw that there is an advantage to wisdom over folly, like the advantage of light over darkness.

**2:14** The wise man's eyes are in his head, and the fool walks in darkness. And I too came to know that one fate will meet all of them.

**2:15** And I said in my heart, "As is the fate of the fool, even to me, it will meet me; and why did I become wise?" I spoke excessively in my heart that this too is futility, because the fool speaks out of excess.

**2:16** For there is no remembrance of the wise man with the fool forever, since already in the coming days all things have been forgotten; and how will the wise man die with the fool?

**2:17** And I hated life, because the work that has been done under the sun was evil to me, for everything is futility and a choice of spirit.

**2:18** And I hated all my toil at which I labor under the sun, because I leave it to the man who comes after me.

**2:19** And who knows whether he will be wise or a fool? And will he have authority over all my toil at which I toiled and acted wisely under the sun? This too is futility.

**2:20** And I turned to renounce my heart concerning all my toil at which I toiled under the sun,

**2:21** because there is a man whose toil is in wisdom and in knowledge and in courage, and to a man who did not toil in it he will give his portion; this too is futility and a great evil.

**2:22** For it happens to man in all his toil and in the choice of his heart at which he toils under the sun.

**2:23** For all his days are of pains, and his preoccupation is of anger; even at night his heart does not sleep. This too is futility.

**2:24** There is no good for a man, that he should eat and that he should drink and that he should show his soul good in his toil; this too I saw, that it is from the hand of God.

**2:25** For who will eat and who will drink apart from Him?

**2:26** For to the man who is good before His face He has given wisdom and knowledge and gladness; and to the one who sins He has given the preoccupation of adding and of gathering, to give to the one who is good before the face of God. For this too is futility and a choice of spirit.

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — Greek reads "like wine" (hos oinon, vs. MT "with wine") and "gladness" (euphrosyne, vs. MT "folly").
- **2:8** — Greek renders the obscure Hebrew phrase shiddah weshiddot as "a male cupbearer and female cupbearers" (oinochoon kai oinochoas, often rendered "concubines" from the Hebrew).
- **2:12** — Greek reads "the counsel" (tes boules, reflecting the Aramaic sense of the root for "king"); the closing clause ta hosa epoiesen auten ("all the things that he did to it") is syntactically compressed and difficult.
- **2:15** — The closing clause "because the fool speaks out of excess" is a distinctive Greek reading absent from the Hebrew MT, explaining the preceding words "I spoke excessively in my heart."
- **2:22** — Greek reads a declarative statement ("For it happens to man...") rather than the Hebrew rhetorical question ("For what does a man have...?"). In v. 24, Greek (like the unemended Hebrew) lacks "except/better," implying that enjoying toil is not a good man produces himself but comes from God (cf. v. 25, where Greek reads "drink apart from Him" vs. MT "have enjoyment apart from me").
