## Text

**7:1** A name is better than good oil, and the day of death than the day of birth.

**7:2** It is better to go to a house of mourning than to go to a house of drinking, because this is the end of every man, and the living one will put good into his heart.

**7:3** Anger is better than laughter, because through badness of face it will be made good.

**7:4** The heart of the wise is in a house of mourning, and the heart of fools is in a house of gladness.

**7:5** It is better to hear the rebuke of a wise man than for a man to hear the song of fools.

**7:6** Like the sound of thorns under the pot, so is the laughter of fools. Indeed, this also is futility.

**7:7** For oppression drives a wise man mad and destroys the heart of his nobility.

**7:8** The end of words is better than their beginning; one who is patient is better than one who is lofty in spirit.

**7:9** Do not be hasty in your spirit to be angry, for anger will rest in the bosom of fools.

**7:10** Do not say, "Why did it happen that the former days were better than these?" for it is not in wisdom that you asked about this.

**7:11** Wisdom is good with an inheritance, and an advantage for those who see the sun.

**7:12** For in its shadow wisdom is like the shadow of silver, and the advantage of the knowledge of wisdom will give life to the one who belongs to it.

**7:13** Look at the works of God, for who will be able to set in order the one whom God has made crooked?

**7:14** In a day of goodness live in good, and consider in a day of adversity. Look, God also made this in harmony with that concerning speech, so that man may find nothing after him.

**7:15** I saw all things in the days of my futility: there is a righteous man perishing in his righteousness, and there is an ungodly man remaining in his evil.

**7:16** Do not become greatly righteous, nor make yourself excessively wise, so that you may not be stunned.

**7:17** Do not be greatly ungodly, and do not become hard, so that you may not die when it is not your time.

**7:18** It is good that you hold fast to this, and from this also do not defile your hand, for all things will come forth for those who fear God.

**7:19** Wisdom will help the wise man more than ten rulers who are in the city.

**7:20** For there is no righteous man on the earth who will do good and will not sin.

**7:21** Also, do not set your heart on all the words that the ungodly will speak, so that you may not hear your servant cursing you;

**7:22** for very often he will treat you wickedly and many times he will afflict your heart, because in the same way you also cursed others.

**7:23** All these things I tested in wisdom. I said, "I will become wise," yet it was far removed from me.

**7:24** Far beyond what was, and a deep depth, who will find it?

**7:25** I myself turned about, and my heart, to know and to examine and to seek wisdom and a reckoning, and to know the foolishness of an ungodly man, and disturbance and madness.

**7:26** And I find her, and I will declare what is more bitter than death: the woman who is a snare, and her heart is dragnets, a bond on her hands. One who is good before the face of God will be delivered from her, and a sinner will be captured by her.

**7:27** Look, this I found, said the Assembler, one by one, to find reasoning,

**7:28** which my soul sought, and I did not find; and one man out of a thousand I found, but a woman among all these I did not find.

**7:29** Only look, this I found: that God made man upright, yet they themselves sought many reasonings. Who knows the wise? And who knows the interpretation of a word?

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — Greek uses "anger" (matching 7:9) and "badness of face" (a literal rendering of the Hebrew idiom for a sad countenance), and omits the noun "heart" found in the Hebrew text before "will be made good."
- **7:7** — Greek reads "the heart of his nobility" as the object of "destroys," diverging from the Hebrew text ("a bribe destroys the heart").
- **7:18** — Greek reads "do not defile your hand" rather than the Hebrew "do not withdraw your hand," and makes "all things" the subject of "will come forth" for those who fear God.
- **7:22** — Greek diverges markedly from the Hebrew ("your heart knows that many times you yourself have cursed others"), stating instead that the servant will often treat you wickedly and afflict your heart.
- **7:29** — Greek marks the direct object "man" with the preposition syn ("with"), a mechanical rendering of the Hebrew direct-object particle also seen in 7:26, 8:8, 8:15, 8:17, and 9:15. Following Septuagint versification, the closing two questions belong to 7:29 rather than 8:1, with Greek reading "Who knows the wise?" (plural) instead of Hebrew "Who is like the wise man?"
