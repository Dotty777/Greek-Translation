## Text

**11:1** Send out your bread upon the surface of the water, for in a multitude of days you will find it.

**11:2** Give a portion to seven, and even to eight, for you do not know what evil will be on the earth.

**11:3** If the clouds are filled with rain, they pour it out on the earth; and if a tree falls in the south or if in the north, in the place where the tree will fall, there it will be.

**11:4** One who observes the wind will not sow, and one who looks at the clouds will not harvest.

**11:5** In matters where there is no one who knows what the way of the spirit is, like bones in the womb of a pregnant woman, so you will not know the works of God, all that He will make, all things together.

**11:6** In the morning sow your seed, and in the evening do not let your hand slacken, for you do not know which one will succeed, either this or that, or whether both alike are good.

**11:7** And sweet is the light, and it is good for the eyes to see the sun.

**11:8** For even if a man lives many years, he will rejoice in all of them, and he will remember the days of darkness, for they will be many; everything that is coming is futility.

**11:9** Rejoice, young man, in your youth, and let your heart cheer you in the days of your youth, and walk blameless in the ways, and not in the sight of your eyes; and know that for all these things God will bring you into judgment.

**11:10** And remove anger from your heart, and put away evil from your flesh, for youth and folly are futility.

## Translator Notes

- **11:5** — The Greek begins with third-person "there is no one who knows" (rather than second-person "you do not know") and uses pneuma, which can mean either "spirit" or "wind."
- **11:7** — The Greek idiom syn with the accusative (syn ton helion, also in 12:9) reflects an ultra-literal rendering of the Hebrew direct-object marker rather than the preposition "with."
- **11:9** — The Greek text omits "of your heart," adds "blameless," and inserts the negative "and not" before "in the sight of your eyes," differing markedly from the Hebrew text.
- **11:10** — The Greek reads "folly" (anoia, literally senselessness) where the Hebrew text refers to dark hair or the prime of youth.
