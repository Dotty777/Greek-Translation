## Text

**5:1** Do not be hasty with your mouth, and do not let your heart hurry to bring out a word before the face of God; for God is in heaven above, and you are on the earth; therefore let your words be few.

**5:2** For a dream comes in an abundance of trial, and the voice of a fool in an abundance of words.

**5:3** When you vow a vow to God, do not delay to pay it. There is no pleasure in fools; you, therefore, pay whatever you vow.

**5:4** It is better for you not to vow than for you to vow and not pay.

**5:5** Do not let your mouth cause your flesh to sin, and do not say before the face of God, "It is an oversight," so that God does not become angry at your voice and destroy the works of your hands.

**5:6** For in an abundance of dreams and futilities and many words—rather, as for you, fear God.

**5:7** If you see oppression of a poor person and robbery of justice and righteousness in a province, do not be amazed at the matter; a high one is over a high one to watch, and high ones are over them;

**5:8** and the advantage of the land; over all is a king of the cultivated field.

**5:9** One who loves silver will not be satisfied with silver; and who has loved yield in their abundance? This too is futility.

**5:10** In an abundance of goodness, those who eat it multiply; and what prowess is there for its owner? For it is a beginning for his eye to see.

**5:11** Sweet is the sleep of the servant, whether he eats little or much; but for the one who is filled with becoming rich, there is no one letting him sleep.

**5:12** There is a sickness that I saw under the sun: wealth guarded for its owner to his harm,

**5:13** and that wealth will perish in an evil preoccupation, and he fathered a son, and there is nothing in his hand;

**5:14** just as he came out from his mother's womb naked, he will return to go as he has come, and he will take nothing in his toil so that it may go in his hand.

**5:15** This too is an evil sickness: for just as he came, so also he will depart; and the advantage to him for which he toils is for the wind.

**5:16** Moreover, all his days are in darkness and in mourning and much anger and sickness and wrath.

**5:17** Look, I have seen a good thing that is fitting: to eat and to drink and to see goodness in all his toil in which he toils under the sun for the number of the days of his life that God has given him; for this is his portion.

**5:18** Moreover, every person to whom God has given wealth and possessions and granted authority over them, to eat from it and to take his share and to rejoice in his toil—this is a gift of God.

**5:19** For he will not remember other things during the days of his life, because God preoccupies him in the gladness of his heart.

## Translator Notes

- **5:5** — The Greek reads "before the face of God" where the Hebrew reads "before the messenger," and ἄγνοια denotes an unwitting error or oversight.
- **5:6** — The opening clause in Greek is elliptical, placing "dreams," "futilities," and "many words" all in the genitive governed by "in an abundance of."
- **5:9** — Unlike the Hebrew negative statement, the Greek turns the second clause into a question without a negative ("and who has loved yield in their abundance?").
- **5:10** — The Greek reads ἀρχή ("a beginning," likely an early scribal variant from ἀλλ᾽ ἤ "except") and uses the literal idiom τῷ παρ᾽ αὐτῆς ("the one beside it") to mean "its owner" (similarly in 5:12).
- **5:19** — The Greek reads οὐκ ἄλλα ("not other things"), a distinctive Septuagint reading corresponding to "not much" (οὐ πολλά) in other Greek witnesses and the Hebrew.
