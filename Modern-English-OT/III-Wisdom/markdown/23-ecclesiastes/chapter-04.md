## Text

**4:1** And I turned back and saw all the oppressions that take place under the sun; look, the tear of those who are oppressed, and there is no one comforting them; and from the hand of those who oppress them there is power, and there is no one comforting them.

**4:2** And I praised all the dead who have already died more than the living, as many as are themselves living until now;

**4:3** and better than these two is the one who has not yet come into being, who has not seen all the evil work that has been done under the sun.

**4:4** And I saw all the toil and all the prowess of the work, that jealousy comes to a man from his companion; this too is futility and choice of spirit.

**4:5** The fool clasps his hands together and eats his own flesh.

**4:6** Better is a full handful of rest than two full handfuls of toil and choice of spirit.

**4:7** And I turned back and saw futility under the sun.

**4:8** There is one, and there is no second; indeed, he has neither son nor brother, and there is no end to all his toil, and even his eye is not satisfied with wealth. And for whom am I toiling and depriving my soul of goodness? This too is futility and an evil preoccupation.

**4:9** Two are better than one, who have a good reward in their toil;

**4:10** for if they fall, the one will lift up his partner; and woe to that single one when he falls and there is no second to lift him up.

**4:11** Moreover, if two lie down, then there is warmth for them; and how can the single one be warmed?

**4:12** And if one prevails, the two will stand against him, and the threefold cord will not quickly be torn apart.

**4:13** Better is a poor and wise youth than an old and foolish king who no longer knows how to pay attention;

**4:14** for from the house of the prisoners he will come out to reign, because even in his kingdom he became poor.

**4:15** I saw all the living who walk under the sun with the second young man, who will stand in his place;

**4:16** there is no end to all the people, to all who were before them; yet even the later ones will not rejoice in him. For this too is futility and choice of spirit.

**4:17** Guard your foot whenever you go to the house of God, and be near to listen; better than the gift of fools is your sacrifice, for they are not aware of doing evil.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Greek includes the preposition σὺν before πᾶν τὸ ποίημα ("all the evil work"), an ultra-literal rendering of the Hebrew direct-object marker characteristic of Septuagint Ecclesiastes.
- **4:4** — Greek ἀνδρεία literally means "manliness," "vigor," or "prowess" (used in Septuagint Ecclesiastes for skill or capability), and προαίρεσις πνεύματος literally means "choice" or "preference of spirit," rather than "striving after wind."
- **4:12** — Greek ἐπικραταιωθῇ is a middle/passive verb meaning "prevails" or "grows strong," making "one" the assailant against whom "the two will stand."
- **4:17** — Unlike the Hebrew (5:1), the Greek divides the clause so that "your sacrifice" is better than "the gift of fools," and ends literally "they are not knowing of doing evil."
