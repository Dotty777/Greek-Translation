## Text

**3:1** For all things there is a time, and a season for every matter under heaven.

**3:2** A season to give birth and a season to die, a season to plant and a season to uproot what has been planted,

**3:3** a season to kill and a season to heal, a season to tear down and a season to build,

**3:4** a season to weep and a season to laugh, a season to mourn and a season to dance,

**3:5** a season to throw stones and a season to gather stones, a season to embrace and a season to keep far from embracing,

**3:6** a season to seek and a season to lose, a season to keep and a season to cast away,

**3:7** a season to tear and a season to sew, a season to keep silent and a season to speak,

**3:8** a season to love and a season to hate, a season of war and a season of peace.

**3:9** What is the advantage of the worker in the things at which he toils?

**3:10** I saw all the preoccupation that God has given to the sons of men to be preoccupied with it.

**3:11** Everything that He has made is beautiful in its season; He has also put all eternity in their heart, so that man may not find the work that God has done from the beginning even to the end.

**3:12** I came to know that there is no good among them, except to be glad and to do good in his life;

**3:13** and also, every man who will eat and drink and see good in all his toil—it is a gift of God.

**3:14** I came to know that all things that God has done, they will be forever; there is no adding to it, and there is no taking away from it; and God has done so, that they may fear before His face.

**3:15** What has come to be already is, and all things that are to come to be have already come to be; and God will seek the one who is pursued.

**3:16** And moreover I saw under the sun the place of judgment—there was the ungodly one; and the place of the righteous one—there was the godly one.

**3:17** And I said in my heart, "Both the righteous and the ungodly God will judge, for there is a season for every matter and for every work."

**3:18** And I said in my heart concerning the speech of the sons of man, that God will judge them, and to show that they themselves are beasts.

**3:19** And indeed for them, is there not a fate of the sons of man and a fate of the beast? One fate belongs to them: as is the death of the one, so is the death of the other, and there is one spirit for all of them. And what advantage has man over the beast? Nothing, for everything is futility.

**3:20** All things go to one place; all things came to be from the dust, and all things will return to the dust.

**3:21** And who knows the spirit of the sons of man, whether it goes upward? And the spirit of the beast, whether it goes downward into the earth?

**3:22** And I saw that there is no good, except that a man should be glad in his works, for that is his portion; for who will bring him to see whatever may come to be after him?

## Translator Notes

- **3:11** — Greek sympanta ton aiona literally means "all eternity" or "the whole age," adding "all" compared to the Hebrew MT; "in its season" (en kairo autou) could also be read "in His season."
- **3:15** — Greek ton diokomenon is masculine singular ("the one who is pursued" or "persecuted"), rather than neuter ("what has passed away").
- **3:16** — Greek reads "there was the godly one" (ho eusebes) in the second clause, contrasting with "the ungodly one" (ho asebes) in the first, whereas the Hebrew MT has "wickedness" in both places.
- **3:19** — Greek opens with a rhetorical question ("And indeed for them, is there not...?") and answers with "Nothing" (ouden); Greek pneuma here and in v. 21 can mean either "spirit" or "breath."
