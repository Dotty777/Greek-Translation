## Text

**5:1** Remember, Lord, what has happened to us; look and see our reproach.

**5:2** Our inheritance has been transferred to outsiders, our houses to foreigners.

**5:3** We have become orphans, there is no father; our mothers are like widows.

**5:4** From our days, our wood came at a price; upon our neck

**5:5** we were pursued; we grew weary, we were not given rest.

**5:6** Egypt gave a hand, Assyria, for their satisfaction.

**5:7** Our fathers sinned, they are no more; we have borne their lawless deeds.

**5:8** Servants have ruled over us; there is no redeemer from their hand.

**5:9** With our lives we will bring in our bread because of the sword of the wilderness.

**5:10** Our skin turned livid like an oven; they were shriveled up because of the storms of famine.

**5:11** They humiliated women in Zion, virgins in the cities of Judah.

**5:12** Rulers were hung by their hands; elders were not honored.

**5:13** Chosen men took up weeping, and young men grew weak under wood.

**5:14** And old men ceased from the gate; chosen men ceased from their songs.

**5:15** The joy of our heart has come to an end; our dance has been turned into mourning.

**5:16** The crown of our head has fallen. Woe now to us, for we have sinned.

**5:17** Because of this our heart has become a painful grief; because of this our eyes have grown dark.

**5:18** Over Mount Zion, because it has been devastated, foxes have passed through it.

**5:19** But You, Lord, will dwell forever; Your throne is to generation and generation.

**5:20** Why will You utterly forget us? Will You forsake us for length of days?

**5:21** Turn us back, Lord, to Yourself, and we will be turned back; and renew our days as before.

**5:22** For You have completely rejected us; You have become exceedingly angry with us.

## Translator Notes

- **5:4** — The Greek omits the Hebrew clause about buying water for silver, beginning instead with "From our days" (ex hemeron hemon). The closing phrase "upon our neck" continues directly into the opening verb of 5:5 ("we were pursued").
- **5:6** — The Greek makes "Egypt" the subject of a third-person singular verb ("gave a hand") and omits "bread," which appears in the Hebrew.
- **5:10** — The Greek pairs a singular verb ("turned livid," epeliothe) with a plural verb ("they were shriveled up," synespasthesan) and reads "storms of famine" (kataigidon limou).
- **5:13** — The Greek reads "chosen men" (eklektoi, also in 5:14) and "took up weeping" (klauthmon anelabon), where the Hebrew refers to young men bearing the millstone.
- **5:20** — "Utterly" translates Greek eis nikos (literally "to victory"), an established Septuagint idiom meaning "completely" or "permanently."
