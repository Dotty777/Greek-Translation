## Text

**21:1** For the end; concerning the morning aid. A psalm of David.

**21:2** God, my God, attend to me; why have You abandoned me? Far from my salvation are the words of my transgressions.

**21:3** My God, I will cry out by day to You, and You will not hear; and by night, and not to folly for me.

**21:4** Yet You dwell in holy places, the praise of Israel.

**21:5** In You our fathers hoped; they hoped, and You rescued them.

**21:6** To You they cried out and were saved; in You they hoped and were not put to shame.

**21:7** But I am a worm and not a man, a reproach of man and an object of contempt to the people.

**21:8** All who look at me sneered at me; they spoke with their lips, they shook their head:

**21:9** "He hoped in the Lord; let Him rescue him; let Him save him, because He delights in him."

**21:10** For You are the One who drew me out from the womb, my hope from my mother's breasts.

**21:11** On You I was cast from the womb; from my mother's womb You are my God.

**21:12** Do not withdraw from me, for affliction is near, for there is no one who helps.

**21:13** Many calves encircled me; fat bulls hemmed me in.

**21:14** They opened their mouth against me, like a lion that seizes and roars.

**21:15** Like water I was poured out, and all my bones were scattered; my heart became like wax melting in the midst of my belly.

**21:16** My strength dried up like a potsherd, and my tongue has clung to my throat, and into the dust of death You brought me down.

**21:17** For many dogs surrounded me; a gathering of evildoers hemmed me in; they pierced my hands and feet.

**21:18** They counted all my bones; and they themselves observed and looked at me.

**21:19** They divided my garments among themselves, and for my clothing they cast a lot.

**21:20** But You, Lord, do not put my help far away; attend to my aid.

**21:21** Rescue my soul from the sword, and my only one from the hand of a dog.

**21:22** Save me from the mouth of a lion, and my lowliness from the horns of unicorns.

**21:23** I will recount Your name to my brothers; in the midst of the assembly I will sing hymns to You.

**21:24** You who fear the Lord, praise Him; all the offspring of Jacob, glorify Him; let all the offspring of Israel fear Him.

**21:25** For He did not treat with contempt nor loathe the petition of the poor, nor did He turn His face away from me; and when I cried out to Him, He heard me.

**21:26** From You is my praise in a great assembly; I will pay my vows before those who fear Him.

**21:27** The needy will eat and be filled, and those who seek Him out will praise the Lord; their hearts will live forever and ever.

**21:28** All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations will worship before Him;

**21:29** for the kingdom belongs to the Lord, and He Himself rules over the nations.

**21:30** All the fat ones of the earth ate and worshiped; all who go down into the earth will fall prostrate before Him, and my soul lives for Him.

**21:31** And my offspring will serve Him; the coming generation will be declared to the Lord,

**21:32** and they will declare His righteousness to a people that will be born, whom the Lord made.

## Translator Notes

- **21:1** — The Greek superscription literally reads "For the end" (rendering the Hebrew musical heading) and "concerning the morning aid," where the Septuagint read "aid/strength" rather than the Masoretic Hebrew "doe."
- **21:2** — The Septuagint adds "attend to me" after "God, my God" and reads "the words of my transgressions" where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "the words of my groaning."
- **21:3** — The Greek phrase "not to folly for me" (eis anoian) differs from the Masoretic Hebrew, which reads "there is no rest/silence for me."
- **21:17** — The Greek verb oryxan literally means "they dug" (used here in the sense of boring into or piercing), contrasting with the Masoretic Hebrew reading "like a lion."
- **21:22** — Greek monokeroton literally means "of unicorns" (one-horned beasts), rendering the Hebrew for "wild oxen"; "my lowliness" reflects a different vocalization of the Hebrew read in the Masoretic Text as "You have answered me."
