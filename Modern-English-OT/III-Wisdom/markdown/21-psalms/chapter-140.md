# Psalm 140 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**140:1** A psalm for David. Lord, I cried out to You; hear me; pay attention to the voice of my supplication when I cry out to You.

**140:2** Let my prayer be directed as incense before You, the lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice.

**140:3** Lord, set a guard over my mouth and a door of enclosure around my lips.

**140:4** Do not turn my heart aside to words of wickedness, to make excuses in sins with people who work lawlessness, and I will never associate with their chosen ones.

**140:5** A righteous one will discipline me in mercy and reprove me, but let the oil of a sinner not anoint my head; for yet again and again my prayer is in their pleasures.

**140:6** Their strong ones were swallowed up beside a rock; they will hear my words, because they prevailed.

**140:7** As a clod of earth was broken apart upon the earth, our bones were scattered beside Hades.

**140:8** For toward You, Lord, Lord, are my eyes; in You I have hoped; do not take away my soul.

**140:9** Guard me from the snare that they set up for me, and from the stumbling blocks of those who work lawlessness.

**140:10** Sinners will fall into his net; I am alone until I pass by.

## Translator Notes

- **140:3** — Greek "thyran perioches" literally means "a door of enclosure" (or "fortification"), an LXX phrase for a protective barrier restraining speech.
- **140:4** — Greek "syndoiaso" (from "syndoiazo", a spelling variant of "syndyazo", "join" or "associate with") and "ton eklekton auton" ("their chosen ones", or neuter "their choice things") reflect an LXX reading distinct from the Hebrew MT ("eat of their delicacies").
- **140:5** — The LXX reads "in their pleasures" (Greek "eudokiais", good pleasures or delights) rather than the Hebrew MT "against their evil deeds," and the doubled adverb "eti kai eti" emphasizes persistence ("yet again and again").
- **140:6** — This Greek text reads "hoi krataioi auton" ("their strong ones") and "edynethesan" ("they prevailed", from "dynamai"), whereas other LXX witnesses read "their judges" and "hedynthesan" ("they were sweet").
- **140:10** — Greek "autou" ("his") is singular, either used distributively for each sinner's own net or referring to the Lord's net; "kata monas" ("alone") is an LXX reading differing from the Hebrew MT.
