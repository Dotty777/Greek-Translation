# Psalm 57 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**57:1** For the end; do not destroy; for David, for a pillar inscription.

**57:2** Do you then truly speak righteousness? Do you judge uprightly, sons of men?

**57:3** For indeed, in your heart you work acts of lawlessness on the earth; your hands weave together unrighteousness.

**57:4** Sinners were estranged from the womb; they went astray from the belly; they spoke falsehoods.

**57:5** They have fury according to the likeness of a serpent, like a deaf asp that stops up its ears,

**57:6** which will not listen to the voice of charmers or to a charm being worked by a wise one.

**57:7** God crushed their teeth in their mouth; the Lord shattered the molars of the lions.

**57:8** They will be brought to nothing like water flowing away; He will bend His bow until they grow weak.

**57:9** Like wax that has melted they will be taken away; fire fell, and they did not see the sun.

**57:10** Before your thorns understand the bramble, as if alive, as if in wrath, He will swallow you up.

**57:11** A righteous one will rejoice when he sees vengeance on the ungodly; he will wash his hands in the blood of the sinner.

**57:12** And a person will say, "Is there then fruit for the righteous one? Indeed, God is judging them on the earth."

## Translator Notes

- **57:5** — The Greek reads thymos ("fury" or "wrath"), reflecting a translation of Hebrew chemah in its sense of anger rather than "venom."
- **57:6** — The Greek phrase pharmakou te pharmakeuomenou para sophou literally refers to a charm or potion being worked or prepared by a wise or skilled person.
- **57:9** — The Greek reads "like wax that has melted" (hosei keros ho takeis) and "fire fell" (epepesen pyr), differing markedly from the Masoretic Text ("like a snail" and "a stillborn child").
- **57:10** — This difficult Greek line literally reads "before your thorns understand the bramble," rendering terms for pots and thornbush with two words for thorny plants.
- **57:11** — The Greek explicitly specifies vengeance "on the ungodly" (asebon) and reads "his hands" (tas cheiras autou) rather than "his feet" as in the Masoretic Text.
