## Text

**151:1** This psalm is written in David's own hand and is outside the number, when he fought in single combat against Goliath. I was small among my brothers and the youngest in my father's house; I was shepherding my father's sheep.

**151:2** My hands made a musical instrument; my fingers tuned a harp.

**151:3** And who will tell my Lord? The Lord Himself, He Himself hears.

**151:4** He Himself sent out His messenger, and took me from my father's sheep, and anointed me with the oil of His anointing.

**151:5** My brothers were handsome and tall, and the Lord did not take pleasure in them.

**151:6** I went out to meet the foreigner, and he cursed me by his idols.

**151:7** But I drew the sword from him, beheaded him, and took away reproach from the sons of Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **151:1** — Psalm 151 stands in the Septuagint outside the 150-psalm collection; Greek idiographos eis Dauid literally means 'written in his own hand, pertaining to David.'
- **151:4** — Or 'His angel'; Greek angelos can mean either a human messenger (here referring to the prophet Samuel, who anointed David in 1 Samuel 16) or a heavenly angel.
- **151:6** — Greek allophylos literally means 'foreigner' or 'one of another tribe' and is the Septuagint's regular designation for a Philistine.
- **151:7** — Greek ten par autou machairan literally means 'the sword from him,' referring to Goliath's own sword taken from his side.
