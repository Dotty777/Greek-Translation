## Text

**34:1** For David. Judge, Lord, those who wrong me; wage war against those who wage war against me.

**34:2** Take hold of weapon and shield, and rise up to my help.

**34:3** Pour out a sword and close in against those who pursue me; say to my soul, "I am your salvation."

**34:4** May those who seek my soul be shamed and disgraced; may those who devise evil against me be turned backward and utterly shamed.

**34:5** Let them become like dust before the face of the wind, with an angel of the Lord pressing them hard.

**34:6** Let their way become darkness and slipperiness, with an angel of the Lord pursuing them.

**34:7** For without cause they hid for me the destruction of their snare; without reason they reproached my soul.

**34:8** Let a snare come upon them which they do not know, and let the trap which they hid seize them; and into the snare itself they will fall.

**34:9** But my soul will exult in the Lord; it will take delight in His salvation.

**34:10** All my bones will say, "Lord, who is like You, rescuing a poor man from the hand of those stronger than he, and a poor and needy man from those who plunder him?"

**34:11** Rising up, unjust witnesses questioned me about things I did not know.

**34:12** They were repaying me evil for good, and childlessness to my soul.

**34:13** But I, when they were troubling me, would clothe myself with sackcloth and would humble my soul with fasting; and my prayer will return to my bosom.

**34:14** As to a neighbor, as to our brother, so I acted pleasingly; like one mourning and downcast, so I humbled myself.

**34:15** And against me they rejoiced and gathered together; scourges gathered together against me, and I did not know; they were torn apart and felt no remorse.

**34:16** They tested me, they mocked me with mockery, they gnashed their teeth against me.

**34:17** Lord, when will You look on? Restore my soul from their malice, my only one from lions.

**34:18** I will give thanks to You, Lord, in a great assembly; among a mighty people I will praise You.

**34:19** May they not gloat over me, those who are hostile to me without reason, those who hate me without cause and wink with their eyes.

**34:20** For to me they were speaking peaceful things, yet in anger they were devising deceits.

**34:21** And they opened wide their mouth against me; they said, "Aha, aha, our eyes have seen!"

**34:22** You have seen, Lord; do not remain silent; Lord, do not withdraw from me.

**34:23** Rouse Yourself, Lord, and attend to my judgment, my God and my Lord, to my cause.

**34:24** Judge me, Lord, according to Your righteousness, Lord my God, and may they not gloat over me.

**34:25** May they not say in their hearts, "Aha, aha, for our soul!" nor may they say, "We have swallowed him up."

**34:26** May those who gloat over my misfortunes be shamed and disgraced together; let those who speak boastfully against me clothe themselves with shame and disgrace.

**34:27** May those who desire my righteousness exult and rejoice; and let those who desire the peace of His servant say continually, "May the Lord be magnified."

**34:28** And my tongue will rehearse Your righteousness, all the day Your praise.

## Translator Notes

- **34:3** — The Greek literally reads "Pour out a sword" (ἔκχεον ῥομφαίαν) and "close in" (σύνκλεισον), reflecting the Septuagint's distinct rendering of the Hebrew weaponry and blocking imagery.
- **34:7** — The Greek reads "the destruction of their snare" (διαφθορὰν παγίδος αὐτῶν) and "reproached" (ὠνείδισαν), differing from the Hebrew imagery of a hidden pit dug for the psalmist.
- **34:13** — The Greek reads "when they were troubling me" (ἐν τῷ αὐτοὺς παρενοχλεῖν μοι) rather than "when they were sick," and uses a future verb ("will return," ἀποστραφήσεται) in the final clause.
- **34:20** — The Greek reads "to me they were speaking peaceful things" (ἐμοὶ μὲν εἰρηνικὰ ἐλάλουν) and "in anger" (ἐπ᾽ ὀργῇ), contrasting with the Hebrew negative ("they do not speak peace") and "quiet ones of the land."
- **34:27** — In the Greek text, "those who desire" (οἱ θέλοντες) is plural, serving as the subject of "let them say" rather than modifying the singular "Lord."
