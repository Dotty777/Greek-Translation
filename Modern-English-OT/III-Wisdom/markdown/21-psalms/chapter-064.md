## Text

**64:1** For the end; a psalm of David, a song.

**64:2** To You a hymn is fitting, God, in Zion, and to You a vow will be paid.

**64:3** Hear my prayer; to You all flesh will come.

**64:4** Words of the lawless overpowered us, and You will pardon our impieties.

**64:5** Blessed is the one whom You have chosen and taken to Yourself; he will dwell in Your courts. We will be filled with the good things of Your house; holy is Your temple,

**64:6** marvelous in righteousness. Answer us, God our Savior, the hope of all the ends of the earth,

**64:7** preparing mountains in Your strength, girded with might,

**64:8** the One who troubles the water of the sea, the sounds of its waves. The nations will be troubled,

**64:9** and those who inhabit the ends will fear at Your signs; You will delight the outgoings of morning and evening.

**64:10** You visited the earth and drenched it; You multiplied to enrich it; the river of God was filled with waters; You prepared their food, for thus is Your preparation.

**64:11** Drench its furrows, multiply its crops; in its drops it will rejoice as it sprouts.

**64:12** You will bless the crown of the year of Your goodness, and Your plains will be filled with fatness;

**64:13** the mountains of the wilderness will grow fat, and the hills will gird themselves with exultation.

**64:14** The rams of the sheep have clothed themselves, and the valleys will multiply grain; they will shout, for indeed they will sing hymns.

## Translator Notes

- **64:3** — Greek has the imperative "Hear my prayer" (εἰσάκουσον προσευχῆς μου), whereas the Hebrew MT has a participial address, "O You who hear prayer."
- **64:6** — Greek joins "marvelous in righteousness" to the temple from v. 5 and follows with the imperative "Answer us" (ἐπάκουσον), differing from the Hebrew MT ("By awesome deeds in righteousness You answer us"); this Greek text also ends at "the ends of the earth."
- **64:8** — Greek "troubles" (συνταράσσων) and "The nations will be troubled" diverge from the Hebrew MT, which speaks of God stilling the roaring of the seas and the tumult of the peoples.
- **64:12** — Greek "You will bless the crown of the year of Your goodness" brings the verb "bless" from v. 11 and treats "crown" as a noun, whereas the Hebrew MT reads "You crown the year with Your goodness."
- **64:14** — Greek "The rams of the sheep have clothed themselves" renders a Hebrew word that can mean either "rams" or "meadows," the latter yielding the MT sense "the meadows clothe themselves with flocks."
