## Text

**18:1** For the end; a psalm for David.

**18:2** The heavens recount the glory of God, and the expanse announces the work of His hands.

**18:3** Day pours forth an utterance to day, and night announces knowledge to night.

**18:4** There are neither speeches nor words whose voices are not heard.

**18:5** Into all the earth their sound has gone out, and to the ends of the inhabited world their utterances.

**18:6** In the sun He set His tent; and it, like a bridegroom coming out from his bridal chamber, will exult like a giant to run its course.

**18:7** From the end of heaven is its going out, and its destination is to the end of heaven; and there is no one who will be hidden from its heat.

**18:8** The law of the Lord is blameless, restoring souls; the testimony of the Lord is faithful, making infants wise.

**18:9** The ordinances of the Lord are upright, gladdening the heart; the commandment of the Lord is radiant, illuminating the eyes.

**18:10** The fear of the Lord is pure, remaining forever and ever; the judgments of the Lord are true, justified altogether,

**18:11** more desirable than gold and much precious stone, and sweeter than honey and honeycomb.

**18:12** For indeed Your servant keeps them; in keeping them there is much reward.

**18:13** Who will understand transgressions? Cleanse me from my hidden things,

**18:14** and spare Your servant from strangers; if they do not rule over me, then I will be blameless and I will be cleansed from great sin.

**18:15** And the sayings of my mouth and the meditation of my heart will be well-pleasing before You continually, Lord, my helper and my redeemer.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — Greek eis to telos ("for the end") is the standard Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew musical heading often translated "for the choir director," and to Dauid is a dative ("for/of David"). In Septuagint numbering, Psalms 18-20 correspond to Psalms 19-21 in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **18:5** — Greek ho phthongos auton ("their sound") is the Septuagint reading quoted in Romans 10:18, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "their measuring line."
- **18:6** — The Greek reads "In the sun He set His tent" (and gigas, "giant"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "In them He set a tent for the sun" (and "strong man").
- **18:11** — Greek lithon timion polyn ("much precious stone") corresponds to "much fine gold" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text (compare 20:4).
- **18:14** — Greek apo allotrion pheisai ("spare ... from strangers") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("keep back ... from presumptuous ones"), reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew consonants.
