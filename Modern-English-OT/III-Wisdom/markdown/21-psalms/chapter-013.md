## Text

**13:1** For the end; a psalm for David. A fool said in his heart, "There is no God." They acted corruptly and became abominable in practices; there is no one doing goodness, there is not even one.

**13:2** The Lord looked down from heaven upon the sons of men, to see whether there is anyone who understands or seeks out God.

**13:3** All turned aside, together they became worthless; there is no one doing goodness, there is not even one. Their throat is an open tomb; with their tongues they practiced deceit. Venom of asps is beneath their lips, whose mouth is filled with cursing and bitterness. Their feet are quick to shed blood; ruin and wretchedness are in their ways, and they did not know the way of peace. There is no fear of God in front of their eyes.

**13:4** Will all who work unrighteousness not know, those who devour My people like eating bread? They did not call upon the Lord.

**13:5** There they cowered in fear where there was no fear, because God is in a righteous generation.

**13:6** You put to shame the counsel of a poor man, because the Lord is his hope.

**13:7** Who will give from Zion the salvation of Israel? When the Lord turns back the captivity of His people, let Jacob exult and let Israel be glad.

## Translator Notes

- **13:1** — "For the end" literally renders the Septuagint superscription phrase Εἰς τὸ τέλος, which corresponds to the Hebrew musical heading often rendered "For the choir director."
- **13:3** — The extended indictment of human wickedness from "Their throat is an open tomb" through "in front of their eyes" is a notable Septuagint reading (quoted in Romans 3:13-18) that is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text of Psalm 14:3.
- **13:5** — The clause "where there was no fear" is present in the Septuagint here (matching Psalm 52:6 LXX / 53:5 MT) though absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text of Psalm 14:5.
- **13:7** — "Who will give...?" literally renders the Greek idiomatic wish τίς δώσει (reflecting Hebrew "Oh that...").
