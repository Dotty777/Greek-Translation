## Text

**134:1** Hallelujah. Praise the name of the Lord; praise the Lord, servants,

**134:2** you who stand in the house of the Lord, in the courts of the house of our God.

**134:3** Praise the Lord, for the Lord is good; sing praise to His name, for it is lovely.

**134:4** For the Lord chose Jacob for Himself, Israel as His special possession.

**134:5** For I know that the Lord is great, and our Lord is above all the gods.

**134:6** All that He willed, the Lord did in heaven and on earth, in the seas and in the depths.

**134:7** Bringing up clouds from the end of the earth, He made flashes of lightning for rain, He who brings out winds from His storehouses,

**134:8** who struck the firstborn of Egypt, from man to beast.

**134:9** He sent out signs and wonders in your midst, Egypt, against Pharaoh and against all his servants.

**134:10** He struck many nations and killed mighty kings:

**134:11** Sihon king of the Amorites, and Og king of Bashan, and all the kingdoms of Canaan;

**134:12** and He gave their land as an inheritance, an inheritance to Israel His servant.

**134:13** Lord, Your name is forever; Lord, Your remembrance is for generation after generation.

**134:14** The Lord has compassion on His people, and He will be comforted over His servants.

**134:15** The idols of the nations are silver and gold, works of human hands.

**134:16** They have a mouth and do not speak; they have eyes and will not see.

**134:17** They have ears and will not listen, for there is not even breath in their mouth.

**134:18** May all who make them become like them, and all who trust in them.

**134:19** House of Israel, bless the Lord; house of Aaron, bless the Lord.

**134:20** House of Levi, bless the Lord; you who fear the Lord, bless the Lord.

**134:21** Blessed be the Lord from Zion, who inhabits Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **134:1** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("servants of the LORD"), the Greek places "the Lord" in the accusative as the direct object of "praise" and leaves "servants" as a vocative.
- **134:12** — The Greek reads "Israel His servant" (matching 135:22), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Israel his people."
- **134:14** — Our Greek text reads "has compassion on" (oikteirei) rather than "will judge" (krinei, as in other Septuagint witnesses and the Hebrew). The passive "will be comforted" (paraklethesetai) is a Septuagint idiom conveying relenting or being moved to pity.
- **134:21** — The Greek psalm ends without a concluding "Hallelujah," which instead stands at the opening of Psalm 135 (MT 136).
