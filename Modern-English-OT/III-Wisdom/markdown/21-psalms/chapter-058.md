## Text

**58:1** For the end; do not destroy; for David, for a pillar inscription, when Saul sent and watched his house to put him to death.

**58:2** Deliver me from my enemies, God, and redeem me from those who rise up against me.

**58:3** Rescue me from those who work lawlessness, and save me from men of bloodshed.

**58:4** For look, they hunted my soul; strong ones attacked me—it is neither my lawlessness nor my sin, Lord.

**58:5** Without lawlessness I ran and kept a straight course; awake to meet me and see,

**58:6** and You, Lord God of powers, God of Israel, attend to visiting all the nations; do not have pity on any of those who work lawlessness. Interlude.

**58:7** They will return toward evening and go hungry like a dog, and they will go around a city.

**58:8** Look, they will speak out with their mouth, and a sword is on their lips; for, "Who has heard?"

**58:9** And You, Lord, will laugh at them; You will bring all the nations to nothing.

**58:10** I will keep my strength for You, for You are God, my protector.

**58:11** My God—His mercy will go before me; my God will show me among my enemies.

**58:12** Do not kill them, so that they may never forget Your law; scatter them by Your power and bring them down, my defender, Lord.

**58:13** For the sin of their mouth, the word of their lips, let them also be caught in their pride; and because of cursing and falsehood there will be proclaimed

**58:14** destructions, in wrath of destruction, and they will certainly not exist; and they will know that the God of Jacob rules over the ends of the earth. Interlude.

**58:15** They will return toward evening and go hungry like a dog, and they will go around a city;

**58:16** They will be scattered to eat, and if they are satisfied, they will also grumble.

**58:17** But I will sing of Your power, and in the morning I will exult in Your mercy, for You have become my protector and a refuge in a day of affliction.

**58:18** My helper, to You I will make melody, my God, for You are my protector, my God, my mercy.

## Translator Notes

- **58:5** — In the Greek, both verbs ("I ran and kept a straight course," edramon kai kateuthyna) are first-person singular declarations of the speaker's innocence rather than third-person descriptions of his enemies.
- **58:6** — "Lord God of powers" literally renders Kyrie ho theos ton dynameon, the Septuagint equivalent of "Lord God of hosts" (armies or heavenly powers).
- **58:12** — The Greek reads "Your law" (tou nomou sou), whereas the Masoretic Text reads "my people."
- **58:13** — The Greek clause runs across the boundary of verses 13 and 14, with the passive verb "will be proclaimed" (diangelesontai) in verse 13 governing the plural subject "destructions" (synteleiai) in verse 14.
- **58:16** — This Greek text lacks a negative before "satisfied" ("if they are satisfied, they will also grumble"), whereas some other Septuagint witnesses include "not"; "grumble" (gongysousin) also differs from the Masoretic Text's "spend the night."
