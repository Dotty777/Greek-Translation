## Text

**108:1** For the end; a psalm for David. God, do not pass over my praise in silence,

**108:2** for the mouth of a sinner and the mouth of a deceitful man were opened against me; they spoke against me with a deceitful tongue,

**108:3** and with words of hatred they surrounded me, and they fought against me without cause.

**108:4** Instead of loving me, they were slandering me, but I was praying;

**108:5** and they set evil against me in return for good, and hatred in return for my love.

**108:6** Appoint a sinner over him, and let a slanderer stand at his right hand;

**108:7** when he is judged, may he come out condemned, and let his prayer become sin.

**108:8** Let his days be few, and may another take his oversight;

**108:9** let his sons become orphans, and his wife a widow;

**108:10** tossed about, let his sons wander from home and beg; let them be driven out from their ruined homes.

**108:11** And let a creditor search out all that belongs to him; let strangers plunder all his labors;

**108:12** let there be no supporter for him, nor let there be anyone compassionate toward his orphans;

**108:13** let his children come to utter destruction; in one generation let his name be blotted out.

**108:14** May the sin of his fathers be remembered before the Lord, and may the sin of his mother not be blotted out;

**108:15** let them be before the Lord continually, and may their memory be utterly destroyed from the earth,

**108:16** because he did not remember to show mercy, and he pursued a needy and poor man, and one pierced in heart, to put him to death.

**108:17** And he loved a curse, and it will come to him; and he did not desire a blessing, and it will be far removed from him.

**108:18** And he clothed himself with a curse like a garment, and it entered like water into his inward parts and like oil in his bones;

**108:19** let it be to him like a garment that he wraps around himself, and like a belt that he continually fastens around himself.

**108:20** This is the work of those who slander me before the Lord and of those who speak evil things against my soul.

**108:21** And You, Lord, Lord, show mercy to me for the sake of Your name, for Your mercy is good.

**108:22** Rescue me, for I am poor and needy, and my heart is troubled within me.

**108:23** As a shadow, when it declined, was taken away, I was shaken off like locusts.

**108:24** My knees grew weak from fasting, and my flesh was altered on account of oil.

**108:25** And I became a reproach to them; they saw me; they shook their heads.

**108:26** Help me, Lord my God; save me according to Your great mercy,

**108:27** and let them know that this is Your hand, and You, Lord, did it.

**108:28** They will curse, and You will bless; let those who rise up against me be put to shame, but Your servant will rejoice.

**108:29** Let those who slander me clothe themselves with disgrace, and let them wrap shame around themselves like their cloak.

**108:30** I will give thanks to the Lord greatly with my mouth, and in the midst of many I will praise Him,

**108:31** for He stood at the right hand of a needy man, to save me from those who pursue my soul.

## Translator Notes

- **108:1** — "For the end" translates the standard Septuagint superscription phrase (Greek eis to telos). The Greek also makes "my praise" the direct object of "do not pass over in silence," rather than reading "God of my praise."
- **108:6** — Greek diabolos literally means "slanderer" or "accuser," matching the cognate verb "slander" in vv. 4, 20, and 29; elsewhere in biblical Greek the word also denotes the devil.
- **108:20** — The Greek text reads para Kyrio in the dative ("before the Lord"), whereas other witnesses and the Hebrew read "from the Lord" (referring to recompense from the Lord).
- **108:23** — The Greek text reads the third-person passive antanerethe ("was taken away"), referring to the declining shadow, rather than the variant first-person reading ("I was taken away").
- **108:24** — Greek di' elaion literally means "on account of oil," representing the Hebrew sense "for lack of oil."
