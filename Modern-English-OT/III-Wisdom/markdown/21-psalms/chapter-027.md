## Text

**27:1** Of David. To You, Lord, I have cried out; my God, do not keep silent toward me, so that You never keep silent toward me and I become like those who go down into a pit.

**27:2** Hear the voice of my plea when I plead to You, when I lift my hands toward Your holy temple.

**27:3** Do not drag my soul away with sinners, and do not destroy me together with those who work injustice, who speak peace with their neighbors, while evil things are in their hearts.

**27:4** Give to them according to their works and according to the wickedness of their practices; according to the works of their hands give to them; repay their recompense to them.

**27:5** Because they did not understand the works of the Lord and the works of His hands, You will tear them down and will never build them up.

**27:6** Blessed is the Lord, because He has heard the voice of my plea.

**27:7** The Lord is my helper and my defender; in Him my heart hoped, and I was helped, and my flesh flourished anew; and from my will I will give thanks to Him.

**27:8** The Lord is the strength of His people and the defender of the deliverances of His anointed one.

**27:9** Save Your people and bless Your inheritance, and shepherd them and raise them up forever.

## Translator Notes

- **27:1** — Greek reads "my God" (where Hebrew has "my rock") and uses the same verb parasiōpaō ("keep silent") in both clauses.
- **27:5** — Greek shifts to second-person verbs ("You will tear them down and will never build them up") addressing God directly, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text uses third-person verbs.
- **27:7** — Greek "my flesh flourished anew" and "from my will" are distinctive Septuagint readings compared with Hebrew "my heart exults" and "with my song."
- **27:8** — Greek reads "of His people" (where Hebrew has "to them") and plural sōtēria ("deliverances" or "salvations") of "His anointed one" (christou autou).
