## Text

**2:1** Why did nations rage, and peoples meditate on empty things?

**2:2** The kings of the earth took their stand, and the rulers were gathered together against the Lord and against His anointed. Interlude.

**2:3** "Let us tear apart their bonds and cast away their yoke from us."

**2:4** He who dwells in the heavens will laugh at them, and the Lord will mock them.

**2:5** Then He will speak to them in His anger, and in His fury He will dismay them.

**2:6** But I was established by Him upon Zion, His holy mountain,

**2:7** proclaiming the decree of the Lord. The Lord said to Me, "You are My Son; I have fathered You today.

**2:8** Ask from Me, and I will give You nations as Your inheritance, and as Your possession the ends of the earth;

**2:9** You will shepherd them with an iron rod; like a potter's vessel You will shatter them."

**2:10** And now, kings, understand; be instructed, all you who judge the earth.

**2:11** Serve the Lord with fear, and exult in Him with trembling.

**2:12** Take hold of instruction, or else the Lord may become angry, and you will perish from the righteous way, when His fury is quickly kindled. Blessed are all who trust in Him.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — Greek diapsalma ("Interlude") is a musical term indicating a pause or instrumental interlude, corresponding to the Hebrew term Selah (also in 3:3, 3:5).
- **2:6** — The Greek verb is passive ("was established by Him"), making the anointed ruler the speaker rather than God as in the Hebrew text; this Greek witness also omits the word "king" found in some manuscripts.
- **2:7** — Pronouns and "Son" for the speaker (here and in vv. 8-9) are capitalized following traditional messianic reading; in Greek script no uppercase/lowercase distinction exists, and the same speaker is introduced as "His anointed" (2:2) and "king" (2:6).
- **2:9** — Greek poimaneis means "you will shepherd" (echoed in Revelation 2:27; 12:5; 19:15), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonants than the traditional Hebrew "you will break."
- **2:12** — Greek draxasthe paideias ("Take hold of instruction") and "from the righteous way" follow the Septuagint wording, differing from the traditional Hebrew ("Kiss the son" and "in the way").
