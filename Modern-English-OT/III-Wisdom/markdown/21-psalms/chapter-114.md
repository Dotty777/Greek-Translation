## Text

**114:1** Hallelujah. I loved, because God will hear the voice of my plea,

**114:2** because He inclined His ear to me, and in His days I called out.

**114:3** Pangs of death encompassed me, dangers of Hades found me; I found distress and sorrow.

**114:4** And I called upon the name of the Lord: "O Lord, rescue my soul."

**114:5** Merciful and righteous is the Lord, and the Lord our God shows mercy.

**114:6** The Lord guards the infants; I was humbled, and He saved me.

**114:7** Return, my soul, to your rest, for the Lord has done good to you;

**114:8** for He delivered my soul from death, my eyes from tears, and my feet from slipping.

**114:9** I will be well-pleasing before the Lord in the land of the living.

## Translator Notes

- **114:1** — The Greek text here reads 'God' (ho theos), whereas most Septuagint witnesses and the Hebrew MT (Ps 116:1) read 'the Lord.' Septuagint Psalms 114 and 115 together correspond to Hebrew Psalm 116.
- **114:2** — The Greek text reads 'in His days' (en tais hemerais autou) with the past-tense verb 'I called out,' whereas other Greek witnesses and the Hebrew MT read 'in my days' with a future verb ('I will call').
- **114:5** — The Greek text reads 'the Lord our God' (Kyrios ho theos hemon), where other Septuagint witnesses and the Hebrew MT read simply 'our God.' Both 'merciful' (eleemon) and 'shows mercy' (elea) derive from the same Greek root.
- **114:6** — Greek ta nepia literally means 'the infants' or 'little children,' reflecting the Septuagint's rendering of the Hebrew term for 'the simple.'
- **114:9** — Greek euaresteso ('I will be well-pleasing') follows a characteristic Septuagint idiom for the Hebrew phrase 'I will walk before the Lord'; similarly, verse 8 preserves the Septuagint third person ('He delivered') rather than the Hebrew second person ('You delivered').
