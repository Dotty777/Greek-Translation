## Text

**136:1** For David. Beside the rivers of Babylon, there we sat down and wept as we remembered Zion.

**136:2** On the willows in its midst we hung our musical instruments.

**136:3** For there those who took us captive asked us for words of songs, and those who led us away said, "Sing for us from the songs of Zion."

**136:4** How can we sing the song of the Lord in a foreign land?

**136:5** If I forget you, Jerusalem, may my right hand be forgotten.

**136:6** May my tongue cling to my throat if I do not remember you, if I do not place Jerusalem first at the head of my gladness.

**136:7** Remember, Lord, the sons of Edom on the day of Jerusalem, those who said, "Empty it, empty it, down to the foundation in it."

**136:8** Daughter of Babylon, you wretched one, blessed is the one who will repay you your repayment that you repaid to us;

**136:9** blessed is the one who will seize and dash your infants against the rock.

## Translator Notes

- **136:1** — The Septuagint includes the superscription "For David" (To Dauid), which is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text (MT Psalm 137).
- **136:2** — The Greek "our musical instruments" (ta organa hemon) is broader than the Hebrew "our lyres."
- **136:3** — The Greek refers to "those who led us away" (rather than "our tormentors") and omits a word for "mirth" or "hymn" before the quotation, with "said" supplied for the elliptical syntax.
- **136:5** — Unlike the active Hebrew verb ("forget [its skill]"), the Greek uses the passive optative "be forgotten" (epilestheie).
- **136:8** — The Greek reads "you wretched one" (he talaiporos) rather than "doomed to be destroyed," and repeats the same root three times ("repay ... repayment ... repaid").
