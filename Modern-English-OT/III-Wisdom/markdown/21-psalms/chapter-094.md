## Text

**94:1** A praise of a song for David. Come, let us exult to the Lord; let us shout joyfully to God our Savior.

**94:2** Let us come before His presence with thanksgiving, and with psalms let us shout joyfully to Him.

**94:3** For the Lord is a great God, and a great King over all the gods.

**94:4** For the Lord will not reject His people, for in His hand are the ends of the earth, and the heights of the mountains are His.

**94:5** For the sea is His, and He Himself made it, and His hands formed the dry land.

**94:6** Come, let us worship and fall down before Him, and let us weep before the Lord who made us.

**94:7** For He is our God, and we are the people of His pasture and the sheep of His hand.

**94:8** Today, if you hear His voice, do not harden your hearts as in the provocation, as on the day of testing in the wilderness,

**94:9** where your fathers tested, examined, and saw My works.

**94:10** For forty years I loathed that generation, and said, "They always go astray in their heart, and they themselves did not know My ways."

**94:11** So I swore in My wrath, "They will certainly not enter into My rest."

## Translator Notes

- **94:4** — The opening clause "For the Lord will not reject His people" (echoing 93:14) is a Septuagint reading absent from the Hebrew MT, and πέρατα means "ends" rather than the Hebrew "depths."
- **94:6** — Greek κλαύσωμεν ("let us weep") reflects a reading that differs from the Hebrew MT "let us kneel."
- **94:8** — The Septuagint renders the Hebrew place names Meribah and Massah as common nouns: παραπικρασμός ("provocation/rebellion") and πειρασμός ("testing").
- **94:11** — Greek literally reads "If they will enter into My rest," a Semitic oath idiom expressing an emphatic negative.
