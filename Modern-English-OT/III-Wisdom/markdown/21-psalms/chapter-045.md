## Text

**45:1** For the end; for the sons of Korah, concerning the hidden things, a psalm.

**45:2** Our God is a refuge and strength, a helper in afflictions that found us exceedingly.

**45:3** Therefore we will not fear when the earth is troubled and mountains are moved into the hearts of the seas.

**45:4** Their waters roared and were troubled; the mountains were troubled by His might. Interlude.

**45:5** The rushing currents of the river gladden the city of God; the Most High sanctified His tabernacle.

**45:6** God is in its midst; it will not be shaken; God will help it with His presence.

**45:7** Nations were troubled, kingdoms tottered; He gave His voice, the earth was shaken.

**45:8** The Lord of powers is with us; our protector is the God of Jacob. Interlude.

**45:9** Come, see the works of the Lord, what wonders He set on the earth.

**45:10** Removing wars to the ends of the earth, He will shatter the bow and break the weapon, and He will burn up shields in fire.

**45:11** "Be at rest, and know that I am God; I will be exalted among the nations, I will be exalted in the earth."

**45:12** The Lord of powers is with us; our protector is the God of Jacob.

## Translator Notes

- **45:1** — The Greek phrase ὑπὲρ τῶν κρυφίων ('concerning the hidden things') interprets the Hebrew musical heading Alamoth through the root meaning 'hide'.
- **45:2** — The Greek begins with 'Our God' (Ὁ θεὸς ἡμῶν) and makes the participle modify 'afflictions' ('afflictions that found us exceedingly') rather than describing God as 'very present'.
- **45:4** — Because 'seas' and 'waters' are plural in Greek, singular αὐτοῦ ('His') refers back to God. Greek διάψαλμα ('Interlude') is the Septuagint equivalent of Hebrew Selah.
- **45:6** — The Greek text reads τῷ προσώπῳ (literally 'with the face', i.e., 'with His presence'), an ancient manuscript reading where other Greek witnesses read τὸ πρὸς πρωί ('toward morning', matching the Hebrew).
- **45:11** — Greek σχολάσατε literally means 'cease from activity' or 'be at leisure' (hence 'be at rest' or 'be still'). In vv. 9-10, the Greek also distinctively reads 'wonders' (τέρατα), 'weapon' (ὅπλον), and 'shields' (θυρεούς).
