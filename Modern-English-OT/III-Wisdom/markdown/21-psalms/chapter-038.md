## Text

**38:1** For the end, for Jeduthun; a song of David.

**38:2** I said, "I will guard my ways so as not to sin with my tongue." I set a guard over my mouth while the sinner stood against me.

**38:3** I became mute and was humbled and kept silent from good things, and my pain was renewed.

**38:4** My heart grew hot within me, and in my meditation a fire will be kindled; I spoke with my tongue:

**38:5** "Make known to me, Lord, my end, and what the number of my days is, so that I may know what I lack.

**38:6** Look, You have made my days old, and my existence is as nothing before You; yet all things are futility, every living man. Interlude.

**38:7** Surely man passes through as an image, yet they are troubled in vain; he stores up treasure and does not know for whom he will gather these things.

**38:8** And now, what is my endurance? Is it not the Lord? And my existence is from You.

**38:9** Deliver me from all my lawless deeds; You have made me a reproach to a fool.

**38:10** I became mute and did not open my mouth, because You are the One who made me.

**38:11** Remove Your scourges from me; because of the strength of Your hand I have fainted.

**38:12** With reproofs for lawlessness You disciplined man, and You melted away his soul like a spider's web; yet every man is troubled in vain. Interlude.

**38:13** Hear my prayer and my supplication; give ear to my tears. Do not remain silent, because I am a stranger on the earth and a temporary resident, just as all my fathers were.

**38:14** Relent toward me so that I may be refreshed before I depart and exist no more."

## Translator Notes

- **38:1** — "For the end" translates the standard Septuagint superscription phrase (Eis to telos), corresponding to "For the choir director" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Psalm 39 in Hebrew numbering).
- **38:6** — The Greek reads "old" (palaias), where the Hebrew has "handbreadths," likely reflecting an inner-Greek shift or wordplay with palaistas ("handbreadths"). "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, corresponding to Hebrew selah.
- **38:9** — The Greek states indicatively, "You have made me a reproach to a fool," rather than the Hebrew negative petition ("Do not make me the reproach of the foolish").
- **38:12** — The Greek reads "like a spider's web [or spider] his soul" (hos arachnen ten psychen autou), whereas the Hebrew has "like a moth what is dear to him."
- **38:13** — The Greek reads "on the earth" (en te ge), whereas the Hebrew has "with you."
