## Text

**145:1** Hallelujah. Of Haggai and Zechariah. Praise the Lord, my soul.

**145:2** I will praise the Lord in my life; I will make melody to my God as long as I exist.

**145:3** Do not trust in rulers and in sons of men, in whom there is no salvation.

**145:4** His spirit will go out, and he will return to his earth; in that day all their thoughts will perish.

**145:5** Blessed is the one whose helper is the God of Jacob; his hope is in the Lord his God,

**145:6** who made heaven and earth, the sea and all things in them; who keeps truth forever,

**145:7** executing justice for those who are wronged, giving food to those who are hungry. The Lord frees those who are shackled.

**145:8** The Lord raises up those who are cast down; the Lord makes the blind wise; the Lord loves the righteous.

**145:9** The Lord keeps the proselytes; He will uphold an orphan and a widow, and He will wipe out the way of sinners.

**145:10** The Lord will reign forever, your God, Zion, to generation and generation.

## Translator Notes

- **145:1** — The Septuagint superscription uniquely attributes this psalm to the post-exilic prophets Haggai and Zechariah.
- **145:4** — The Greek shifts from singular pronouns ("His spirit... his earth") in the first half of the verse to plural ("all their thoughts") in the second half.
- **145:8** — The Greek reverses the order of the first two clauses relative to the Masoretic Text and reads σοφοῖ ("makes wise") rather than "opens the eyes of" the blind.
- **145:9** — The Greek term προσήλυτος ("proselyte") renders the Hebrew word for resident alien or newcomer and in Hellenistic Jewish usage also denotes a Gentile convert living under God's care.
