## Text

**40:1** For the end; a psalm for David.

**40:2** Blessed is the one who shows understanding toward the poor and needy; in an evil day the Lord will deliver him.

**40:3** May the Lord guard him and pronounce him blessed on the earth, and may He not hand him over into the hands of his enemy.

**40:4** May the Lord help him on his bed of pain; You turned his entire bed in his illness.

**40:5** I said, "Lord, have mercy on me; heal my soul, because I have sinned against You."

**40:6** My enemies spoke evil things against me: "When will he die and his name be lost?"

**40:7** And if he came in to see, he would speak in vain; his heart gathered lawlessness for himself; he would go outside and speak

**40:8** together against me. All my enemies were whispering against me; they were devising evil things against me;

**40:9** they laid down a lawless word against me: "Will the one who sleeps not rise up again?"

**40:10** For even the man of my peace, in whom I hoped, the one who ate my bread, has magnified treachery against me.

**40:11** But You, Lord, have mercy on me and raise me up, and I will repay them.

**40:12** By this I have known that You have delighted in me, because my enemy will certainly not gloat over me.

**40:13** As for me, You upheld me because of innocence, and You established me before You forever.

**40:14** Blessed be the Lord, the God of Israel, from eternity and to eternity. May it be so, may it be so.

## Translator Notes

- **40:3** — This Greek text omits the clause "and keep him alive" found in the Hebrew and some Septuagint manuscripts, and reads singular "his enemy" rather than plural "his enemies."
- **40:8** — The Greek joins "together against me" (epi to auto kat' emou) with "spoke" at the end of v. 7 rather than with "All my enemies were whispering" as in the Hebrew.
- **40:9** — The Greek frames the enemies' statement as a question ("Will the one who sleeps not rise up again?"), whereas the Hebrew states declaratively that the one who lies down will not rise again.
- **40:10** — "Treachery" translates Greek pternismon ("tripping up by the heel, supplanting, treachery"), a verbal noun from pterna ("heel"), whereas John 13:18 echoes the verse with "lifted his heel against me."
- **40:14** — "May it be so, may it be so" translates the Greek optative genoito, genoito, rendering the Hebrew "Amen and Amen."
