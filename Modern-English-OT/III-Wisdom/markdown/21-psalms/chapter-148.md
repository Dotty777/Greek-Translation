## Text

**148:1** Hallelujah. Of Haggai and Zechariah. Praise the Lord from the heavens; praise Him in the highest places.

**148:2** Praise Him, all His angels; praise Him, all His powers.

**148:3** Praise Him, sun and moon; praise Him, all the stars and the light.

**148:4** Praise Him, heavens of the heavens, and the water that is above the heavens.

**148:5** Let them praise the name of the Lord, for He Himself spoke and they came into being; He Himself commanded and they were created.

**148:6** He established them forever, and forever and ever; He set a decree, and it will not pass away.

**148:7** Praise the Lord from the earth, dragons and all depths;

**148:8** fire, hail, snow, ice, stormy wind, which do His word;

**148:9** mountains and all hills, fruit-bearing trees and all cedars;

**148:10** wild animals and all livestock, creeping things and winged birds;

**148:11** kings of the earth and all peoples, rulers and all judges of the earth;

**148:12** young men and virgins, old men with younger ones;

**148:13** let them praise the name of the Lord, for His name alone has been exalted; thanksgiving to Him is over earth and heaven.

**148:14** And He will exalt the horn of His people, for the sons of Israel, a people drawing near to Him.

## Translator Notes

- **148:3** — The Greek coordinates 'and the light' (καὶ τὸ φῶς) as a separate element rather than the Hebrew construct phrase 'all stars of light' (shining stars).
- **148:5** — The clause 'He Himself spoke and they came into being' (αὐτὸς εἶπεν καὶ ἐγενήθησαν) appears in the Septuagint (echoing Psalm 32:9 LXX / 33:9 MT) but is not in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **148:8** — The Greek reads 'ice' (κρύσταλλος) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'smoke' or 'vapor.'
- **148:13** — Literally 'His thanksgiving' or 'His acknowledgment' (ἡ ἐξομολόγησις αὐτοῦ), taken as an objective genitive ('thanksgiving to Him'); the Septuagint links the underlying Hebrew word for 'majesty/splendor' to the root meaning 'give thanks.'
- **148:14** — The Greek text translated here moves directly from 'the horn of His people' to 'for the sons of Israel, a people drawing near to Him,' without the phrase 'praise for all His godly ones' or the closing 'Hallelujah' found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and other witnesses.
