## Text

**35:1** For the end; for the servant of the Lord, David.

**35:2** The lawless one speaks within himself of sinning; there is no fear of God before his eyes.

**35:3** For he acted deceitfully before him, to find his lawlessness and hate it.

**35:4** The words of his mouth are lawlessness and deceit; he was unwilling to understand so as to do good.

**35:5** He devised lawlessness upon his bed; he took his stand on every way that is not good, and he did not loathe evil.

**35:6** Lord, Your mercy is in heaven, and Your truth reaches to the clouds.

**35:7** And righteousness is like mountains of God; Your judgments are like a great abyss. You will save men and beasts, Lord,

**35:8** as You have multiplied Your mercy, God; and the sons of men will hope in the shelter of Your wings.

**35:9** They will be intoxicated with the fatness of Your house, and You will give them to drink from the torrent of Your delight.

**35:10** For with You is a spring of life; in Your light we will see light.

**35:11** Extend Your mercy to those who know You, and Your righteousness to the upright in heart.

**35:12** Let not a foot of arrogance come upon me, and may a hand of sinners not shake me.

**35:13** There all who work lawlessness have fallen; they have been thrust out and will by no means be able to stand.

## Translator Notes

- **35:1** — The Greek heading "For the end" (Εἰς τὸ τέλος) is the regular Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew musical superscription.
- **35:2** — Literally, "The lawless one says to sin within himself" (Φησὶν ὁ παράνομος τοῦ ἁμαρτάνειν ἐν ἑαυτῷ), a compressed Septuagint rendering of the opening statement about transgression.
- **35:3** — The pronoun in "before him" (ἐνώπιον αὐτοῦ) may refer either to the lawless one in his own sight or to God, and the following infinitive clause ("to find his lawlessness and hate it") is compressed and debated.
- **35:7** — The Greek text reads "righteousness" (ἡ δικαιοσύνη) without a possessive pronoun, though contextually referring to God's righteousness; in v. 8, "as You have multiplied" (ὡς ἐπλήθυνας) continues the sentence from v. 7 and can also be read exclamatorily ("How You have multiplied!").
