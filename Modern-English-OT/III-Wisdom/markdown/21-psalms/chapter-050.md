## Text

**50:1** For the end: a psalm for David,

**50:2** when Nathan the prophet came to him, when he had gone in to Bathsheba.

**50:3** Have mercy on me, God, according to Your great mercy, and according to the abundance of Your compassions blot out my lawless deed.

**50:4** Wash me all the more from my lawlessness, and cleanse me from my sin.

**50:5** For I myself know my lawlessness, and my sin is before me continually.

**50:6** Against You alone I have sinned, and I have done what is evil before You, so that You may be justified in Your words and prevail when You are judged.

**50:7** For look, in lawless deeds I was conceived, and in sins my mother conceived me.

**50:8** For look, You have loved truth; You have made clear to me the unseen and hidden things of Your wisdom.

**50:9** You will sprinkle me with hyssop, and I will be cleansed; You will wash me, and I will be made whiter than snow.

**50:10** You will make me hear exultation and gladness; humbled bones will exult.

**50:11** Turn Your face away from my sins, and blot out all my lawless deeds.

**50:12** Create a clean heart in me, God, and renew an upright spirit in my inward parts.

**50:13** Do not cast me away from Your face, and do not remove Your Holy Spirit from me.

**50:14** Restore to me the exultation of Your salvation, and uphold me with a governing spirit.

**50:15** I will teach the lawless Your ways, and the ungodly will return to You.

**50:16** Rescue me from bloodshed, God, the God of my salvation; my tongue will exult in Your righteousness,

**50:17** Lord. You will open my lips, and my mouth will declare Your praise.

**50:18** For if You had desired a sacrifice, I would have given it; You will not take pleasure in whole burnt offerings.

**50:19** A sacrifice to God is a broken spirit; a broken and humbled heart God will not despise.

**50:20** Do good to Zion, Lord, in Your good pleasure, and let the walls of Jerusalem be built.

**50:21** Then You will take pleasure in a sacrifice of righteousness, an offering and whole burnt offerings; then they will offer up calves on Your altar.

## Translator Notes

- **50:1** — Greek "eis to telos" ("For the end") is the Septuagint's standard rendering of the Hebrew musical superscription "lamnatseach" ("for the choir director"), and Psalm 50 in the Greek numbering corresponds to Psalm 51 in the Hebrew MT.
- **50:6** — Greek "en to krinesthai se" ("when You are judged," passive or middle, quoted in Rom 3:4) differs from the Hebrew active "when you judge," and "nikeses" ("prevail") corresponds to Hebrew "be blameless."
- **50:8** — The Greek reads "the unseen and hidden things of Your wisdom You have made clear to me," whereas the Hebrew MT speaks of truth in the inward parts and wisdom taught in the secret place.
- **50:14** — Greek "hegemoniko" ("governing" or "ruling") translates Hebrew "nedibah" ("willing" or "noble").
- **50:17** — The Greek punctuation joins "Lord" to the end of the preceding clause in 50:16, and "anoixeis" ("You will open") is a future indicative rather than an imperative.
