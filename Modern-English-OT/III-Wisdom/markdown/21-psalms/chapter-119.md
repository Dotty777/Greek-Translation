## Text

**119:1** A song of the steps. To the Lord, in my distress, I cried out, and He listened to me.

**119:2** Lord, rescue my soul from unjust lips and from a deceitful tongue.

**119:3** What might be given to you, and what might be added to you, against a deceitful tongue?

**119:4** The arrows of the mighty one, sharpened, with the coals of the desert.

**119:5** Woe to me, because my sojourn has been prolonged; I have camped among the tents of Kedar.

**119:6** My soul has long sojourned with those who hate peace.

**119:7** I was peaceable; whenever I spoke to them, they made war against me without cause.

## Translator Notes

- **119:1** — Greek anabathmōn literally means "steps" or "stairs," the Septuagint rendering of the superscription traditionally known as "Songs of Ascents."
- **119:3** — The Greek uses the preposition pros ("against" or "in response to") with "a deceitful tongue," whereas the Hebrew treats the phrase as a vocative ("O deceitful tongue").
- **119:4** — Greek tois anthraxin tois erēmikois literally reads "the coals of the desert," rendering the Hebrew reference to coals of the broom tree (a desert shrub).
- **119:5** — Greek hē paroikia mou emakrynthē ("my sojourn has been prolonged") interprets the Hebrew consonants mšk verbally ("prolonged") rather than as the place name Meshech.
- **119:7** — The Greek adds dōrean ("without cause" or "for nothing"), a word not present in the Masoretic Hebrew text.
