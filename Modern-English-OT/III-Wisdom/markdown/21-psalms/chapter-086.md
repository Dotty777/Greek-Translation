# Psalm 86 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**86:1** For the sons of Korah, a psalm of a song. His foundations are in the holy mountains;

**86:2** the Lord loves the gates of Zion above all the tents of Jacob.

**86:3** Glorious things were spoken about you, city of God. Interlude.

**86:4** "I will mention Rahab and Babylon to those who know Me; and look, foreigners and Tyre and the people of the Ethiopians—these were born there."

**86:5** "Mother Zion," a man will say, and "A man was born in her," and "He Himself, the Most High, founded her."

**86:6** The Lord will recount in a register of peoples and rulers, of these who have been born in her. Interlude.

**86:7** The dwelling in you is like that of all who rejoice.

## Translator Notes

- **86:1** — "His foundations" translates the masculine singular Greek autou, referring to the Lord (v. 2) or, by carryover from the Hebrew suffix, to the sanctuary or city itself.
- **86:4** — "Foreigners" translates Greek allophyloi, the regular Septuagint term for the Philistines, and "the people of the Ethiopians" reflects Greek laos Aithiopon where the Hebrew simply has "Cush."
- **86:5** — Whereas the Hebrew reads "Of Zion it shall be said, 'This one and that one was born in her,'" the Greek reads "'Mother Zion,' a man will say." In this Greek text the capitals on Anthropos and Autos mark the start of quoted words, as elsewhere in the Psalms (e.g., 81:6).
- **86:6** — The Greek adds "and rulers" (kai archonton) and uses the plural "of these who have been born in her," differing from the singular "this one was born there" in the Hebrew.
- **86:7** — The Greek reads katoikia ("dwelling") with a compact comparative genitive ("like that of all who rejoice"), where the Hebrew reads "Singers and dancers alike say, 'All my springs are in you.'"
