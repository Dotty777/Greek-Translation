## Text

**12:1** For the end. A psalm for David. How long, Lord, will You forget me to the end? How long will You turn Your face away from me?

**12:2** How long will I place counsels in my soul, sorrows in my heart by day? How long will my enemy be exalted over me?

**12:3** Look, hear me, Lord my God; give light to my eyes, so that I do not fall asleep in death,

**12:4** so that my enemy may not say, "I have prevailed against him"; those who afflict me will rejoice if I am shaken.

**12:5** so that my enemy does not say, "I have prevailed against him." Those who afflict me will rejoice if I am shaken. But I have hoped in Your mercy; my heart will rejoice in Your salvation.

**12:6** I will sing to the Lord, who has done good to me, and I will sing praise to the name of the Lord, the Most High.

## Translator Notes

- **12:1** — The Greek phrase "to the end" (eis telos) literally echoes the superscription "For the end" and carries the idiomatic sense "completely" or "forever."
- **12:3** — This Greek witness reads "the eyes" (tous ophthalmous) without an explicit possessive pronoun, using the definite article in a possessive sense ("my eyes").
- **12:4** — The Saints Greek Critical Text has this clause twice: here (with the optative εἴποι) and again at the opening of verse 5 (with the subjunctive εἴπῃ); both are translated as they stand.
- **12:6** — The final clause "and I will sing praise to the name of the Lord, the Most High" is a Septuagint addition absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text (compare Psalm 7:18).
