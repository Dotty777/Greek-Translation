## Text

**80:1** For the end, concerning the winepresses; a psalm for Asaph.

**80:2** Exult in God our helper; shout aloud to the God of Jacob.

**80:3** Take up a psalm and sound a tambourine, a pleasant harp with a lyre.

**80:4** Blow a trumpet at the new moon, on the notable day of our feast,

**80:5** for it is a statute for Israel and a judgment for the God of Jacob.

**80:6** He appointed it as a testimony in Joseph when he went out from the land of Egypt; he heard a language that he did not know.

**80:7** He removed his back from burdens; his hands slaved at the basket.

**80:8** "In affliction you called upon Me and I delivered you; I answered you in the secret place of a storm; I tested you at the water of Contention." Interlude.

**80:9** "Listen, My people, and I will speak to you; Israel, and I testify to you. If you listen to Me,

**80:10** there will not be a new god among you, nor will you worship a foreign god.

**80:11** For I am the Lord your God, who brought you up from the land of Egypt; widen your mouth, and I will fill it.

**80:12** And My people did not listen to My voice, and Israel did not pay attention to Me.

**80:13** And I sent them away according to the practices of their hearts; they will walk in their practices.

**80:14** If the people Israel had listened to Me, if they had walked in My ways,

**80:15** in no time I would have humbled their enemies, and I would have laid My hand upon those who afflict them."

**80:16** The enemies of the Lord lied to Him, and their time will be forever.

**80:17** And He fed them from the fat of wheat, and with honey from a rock He satisfied them.

## Translator Notes

- **80:1** — Greek hyper ton lenon ("concerning the winepresses") renders the Hebrew musical heading haggitit ("the Gittith") from the related Hebrew word for a winepress.
- **80:4** — Greek en eusemo hemera ("on the notable day" or "well-marked day") differs from the Hebrew reading "at the full moon."
- **80:6** — In verses 6-7, the Greek text uses third-person verbs ("heard," "removed") where the Hebrew shifts to first-person speech, and in verse 7 reads edouleusan ("slaved/served") where the Hebrew reads "were freed."
- **80:8** — Greek hydatos Antilogias ("the water of Contention," singular "water") translates the Hebrew place-name "waters of Meribah."
- **80:16** — In verses 16-17, the Greek text uses past-tense verbs ("lied," "fed," "satisfied") and third-person "them" throughout verse 17, rather than continuing the conditional wish or shifting to second-person "you" as in the Hebrew.
