## Text

**7:1** A psalm for David, which he sang to the Lord concerning the words of Chusi son of Jemini.

**7:2** Lord, my God, in You I have hoped; save me from all who pursue me and rescue me,

**7:3** so that he may never seize my soul like a lion, while there is no one who redeems or saves.

**7:4** Lord, my God, if I have done this, if there is injustice in my hands,

**7:5** if I have repaid those who repay me with evil, then may I fall away from my enemies empty;

**7:6** then may the enemy pursue my soul and overtake it, and trample my life into the ground, and make my glory dwell in the dust. Interlude.

**7:7** Arise, Lord, in Your wrath; be exalted in the boundaries of my enemies; awaken, Lord, my God, in the decree that You have commanded.

**7:8** And an assembly of peoples will surround You; and above it return on high.

**7:9** The Lord will judge peoples; judge me, Lord, according to my righteousness and according to my innocence upon me.

**7:10** Now let the wickedness of sinners come to an end, and You will direct a righteous person; God examines hearts and kidneys.

**7:11** Righteous is my help from God, who saves the upright in heart.

**7:12** God is a righteous judge, strong and patient, not bringing wrath each day.

**7:13** If you do not turn back, He will polish His sword; He has bent His bow and prepared it;

**7:14** and in it He has prepared instruments of death; He has fashioned His arrows for those who burn.

**7:15** Look, he was in labor with lawlessness; he conceived trouble and gave birth to injustice;

**7:16** he dug a pit and dug it out, and he will fall into the hole that he made;

**7:17** his trouble will return upon his head, and his injustice will come down upon the crown of his head.

**7:18** I will give thanks to the Lord according to His righteousness, and I will sing praise to the name of the Lord Most High.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The Greek reads "Chusi son of Jemini" (Chousei son of Iemenei), corresponding to "Cush, a Benjamite" in the Hebrew text.
- **7:5** — Following the Greek, the second clause begins the self-curse ("then may I fall away from my enemies empty"), whereas the Hebrew text reads a further condition ("or plundered my adversary without cause").
- **7:11** — The Greek attaches "Righteous" to the start of v. 11 modifying "my help" (where Hebrew has "my shield"), whereas the Hebrew places "righteous" at the end of v. 10 modifying "God." In v. 10, "kidneys" is the literal idiom for the innermost seat of emotion and conscience.
- **7:12** — The Greek reads "strong and patient, not bringing wrath each day," contrasting with the Hebrew text ("a God who has indignation every day").
