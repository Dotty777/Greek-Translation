# Psalm 120 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**120:1** A song of the steps. I lifted up my eyes to the mountains; from where will my help come?

**120:2** My help is from the Lord, the One who made heaven and earth.

**120:3** Do not give your foot to slipping, and the One who guards you will not grow drowsy.

**120:4** Look, the One who guards Israel will neither grow drowsy nor awaken.

**120:5** The Lord will guard you; the Lord is your shelter at your right hand.

**120:6** By day the sun will not burn you, nor the moon by night.

**120:7** The Lord will guard you from all evil; may He guard your soul.

**120:8** The Lord will guard your coming in and your going out from now on and forever.

## Translator Notes

- **120:3** — Greek mē dōs is second-person singular ("Do not give"), contrasting with the third-person singular in Hebrew ("May He not let your foot slip").
- **120:4** — The Greek source text reads exypnōsei ("awaken" or "wake up"), a striking textual variant where most Greek witnesses read hypnōsei ("sleep," matching the Hebrew); taken literally, it implies that Israel's guardian never enters the cycle of drowsiness and waking.
- **120:5** — Greek skepē literally means "shelter" or "covering," where the Hebrew text reads "shade."
- **120:7** — The second verb is the aorist optative phylaxai ("may He guard," also formally identical to the infinitive "to guard") without a repeated subject, unlike witnesses that read the future indicative phylaxei ("will guard").
- **120:8** — The Greek reverses the Hebrew word order, reading "your coming in and your going out" (eisodon ... exodon) rather than "your going out and your coming in."
