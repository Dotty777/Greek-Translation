## Text

**143:1** For David, against Goliath. Blessed is the Lord my God, who teaches my hands for battle, my fingers for war,

**143:2** my mercy and my refuge, my supporter and my deliverer, my defender, and in Him I have hoped; the One who subjects my people under me.

**143:3** Lord, what is man that You were made known to him, or a son of man that You consider him?

**143:4** Man has become like emptiness; his days pass by like a shadow.

**143:5** Lord, bow Your heavens and come down; touch the mountains, and they will smoke.

**143:6** Flash lightning, and You will scatter them; send out Your arrows, and You will throw them into confusion.

**143:7** Send out Your hand from on high; rescue me and deliver me out of many waters, out of the hand of sons of foreigners,

**143:8** whose mouth has spoken emptiness, and their right hand is a right hand of unrighteousness.

**143:9** God, I will sing a new song to You; on a ten-stringed harp I will make melody to You,

**143:10** to Him who gives salvation to kings, to Him who redeems David His servant. From an evil sword

**143:11** deliver me, and rescue me out of the hand of sons of foreigners, whose mouth has spoken emptiness, and their right hand is a right hand of unrighteousness;

**143:12** whose sons are like young plants grown sturdy in their youth, their daughters beautified, adorned all around as a likeness of a temple;

**143:13** their storehouses are full, pouring forth from this into that; their sheep are prolific, multiplying in their goings-out,

**143:14** their oxen are fat; there is no collapse of a fence nor a way out nor an outcry in their courtyards.

**143:15** They called the people blessed to whom these things belong; blessed is the people whose God is the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **143:1** — The Septuagint superscription uniquely adds "against Goliath" (or "concerning Goliath", Greek πρὸς τὸν Γολιάδ), linking the psalm to David's contest with Goliath, and reads "my God" where the Masoretic Text has "my rock".
- **143:3** — The Greek passive verb ἐγνώσθης ("You were made known to him") differs from the active reading of the Masoretic Text ("that You take knowledge of him").
- **143:10** — Following the Greek punctuation, "From an evil sword" begins the plea completed by "deliver me" in 143:11, rather than modifying "redeems David His servant" as in the Masoretic Text.
- **143:12** — Throughout verses 12-14 the Septuagint uses third-person pronouns ("whose sons... their daughters..."), describing the material prosperity of the foreigners rather than praying for "our" sons as in the Masoretic Text; it also reads "temple" (ναοῦ) rather than "palace".
- **143:15** — The Greek begins with a finite verb, ἐμακάρισαν ("They called blessed"), contrasting popular admiration of material prosperity with the psalmist's declaration that the people whose God is the Lord are truly blessed.
