## Text

**85:1** A prayer of David. Incline Your ear, Lord, and hear me, for I am poor and needy.

**85:2** Guard my soul, for I am devout; save Your servant, my God, who hopes in You.

**85:3** Have mercy on me, Lord, for I will cry out to You all day long.

**85:4** Gladden the soul of Your servant, for to You, Lord, I have lifted up my soul.

**85:5** For You, Lord, are good and gentle and abounding in mercy to all who call on You.

**85:6** Give ear to my prayer, Lord, and pay attention to the voice of my supplication.

**85:7** In the day of my distress I cried out to You, for You heard me.

**85:8** There is no one like You among the gods, Lord, and there is nothing like Your works.

**85:9** All the nations that You made will come and bow down before You, Lord, and will glorify Your name;

**85:10** for You are great and do wondrous things; You alone are God, the great One.

**85:11** Guide me, Lord, in Your way, and I will walk in Your truth; let my heart rejoice to fear Your name.

**85:12** I will give You thanks, Lord my God, with all my heart, and I will glorify Your name forever;

**85:13** for Your mercy is great toward me, and You rescued my soul from the lowest Hades.

**85:14** God, lawless men rose up against me, and an assembly of mighty men sought my soul; they did not set You before them.

**85:15** And You, Lord God, are compassionate and merciful, slow to anger and abounding in mercy and true.

**85:16** Look upon me and have mercy on me; give Your might to Your servant, and save the son of Your maidservant.

**85:17** Work a sign with me for good, and let those who hate me see and be put to shame, because You, Lord, helped me and comforted me.

## Translator Notes

- **85:7** — Both verbs ("I cried out" and "You heard me") are past-tense aorists in the Greek, contrasting with the present or future sense ("I call ... you answer") in the Hebrew.
- **85:10** — The Greek concludes with the emphatic appositive "the great One" (ho megas), an addition not present in the Hebrew text.
- **85:11** — The Greek reads "Guide me" (hodegeson me) and "let my heart rejoice" (euphrantheto he kardia mou), where the Hebrew reads "Teach me" and "unite my heart."
- **85:13** — "Hades" preserves Greek hades, the standard Septuagint rendering of Hebrew Sheol, the realm of the dead.
