## Text

**10:1** For the end. A psalm for David. In the Lord I have put my trust; how will you say to my soul, "Flee away to the mountains like a sparrow"?

**10:2** For look, the sinners have bent a bow; they have prepared arrows in a quiver to shoot down the upright in heart in moonless darkness.

**10:3** For what You established, they have torn down; and what has the righteous one done?

**10:4** The Lord is in His holy temple; the Lord—in heaven is His throne. His eyes look toward the needy; His eyelids examine the sons of men.

**10:5** The Lord examines the righteous and the ungodly, but the one who loves unrighteousness hates his own soul.

**10:6** He will rain snares upon sinners; fire and sulfur and a storm wind will be the portion of their cup.

**10:7** For the Lord is righteous and has loved righteous deeds; His face has seen uprightness.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The Septuagint superscription "For the end" (Greek eis to telos) renders a Hebrew musical heading ("for the director") by linking it with completion or finality. The Greek also reads plural "mountains" and "sparrow" where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has singular "your mountain" and "bird."
- **10:2** — The Greek reads "in a quiver" (eis pharetran), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "on the bowstring."
- **10:3** — The Septuagint reads "what You established, they have torn down" (ha katērtisō katheilon), addressing God directly, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "if the foundations are destroyed."
- **10:5** — In the Greek text, "the one who loves unrighteousness" is the subject who "hates his own soul" (tēn heautou psychēn), whereas in the Hebrew Masoretic Text God's soul hates the wicked and the lover of violence.
- **10:7** — In the Greek, "uprightness" (euthytēta) is accusative, so "His face" (to prosōpon autou) is the subject; the Hebrew is commonly read with the upright as those who see His face.
