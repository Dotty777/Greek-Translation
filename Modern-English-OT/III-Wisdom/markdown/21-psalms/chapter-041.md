## Text

**41:1** For the end; for understanding, to the sons of Korah.

**41:2** Just as the deer yearns for the springs of water, so my soul yearns for You, God.

**41:3** My soul has thirsted for the living God; when will I come and appear before the face of God?

**41:4** My tears have become bread day and night, while it is said to me each day, "Where is your God?"

**41:5** These things I remembered, and I poured out my soul upon myself; for I will pass through the place of a wondrous tent as far as the house of God, with a voice of exultation and thanksgiving, a sound of those celebrating a festival.

**41:6** Why are you deeply grieved, soul, and why do you greatly trouble me? Hope in God, for I will give thanks to Him; the salvation of my face

**41:7** is my God. Within myself my soul has been troubled; therefore I will remember You from the land of Jordan and Hermoniim, from a small mountain.

**41:8** Abyss calls upon abyss at the voice of Your waterfalls; all Your billows and Your waves have passed over me.

**41:9** By day the Lord will command His mercy, and by night He will make it known; with me is a prayer to the God of my life.

**41:10** I will say to God, "You are my protector; why have You forgotten me? Why do I walk about downcast while my enemy oppresses me?"

**41:11** In crushing my bones, those who oppress me reproached me, while they say to me each day, "Where is your God?"

**41:12** Why are you deeply grieved, soul, and why do you greatly trouble me? Hope in God, for I will give thanks to Him; the salvation of my face is my God.

## Translator Notes

- **41:1** — Septuagint Psalm 41 corresponds to Hebrew Psalm 42, with verse numbering offset by one because the superscription is counted as verse 1; "For the end; for understanding" is the standard Greek rendering of the Hebrew heading.
- **41:6** — The Greek divides the final clause of the refrain across verses 6 and 7 ("the salvation of my face / is my God"), reading "my face" as in 41:12 and 42:5.
- **41:7** — "Hermoniim" transliterates the Greek plural form of Hermon (Hermonieim), and "from a small mountain" reflects the Septuagint rendering the Hebrew place name Mizar as the adjective "small."
- **41:9** — The Greek text here reads "He will make it known" (delosei, attested in major uncials such as Vaticanus and Sinaiticus), rather than "His song is with me" as in the Hebrew text and other Greek witnesses.
