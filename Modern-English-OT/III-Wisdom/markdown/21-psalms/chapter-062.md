## Text

**62:1** A psalm for David, when he was in the wilderness of Idumea.

**62:2** God, my God, to You I rise early; my soul has thirsted for You, how many times my flesh for You, in a desolate and untrodden and waterless land,

**62:3** so in the sanctuary I have appeared to You, to see Your power and Your glory.

**62:4** For Your mercy is better than lives; my lips will praise You.

**62:5** So I will bless You in my life; in Your name I will lift up my hands.

**62:6** As with tallow and fatness may my soul be filled, and lips of exultation will praise Your name.

**62:7** If I remembered You on my bed, in my early morning I meditated on You,

**62:8** for You have become my helper, and in the shelter of Your wings I will exult.

**62:9** My soul has clung behind You; Your right hand has taken hold of me.

**62:10** But they sought my soul in vain; they will enter into the lowest parts of the earth,

**62:11** they will be delivered into the hands of a sword; they will be portions for foxes.

**62:12** But the king will rejoice in God; everyone who swears by Him will be praised, because the mouth of those who speak unrighteous things has been stopped.

## Translator Notes

- **62:1** — Greek reads "Idumea" (Ἰδουμαίας), whereas the Hebrew MT reads "Judah."
- **62:2** — Greek "how many times" (ποσαπλῶς) reflects reading the Hebrew verb for "long/faint" as the interrogative "how many/often."
- **62:3** — Greek has the passive "I have appeared to You" (ὤφθην σοι), whereas the Hebrew MT has the active "I have looked upon You."
- **62:7** — Greek "If" (εἰ) renders a Hebrew conditional-temporal particle ("when/whenever"), and "in my early morning" (ἐν τῷ ὄρθρῳ μου) differs from the Hebrew MT's "in the night watches."
- **62:10** — Greek "in vain" (εἰς μάτην) differs from the Hebrew MT's "for destruction/ruin."
