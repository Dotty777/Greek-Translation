## Text

**6:1** For the end, with hymns, concerning the eighth; a psalm for David.

**6:2** Lord, in Your wrath do not rebuke me, nor in Your anger discipline me.

**6:3** Have mercy on me, Lord, for I am weak; heal me, for my bones have been troubled.

**6:4** And my soul has been greatly troubled; and You, Lord, how long?

**6:5** Turn back, Lord, rescue my soul; save me for the sake of Your mercy.

**6:6** For in death there is no one who remembers You; and in Hades, who will give thanks to You?

**6:7** I have grown weary in my groaning; every night I will wash my bed; with my tears I will drench my bedding.

**6:8** My eye has been troubled because of wrath; I have grown old among all my enemies.

**6:9** Depart from me, all you who practice lawlessness, because the Lord has heard the voice of my weeping.

**6:10** The Lord has heard my supplication; the Lord has accepted my prayer.

**6:11** May all my enemies be put to shame and greatly troubled; may they be turned back and be greatly put to shame swiftly.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — "Concerning the eighth" (hyper tes ogdoes) translates the Hebrew musical term al-hasheminith, likely referring to an eight-stringed instrument or a lower pitch register. Greek dative τῷ Δαυίδ (rendered "for David") is multi-valent and can also mean "of/by David" or "pertaining to David" (see note on 3:1).
- **6:6** — "Hades" is the Septuagint's Greek rendering of Hebrew Sheol, the realm of the dead. "Give thanks to You" translates exomologesetai soi, which carries the sense of open acknowledgment, praise, or confession.
- **6:8** — The Greek reads "because of wrath" (apo thymou, echoing 6:2) rather than Hebrew "grief," and shifts to first-person singular "I have grown old among all my enemies" where the Hebrew keeps "my eye" as the subject.
- **6:11** — All four verbs in the Greek are aorist passive optatives expressing a wish ("May..."), and the Greek repeats sphodra ("greatly") before dia tachous ("swiftly").
