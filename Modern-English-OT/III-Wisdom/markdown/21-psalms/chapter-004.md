## Text

**4:1** For the end, with psalms; a song for David.

**4:2** When I called out, the God of my righteousness heard me; in distress You made room for me. Have compassion on me and hear my prayer.

**4:3** Sons of men, how long will you be heavy-hearted? Why do you love emptiness and seek falsehood? Interlude.

**4:4** And know that the Lord has made His devout one wondrous; the Lord will hear me when I cry out to Him.

**4:5** Be angry, and do not sin; for the things you say in your heart, be pierced with remorse on your beds. Interlude.

**4:6** Offer a sacrifice of righteousness, and hope in the Lord.

**4:7** Many say, "Who will show us good things?" The light of Your face has been marked upon us, Lord.

**4:8** You have put gladness into my heart; from the fruit of their grain and wine and oil they were multiplied.

**4:9** In peace I will at once lie down and sleep, because You, Lord, alone have settled me in hope.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — "For the end" translates the Greek superscription phrase eis to telos, the Septuagint's standard rendering of the Hebrew musical directive (often translated "for the choir director"). Likewise, "with psalms" renders en psalmois where the Hebrew specifies stringed instruments.
- **4:3** — "Heavy-hearted" (barykardioi) reflects the Greek reading where the Hebrew has "my honor turned to shame." "Interlude" translates diapsalma, the Septuagint term for Hebrew selah, indicating a musical pause.
- **4:7** — "Has been marked upon us" translates the Greek passive indicative esemeiothe ("signed" or "stamped"), contrasting with the Hebrew imperative "lift up."
- **4:8** — Following the Greek text, "and oil" is included alongside grain and wine, and the final verb is third-person plural passive ("they were multiplied") rather than a comparative clause as in the Hebrew.
