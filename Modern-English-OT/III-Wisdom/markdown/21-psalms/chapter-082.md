## Text

**82:1** A song of a psalm for Asaph.

**82:2** God, who will be likened to You? Do not keep silent, nor remain calm, God.

**82:3** For look, Your enemies have roared, and those who hate You have lifted up their head.

**82:4** Against Your people they craftily devised a plan, and they took counsel against Your holy ones.

**82:5** They said, "Come, and let us utterly destroy them from being a nation, and the name of Israel will never be remembered again."

**82:6** For they took counsel together with one mind; against You they made a covenant:

**82:7** the tents of the Idumeans and the Ishmaelites, Moab and the Hagarenes,

**82:8** Naibal and Ammon and Amalek, and foreigners with those who dwell in Tyre.

**82:9** For indeed Asshur also joined with them; they became a help to the sons of Lot. Interlude.

**82:10** Do to them as to Midian and to Sisera, like Jabin at the brook Kishon.

**82:11** They were utterly destroyed at Endor; they became like dung for the ground.

**82:12** Make their rulers like Oreb and Zeeb and Zebah and Zalmunna, all their rulers,

**82:13** who said, "Let us inherit for ourselves the altar of God."

**82:14** My God, make them like a wheel, like stubble in the face of the wind.

**82:15** Like fire that will blaze through a forest, as if a flame were to burn up mountains,

**82:16** so You will pursue them in Your tempest, and in Your wrath You will dismay them.

**82:17** Fill their faces with dishonor, and they will seek Your name, Lord.

**82:18** Let them be put to shame and dismayed forever and ever, and let them be disgraced and perish,

**82:19** and let them know that Your name is the Lord; You alone are Most High over all the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **82:2** — Greek tis homoiothesetai soi ("who will be likened to You?") reflects reading Hebrew domi from the root meaning "be like" rather than "be silent" (as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text, "do not keep silence").
- **82:4** — Greek ton hagion sou ("Your holy ones") stands where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Your treasured ones" (or "hidden ones").
- **82:8** — The Greek text reads Naibal (attested in Codex Vaticanus) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text and other Greek manuscripts read Gebal. Greek allophyloi literally means "foreigners" and is the Septuagint's standard rendering of "Philistines."
- **82:13** — The Greek text reads thysiasterion ("altar"), contrasting with other Greek manuscripts reading hagiasterion ("sanctuary") and the Hebrew Masoretic Text reading "pastures" (or "dwellings").
- **82:19** — Greek Kyrios ("the Lord") here renders the Hebrew divine name (YHWH).
