## Text

**31:1** Of understanding, for David. Blessed are those whose lawless deeds were forgiven, and whose sins were covered over.

**31:2** Blessed is the man whose sin the Lord will by no means reckon against him, nor is there deceit in his mouth.

**31:3** Because I kept silent, my bones grew old from my crying out all day long.

**31:4** For day and night Your hand was heavy upon me; I was turned into misery while a thorn was driven in. Interlude.

**31:5** I made my sin known, and I did not cover my lawlessness; I said, "I will confess my sin against myself to the Lord," and You forgave the ungodliness of my heart. Interlude.

**31:6** For this every devout one will pray to You at a fitting time; yet in a flood of many waters, they will not draw near to him.

**31:7** You are my refuge from the affliction that encompasses me; my exultation, redeem me from those who have surrounded me. Interlude.

**31:8** I will give you understanding and instruct you in this way in which you will go; I will fix My eyes upon you.

**31:9** Do not become like a horse and a mule, which have no understanding; with bridle and muzzle curb the jaws of those who do not draw near to you.

**31:10** Many are the scourges of the sinner, but mercy will surround the one who hopes in the Lord.

**31:11** Be glad in the Lord and exult, you righteous; and boast, all you who are upright in heart.

## Translator Notes

- **31:2** — The Greek reads "in his mouth," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "in his spirit."
- **31:4** — The Greek reads "I was turned into misery while a thorn was driven in," diverging from the Masoretic Text ("my vitality was turned into the drought of summer"). "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, corresponding to Hebrew selah as a musical pause.
- **31:5** — The Greek reads "the ungodliness of my heart," whereas the Masoretic Text reads "the guilt of my sin."
- **31:9** — Greek anxai can be taken either as an imperative ("curb") or as an infinitive ("to curb"). Some interpreters also read "to you" as addressed to God ("to You") rather than to the human addressee of verse 8.
