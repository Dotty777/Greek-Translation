## Text

**16:1** A prayer of David. Hear, Lord, my righteousness; pay attention to my supplication; give ear to my prayer, not with deceitful lips.

**16:2** May my judgment come forth from Your presence; let my eyes see uprightness.

**16:3** You tested my heart; You visited by night; You refined me by fire, and no unrighteousness was found in me.

**16:4** So that my mouth might not speak the works of people, because of the words of Your lips I kept hard ways.

**16:5** Make firm my steps in Your paths, so that my steps may not be shaken.

**16:6** I cried out, because You listened to me, God; incline Your ear to me and hear my words.

**16:7** Make Your mercies wondrous, You who save those who hope in You from those who resist Your right hand.

**16:8** Guard me as the pupil of an eye. In the shelter of Your wings You will shelter me

**16:9** from the face of the ungodly who afflicted me. My enemies surrounded my soul;

**16:10** they closed up their fat; their mouth spoke arrogance.

**16:11** Casting me out, they have now encircled me; they set their eyes to incline to the ground.

**16:12** They laid hold of me like a lion ready for prey, and like a young lion dwelling in hidden places.

**16:13** Arise, Lord, confront them and trip them up; deliver my soul from an ungodly one, Your sword

**16:14** from enemies of Your hand. Lord, dismissing from the earth, divide them in their life; and their belly was filled with Your hidden things; they were satisfied with pork, and they left the remnants to their infants.

**16:15** But as for me, in righteousness I will appear before Your face; I will be satisfied when Your glory appears.

## Translator Notes

- **16:2** — The Greek reads "my eyes" (οἱ ὀφθαλμοί μου) rather than the Hebrew "your eyes," with the plural noun εὐθύτητας ("uprightness").
- **16:7** — In the Greek syntax, "Your right hand" is the dative object of "those who resist" rather than an instrumental phrase ("save by Your right hand").
- **16:13** — The accusative "Your sword" (ῥομφαίαν σου) literally reflects the underlying Hebrew syntax and connects directly into the opening phrase of verse 14 ("from enemies of Your hand").
- **16:14** — This Greek text reads "dismissing from the earth" (ἀπολύων ἀπὸ γῆς, versus the variant ἀπὸ ὀλίγων "from a few") and "pork" (ὑείων, "swine's flesh," contrasting with the variant υἱῶν "sons" and the Hebrew).
