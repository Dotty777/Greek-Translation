# Psalm 59 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**59:1** For the end: for those who will be changed, still for a pillar inscription for David, for instruction,

**59:2** when he set fire to Mesopotamia of Syria and Syria of Sobal, and Joab returned and struck the Valley of Salt, twelve thousand.

**59:3** God, You rejected us and tore us down; You were angry and had compassion on us.

**59:4** You shook the earth and threw it into turmoil; heal its fractures, for they have been shaken.

**59:5** You showed Your people hard things; You made us drink wine of stupor.

**59:6** You gave a signal to those who fear You, to flee from before the bow. Interlude.

**59:7** So that Your beloved ones may be delivered, save with Your right hand and hear me.

**59:8** God spoke in His holy place: "I will exult and divide Shechem, and I will measure out the Valley of Tents.

**59:9** Mine is Gilead, and Mine is Manasseh, and Ephraim is the strength of My head; Judah is My king;

**59:10** Moab is the basin of My hope; over Idumea I will stretch out My sandal; to Me foreigners have been subjected."

**59:11** Who will lead me into a fortified city? Who will guide me as far as Idumea?

**59:12** Is it not You, God, who rejected us? And will You not go out, God, with our armies?

**59:13** Give us help from affliction, and futile is the salvation of man.

**59:14** But in God we will act with power, and He Himself will bring to nothing those who afflict us.

## Translator Notes

- **59:2** — Greek reads 'set fire to' (where Hebrew has 'fought with'), uses the Septuagint form 'Sobal' for Hebrew Zobah (and 'Mesopotamia of Syria' for Aram-naharaim), and reads 'struck the Valley of Salt' without the Hebrew mention of Edom.
- **59:3** — Greek reads the past indicative 'had compassion on us' rather than the Hebrew petition 'restore us.'
- **59:6** — Greek 'from before the bow' reflects reading Hebrew 'truth' (qoshet) as 'bow' (qeshet). 'Interlude' renders Greek diapsalma, the Septuagint equivalent of Hebrew Selah.
- **59:8** — Greek translates the place name Hebrew 'Succoth' literally as 'Tents' (skenon).
- **59:10** — Greek reads 'basin of My hope' (for Hebrew 'my washbasin'), uses 'Idumea' (the Greek name for Edom), and renders Hebrew 'Philistia' as 'foreigners' (allophyloi) with 'have been subjected' rather than Hebrew 'shout in triumph.'
