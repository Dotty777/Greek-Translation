## Text

**19:1** For the end; a psalm for David.

**19:2** May the Lord hear you in the day of distress; may the name of the God of Jacob shield you.

**19:3** May He send out help to you from the holy place, and from Zion may He support you.

**19:4** May He remember every sacrifice of yours, and may He make your whole burnt offering fat. Interlude.

**19:5** May He give to you according to your heart, and may He fulfill your whole plan.

**19:6** Let us exult in your salvation, and in the name of our God we will be magnified. May the Lord fulfill all your requests.

**19:7** Now I know that the Lord has saved His anointed; He will hear him from His holy heaven; in mighty deeds is the salvation of His right hand.

**19:8** Some in chariots and some in horses, but we will be magnified in the name of the Lord our God.

**19:9** They were fettered and fell, but we rose up and were set upright.

**19:10** Lord, save Your king, and hear us on whatever day we call upon You.

## Translator Notes

- **19:4** — Greek pianato literally means "make fat" (an idiom for accepting an offering as rich and pleasing), and diapsalma ("Interlude") is the Septuagint musical term corresponding to Hebrew selah.
- **19:6** — Greek megalynthesometha ("we will be magnified," repeated in 19:8) reflects a Septuagint reading distinct from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("we will raise banners" in v. 6 and "we will boast/remember" in v. 8).
- **19:8** — Greek leaves the verb unexpressed in the first clause, carrying the contrast directly into "we will be magnified."
- **19:10** — The Greek reads "Your king" and a second-person imperative addressed to the Lord ("and hear us"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text lacks "Your" and has a third-person verb often rendered "may the King answer us."
