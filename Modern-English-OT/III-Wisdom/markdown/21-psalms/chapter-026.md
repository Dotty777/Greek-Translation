## Text

**26:1** Of David, before being anointed. The Lord is my illumination and my Savior; whom will I fear? The Lord is the defender of my life; of whom will I be fearful?

**26:2** When wrongdoers drew near against me to eat my flesh, those who afflict me and my enemies, they themselves grew weak and fell.

**26:3** If an army arrays itself against me, my heart will not fear; if war rises up against me, in this I hope.

**26:4** One thing I have asked from the Lord; this I will seek out: that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, to gaze upon the delightfulness of the Lord and to visit His temple.

**26:5** For He hid me in a tent in the day of my troubles; He sheltered me in the hidden place of His tent; on a rock He lifted me up.

**26:6** And now, look, He has lifted up my head above my enemies; I went around and offered in His tent a sacrifice of shouting; I will sing and make music to the Lord.

**26:7** "Hear, Lord, my voice with which I have cried out; have mercy on me and hear me,"

**26:8** my heart said to You. I have sought out Your face; Your face, Lord, I will seek.

**26:9** Do not turn Your face away from me; do not turn aside in anger from Your servant. Become my helper; do not abandon me and do not disregard me, God my Savior.

**26:10** For my father and my mother abandoned me, but the Lord took me in.

**26:11** Set a law for me, Lord, in Your way, and guide me on a straight path because of my enemies.

**26:12** Do not hand me over to the souls of those who afflict me, for unjust witnesses have risen up against me, and injustice has lied to itself.

**26:13** I will believe that I will see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living.

**26:14** Wait patiently for the Lord; be brave, and let your heart grow strong, and wait patiently for the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **26:1** — The superscription phrase "before being anointed" is characteristic of the Septuagint, and "my illumination and my Savior" (phōtismos ... sōtēr) reflects the Greek nouns directly rather than Hebrew "my light and my salvation."
- **26:6** — Greek ekyklōsa ("I went around") reflects a verbal reading of the Hebrew expression for "around me" at the end of the previous clause, and the Greek uses past aorist verbs here and in verse 5.
- **26:8** — Following the Greek punctuation, "my heart said to You" closes the appeal of verse 7, and the first-person verb exezētēsa ("I have sought out Your face") contrasts with the imperative in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **26:12** — Greek "souls" (psychas) reflects a Hebrew idiom for the desires or appetite of one's foes; the final clause ("injustice has lied to itself") is a distinctive Septuagint reading.
- **26:13** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text's conditional clause ("Unless I had believed..."), the Greek states this directly without "unless," and this text reads the future tense pisteusō ("I will believe").
