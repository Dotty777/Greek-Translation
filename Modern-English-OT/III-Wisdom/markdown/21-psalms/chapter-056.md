## Text

**56:1** For the end; do not destroy; for David, for a pillar inscription, when he was fleeing from the presence of Saul into the cave.

**56:2** Have mercy on me, God, have mercy on me, for my soul has trusted in You, and in the shadow of Your wings I will hope until lawlessness passes by.

**56:3** I will cry out to God the Most High, the God who has done good to me. Interlude.

**56:4** He sent out from heaven and saved me; He gave over to reproach those who trample me. God sent out His mercy and His truth,

**56:5** and rescued my soul from the midst of young lions. I lay down troubled. Sons of men: their teeth are a weapon and arrows, and their tongue is a sharp sword.

**56:6** Be exalted over the heavens, God, and may Your glory be over all the earth.

**56:7** They prepared traps for my feet, and they bowed down my soul; they dug a pit before my face, and they fell into it. Interlude.

**56:8** My heart is ready, God, my heart is ready; I will sing and make melody.

**56:9** Awake, my glory; awake, harp and lyre; I will awake at dawn.

**56:10** I will give thanks to You among peoples, Lord; I will make melody to You among nations,

**56:11** because Your mercy has been magnified up to the heavens, and Your truth up to the clouds.

**56:12** Be exalted over the heavens, God, and may Your glory be over all the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **56:1** — The superscription phrases "For the end" (eis to telos), "do not destroy" (me diaphtheires), and "for a pillar inscription" (eis stelographian) are characteristic Septuagint renderings of Hebrew psalm headings.
- **56:2** — The Greek reads "I will hope" (elpio) and "lawlessness" (he anomia), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "I will take refuge" and "calamities."
- **56:3** — "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, denoting a musical pause or interlude in the psalm; "who has done good to me" reflects the Greek participle euergetesanta me.
- **56:5** — The Greek links this verse directly to verse 4 with "and rescued my soul from the midst of young lions," followed by "I lay down troubled" (ekoimethen tetaragmenos), differing from the Masoretic Text.
