# Psalm 51 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**51:1** For the end: of understanding, for David,

**51:2** when Doeg the Idumean came and reported to Saul and said to him, "David has come into the house of Abimelech."

**51:3** Why do you boast in evil, mighty one—lawlessness all day long?

**51:4** Your tongue devised unrighteousness; like a sharpened razor, you worked deceit.

**51:5** You loved evil more than goodness, unrighteousness more than speaking righteousness. Interlude.

**51:6** You loved all the words of engulfment, a deceitful tongue.

**51:7** Therefore God will tear you down forever; may He pluck you out and banish you from a dwelling, and your root from the land of the living. Interlude.

**51:8** And the righteous will see and fear, and they will laugh at him and say,

**51:9** "Look, a man who did not make God his helper, but put his hope in the abundance of his wealth, and grew strong in his futility."

**51:10** But I am like a fruitful olive tree in the house of God; I have hoped in the mercy of God forever, and forever and ever.

**51:11** I will give thanks to You forever, because You have acted; and I will wait for Your name, for it is good before Your devout ones.

## Translator Notes

- **51:2** — The Greek names the priest Abimelech, whereas the Hebrew MT reads Ahimelech (cf. 1 Sam 21:1), and calls Doeg "the Idumean" (Ἰδουμαῖον, the Septuagint form for Edomite).
- **51:3** — Greek "anomian" ("lawlessness") differs markedly from the Hebrew MT ("the steadfast love of God"), likely reflecting a different reading or the rare homonymous Hebrew sense "reproach/disgrace."
- **51:5** — Greek "diapsalma" ("Interlude") denotes a musical pause or instrumental interlude, corresponding to Hebrew "selah" (also in 51:7).
- **51:7** — The Greek shifts from the future indicative ("will tear you down") to third-person aorist optatives ("may He pluck you out and banish you"), and reads "from a dwelling" without the possessive "your."
- **51:9** — The Greek has "helper" ("boethon") and "futility" ("mataioteti"), where the Hebrew MT reads "refuge" and "destruction" or "desire."
