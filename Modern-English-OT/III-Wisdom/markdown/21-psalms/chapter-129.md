# Psalm 129 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**129:1** A song of the steps. From the depths I cried out to You, Lord.

**129:2** Lord, listen to my voice; let Your ears become attentive to the voice of my supplication.

**129:3** If You should closely watch lawless deeds, Lord, Lord, who could stand?

**129:4** For with You is propitiation. For the sake of Your name,

**129:5** I waited, Lord; my soul waited for His law.

**129:6** My soul hoped in the Lord from the morning watch until night;

**129:7** for with the Lord is mercy, and with Him is plentiful redemption,

**129:8** and He Himself will redeem Israel from all his lawless deeds.

## Translator Notes

- **129:4** — Greek "ho hilasmos" means "propitiation" or "atonement" (rendering the Hebrew "forgiveness"). The Septuagint also reads "For the sake of Your name" (joining it to verse 5), where the Hebrew reads "so that You may be feared."
- **129:5** — The Greek reads "eis ton nomon autou" ("for His law"), contrasting with the Hebrew "in His word." This Greek text also has no explicit direct object ("for You") after "hypemeina" ("I waited").
- **129:6** — The Greek reads "from the morning watch until night" (contrasting with the Hebrew "more than watchmen for the morning"). This Greek witness also omits the exhortation "let Israel hope in the Lord" present in other manuscripts and the Hebrew of verse 7.
