## Text

**139:1** For the end; a psalm for David.

**139:2** Rescue me, Lord, from an evil person; deliver me from an unjust man,

**139:3** who planned injustices in their heart; all day long they were marshaling wars;

**139:4** they sharpened their tongue like that of a serpent; venom of asps is under their lips. Interlude.

**139:5** Guard me, Lord, from the hand of a sinner; rescue me from unjust people, who planned to trip up my steps;

**139:6** arrogant ones hid a snare for me, and they stretched out cords, snares for my feet; beside the path they set a stumbling block for me. Interlude.

**139:7** I said to the Lord, "You are my God." Give ear, Lord, to the voice of my supplication.

**139:8** Lord, Lord, strength of my salvation, You overshadowed my head in the day of war.

**139:9** Do not hand me over, Lord, away from my desire to a sinner; they plotted against me; do not abandon me, so that they may never be exalted. Interlude.

**139:10** As for the head of their encirclement, the trouble of their lips will cover them.

**139:11** Coals of fire will fall upon them on the earth, and You will cast them down. In miseries they will by no means endure.

**139:12** Evils will hunt down an unjust man to destruction.

**139:13** I know that the Lord will carry out the judgment of the poor and the justice of the needy.

**139:14** Surely righteous ones will give thanks to Your name, and upright ones will dwell in Your presence.

## Translator Notes

- **139:4** — "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, a musical or liturgical pause marker corresponding to Hebrew Selah (also in verses 6 and 9).
- **139:9** — The Greek literally reads "away from my desire" (apo tes epithymias mou), reflecting a different division and reading of the Hebrew than "the desires of the wicked."
- **139:11** — The Greek text reads "coals of fire ... on the earth, and You will cast them down," whereas other Septuagint witnesses read "coals; into fire You will cast them down."
- **139:12** — The Greek text here is shorter than other witnesses, omitting an opening clause concerning a talkative or slanderous man not being established on the earth.
