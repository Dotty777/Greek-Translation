## Text

**83:1** For the end, concerning the winepresses; a psalm for the sons of Korah.

**83:2** How beloved are Your dwellings, Lord of powers!

**83:3** My soul longs and faints for the courts of the Lord; my heart and my flesh have exulted in the living God.

**83:4** For indeed a sparrow has found a house for itself, and a turtledove a nest for herself where she will place her young: Your altars, Lord of powers, my King and my God.

**83:5** Blessed are all who dwell in Your house; forever and ever they will praise You. Interlude.

**83:6** Blessed is the man whose help is from You, Lord; he arranged ascents in his heart,

**83:7** in the valley of weeping, to the place that he appointed. For indeed the Lawgiver will give blessings;

**83:8** they will go from power to power; the God of gods will be seen in Zion.

**83:9** Lord God of powers, hear my prayer; give ear, God of Jacob. Interlude.

**83:10** Look, God, our defender, and gaze upon the face of Your anointed one.

**83:11** For one day in Your courts is better than thousands; I have chosen to be cast aside in the house of God rather than to dwell in the tents of sinners.

**83:12** For the Lord God loves mercy and truth; He will give grace and glory. The Lord will not withhold good things from those who walk in innocence.

**83:13** Lord of powers, blessed is the person who hopes in You.

## Translator Notes

- **83:1** — Greek eis to telos ("For the end") and hyper ton lenon ("concerning the winepresses") are the Septuagint's renderings of the Hebrew headings traditionally understood as "For the choirmaster" and "on the Gittith."
- **83:7** — Greek renders Hebrew "valley of Baca" etymologically as "valley of weeping," reads "to the place that he appointed" (grammatically referring to the pilgrim of v. 6, though sometimes taken of God), and translates Hebrew moreh ("early rain") as ho nomotheton ("the Lawgiver").
- **83:8** — Greek reads ho theos ton theon ("the God of gods") as the subject of passive ophthesetai ("will be seen"), whereas the Hebrew reads "each one appears before God in Zion." The phrase "from power to power" (ek dynameos eis dynamin) also echoes the Greek title "Lord of powers" (ton dynameon, vv. 2, 4, 9, 13, rendering Hebrew tsebaoth).
- **83:11** — Greek has plural chiliadas ("thousands") rather than singular "a thousand," uses pararipteisthai ("to be cast aside") where the Hebrew reads "stand at the threshold," and reads "of God" rather than "of my God."
- **83:12** — Greek reads "loves mercy and truth" where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "is a sun and shield." Following the Greek punctuation, "the Lord" begins the final sentence.
