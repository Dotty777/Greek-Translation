## Text

**25:1** Of David. Judge me, Lord, for I have walked in my innocence, and hoping in the Lord, I will never be shaken.

**25:2** Examine me, Lord, and test me; refine my kidneys and my heart by fire.

**25:3** For Your mercy is before my eyes, and I have been well-pleasing in Your truth.

**25:4** I have not sat with a council of vanity, and with lawbreakers I will never go in.

**25:5** I have hated an assembly of evildoers, and with the ungodly I will never sit.

**25:6** I will wash my hands among the innocent, and I will go around Your altar,

**25:7** to hear a voice of praise and to recount all Your wondrous works.

**25:8** Lord, I have loved the beauty of Your house and the place of the tent of Your glory.

**25:9** Do not destroy my soul together with the ungodly, or my life with men of blood,

**25:10** in whose hands are acts of lawlessness; their right hand has been filled with bribes.

**25:11** But I have walked in my innocence; redeem me and have mercy on me.

**25:12** For my foot has stood in uprightness; in assemblies I will bless You, Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **25:2** — Greek "kidneys" (nephroi) denotes the innermost thoughts and emotions in biblical idiom, paired here with "heart."
- **25:3** — Greek euērestēsa reflects the Septuagint rendering of Hebrew "walk" (in the sense of living pleasingly before God) and can be understood either as "have been well-pleasing" or "have been well pleased."
- **25:6** — Greek en athōois is plural ("among the innocent," or possibly neuter plural "in innocence"), contrasting with "the ungodly" in verse 5; verse 7 then reads "to hear" (akousai) rather than Hebrew "to proclaim."
- **25:12** — Greek euthytēs carries both the concrete sense of straightness or level ground and the moral sense of uprightness; the closing clause addresses the Lord directly in the second person ("bless You, Lord").
