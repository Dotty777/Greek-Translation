## Text

**24:1** A psalm of David. To You, Lord, I have lifted up my soul,

**24:2** my God. In You I have trusted; may I not be put to shame, nor let my enemies laugh at me.

**24:3** For indeed all who wait for You will never be put to shame; let those who act lawlessly without cause be shamed.

**24:4** Make Your ways known to me, Lord, and teach me Your paths;

**24:5** guide me to Your truth and teach me, for You are God my Savior, and for You I have waited all the day.

**24:6** Remember Your compassions and Your mercies, for they are from eternity.

**24:7** Do not remember the sins of my youth and of ignorance; according to Your mercy remember me, for the sake of Your goodness, Lord.

**24:8** Good and upright is the Lord; therefore He will instruct those who sin in the way.

**24:9** He will guide the gentle in judgment; He will teach the gentle His ways.

**24:10** All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth to those who seek out His covenant and His testimonies.

**24:11** For the sake of Your name, Lord, You will pardon my sin, for it is great.

**24:12** Who is the man who fears the Lord? He will instruct him in the way that he chose.

**24:13** His soul will abide in good things, and his offspring will inherit the land.

**24:14** The Lord is a stronghold for those who fear Him, and the name of the Lord belongs to those who fear Him, and His covenant is to be made known to them.

**24:15** My eyes are continually toward the Lord, for He Himself will draw my feet out of the snare.

**24:16** Look upon me and have mercy on me, for I am alone and poor.

**24:17** The afflictions of my heart have multiplied; bring me out of my distresses.

**24:18** See my lowliness and my toil, and forgive all my sins.

**24:19** See my enemies, for they have multiplied, and with an unjust hatred they have hated me.

**24:20** Guard my soul and rescue me; may I not be put to shame, for I have hoped in You.

**24:21** Innocent and upright ones clung to me, for I waited for You, Lord.

**24:22** Redeem Israel, God, from all his afflictions.

## Translator Notes

- **24:8** — The Greek verb nomothetesei literally means "will lay down law for" or "give legal instruction to," used in the Septuagint to render Hebrew "instruct."
- **24:12** — In Greek, the subject of the verb "chose" (heretisato) is unstated and could refer either to the God-fearing man ("that he chose") or to the Lord ("that He chose").
- **24:14** — Greek krataioma means "stronghold" (differing from the Masoretic Hebrew "friendship/counsel"), and this Septuagint text includes the additional clause "and the name of the Lord belongs to those who fear Him."
- **24:21** — Where the Masoretic Hebrew has a prayer ("May integrity and uprightness preserve me"), the Septuagint reads personal subjects with a past verb ("Innocent and upright ones clung to me") and adds "Lord."
