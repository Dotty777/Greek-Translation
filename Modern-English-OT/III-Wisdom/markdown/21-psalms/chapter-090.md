## Text

**90:1** A praise of a song, for David. The one who dwells in the help of the Most High will lodge in the shelter of the God of heaven.

**90:2** He will say to God, "You are my protector, and my God is my refuge; I will hope in Him."

**90:3** For He Himself will rescue from the snare of hunters, and from a troublesome word.

**90:4** With His shoulders He will overshadow you, and under His wings you will hope; with a shield His truth will surround you.

**90:5** You will not be afraid of nightly terror, of an arrow flying by day,

**90:6** of a thing passing through in darkness, of calamity and a midday demon.

**90:7** A thousand will fall at your side, and ten thousand at your right hand, yet it will not draw near to you.

**90:8** Only with your eyes will you observe, and you will see the repayment of sinners.

**90:9** For You, Lord, are my hope; you have made the Most High your refuge.

**90:10** Evils will not come to you, and a scourge will not draw near to your dwelling.

**90:11** For He will command His angels concerning you to guard you in your ways.

**90:12** On their hands they will lift you up, so that you never strike your foot against a stone.

**90:13** Upon an asp and a basilisk you will tread, and you will trample down a lion and a dragon.

**90:14** "Because he has hoped in Me, I will rescue him; I will shelter him, because he has known My name.

**90:15** He will call upon Me, and I will hear him; I am with him in affliction, and I will deliver and glorify him.

**90:16** With length of days I will satisfy him, and I will show him My salvation."

## Translator Notes

- **90:1** — The superscription ("A praise of a song, for David") appears in the Septuagint but not the Hebrew MT; the Greek also reads "in the help of the Most High" and "the God of heaven" where the MT has "shelter" and "the Almighty" (Shaddai).
- **90:3** — The Saints Greek text omits "you" after "will rescue" (present in some manuscripts) and follows the Septuagint in reading "hunters" and "a troublesome word" (reading Hebrew dabar "word" rather than MT deber "pestilence").
- **90:6** — The Septuagint reads "a thing" (again reading Hebrew dabar rather than MT deber "pestilence") and "a midday demon," differing from the MT's "destruction that wastes at noonday."
- **90:11** — The Saints Greek text reads simply "in your ways," omitting "all" found in the MT and some other Greek manuscripts.
- **90:13** — Following the Septuagint, the Greek names two venomous serpents ("an asp and a basilisk") where the Hebrew MT has "the lion and the cobra."
