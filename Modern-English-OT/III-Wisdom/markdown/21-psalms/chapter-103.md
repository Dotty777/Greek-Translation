## Text

**103:1** Of David. Bless the Lord, my soul. Lord, Lord, my God, how exceedingly You have been magnified! You clothed Yourself with thanksgiving and splendor,

**103:2** wrapping Yourself in light like a garment, stretching out heaven like a curtain;

**103:3** the One who roofs His upper chambers with waters, the One who makes clouds His ascent, the One who walks on wings of winds;

**103:4** the One who makes His angels spirits, and His ministers flaming fire.

**103:5** He founded the earth upon its stability; it will not tilt forever and ever.

**103:6** The deep, like a garment, is His covering; waters will stand upon the mountains.

**103:7** At Your rebuke they will flee; at the sound of Your thunder they will be terrified.

**103:8** Mountains ascend, and plains descend to the place that You founded for them.

**103:9** You set a boundary that they will not pass over, nor will they turn back to cover the earth.

**103:10** The One who sends springs in ravines: between the mountains waters will pass through;

**103:11** they will give drink to all the wild beasts of the field; wild donkeys will wait in their thirst.

**103:12** Beside them the birds of heaven will dwell; from the midst of the rocks they will give voice.

**103:13** Watering mountains from His upper chambers; from the fruit of Your works the earth will be satisfied.

**103:14** Making grass sprout for the livestock and green plants for the service of mankind, to bring forth bread from the earth;

**103:15** and wine gladdens the heart of man, to brighten a face with oil, and bread strengthens the heart of man.

**103:16** The trees of the plain will be satisfied, the cedars of Lebanon that He planted.

**103:17** There sparrows will nest; the house of the heron leads it.

**103:18** The high mountains are for the deer; a rock is a refuge for the rock badgers.

**103:19** He made the moon for seasons; the sun knows its setting.

**103:20** You set darkness, and it becomes night; in it all the wild beasts of the forest will pass through,

**103:21** young lions roaring to seize and to seek food for themselves from God.

**103:22** The sun rises, and they will be gathered together, and in their dens they will lie down.

**103:23** Man will go out to his work, and to his labor until evening.

**103:24** How Your works have been magnified, Lord! You made all things in wisdom; the earth has been filled with Your creation.

**103:25** This is the sea, great and spacious; there are creeping things of which there is no number, living creatures, small with great,

**103:26** this dragon that You formed to play with.

**103:27** All things look expectantly to You, to give them their food in due season;

**103:28** when You give to them, they will gather; and when You open Your hand, all things together will be filled with goodness.

**103:29** But when You turn away Your face, they will be troubled; You will take away their spirit, and they will expire, and to their dust they will return.

**103:30** You will send forth Your Spirit, and they will be created, and You will renew the face of the earth.

**103:31** Let the glory of the Lord be forever; the Lord will be glad in His works;

**103:32** the One who looks upon the earth and makes it tremble, the One who touches the mountains and they smoke.

**103:33** I will sing to the Lord during my life; I will sing praise to my God as long as I exist.

**103:34** May my reflection be sweet to Him, and I will be glad in the Lord.

**103:35** May sinners vanish from the earth, and lawless ones, so that they do not exist. Bless the Lord, my soul.

## Translator Notes

- **103:1** — The Greek text reads a doubled address ('Lord, Lord') with exclamatory 'how,' and uses exomologesis ('thanksgiving' or 'praise/confession') where the Hebrew text has 'splendor.'
- **103:4** — Greek angelous can mean 'angels' or 'messengers,' and pneumata can mean 'spirits' or 'winds' (the latter paralleling 'flaming fire').
- **103:6** — The Greek masculine genitive pronoun autou ('His') refers to God as wearer of the deep like a garment (echoing v. 2), unlike the Hebrew text's 'You covered it [the earth].'
- **103:17** — The Septuagint identifies the bird as a heron (erodios) and renders the clause with hegeitai auto ('leads it,' dative singular), differing markedly from the Hebrew wording about the stork's home in the fir trees.
- **103:26** — This Greek text lacks the clause about ships sailing there, uses drakon ('dragon') for the sea creature, and reads empaizein auto ('to play with' or 'to mock' it).
