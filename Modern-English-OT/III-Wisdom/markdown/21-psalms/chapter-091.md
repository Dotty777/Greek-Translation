## Text

**91:1** A psalm of a song, for the day of the Sabbath.

**91:2** It is good to give thanks to the Lord and to sing psalms to Your name, Most High,

**91:3** to declare Your mercy in the morning and Your truth by night,

**91:4** on a ten-stringed harp, with a song on a lyre.

**91:5** For You have made me glad, Lord, in Your work, and in the works of Your hands I will exult.

**91:6** How Your works have been magnified, Lord; Your thoughts have become exceedingly weighty.

**91:7** A foolish man will not know, and one without understanding will not understand these things.

**91:8** When the sinners sprout up like grass and all those who work lawlessness have peered forth, it is so that they may be utterly destroyed forever and ever.

**91:9** But You are Most High forever, Lord.

**91:10** For look, Your enemies will perish, and all those who work lawlessness will be scattered.

**91:11** And my horn will be exalted like that of a unicorn, and my old age will be in rich oil.

**91:12** And my eye has looked upon my enemies, and my ear will hear concerning the evildoers who rise up against me.

**91:13** A righteous one will blossom like a palm tree; like the cedar in Lebanon he will be multiplied.

**91:14** Planted in the house of the Lord, in the courts of our God they will blossom forth.

**91:15** Then they will be multiplied in a rich old age, and they will be thriving,

**91:16** to declare that the Lord my God is upright, and there is no unrighteousness in Him.

## Translator Notes

- **91:6** — The Saints Greek text reads ebarynthesan ("have become weighty/heavy"), a textual variant where other Greek manuscripts read ebathynthesan ("have become deep," matching the Hebrew MT).
- **91:8** — Greek diekypsan literally means "peered forth" or "peered out," rendering the Hebrew verb for "flourished" by a similarly spelled Hebrew verb meaning "peer out."
- **91:10** — The Saints Greek text has the shorter single clause "For look, Your enemies will perish," without the repeated clause found in the Hebrew MT and some Greek manuscripts.
- **91:11** — Greek monokeros literally means "one-horned creature" or "unicorn" (the standard Septuagint rendering of Hebrew re'em, "wild ox"), and the Septuagint reads "my old age" where the MT has "I have been anointed."
- **91:15** — The Saints Greek text reads tote ("Then") rather than eti ("Still") found in the MT and other Greek manuscripts.
