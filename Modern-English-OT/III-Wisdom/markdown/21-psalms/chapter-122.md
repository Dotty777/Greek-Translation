## Text

**122:1** A song of the steps. To You I have lifted up my eyes, You who dwell in heaven.

**122:2** Look, as the eyes of servants are toward the hands of their masters, as the eyes of a maidservant are toward the hands of her mistress, so our eyes are toward the Lord our God, until He has compassion on us.

**122:3** Have mercy on us, Lord, have mercy on us, for we have been greatly filled with contempt.

**122:4** Our soul has been filled even more; reproach to those who prosper, and contempt to the proud.

## Translator Notes

- **122:1** — Greek anabathmōn literally means "of the steps," the Septuagint title corresponding to the Songs of Ascents; Greek also reads singular "in heaven" (en tō ouranō) with "who dwells" (katoikounta).
- **122:2** — Greek has plural "hands" (cheiras) in both comparisons, uses the Septuagintal infinitive construction heōs hou oikteirēsai hēmas ("until He has compassion on us"), and distinguishes "have compassion" (oikteireō) here from "have mercy" (eleeō) in v. 3.
- **122:4** — Greek uses nominatives with datives ("reproach to those who prosper, and contempt to the proud"), which can be read either as an imprecation against the arrogant or as a literalistic rendering of the Hebrew idiom for scorn coming from them. In vv. 3-4, "filled" renders passive plēthynō ("be multiplied / filled abundantly").
