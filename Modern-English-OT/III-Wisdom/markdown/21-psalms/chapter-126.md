# Psalm 126 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

> **Status:** `draft: true` (5 verses, 4 translator notes)

## Text

**126:1** A song of the steps. Unless the Lord builds a house, those who build it have labored in vain; unless the Lord guards a city, the guard has stayed awake in vain.

**126:2** It is in vain for you to rise early, to get up after sitting down, you who eat bread of sorrow, when He gives sleep to His beloved ones.

**126:3** Look, sons are the inheritance of the Lord, the reward of the fruit of her womb.

**126:4** Like arrows in the hand of a mighty man, so are the sons of those who have been shaken out.

**126:5** Blessed is he who will fulfill his desire from them; he will not be put to shame when they speak to their enemies in the gate.

## Translator Notes

- **126:2** — Following the Septuagint closely, the Greek reads "to get up after sitting down" (rather than staying up late) and introduces the final clause with temporal "when" (ὅταν) rather than "for so."
- **126:3** — In this Greek text, "the reward of the fruit of her womb" places "fruit" in the genitive (τοῦ καρποῦ) and includes "her" (αὐτῆς), placing the phrase in apposition to "sons" as the Lord's inheritance.
- **126:4** — "Those who have been shaken out" (τῶν ἐκτετιναγμένων) reflects the Septuagint reading the underlying Hebrew term from the root meaning "shake out" rather than "youth."
- **126:5** — The Septuagint reads "his desire" (τὴν ἐπιθυμίαν αὐτοῦ) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "his quiver," and this Greek text shifts from singular "he will not be put to shame" (καταισχυνθήσεται) to plural "when they speak to their enemies."
