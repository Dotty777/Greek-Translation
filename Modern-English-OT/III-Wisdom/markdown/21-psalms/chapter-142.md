## Text

**142:1** A psalm for David, when his son pursues him. Lord, hear my prayer; give ear to my supplication in Your truth; listen to me in Your righteousness.

**142:2** And do not enter into judgment with Your servant, for no one living will be justified before You.

**142:3** He brought my life low to the ground; he made me sit in dark places like the dead of long ago.

**142:4** And my spirit grew despondent upon me; within me my heart was troubled.

**142:5** I remembered ancient days, and I meditated on all Your works; on the deeds of Your hands I kept meditating.

**142:6** I spread out my hands to You; my soul is like a waterless land for You. Interlude.

**142:7** Hear me quickly, Lord; my spirit has failed. Do not turn Your face away from me, or I will be made like those who descend into the pit.

**142:8** Make me hear Your mercy in the morning, for in You I have hoped; make known to me, Lord, the way in which I will walk, for to You I have lifted up my soul.

**142:9** Deliver me from my enemies, Lord, for to You I have fled for refuge.

**142:10** Teach me to do Your will, for You are my God; Your Holy Spirit will guide me in the straight way.

**142:11** For the sake of Your name, Lord, You will keep me alive in Your righteousness; You will bring my soul out of affliction.

**142:12** And in Your mercy You will wipe out my enemies, and You will destroy all those who afflict my soul, for I am Your servant.

## Translator Notes

- **142:1** — The historical clause "when his son pursues him" (referring to Absalom) is an LXX superscription absent from the Hebrew MT, using the historical present verb "katadiokei."
- **142:3** — This Greek text omits the opening clause ("For the enemy has pursued my soul") found in other witnesses, leaving the adversary as the implicit subject of "He"; Greek "nekrous aionos" literally means "dead ones of an age" (those long dead, or dead forever).
- **142:6** — "Interlude" translates Greek "diapsalma," the LXX rendering of the Hebrew liturgical or musical term "selah."
- **142:10** — This Greek text reads "Your Holy Spirit" ("to hagion") rather than "Your good Spirit" ("to agathon") as in other LXX and Hebrew witnesses, and "en te eutheia" ("in the straight way") lacks the noun "land" ("ge").
- **142:11** — In verses 11-12 the LXX uses future indicative verbs ("You will...") rather than imperatives, and the Greek punctuation links "in Your righteousness" with the preceding clause "You will keep me alive."
