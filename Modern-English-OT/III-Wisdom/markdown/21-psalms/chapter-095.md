## Text

**95:1** When the house is being built after the captivity; a song for David. Sing a new song to the Lord; sing to the Lord, all the earth.

**95:2** Sing to the Lord, bless His name; proclaim the good news of His salvation from day to day.

**95:3** Declare His glory among the nations, His wonders among all the peoples.

**95:4** For the Lord is great and greatly praiseworthy; He is to be feared above all the gods.

**95:5** For all the gods of the nations are demons, but the Lord made the heavens.

**95:6** Thanksgiving and beauty are before Him; holiness and magnificence are in His sanctuary.

**95:7** Bring to the Lord, families of the nations, bring to the Lord glory and honor.

**95:8** Bring to the Lord glory for His name; take up sacrifices and enter into His courts.

**95:9** Worship the Lord in His holy court; let all the earth be shaken before His face.

**95:10** Say among the nations, "The Lord has become king; for indeed He firmly established the inhabited world, which will not be shaken; He will judge peoples in uprightness."

**95:11** Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth exult; let the sea and its fullness be shaken.

**95:12** The fields will rejoice, and all things in them; then all the trees of the forest will exult

**95:13** before the face of the Lord, for He is coming, for He is coming to judge the earth; He will judge the inhabited world in righteousness and peoples in His truth.

## Translator Notes

- **95:1** — The Greek superscription attributes this psalm to David in connection with the rebuilding of the temple after the exile; Hebrew Psalm 96 has no superscription.
- **95:5** — Greek renders the gods of the nations as "demons" (δαιμόνια), contrasting them with the Lord who made the heavens.
- **95:9** — Greek reads "in His holy court" (ἐν αὐλῇ ἁγίᾳ αὐτοῦ), continuing the temple court imagery from verse 8, where the Hebrew has "in the splendor of holiness."
- **95:11** — Greek uses σαλευθήτω ("be shaken"), echoing the same verb used of the earth in verses 9-10 rather than "roar" as in the Hebrew.
