## Text

**23:1** A psalm of David; for the first day of the week. To the Lord belong the earth and its fullness, the inhabited world and all who dwell in it.

**23:2** He Himself founded it upon the seas, and prepared it upon the rivers.

**23:3** Who will go up to the mountain of the Lord, and who will stand in His holy place?

**23:4** One innocent in hands and pure in heart, who has not taken his soul in vain, and has not sworn with deceit to his neighbor.

**23:5** This one will receive a blessing from the Lord, and mercy from God his Savior.

**23:6** This is the generation of those who seek Him, of those who seek the face of the God of Jacob. Interlude.

**23:7** Lift up the gates, you rulers among you, and be lifted up, eternal gates, and the King of glory will enter.

**23:8** Who is this King of glory? The Lord, strong and powerful, the Lord, powerful in war.

**23:9** Lift up the gates, you rulers among you, and be lifted up, eternal gates, and the King of glory will enter.

**23:10** Who is this King of glory? The Lord of powers, He Himself is this King of glory.

## Translator Notes

- **23:1** — The Septuagint adds the liturgical designation "for the first day of the week" (Sunday), which is not found in the Masoretic Hebrew superscription.
- **23:6** — "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, the Septuagint's rendering of the Hebrew musical term Selah; the Greek also specifies "the God of Jacob" rather than simply "Jacob."
- **23:7** — Greek arate pylas, hoi archontes hymon literally means "Lift up gates, your rulers," taking "gates" as the direct object and translating Hebrew "your heads" as "your rulers."
- **23:10** — "The Lord of powers" translates Greek Kyrios ton dynameon (literally "powers," "forces," or "armies"), the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew title "Lord of hosts" (Sabaoth).
