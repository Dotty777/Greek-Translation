## Text

**149:1** Hallelujah. Sing to the Lord a new song; His praise is in the assembly of the devout.

**149:2** Let Israel be glad in the One who made him, and let the sons of Zion exult in their King.

**149:3** Let them praise His name with dancing; with tambourine and harp let them make music to Him.

**149:4** For the Lord takes pleasure in His people, and He will exalt the meek with salvation.

**149:5** The devout will boast in glory, and they will exult on their beds.

**149:6** The exaltations of God are in their throats, and two-edged swords are in their hands,

**149:7** to carry out vengeance among the nations, rebukes among the peoples,

**149:8** to bind their kings with fetters and their honored ones with iron manacles,

**149:9** to carry out on them a written judgment. This is glory for all His devout ones.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**149:14** *(after `149:9`)* Hallelujah.

## Translator Notes

- **149:4** — The Septuagint reads 'will exalt' (Greek hypsosei), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'will adorn' or 'will beautify.'
- **149:6** — Greek 'throats' is literally singular ('in their throat'), used distributively; 'exaltations' echoes the verb 'will exalt' in verse 4.
- **149:9** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text, the Greek text has no closing 'Hallelujah' at the end of Psalm 149, placing it instead at the beginning of Psalm 150.
