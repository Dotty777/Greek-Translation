## Text

**11:1** For the end, concerning the eighth. A psalm for David.

**11:2** Save me, Lord, for the devout one has vanished, for truths have diminished from among the sons of men.

**11:3** Each one has spoken empty things to his neighbor; deceitful lips have spoken with a heart and a heart.

**11:4** May the Lord utterly destroy all the deceitful lips and a boastful tongue,

**11:5** those who have said, "We will magnify our tongue, our lips belong to us; who is lord over us?"

**11:6** "Because of the misery of the poor and because of the groaning of the needy, now I will rise up," says the Lord. "I will set them in salvation; I will speak boldly in it."

**11:7** The utterances of the Lord are pure utterances, silver refined by fire, tested in the earth, purified sevenfold.

**11:8** You, Lord, will guard us and will preserve us from this generation and forever.

**11:9** The ungodly walk all around; according to Your height You have greatly esteemed the sons of men.

## Translator Notes

- **11:2** — The Septuagint renders the Hebrew with the abstract plural "truths" (hai alētheiai), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is commonly read as "the faithful."
- **11:3** — "With a heart and a heart" literally preserves a Hebraic idiom in the Greek (en kardia kai en kardia) denoting a double or insincere heart.
- **11:6** — The Greek verb "I will set" (thēsomai) leaves its object unexpressed, supplied as "them" from "the poor" and "the needy." The closing clause "I will speak boldly in it" (parēsiasomai en autō) is a distinctive Septuagint rendering differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **11:7** — The Greek phrase "tested in the earth" (dokimion tē gē) uses a dative without mentioning a furnace, which appears in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **11:9** — The Septuagint reading "according to Your height You have greatly esteemed the sons of men" diverges sharply from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("when vileness is exalted among the sons of men").
