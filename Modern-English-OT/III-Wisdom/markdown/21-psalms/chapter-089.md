## Text

**89:1** A prayer of Moses, the man of God. Lord, You have become a refuge for us in generation after generation.

**89:2** Before the mountains came into being and the earth and the inhabited world were formed, even from eternity to eternity, You are.

**89:3** Do not turn man back to humiliation, and did You say, "Return, sons of men"?

**89:4** For a thousand years in Your eyes are like yesterday, which passed by, and a watch in the night.

**89:5** Their things of no account will be years; in the morning may it pass away like green grass.

**89:6** In the morning may it blossom and pass away; in the evening may it fall off, grow hard, and dry up.

**89:7** For we have come to an end in Your wrath, and in Your fury we have been troubled.

**89:8** You have set our lawless deeds before You; our lifetime is in the illumination of Your face.

**89:9** For all our days have come to an end, and in Your wrath we have come to an end; our years devised, as it were, a spider's web.

**89:10** The days of our years, in years, are seventy years, and if in strength, eighty years, and the greater part of them is toil and pain; for gentleness has come upon us, and we will be disciplined.

**89:11** Who knows the power of Your wrath, and out of the fear of Your fury how to reckon?

**89:12** Make known Your right hand, and those who have been disciplined in heart with wisdom.

**89:13** Return, Lord; how long? And be comforted over Your servants.

**89:14** We were satisfied in the morning with Your mercy, and we exulted and were glad in all our days,

**89:15** in return for the days in which You humbled us, the years in which we saw evils.

**89:16** And look upon Your servants and Your works, and guide their sons.

**89:17** And let the splendor of the Lord our God be upon us, and direct the works of our hands upon us.

## Translator Notes

- **89:3** — Unlike the Hebrew MT ("You return man to dust"), the Septuagint begins with a prohibition ("Do not turn ... back") and reads "humiliation" (tapeinosis); the closing question mark reflects the punctuation of the Greek source text (some editions punctuate the second clause as a statement).
- **89:5** — The Greek reads "Their things of no account will be years," reflecting a Hebrew vocalization meaning "year" rather than MT "sleep," followed by a series of optative verbs ("may it...") in vv. 5-6.
- **89:9** — The closing Greek clause is notoriously difficult: the accusative arachnen can mean "spider" or "spider's web," and the imperfect verb emeleton is ambiguous in form between third-person plural ("our years devised/practiced") and first-person singular ("I pondered our years like a spider's web").
- **89:10** — The Saints Greek text reads en eniautois ("in years," where many Greek manuscripts read en autois, "in them") and follows the Septuagint in reading "for gentleness has come upon us, and we will be disciplined" (differing from the MT).
- **89:11** — The Greek attaches the infinitive exarithmesasthai ("to reckon" or "to number") to v. 11 rather than v. 12 (as in the MT) and reads the genitive phrase "the fear of Your fury."
