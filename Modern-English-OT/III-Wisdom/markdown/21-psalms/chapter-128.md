# Psalm 128 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

> **Status:** `draft: true` (8 verses, 3 translator notes)

## Text

**128:1** A song of the steps. "Many times they fought against me from my youth," let Israel now say:

**128:2** "Many times they fought against me from my youth, and yet they could not prevail against me.

**128:3** On my back the sinners were building; they lengthened their lawlessness.

**128:4** The righteous Lord cut through the neck of sinners."

**128:5** Let all those who hate Zion be put to shame and turned backward.

**128:6** Let them become like grass of the rooftops, which withered before being plucked up,

**128:7** with which the reaper did not fill his hand, nor the one gathering the sheaves his bosom;

**128:8** and those passing by did not say, "The blessing of the Lord be upon us; we will bless you in the name of the Lord."

## Translator Notes

- **128:3** — Greek "etektainon" literally means "were building" or "worked as craftsmen" (contrasting with the Hebrew "plowed"), and "ten anomian auton" means "their lawlessness" (contrasting with the Hebrew "their furrows").
- **128:4** — The Greek reads "auchena" ("neck," singular), where the Hebrew text reads "cords."
- **128:8** — The Greek text here reads "eph' hemas" ("upon us") and the future "eulogesomen" ("we will bless"), where other Greek manuscripts and the Hebrew read "upon you" and "we bless" or "we have blessed."
