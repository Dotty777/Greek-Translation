## Text

**5:1** For the end, concerning her who inherits; a psalm for David.

**5:2** Give ear to my words, Lord; understand my cry.

**5:3** Pay attention to the voice of my supplication, my King and my God, because to You I will pray, Lord.

**5:4** In the morning You will hear my voice; in the morning I will stand before You and watch.

**5:5** For You are not a God who desires lawlessness, nor will one who does evil sojourn with You.

**5:6** Lawbreakers will not remain before Your eyes; You have hated, Lord, all who practice lawlessness.

**5:7** You will destroy all who speak falsehood; the Lord detests a man of bloodshed and a deceitful man.

**5:8** But I, in the abundance of Your mercy, will enter into Your house; I will worship toward Your holy temple in fear of You.

**5:9** Lord, guide me in Your righteousness because of my enemies; make my way straight before You.

**5:10** For there is no truth in their mouth; their heart is empty. Their throat is an open tomb; with their tongues they practiced deceit.

**5:11** Judge them, God; let them fall away from their counsels. According to the multitude of their acts of ungodliness, drive them out, because they embittered You, Lord.

**5:12** And let all who hope in You be glad in You; they will exult forever, and You will dwell among them. And all who love Your name will boast in You,

**5:13** because You bless a righteous person. Lord, as with a shield of favor You have crowned us.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — "Concerning her who inherits" renders the Greek feminine participle hyper tes kleronomouses, where the Septuagint connects the obscure Hebrew musical term (hannechiloth) to the root for inheriting.
- **5:9** — The Greek text reads "my way before You," reversing the possessive pronouns compared to the Hebrew ("Your way before me").
- **5:10** — The final verb is a Hellenistic imperfect form (edoliousan, "they practiced deceit"); Paul quotes the second half of this verse directly from the Septuagint in Romans 3:13.
- **5:13** — The Greek reads first-person plural "You have crowned us" (estephanosas hemas), whereas the Hebrew has third-person singular "You surround him."
