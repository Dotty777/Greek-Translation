# Psalm 20 — Saints Modern English (Greek Septuagint)

## Text

**20:1** For the end; a psalm for David.

**20:2** Lord, in Your power the king will be glad, and over Your salvation he will exult greatly.

**20:3** You gave him the desire of his soul, and You did not deprive him of the petition of his lips. Interlude.

**20:4** For You met him beforehand with blessings of goodness; You set on his head a crown of precious stone.

**20:5** He asked You for life, and You gave him length of days forever and ever.

**20:6** Great is his glory in Your salvation; You will place glory and majesty upon him.

**20:7** For You will give him a blessing forever and ever; You will gladden him in joy with Your presence.

**20:8** For the king hopes in the Lord, and in the mercy of the Most High he will certainly not be shaken.

**20:9** May Your hand be found by all Your enemies; may Your right hand find all those who hate You.

**20:10** You will make them like a furnace of fire at the time of Your presence; Lord, in Your anger You will throw them into confusion, and fire will devour them.

**20:11** You will destroy their fruit from the earth, and their seed from the sons of men.

**20:12** For they directed evil things against You; they devised a plan which they will certainly not be able to establish.

**20:13** For You will make them a back; in Your remnants You will prepare their face.

**20:14** Be exalted, Lord, in Your power; we will sing and make music to Your mighty deeds.

## Translator Notes

- **20:4** — Greek ek lithou timiou ("of precious stone") renders the same Hebrew term translated "fine gold" in the Masoretic Text (compare 18:11).
- **20:9** — The Greek uses two optative wishes, first passive (heuretheie, "may ... be found by") and then active (heuroi, "may ... find"), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text uses active verbs.
- **20:10** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text (which shifts to third person, "the LORD in His wrath will swallow them up"), the Septuagint addresses the Lord directly in the vocative ("Lord, in Your anger You will throw them into confusion"), showing that 20:9-14 are addressed to God.
- **20:13** — "Make them a back" literally renders the Greek idiom for putting enemies to flight; in the second clause, Greek en tois periloipois sou ("in Your remnants") and hetoimaseis ("You will prepare") differ from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("with Your bowstrings You will aim").
