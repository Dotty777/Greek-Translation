## Text

**84:1** For the end; for the sons of Korah, a psalm.

**84:2** You were pleased with Your land, Lord; You turned back the captivity of Jacob.

**84:3** You forgave Your people their lawless deeds; You covered their sins. Interlude.

**84:4** You put an end to Your wrath; You turned back from the wrath of Your fury.

**84:5** Turn us back, God of our salvation, and turn Your fury away from us.

**84:6** Will You be angry with us forever? Or will You extend Your wrath from generation to generation?

**84:7** God, You will turn back and make us alive, and Your people will rejoice in You.

**84:8** Show us Your mercy, Lord, and may You give us Your salvation, Lord.

**84:9** I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me, for He will speak peace to His people and to His devout ones, and to those who turn their heart toward Him.

**84:10** Indeed, His salvation is near to those who fear Him, so that glory may dwell in our land.

**84:11** Mercy and truth met; righteousness and peace kissed.

**84:12** Truth sprang up from the earth, and righteousness peered down from heaven.

**84:13** For indeed, the Lord will give goodness, and our land will give its fruit.

**84:14** Righteousness will proceed before Him and will set His steps on a way.

## Translator Notes

- **84:1** — "For the end" translates Greek eis to telos, the standard Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew musical superscription often translated "for the choir director."
- **84:3** — "Interlude" translates Greek diapsalma, the Septuagint equivalent of Hebrew Selah, indicating a musical pause or interlude.
- **84:7** — Unlike the Hebrew text, which frames this verse as a question ("Will you not revive us again?"), the Greek expresses it as a declarative statement of confident expectation.
- **84:9** — The Greek includes "in me" (en emoi) and concludes with "those who turn their heart toward Him," whereas the Hebrew reads "let them not turn back to folly."
- **84:14** — "Will set His steps on a way" translates Greek thesei eis hodon ta diabemata autou, which can also be rendered idiomatically as "will make His steps into a way."
