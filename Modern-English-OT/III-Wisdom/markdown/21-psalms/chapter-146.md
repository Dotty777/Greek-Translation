## Text

**146:1** Hallelujah. Of Haggai and Zechariah. Praise the Lord, for a psalm is good; may praise be sweet to our God.

**146:2** The Lord is building Jerusalem, and He will gather together the dispersions of Israel.

**146:3** He is the One who heals those who are crushed in heart and binds up their fractures.

**146:4** He is the One who numbers multitudes of stars and calls all of them by name.

**146:5** Great is our Lord, and great is His strength, and there is no number to His understanding.

**146:6** The Lord lifts up the meek, but brings sinners low to the ground.

**146:7** Begin with thanksgiving to the Lord, sing psalms to our God on a lyre,

**146:8** to the One who clothes heaven with clouds, who prepares rain for the earth, who makes grass spring up on mountains, and green vegetation for the service of people,

**146:9** and who gives to the livestock their food and to the young of the ravens that call upon Him.

**146:10** He will not delight in the power of the horse, nor does He take pleasure in the legs of a man.

**146:11** The Lord takes pleasure in those who fear Him, and in all those who hope in His mercy.

## Translator Notes

- **146:1** — In the Septuagint, Psalms 146 and 147 divide Hebrew Psalm 147 into two psalms (MT 147:1-11 and 147:12-20), and Psalms 146-148 share the superscription attributing them to Haggai and Zechariah. The Greek reads 'for a psalm is good' rather than the Hebrew 'for it is good to sing praises to our God.'
- **146:4** — The Greek reads 'multitudes of stars' (πλήθη ἄστρων) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'the number of the stars.'
- **146:7** — The Greek imperative ἐξάρξατε literally means 'begin' or 'lead off' in song or musical performance.
- **146:8** — The clause 'and green vegetation for the service of people' is present in the Septuagint (paralleling Psalm 103:14 LXX / 104:14 MT) but absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
