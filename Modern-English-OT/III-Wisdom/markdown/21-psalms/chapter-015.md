## Text

**15:1** A pillar inscription of David. Guard me, Lord, for I have hoped in You.

**15:2** I said to the Lord, "You are my Lord."

**15:3** For the holy ones who are in His land, He made all His desires wonderful in them.

**15:4** Their weaknesses were multiplied; after these things they hurried. I will by no means assemble their assemblies of blood, nor will I ever make mention of their names through my lips.

**15:5** The Lord is the portion of my inheritance and of my cup; You are the One who restores my inheritance to me.

**15:6** Measuring lines have fallen to me in the finest places; for indeed, my inheritance is the finest to me.

**15:7** I will bless the Lord who gave me understanding; furthermore, even until night my kidneys instructed me.

**15:8** I kept seeing the Lord before me at all times, because He is at my right hand, so that I may not be shaken.

**15:9** For this reason my heart became glad and my tongue exulted; furthermore, my flesh also will dwell in hope.

**15:10** For You will not leave my soul behind in Hades, nor will You permit Your devout one to see corruption.

**15:11** You made known to me ways of life; You will fill me with gladness in Your presence; delights are in Your right hand forever.

## Translator Notes

- **15:1** — "A pillar inscription" renders Greek Στηλογραφία, the Septuagint equivalent for the obscure Hebrew psalm heading miktam. The Greek also addresses "Lord" (Κύριε) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "God."
- **15:2** — This Greek text ends the verse after "You are my Lord," without the additional clause ("for You have no need of my good things") found in some Septuagint manuscripts and corresponding to Hebrew Psalm 16:2b.
- **15:4** — The Septuagint reflects distinct readings of the underlying Hebrew here, yielding "weaknesses" (for MT "sorrows"), "after these things they hurried" (for MT "barter for another god"), and "assemble their assemblies of blood" (for MT "pour out their drink offerings of blood").
- **15:7** — "my kidneys" literally renders οἱ νεφροί μου, a biblical idiom for the inward mind, conscience, or seat of reflection.
- **15:10** — "corruption" renders Greek διαφθοράν (as quoted in Acts 2:27), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text word in Psalm 16:10 is often rendered "pit."
