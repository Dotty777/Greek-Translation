## Text

**47:1** A psalm of a song for the sons of Korah, on the second day of the week.

**47:2** Great is the Lord and exceedingly worthy of praise in the city of our God, His holy mountain,

**47:3** well-rooted, the exultation of all the earth; the mountains of Zion, the sides of the north, the city of the great King.

**47:4** God is known in her citadels when He comes to her aid.

**47:5** For look, the kings assembled; they came together.

**47:6** They themselves saw and thus marveled; they were troubled, they were shaken.

**47:7** Trembling took hold of them; there were labor pains like those of a woman giving birth.

**47:8** With a violent wind You will shatter ships of Tarshish.

**47:9** Just as we heard, so we saw in the city of the Lord of powers, in the city of our God; God founded her forever. Interlude.

**47:10** We have received, God, Your mercy in the midst of Your people.

**47:11** According to Your name, God, so also is Your praise to the ends of the earth; full of righteousness is Your right hand.

**47:12** Let Mount Zion be glad, let the daughters of Judea exult because of Your judgments, Lord.

**47:13** Go around Zion and encircle her; recount it in her towers.

**47:14** Set your hearts on her strength, and divide up her citadels, so that you may recount it to another generation.

**47:15** For this is our God forever and to the age of the age.

## Translator Notes

- **47:1** — The Greek superscription adds 'on the second day of the week' (literally 'on the second of the Sabbath'), reflecting the ancient liturgical assignment of this psalm to Monday; this phrase is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **47:9** — Greek 'diapsalma' ('Interlude') indicates a musical pause or interlude corresponding to Hebrew 'selah'; 'the Lord of powers' literally renders Greek 'Kyriou ton dynameon.'
- **47:10** — The Greek text here reads 'Your people' (laou sou), whereas some Greek witnesses and the Hebrew Masoretic Text read 'Your temple.'
- **47:12** — The Greek reads 'Judea' (Ioudaias) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'Judah.'
- **47:15** — The Greek text ends at 'to the age of the age,' without the concluding clause ('He will guide us...') present in some witnesses and the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
