## Text

**98:1** A psalm of David. The Lord has become king; let peoples be angry. He who sits upon the cherubim—let the earth be shaken.

**98:2** The Lord is great in Zion, and He is high above all the peoples.

**98:3** Let all give thanks to Your great name, for it is fearsome and holy.

**98:4** And the honor of a king loves justice; You have prepared uprightness; You have done justice and righteousness in Jacob.

**98:5** Exalt the Lord our God, and bow down to the footstool of His feet.

**98:6** He is holy. Moses and Aaron were among His priests, and Samuel among those who call upon His name; they called upon the Lord, and He listened to them.

**98:7** In a pillar of cloud He spoke to them; they kept His testimonies and the ordinances that He gave to them.

**98:8** Lord our God, You listened to them; God, You were forgiving to them, and taking vengeance upon all their practices.

**98:9** Exalt the Lord our God, and bow down toward His holy mountain, for holy is the Lord our God.

## Translator Notes

- **98:1** — The Septuagint includes the superscription "A psalm of David" (absent in Hebrew Ps 99) and renders the verb as "be angry" (ὀργιζέσθωσαν) rather than "tremble." The aorist ἐβασίλευσεν is ingressive ("has become king" or "reigned").
- **98:3** — The Greek text includes πάντες ("all"), which is absent in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and some Septuagint witnesses.
- **98:4** — The Septuagint reads τιμὴ βασιλέως ("the honor of a king") where the Hebrew has "the strength of a king," and εὐθύτητας is literally plural ("upright things" or "equities").
- **98:6** — This Greek text omits ὅτι ("for") at the end of v. 5 and places ἅγιός ἐστιν ("He is holy") at the beginning of v. 6, as in major uncials such as Sinaiticus and Vaticanus. Punctuated here as an independent refrain referring to the Lord (matching vv. 3 and 9), it could alternatively be read syntactically with the following name ("Holy is Moses...").
