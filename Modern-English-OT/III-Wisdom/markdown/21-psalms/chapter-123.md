## Text

**123:1** A song of the steps. "If the Lord had not been among us," let Israel now say,

**123:2** "if the Lord had not been among us when people rose up against us,

**123:3** then they would have swallowed us alive when their wrath raged against us.

**123:4** Then the water would have drowned us; our soul would have passed through a torrent;

**123:5** then our soul would have passed through the overwhelming water."

**123:6** Blessed is the Lord, who did not give us as prey to their teeth.

**123:7** Our soul was rescued like a sparrow from the snare of the hunters; the snare was shattered, and we were rescued.

**123:8** Our help is in the name of the Lord, the One who made heaven and earth.

## Translator Notes

- **123:1** — Greek en hēmin literally means "among us" (or "in us"), distinct from the Hebrew idiom "for us / on our side."
- **123:4** — In vv. 4-5, Greek makes "our soul" the grammatical subject ("our soul would have passed through") rather than the waters sweeping over the soul, and uses singular "the water" (to hydōr). The aorist indicatives continue the contrary-to-fact apodosis introduced with an in v. 3.
- **123:5** — Greek anypostaton literally means "not to be withstood" or "offering no footing," hence "overwhelming."
- **123:7** — Greek uses the passive of rhyomai ("was rescued ... were rescued"), emphasizing divine deliverance rather than an active escape, and pairs "hunters" (thēreuontōn) with "prey" (thēran) in v. 6.
