## Text

**100:1** A psalm of David. Mercy and justice I will sing to You, Lord;

**100:2** I will sing praise and understand in a blameless song. When will You come to me? I walked in the innocence of my heart, in the midst of my house.

**100:3** I did not set a lawless thing before my eyes; I hated those who commit transgressions; a crooked heart did not cling to me;

**100:4** when the evil person turned away from me, I did not know.

**100:5** The one secretly slandering his neighbor, him I drove out; one with an arrogant eye and an insatiable heart, with him I did not eat.

**100:6** My eyes were upon the faithful of the land, so that they might sit with me; one walking in a blameless way, he ministered to me.

**100:7** One practicing arrogance did not dwell in the midst of my house; one speaking unjust things did not prosper before my eyes.

**100:8** In the mornings I put to death all the sinners of the land, to utterly destroy from the city of the Lord all those who work injustice.

## Translator Notes

- **100:2** — This Greek text reads ἐν ᾠδῇ ἀμώμῳ ("in a blameless song") rather than ἐν ὁδῷ ἀμώμῳ ("in a blameless way," as in v. 6 and other witnesses). From διεπορευόμην ("I walked") through v. 8, the Septuagint consistently uses past tenses (imperfect and aorist) where the Hebrew uses future/modal imperfects.
- **100:4** — The Septuagint divides the clauses differently from the Hebrew, using a genitive absolute (ἐκκλίνοντος ἀπ᾽ ἐμοῦ τοῦ πονηροῦ, "when the evil person [or evil] turned away from me") followed by οὐκ ἐγίνωσκον ("I did not know") with no expressed object (implying "it" or "him").
- **100:5** — Greek οὐ συνήσθιον ("I did not eat with") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("I will not endure"), reflecting a different reading of the Hebrew consonants.
- **100:7** — The Septuagint reads ποιῶν ὑπερηφανίαν ("one practicing arrogance") and λαλῶν ἄδικα ("one speaking unjust things") where the Hebrew has "one who practices deceit" and "one who speaks falsehood."
