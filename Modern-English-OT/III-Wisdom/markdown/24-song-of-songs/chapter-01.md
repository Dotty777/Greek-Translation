## Text

**1:1** Song of Songs, which belongs to Solomon.

**1:2** Let him kiss me with kisses of his mouth, for your breasts are better than wine, and the fragrance of your perfumes is beyond all spices.

**1:3** Your name is perfume poured out. Therefore young women have loved you,

**1:4** they have drawn you; we will run after you, toward the fragrance of your perfumes. The king has brought me into his chamber. Let us exult and be glad in you; we will love your breasts more than wine; uprightness has loved you.

**1:5** I am black and beautiful, daughters of Israel, like the tents of Kedar, like the curtain of Solomon.

**1:6** Do not look at me because I am blackened, because the sun has looked sideways at me. The sons of my mother fought against me; they set me as a guard in vineyards; my own vineyard I did not guard.

**1:7** Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you pasture, where you rest at noon, so that I never become like one veiled beside the herds of your comrades.

**1:8** If you do not know yourself, beautiful one among women, go out yourself on the heels of the flocks, and pasture your young goats beside the tents of the shepherds.

**1:9** To my mare among the chariots of Pharaoh I have compared you, my companion.

**1:10** How lovely your cheeks have become like turtledoves, your neck like necklaces!

**1:11** We will make likenesses of gold for you with dots of silver.

**1:12** While the king was on his reclining couch, my nard gave forth its fragrance.

**1:13** A bundle of myrrh is my kinsman to me,

**1:14** in the vineyards of En-gedi.

**1:15** Look, you are beautiful, my companion; look, you are beautiful; your eyes are doves.

**1:16** Look, you are handsome, my kinsman, and indeed lovely; moreover, our couch is shaded;

**1:17** the beams of our houses are cedars, our paneled ceilings are cypresses.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — Greek reads "your breasts" (mastoi sou, also in 1:4), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonants from the Masoretic Text ("your love").
- **1:5** — This Greek text reads "daughters of Israel," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text and some other Greek witnesses read "daughters of Jerusalem."
- **1:8** — Greek translates the Hebrew ethical dative literally as a reflexive object, "If you do not know yourself" (ean me gnos seauten), rather than simply "If you do not know."
- **1:10** — Greek reads "like turtledoves" (hos trygones), taking the Hebrew word torim in the sense of "turtledoves" rather than "ornaments" or "plaits."
- **1:13** — Greek adelphidos literally denotes a nephew or close male relative ("kinsman") and is used throughout the Septuagint Song of Songs for the male beloved (Hebrew dod). This Greek text also omits the clauses at the end of 1:13 and start of 1:14 found in the Hebrew MT and some Greek witnesses, likely due to scribal eye-skip across the repeated phrase "my kinsman to me."
