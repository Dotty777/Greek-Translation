## Text

**2:1** I am a flower of the plain, a lily of the valleys.

**2:2** Like a lily in the midst of thorns, so is my companion among the daughters.

**2:3** Like an apple tree among the trees of the forest, so is my kinsman among the sons. In his shade I longed and sat down, and his fruit is sweet in my throat.

**2:4** Bring me into the house of wine; set love over me.

**2:5** Strengthen me with perfumes, cushion me with apples, for I am wounded by love.

**2:6** His left hand is under my head, and his right hand will embrace me.

**2:7** I have put you under oath, daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers and by the strengths of the field, that you do not rouse and awaken love until it wishes.

**2:8** The voice of my kinsman! Look, here he comes, leaping upon the mountains, bounding over the hills.

**2:9** My kinsman is like a gazelle or a fawn of stags upon the mountains of Bethel. Look, there he is behind our wall, peering through the windows, peeking through the lattices.

**2:10** My kinsman answers and says to me, "Rise up, come, my companion, my beautiful one, my dove.

**2:11** For look, the winter has passed; the rain has gone away, it has gone on its way;

**2:12** the flowers have appeared in the land, the time of pruning has arrived, the voice of the turtledove has been heard in our land;

**2:13** the fig tree has put forth its early figs; the vines are in blossom, they have given forth fragrance. Rise up, come, my companion, my beautiful one, my dove. And come,

**2:14** you, my dove, in the shelter of the rock, next to the outer wall, show me your appearance, and let me hear your voice, for your voice is sweet, and your appearance is lovely."

**2:15** Catch for us little foxes that ruin vineyards, and our vines are in blossom.

**2:16** My kinsman is mine, and I am his, the one who pastures among the lilies,

**2:17** until the day breathes and the shadows shift. Turn back; you, kinsman, be like a gazelle or a fawn of stags upon mountains of hollows.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — Greek renders the Hebrew geographic term Sharon as a common noun, "of the plain" (tou pediou), and reads "flower" (anthos) rather than "rose."
- **2:4** — Greek uses plural imperatives ("Bring me... set love over me") where the Hebrew MT has third-person narrative ("He brought me... his banner over me was love"), and in 2:5 reads "with perfumes" and "wounded" rather than "with raisin cakes" and "lovesick."
- **2:7** — Greek renders the Hebrew terms for gazelles and does as "powers" (dynamesin) and "strengths" (ischysesin, so also in 3:5). The conditional particle ean (literally "if") follows the Hebraic oath idiom functioning as a solemn prohibition ("that you do not").
- **2:9** — Greek includes the place-name "Bethel" ("upon the mountains of Bethel"), which is not in the Hebrew MT here; in 2:17 Greek translates Hebrew "mountains of Bether" as "mountains of hollows."
- **2:12** — Greek specifically renders the ambiguous Hebrew noun zamir as "pruning" (tes tomes) rather than "singing."
