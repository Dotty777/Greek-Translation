## Text

**5:1** Let my beloved come down into his garden and eat the fruit of his choice trees. I have entered into a garden, my sister, my bride; I have gathered my myrrh with my spices, I have eaten my bread with my honey, I have drunk my wine with my milk. Eat, companions, and drink and become drunk, brothers.

**5:2** I am sleeping, and my heart is awake. The voice of my beloved! He is knocking at the door: "Open to me, my sister, my companion, my dove, my perfect one, for my head has been filled with dew and my locks with the drops of the night."

**5:3** I have taken off my tunic; how should I put it on? I have washed my feet; how will I soil them?

**5:4** My beloved sent his hand from the opening, and my belly was stirred for him.

**5:5** I rose to open to my beloved; my hands dripped myrrh, my fingers full myrrh, on the handles of the bolt.

**5:6** I opened to my beloved; my beloved had passed by; my soul went out at his word; I sought him and did not find him, I called him and he did not answer me.

**5:7** The watchmen who go around in the city found me; they struck me, they wounded me; the watchmen of the walls took my shawl from me.

**5:8** I have put you under oath, daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers and by the strengths of the field: if you find my beloved, what should you report to him? That I am wounded with love.

**5:9** What is your beloved more than another beloved, beautiful one among women, what is your beloved more than another beloved, that you have put us under oath like this?

**5:10** My beloved is white and ruddy, selected out from ten thousands.

**5:11** His head is gold and phaz; his locks are palm shoots, black as a raven.

**5:12** His eyes are like doves beside full pools of water, washed in milk, sitting beside full pools.

**5:13** His cheeks are like bowls of spice producing aromatics; his lips are lilies dripping full myrrh.

**5:14** His hands are turned gold, filled with tarshish. His belly is an ivory tablet on a sapphire stone.

**5:15** His legs are marble pillars founded on golden bases; his appearance is like Lebanon, choice as cedars.

**5:16** His throat is sweetnesses, and he is wholly desire. This is my beloved, and this is my companion, daughters of Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Greek adelphidos (literally "nephew" or "dear kinsman") serves throughout Septuagint Song of Songs as a term of endearment ("beloved"). The Greek text here also reads "into a garden" (without "my"), "my bread" (where the Hebrew has "my honeycomb"), and "brothers" (where the Hebrew has "beloved/lovers").
- **5:8** — The Septuagint includes the oath formula "by the powers and by the strengths of the field" (absent from Hebrew 5:8, echoing 2:7 and 3:5) and reads tetromene agapes ("wounded with love") rather than "lovesick."
- **5:11** — Greek chrysion kai phaz ("gold and phaz") partially transliterates the Hebrew expression ketem paz ("pure gold").
- **5:13** — Greek phialai tou aromatos literally means "bowls of spice" (also in 6:2), and phyousai myrepsika ("producing aromatics") reflects a Hebrew participle read as "growing" rather than "towers."
- **5:14** — Greek tharseis ("tarshish") transliterates the Hebrew name of a precious stone (often identified as chrysolite or beryl), and the second clause literally places the ivory tablet "on a sapphire stone."
