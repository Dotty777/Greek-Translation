## Text

**4:1** Look, you are beautiful, my companion; look, you are beautiful. Your eyes are doves, apart from your silence. Your hair is like flocks of goats that were revealed from Gilead.

**4:2** Your teeth are like flocks of the shorn ones that came up from the washing, all of them bearing twins, and there is not a childless one among them.

**4:3** Like a scarlet cord are your lips, and your speech is lovely; like the rind of a pomegranate is your cheek, apart from your silence.

**4:4** Like the tower of David is your neck, built for Thalpioth; a thousand shields hang on it, all the javelins of the mighty ones.

**4:5** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle, that graze among lilies.

**4:6** Until the day breathes and the shadows move, I will go for myself to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of Lebanon.

**4:7** You are entirely beautiful, my companion, and there is no blemish in you.

**4:8** Come from Lebanon, bride, come from Lebanon; you will come and pass through from the beginning of faith, from the peak of Senir and Hermon, from dens of lions, from mountains of leopards.

**4:9** You have heartened us, my sister, my bride; you have heartened us with one of your eyes, with one, with a necklace of your neck.

**4:10** How much more beautiful have your breasts become than wine, and the fragrance of your garments above all spices?

**4:11** Your lips drip honeycomb, bride; honey and milk are under your tongue, and the fragrance of your garments is like the fragrance of Lebanon.

**4:12** A closed garden is my sister, my bride, a closed garden, a sealed spring.

**4:13** Your shoots are an orchard with fruit of choice trees, henna plants with spikenards;

**4:14** spikenard and saffron, calamus and cinnamon with all the trees of Lebanon, myrrh, aloes, with all the finest perfumes;

**4:15** a spring of a garden and a well of water, living and rushing from Lebanon.

**4:16** Awake, north wind; and come, south wind, and blow through my garden, and let my spices flow.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Greek ektos tes siopeseos sou literally reads "apart from your silence" (also in 4:3 and 6:7), reflecting the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew term for "veil," and apekalyphthesan literally means "were revealed" (contrast "appeared" in 6:5).
- **4:4** — Thalpioth is the Septuagint transliteration of an obscure Hebrew word often understood as "courses" or "armory," and Greek bolides specifically means "javelins" or "darts" rather than shields.
- **4:8** — The Septuagint translates the Hebrew mountain name Amana etymologically as pisteos ("faith"), yielding "from the beginning of faith."
- **4:9** — Greek ekardiosas hemas uses a rare verb coined from "heart" ("heartened") with a plural object ("us"), and the closing phrase literally reads "with one, with a necklace of your necks."
- **4:10** — Greek mastoi sou literally reads "your breasts" (compare 6:11), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonantal text where the Masoretic Text reads "your love."
