## Text

**6:1** Where has your kinsman gone, beautiful one among women? Where has your kinsman looked? And we will seek him with you.

**6:2** My brother has gone down into his garden, to bowls of spice, to shepherd in gardens and to gather lilies.

**6:3** I belong to my kinsman, and my kinsman belongs to me, the one who shepherds among the lilies.

**6:4** You are beautiful, my companion, like good pleasure, lovely as Jerusalem, astonishment like those drawn up in array.

**6:5** Turn away your eyes from facing me, for they have set me aflutter. Your hair is like flocks of goats that appeared from Gilead,

**6:6** your teeth are like flocks of the shorn ones that came up from the washing, all of them bearing twins, and there is not a childless one among them. Like a scarlet cord are your lips, and your speech is lovely.

**6:7** Like the rind of a pomegranate is your cheek, apart from your silence.

**6:8** There are sixty queens, and eighty concubines, and young women of whom there is no number;

**6:9** one is my dove, my perfect one; she is the only one to her mother, she is choice to the one who bore her. Daughters saw her and will call her blessed, queens and even concubines, and they will praise her.

**6:10** Who is this who peers forth like the dawn, beautiful as the moon, choice as the sun, astonishment like those drawn up in array?

**6:11** Into a nut-tree garden I went down to look among the produce of the brook, to see whether the vine blossomed, the pomegranates bloomed forth; there I will give my breasts to you.

**6:12** My soul did not know; it made me the chariots of Amminadab.

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — Here the Greek text reads Adelphos mou ("My brother") rather than adelphidos ("kinsman"), which appears in 6:1 and 6:3.
- **6:4** — The Septuagint translates the Hebrew city name Tirzah etymologically as eudokia ("good pleasure"), and renders the closing phrase literally as "astonishment like those drawn up in array" (repeated in 6:10).
- **6:6** — The second sentence ("Like a scarlet cord are your lips, and your speech is lovely.") is present in the Septuagint here (matching 4:3), though absent from the Hebrew text.
- **6:11** — The closing clause "there I will give my breasts to you" is a Septuagint addition in this verse, echoing 7:13 (Eng. 7:12).
- **6:12** — This verse is difficult in both Hebrew and Greek; following the Greek punctuation and double accusative, "my soul" is the subject of "did not know," and Ameinadab (Amminadab) is rendered as a proper name.
