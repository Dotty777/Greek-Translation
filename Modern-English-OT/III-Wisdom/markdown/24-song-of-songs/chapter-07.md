## Text

**7:1** Return, return, Shunammite; return, return, and we will look on you. What will you see in the Shunammite? She who comes like dances of the camps.

**7:2** Your steps are beautiful in sandals, daughter of Nadab. The proportions of your thighs are like necklaces, the work of a craftsman.

**7:3** Your navel is a carved mixing bowl, not lacking mixed wine; your belly is a heap of wheat fenced in with lilies.

**7:4** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle.

**7:5** Your neck is like an ivory tower. Your eyes are like pools in Heshbon, at the gates of the daughter of many. Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon, looking out toward the face of Damascus.

**7:6** Your head upon you is like Carmel, and the braid of your head is like purple; a king is bound in galleries.

**7:7** How beautiful you are, and how sweet you are, love, in your delights!

**7:8** This is your stature: you are likened to the palm tree, and your breasts to the clusters.

**7:9** I said, "I will climb the palm tree; I will take hold of its heights." And now your breasts will be like clusters of the vine, and the fragrance of your nose like apples,

**7:10** and your throat like the good wine, going straight to my kinsman, sufficing for my lips and teeth.

**7:11** I belong to my kinsman, and his turning is toward me.

**7:12** Come, my kinsman, let us go out into the field; let us lodge in the villages.

**7:13** Let us go early to the vineyards; let us see whether the vine has bloomed, the vine-blossom has bloomed, the pomegranates have bloomed; there I will give my breasts to you.

**7:14** The mandrakes have given off fragrance, and at our doors are all choice fruits; new alongside old, my kinsman, I have kept for you.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The Greek reads "Shunammite" (Σουμανεῖτις, the Septuagint form for the Shunammite in 1-2 Kings) rather than the Masoretic Text's "Shulammite," and introduces the response with "She who comes" (ἡ ἐρχομένη).
- **7:2** — The Greek takes Hebrew nadiv ("noble") as the proper name "Nadab" (Ναδάβ).
- **7:5** — The Greek translates the Hebrew place name Bath-rabbim literally as "daughter of many" (θυγατρὸς πολλῶν).
- **7:10** — Greek ἀδελφιδός (literally "nephew" or "kinsman") is used consistently throughout the Greek Song of Songs as the term of affection for the beloved (including in 8:1). The closing clause "sufficing for my lips and teeth" (ἱκανούμενος χείλεσίν μου καὶ ὀδοῦσιν) reflects a Greek reading distinct from the Masoretic Text ("gliding over the lips of sleepers").
- **7:13** — The Greek reads "my breasts" (τοὺς μαστούς μου) where the Masoretic Text reads "my love," differing only in Hebrew vocalization.
